A **missile** is an airborne ranged weapon capable of [self-propelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propulsion) flight aided usually by a [propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propellant), [jet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine) or [rocket engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine).

Historically, 'missile' referred to any [projectile](144553.md) that is thrown, shot or propelled towards a target; this usage is still recognized today with any unguided jet- or rocket-propelled weapons generally described as [rocket artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_artillery). Airborne [explosive devices](445224.md) without propulsion are referred to as [shells](337004.md) if fired by an [artillery piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_piece) and [bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb) if dropped by an aircraft.

Missiles are also generally [guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_guidance) towards specific targets termed as [guided missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missiles) or guided [rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_\(weapon\)). Missile systems usually have five system components: [targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeting_\(warfare\)), [guidance system](319341.md), flight system, [engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine), and [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead). Missiles are primarily classified into different types based on firing source and target such as [surface-to-surface](1483499.md), [air-to-surface](1544116.md), [surface-to-air](182664.md) and [air-to-air missiles](320568.md).

## Terminology

Missile is derived from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) "missilis" meaning "that may be thrown".[^cite_note-OE-1][^cite_note-CD-2] The first use of the word was in the early 1600s by [Philemon Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philemon_Holland).[^cite_note-OE-1] Historically, 'missile' referred to any [projectile](144553.md) that is capable of being thrown or a weapon that is shot or propelled towards a target.[^cite_note-CD-2][^cite_note-OED_missile-3] Later military definition refers to a missile as an airborne ranged weapon capable of [self-propelled flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propulsion) [guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_guidance) towards a specific target.[^cite_note-OED_missile-3][^cite_note-4] Any unguided [jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_propulsion) or [rocket](26301.md)-propelled weapons are generally described as [rocket artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_artillery),[^cite_note-5] and airborne [explosive devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_devices) without propulsion are referred to as [shells](337004.md) if fired by an [artillery piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_piece) and [bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombs) if dropped by a vehicle such as an [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## History

See also: [History of rockets and missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rockets_and_missiles)



A [V-1 flying bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb), amongst the first guided missiles

[Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_\(weapon\)) were the precursor to modern missiles and the first rockets were used as propulsion systems for [arrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow) as early as the 10th century in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_China).[^cite_note-8] Usage of rockets as weapons before modern rocketry is attested to in China, [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) and [Europe](9239.md). In the 18th century, [iron-cased rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysorean_rockets) were used in India by the [Kingdom of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore) and [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy) against the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) which was developed into [Congreve rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congreve_rocket) and used in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In the early 20th century, [American](19792942.md) [Robert Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Goddard_\(scientist\)) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) [Hermann Oberth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Oberth) developed early rockets propelled by jet engines.[^cite_note-11] In the 1920s, [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) developed solid fuel rockets at the [Gas Dynamics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_Dynamics_Laboratory).[^cite_note-12] Later, the first missiles to be used operationally were a series of rocket based [missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_War_II_guided_missiles_of_Germany) developed by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) during [World War II](32927.md) including the [V-1 flying bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb) and [V-2 rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket) which used mechanical [autopilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopilot) to keep the missile flying along a pre-chosen route.[^cite_note-VW-13] Less well known were a series of [anti-ship](140725.md) and [anti-aircraft](182664.md) missiles, typically based on a simple [radio control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_control) ([command guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_guidance)) system directed by the operator. However, these early systems in World War II were only built in small numbers.[^cite_note-VW-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] After World War II, the advent of the [Cold War](325329.md) and development of [nuclear weapons](21785.md) necessitated faster, more accurate and more versatile missiles with longer range and missile development was pursued by multiple countries.



Signatories of the [International Code of Conduct against Ballistic Missile Proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Code_of_Conduct_against_Ballistic_Missile_Proliferation).

### Proliferation restrictions

Various attempts have been made to control the spread of long range missiles capable of carrying [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction), such as the [Missile Technology Control Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Technology_Control_Regime) (1987) and the [International Code of Conduct against Ballistic Missile Proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Code_of_Conduct_against_Ballistic_Missile_Proliferation) (2002). These were voluntary and not [international treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_treaties). Though not legally binding, more than 140 countries have been part of these agreements, and provide prior information on missile programs, expected launches, and tests.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-IISS-17] The gradual introduction of missile launched [hypersonic glide vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_glide_vehicles) since 2019, [anti-satellite missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_missiles), and the deployment of dual use missiles capable of carrying both conventional and nuclear warheads are proliferation concerns.[^cite_note-IISS-17]

## Components

### Guidance, targeting and flight systems

Main article: [Missile guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_guidance)



A semi-active missile [homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homing_\(missile_guidance\)) system

A missile is most often guided by a guidance system though there are missiles that are unguided during some phases of flight.[^cite_note-Siouris-18] Missile guidance refers to methods of guiding a missile to its intended target. Effective guidance is important because reaching the target position accurately and precisely is a critical factor for its effectiveness.[^cite_note-19] The missile guidance system accomplishes this by four steps: tracking the target, computing the directions using tracking information, directing the computed inputs to steering control and steering the missile by directing inputs to motors or flight control surfaces.[^cite_note-USO-20] The guidance system consists of three sections: launch, mid-course and terminal with same or different systems employed across sections.[^cite_note-USO-20]



A simplified diagram of a solid-fuel rocket.&#x20;

1. A [propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_propellant) is packed with a hole in the middle

2. An [igniter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrotechnic_initiator) combusts the propellant

3. The hole acts as a [combustion chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion_chamber)

4. The hot exhaust is choked at the throat

5. Exhaust exits the rocket

The guidance and [homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homing_\(missile_guidance\)) systems are generally classified broadly into [active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_radar_homing), [semi-active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-active_radar_homing) and [passive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_radar_homing).[^cite_note-Siouris-18] In active homing systems, the missile carries the equipment needed to transmit the radiation needed to illuminate the target and receive the reflected energy. Once the homing is initiated, the missile [directs independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-and-forget) towards the target.[^cite_note-21] In semi-active systems, the source of the radiation is located outside the missile usually in the launch vehicle which might be an aircraft or a ship and the missile will receive the radiation to direct towards the target. As the source is located outside, the launch vehicle needs to continue supporting the missile till it is guided to the intended target.[^cite_note-Ctr-22] In a passive system, the missile relies solely on the information from the target.[^cite_note-Ctr-22] The homing system might use [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light) such as [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing), [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guidance) or [visible light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_light), [radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_guidance) or other [electromagnetic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation) to illuminate the target. Once the guidance system identifies the target, the target might required to be tracked continuously if it is in motion. A guidance system might use [INS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system) which consists of a [gyroscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroscope) and [accelerometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerometer) or might use [satellite guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_guidance) (such as [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System)) to track the missile’s position relative to a known target.[^cite_note-23] The missile computers will compute the flight path required to steer the missile towards the target.[^cite_note-Ctr-22] In [command guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_guidance), a human operator may operate it manually or a support or launch system will transmit commands by using either [optic fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optic_fiber) or [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-control) to guide the missile.[^cite_note-24] The flight system uses the data from the targeting or guidance system to maneuver the missile in flight which might be accomplished using [vectored thrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_vectoring) of engines or aerodynamic maneuvering using [flight control surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_control_surface) such as [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane_wing), fins and [canards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canard_\(aeronautics\)).[^cite_note-25]

### Propulsion

Missiles are powered by [propellants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propellants) igniting to produce thrust and might employ types of [rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine) or [jet engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine).[^cite_note-26] Rockets might be fueled by [solid-propellants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-propellant_rocket) which are comparatively easier to maintain and enables faster deployment. These propellants contain a [fuel](21188370.md) and [oxidizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidizer) mixed in select proportions with the grain size and burn chamber determining the rate and time of burn.[^cite_note-27] Larger missiles might use [liquid-propellant rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-propellant_rocket) where propulsion is provided by a single or combination of liquid fuels.[^cite_note-Sutton-28] A hybrid system uses solid rocket fuel with a liquid oxidizer.[^cite_note-Sutton-28] Jet engines are generally used in [cruise missiles](6590.md), most commonly of the [turbojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbojet) type, because of their relative simplicity and low frontal area while [turbofans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) and [ramjets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramjet) can also be theoretically used.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

From the 1950s to early 1970s, the United States operated [Project Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Rover) and [Project Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pluto) for developing [nuclear thermal rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_thermal_rocket) and nuclear-powered ramjet engines for powering cruise missiles respectively. Both performed static engine tests but were cancelled before deployment.[^cite_note-31] Ballistic missiles often have multiple stages and might use similar type or a mix of propulsion types. Some missiles may have additional propulsion from another source at launch such as a [catapult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catapult), [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) or [tank gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_gun).[^cite_note-32]

### Warhead

Missiles have one or more [explosive](10192.md) [warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead), although other weapon types may also be used.[^cite_note-33] The warheads of a missile provide its primary destructive power which might cause secondary destruction due to the [kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy_weapon) of the weapon.[^cite_note-WH-34]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> Warheads are most commonly of the [high explosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_explosive) type, often employing [shaped charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaped_charge) to exploit the accuracy of a guided weapon to destroy hardened targets. Warhead might carry [conventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_weapons), [incendiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incendiary_device), [nuclear](21785.md), [chemical](27179600.md), [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_weapon) or [radiological weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiological_weapon).[^cite_note-WH-34]<sup>: 65 </sup>

## Classification

See also: [List of missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_missiles)

Missiles can be classified into categories by various parameters such as type, launch platform and target, range, propulsion and guidance system.[^cite_note-35] Missiles are generally categorized into strategic or tactical missile systems. Tactical missile systems are short-range systems used to carry out a limited strike in a smaller area and might carry conventional or nuclear warheads.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-Brit1-37] Strategic missiles are long-range weapons used to target beyond the immediate vicinity and are mostly designed to carry nuclear warheads though other warheads can also be fitted.[^cite_note-Brit1-37]



Typical ballistic missile sequence:&#x20;

- 1\. Launches by firing motor1(A)

- 2\. Stage1 drops, motor2(B) ignites & shroud(E) ejects

- 3\. Motor3(C) ignites and separates

- 4\. Stage3 terminates and post-boost vehicle(D) separates

- 5\. Vehicle maneuvers itself and prepares re-entry vehicle (RV)

- 6\. RV with decoys and chaffs deploys

- 7\. RV re-enters the atmosphere

- 8\. Warhead(s) detonate on target

### Strategic

Strategic weapons are often classified into [cruise](6590.md) and [ballistic missiles](38025.md).[^cite_note-BCM-38] Ballistic missiles are powered by rockets during launch and follow a trajectory that arches upwards before descending to reach its intended target while cruise missiles are continuously powered by jet engines and travel at a flatter trajectory.[^cite_note-BCM-38]

#### Ballistic

A ballistic missile is powered by single or multiple rockets in stages initially before following an unpowered trajectory that arches upwards before descending to reach its intended target. It can carry both nuclear and conventional warheads.[^cite_note-ACS-39] A ballistic missile might reach [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic) or [hypersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic) speed and often travel [out of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoatmospheric) the [Earth's atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere_of_earth) before re-entry.[^cite_note-40] It usually has three stages of flight:[^cite_note-ACS-39]

- Boost phase: First phase at launch when one or more stages of rocket engine(s) fire propelling the missile

- Mid-course phase: Second phase when the rocket engines stop firing and the missile continues ascending upwards on the given trajectory

- Terminal phase: Final phase when the warhead(s) detach and descend towards the target



A [Tomahawk cruise missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) in flight

Ballistic missiles are categorized based on range as:[^cite_note-Brit-41][^cite_note-BCM-38]

- [Short range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_range_missile): less than 1,000 kilometres (620 mi)

- [Medium range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_range_ballistic_missile): 1,000 kilometres (620 mi) to 3,000 kilometres (1,900 mi)

- [Intermediate range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_range_ballistic_missile): 3,000 kilometres (1,900 mi) to 5,500 kilometres (3,400 mi)

- [Intercontinental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile): greater than 5,500 kilometres (3,400 mi)

#### Cruise

A [cruise missile](6590.md) is a guided missile that remains in the atmosphere and flies the major portion of its flight at a constant speed.[^cite_note-42] It is designed to deliver a large warhead over long distances with high precision, and is propelled by jet engines.[^cite_note-ACS-39] A cruise missile can be launched from multiple platforms and is often self-guided. It flies at lower speeds (often [subsonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsonic_flight) or [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic)) and close to the surface of the Earth, which expends more fuel but makes it difficult to detect.[^cite_note-BCM-38]

Main articles: [Anti-ship missile](140725.md), [anti-ship ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ship_ballistic_missile), and [anti-submarine missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_missile)

An anti-ship missile (AShM) is designed for use against large [boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boats) and [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ships) such as [destroyers](8779.md) and [aircraft carriers](2219.md). Most anti-ship missiles are of the [sea skimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_skimming) variety, and many use a combination of [inertial guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system) and [active radar homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_radar_homing). A large number of other anti-ship missiles use [infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing) to follow the heat that is emitted by a ship; it is also possible for anti-ship missiles to be guided by radio command all the way. Many anti-ship missiles can be launched from a variety of weapons systems including [surface warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_combatant), [submarines](28825.md), [fighter aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_plane), [maritime patrol aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft), [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_helicopter), [shore batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_battery), land vehicles and by [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry).[^cite_note-43]

[Anti-submarine missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_missile) is a [standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standoff_missile) [anti-submarine weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_weapon) variant of anti-ship missiles used to deliver an [explosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_material) warhead aimed directly at a [submarine](28825.md), a [depth charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_charge), or a [homing torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homing_torpedo).[^cite_note-44]

#### Anti-tank

Main article: [Anti-tank guided missile](37517.md)

An anti-tank guided missile (ATGM) is a guided missile primarily designed to hit and destroy [heavily armored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicle) [military vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_vehicle). ATGMs range in size from [shoulder-launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder-fired_missile) weapons, which can be transported by a single soldier, to larger tripod-mounted or vehicle and aircraft mounted missile systems. Earlier man-portable [anti-tank weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare) like [anti-tank rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_rifle) and magnetic [anti-tank mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_mine) had a short range but sophisticated antitank missiles can be directed to a longer target by several different guidance systems, including laser guiding, television camera, or [wire guiding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire-guided_missile).[^cite_note-45]

#### Air-to-air

Main article: [Air-to-air missile](320568.md)

An air-to-air missile (AAM) is a missile fired from a [fighter aircraft](10929.md) for the purpose of destroying another aircraft. AAMs are typically powered by one or more [rocket motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_motor), usually [solid fueled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-fuel_rocket) but sometimes [liquid fueled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-fuel_rocket). A [radar](25676.md) or [heat emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing) based homing system is generally used and sometimes can use a combination. Short range missiles used to [engage opposing aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfight) at ranges of less than 16 km often use infrared guidance while [long range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond-visual-range_missile) missiles mostly rely upon radar guidance.[^cite_note-Tact-46]

#### Air-to-surface

Main article: [Air-to-surface missile](1544116.md)

An air-to-surface missile (ASM) is a missile fired from an [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft), [strike fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_fighter) or an [attack helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter) for the purpose of destroying land based targets. Missiles are typically guided and unguided [glide bombs](233100.md) not considered missiles. The most common propulsion systems are rocket motor for short range and jet engines for long-range but [ramjets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramjet) are also used. Missile guidance is typically via [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guidance), infrared homing, [optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_guidance) or [satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_guidance). Air-to-surface missiles for ground attack by aircraft provide a higher [standoff distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standoff_distance) engaging targets from far away and out of range of low range air defenses.[^cite_note-Tact-46]

#### Surface-to-air

Main article: [Surface-to-air missile](182664.md)

A surface-to-air missile (SAM) is a missile designed to be launched from the ground to destroy [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), other missiles or flying objects. It is a type of [anti-aircraft system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiaircraft_warfare) and missiles have replaced most other forms of anti-aircraft weapons due to the increased range and accuracy. [Anti-aircraft guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_gun) are being used only for specialized close-in firing roles.[^cite_note-Wragg-47] Missiles can be mounted in clusters on vehicles or towed on trailers and can be hand operated by infantry. SAMs frequently use solid-propellants and may be guided by radar or infrared sensors or by a human operator using optical tracking.[^cite_note-Tact-46]

#### Surface-to-surface

Main article: [Surface-to-surface missile](1483499.md)

A surface-to-surface missile (SSM) is a missile designed to be launched from the ground or the sea and strike targets on land.[^cite_note-48] They may be fired from hand-held or vehicle mounted devices, from fixed installations or from a ship. They are often powered by a rocket engine or sometimes fired by an explosive charge, since the launching platform is typically stationary or moving slowly. They usually have fins and/or wings for [lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_\(force\)) and stability, although hyper-velocity or short-ranged missiles may use body lift or fly a ballistic trajectory.[^cite_note-Wragg-47] Most anti-tank and anti-ship missiles are part of surface-to-surface missile systems.[^cite_note-Tact-46]

#### Anti-satellite

Main article: [Anti-satellite weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon)

An anti-satellite weapon (ASAT) is a [space weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weapon) designed to incapacitate or destroy [satellites](27683.md) for [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_military) or [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics) purposes.[^cite_note-49] Although no ASAT system has yet been utilized in [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfare), a few countries have successfully shot down their own satellites to demonstrate their ASAT capabilities in a [show of force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_of_force).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] ASATs have also been used to remove decommissioned satellites.[^cite_note-53] ASAT roles include defensive measures against an adversary's space-based and nuclear weapons, a [force multiplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_multiplier) for a nuclear [first strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-emptive_nuclear_strike), a countermeasure against an adversary's [anti-ballistic missile defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ballistic_missile_defense_countermeasure) (ABM), an [asymmetric](46863.md) counter to a technologically superior adversary, and a [counter-value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countervalue) weapon.[^cite_note-54]
