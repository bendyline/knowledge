**Mushahid Hussain Syed** ([Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): مشاہد حسین سید‬; born 2 November 1952) is a Pakistani politician, and journalist who is currently the [Pakistan Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_senators_of_Pakistan) from the [Islamabad Capital Territory](15294.md) on the platform of the [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)), since 3 March 2018. He left PML-N in 2024.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

A [political journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_journalist) by profession, Hussain briefly served in the [Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif) administrations in 1990s and is known for his political positions that reflects the [national conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism) towards supporting the idea of [civilian control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military) of the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_military).[^cite_note-4] After playing a crucial role in the support of the decision-making towards the [nuclear weapons-testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-I) in response to [India](14533.md)'s in 1998, Hussain's tenure was abrupted by the [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) staged by [Pakistan Army Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) Gen. [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) and was held in prison on treason conspiracy in 1999.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Mushahid_Hussain-6] In 2003, he later defected to the [forward block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML\(Q\)) supporting the [presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Pakistani_presidential_election) of [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) and was elected the [Pakistan Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_senators_of_Pakistan) of the [Senate](761025.md).

Despite his support for the [PML(Q)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML\(Q\)), the forward block supporting Musharraf for several years, Hussain remained sympathetics towards the [PML(N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)) and eventually joined his original party after being ousted by the [forward block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML\(Q\)) in 2016.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] He is known as a proponent for strengthening the [foreign relations](10480332.md) with [China](5405.md) and [Central Asia](6742.md), having served the Chairman of the China-Pakistan Institute, a lobbying firm based in Islamabad.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Mushahid_Hussain-6]

## Early life and education

Mushahid Hussain Syed was born in [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot), [Punjab](24751.md) in [Pakistan](23235.md) into a military [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis) family on 2 November 1952.[^cite_note-Senate_Secretariat-11] His father, Amjad Hussain Syed, was an [army officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ranks_and_insignia_of_Pakistan) in the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md), retiring with rank of [army colonel](199023.md); his mother, Sameen Sayed, was a social activist.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_staff,_2013-12] Hussain is the fourth of five children, and both his parents were [activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Pakistan_Movement_activists) in the [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement) prior to 1947.[^cite_note-The_Nation,_et.al._2018-13] His mother, Sameen, died at the age of 83 in 2010.[^cite_note-The_Nation,_et.al._2018-13]

After his [matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation_in_Pakistan) from [Lahore](125315.md), Hussain went to attend the [Forman Christian College University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forman_Christian_College_University), where he graduated with [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in [journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalism) in 1974.[^cite_note-Senate_Secretariat-11] During this time, he went to the [United States](3434750.md) under the [Fulbright Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) to attend the [School of Foreign Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsh_School_of_Foreign_Service) of the [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University) in [Washington D.C.](108956.md) and graduated with the [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science_in_Foreign_Service_\(MSFS\)) in [foreign service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations), and briefly interned at the [United States Congress](31756.md).[^cite_note-MH_Syed,_2018-14] While studying in the United States, he was president of the Pakistan Students Association and was awarded a Congressional Internship to work in the United States Congress.[^cite_note-MH_Syed,_2018-14]

## Academic career

After completing his education, Hussain returned to [Pakistan](23235.md) and joined the directing Staff of the [Administrative Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Administrative_Staff_College) in Lahore to instruct courses on [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Relations) to the civil servants prior to their joining of the [Pakistan Foreign Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Service_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-MH_Syed,_2018-14] He also served as the [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) on the topics of [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) at the [Punjab University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_University,_Lahore) but was terminated from his employment when he spoke on a rally to oppose the [martial law government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fair_Play) in October 1979.[^cite_note-Pakistan_leadersonline-15]

## Journalistic career

After his termination, he began writing [syndicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicated_columnist) political columns on conservatism and joined the English daily [*The Muslim*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Pakistan), of which he became its [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) in 1982.[^cite_note-Pakistan_leadersonline-15] He also joined *[The Hindustan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindustan_Times)* where he wrote the [syndicated column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicated_columnist).[^cite_note-Pakistan_leadersonline-15]

In 1983, he joined the [Non-Aligned News Agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_News_Agencies_Pool) which he co-chaired in [New Delhi](51585.md).[^cite_note-Pakistan_leadersonline-15][^cite_note-Senate_Secretariat-11] In 1985–86, Hussain had established himself as a journalist and had enjoyed considerable fame when he wrote articles on politics and security.<sup>: 228 </sup>[^cite_note-Stanford_University_Press,_Khan,_2012-16]

In 1988, he reportedly lost his job as a journalist when he published an interview in [*The Muslim*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Pakistan) on the issue of [the country's covert atomic bomb program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_its_Nuclear_Deterrent_Program) when [President Zia-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Zia-ul-Haq) froze the funding of the news correspondent<sup>: 227 </sup>[^cite_note-Stanford_University_Press,_Khan,_2012-16]

## Political career

### Information minister in Sharif administrations and imprisonment

See also: [Conservatism in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Pakistan)



Hussain (left) discussing the Fair Trial bill at the RSIL meeting in 2017.

After covering the nationwide [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Pakistani_general_election) held in 1990, Hussain joined the [IDA government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islami_Jamhoori_Ittehad) led by [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif) and was inducted as an adviser on External Publicity and Foreign Affairs in the [first administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sharif_ministry), which he served until 1993.[^cite_note-Senate_Secretariat-11] He advised the Prime Minister Sharif on the issues relating to the foreign relations with the [United States](3434750.md), [India](14533.md), and the geostrategy in [Central Asia](6742.md), and was later elevated as the [Minister of Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Communications_\(Pakistan\)).<sup>: 43 </sup>[^cite_note-17] In 1994, he was appointed as the information secretary of the [PML(N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League) while he continued to write columns and articles on the issue of [national conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism) and security in country's major news and political correspondents.<sup>: 264 </sup>[^cite_note-18]

In 1997, he played a crucial role in the managing the [public relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations) of the election campaign of Nawaz Sharif in [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Pakistani_general_election) held in 1997, and reportedly quoted in favor of Nawaz Sharif: "*Nawaz will be the [Erbakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necmettin_Erbakan) of Pakistan*."<sup>: 155 </sup>[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Nasr-19] He participated and defended the [NA-125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NA-125_\(Lahore-III\)) constituency based on [Lahore](125315.md) and was elected as the member of national assembly on the platform of the [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)) whereas joining the Prime Minister Sharif's second administration as the [Information Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Minister_of_Pakistan) in his cabinet.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_staff,_2013-12]

In 1998, when [Indian Prime Minister](24452.md) [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee) ordered the [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shakti) in [Rajasthan](26291.md), Hussain was the first cabinet minister who spoke in the favor of conducting the [reciprocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-I) [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_nuclear_weapons_tests_of_Pakistan) during the decision-making process.<sup>: contents </sup>[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Hussain was reportedly known in the political circles as a "[War hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_hawk)."[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspapers,_Husain,_1999-22] He repeatedly praised Prime Minister Sharif on various public convention of authorizing the nuclear testing program to counter the India's aggression in the region to ensure the [mutual assured destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_assured_destruction) to India in case of a war.[^cite_note-Business_Recorder,_2016-23] He later went onto speak on the [CNN](62028.md), [Fox News](11121.md), and the [Face the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Nation) on [CBS News](359551.md) to defend the decision to conduct nuclear tests.[^cite_note-24] In 1998, he supported the Prime Minister Sharif's [relief of commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_Nawaz_Sharif's_relief_of_General_Jehangir_Karamat) of Gen. [Jehangir Karamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehangir_Karamat) as the [Chairman joint chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee), which he viewed as vital for government's [civilian control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military) of the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_military) at that time.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times,_CW_Dugger,_1998-25]

About the [Kargil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_war), he reportedly spoke in favor of the conflict and reportedly noted in the televised press conference: bitter fighting has "internationalized Kashmir", and hence was worth the loss of life suffered by the Pakistan Army as well as the Kashmiri freedom fighters involved.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspapers,_Husain,_1999-22] In 1999, his tenure was abrupted when Chairman joint chiefs Gen. [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) [staged a martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) against the elected civilian government, and placed him trial that sentenced him on a [solitary confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_confinement) on a conspiracy of treason; he was declared as the [prisoner of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_conscience).[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times,_Hussain,_2001-26] In 2000–01, he voiced for the [civilian control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military) of the military and the called for the [support for the democratic movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Pakistan) in an opined article which *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* profiled him as: "Pakistan Keeps Gag on Former Spokesman."[^cite_note-Pakistan_leadersonline-15]

### PML(Q)'s secretary and presidential campaign in 2008

In 2003, Hussain agreed upon a deal with the [Musharraf administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) and joined the [forward block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML\(Q\)), the [PML(Q)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(Q\)), led by [Shujaat Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shujaat_Hussain) and ultimately ran for the senate elections held in 2002.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_Ziauddin,_2003-27] His decision to defect against the [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality) lost the credibility that he had built over the several decades as a "respected journalist and disciplined politician",[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspapers,_Siddiqui,_2005-28] and was received severe public criticism from news correspondents of defending the Musharraf's [presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Pakistani_presidential_election) while serving as sitting [army chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-Dawn_Newspapers,_Siddiqui,_2005-28]

His credibility was greatly questioned in an editorial written in [*Dawn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_\(newspaper\)), the political correspondent, where Ziauddin claimed that Hussain filed for "nomination papers for the Senate elections reportedly with a letter of recommendation from [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)"[^cite_note-30] In 2004, he joined the taskforce as the President Musharraf's special envoy to begin negotiate with the Baloch political leader [Akbar Bugti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab_Akbar_Bugti), an ally of [Benazir Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto), which eventually failed.[^cite_note-Newsline_Magazine,_Hussain,_2004-31] he further earned notoriety when he was part of the same government which was eventually implicated in the Bugti's killing.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_staff,_2013-12] After the resignation of [President](419460.md) [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf), Hussain earned the nomination from the [PML-Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML-Q) to run his presidential campaign during the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Pakistani_presidential_election) held in 2008.[^cite_note-The_Nation,_Abrar_and_et.al,_2008-32] Hussain eventually lost the presidential elections with the largest margin, receiving only 44 votes out of 700 from the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_of_Pakistan) as opposed top [Asif Zardari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Zardari) (409/700) and [S.Z. Siddiqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeeduzzaman_Siddiqui) (216/700).[^cite_note-New_York_Times,_Parlez_&_Masood,_2008-33][^cite_note-34]

After [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Pakistani_Senate_election) as [Pakistan Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_senators) from [Punjab](24751.md) in 2012, Hussain came under severe public criticism when it was reported that he only paid [₨.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Rupee) 82. ([US¢](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_cents) 0.82) in filing the [tax returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_returns), and was called as a tax evader by various news correspondents.[^cite_note-The_Guardian._Boone,_2012-35][^cite_note-36]

On 4 June 2012, Hussain was unanimously elected as the Chairman of the Senate Defence Committee chairman.[^cite_note-37]

### Rejoining PML(N) and Pakistan Senator

In October 2016, [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif) appointed Hussain and [Shizra Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizra_Mansab_Ali_Khan) as the Prime Minister's Special Envoy on [unrest in Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kashmir_unrest) and went to attend the [Atlantic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Council), where he termed the violation of the [Indus Waters Treaty](3603967.md) as "an act of war."[^cite_note-38] He viewed the initiation of the [strategic corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPEC) as a crucial and one of the largest political achievement of Prime Minister Sharif, and has risen a strong political voice in support Prime Minister Sharif's economic policy.[^cite_note-39]

On 3 January 2017, the [PML-Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML-Q) ousted Hussain from the party due to his "tacit alignment" and support for the [PML(N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML-N)'s cause.[^cite_note-40] On 4 February 2018, he rejoined the [PML(N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PML-N) and spoke very high of Nawaz's services to the country.[^cite_note-Geo_News,_Ansari,_2018-41] In 2018, he was elected as the [Pakistan Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_senators) from [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FATA) on the platform of PML(N) after participating in the [senator elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistani_Senate_election) held in March 2018.[^cite_note-42]

## Other interests

### Lobbyist and Sinologist

On 1 October 2009, Hussain founded the Pakistan China Institute to promote the [bilateral relations](10480332.md) with China.[^cite_note-43] He has consistently lobbied for strengthening the relations with [China](5405.md) and the [Central Asian](6742.md) republics.[^cite_note-Pakistan_leadersonline-15] He also expressed apprehension about the United States’ role in Pakistan while endorsing a deepening of relations with China.[^cite_note-44]

## Books

Hussain has written political books covering issues such as the [civilian control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military), [geostrategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostrategy), and governance.[^cite_note-45]

- *An Apology to the Puppet Government, With a Policy of neither East nor the West*, Quetta: ARPE, 1986, 48 p.

- *Pakistan and the Changing Regional Scenario: Reflections of a Journalist*, Lahore: Progressive Publishers, 1988, 339 p.

- *Pakistan's Politics: The Zia Years*, Lahore: Progressive Publishers, 1990, 293 p.

- *Pakistan: Problems of Governance*, New Delhi: Konark Publishers, 1993, 166 p.
