The [United States of America](3434750.md) has formal diplomatic relations with all [United Nations member states](31969.md) except for [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan%E2%80%93United_States_relations), [Iran](310477.md), and [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea%E2%80%93United_States_relations). Between the two [United Nations observer states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_observers), the U.S. maintains official ties only with the [Holy See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_See%E2%80%93United_States_relations); and while it does not recognize [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93United_States_relations), it has engaged in diplomacy with the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) through dedicated initiatives within the [U.S. Embassy to Israel in Jerusalem](57417950.md). The U.S. also has a close but informal relationship with [Taiwan](15996902.md), which has had [no United Nations status since 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_2758_\(XXVI\)). Additionally, the U.S. recognized [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93United_States_relations) in 2008 and has since maintained official relations with it in spite of the country's [lack of any United Nations status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_and_the_United_Nations).

The [U.S. federal statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._federal_statute) concerning foreign relations can be found in [Title 22 of the United States Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_22_of_the_United_States_Code). The U.S. has the [second-most diplomatic missions](6654039.md) of any country in the world after [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_China).[^cite_note-1]

The era of Somoza family rule was characterized by rising inequality and political corruption, strong U.S. support for the government and its military, as well as a reliance on U.S.-based multinational corporations. The United States has promoted national reconciliation, encouraging Nicaraguans to resolve their problems through dialogue and compromise. In the Summer 2003 Nicaragua sent around 370 soldiers to the [Iraq War](5043324.md) as part of the coalition of countries that were engaging in war in this country. #### West Asia and Middle East

Main article: [United States foreign policy in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy_in_the_Middle_East)

The United States has many important allies in the [Greater Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Middle_East) region. These allies are [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey-United_States_relations), [Saudi Arabia](9753135.md), [Morocco](15993528.md), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan%E2%80%93United_States_relations), [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan%E2%80%93United_States_relations) (formerly), [Israel](3449549.md), [Egypt](15982815.md), [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait%E2%80%93United_States_relations), [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain%E2%80%93United_States_relations) and [Qatar](15994161.md). Israel and Egypt are leading recipients of United States foreign aid, receiving \$2.775 billion[^cite_note-181] and 1.75 billion[^cite_note-182] in 2010. Turkey is an ally of the United States through its membership in [NATO](21133.md), while all of the other countries except Saudi Arabia and Qatar are [major non-NATO allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_non-NATO_allies).

The United States toppled the government of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) during the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md).[^cite_note-183] [Turkey](11125639.md) is host to approximately 90 [B61 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb) at [Incirlik Air Base](1597543.md).[^cite_note-184] Other allies include [Qatar](8238258.md), where 3,500 US troops are based,[^cite_note-185] and [Bahrain](18933277.md), where the [United States Navy](20518076.md) maintains [NSA Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain), home of [NAVCENT](9880750.md) and the [Fifth Fleet](418867.md).

#### Southeast Asia

Many countries in the [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](106539.md) (ASEAN) are important partners for United States in both economic and geostrategic aspects. ASEAN's geostrategic importance stems from many factors, including: the strategic location of member countries, the large shares of global trade that pass through regional waters, and the alliances and partnerships which the United States shares with ASEAN member states. In July 2009, the United States signed ASEAN's Treaty of Amity and Cooperation, which establishes guiding principles intended to build confidence among its signatories with the aim of maintaining regional peace and stability.[^cite_note-260] Trade flows are robust and increasing between America and the ASEAN region.

At the strategic level, the relationship really came to prominence in the [Second World War](32927.md), when the two nations worked extremely closely in the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) against Japan, with General [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) undertaking his role as Supreme Allied Commander based in Australia, effectively having Australian troops and resources under his command. During this period, the cultural interaction between Australia and the United States were elevated to a higher level as over 1 million US military personnel moved through Australia during the course of the war. The relationship continued to evolve throughout the second half of the 20th century, and today now involves strong relationships at the executive and mid levels of government and the military, leading [Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_State_for_East_Asian_and_Pacific_Affairs), [Kurt M. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_M._Campbell) to declare that "in the last ten years, \[Australia] has ascended to one of the closest one or two allies \[of the US] on the planet".[^cite_note-287] It was also strengthened its relationship with the United States as Britain's influence in Asia declined. At the governmental level, United States-Australia relations are formalized by the [ANZUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZUS) treaty and the [Australia–United States Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93United_States_Free_Trade_Agreement). |
