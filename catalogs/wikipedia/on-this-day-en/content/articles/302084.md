# Decolonization

This article is about the undoing of colonialism. For the medical intervention pertaining to bacterial colonies, see [Decolonization (medicine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_\(medicine\)).

**Decolonization** is the ending of [colonialism](7299.md), the latter being the process whereby [imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism) nations establish and dominate foreign territories, often overseas.[^cite_note-1] The meanings and applications of the term are disputed. Some scholars of decolonization focus especially on [independence movements](286527.md) in the [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) and the collapse of global [colonial empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_empire).[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3]

As a movement to establish independence for colonized territories from their respective [metropoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropole), decolonization began in 1775 with the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) in North America against the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). The [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) in the 19th century saw the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire), the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), and [Portugal](23033.md) face decolonization with the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution), the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence), and the [independence of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil) from Portugal. A major wave of decolonization occurred in the [aftermath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Imperialism) of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), including in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_imperialism) and the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan). Another wave of decolonization occurred after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), and many countries gained their independence in the following years. The last wave of decolonization occurred after the [Cold War](325329.md) with the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_period_and_cessation_of_the_existence_of_the_Soviet_Union), the [independence of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_of_Free_Association#Associated_states), and the [handovers of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong) [and Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Macau).[^cite_note-4] Seventeen territories remain under the [United Nations](31769.md) classification of [non-self-governing territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories).

## Scope

According to David Strang, decolonization is achieved through the attainment of sovereign statehood with *de jure* recognition by the international community or through full incorporation into an existing sovereign state.[^cite_note-Strang90-5]

The [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) states that the [fundamental right](13831.md) to [self-determination](29269.md) is the core requirement for decolonization, and that this right can be exercised with or without political independence.[^cite_note-6] A [UN General Assembly Resolution in 1960](6075560.md) characterised colonial foreign rule as a violation of human rights.[^cite_note-\:02-7][^cite_note-UN-8] In states that have won independence, [Indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_decolonization) living under [settler colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler_colonialism) continue to make demands for decolonization and self-determination.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]



French anti-colonialist cartoon published in 1899 in *[Le Cri de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Cri_de_Paris)*. It denounces Western hypocrisy, which views indigenous revolt as an act of barbarism and colonial violence as an act of civilization.

[^cite_note-13]

Although discussions of [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony) and power, central to the concept of decolonization, can be found as early as the writings of [Thucydides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thucydides),[^cite_note-14] there have been several particularly active periods of decolonization in modern times. These include the [decolonization of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa), the [breakup of the Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) in the 19th century; of the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [Ottoman](22278.md), and [Russian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) following [World War I](4764461.md); of the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Empire), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_colonial_empire), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Empire), and [Japanese Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_colonial_empire) following [World War II](32927.md); and of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) at the end of the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-15]

Early studies of decolonisation appeared in the 1960s and 1970s. An important book from this period was *[The Wretched of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wretched_of_the_Earth)* (1961) by Martiniquan author [Frantz Fanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frantz_Fanon), which established many aspects of decolonisation that would be considered in later works. Subsequent studies of decolonisation addressed economic disparities as a legacy of colonialism as well as the annihilation of people's cultures. [Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C5%A9g%C4%A9_wa_Thiong'o) explored the cultural and linguistic legacies of colonialism in the influential book *[Decolonising the Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonising_the_Mind)* (1986).[^cite_note-\:6-16]

"Decolonization" has also been used to refer to the [intellectual decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_knowledge) from the colonizers' ideas that made the colonized feel inferior.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:1-18][^cite_note-19] Issues of decolonization persist and are raised contemporarily. In the [Americas](29833.md) and [South Africa](17416221.md), such issues are increasingly discussed under the term [decoloniality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoloniality).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## By area

In the two hundred years following the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in 1783, 165 colonies have gained independence from Western imperial powers.[^cite_note-\:7-22] Several analyses point to different reasons for the spread of anti-colonial political movements. Institutional arguments suggest that increasing levels of education in the colonies led to calls for popular sovereignty; [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism) analyses view decolonization as a result of economic shifts toward wage labor and an enlarged [bourgeois class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie); yet another argument sees decolonization as a diffusion process wherein earlier revolutionary movements inspired later ones.[^cite_note-\:7-22][^cite_note-Strang90-5][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-\:4-24] Other explanations emphasize how the lower profitability of colonization and the costs associated with empire prompted decolonization.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Some explanations emphasize how colonial powers struggled militarily against insurgents in the colonies due to a shift from 19th century conditions of "strong political will, a permissive international environment, access to local collaborators, and flexibility to pick their battles" to 20th century conditions of "apathetic publics, hostile superpowers, vanishing collaborators, and constrained options".[^cite_note-\:8-27] In other words, colonial powers had more support from their own region in pursuing colonies in the 19th century than they did in the 20th century, where holding on to such colonies was often understood to be a burden.[^cite_note-\:8-27]

A great deal of scholarship attributes the ideological origins of national independence movements to the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment). Enlightenment social and political theories such as individualism and [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) were central to the debates about national constitutions for newly independent countries.[^cite_note-28] Contemporary [decolonial scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoloniality) has critiqued the emancipatory potential of Enlightenment thought, highlighting its [erasure of Indigenous epistemologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_knowledge) and failure to provide [subaltern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaltern_\(postcolonialism\)) and [Indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) with liberty, equality, and dignity.[^cite_note-29]

### American Revolution

Main article: [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution)

Great Britain's [Thirteen North American colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) were the first to [declare independence](31874.md), forming the [United States of America](3434750.md) in 1776, and defeating Britain in the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Haitian Revolution

Main article: [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution)

The [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) was a revolt in 1789 and subsequent slave uprising in 1791 in the French colony of [Saint-Domingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue), on the [Caribbean](7822.md) island of [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola). In 1804, [Haiti](13373.md) secured independence from France as the [Empire of Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Empire_of_Haiti), which later became a republic.

### Spanish America

Main article: [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence)

The [Chilean Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Declaration_of_Independence) on 18 February 1818

The chaos of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) in Europe cut the direct links between Spain and its American colonies, allowing for the process of decolonization to begin.[^cite_note-32]

With the invasion of Spain by [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) in 1806, the American colonies declared autonomy and loyalty to King Ferdinand VII. The contract was broken and each of the regions of the Spanish Empire had to decide whether to show allegiance to the Junta of Cadiz (the only territory in Spain free from Napoleon) or have a junta (assembly) of its own. The economic monopoly of the metropolis was the main reason why many countries decided to become independent from Spain. In 1809, the independence wars of Latin America began with a revolt in La Paz, [Bolivia](3462.md). In 1807 and 1808, the [Viceroyalty of the River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_the_River_Plate) was invaded by the British. After their 2nd defeat, a Frenchman called Santiague de Liniers was proclaimed a new Viceroy by the local population and later accepted by Spain. In May 1810 in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), a Junta was created, but in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo) it was not recognized by the local government who followed the authority of the Junta of Cadiz. The rivalry between the two cities was the main reason for the distrust between them. During the next 15 years, the Spanish and Royalist on one side, and the rebels on the other fought in South America and Mexico. Numerous countries declared their independence. In 1824, the Spanish forces were defeated in the [Battle of Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ayacucho). The mainland was free, and in 1898, Spain lost [Cuba](5042481.md) and [Puerto Rico](23041.md) in the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War). Puerto Rico became an [unincorporated territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_territory) of the US, but Cuba became independent in 1902.

### Portuguese America

Main article: [Independence of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil)



[Prince Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_I_of_Brazil) proclaims himself Emperor of an independent Brazil on 7 September 1822.

The Napoleonic Wars also led to the severing of the direct links between Portugal and its only American colony, [Brazil](3383.md). Days before Napoleon invaded Portugal, in 1807 the Portuguese royal court [fled to Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_the_Portuguese_court_to_Brazil). In 1820 there was a [Constitutionalist Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Revolution_of_1820) in Portugal, which led to the return of the Portuguese court to Lisbon. This led to distrust between the Portuguese and the Brazilian colonists, and finally, in 1822, to the colony becoming independent as the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil), which later became a republic.

### British Empire

Main article: [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire)

The emergence of Indigenous political parties was especially characteristic of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), which was less ruthless than, for example, Belgium, in controlling political dissent. Driven by pragmatic demands of budgets and manpower the British made deals with the local politicians. Across the empire, the general protocol was to convene a constitutional conference in London to discuss the transition to greater self-government and then independence, submit a report of the constitutional conference to parliament, if approved submit a bill to Parliament at Westminster to terminate the responsibility of the United Kingdom (with a copy of the new constitution annexed), and finally, if approved, issuance of an Order of Council fixing the exact date of independence.[^cite_note-33]

After [World War I](4764461.md), several former German and Ottoman territories in the Middle East, Africa, and the Pacific were governed by the UK as [League of Nations mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate). Some were administered directly by the UK, and others by British dominions – [Nauru](21302.md) and the [Territory of New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_New_Guinea) by [Australia](4689264.md), [South West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Africa) by the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa), and [Western Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Samoa) by [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_New_Zealand).



Members of the Irish delegation for the [Anglo-Irish Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish_Treaty) negotiations in December 1921

[Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Egypt) became independent in 1922, although the UK retained security prerogatives, control of the [Suez Canal](29323.md), and effective control of the [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan). The [Balfour Declaration of 1926](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration_of_1926) declared the British Empire [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) as equals, and the 1931 [Statute of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931) established full legislative independence for them. The equal dominions were six– [Canada](5042916.md), [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Newfoundland), Australia, the [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State), New Zealand, and the Union of South Africa; Ireland had been brought into a union with Great Britain in 1801 creating the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland until the formation of the Irish Free State in 1922. However, some of the Dominions were already independent de facto, and even de jure and recognized as such by the international community. Thus, Canada was a founding member of the League of Nations in 1919 and served on the council from 1927 to 1930.[^cite_note-34] That country also negotiated on its own and signed bilateral and multilateral treaties and conventions from the early 1900s onward. Newfoundland ceded self-rule back to London in 1934. [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq), a League of Nations mandate, became independent in 1932.

In response to a growing [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), the UK made successive reforms to the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), culminating in the [Government of India Act 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935). These reforms included creating elected legislative councils in some of the [provinces of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India). [Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohandas_Karamchand_Gandhi), India's independence movement leader, led a peaceful resistance to British rule. By becoming a symbol of both peace and opposition to British imperialism, many Indians began to view the British as the cause of India's problems leading to a newfound sense of [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) among its population. With this new wave of Indian nationalism, Gandhi was eventually able to garner the support needed to push back the British and create an independent India in 1947.[^cite_note-35]



British Empire in 1952

Africa was only fully drawn into the colonial system at the end of the 19th century. In the north-east the continued independence of the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire) remained a beacon of hope to pro-independence activists. However, with the anti-colonial wars of the 1900s (decade) barely over, new modernizing forms of Africa nationalism began to gain strength in the early 20th century with the emergence of Pan-Africanism, as advocated by the Jamaican journalist [Marcus Garvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Garvey) (1887–1940) whose widely distributed newspapers demanded swift abolition of European imperialism, as well as republicanism in Egypt. [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah) (1909–1972) who was inspired by the works of Garvey led the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)) (Ghana) to independence from colonial rule.

Independence for the colonies in Africa began with the independence of [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan) in 1956, and the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)) (Ghana) in 1957. All of the British colonies on mainland Africa became independent by 1966, although [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia)'s unilateral declaration of independence in 1965 was not recognized by the UK or internationally.

Some of the British colonies in Asia were directly administered by British officials, while others were ruled by local monarchs as [protectorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) or in [subsidiary alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary_alliance) with the UK.

In 1947, [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) was [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) into the independent dominions of the [Union of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India) and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan). Hundreds of [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state), states ruled by monarchs in a treaty of subsidiary alliance with Britain, were [integrated into India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_integration_of_India) and Pakistan. India and Pakistan fought several wars over the former princely state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)). [French India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_India) was integrated into India between 1950 and 1954, and India annexed [Portuguese India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India) in 1961, and the [Kingdom of Sikkim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sikkim) merged with India by popular vote in 1975.

#### Violence, civil warfare, and partition



Surrender of [Lord Cornwallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Cornwallis) at [Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown) in 1781

Significant violence was involved in several prominent cases of decolonization of the British Empire; partition was a frequent solution. In 1783, the North American colonies were divided between the independent United States, and [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America), which later became Canada.

The [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857) was a major uprising in India against British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company). It was characterized by massacres of civilians on both sides. It was not a movement for independence, however, and only a small part of India was involved. In the aftermath, the British pulled back from modernizing reforms of Indian society, and the level of organised violence under the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) was relatively small. Most of that was initiated by repressive British administrators, as in the [Amritsar massacre of 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar#Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre), or the police assaults on the [Salt March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_March) of 1930.[^cite_note-36] Large-scale communal violence broke out between Hindus and Muslims and between Muslims and Sikhs after the British left in 1947 in the newly independent [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) of India and Pakistan. Much later, in 1970, further communal violence broke out within Pakistan in the detached eastern part of East Bengal, which became independent as [Bangladesh](3454.md) in 1971.

[Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cyprus), which came under full British control in 1914 from the Ottoman Empire, was culturally divided between the majority [Greek element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Cypriots) (which demanded "[enosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enosis)" or union with Greece) and the minority Turks. London for decades assumed it needed the island to defend the Suez Canal; but after the Suez crisis of 1956, that became a minor factor, and Greek violence became a more serious issue. [Cyprus](5593.md) became an independent country in 1960, but ethnic violence escalated until 1974 when [Turkey](11125639.md) invaded and partitioned the island. Each side rewrote its own history, blaming the other.[^cite_note-37]

[Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) became a [British mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) from the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) after World War I, initially including [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan). During that war, the British gained support from Arabs and Jews by making promises to both (see [McMahon–Hussein Correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMahon%E2%80%93Hussein_Correspondence) and [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration)). Decades of [ethno—religious violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercommunal_conflict_in_Mandatory_Palestine) reached a climax with the [UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Partition_Plan) and the [ensuing war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_War). The British eventually pulled out, and the former Mandate territory was divided between [Israel](9282173.md), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Palestine_Protectorate).[^cite_note-38]

### French Empire

Further information: [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire)



French poster about the "[Madagascar War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Hova_Wars)"

After World War I, the colonized people were frustrated at France's failure to recognize the effort provided by the French colonies (resources, but more importantly colonial troops – the famous *[tirailleurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirailleurs)*). Although in [Paris](22989.md) the [Great Mosque of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Paris) was constructed as recognition of these efforts, the French state had no intention to allow [self-rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-rule), let alone grant [independence](62450.md) to the colonized people. Thus, [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) in the colonies became stronger in between the two wars, leading to [Abd el-Krim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ibn_'Abd_al-Karim_al-Khattabi)'s [Rif War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif_War_\(1920\)) (1921–1925) in [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Morocco) and to the creation of [Messali Hadj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messali_Hadj)'s [Star of North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_North_Africa) in [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism_and_resistance_in_Algeria) in 1925. However, these movements would gain full potential only after World War II.

After World War I, France administered the former Ottoman territories of [Syria](7515849.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md), and the former German colonies of [Togoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Togoland) and [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cameroons), as League of Nations mandates. Lebanon declared its independence in 1943, and Syria in 1945.

In some instances, decolonization efforts ran counter to other concerns, such as the rapid increase of [antisemitism](1078.md) in Algeria in the course of the nation's resistance to French rule.[^cite_note-39]

Although France was ultimately a victor of World War II, Nazi Germany's occupation of France and its North African colonies during the war had disrupted colonial rule. On 27 October 1946, France adopted a new constitution creating the [Fourth Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fourth_Republic), and substituted the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union) for the colonial empire. However power over the colonies remained concentrated in France, and the power of local assemblies outside France was extremely limited. On the night of 29 March 1947, a [Madagascar](18964.md) [nationalist uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar_revolt) led the French government headed by [Paul Ramadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ramadier) ([Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_Fran%C3%A7aise_de_l'Internationale_Ouvri%C3%A8re)) to violent repression: one year of bitter fighting, 11,000–40,000 Malagasy died.[^cite_note-40]



Captured [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union) soldiers from [Điện Biên Phủ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dien_Bien_Phu), escorted by Vietnamese communist troops, 1954

After the end of World War II, the Viet Minh launched the [August Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Revolution) and declared Vietnamese independence in September, although Allied troops reoccupied the territory afterwards. In late 1946, the Viet Minh [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hanoi_\(1946\)) French troops in Hanoi, leading to the [Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War) (1946–54). France later recognized the independence of the [State of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Vietnam), the [Kingdom of Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Laos), and the [Kingdom of Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cambodia_\(1953%E2%80%931970\)), while also [recognizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lys%C3%A9e_Accords) the unity of Vietnam (whose territories has been split into three separate regions under French colonial rule) and supported the anti-communist faction in this country against the [communists](400546.md) who fought in the name of anti-colonialism in 1949. The war thus became part of the world-wide [Cold War](325329.md). Cambodia and Laos became fully independent in late 1953, Vietnam became fully independent on 4 June 1954, and the [Geneva Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conference_\(1954\)) of 21 July 1954 left Vietnam divided into the [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam) and [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam) with the fact that France recognized communists gaining the North. After North Vietnamese [military victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon) in April 1975, Vietnam would be *de jure* united under a communist government on 2 July 1976.

In 1956, [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Morocco) and [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tunisia) gained their independence from France. In 1960, eight independent countries emerged from [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa), and five from [French Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Equatorial_Africa). The [Algerian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War_of_Independence) raged from 1954 to 1962. To this day, the Algerian war – officially called a "public order operation" until the 1990s – remains a trauma for both France and Algeria. Philosopher [Paul Ricœur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ric%C5%93ur) has spoken of the necessity of a "decolonisation of memory", starting with the recognition of the [1961 Paris massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Paris_massacre) during the Algerian war, and the decisive role of African and especially North African immigrant manpower in the *[Trente Glorieuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trente_Glorieuses)* post–World War II economic growth period. In the 1960s, due to economic needs for post-war reconstruction and rapid economic growth, French employers actively sought to recruit manpower from the colonies, explaining today's [multiethnic population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_France).

### Ottoman Empire

The Ottoman Empire saw its periphery also through the lens of a [civilizing mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilizing_mission).[^cite_note-q025-41][^cite_note-r101-42] The integration of some provinces such as [Yemen Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_Eyalet) was contested.[^cite_note-b871-43] Some authors view the [dissolution of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) as a form of decolonization.[^cite_note-g773-44][^cite_note-k717-45]

### After 1918

Further information: [New Imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Imperialism)

#### United States

Main articles: [American imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism) and [Timeline of United States military operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_United_States_military_operations)

A union of former colonies itself, the United States approached imperialism differently from the other Powers. Much of its energy and rapidly expanding population was directed westward across the North American continent against English and French claims, the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) and Mexico. The [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) were sent to [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation), often unwillingly. With support from Britain, its [Monroe Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Doctrine) reserved the Americas as its sphere of interest, prohibiting other states (particularly Spain) from recolonizing the newly independent polities of [Latin America](18524.md). However, France, taking advantage of the American government's distraction during the Civil War, intervened militarily in Mexico and set up a French-protected monarchy. Spain took the step to [occupy the Dominican Republic and restore colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_occupation_of_the_Dominican_Republic). The Union victory in the Civil War in 1865 forced both France and Spain to accede to American demands to evacuate those two countries. America's only African colony, [Liberia](17791.md), was formed privately and achieved independence early; Washington unofficially protected it. By 1900, the U.S. advocated an [Open Door Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Door_Policy) and opposed the direct division of China.[^cite_note-46]



[Manuel L. Quezón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quez%C3%B3n), the first president of the [Commonwealth of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) (from 1935 to 1944)



[Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands) in [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia) administered by the United States from 1947 to 1986

After 1898 direct intervention expanded in Latin America. The United States purchased Alaska from the Russian Empire in 1867 and annexed Hawaii in 1898. Following the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) in 1898, the US added most of Spain's remaining colonies: [Puerto Rico](23041.md), [Philippines](23440.md), and [Guam](11974.md). Deciding not to annex Cuba outright, the U.S. established it as a [client state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state) with obligations including the perpetual lease of [Guantánamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guant%C3%A1namo_Bay) to the U.S. Navy. The attempt of the first governor to void the island's constitution and remain in power past the end of his term provoked a rebellion that provoked a reoccupation between 1906 and 1909, but this was again followed by devolution. Similarly, the [McKinley administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinley_administration), despite prosecuting the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War) against a [native republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_the_Philippines), set out that the [Territory of the Philippine Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States#Former_unincorporated_territories_of_the_United_States_\(incomplete\)) was eventually granted independence.[^cite_note-47] In 1917, the U.S. purchased the [Danish West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_West_Indies) (later renamed the [US Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Virgin_Islands)) from [Denmark](76972.md) and Puerto Ricans became full U.S. citizens that same year.[^cite_note-48] The US government declared Puerto Rico the territory was no longer a colony and stopped transmitting information about it to the United Nations Decolonization Committee.[^cite_note-49] As a result, the [UN General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_General_Assembly_Resolution_748) removed Puerto Rico from the [U.N. list of non-self-governing territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories). Four referendums showed little support for independence, but much interest in statehood such as Hawaii and Alaska received in 1959.[^cite_note-50]

The Monroe Doctrine was expanded by the [Roosevelt Corollary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Corollary) in 1904, providing that the United States had a right and obligation to intervene "in flagrant cases of such wrongdoing or impotence" that a nation in the Western Hemisphere became vulnerable to European control. In practice, this meant that the United States was led to act as a collections agent for European creditors by administering customs duties in the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) (1905–1941), [Haiti](13373.md) (1915–1934), and elsewhere. The intrusiveness and bad relations this engendered were somewhat checked by the [Clark Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Memorandum) and renounced by President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt)'s "[Good Neighbor Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Neighbor_Policy)".

The [Fourteen Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points) were preconditions addressed by President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) to the European powers at the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)) following [World War I](4764461.md). In allowing allies France and Britain the former colonial possessions of the German and Ottoman Empires, the US demanded of them submission to the [League of Nations mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate), in calling for *V. A free, open-minded, and absolutely impartial adjustment of all colonial claims, based upon a strict observance of the principle that in determining all such questions of sovereignty **the interests of the populations concerned must have equal weight** with the equitable government whose title is to be determined.* See also point XII.

After [World War II](32927.md), the U.S. poured tens of billions of dollars into the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan), and other grants and loans to Europe and Asia to rebuild the world economy. At the same time American military bases were established around the world and direct and indirect interventions continued in [Korea](16772.md), [Indochina](32611.md), Latin America (*inter alia*, the [1965 occupation of the Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Civil_War)), Africa, and the Middle East to oppose Communist movements and insurgencies. Since the dissolution of the Soviet Union, the United States has been far less active in the Americas, but invaded [Afghanistan](19666611.md) and [Iraq](5043324.md) following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001, establishing army and air bases in [Central Asia](6742.md).

#### Japan



U.S. troops in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), September 1945

Before World War I, Japan had gained several substantial colonial possessions in East Asia such as Taiwan (1895) and Korea (1910). Japan joined the allies in World War I, and after the war acquired the [South Seas Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Seas_Mandate), the former German colony in Micronesia, as a [League of Nations Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_Mandate). Pursuing a colonial policy comparable to those of European powers, Japan settled significant populations of ethnic Japanese in its colonies while simultaneously suppressing Indigenous ethnic populations by enforcing the learning and use of the [Japanese language](15606.md) in schools. Other methods such as public interaction, and attempts to eradicate the use of [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language), [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien), and [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) among the Indigenous peoples, were seen to be used. Japan also set up the [Imperial Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Universities) in Korea ([Keijō Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keij%C5%8D_Imperial_University)) and Taiwan ([Taihoku Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University)) to compel education.

In 1931, Japan seized [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria) from the Republic of China, setting up a puppet state under [Puyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyi), the last Manchu emperor of China. In 1933 Japan seized the Chinese province of [Rehe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehe_Province), and incorporated it into its Manchurian possessions. The [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) started in 1937, and Japan occupied much of eastern China, including the Republic's capital at [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing). An estimated 20 million Chinese died during the 1931–1945 war with Japan.[^cite_note-51]

In December 1941, the empire of Japan joined [World War II](32927.md) by invading the European and U.S. colonies in Southeast Asia and the Pacific, including [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina), [Hong Kong](13404.md), the Philippines, Burma, [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya), [Indonesia](14579.md), [Portuguese Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Timor), and others. Following its surrender to the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in 1945, Japan was deprived of all its colonies with a number of them being returned to the original colonizing Western powers. The [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [declared war on Japan in August 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_War_\(1945\)), and shortly after occupied and annexed the southern [Kuril Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuril_Islands), which Japan [still claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuril_Islands_dispute).

### After 1945

#### Planning for decolonization

Decolonization was often not extensively planned, instead occurring as a response to politics in the colony, politics at home, and increasing international pressure. Immediately following the war there was a wave of decolonization throughout Asia. This was followed by the Middle East, and in the 1960s sub-Saharan Africa. These waves saw most large colonies become independent, with many remaining colonies being smaller islands. Many of the smallest colonies would not become independent, instead joining either with nearby colonies and countries or becoming full parts of their administering country.[^cite_note-Christopher2002-52]

##### U.S. and Philippines

In the United States, the two major parties were divided on the acquisition of the Philippines, which became a major campaign issue in 1900. The Republicans, who favored permanent acquisition, won the election, but after a decade or so, Republicans turned their attention to the Caribbean, focusing on building the [Panama Canal](24849.md). President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson), a Democrat in office from 1913 to 1921, ignored the Philippines, and focused his attention on Mexico and Caribbean nations. By the 1920s, the peaceful efforts by the Filipino leadership to pursue independence proved convincing. When the Democrats returned to power in 1933, they worked with the Filipinos to plan a smooth transition to independence. It was scheduled for 1946 by [Tydings–McDuffie Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tydings%E2%80%93McDuffie_Act) of 1934. In 1935, the Philippines transitioned out of territorial status, controlled by an appointed governor, to the semi-independent status of the [Commonwealth of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines). Its constitutional convention wrote a new constitution, which was approved by Washington and went into effect, with an elected governor [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) and legislature. Foreign Affairs remained under American control. The Philippines built up a new army, under general [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur), who took leave from his U.S. Army position to take command of the new army reporting to Quezon. The Japanese occupation 1942 to 1945 disrupted but did not delay the transition. It took place on schedule in 1946 as [Manuel Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Roxas) took office as president.[^cite_note-53]

##### Portugal



[Portuguese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Army) special *[caçadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%A7adores)* advancing in the African jungle in the early 1960s, during the [Angolan War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_War_of_Independence)



The territory of the Portuguese empire in the 1800s

As a result of its pioneering [discoveries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discoveries), [Portugal](23033.md) had a large and particularly long-lasting colonial empire which had begun in 1415 with the [conquest of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Ceuta) and ended only in 1999 with the handover of [Portuguese Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau) to China. In 1822, Portugal [lost control of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil), its largest colony.

From 1933 to 1974, [Portugal was an authoritarian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)) (ruled by [António de Oliveira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Oliveira_Salazar)). The regime was fiercely determined to maintain the country's colonial possessions at all costs and to aggressively suppress any insurgencies. In 1961, [India annexed Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Goa) and by the same year nationalist forces had begun organizing in Portugal. Revolts (preceding the [Portuguese Colonial War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Colonial_War)) spread to [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola), [Guinea Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Guinea) and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Mozambique).[^cite_note-54] [Lisbon](18091.md) escalated its effort in the war: for instance, it increased the number of natives in the colonial army and built strategic hamlets. Portugal sent another 300,000 European settlers into Angola and Mozambique before 1974. That year, [a left-wing revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution) inside Portugal overthrew the existing regime and encouraged pro-Soviet elements to attempt to seize control in the colonies. The result was a very long and extremely difficult multi-party [Civil War in Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War), and lesser insurrections in Mozambique.[^cite_note-55]

##### Belgium

Belgium's empire began with the annexation of the Congo in 1908 in response to international pressure to bring an end to the [terrible atrocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrocities_in_the_Congo_Free_State) that had taken place under [King Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Leopold_II_of_Belgium)'s privately run [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State). It added [Rwanda and Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruanda-Urundi) as League of Nations mandates from the former German Empire in 1919. The colonies remained independent during the war, while Belgium was occupied by the Germans. There was no serious planning for independence, and exceedingly little training or education provided. The [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo) was especially rich, and many Belgian businessmen lobbied hard to maintain control. Local revolts grew in power and finally, the Belgian king suddenly announced in 1959 that independence was on the agenda – and it was hurriedly arranged in 1960, for country bitterly and deeply divided on social and economic grounds.[^cite_note-56]

##### Netherlands



Dutch soldiers in the East Indies during the [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution), 1946



The territory of the Dutch empire before [World War II](32927.md)

The Netherlands had spent centuries building up its empire. By 1940 it consisted mostly of the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md), corresponding to what is now Indonesia. Its massive oil reserves provided about 14 percent of the Dutch national product and supported a large population of ethnic Dutch government officials and businessmen in [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies) (now Jakarta) and other major cities. The Netherlands was overrun and almost starved to death [by the Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Niederlande) during the war, and Japan sank the Dutch fleet in seizing the East Indies. In 1945 the Netherlands could not regain these islands on its own; [it did so by depending on British military help](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Surabaya) and [American financial grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan). By the time Dutch soldiers returned, an independent government under [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) was in power, originally set up by the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan). The Dutch both abroad and at home generally agreed that Dutch power depended on an expensive war to regain the islands. Compromises were negotiated, but were trusted by neither side. When the [Indonesian Republic successfully suppressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madiun_Affair) a large-scale communist revolt, the United States realized that it needed the nationalist government as an ally in the Cold War. Dutch possession was an obstacle to American Cold War goals, so Washington forced the Dutch to grant full independence. A few years later, Sukarno nationalized all [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) properties and expelled all [ethnic Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo_people)—over 300,000—as well as several hundred thousand ethnic Indonesians who supported the Dutch cause. In the aftermath, the Netherlands prospered greatly in the 1950s and 1960s but nevertheless public opinion was bitterly hostile to the United States for betrayal. The Dutch government eventually gave up on claims to Indonesian sovereignty in 1949, after American pressure.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] The Netherlands also had one other major colony, Dutch Guiana in [South America](26769.md), which became independent as [Suriname](26828.md) in 1975.

#### United Nations trust territories

Main article: [United Nations trust territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_trust_territories)

When the United Nations was formed in 1945, it established trust territories. These territories included the [League of Nations mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) territories which had not achieved independence by 1945, along with the former [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland). The [Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands) was transferred from Japanese to US administration. By 1990 all but one of the trust territories had achieved independence, either as independent states or by merger with another independent state; the [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md) elected to become a commonwealth of the United States.

#### The emergence of the Third World (1945–present)

Newly independent states organised themselves in order to oppose continued economic colonialism by former imperial powers. The [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) constituted itself around the main figures of [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), the first Prime Minister of India, [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno), the Indonesian president, [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito) the Communist leader of [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), and [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser), head of Egypt.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] In 1955 these leaders gathered at the [Bandung Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_Conference) along with [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno), the leader of Indonesia, and [Zhou Enlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Enlai), Premier of the People's Republic of China.[^cite_note-maounknown-62][^cite_note-Bogetić-63] In 1960, the [UN General Assembly](31957.md) voted on the [Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples](6075560.md). The next year, the first Non-Aligned Movement conference was held in [Belgrade](55904.md) (1961),[^cite_note-64] and was followed in 1964 by the creation of the [United Nations Conference on Trade and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_Trade_and_Development) (UNCTAD) which tried to promote a [New International Economic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_International_Economic_Order) (NIEO).[^cite_note-auto4-65][^cite_note-66] The NIEO was opposed to the 1944 [Bretton Woods system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_system), which had benefited the leading states which had created it, and remained in force until 1971 after the United States' suspension of convertibility from dollars to gold. The main principles of the NIEO are:

1. The sovereign equality of all States, with non-interference in their internal affairs, their effective participation in solving world problems and the right to adopt their own economic and social systems;

2. Full sovereignty of each State over its natural resources and other economic activities necessary for development, as well as regulation of transnational corporations;

3. Just and equitable relationship between the price of raw materials and other goods exported by developing countries, and the prices of raw materials and other goods exported by the developed countries;

4. Strengthening of bilateral and multilateral international assistance to promote industrialization in the developing countries through, in particular, the provisioning of sufficient financial resources and opportunities for transfer of appropriate techniques and technologies.

   [^cite_note-auto1-67]



The [UN Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Human_Development_Index) (HDI) is a quantitative index of development, an alternative to the classic [Gross Domestic Product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_Domestic_Product) (GDP), which some use as a proxy to define the [Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World). While the GDP only calculates economic wealth, the HDI includes [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy), [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) and [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) as fundamental factors of a good [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life). Countries in [North America](21139.md), the [Southern Cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cone), [Europe](9239.md), [East Asia](19605700.md), and [Oceania](22621.md) generally have better standards of living than countries in [Central Africa](539606.md), [East Africa](260987.md), parts of the [Caribbean](18956035.md), and [South Asia](21566765.md).

The UNCTAD however was not very effective in implementing the NIEO, and social and economic inequalities between industrialized countries and the Third World grew throughout the 1960s until the 21st century. The [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) which followed the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) (October 1973) was triggered by the OPEC which decided an embargo against the US and Western countries, causing a fourfold increase in the price of oil, which lasted five months, starting on 17 October 1973, and ending on 18 March 1974. OPEC nations then agreed, on 7 January 1975, to raise crude oil prices by 10%. At that time, OPEC nations – including many who had recently nationalized their oil industries – joined the call for a New International Economic Order to be initiated by coalitions of primary producers. Concluding the First OPEC Summit in Algiers they called for stable and just commodity prices, an international food and agriculture program, technology transfer from North to South, and the democratization of the economic system. But industrialized countries quickly began to look for substitutes to OPEC petroleum, with the oil companies investing the majority of their research capital in the US and European countries or others, politically sure countries. The OPEC lost more and more influence on the world prices of oil.

The [second oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_energy_crisis) occurred in the wake of the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md). Then, the 1982 [Latin American debt crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_debt_crisis) exploded in Mexico first, then Argentina and Brazil, which proved unable to pay back their debts, jeopardizing the existence of the international economic system.

The 1990s were characterized by the prevalence of the [Washington consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_consensus) on [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) policies, "[structural adjustment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_adjustment)" and "[shock therapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_therapy_\(economics\))" for the former Communist states.

#### Decolonization of Africa

Main article: [Decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa)



British decolonisation in Africa

The decolonization of North Africa and sub-Saharan Africa took place in the mid-to-late 1950s, very suddenly, with little preparation. There was widespread unrest and organized revolts, especially in French Algeria, Portuguese Angola, the Belgian Congo and British Kenya.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

In 1945, Africa had four independent countries – Egypt, Ethiopia, Liberia, and South Africa.

After Italy's defeat in World War II, France and the UK occupied the former Italian colonies. [Libya](17633.md) became an independent kingdom in 1951. [Eritrea](17238590.md) was merged with Ethiopia in 1952. Italian Somaliland was governed by the UK, and by Italy after 1954, until its independence in 1960.



Comorians protest against [Mayotte referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Mahoran_status_referendum) on becoming an overseas department of France, 2009

By 1977, European colonial rule in mainland Africa had ended. Most of Africa's island countries had also become independent, although [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) and [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) remain part of France. However the black majorities in [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia) and South Africa were disenfranchised until 1979 in [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia), which became [Zimbabwe-Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe-Rhodesia) that year and Zimbabwe the next, and until 1994 in South Africa. [Namibia](21292.md), Africa's last UN Trust Territory, became independent of South Africa in 1990.

Most independent African countries exist within prior colonial borders. However [Morocco](19291.md) merged [French Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Morocco) with [Spanish Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Morocco), and [Somalia](27358.md) formed from the merger of [British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somaliland) and [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland). Most African countries became independent as [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). [Morocco](19291.md), [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho), and [Eswatini](27451.md) remain monarchies under dynasties that predate colonial rule. African countries cooperate in various multi-state associations. The [African Union](10634933.md) includes all 55 African states. There are several regional associations of states, including the [East African Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Community), [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community), and [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States), some of which have overlapping membership.

## Aspects

Typical challenges of decolonization include [state-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-building), [nation-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-building), and [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development).

### State-building

Main article: [State-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-building)

After independence, the new states needed to establish or strengthen the institutions of a sovereign state, i.e. governments, laws, a military, schools, administrative systems, and so on. The amount of self-rule granted prior to independence, and assistance from the colonial power and/or international organizations after independence, varied greatly between colonial powers, and between individual colonies.[^cite_note-Glassner,_Martin_Ira_1980-80]

Except for a few absolute monarchies, most post-colonial states are either [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) or [constitutional monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy). These new states had to devise [constitutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution), [electoral systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_system), and other institutions of [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy).

### Nation-building

Main article: [Nation-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-building)



The **Black Star Monument** in [Accra](56824.md), built by [Ghana](12067.md)'s first president [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah) to commemorate the country's independence

Nation-building is the process of creating a sense of identification with, and loyalty to, the state.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Nation-building projects seek to replace loyalty to the old colonial power, and/or tribal or regional loyalties, with loyalty to the new state. Elements of nation-building include creating and promoting symbols of the state like a flag, a coat of arms and an anthem, monuments, official histories, national sports teams, codifying one or more Indigenous [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language), and replacing colonial place-names with local ones.[^cite_note-Glassner,_Martin_Ira_1980-80] Nation-building after independence often continues the work began by independence movements during the colonial period.

#### Language policy

From the perspective of [language policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_policy) (or [language politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_politics)), "linguistic decolonization" entails the replacement of a colonizing (imperial) power's language with a given colony's indigenous language in the function of [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language). With the exception of colonies in [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia), linguistic decolonization did not take place in the former colonies-turned-independent states on the other continents ("Rest of the World").[^cite_note-83] [Linguistic imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_imperialism) is the imposition and enforcement of one dominant language over other languages, and one response to this form of imperialism is linguistic decolonization.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

#### Cinematography

Kenyan writer [Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C5%A9g%C4%A9_wa_Thiong'o) has written about colonization and decolonization in the film universe. Born in Ethiopia, filmmaker [Haile Gerima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Gerima) describes the "colonization of the unconscious" he describes experiencing as a child:[^cite_note-kato2007-86]

> ...as kids, we tried to act out the things we had seen in the movies. We used to play cowboys and Indians in the mountains around Gondar...We acted out the roles of these heroes, identifying with the cowboys conquering the Indians. We didn't identify with the Indians at all and we never wanted the Indians to win. Even in Tarzan movies, we would become totally galvanized by the activities of the hero and follow the story from his point of view, completely caught up in the structure of the story. Whenever Africans sneaked up behind Tarzan, we would scream our heads off, trying to warn him that 'they' were coming".

In Asia, [kung fu cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kung_fu_film) emerged at a time Japan wanted to reach Asian populations in other countries by way of its cultural influence. The surge in popularity of kung fu movies began in the late 1960s through the 1970s. Local populations were depicted as protagonists opposing "imperialists" (foreigners) and their "Chinese collaborators".[^cite_note-kato2007-86]

### Repatriation

In a 2023 paper on the political theory of settler colonialism, Canadian academics Yann Allard-Tremblay and Elaine Coburn posit that: "In Africa, the Middle East, South America, and much of the rest of the world, decolonization often meant the expulsion or departure of most colonial settlers. In contrast, in settler colonial states like [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [Canada](5042916.md), and the [United States](3434750.md), settlers have not left, even as independence from the metropole was gained... The systemic oppression and domination of the colonized by the colonizer is not historical — firmly in the past — but ongoing and supported by radically unequal political, social, economic, and legal institutions."[^cite_note-87]

Decolonization is not an easy matter in colonies with large settler populations, particularly if they have been there for several generations. When settlers remain in former colonies after independence, colonialism is ongoing and takes the form of [settler colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler_colonialism), which is highly resistant to decolonisation.[^cite_note-88] [Repatriation](1481067.md) of existing colonizers or prevention of immigration of additional [colonizers](7299.md) can be seen as [return migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_migration) and [opposition to immigration](6491626.md).[^cite_note-89]

In a few cases, settler populations have been [repatriated](1481067.md). For instance, the decolonization of [Algeria](358.md) by France was particularly uneasy due to the large European population (see also *[pied noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied_noir)*),[^cite_note-Cook-90] which largely evacuated to France when Algeria became independent.[^cite_note-ladepeche-91] In [Zimbabwe](34399.md), former [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia), [Robert Mugabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mugabe) seized property from white African farmers, killing several of them, and forcing the survivors to emigrate.[^cite_note-Cybriwsky-92][^cite_note-93] A large Indian community lived in [Uganda](31816.md) as a result of Britain colonizing both India and East Africa, and [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin) [expelled them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Asians_from_Uganda) for domestic political gain.[^cite_note-Drive-94]

### Economic development

Main article: [Economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development)

Newly independent states also had to develop independent economic institutions – a national currency, banks, companies, regulation, tax systems, etc.

Many colonies were serving as resource colonies which produced raw materials and agricultural products, and as a captive market for goods manufactured in the colonizing country. Many decolonized countries created programs to promote [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization). Some [nationalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalized) industries and infrastructure, and some engaged in [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform) to redistribute land to individual farmers or create collective farms.

Some decolonized countries maintain strong economic ties with the former colonial power. The [CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc) is a currency shared by 14 countries in West and Central Africa, mostly former French colonies. The CFA franc is guaranteed by the French treasury.

After independence, many countries created regional economic associations to promote trade and economic development among neighboring countries, including the [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](106539.md) (ASEAN), the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS), and the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md).

#### Effects on the colonizers

[John Kenneth Galbraith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kenneth_Galbraith) argues that the post–World War II decolonization was brought about for economic reasons. In *A Journey Through Economic Time*, he writes:&#x20;

> "The engine of economic well-being was now within and between the advanced industrial countries. Domestic [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) – as now measured and much discussed – came to be seen as far more important than the erstwhile colonial trade.... The economic effect in the United States from the granting of independence to the Philippines was unnoticeable, partly due to the [Bell Trade Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Trade_Act), which allowed American monopoly in the economy of the Philippines. The departure of India and Pakistan made small economic difference in the United Kingdom. [Dutch](21148.md) economists calculated that the economic effect from the loss of the great Dutch empire in Indonesia was compensated for by a couple of years or so of domestic post-war economic growth. The end of the colonial era is celebrated in the history books as a triumph of national aspiration in the former colonies and of benign good sense on the part of the colonial powers. Lurking beneath, as so often happens, was a strong current of economic interest – or in this case, disinterest."

In general, the release of the colonized caused little economic loss to the colonizers. Part of the reason for this was that major costs were eliminated while major benefits were obtained by alternate means. Decolonization allowed the colonizer to disclaim responsibility for the colonized. The colonizer no longer had the burden of obligation, financial or otherwise, to their colony. However, the colonizer continued to be able to obtain cheap goods and labor as well as economic benefits (see [Suez Canal Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal_Crisis)) from the former colonies. Financial, political and military pressure could still be used to achieve goals desired by the colonizer. Thus decolonization allowed the goals of colonization to be largely achieved, but without its burdens.[^cite_note-95]

On 10 December 2010, the [United Nations](31769.md) published its official [decree](322949.md), announcing the *[Third International Decade for the Eradication of Colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Decade_for_the_Eradication_of_Colonialism)* wherein the United Nations declared its "renewal of the call to States Members of the United Nations to speed up the process of decolonization towards the complete elimination of colonialism".[^cite_note-100] In an article, scholar John Quintero writes, "given the modern emphasis on the equality of states and inalienable nature of their sovereignty, many people do not realize that these non-self-governing structures still exist".[^cite_note-101] Sergei Chernaivsky reportedly "said that \[...] hot-spot insurgencies" and the ensuing "crisis in international relations" which impeded "peacemaking efforts of the United Nations" caused the "attention" of the United Nations to get "further diverted from the social and economic agenda" of the "decolonization process" and pivoted "towards 'firefighting and extinguishing' armed conflicts". Activist advocates have stressed that the United Nations "\[remains] the last refuge of hope for peoples under the yolk  \[*sic*] of colonialism".[^cite_note-\:3-102] Furthermore, on 19 May 2015, [UN Secretary-General](162415.md) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) addressed the attendants of the Caribbean Regional Seminar on Decolonization, urging international political leaders to "build on \[the success of precedent decolonization efforts and] towards fully eradicating colonialism by 2020".[^cite_note-\:3-102]

The sovereignty of the [Chagos Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago) in the Indian Ocean is [disputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago_sovereignty_dispute) between the United Kingdom and [Mauritius](19201.md). In February 2019, the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) in [The Hague](30269.md) ruled that the United Kingdom must transfer the islands to Mauritius as they were not legally separated from the latter in 1965.[^cite_note-103] On 22 May 2019, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) debated and adopted a resolution that affirmed that the Chagos Archipelago "forms an integral part of the territory of Mauritius".[^cite_note-104] The UK does not recognize Mauritius' sovereignty claim over the Chagos Archipelago.[^cite_note-105] In October 2020, Mauritian Prime Minister [Pravind Jugnauth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravind_Jugnauth) described the British and American governments as "hypocrites" and "champions of double talk" over their response to the dispute.[^cite_note-106]

In May 2026, the [East Turkistan Government in Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Turkistan_Government_in_Exile) and the East Turkistan National Movement petitioned the [Special Committee on Decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Committee_on_Decolonization) to list [East Turkistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Turkistan) (which China administers as the [Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Uyghur_Autonomous_Region)) as a Non-Self-Governing Territory and to apply the [Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples](6075560.md) to it, describing it as under Chinese colonial occupation rather than a willing part of China.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] As of June 2026, the Committee had not added the territory to its list.[^cite_note-112]

## Effects of decolonization

A 2019 study found that "[democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) levels increased sharply as colonies gained internal autonomy in the period immediately before their independence. However, conflict, revenue growth, and economic growth did not systematically differ before and after independence."[^cite_note-113]

David Strang writes that the loss of their empires turned France and Britain into "second-rate powers".[^cite_note-114]

## Criticism

Some articles extend the meaning of decolonization beyond independence or equal rights for colonized peoples to include broader economic, cultural and psychological aspects of the colonial experience.[^cite_note-\:6-16][^cite_note-115] Extending the meaning of decolonization beyond political [independence](62450.md) has been disputed and received criticism.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

In the *[American Political Science Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Political_Science_Review)*, Kevin Duong argued that decolonization movements eventually failed on their own terms or "terminat\[ed]": "decolonization's termination in a world of independent states might have been the twentieth century's greatest act of [disenfranchisement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disenfranchisement)." He lamented that the anti-colonial goals for self-determination had given way to "minimalist [electoral democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_democracy) and an [individuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individuate) conception of [the \[universal\] suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage)" alongside "universalization of the [nation-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-state) form" through national independence: "As dependent territories became nation-states, they lost their voice in [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropole) assemblies whose affairs affected them long after independence."[^cite_note-119]
