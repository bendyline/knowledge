The **People's Rally for Progress** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): التجمع الشعبي من أجل التقدم, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ạltãjamũʿu ạlsẖãʿ̊bīũ min ạảjal ạltãqadũma*; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Rassemblement populaire pour le Progrès*, **RPP**) is a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) in [Djibouti](17207794.md). It has dominated politics in the country since 1979, initially under the rule of President [Hassan Gouled Aptidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Gouled_Aptidon). Today it is led by President [Ismaïl Omar Guelleh](392341.md) and is in a coalition government with [Front for the Restoration of Unity and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_for_the_Restoration_of_Unity_and_Democracy) (FRUD) and other parties. The RPP tends to hold more influence among the [Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issa_\(clan\)) population.

The RPP was founded in [Dikhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikhil) on 4 March 1979.[^cite_note-LaNation-5] It was declared the sole legal party in October 1981, retaining this status until multiparty politics was introduced in the [September 1992 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_constitutional_referendum,_1992).[^cite_note-6] At the party congress held on 19–20 March 1997, Gouled Aptidon was re-elected as RPP President and a 125-member Central Committee was elected.[^cite_note-7] It contested the [December 1997 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_parliamentary_election,_1997) in alliance with the moderate faction of FRUD (which had signed a peace agreement with the government in December 1994), and this alliance won 79% of the vote, taking all 65 seats in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Djibouti).[^cite_note-8]

On 4 February 1999, President Gouled Aptidon announced that he would retire at the time of the next election, and an extraordinary congress of the RPP, chose Guelleh as its presidential candidate.[^cite_note-9] As the joint candidate of the RPP and moderate wing of the [Front for the Restoration of Unity and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_for_the_Restoration_of_Unity_and_Democracy) (FRUD), Guelleh won the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_presidential_election,_1999) held on 9 April 1999 with 74% of the vote, defeating his only challenger, the independent candidate [Moussa Ahmed Idriss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Ahmed_Idriss?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-10]

In the [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_parliamentary_election,_2003) held on 10 January 2003, the party was part of the Union for a Presidential Majority (*Union pour la Majorité Présidentielle*, UMP), that won 63% of the popular vote and all 65 seats.

The RPP opened its Eighth Ordinary Congress on 4 March 2004,[^cite_note-LaNation-5][^cite_note-11] coinciding with the party's 25th anniversary. At this congress, Guelleh was unanimously re-elected as RPP President by acclamation for another three-year term, and the RPP Central Committee was expanded from 180 to 250 members.[^cite_note-LaNation-5] On 4 March 2007, the RPP held its Ninth Ordinary Congress; Guelleh was again re-elected as RPP President, and three women were added to the Political Bureau, expanding it to 17 members.[^cite_note-12] It contested the [February 2008 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_parliamentary_election,_2008) together with its UMP coalition partners,[^cite_note-IPU-13][^cite_note-14] and the UMP again won all 65 seats amidst an opposition boycott.[^cite_note-IPU-13]
