**Russians** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): русские, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *russkiye* [\[ˈruskʲɪje\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/84/Ru-%D1%80%D1%83%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5.ogg/Ru-%D1%80%D1%83%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/84/Ru-%D1%80%D1%83%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5.ogg/Ru-%D1%80%D1%83%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) are an [East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) native to [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). Their [mother tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_tongue) is [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), the most spoken [Slavic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages). The majority of Russians adhere to [Orthodox Christianity](10186.md), ever since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). By total numbers, they compose the largest [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) and [European nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998173–4The_Russians-46]

Genetic studies show that Russians are closely related to [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians), [Ukrainians](145144.md), as well as [Estonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonians), [Latvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvians), [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians), and [Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finns).[^cite_note-2008ydna-45][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalyarchukDerenko2004877–900-47][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovskyRootsi2008236–50-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovsky201223-49] They were formed from East Slavic tribes, and their cultural ancestry is based in [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'). The Russian word for the Russians is derived from the [people of Rus' and the territory of Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Rus',_Russia_and_Ruthenia). Russians share many historical and cultural traits with other European peoples, and especially with other East Slavic ethnic groups, specifically [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) and [Ukrainians](145144.md).

The vast majority of Russians live in native [Russia](25391.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998173–4The_Russians-46] but notable minorities are scattered throughout other [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states) such as [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Belarus), [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Kazakhstan), [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Moldova), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine), and the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_the_Baltic_states). A large [Russian diaspora](5835042.md) (sometimes including Russian-speaking non-Russians), estimated at 25 million people,[^cite_note-50] has developed all over the world, with notable numbers in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Americans), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Germany), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Brazilians), and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Canadians).

## Ethnonym

There are two [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) words which are commonly translated into English as "Russians". One is [русские](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D1%80%D1%83%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9#Russian) (*russkiye*), which in modern Russia most often means "ethnic Russians". The other one is [россияне](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%8F%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BD#Russian) (*rossiyane*), derived from [Россия](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%A0%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%8F#Russian) (*Rossiya*, Russia), which denotes "people of Russia", regardless of ethnicity or religious affiliation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMilner-Gulland19971–4-51] In daily usage, those terms are often mixed up, and since [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) became president, the ethnic term русские has supplanted the non-ethnic term.[^cite_note-KappelerBrothers-52]<sup>: 26 </sup>

The name of the Russians derives from the early medieval [Rus' people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rus'_people), a group of [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) merchants and warriors who relocated from across the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) and played an important part in the foundation of the first [East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavic_peoples) state that later became the [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus').[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

The idea of a single "[all-Russian nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Russian_nation)" encompassing the [East Slavic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs), or a "triune nation" of three brotherly "[Great Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Russia)", "[Little Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Russia)" (i.e. [Ukrainian](145144.md)), and "[White Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Ruthenia)" (i.e. [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians)) peoples became the official doctrine of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) from the beginning of the 19th century onwards.[^cite_note-KappelerBrothers-52]<sup>: 25–26 </sup>

## History

### Ancient history

Further information: [Rus' people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rus'_people) and [Old Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Russians)



[East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) tribes and peoples, 8th–9th century

The ancestors of modern Russians are the [Slavic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ancient_Slavic_peoples), whose original home is thought by some scholars to have been the wooded areas of the [Pinsk Marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripet_Marshes), one of the largest [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) in Europe.[^cite_note-55] The East Slavs gradually settled Western Russia with [Moscow](19004.md) included in two waves: one moving from [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) toward present-day [Suzdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzdal) and [Murom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murom) and another from [Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polotsk) toward [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod) and [Rostov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov,_Yaroslavl_Oblast).[^cite_note-Christian-56] Prior to the Slavic migration in the 6-7th centuries, the Suzdal-Murom and Novgorod-Rostov areas were populated by [Finnic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnic_peoples),[^cite_note-backus-57] including the [Merya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryans),[^cite_note-58] the [Muromians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muromians),[^cite_note-59] and the [Meshchera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshchera).[^cite_note-60]

From the 7th century onwards, the East Slavs slowly assimilated the native Finnic peoples,[^cite_note-61] so that by year 1100, the majority of the population in Western Russia was Slavic-speaking.[^cite_note-Christian-56][^cite_note-backus-57] Recent genetic studies confirm the presence of a Finnic substrate in modern Russian population.[^cite_note-62]

Outside archaeological remains, little is known about the predecessors to Russians in general prior to 859 AD, when the *[Primary Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Chronicle)* starts its records.[^cite_note-63] By 600 AD, the [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) are believed to have split linguistically into [southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Slavs), [western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs), and eastern branches.

### Medieval history

Main articles: [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow), and [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia)



*The [Baptism of Kievans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism_of_Kievans)*, by [Klavdy Lebedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klavdy_Lebedev)

The Rus' state was established in northern Russia in the year 862,[^cite_note-64] which was ruled by the [Varangians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangians).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorrero20043-65][^cite_note-66] [Staraya Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga) and [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliky_Novgorod) became the first major cities of the new union of immigrants from [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) with the Slavs and [Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnic_peoples).[^cite_note-67] In 882, the prince [Oleg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_the_Wise) seized [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev), thereby uniting the northern and southern lands of the [East Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) under one authority.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorrero20043-65] The state [adopted Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Kievan_Rus') from the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) in 988. [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') ultimately disintegrated as a state as a result of in-fighting between members of the princely family that ruled it collectively.[^cite_note-68]

After the 13th century, [Moscow](19004.md) became a political and cultural center. Moscow has become a center for the [unification of Russian lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collector_of_Russian_lands).[^cite_note-69] By the end of the 15th century, Moscow united the northeastern and northwestern Russian principalities, overthrew the "Mongol yoke" in 1480,[^cite_note-70] and would be transformed into the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) after [Ivan IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_the_Terrible) was crowned tsar in 1547.[^cite_note-71]

### Modern history

Main articles: [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and [Russia](25391.md)



*Grandma's Fairy Tales*, by [Vassily Maximov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassily_Maximov)

In 1721, Tsar [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) renamed his state as the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), hoping to associate it with historical and cultural achievements of ancient Rus' – in contrast to his policies oriented towards Western Europe. The state now extended from the eastern borders of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) to the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), and became a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power); and one of the most powerful states in Europe after the [victory over Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Russia). Peasant revolts were common, and all were fiercely suppressed. The Emperor [Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Russia) [abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_reform_of_1861) [Russian serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom_in_Russia) in 1861, but the peasants fared poorly and revolutionary pressures grew. In the following decades, reform efforts such as the [Stolypin reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolypin_reform) of 1906–1914, the [constitution of 1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Constitution_of_1906), and the [State Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Duma_\(Russian_Empire\)) (1906–1917) attempted to open and liberalize the economy and political system, but the Emperors refused to relinquish [autocratic rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsarist_autocracy) and resisted sharing their power.

A combination of economic breakdown, [war-weariness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_in_World_War_I), and discontent with the autocratic system of government triggered [revolution in Russia in 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution). The [overthrow of the monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Revolution) initially brought into office a coalition of liberals and moderate socialists, but their failed policies led to [seizure of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) by the [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) on 25 October 1917 (7 November [New Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates)). In 1922, Soviet Russia, along with [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine), [Soviet Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Belarus), and the [Transcaucasian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_SFSR) signed the [Treaty on the Creation of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Creation_of_the_USSR), officially merging all four republics to form the Soviet Union as a country. Between 1922 and 1991, the history of Russia became essentially the [history of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union), effectively an ideologically based state roughly conterminous with the Russian Empire before the 1918 [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk_\(Russia%E2%80%93Central_Powers\)). From its first years, government in the Soviet Union based itself on the one-party rule of the Communists, as the Bolsheviks called themselves, beginning in March 1918. The approach to the building of socialism, however, varied over different periods in Soviet history: from the [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Economic_Policy) and diverse society and culture of the 1920s through the [command economy and repressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism) of the [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) era to the ["era of stagnation"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Stagnation) from the 1960s to the 1980s. The actions of the Soviet government caused the death of millions of citizens in the [famine of 1930–1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_famine_of_1930%E2%80%931933) and the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge). The [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) and the ensuing [war](32927.md), together with [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), again claimed [millions of lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties_of_the_Soviet_Union). Millions of Russian civilians and [prisoners of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_atrocities_committed_against_Soviet_prisoners_of_war) were killed or starved to death during Nazi Germany's genocidal policies called the [Hunger Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunger_Plan) and the [Generalplan Ost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost), including one million civilian casualties during the [Siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad). After the victory of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the [Western Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II), the Soviet Union became a [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower) opposing Western countries during the [Cold War](325329.md).

By the mid-1980s, with Soviet economic and political weaknesses becoming acute, Soviet leader [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev) embarked on major reforms; these culminated in the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), leaving Russia again alone and marking the beginning of the [post-Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Russia_\(1991%E2%80%93present\)) Russian period. The [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) renamed itself the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) and became the [successor state to the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_of_states#Soviet_Union_and_the_Russian_Federation). One of the negative consequences of the collapse of the Soviet Union was the problem of discrimination against the 25 million ethnic Russians living in a number of [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states).[^cite_note-73]

## Geographic distribution

Main articles: [Ethnic Russians in post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Russians_in_post-Soviet_states) and [Russian diaspora](5835042.md)



Ethnic Russians in former Soviet Union states in 1994

&#x20;Ethnic Russians historically migrated within the areas of the former [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), though they were sometimes encouraged to re-settle in borderland areas by the Tsarist and later Soviet government.[^cite_note-74] Sometimes ethnic Russian communities, such as the [Lipovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovans) who settled in the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) or the [Doukhobors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doukhobor) in Canada, emigrated as religious dissidents fleeing the central authority.[^cite_note-75]



[Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois Russian Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Genevi%C3%A8ve-des-Bois_Russian_Cemetery) in Paris, the resting place of many eminent [Russian émigrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9) after 1917

There are also small Russian communities in the [Balkans](4829.md) — including [Lipovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovans) in the Danube delta[^cite_note-76] — Central European nations such as [Germany](11867.md) and [Poland](22936.md), as well as Russians settled in [China](5405.md), [Japan](15573.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Argentina](18951905.md) and [Australia](4689264.md). These communities identify themselves to varying degrees as Russians, citizens of these countries, or both.

Significant numbers of Russians emigrated to [Canada](5042916.md), [Australia](4689264.md) and the [United States](3434750.md). [Brighton Beach, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Beach,_Brooklyn) and [South Beach, Staten Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Beach,_Staten_Island) in [New York City](645042.md) are examples of large communities of recent Russian and [Russian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Jews) immigrants. Other examples are [Sunny Isles Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunny_Isles_Beach), a northern suburb of [Miami](53846.md), and [West Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hollywood) of the [Los Angeles](18110.md) area.

After the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917) in 1917, many Russians who were identified with the [White army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_army) moved to [China](5405.md) — most of them settling in [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin) and [Shanghai](27643.md).[^cite_note-77] By the 1930s, Harbin had 100,000 Russians. Many of these Russians moved back to the Soviet Union after [World War II](32927.md). Today, a large group in northern China still speak Russian as a second language. [Russians (*eluosizu*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Russians_in_China) are one of the [56 ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalities_of_China) officially recognized by the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (as *the Russ*); there are approximately 15,600 Russian Chinese living mostly in northern [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang), and also in [Inner Mongolia](55064.md) and [Heilongjiang](173816.md).

According to the [2021 Russian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census), the number of ethnic Russians in the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) decreased by nearly 5.43 million, from roughly 111 million people in 2010 to approximately 105.5 million in 2021.[^cite_note-78]

## Ethnographic groups



[Lipovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovans) in the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta)

Among Russians, a number of [ethnographic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnographic_group) stand out, such as: the [Northern Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Russian_dialects), the [Southern Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russian_dialects), the [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks), the [Goryuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryuns), the [Kamchadals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchadals), the [Polekhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polekhs), the [Pomors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomors), the [Russian Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_China), the [Siberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberians) (*Siberiaks*), [Starozhily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starozhily), some groupings of [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers) ([Kamenschiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenschik), [Lipovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovans), [Semeiskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semeiskie)), and others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlexandrovVlasovaPolishchuk1997107–123-79]

The main ones are the Northern and Southern Russian groups. At the same time, the proposal of the ethnographer [Dmitry Zelenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Konstantinovich_Zelenin) in his major work of 1927 *Russian (East Slavic) Ethnography* to consider them as separate East Slavic peoples[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZelenin1991§§_1–4-80] did not find support in scientific circles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShmeleva1994283-81]

Russia's Arctic coastline had been explored and settled by [Pomors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomors), Russian settlers from [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Republic).[^cite_note-82]

[Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) inhabited sparsely populated areas in the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_\(river\)), [Terek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terek_\(river\)), and [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(river\)) river basins, and played an important role in the historical and cultural development of parts of Russia.[^cite_note-83]

## Genetics

Main article: [Genetic studies on Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_studies_on_Russians)

See also: [Yamnaya culture § Eastern Europe and Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture#Eastern_Europe_and_Finland), and [Fatyanovo–Balanovo culture § Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatyanovo%E2%80%93Balanovo_culture#Genetics)

In accordance with the 2008 research results of Russian and Estonian geneticists, two groups of Russians are distinguished: the northern and southern populations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovskyRootsi2008236–50-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovsky201224-84]

Central and Southern Russians, to which the majority of Russian populations belong, according to [Y chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_chromosome) R1a, are included in the general "East European" [gene cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_cluster) with the rest [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) and [West Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs) (Poles, Czechs and Slovaks), as well as the non-Slavic [Hungarians](17106358.md) and [Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalyarchukDerenko2004877–900-47][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovskyRootsi2008236–50-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovsky201223-49] Genetically, East Slavs are quite similar to West Slavs; such genetic similarity is somewhat unusual for genetics with such a wide settlement of the Slavs, especially Russians.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovsky201213-85] [Northern Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Russian_dialects), according to [mtDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA), Y chromosome and autosomal marker CCR5de132, are included in the "North European" gene cluster (the [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), the [Balts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts), [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic-speaking_Europe) and [Baltic Finnic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Finnic_peoples)). On top of their closeness to other Slavic peoples, Russians are found to share a significant part of their DNA with speakers of Finno-Ugric languages inhabiting present-day Russia, which indicates a likely scenario of genetic admixture with indigenous populations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovskyRootsi2008236–50-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalyarchukDerenko2004877–900-47][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovsky201226-86][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESankina200098-87] A common origin for all peoples of the Nordic gene pool has been hypothesised, with descendants of a [Paleo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comb_Ceramic_culture) population having remained around the [Baltic Sea](3335.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovskyRootsi2008236–50-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalanovsky201226-86]

At the same time, according to other scholars, the Russians have close genetic affinities to surrounding Northeast and Eastern European populations. They also display evidence for multiple genetic ancestries and admixture events, and high identity-by-descent sharing with the [Finnic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnic_peoples).[^cite_note-88]

While modern European populations derive most of their ancestry from three major sources: [Western hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_hunter-gatherer), [Early European Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers), and [Western Steppe Herders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders) (Yamnaya), this three-way model is insufficient to explain the ethnogenesis of populations from north-eastern Europe such as Saami, Russians, Mordovians, Chuvash, Estonians, Hungarians, and Finns. They carry an additional Siberian/[Nganasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nganasan_people)-related genetic component and increased allele sharing with modern East Asians.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

The most common [human Y-chromosome DNA haplogroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Y-chromosome_DNA_haplogroup) is haplogroup [R1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1a) (c. 46,7%), followed by haplogroup [N-M231](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_N-M231) (c. 21,6%), haplogroup [I-M170](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I-M170) (c. 17,6%), and haplogroup [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1b) (c. 5,8%). The remainder (c. 8,3%) are other less frequent haplogroups (E3b, J2, etc.).[^cite_note-91]

Russians have sometimes found it useful to emphasize their self-perceived ability to [assimilate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation) other people [to the Russian ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) - and as a historic great power with imperial expansionist tendencies the Russian state has sometimes encouraged Russian-centred monoculturalism. Steppe peoples, Tatars, Baltic Germans, Lithuanians and native Siberians in [Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), [Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscovy_\(disambiguation\)) or the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) could in theory become "Russians" ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): русские) simply by accepting [Russian Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodoxy) as their faith.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] The attitude of ready inclusivity is summed up in the popular phrase (sometimes attributed to Emperor [Alexander III of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_III_of_Russia)) - Хочешь быть русским - будь им! (transl.  You want to be Russian - be that!).[^cite_note-94]

## Language

Main article: [Russian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language)

[Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) is the [official](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) and the predominantly spoken language in Russia.[^cite_note-95] It is the most-spoken native language in Europe,[^cite_note-96] the most geographically widespread language of Eurasia,[^cite_note-language-97] as well as the world's most widely spoken [Slavic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages).[^cite_note-language-97] Russian is the third-most used language on the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) after [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Spanish](26825.md),[^cite_note-98] and is one of two official languages aboard the [International Space Station](15043.md),[^cite_note-99] as well as one of the six [official languages of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages_of_the_United_Nations).[^cite_note-100]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Russia](2012490.md)

### Literature



[Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy)'s (1828–1910) notable works include the novels *[War and Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_and_Peace)* and *[Anna Karenina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Karenina)*, often cited as pinnacles of [realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_realism) fiction.

[Russian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_literature) is considered to be among the world's most influential and developed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKahnLipovetskyReyfmanSandler2018-101] It can be traced to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), when epics and chronicles in vernacular [Old East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_East_Slavic) were composed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKahnLipovetskyReyfmanSandler2018-101][^cite_note-102] By the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment), literature had grown in importance, with works from [Mikhail Lomonosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Lomonosov), [Denis Fonvizin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Fonvizin), [Gavrila Derzhavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavrila_Derzhavin), and the [Sentimentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentimentalism_\(literature\)) [Nikolay Karamzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Karamzin).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103] From the early 1830s, during the [Golden Age of Russian Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Russian_Poetry), literature underwent an astounding golden age in poetry, prose and drama.[^cite_note-104] [Romantic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_literature) permitted a flowering of poetic talent: [Vasily Zhukovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Zhukovsky) and later his protégé [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin) came to the fore.[^cite_note-105] Following Pushkin's footsteps, a new generation of poets were born, including [Mikhail Lermontov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Lermontov), [Nikolay Nekrasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Nekrasov), [Aleksey Konstantinovich Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksey_Konstantinovich_Tolstoy), [Fyodor Tyutchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Tyutchev) and [Afanasy Fet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afanasy_Fet).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103]

The first great Russian novelist was [Nikolai Gogol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Gogol).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103] Then, during the Age of [Realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_realism), came [Ivan Turgenev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Turgenev), who mastered both short stories and novels.[^cite_note-107] [Fyodor Dostoevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Dostoyevsky) and [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) soon became internationally renowned.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103] [Ivan Goncharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Goncharov) is remembered mainly for his novel [Oblomov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblomov).[^cite_note-108] [Mikhail Saltykov-Shchedrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Saltykov-Shchedrin) wrote prose satire,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103][^cite_note-109] while [Nikolai Leskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Leskov) is best remembered for his shorter fiction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103][^cite_note-110] In the second half of the century [Anton Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov) excelled in short stories and became a leading dramatist.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103][^cite_note-111] Other important 19th-century developments included the fabulist [Ivan Krylov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Krylov),[^cite_note-112] non-fiction writers such as the critic [Vissarion Belinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vissarion_Belinsky),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103][^cite_note-113] and playwrights such as [Aleksandr Griboyedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Griboyedov) and [Aleksandr Ostrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Ostrovsky).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] The beginning of the 20th century ranks as the [Silver Age of Russian Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Age_of_Russian_Poetry). This era had poets such as [Alexander Blok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Blok), [Anna Akhmatova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Akhmatova), [Boris Pasternak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Pasternak), [Konstantin Balmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Balmont),[^cite_note-116] [Marina Tsvetaeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Tsvetaeva), [Vladimir Mayakovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Mayakovsky), and [Osip Mandelstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osip_Mandelstam).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103] It also produced some first-rate novelists and short-story writers, such as [Aleksandr Kuprin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Kuprin), Nobel Prize winner [Ivan Bunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Bunin), [Leonid Andreyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Andreyev), [Yevgeny Zamyatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Zamyatin), [Dmitry Merezhkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Merezhkovsky) and [Andrei Bely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Bely).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103]

After the Russian Revolution of 1917, Russian literature split into Soviet and [white émigré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9) parts. In the 1930s, [Socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism) became the predominant trend in Russia. Its leading figure was [Maxim Gorky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Gorky), who laid the foundations of this style.[^cite_note-117] [Mikhail Bulgakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bulgakov) was one of the leading writers of the Soviet era.[^cite_note-118] [Nikolay Ostrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Ostrovsky)'s novel [How the Steel Was Tempered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_the_Steel_Was_Tempered) has been among the most successful works of Russian literature. Influential émigré writers include [Vladimir Nabokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Nabokov).[^cite_note-119] Some writers dared to oppose Soviet ideology, such as Nobel Prize-winning novelist [Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Solzhenitsyn), who wrote about life in the Gulag camps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103][^cite_note-120]

During the post-Soviet 1990s writers are already not recognised as very special guides by most Russians.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998222–228Literature-103] At the beginning of the 21st century, the most discussed figures, [postmodernists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_postmodernism) [Victor Pelevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Pelevin) and [Vladimir Sorokin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Sorokin) remained the leading Russian writers.[^cite_note-121]

### Philosophy

Main articles: [Russian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_philosophy) and [List of Russian philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_philosophers)

Russian philosophy has been greatly influential. Religious and spiritual philosophy is represented by works of [Vladimir Solovyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Solovyov_\(philosopher\)), [Nikolai Berdyaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Berdyaev), [Pavel Florensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Florensky), [Semyon Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Frank), [Nikolay Lossky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Lossky), [Vasily Rozanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Rozanov), and others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELossky1952-122] [Helena Blavatsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Blavatsky) gained international following as the leading theoretician of [Theosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophy), and co-founded the [Theosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophical_Society).[^cite_note-123]

Social and political philosophy is also remarkable. [Alexander Herzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Herzen) is known as one of the fathers of [agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarianism) [populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism).[^cite_note-124] [Mikhail Bakunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin) is referred to as the father of [anarchism](12.md).[^cite_note-125] [Peter Kropotkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kropotkin) was the most important theorist of [anarcho-communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-communism).[^cite_note-126] [Mikhail Bakhtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakhtin)'s writings have significantly inspired scholars.[^cite_note-127] [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin), a major revolutionary, developed a variant of communism known as [Leninism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninism). [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky), on the other hand, founded [Trotskyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyism). [Alexander Zinoviev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zinoviev) was a prominent philosopher and writer in the second half of the 20th century.[^cite_note-128] [Aleksandr Dugin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Dugin), known for his [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) views, has been regarded as the "guru of geopolitics".[^cite_note-129]

### Science

Main article: [Science and technology in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Russia)



[Dmitri Mendeleev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Mendeleev) (1837–1906) is best known for formulating the [Periodic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_Law) and creating a version of the [periodic table of elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table).

See also: [Timeline of Russian inventions and technology records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Russian_inventions_and_technology_records), [List of Russian scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_scientists), and [List of Russian inventors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_inventors)

[Mikhail Lomonosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Lomonosov) proposed the [conservation of mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_mass) in [chemical reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reactions), discovered the [atmosphere of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere_of_Venus), and founded modern [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology).[^cite_note-130] Since the times of [Nikolay Lobachevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Lobachevsky), who pioneered the [non-Euclidean geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Euclidean_geometry), and a prominent tutor [Pafnuty Chebyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pafnuty_Chebyshev), Russian [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_mathematicians) became among the world's most influential.[^cite_note-131] [Dmitry Mendeleev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Mendeleev) invented the [Periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table), the main framework of modern [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry).[^cite_note-132] [Sofya Kovalevskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofya_Kovalevskaya) was a pioneer among [women in mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_women_in_mathematics#19th_Century) in the 19th century.[^cite_note-133] [Grigori Perelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Perelman) was offered the first ever Clay [Millennium Prize Problems](27599234.md) Award for his final proof of the [Poincaré conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincar%C3%A9_conjecture) in 2002, as well as the Fields Medal in 2006, both of which he declined.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

[Alexander Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stepanovich_Popov) was among the [inventors of radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention_of_radio),[^cite_note-136] while [Nikolai Basov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Basov) and [Alexander Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Prokhorov) were co-inventors of [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser) and [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser).[^cite_note-137] [Zhores Alferov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhores_Alferov) contributed significantly to the creation of [modern heterostructure physics and electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterojunction).[^cite_note-138] [Oleg Losev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Losev) made crucial contributions in the field of [semiconductor junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_junction), and discovered [light-emitting diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode).[^cite_note-139] [Vladimir Vernadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vernadsky) is considered one of the founders of [geochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geochemistry), [biogeochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeochemistry), and [radiogeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometric_dating).[^cite_note-140] [Élie Metchnikoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lie_Metchnikoff) is known for his groundbreaking research in [immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology).[^cite_note-141] [Ivan Pavlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov) is known chiefly for his work in [classical conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_conditioning).[^cite_note-142] [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau) made fundamental contributions to many areas of [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics).[^cite_note-143]

[Nikolai Vavilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Vavilov) was best known for having identified the [centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vavilov_center) of origin of [cultivated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horticulture) plants.[^cite_note-144] Many famous Russian scientists and inventors were [émigrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89migr%C3%A9s). [Igor Sikorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Sikorsky) was an [aviation pioneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aviation_pioneers).[^cite_note-145] [Vladimir Zworykin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Zworykin) was the inventor of the [iconoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconoscope) and [kinescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinescope) television systems.[^cite_note-146] [Theodosius Dobzhansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_Dobzhansky) was the central figure in the field of [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology) for his work in shaping the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_\(20th_century\)).[^cite_note-147] [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow) was one of the foremost advocates of the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang) theory.[^cite_note-148] [Konstantin Tsiolkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Tsiolkovsky) is called the father of theoretical [astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronautics), whose works had inspired leading Soviet rocket engineers, such as [Valentin Glushko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Glushko), and many others.[^cite_note-149]<sup>: 6–7, 333 </sup>

In 1961, the first human trip into space was successfully made by [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin). In 1963, [Valentina Tereshkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentina_Tereshkova) became the first and youngest [woman in space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_space), having flown a solo mission on [Vostok 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_6).[^cite_note-150] In 1965, [Alexei Leonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Leonov) became the first human to conduct a [spacewalk](9792.md), exiting the [space capsule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_capsule) during [Voskhod 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskhod_2).[^cite_note-151]

### Painting



Russian artist [Boris Kustodiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Kustodiev)'s *[Maslenitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslenitsa)*, 1916

Early Russian painting is [represented in icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_icons) and vibrant [frescos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco). In the early 15th century, the master icon painter [Andrei Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Rublev) created some of Russia's most treasured religious art.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152] The [Russian Academy of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Arts), which was established in 1757, to train Russian artists, brought Western techniques of secular painting to Russia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998Chapter_1–2._Historical_Setting-153] In the 18th century, academicians [Ivan Argunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Argunov), [Dmitry Levitzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Levitzky), [Vladimir Borovikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Borovikovsky) became influential.[^cite_note-154] The early 19th century saw many prominent paintings by [Karl Briullov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Briullov) and [Alexander Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Andreyevich_Ivanov), both of whom were known for [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) historical canvases.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] [Ivan Aivazovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Aivazovsky), another Romantic painter, is considered one of the greatest masters of [marine art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_art).[^cite_note-157]

In the 1860s, a group of critical [realists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_\(arts\)) ([Peredvizhniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peredvizhniki)), led by [Ivan Kramskoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Kramskoy), [Ilya Repin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Repin) and [Vasiliy Perov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasiliy_Perov) broke with the academy, and portrayed the many-sided aspects of social life in paintings.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] The turn of the 20th century saw the rise of [symbolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolism_\(arts\)); represented by [Mikhail Vrubel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Vrubel) and [Nicholas Roerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Roerich).[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] The [Russian avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_avant-garde) flourished from approximately 1890 to 1930; and globally influential artists from this era were [El Lissitzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Lissitzky),[^cite_note-162] [Kazimir Malevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimir_Malevich), [Natalia Goncharova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Goncharova), [Wassily Kandinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassily_Kandinsky), and [Marc Chagall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Chagall).[^cite_note-163]

### Music

Main articles: [Music of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Russia) and [List of Russian composers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_composers)



The classic ballet of *[Swan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Lake)* was composed by [Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky) (1840–1893)&#x20;

Until the 18th century, music in Russia consisted mainly of church music and folk songs and dances.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998228–230Music-164] In the 19th century, it was defined by the tension between classical composer [Mikhail Glinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Glinka) along with other members of [The Mighty Handful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mighty_Handful), and the [Russian Musical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Musical_Society) led by composers [Anton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Rubinstein) and [Nikolay Rubinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Rubinstein).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998228–230Music-164] The later tradition of [Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky), one of the greatest composers of the [Romantic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_music), was continued into the 20th century by [Sergei Rachmaninoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Rachmaninoff), one of the last great champions of the Romantic style of European classical music.[^cite_note-165] World-renowned composers of the 20th century include [Alexander Scriabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Scriabin), [Alexander Glazunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Glazunov), [Igor Stravinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Stravinsky), [Sergei Prokofiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Prokofiev), [Dmitri Shostakovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Shostakovich), [Georgy Sviridov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Sviridov) and [Alfred Schnittke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Schnittke).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998228–230Music-164]

Soviet and Russian conservatories have turned out generations of world-renowned soloists. Among the best known are violinists [David Oistrakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Oistrakh) and [Gidon Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gidon_Kremer),[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] cellist [Mstislav Rostropovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mstislav_Rostropovich),[^cite_note-168] pianists [Vladimir Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Horowitz),[^cite_note-169] [Sviatoslav Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_Richter),[^cite_note-170] and [Emil Gilels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Gilels),[^cite_note-171] and vocalist [Galina Vishnevskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galina_Vishnevskaya).[^cite_note-172]

During the Soviet times, [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music) also produced a number of renowned figures, such as the two [balladeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_\(Soviet_Union\))—[Vladimir Vysotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vysotsky) and [Bulat Okudzhava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulat_Okudzhava),[^cite_note-music2-173] and performers such as [Alla Pugacheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alla_Pugacheva).[^cite_note-174] [Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz), even with sanctions from Soviet authorities, flourished and evolved into one of the country's most popular musical forms.[^cite_note-music2-173] The [Ganelin Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Ganelin) have been described by critics as the greatest ensemble of free-jazz in [continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe).[^cite_note-175] By the 1980s, [rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music_in_Russia) became popular across Russia, and produced bands such as [Aria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aria_\(band\)), [Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_\(band\)),[^cite_note-176] [DDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDT_\(band\)),[^cite_note-177] and [Kino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kino_\(band\)).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] [Pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_pop) in Russia has continued to flourish since the 1960s, with globally famous acts such as [t.A.T.u.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T.A.T.u.)[^cite_note-180] In the recent times, [Little Big](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Big_\(band\)), a [rave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rave_music) band, has gained popularity in Russia and across Europe.[^cite_note-181]

### Cinema

Main articles: [Cinema of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Russia) and [Cinema of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_Soviet_Union)



Poster of *[Battleship Potemkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battleship_Potemkin)* (1925) by Sergei Eisenstein, which was named the [greatest film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_considered_the_best) at the [Brussels World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_58) in 1958.

[^cite_note-182]

Russian and later [Soviet cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_cinema) was a hotbed of invention, resulting in world-renowned films such as *[The Battleship Potemkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battleship_Potemkin)*.[^cite_note-183] Soviet-era filmmakers, most notably [Sergei Eisenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Eisenstein) and [Andrei Tarkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Tarkovsky), would go on to become among of the world's most innovative and influential directors.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] Eisenstein was a student of [Lev Kuleshov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Kuleshov), who developed the groundbreaking [Soviet montage theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory) of film editing at the world's first [film school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_school), the [All-Union Institute of Cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerasimov_Institute_of_Cinematography).[^cite_note-186] [Dziga Vertov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dziga_Vertov)'s "[Kino-Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kino-Eye)" theory had a huge impact on the development of documentary filmmaking and cinema realism.[^cite_note-187] Many Soviet socialist realism films were artistically successful, including *[Chapaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapaev_\(film\))*, *[The Cranes Are Flying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cranes_Are_Flying)*, and *[Ballad of a Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad_of_a_Soldier)*.

The 1960s and 1970s saw a greater variety of artistic styles in Soviet cinema. The comedies of [Eldar Ryazanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldar_Ryazanov) and [Leonid Gaidai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Gaidai) of that time were immensely popular, with many of the catchphrases still in use today.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] In 1961–68 [Sergey Bondarchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Bondarchuk) directed an [Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award)-winning [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_and_Peace_\(film_series\)) of Leo Tolstoy's epic *[War and Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_and_Peace)*, which was [the most expensive film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_most_expensive_film) made in the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-190] In 1969, [Vladimir Motyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Motyl)'s *[White Sun of the Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sun_of_the_Desert)* was released, a very popular film in a genre of [ostern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostern); the film is traditionally watched by [cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmonauts) before any trip into space.[^cite_note-191] In 2002, *[Russian Ark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ark)* was the first feature film ever to be shot in a single take.[^cite_note-192] Today, the Russian cinema industry continues to expand.[^cite_note-193]

### Architecture

Main articles: [Russian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_architecture) and [List of Russian architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_architects)



[Saint Basil's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Basil's_Cathedral), built between 1555 and 1683 and combined earlier [Russian church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_church_architecture) and the [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Tatars) east styles,

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152]

&#x20;Moscow

The history of [Russian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_architecture) begins with early woodcraft buildings of ancient Slavs,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152][^cite_note-194] and the [architecture of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Kievan_Rus').[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152][^cite_note-195] Following the [Christianization of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Kievan_Rus'), for several centuries it was influenced predominantly by the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152][^cite_note-196] Due to Mongol occupation cut ties with the Byzantine Empire Russian architecture inreached some original innovations, among them the church altar screen dividing [iconostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconostasis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152] [Aristotle Fioravanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle_Fioravanti) and other Italian architects brought [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) trends into Russia, especially in reconstruction of [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152][^cite_note-197] The 16th century saw the development of the unique [tent-like churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tent-like_church); and the [onion dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_dome) design, which is a distinctive feature of Russian architecture.[^cite_note-198] In the 17th century, the "fiery style" of ornamentation flourished in Moscow and [Yaroslavl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslavl), gradually paving the way for the [Naryshkin baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naryshkin_baroque) of the 1690s. After the reforms of Peter the Great, Russia's architecture became influenced by Western European styles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152][^cite_note-Shvidkovsky-199] The 18th-century taste for [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) architecture led to the splendid works of [Bartolomeo Rastrelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeo_Rastrelli) and his followers.[^cite_note-200] During the reign of Catherine the Great, Saint Petersburg was transformed into an outdoor museum of [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture).[^cite_note-201] During [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia)'s rule, [Empire style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_style) became the *de facto* architectural style, and [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia) opened the gate of [Eclecticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism) to Russia. The second half of the 19th-century was dominated by the [Neo-Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Byzantine_architecture_in_the_Russian_Empire) and [Russian Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revival) style.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998232–233Architecture_and_Painting-152] In early 20th-century, [Russian neoclassical revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_neoclassical_revival) became a trend.[^cite_note-Shvidkovsky-199] Prevalent styles of the late 20th-century were the [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau_architecture_in_Russia), [Constructivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_\(art\)),[^cite_note-202] and [Socialist Classicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_architecture).[^cite_note-203]

### Religion

[Trinity Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Sunday) in Russia; the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) has experienced a great revival since the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), a country that had a policy of [state atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_atheism).



The communal [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers)' service for the [Bright Easter Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week), [Moscow Oblast](372315.md).

Most religious Russians are [Eastern Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998203–210The_Russian_Orthodox_Church-204][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShmeleva1994270-205] According to differing sociological surveys on religious adherence, between 41% to over 80% of the total population of Russia adhere to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998203–210The_Russian_Orthodox_Church-204][^cite_note-ArenaAtlas2012-206][^cite_note-Ogonek-207][^cite_note-208]

Non-religious Russians may associate themselves with the Orthodox faith for cultural reasons. Some Russian people are [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers): a relatively small [schismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schism_\(religion\)) group of the Russian Orthodoxy that rejected the liturgical reforms introduced in the 17th century. Other schisms from Orthodoxy include [Spiritual Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_Christianity), namely [Doukhobors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doukhobor) which in the 18th century rejected secular government, the Russian Orthodox priests, icons, all church ritual, the Bible as the supreme source of divine revelation and the divinity of Jesus, and later emigrated into Canada. Another Spiritual Christian movement were [Molokans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molokan) which formed in the 19th century and rejected Czar's [divine right to rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_right_of_kings), icons, the [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity) as outlined by the [Nicene Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Creed), Orthodox [fasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting#Eastern_Orthodoxy), military service, and practices including [water baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism).[^cite_note-209]

Other world religions have negligible representation among ethnic Russians. The largest of these groups are [Islam](6037917.md) with over 100,000 followers from national minorities,[^cite_note-ArenaAtlas-210] and [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) with over 85,000 Russian adherents.[^cite_note-211] Others are mostly [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostals), [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicals), [Seventh-day Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventists), [Lutherans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutherans), [The Salvation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Salvation_Army), and [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtisLeighton1998210–220Other_Religions-212]

Since the fall of the Soviet Union various new religious movements have sprung up and gathered a following among ethnic Russians. The most prominent of these are [Rodnovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodnovery), the revival of the Slavic native religion also common to other [Slavic nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs).[^cite_note-213]

### Sports

Main article: [Sport in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Russia)

[Football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in Russia.[^cite_note-214] The [Soviet Union national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) became the first European champions by winning [Euro 1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_1960),[^cite_note-215] and reached the finals of [Euro 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_1988).[^cite_note-216] In 1956 and 1988, the Soviet Union won gold at the [Olympic football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics#Results). Russian clubs [CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_CSKA_Moscow) and [Zenit Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenit_Saint_Petersburg) won the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup) in 2005 and 2008.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218] The [Russian national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_national_football_team) reached the semi-finals of [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_2008).[^cite_note-219] Russia was the host nation for the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup),[^cite_note-220] and the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).[^cite_note-221]

[Ice hockey](19428477.md) is very popular in Russia.[^cite_note-hockey-222] The [Soviet Union men's national ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) dominated the sport internationally throughout its existence,[^cite_note-223] and the modern-day [Russia men's national ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) is among the most successful teams in the sport.[^cite_note-hockey-222] [Bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy) is Russia's national sport, and it has historically been the highest-achieving country in the sport.[^cite_note-224] The [Russian national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_national_basketball_team) won the [EuroBasket 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2007),[^cite_note-225] and the Russian basketball club [PBC CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBC_CSKA_Moscow) is among the most successful European basketball teams. The annual [Formula One](10854.md) [Russian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Grand_Prix) was held at the [Sochi Autodrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi_Autodrom) in the [Sochi Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi_Olympic_Park).[^cite_note-226]

Russia is the leading nation in [rhythmic gymnastics](189053.md); and Russian [synchronized swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronized_swimming) is considered to be the world's best.[^cite_note-227] [Figure skating](11152.md) is another popular sport in Russia, especially [pair skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skating) and [ice dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_dancing).[^cite_note-228] Russia has produced a number of famous [tennis](29773.md) players,[^cite_note-229] such as [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) and [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev). [Chess](5134.md) is also a widely popular pastime in the nation, with many of the world's top chess players being Russian for decades.[^cite_note-230] The [1980 Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympic_Games) were held in Moscow,[^cite_note-231] and the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics) and the [2014 Winter Paralympics](2311504.md) were hosted in Sochi.[^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233]
