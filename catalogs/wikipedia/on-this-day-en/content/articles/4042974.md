**PT Pertamina (Persero)**[^cite_note-3] is an Indonesian [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned) [oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) corporation, headquartered in [Jakarta](16275.md).[^cite_note-4] It was created in August 1968 by the merger of *Pertamin* (established 1961) and *Permina* (established in 1957). In 2020, the firm was the third-largest crude oil producer in Indonesia behind US-based companies [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil)'s Mobil Cepu Ltd. and [Chevron Pacific Indonesia](284749.md).[^cite_note-5] According to the 2020 *[Fortune Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500)* list, Pertamina is [the largest company in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_companies_in_Indonesia).[^cite_note-6]

## History

### Nationalization

In 1957, [Royal Dutch Shell's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc) assets in Indonesia (trading as [Bataafse Petroleum Maatschappij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataafse_Petroleum_Maatschappij)) were nationalised, from which **Permina** was founded as a state-owned oil monopoly, headed by Lieutenant-General [Ibnu Sutowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibnu_Sutowo).[^cite_note-7] Sutowo's position as the second deputy of [Abdul Haris Nasution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Haris_Nasution) was the beginning of the armed forces' involvement in the oil industry.[^cite_note-8] Permina distributed oil for the entire archipelago.

Permina founded the Apprentice Technical School (*Sekolah Kader Teknik*) in [Brandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangkalan_Brandan) to train and produce experts in the field. In 1962, the company went on to establish the Oil Academy in [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung). The academy's curriculum covered the technical aspects of the petroleum industry, and the graduates became the main source of labor for Permina (which was renamed Pertamin in 1961).

In 1960, the [Provisional People's Consultative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_People's_Consultative_Assembly) adopted a policy that oil and gas extraction could only be carried out by the state, through a state-administered company. Pertamin was responsible for the administration, management and controlling of the exploration and production. The policy was short-lived. An agreement between the state and foreign companies was affirmed that gradually, [oil refinery](195137.md) manufactures and other assets in marketing and distribution were to be sold to Indonesia within five to fifteen years.

In 1968, to consolidate the oil and gas industry for its management, exploration, marketing and distribution, Permina and Pertamin merged and became PN. Pertamina. It continued to do little drilling itself but made production-sharing agreements with foreign companies.

### The 1970s

After the merger, Pertamina's production rose considerably (about 15% each in 1968 and 1969, and nearly 20% in 1973).[^cite_note-9] By the end of 1973, it produced 28.2% of Indonesia's oil, with agreements of [Caltex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltex) and [Stanvac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Vacuum_Oil_Company) to produce the rest (67.8% and 3.6%, respectively). Its assets included seven [refineries](195137.md), [oil terminals](3385681.md), 116 [tankers](15923603.md), 102 other vessels and an airline. It was also active in [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement), [fertiliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertiliser), [liquid natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_natural_gas), [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel), [hospitals](18994221.md), [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate), a [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) estate, and [telecommunications](33094374.md).

The 1974 oil price increases produced revenues of USD 4.2 billion in that year, equivalent to approximately one-sixth of Indonesia's gross domestic product. Much of this revenue was used by Sutowo to expand Pertamina's interests far beyond oil production to include investments in oil tankers, steel and construction.[^cite_note-10] Pertamina built the Bina Graha, the presidential executive office building in [Jakarta](16275.md).[^cite_note-R301-11] The global oil crisis of the 1970s greatly increased oil prices and thus Pertamina's profits. The company initially provided a fiscal lift to the hopes of [Indonesia's development planners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_mafia).

For President [Suharto](217513.md) and other members of the ruling elite, revenue from Pertamina was "an ongoing source of funding" without accountability. "They ran this cash-cow into the ground, using it for both military and personal ends."[^cite_note-Vickers,_Adrian_2005._p._185-12] Historian [Adrian Vickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Vickers) describes the endemic corruption at Pertamina:

> "At each stage of the transaction chain, somebody was getting a percentage... If accidents occurred, as in 1972 when eighty impoverished people died... they could be covered up."[^cite_note-13]



[Fokker F.27 Series 200 Friendship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_Friendship) of Pertamina used for transport of employees and equipment during the 1970s

In 1973, the government's ability to borrow money from overseas was constrained, and Pertamina was no longer providing revenues to the state. Instead, the massive enterprise turned out not to be making money but accumulating losses. In February 1975, Pertamina could no longer pay its American and Canadian creditors.[^cite_note-14] An investigation followed, which revealed over [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_dollar)10 billion in debt, mismanagement, and corruption within the company. This debt was equivalent to approximately thirty per cent of Indonesia's [GNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNP) at the time.[^cite_note-Schwartz55-15] Others offer a figure of a \$15 billion debt.[^cite_note-16] A public investigation damaged the reputation of the national elite both among Indonesians and foreigners.&#x20;

However, charges against [Ibnu Sutowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibnu_Sutowo) were dismissed and he and his family remained among the richest and most powerful in Indonesia, well into the 21st century.[^cite_note-Vickers,_Adrian_2005._p._185-12] The government took over the operation of the company and sought means by which to repay its debts.[^cite_note-R301-11] Pertamina's debt problems were eventually solved through a large government bail-out, which nearly doubled Indonesia's foreign debt.[^cite_note-Schwartz55-15]

### Since the 1980s

[Human rights](13831.md) observers have long expressed concerns about Indonesia's hostility to [labour unions](17626.md). According to the *[Multinational Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Monitor):* "In 1985, the government ordered the firing of over 1,600 workers at Pertamina and foreign oil companies, charging that they had been members of the [Indonesian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia), which had been permanently banned 19 years earlier when [Suharto](217513.md) took power."[^cite_note-17]

To execute a clause in the 2001 Oil and Natural Gas Act, in 2003 Pertamina legally became PT PERTAMINA (Persero), as per the enactment of Government Regulation No.31/2003. Pertamina is now under the coordinator of the State Minister of State-owned Enterprises.

Like other contractors, Pertamina holds a Cooperation Contract with the Oil and Gas Regulatory Body. With its transformation into a [limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_company), Pertamina has become a business entity with the main target of making a profit.

### Corporate identity

In October 2020, Pertamina unveiled a new corporate identity, replacing its logo that had been in continuous use since the 1970s. The redesigned symbol was presented by management as representing the company's strategic transformation from a national oil and gas company into a global energy company, aligned with its stated ambition to achieve net zero emissions by 2060.[^cite_note-18]

### 2024 Pertamina LNG graft scandal

In June 2024, [Karen Agustiawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Agustiawan), Pertamina's president director from 2009 to 2014, was sentenced by the Jakarta Corruption Court to nine years in prison for [graft](2717335.md), surrounding the procurement of [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) from [Cheniere Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheniere_Energy).[^cite_note-Reuters20240625-19] News agency [Antara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antara_\(news_agency\)) estimates the that purchase contract resulted in state losses of Rp1.7 trillion (US\$113.84 million).[^cite_note-Reuters20240625-19] In August 2024, the conviction was upheld by the Jakarta High Court.[^cite_note-IndonesiaBusinessPost20241209-20]

Other executives, including Yenni Andayani and Hari Karyuliarto, are also implicated and under investigation for their involvement in decisions that led to substantial financial losses. Luhur Budi Djatmiko, another former executive, faces scrutiny related to a separate corruption case involving a land purchase in South Jakarta that caused the state a loss of Rp348 billion.[^cite_note-IndonesiaBusinessPost20241209-20]

### Export-import and gasoline quality corruption scandals

Main article: [2025 Pertamina corruption case](79447144.md)

On 25 February 2025, the [Attorney General’s Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General's_Office_of_Indonesia) identified seven individuals as suspects in a corruption case involving state-owned energy firm Pertamina, its subsidiaries, and private contractors. The alleged misconduct, which occurred between 2018 and 2023, reportedly resulted in state losses amounting to Rp 193.7 trillion (US\$11.9–12 billion).[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_jakglob-21][^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_tst-22]

The suspects include Riva Siahaan, CEO of [Pertamina Patra Niaga](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertamina_Patra_Niaga); Yoki Firnandi, CEO of [Pertamina International Shipping](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertamina_International_Shipping); and Sani Dinar Saifuddin, Director at [Kilang Pertamina Internasional](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilang_Pertamina_Internasional). Prosecutors claimed that the three executives bypassed a regulation requiring Pertamina to prioritize local crude oil purchases, instead opting for costly imports.

In response, the suspects justified these actions by asserting that domestic crude did not meet refinery specifications, despite evidence proving otherwise. Additionally, Pertamina International Shipping allegedly exaggerated crude oil transportation costs, marked up to 13%-15%, profiting [Muhammad Kerry Andrianto Riza](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Kerry_Adrianto), one of the suspects from private sector.[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_tempo-23][^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_tempo_ago_raid_riza_chalid-24] Oil trader [Mohammad Riza Chalid](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Riza_Chalid), Muhammad Kerry's father, had his house in [South Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jakarta) raided by prosecutors as part of the case investigation, even though he was not yet named as a suspect.[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_jakglob-21]

Additional suspects from the private sectors include: Dimas Werhaspati, Commissioner of PT Navigator Khatulistiwa and PT Jenggala Maritim; and Gading Ramadhan Joedo, Commissioner of PT Jenggala Maritim and PT Orbit Terminal Merak.[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_tempo_ago_uncover-25]

Investigations also uncovered that Pertamina Patra Niaga [adulterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adulterant) subsidized Pertalite RON 90 gasoline with the higher-quality Pertamax RON 92 and sold it at an inflated price,[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_jakglob-21] which was denied by Corporate Secretary of Pertamina Patra Niaga, Heppy Wulansari, on 26 February, without providing any proof.[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_tempo_26_feb-26]

On 25 February, member of [House of Representatives (DPR)](737841.md) [Eko Hendro Purnomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eko_Hendro_Purnomo) stated that the DPR will summon [Minister of State Owned Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_Owned_Enterprises_\(Indonesia\)) [Erick Thohir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erick_Thohir) without providing any details on the date.[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_kompas-27] Erick Thohir himself was too busy taking care of [PSSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSSI) since he still was available to make comments on the Indonesian national team under 23 and under 20.[^cite_note-28]

On 26 February, interviewed by *[Tempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo_\(Indonesian_magazine\))*, [Universitas Gadjah Mada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universitas_Gadjah_Mada) law professor M. Fatahillah Akbar stated that Pertamina consumers could take class action law suits against Pertamina both under criminal and civil law at the same time.[^cite_note-2025_gas_quality_tempo_26_feb_class_action-29]

During 2012 and early 2013, it was announced several times that there were plans to build two more large fuel refineries, each with a capacity of around 300,000 barrels (48,000 cubic metres) per day, perhaps in Balongan, West Java (or, alternatively, in Bontang, East Kalimantan) and in Tuban, East Java. The first facility was planned to be built by Pertamina in partnership with [Kuwait Petroleum](3781829.md), while the second was expected to be built by Pertamina in co-operation with [Saudi Aramco](290527.md). Total investment was expected to be around \$20 billion.[^cite_note-46] One main problem holding up an agreement to build the refineries was the issue of financial concessions to be provided for the foreign investors.[^cite_note-47] Eventually, in September 2013 it was announced that the plans for the first refinery had been cancelled. At the same time, the government said that there were plans for yet a different refinery project which would be constructed solely by Pertamina and funded by the state. The crude oil for this alternative project was expected to be supplied from Iraq.[^cite_note-48] Pending further progress on these large investment plans, Pertamina has announced (late 2014) plans to upgrade the existing refineries so as to add around 500,000 bbl (79,000 m<sup>3</sup>) per day to Pertamina's current refining capacity of around 1 million bbl (160 thousand m<sup>3</sup>) per day.[^cite_note-49]

Pertamina also has two gas reserves and a petrochemical company. Pertamina's products include a great variety of fuels, chemicals, additives, and retail products.

### Gas station

Pertamina has the largest distribution network for petroleum products ([gas stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_stations), etc.) in Indonesia.

### Bright Convenience Store

Along with the gas stations, Pertamina also has a convenience store chain, integrated with their gas stations. The development of Bright convenience stores and cafes is self-governed by PT Pertamina Retail.[^cite_note-50]

## Subsidiaries

Pertamina has 27 subsidiaries.[^cite_note-51]

- **Pertamina EP**

PT Pertamina EP (PEP) is engaged in exploration. PEP has also been undertaking other supporting businesses, which have been intended to back up the main business directly or indirectly.

Presently, Pertamina EP produces around 127.6 million [barrels](594043.md#Oil_barrel) (20.3 million [cubic metres](30125638.md)) of oil per day and around 1,054 million [cubic feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_foot) (29.8 million [cubic metres](30125638.md)) per day at [standard conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_temperature_and_pressure) for gas.

Pertamina EP Working Areas of 140.000 km2 were once largely PT Pertamina (Persero)’s Oil and Gas Mining Authority Zone. The working areas are managed through its own operation and partnership co-operation.

Pertamina EP Working Areas consist of five assets. The operation of those assets comprise 19 Field Areas, namely Rantau, Pangkalan Susu, Lirik, Jambi and Ramba in Asset 1, Prabumulih, Adera, Limau and Pendopo in Asset 2, Tambun, Subang and Jatibarang in Asset 3, Cepu in Asset 4 as well as Sangatta, Sangasanga, Bunyu, Tarakan, Tanjung and Papua in Asset 5.

Besides the management of working areas as stated earlier, other business patterns include management through projects, such as Pondok Makmur Development Project in West Java, Paku Gajah Development Project in South Sumatera, Jawa Gas Development Project in Central Java, and Matindok Gas Development Project in Central Sulawesi.

- **Pertamina Gas**

Pertamina established PT Pertagas on 23 February 2007, and it became PT Pertamina Gas in 2008. The company undertakes gas transportation, trading and processing. In the gas transmission business, Pertamina owns a gas pipeline network with a total volume of 34,000 km-inches in Northern Sumatra, Central Sumatra, Southern [Sumatra](28679.md), Western Java, Eastern [Java](69336.md), and East [Kalimantan](165470.md)

In January 2009, PT Pertamina Gas obtained a Transportation Permit and in February 2009, it received an exclusive right from BPH Migas for [gas](22131.md) transportation along 43 transmission routes. These permits and exclusive rights complemented the business permit that had been issued previously (in September 2008). By obtaining a business license and special rights, PT Pertamina Gas now has a regulatory basis to play the principal role in the gas business in Indonesia.

- **Pertamina Geothermal Energy**

PGE was founded on 12 December 2006. This Pertamina subsidiary carries out geothermal exploration and exploitation in 15 working areas (WKP) in Indonesia, namely: Sibayak-Sinabung, Sibual-buali–Sarulla, Sungai Penuh-Sumurup, Tambang Sawah-Hululais, Lumut Balai, Waypanas-Ulubelu, Cibereum-Parabakti, Pengalengan (Patuha-Wayang Windu), Kamojang-Darajat, Karaha-Telagabodas, Dieng, Iyang-Argopuro, Tabanan-Bali, Lahendong-Tompaso and Kotamobagu. It [went public](63879.md) in 2023 ([IDX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_Stock_Exchange): [PGEO](https://www.idx.co.id/en/listed-companies/company-profiles/PGEO)).

- **Pertamina EP Cepu**

PEP Cepu, which was established on 14 September 2005, is a subsidiary of PT Pertamina (Persero) that focuses on the upstream oil and gas business. In the [Cepu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cepu,_Blora) Block, Pertamina has a 45% interest in partnership with Mobil Cepu Ltd (as the operator) and the Regional Owned Enterprise (BUMD) that manages the KKS for the Cepu Block.

- **Pertamina Drilling Services Indonesia**

PT PDSI was established on 13 June 2008 as a drilling service management business entity. The services provided comprise drilling, workover activities, and drilling services that use a Daily Rate and Integrated Drilling Management (MPT) system for oil, gas, and geothermal wells.

Presently, PT PDSI owns 34 drilling rigs (28 owned by PT PDSI and 6 transferred from PT Usayana)

- **Pertamina Hulu Energy**

PHE is one of the Upstream Directorate subsidiaries working in the oil and gas upstream business and is also an upstream business vehicle for managing the domestic and overseas co-operation portfolio in the form of Production Sharing Contracts (PSC), Joint Operating Body-Production Sharing Contracts (JOB-PSC), Indonesian Participating / Pertamina Participating Interests (IP/PPI) and Badan Operasi Bersama (BOB). PHE’s overseas working areas covered: [Western Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Desert_\(Iraq\)) Block 3, Iraq; Block 10 & 11.1, Offshore South Vietnam; Block SK-305, Offshore Sarawak, Malaysia; Sabratah 17-3 Block, Offshore Libya; Sirte 123-3 Block, Libya; Block 13, Red Sea, Offshore Sudan; Block-3, Offshore Qatar; and Basker Manta Gummy Block, Australia.

- **Pertamina Internasional EP**

Pertamina Internasional Eksplorasi dan Produksi (PIEP) is established on 18 November 2013, based on the need for international asset management that is focused on overseas assets of PT Pertamina (Persero).[^cite_note-52]

- **Pertamina EP Cepu ADK**

PT Pertamina EP Cepu ADK hereinafter referred to as PEPC ADK was established on 15 August 2013 in order to manage Fields of Alas Dara and Kemuning (ADK). Following the prevailing rules and legislation in the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources, the PSC between SKKMigas and PEPC ADK was signed on 26 February 2014. PEPC ADK is the operator of Alas Dara and Kemuning, located in Blora, Central Java, which was previously operated by Mobil Cepu Ltd. (MCL). In line with the commitment to the Government, PEPC ADK shall conduct Well Re-entry, Perform G\&G Study, GGR Study, and exploration drilling. Since established, the PEPC ADK has never changed its name.[^cite_note-53]
