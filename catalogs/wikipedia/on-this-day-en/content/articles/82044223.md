A [diplomatic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_crisis) began in 2025 when the [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [second presidency](78298108.md) of the [United States](3434750.md) administration sought to annex [Greenland](12118.md), an autonomous territory of the [Danish Realm](11746442.md)—a treaty ally of the United States and co-member of [NATO](21133.md), and a member of the [European Union](9317.md) (EU).[^cite_note-3] The crisis intensified in early 2026[^cite_note-Reuters-FranceExercise-4][^cite_note-2026-01-19_Washington_Post-5] when Trump refused to rule out the use of military force for this annexation[^cite_note-bbc_20260107-6][^cite_note-theguardianpeace-7][^cite_note-politico9-8] and threatened a 25% [import tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import_tax) on goods from several European nations unless Denmark ceded the territory to the United States.[^cite_note-2026-01-17_Guardian-9][^cite_note-cnn_20260118_bazooka-10][^cite_note-bbc_20260119_100%-11] Denmark's refusal was supported by the EU and several other NATO members, reigniting [earlier concerns](79617151.md) of a US–EU [trade war](441392.md). Within weeks, Trump [backed down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_Always_Chickens_Out), pledging not to use force or tariffs to annex Greenland.[^cite_note-reversal-12][^cite_note-Global_News_Trump_Full-13]

Trump [had tried to purchase Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_United_States_acquisition_of_Greenland_\(first_Trump_presidency\)) during his [first presidency](52231341.md).[^cite_note-euronews1-14] After [his 2024 re-election](59568546.md), in January 2026, he said "it may be a choice" whether to preserve NATO or seize Greenland,[^cite_note-politico9-8] and in *[Dear Jonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Jonas)*, a letter leaked by Norwegian Prime Minister [Jonas Gahr Støre](2926932.md), Trump had said that he "no longer \[felt] an obligation to think purely of Peace" after not receiving the [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md).[^cite_note-theguardianpeace-7][^cite_note-\:6-15][^cite_note-bbc_20260119_nobel-16] A report by the [Danish Defence Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Defence_Intelligence_Service) mentioned the United States as a potential threat to [national security](240468.md) for the first time in its history,[^cite_note-politico-17][^cite_note-USAToday-18] and Danish officials raised concerns about reports that [Director of National Intelligence](456082.md) [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md) had assigned agents to spy on Greenland.[^cite_note-wsj_20250506-19][^cite_note-bbc1-20]

The Greenlandic and Danish prime ministers rejected any American takeover,[^cite_note-aft15-21] saying they would defend Greenland in the event of an attack.[^cite_note-euronewssh-22][^cite_note-theind1-23][^cite_note-theatlantic-boysen-24] Both NATO[^cite_note-ndtv-25] and EU[^cite_note-Guardian-20260112-26] members would be obliged to assist Denmark in the event of an attack. Denmark and eight NATO allies [deployed forces to defend the territory](82124736.md).[^cite_note-bbcdeployment-27] In response, Trump threatened a [trade war](441392.md) against the EU,[^cite_note-fortunetradewar-28] leading European politicians to suspend a proposed [EU–US trade agreement](80925459.md)[^cite_note-Guardian-20260117-29] and consider placing [sanctions](411315.md) on the US.[^cite_note-Bloombe-30]

Trump's threats led to [large protests](82139265.md) in Greenland and Denmark.[^cite_note-36] A [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) poll found that only 8% of Americans supported an invasion of Greenland, with 73% opposed.[^cite_note-YouGov-37] Trump's actions faced heavy opposition in [Congress](31756.md) from both major parties, with Republican speaker of the House [Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson) describing Trump's threats as "completely inappropriate" and a [bipartisan](70001288.md) congressional delegation travelling to Copenhagen to support [Denmark–United States relations](15982704.md).[^cite_note-politico4-38][^cite_note-Boston_Herald_2026-39] The crisis was described as one of the most erratic episodes involving an American president, prompting scrutiny of [Trump's age and fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_and_health_concerns_about_Donald_Trump) for office.[^cite_note-\:4-40][^cite_note-aol-41]

On 21 January, Trump reversed course,[^cite_note-Reuters-rev-42] first ruling out military force and then abandoning tariff threats after talks with NATO secretary-general [Mark Rutte](4321056.md) reached what Trump called a "framework of a future deal".[^cite_note-latimes1-43][^cite_note-44] Greenland and Denmark ruled out any deal altering the sovereignty of Greenland and Denmark,[^cite_note-eura-45][^cite_note-aaja-46] with Trump's comments referring to pre-existing commitments from a 1951 US–Denmark treaty.[^cite_note-sky6-47][^cite_note-nbcmeet-48] At the [2026 Ankara NATO summit](80796458.md) in July, Trump restated his opinion that it would be better if Greenland was controlled by the US and not by Denmark.[^cite_note-49] On 18 September 2026, the United States, Denmark, and Greenland reached an agreement in which the United States would receive the rights to an expanded military footprint in Greenland with greater security control, without, according to the Danish and Greelandic governments, conditionally transferring much military sovereignty of Greenland to the United States.[^cite_note-security_deal-50] The agreement was signed on 22 September 2026.[^cite_note-France_24-51]

## Background

Main article: [Proposed United States acquisition of Greenland](61530125.md)

During the [first Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Trump_administration), US president Donald Trump said that the United States should buy Greenland. The governments of Denmark and Greenland clarified that Greenland is not for sale and cannot be sold under the Danish constitution, and the Danish government has always rejected such proposals, which Prime Minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) called "an absurd discussion".[^cite_note-euronews1-14] Greenland invited American investment, however, stating that "we're open for business, not for sale".[^cite_note-bbc_49367792-52]

In December 2024, President-elect Trump, before the onset of his [second administration](78298108.md), reiterated his interest in the United States assuming control of Greenland, stating that "for purposes of national security and freedom throughout the world, the United States of America feels that the ownership and control of Greenland is an absolute necessity."[^cite_note-nbcnews_185197-53]

### History of Greenland

Main article: [History of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greenland)



Greenland is part of the [Nordic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries).



Greenland's [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), King [Frederik X](339155.md), and the Greenland prime minister [Múte Bourup Egede](65507782.md) visit [Pituffik Space Base](750735.md) and are greeted on the runway by US base commander Jason Terry, with Greenlandic, Danish, and US flags in the background, 29 June 2024. The King and the Prime Minister made a stop at the base when travelling to [Qaanaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaanaaq) in the north of Greenland and are wearing traditional Greenlandic blue anoraks.

Greenland is an [autonomous territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_territory) of the Kingdom of Denmark, and has been associated with the Scandinavian kingdoms of Denmark and Norway for more than a millennium, beginning in 986 AD when [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) settlers from what is now Norway and Iceland settled Greenland.[^cite_note-Denmark.dk-54] The 13th century saw the arrival of the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit), the ancestors of today's majority population. After around 400 years, the Norse settlement in Greenland is believed to have died out in the late 15th century, but the legends of it persisted in Scandinavia.[^cite_note-dw-history-55] This prompted King [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV) to send [an expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV's_expeditions_to_Greenland) to Greenland in 1605 to locate the lost [Eastern Norse Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Settlement) and reassert sovereignty over Greenland. Norwegian priest [Hans Egede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Egede) reestablished a permanent Dano-Norwegian presence in 1721, founding the town of Godthaab (now [Nuuk](53303.md)).[^cite_note-dw-history-55] Today's population is mainly Inuit, with a smaller Danish population, and many people are of mixed Inuit and Danish origin. Greenlanders often have close family, personal, and cultural ties to Denmark, with thousands living there.[^cite_note-Stat.gl-56]

Greenland formally became part of the [Kingdom of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Norway_\(872%E2%80%931397\)) in 1261, when the Norse inhabitants submitted to the King of Norway. Norway then entered [a union with Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark-Norway) in 1380. Under the 1814 [Treaty of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kiel), Greenland remained with the Danish Crown as part of the settlement with Sweden following the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars). Denmark declared full sovereignty over all of Greenland, including the uninhabited parts, in 1921, in the aftermath of the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles).[^cite_note-diis-57] In 1933, Norway, which occupied [part of Eastern Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_the_Red's_Land), accepted a [ruling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihlen_Declaration#Eastern_Greenland_Case) of the [Permanent Court of International Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_International_Justice) affirming Danish sovereignty over all of Greenland and renounced its claim.

The 1953 [Constitution of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark) ended Greenland's status as a colony, integrating it fully into the Danish state as a regular [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Denmark), as part of [decolonization](302084.md) efforts and with the consent of the [Greenland Provincial Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_Provincial_Council).[^cite_note-diis-57] In the [1979 Greenlandic home rule referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Greenlandic_home_rule_referendum), Denmark granted [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) to Greenland, leading to the establishment of a local government authority with responsibility for local matters, but Greenland remains part of the Kingdom of Denmark, with the central government in Copenhagen solely responsible for defence and foreign policy. As part of the [Nordic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_region) and the Kingdom of Denmark, Greenland is an associate member of the [Nordic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council). Greenland is one of the [Overseas Countries and Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_territories_of_members_of_the_European_Economic_Area#Overseas_countries_and_territories) of the EU. Greenlanders are Danish citizens and therefore also [EU citizens](1882861.md).

In 2009, Greenland was recognised by the Kingdom of Denmark and under international law as a [people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_sovereignty) entitled to external self-determination.[^cite_note-diis-57] Most political parties in Greenland favour [independence from Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_independence) as a long-term goal, with a draft constitution for an independent Greenlandic state presented by lawmakers in 2023, but the majority does not favour severing ties with Denmark in the short term.[^cite_note-cnbc_20260112-58]

### Military presence in Greenland



[Royal Danish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy) frigate [HDMS *Absalon* (F341)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDMS_Absalon_\(F341\)) off the coast of Greenland

Denmark is one of the twelve original founding member states of [NATO](21133.md), and signed the Greenland Defense Agreement with the United States in 1951, allowing the American military to operate in Greenland with Danish consent under a NATO framework.[^cite_note-nytimes_20260107-59] At its peak, approximately 10,000 American military personnel were stationed in Greenland, including about 6,000 at what is now [Pituffik Space Base](750735.md). After the [Cold War](325329.md), Greenland became a lower strategic priority for the United States, which gradually reduced its presence to roughly 150 personnel by 2026.[^cite_note-60] After the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001, Denmark supplied military aid to the United States as part of its NATO Article 5 obligation, incurring [44 deaths in the Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_casualties_in_Afghanistan).

Denmark increased its Arctic defence and led a large NATO exercise in Greenland in 2025.[^cite_note-61] The exercise involved more than 550 soldiers, including [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces), from Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, and Germany.[^cite_note-arcticlight-62][^cite_note-63] The Nordic countries also collaborate on Arctic defence through NATO exercises conducted across the region, such as the Joint Viking exercise in 2025.[^cite_note-64] In 2025, Denmark announced a 14.6 [billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,000,000,000) [kr.](236323.md) plan to boost Arctic defence.[^cite_note-dw_71430346-65]

Greenland is under the military command of the [Joint Arctic Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Arctic_Command), Danish general Søren Andersen.[^cite_note-66] The Arctic Command has around 150 permanent personnel, but regularly deploys units from across the Danish Defence forces, including the Arctic Response Force with aircraft and ships that stand ready to support forces in Greenland.[^cite_note-theint-67] The [Sirius Dog Sled Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius_Dog_Sled_Patrol) special forces unit operates around [Daneborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daneborg) in the northeast of Greenland, a [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park), but only comprises a small part of Danish and allied military capabilities in Greenland.[^cite_note-theint-67] As of 2026, NATO members are in discussions about establishing a permanent NATO mission in Greenland, Arctic Sentry, modelled on the Baltic Sentry, following a proposal by Germany.[^cite_note-bloomberg_20260111-68] [Law enforcement in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Greenland) employs around 350 people.[^cite_note-politiforum_271145-69] As of 2026, hundreds of elite Danish combat soldiers trained in [arctic warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_warfare) have been deployed to Greenland, including the [Chief of the Royal Danish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Royal_Danish_Army), General [Peter Harling Boysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Harling_Boysen), with Denmark stating its intention to send a "substantial contribution" of its armed forces there in response to the Greenland crisis.[^cite_note-theindboysen-70] Denmark said the deployment might stay in Greenland for 1–2 years.[^cite_note-politicosoldiers-71]

### History of US recognition of Danish sovereignty over Greenland

Further information: [Treaty of the Danish West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Danish_West_Indies)

In the *1826 General Convention of Friendship, Commerce and Navigation* with the king of Denmark, the United States recognised Greenland as a northern possession of the king, and accepted the trade restrictions that Denmark imposed there.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] In the [Treaty of the Danish West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Danish_West_Indies), signed in 1916, the US government explicitly recognised Danish sovereignty over all of Greenland.[^cite_note-diis-57] In the 1941 US–Danish agreement, the American Government "reiterate\[d] its recognition of and respect for the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Denmark over Greenland".[^cite_note-cnn-hist-74] During World War II, the United States [defended Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_in_World_War_II) at the request of Danish and Greenlandic officials acting without approval from the central government of Denmark, while it was under Nazi occupation. In the 1951 Greenland Defense Agreement, the United States again recognised the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Denmark over all of Greenland.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] In the 2004 amendment to the Greenland Defense Agreement, the United States recognised Greenland as "an equal part of the Kingdom of Denmark under the Constitution \[with] a wide ranging Greenland Home Rule".[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-cnn-hist-74]

### Donald Trump's first presidency

Since late 2018, Trump repeatedly privately and publicly discussed buying Greenland. The Trump administration "courted Greenlandic politicians with promises of American business investment, educational opportunities, and development aid". According to a report in the [New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Yorker_\(magazine\)), a 2019 delegation of American diplomats and national-security officials, dispatched to discuss mining of mineral and particularly rare-earth mineral resources, included members of the Greenland Policy Coordination Committee, a committee within the National Security Council "working to subvert the Kingdom of Denmark".[^cite_note-newyorker_20260623-78]

### Security deal and ending of the crisis



President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) (left) signs the security agreement with Denmark's Prime Minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) (middle) and Greenland's Prime Minister [Jens-Frederik Nielsen](79438070.md) (right) during the [general debate of the 81st session of the United Nations General Assembly](83962647.md), in [New York City](645042.md), 22 September 2026.

On 18 September, Donald Trump posted on Truth Social that the US, Denmark, and Greenland had agreed to a new security deal that addressed all of the US's concerns, and provided for a larger US military footprint. Trump claimed that the deal gives the US "permanent control" over security in Greenland, while officials from Denmark and Greenland said that the deal does not transfer sovereignty of Greenland to the US.[^cite_note-security_deal-50] Trump also said that his administration would begin cooperating with local authorities to maintain a "large Military presence in the appropriate part of Greenland, of which there are many."[^cite_note-\:7-173] An anonymous [State Department](31975.md) official said the deal would remain in effect even if Greenland became independent in the future, that it bans non-NATO states from maintaining a military presence in Greenland; bars investments in Greenland by Russia and China, and grants the US permanent rights to install military bases and use Greenland's airspace.[^cite_note-\:7-173][^cite_note-174] US Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) said that the deal "permanently and completely addresses our national security concerns in Greenland" and described it as a "huge win for the United States and the American people."[^cite_note-\:7-173][^cite_note-\:8-175]

Danish Prime Minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) said the deal strengthens "security in the Arctic and the North Atlantic area" and that the deal would be signed at the upcoming [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md). She did not discuss specific policies and stressed that it required parliamentary approval to be enacted.[^cite_note-\:8-175] Greenland Prime Minister [Jens-Frederik Nielsen](79438070.md) called the deal "gratifying" and "for the benefit of us all."

On 22 September 2026, during the [general debate of the eighty-first session of the United Nations General Assembly](83962647.md), the United States, Denmark, and Greenland signed a security agreement that increases US involvement in Greenland's defence while affirming Danish sovereignty and Greenland's right to self-determination.[^cite_note-France_24-51]

## Response

### Danish legal framework



The chairman of the Danish government's Defence Committee, [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_People's_Party_\(Denmark\)) [Rasmus Jarlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Jarlov), said that Denmark would defend its territory and invoke [Article 5 of NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_5_of_NATO) if attacked by the US.

[^cite_note-ndtv-25]

In January 2026, Thomas Crosbie, an American military expert working at the [Royal Danish Defence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Defence_College), said that any attempt to seize Greenland would constitute a [criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law) act, and that Denmark, with the backing of its allies, would have the legal right to arrest any Americans involved in such actions and prosecute them under [Danish criminal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_criminal_law).[^cite_note-politico3-176] Danish troops in Greenland are legally obligated to defend Danish territory under [military law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_law). Under a 1952 standing order, Danish troops are ordered to "immediately take up the fight without waiting for, or seeking orders" in "the event of an attack on Danish territory".[^cite_note-politico3-176][^cite_note-theint-67] The Danish government confirmed in January 2026 that the order remains in place and that Danish soldiers would shoot back if Greenland is attacked.[^cite_note-euronewssh-22]

The [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Denmark\)) said Danish troops would immediately respond to an invasion of Greenland with force and the chairman of the Defence Committee, [Rasmus Jarlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Jarlov), said Denmark would invoke [Article 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty#Article_5) if attacked by the US.[^cite_note-theind1-23][^cite_note-ndtv-25] Jarlov said an American attack would mean war with Denmark and that retaliation would include [deadly force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadly_force).[^cite_note-177] As of January 2026, Article 5 has been invoked [only once in NATO history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_NATO_Article_5_contingency): in response to the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) on the US in 2001, when Denmark volunteered "to fight alongside American soldiers in two different conflicts ... \[deploying] tens of thousands of troops to Afghanistan and Iraq over two decades ... \[suffering] the [third-highest per capita casualty rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_casualties_in_Afghanistan#Denmark)".[^cite_note-fra26-178]

### Military and intelligence response

#### Danish Realm



General [Peter Harling Boysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Harling_Boysen), the [Chief of the Royal Danish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Royal_Danish_Army), arrived in Greenland with a "substantial" force on 19–20 January 2026, saying he is ready to defend Greenland. General Boysen has extensive experience from international warzones and special operations.

[^cite_note-theatlantic-boysen-24]



Danish and French soldiers during [Operation Arctic Endurance](82124736.md), widely considered a [tripwire force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripwire_force) to deter a US invasion

Shortly after the start of the crisis, the Danish Realm began rapidly expanding its military capabilities in the [Arctic](36971.md). On 27 January 2025, its governments agreed to the First Agreement on the Arctic and North Atlantic, which invested a total of [kr.](236323.md) 14.6 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)2.05 billion) into [warning systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warning_system) in the Faroe Islands and Greenland, new naval vessels, [drone warfare](38212231.md) training, a new [radiation monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_monitoring) station in East Greenland, upgrades to the [Joint Arctic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Arctic_Command) in [Nuuk](53303.md) and intelligence, [satellite surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_surveillance), two new coastal [radars](25676.md), and Arctic basic military training in [Kangerlussuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangerlussuaq).[^cite_note-179] This was followed by the Second Agreement on 10 October 2025, providing upgrades worth [kr.](236323.md) 27.4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)4.26 billion) for improvements of what was presented in the First Agreement, alongside upgrades to [Kangerlussuaq Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangerlussuaq_Airport), a new specialised Arctic unit under the [Jaeger Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaeger_Corps_\(Denmark\)) of the [Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Operations_Command_\(Denmark\)) that could operate anywhere in Greenland,[^cite_note-flightglobal_166004-180] establishment of radar capability in East Greenland, establishment of a Greenlandic [reconnaissance](146911.md) Unit, and the construction of a new [undersea cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undersea_cable) connecting Greenland to mainland Denmark.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

In summer 2025, the Greenlandic government and the [Danish Defence](8038.md) both announced that Greenland would have increased military presence by September, as part of Operation Arctic Light.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184] On 18 August 2025, the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Denmark\)) allocated a package worth more than [kr.](236323.md) 850 million to their operations in Greenland and the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md).[^cite_note-Pressevagt-2025-185] Alongside promotions of general [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) and upgrades to courts, the package also provided funding for strengthening the [PET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Security_and_Intelligence_Service)'s activities in the North Atlantic, and provided new maritime equipment to Greenlandic and Faroese police in response to the crisis.[^cite_note-Pressevagt-2025-185]

By 19 January 2026, a total of over [kr.](236323.md) 88 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)13.7 billion) had been committed to "strengthen defense and security in the Arctic", with [kr.](236323.md) 29 billion allocated to acquiring [F-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35) fighter jets alone, and [kr.](236323.md) 27.8 billion for [anti-aircraft warfare](146640.md), according to the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Denmark\)),[^cite_note-Forsvarsministeriet-186][^cite_note-187] and at that point Denmark had sent 200 additional soldiers to Greenland.[^cite_note-188] On the same day, more soldiers together with the [Chief of the Royal Danish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Royal_Danish_Army), General [Peter Harling Boysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Harling_Boysen), started arriving in Greenland, and Denmark announced that a "substantial contribution" of its armed forces would be sent there.[^cite_note-theindboysen-70][^cite_note-tv2-189] Also on 19 January, Defence Minister [Troels Lund Poulsen](15575631.md) and Greenlandic Minister of Foreign Affairs [Vivian Motzfeldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Motzfeldt) met NATO secretary general Rutte and their Nordic Security ministers in [Brussels](3708.md) about the situation in Greenland.[^cite_note-Forsvarsministeriet-186] General Boysen said he is ready to defend Greenland.[^cite_note-theatlantic-boysen-24] According to Boysen, the soldiers landed in [Kangerlussuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangerlussuaq) in western Greenland.[^cite_note-Power-190]

On 20 January 2026, Denmark deployed additional troops to Greenland, sending dozens of soldiers and senior military leadership to the Arctic territory to reinforce its presence and participate in ongoing multinational exercises, increasing the Danish Armed Forces' personnel stationed there amid heightened international tensions.[^cite_note-Power-190] The same day, Greenland's government mulled the creation of a task force with representatives from local authorities to prepare residents for potential disruptions to daily life, including guidance recommending that households keep five days of food supplies. Additionally, PM [Jens-Frederik Nielsen](79438070.md) stated that a military conflict could not be ruled out.[^cite_note-\:1-191]

The [Joint Arctic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Arctic_Command) announced plans to expand its military exercises, potentially conducting them on a year-round basis.[^cite_note-\:1-191] A Danish defence analyst interviewed by *[Times Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Radio)* said Denmark discussed plans to station around 1,000 Danish ground forces in Greenland, in addition to other capabilities and allied contributions.[^cite_note-YouTube_2DEn40iN3Js-192] An investigation by *Times Radio* suggested that it would be very hard for the US to successfully invade Greenland due to a combination of lack of US Arctic capabilities, the geography of Greenland, and Danish knowledge of the area, preparedness and Arctic training.[^cite_note-YouTube_RxqN_xWoA3Y-193]

On 14 January 2026, Denmark started to send military reinforcements to Greenland to strengthen its military presence.[^cite_note-drdk1-194][^cite_note-195] Later that day, it became known that both Sweden[^cite_note-DagblSw-196] and Norway sent military forces to support the Danish Defence in protecting Greenland.[^cite_note-dagsavisen_10150179-197] The Ministry of Defence on 14 January 2026 announced "an increased military presence in and around Greenland, comprising aircraft, vessels and soldiers, including from NATO allies".[^cite_note-198] After consultations between a group of European countries,[^cite_note-reuters_20260111-199] Germany announced it was sending a small contingent to Greenland on a reconnaissance mission with other European nations.[^cite_note-reuters_20260115-200] On 15 January 2026, a French military contingent arrived in Greenland.[^cite_note-bbcdeployment-27]

On 16 January, Danish [F-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35) and French [A330 MRTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330_MRTT) jets conducted a training mission in southeast Greenland.[^cite_note-201] On 17 January, Major General Søren Andersen stated that at least 100 units have arrived in [Nuuk](53303.md), and another 100 in [Kangerlussuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangerlussuaq).[^cite_note-202] By 18 January, the deployment involved Belgium,[^cite_note-belrtbf-203][^cite_note-belbrumorn-204] France, Germany,[^cite_note-dw_75533548-205] Sweden, Norway, Finland, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, Estonia, Slovenia, and Iceland, with Estonia's deployment yet to be executed.[^cite_note-206] French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) stated that the deployment would soon be reinforced with land, air, and sea assets as part of [Operation Arctic Endurance](82124736.md).[^cite_note-bbcdeployment-27] Denmark is planning a larger and more permanent NATO presence.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

On 18 January, Germany's reconnaissance team returned to Germany,[^cite_note-dw_75550031-209] with the [Bundeswehr](288188.md) stating that the mission to plan for future engagements had been "completed, as planned".[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] On 19 January it became known that Canada considered sending troops to Greenland to reinforce Danish sovereignty.[^cite_note-212]

Despite Poland's recent interest and participation in military exercises in the Arctic region, and having signed a bilateral defence agreement with Denmark in the 1990s, Polish prime minister [Donald Tusk](648724.md) and Defence Minister [Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz](60544479.md) stated that Poland would not deploy troops in Greenland.\[^cite_note-[www.polskieradio.pl-213](http://www.polskieradio.pl-213/)][^cite_note-214][^cite_note-The_Trump_Report-2026-215] This decision was criticised by former Major General [Roman Polko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Polko) as "sheer cowardice", to which Kosiniak-Kamysz defended it as protecting NATO unity.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217]

Between 19 January and 3 February,[^cite_note-218] [NORAD](42905.md) deployed their aircraft to [Pituffik Space Base](750735.md) in Greenland for long-planned activities, describing the deployment as routine and coordinated with the Kingdom of Denmark with diplomatic clearances and with the [Government of Greenland](23389805.md) informed of the planned operations.[^cite_note-aa_3804555-219]

On 21 January, France proposed a larger NATO exercise in Greenland and said they are willing to participate.[^cite_note-Reuters-FranceExercise-4]

#### Operation Arctic Sentry

On 11 February, [NATO](21133.md) launched [Operation Arctic Sentry](82391936.md), a military exercise led by [JFC Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Force_Command_Norfolk) aimed to strengthen NATO's posture in the Arctic,[^cite_note-220] with [Supreme Allied Commander Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Allied_Commander_Europe) [Alexus Grynkewich](66715028.md) stating that it "underscores the alliance's commitment to safeguard its members and maintain stability."[^cite_note-221] While the operation is not intended to be permanent, Canadian Foreign Affairs minister [Anita Anand](61752870.md) urges it to be otherwise, commenting that "defending the Arctic is defending NATO's northern flank and North America itself".[^cite_note-222]

Despite being officially intended to counter Sino–Russian threats in the Arctic,[^cite_note-YouTube_UdeAxw9OmMs-223] Operation Arctic Sentry has often been interpreted that it's largely meant to "appease [Trump](4848272.md)" regarding Greenland instead.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-\:5-225]

According to [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle), participating countries would include the United States, Canada, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Iceland.[^cite_note-\:5-225]

#### Invasion preparedness

The Greenlandic government has asked citizens to prepare for a possible attack on Greenland by the United States, including by stockpiling supplies.[^cite_note-bloomberg-invasion-226]

According to a report by Danish broadcaster [DR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DR_\(broadcaster\)), published in March 2026, Danish forces also prepared defensive measures in early 2026, including the deployment of special forces units and the pre-positioning of medical supplies such as blood for potential casualties. Troops reportedly brought explosives intended to disable key airstrips in Nuuk and Kangerlussuaq to prevent American landings.[^cite_note-227]

### "Hands off Greenland" anti-Trump protests

Main article: [Hands off Greenland protests](82139265.md)

Large [anti-Trump protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Trump_protests), named "[Hands off Greenland](82139265.md)", took place across Greenland and Denmark on 17 January to protest against Trump's threats against Greenland.[^cite_note-Guardian-20260117-29] The solidarity protests are organised by Greenlandic associations in Copenhagen, Aarhus, Aalborg, Odense and Nuuk.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-dw_75546625-229] Uagut, an association of Greenlanders in Denmark, said the aim of the protests is to send a message of respect for Greenland's democracy and fundamental human rights.[^cite_note-theaustralian_43521973-230] In Copenhagen, thousands of Greenlanders and Danes filled [City Hall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hall_Square,_Copenhagen).[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-Guardian-20260117-29] Protesters chanted "Greenland is not for sale" and "Kalaallit Nunaat"[^cite_note-232] and marched to the [US embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Copenhagen) in Copenhagen in solidarity with Greenlanders.[^cite_note-usatoday_88231100007-233][^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235] The slogan "Make America Go Away" soared in popularity in response to the protests, becoming a symbol of anti-Trump defiance.[^cite_note-241]

In addition to earlier protests, on 21 May 2026, after the opening of a new United States consulate in Nuuk, hundreds of people protested in the capital of Greenland. Demonstrators chanted slogans such as "Greenland for Greenlanders", opposing the expansion of American diplomatic presence in the autonomous territory.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-theguardian_20260521_greenland-243][^cite_note-244]

## US–EU trade war

In response to [Operation Arctic Endurance](82124736.md) and European countries' refusal to support his annexation of Greenland, Trump threatened to increase [tariffs](55551.md)[^cite_note-telegraph-tradewar2-245][^cite_note-theguardianpeace-7][^cite_note-2026-01-19_Washington_Post-5] against several EU and NATO members,[^cite_note-fortunetradewar-28][^cite_note-telegra-246] reigniting concerns of a US-EU trade war.[^cite_note-247] Trump said he would impose [tariffs](78448911.md) on any trading partner that did not support American annexation of Greenland.[^cite_note-apnews_728102-248][^cite_note-dw_75533548-205]

In January 2026, he threatened Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland with tariffs of up to 25% tariffs until they supported his takeover of Greenland,[^cite_note-2026-01-17_Guardian-9][^cite_note-cnbctariffs-249][^cite_note-newsandsentinel_20260117-250] stating that they had "journeyed to Greenland, for purposes unknown. This is a very dangerous situation for the Safety, Security, and Survival of our Planet".[^cite_note-time_7347120-251] On 21 January, Trump announced that he would not impose the tariffs, having agreed to a deal with NATO secretary general [Mark Rutte](4321056.md).[^cite_note-nbcnews_255270-252]

### European response

In response to Trump's threats, EU leaders promised a firm, EU-wide joint response. [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) president [António Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Costa) said the EU will be "very firm in defending international law ... starting within the territory of the European Union's member states".[^cite_note-politicotradewar-253] European Commission president [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) promised an "unflinching, united and proportional" response.[^cite_note-theguardian_20260120_davos-254] [Kaja Kallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Kallas), the EU's High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, said China and Russia "must be having a field day" as a result of Trump's tariffs.[^cite_note-politicotradewar-253] The EU Parliament suspended approval of a proposed [EU–US trade agreement](80925459.md) on 21 January, and substantial retaliatory tariffs against the US were readied.[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-bbc_20260121-256][^cite_note-nbcnews_255199-257][^cite_note-go_129422031-258]

French president Macron described Trump's threats as unacceptable and said that "no intimidation nor threat will influence us, neither [in Ukraine](79400268.md), nor in Greenland".[^cite_note-reuters_20260117-259] Norwegian prime minister [Jonas Gahr Støre](2926932.md) condemned Trump's threats and wrote that "Norway's position is firm: Greenland is part of the Kingdom of Denmark. Norway fully supports the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Denmark."[^cite_note-Guardian-20260117-29] British prime minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) said Trump's trade war is "completely wrong".[^cite_note-STTW_1-260] Danish prime minister Mette Frederiksen said Europe would not be blackmailed by Trump.[^cite_note-bbc_20260117-261] A joint statement by Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom emphasised the principles of sovereignty and territorial integrity and accused Trump of creating a "dangerous downward spiral" with his threats.[^cite_note-Reuters-262][^cite_note-abc7news_18427942-263] On 18 January, ambassadors from all 27 member states of the EU held an emergency meeting on Trump's threats.[^cite_note-cnn_20260119-264] After Trump reversed his decision on implementing tariffs, EU officials were sceptical,[^cite_note-265] but announced on 22 January that approval of the trade agreement would resume.[^cite_note-bloomberg_20260122-266][^cite_note-reuters_20260122-267]

#### Anti-Coercion Instrument

Several European leaders including France's Emmanuel Macron advocated using the EU's [Anti-Coercion Instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Coercion_Instrument), a security and trade policy tool known as the "trade bazooka", to target the United States in retaliation.[^cite_note-nytimes_20260118-268][^cite_note-euronews_20260118-269][^cite_note-270][^cite_note-Bloombe-30][^cite_note-271] Under the not-previously-used instrument, the EU may adopt countermeasures, sometimes described as sanctions, including tariffs, restrictions on public procurement, and measures affecting trade and investment.[^cite_note-Reuters-262][^cite_note-272] [Bernd Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Lange), chair of the [European Parliament Committee on International Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_Committee_on_International_Trade), supported its activation.[^cite_note-273] By 21 January EU countries were described as being increasingly open to using the ACI against the United States, with Germany stating it will ask the Commission to explore its use.[^cite_note-274]

### US opposition

Senior Republicans as well as Democrats described Trump's tariff threats as bad for the United States, bad for business, and a strategic gift to [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) and [Xi Jinping](2017814.md).[^cite_note-275] On the other hand, Trump said that Greenland was a US national security priority because he did not want Russia or China as neighbours,[^cite_note-msn_20260113-276] although Russia is already a neighbour of the United States; at their closest points, the American island of [Little Diomede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Diomede_Island) and the Russian island of [Big Diomede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Diomede_Island) in the [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait) are only 3.8 km (2.4 mi) away from each other.[^cite_note-yesterday-277][^cite_note-279] Senate minority leader [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer) announced that Democrats would introduce legislation to block any tariffs "before they do further damage to the American economy and our allies in Europe".[^cite_note-cnn_20260118_protest-280][^cite_note-thehill_5694564-281]

### Russian reaction

Russia welcomed Trump's tariffs against its [NATO](21133.md) allies, with [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) officials saying it was evidence that the transatlantic alliance was collapsing.[^cite_note-kyivindependent_20260118-282] The Russian government newspaper *[Rossiyskaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossiyskaya_Gazeta)* praised Trump's push to take over Greenland and welcomed the strain it was causing between the US and Europe.[^cite_note-bbc_20260119_gloats-283]

On 16 and 17 June 2025, the governments of Greenland, Denmark, and the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md) participated in their biannual meeting in [Tórshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B3rshavn) in light of the crisis.[^cite_note-284] While Danish prime minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) didn't want to elaborate on her negotiations with Greenland and the Faroe Islands at the time, she called for unity among all three governments of the Kingdom of Denmark, with Faroese PM [Aksel V. In response to US threats against Greenland and the Kingdom of Denmark, Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen reminded Trump that an attack on the Kingdom of Denmark is an attack on [NATO](21133.md) and that all members are obligated to come to Denmark's defence, urging the US to cease its threats,[^cite_note-euint-285] which threaten the existence of NATO and the security framework established since the end of [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-cnn_20260109-286][^cite_note-theguardian11-287]

He said Denmark has no other choice than to defend its territory, and that non-resistance is not an option.[^cite_note-YouTube_hgB80WMRyJg-293] He further said that Trump is "the biggest threat to world peace."[^cite_note-chan4-294] He said Trump speaks like a [gangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangster) in his threats against Greenland.[^cite_note-295][^cite_note-2026-01-19_Washington_Post-5] He also stated that time for "flattering Trump" was over while suggesting hard economic actions by Europe.[^cite_note-reuters_20260120_Gray-296] He also suggested the establishment of a "European NATO" without the US.[^cite_note-chan4-294]

On 23 January 2026, Danish prime minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) visited Greenland to express support for the people and the island's self-governing government, following comments by US president Donald Trump indicating his willingness to annex the territory. ## Public opinion

In January 2026, [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) published a poll on American public opinion regarding the Greenland crisis. The results showed that 58% of respondents oppose efforts to take over Greenland, either strongly (45%) or somewhat (13%), while 22% support either strongly (10%) or somewhat (12%), and another 20% were unsure.[^cite_note-369]

In Greenland, a poll from January 2025 showed that public opinion was overwhelmingly against American takeover, with 85% reporting opposition to becoming a part of the United States, while 6% supported and 9% were unsure.[^cite_note-370]

In a [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) poll in February 2026 among major Europeans countries, a majority of respondents in all countries polled reported unfavourable views of the United States because of the Greenland crisis. At the same time, a majority in all polled countries reported are sceptical that European countries collectively are able to stand up to the United States, and a plurality of respondents in all countries (except Germany) reported their belief that this US foreign policy is temporary will only last during the [Trump presidency](78298108.md).[^cite_note-371]

## Analysis

### Use of hybrid warfare

The Trump administration's actions against Greenland and Denmark, initially referred to as the Greenland crisis,[^cite_note-DR-2025-372] have been described by academics and commentators as [hybrid warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_warfare),[^cite_note-373][^cite_note-ap3-98][^cite_note-374][^cite_note-375] a broad umbrella concept that may include the use of [political warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_warfare), [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news), diplomacy, [lawfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawfare), regime change, and [foreign electoral intervention](52846360.md), among other methods. Greenland was united in condemnation of the US,[^cite_note-morgenbladet_9619276-376] describing it as "an entirely new and unsettling situation",[^cite_note-bt1-377] given that Greenland lacked realistic countermeasures.[^cite_note-378]

Danish media interviewed several experts who said that the US actions against Greenland clearly amount to hybrid warfare.[^cite_note-dab-379] André Ken Jakobsson, a researcher on hybrid warfare, said, already in the spring of 2025, that "the tools the United States uses to achieve its objectives \[in Greenland] also fall within the gray zone typically associated with hybrid warfare".[^cite_note-380] Jakobsson believes the United States is now deliberately seeking to fracture the Kingdom of Denmark with the use of intelligence agencies[^cite_note-bt1-377] and that the US has been using all of its means short of [conventional war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_warfare) in its campaign to try to get Greenland.[^cite_note-Talbot-381]

Speaking on US hybrid warfare and espionage, [DR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DR_\(broadcaster\)) commentator Niels Fastrup said that the US has shifted from an attempt to buy Greenland in 2019 to hybrid influence operations, conducted by private individuals close to Trump.[^cite_note-382] Swedish foreign affairs commentator and terrorism expert [Wolfgang Hansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Hansson?action=edit\&redlink=1) compared Trump's actions to [Russian hybrid warfare](78521073.md), stating that "Trump realised that taking over Greenland militarily would be expensive, risky, and look very bad, but hybrid warfare is cheap and easy to deny".[^cite_note-omni-383]

### Implications for NATO



[Donald Trump](4848272.md) and [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the [2025 Alaska summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Russia%E2%80%93United_States_summit)

Trump's threats against Greenland have been described as a new, potentially unprecedented challenge to [NATO](21133.md),[^cite_note-Denmark.dk-54] given that the US military [already has full access to Greenland](12126.md), Denmark has been a very loyal ally of the US, and has kept [Chinese investments and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China-Denmark_relations) out of Greenland.[^cite_note-Stat.gl-56] Former head of the [NATO Defense College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Defense_College), Arne Bård Dalhaug, said that Trump's threats against Greenland "comes across as a gift-wrapped present from Trump to Putin", allowing him free hands in Eastern Europe.[^cite_note-db52-384] According to international relations scholar [Iver B. Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iver_B._Neumann), Trump is splitting the West on behalf of the Russians, which has been a key aim of Russian and [Soviet foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_foreign_policy) for years.[^cite_note-db52-384]

Anders Puck Nielsen, a military analyst at the [Royal Danish Defence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Defence_College), and [Andrius Kubilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrius_Kubilius), the EU Commissioner for Defence and Space, agree that, if an American invasion of Greenland were carried out, it would be the end of NATO.[^cite_note-elk262-385][^cite_note-reuters_20260112-386] Nielsen emphasised that it is now clear Denmark can no longer rely on the US, and that the future lies in a European defence cooperation without US involvement.[^cite_note-elk262-385]

[Kori Schake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kori_Schake) said it will take a generation to repair the damage and collapse of trust among the US's closest allies that Trump has caused.[^cite_note-The_Trump_Report-2026-215] [Richard N. Haass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_N._Haass) said Europeans have come to see the US as a threat and no longer trust the US, and that Trump's actions are "turning upside down" what America has worked for over 75 years.[^cite_note-387] In an editorial, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* said Trump was enabling "the fondest dream of Russian strategy ... to divide Western Europe from the US and break the NATO alliance."[^cite_note-388] European leaders warned that Trump's threats have fundamentally undermined trust in the United States as a security and economic partner, accelerating efforts to reduce reliance on them and to develop independent European defence, security, and political coordination structures.[^cite_note-389]

Christopher S. Chivvis, a senior fellow and director of the American Statecraft Program at the [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace), argues that the tariff threat to facilitate a US annexation of Greenland represents a fundamental break with the post-1945 transatlantic order. He contends that by linking trade access to the territorial sovereignty of a NATO ally, the United States would shift from a security guarantor to a form of imperial power. Chivvis describes the demand as a revival of nineteenth-century imperial practices, contrasting it with China's tendency to frame its territorial ambitions as the recovery of historically claimed lands.[^cite_note-theguardian_20260120_trump-390]

[David Ignatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ignatius) argued that Trump's efforts to annex Greenland had triggered a serious crisis that could undermine US security for decades, with potential costs far exceeding any strategic benefits of controlling the island. He characterised the initiative as self-destructive, remarking that it amounted to "shooting yourself in the head."[^cite_note-washingtonpost_20260107-391] Joshua Yaffa wrote that Trump needlessly caused a crisis in NATO and exacerbated European distrust toward the US only to "end up with basically the same set of options that existed months ago."[^cite_note-newyorker_20260125-392]

Former Premier of Ontario and [Permanent Representative of Canada to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_Canada_to_the_United_Nations) [Bob Rae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Rae) stated that Trump "doesn't take Canada's sovereignty seriously", while former [Principal Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Secretary_\(Canada\)) to the Prime Minister [Gerald Butts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Butts) stated that, although he didn't believe Trump would invade Canada directly, "we're going to see a lot of threats, and we're going to see a lot of seeding of pro-American activity by the Americans in Canadian life".[^cite_note-CBC_With_Venezuela_Greenland-393] NBC News reported in mid-January 2026 that, according to senior American officials, Trump was "privately ramping up his focus on another target in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md), increasingly complaining to aides in recent weeks about Canada's vulnerability to US adversaries in the Arctic".[^cite_note-nbcnews_254552-394]

Trump's aggressive negotiation tactics have prompted comparisons with the [madman theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madman_theory). [James D. Political scientist Hilmar Mjelde called Trump's actions a "political [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) of historic proportions", and said he believes Trump has underestimated European steadfastness.[^cite_note-404] [Annie Genevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Genevard) said the EU has strong leverage and can retaliate in a trade war with the US, and that it could be "deadly for the United States", emphasising that Europeans will not let the US do as it pleases.[^cite_note-france24_20260118-405]

### US bases

In legal analysis of the crisis, some commentators suggested [sovereign bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_bases) as a solution,[^cite_note-Webber-406] similar to the United Kingdom's retention of [sovereign base areas in Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_base_areas_in_Cyprus), under which limited territory is held under foreign sovereignty primarily for military purposes.[^cite_note-ouplaw_978019923169-407] Commentators suggested that such a model could allow a foreign power to exercise sovereign control over specific installations in Greenland without a broader transfer of territory, though the proposal was described as legally complex and politically contentious.[^cite_note-Webber-406] Danish MP [Sascha Faxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sascha_Faxe) said giving up any sovereignty is completely out of the question, specifically stating that the Cyprus situation is not an option, whilst emphasising that military bases already have a significant degree of operational autonomy and jurisdiction under existing agreements, in line with what is normal for US bases abroad.[^cite_note-YouTube_GbxgtX49jO8-408] Both Danish and Greenland officials said sovereignty is not negotiable.[^cite_note-auto-154] On 26 January, Greenland's government said the idea of transferring "sovereignty" of any Greenlandic land, including bases, is not possible and described it as a "red line."[^cite_note-usatoday_88357944007-409]

### Age and mental health of Donald Trump

Further information: [Age and health concerns about Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_and_health_concerns_about_Donald_Trump)

Trump's behaviour drew commentary on his fitness for office. [Vin Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin_Gupta_\(pulmonologist\)), a US doctor who works as a medical analyst for NBC News, suggested that Trump's behaviour could be signs of early [Alzheimer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease) or [frontotemporal dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontotemporal_dementia). He said that [Trump's letter to Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Jonas) "crossed a line of proper adult behavior" and should have had a "more thorough public assessment of his neurological fitness."[^cite_note-aol_182106716-410] Trump also repeatedly confused Greenland with Iceland. Democratic lawmakers called for invoking the US [25th Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution).[^cite_note-aol-41]

### Mercator projection

Main article: [Mercator projection](20771.md)

Analysts noted that Trump's fixation on Greenland was likely fuelled by its appearance on the common Mercator projection of the globe, which greatly exaggerates the island's area relative to other landmasses. In 2021, Trump exclaimed, "I love maps. And I always said, 'Look at the size of this. It's massive, and that should be part of the United States.'"[^cite_note-AFR-411]

## Impact

### Domestic

The January 2026 escalation was cited by Danish prime minister [Mette Frederiksen](26040044.md) as a factor behind her decision to call [snap elections](72208286.md), stating that "We must define our relationship with the U.S. We must arm ourselves to ensure peace on our continent. We must keep Europe together".[^cite_note-Brooks_&_Grieshaber_2026-412] Opinion polls indicated that the crisis boosted the popularity of Frederiksen's party, the [Social Democrats](403131.md).[^cite_note-Brooks_&_Grieshaber_2026-412]

### Deterioration of Euro-American relations

#### Far-right politics

Many far right and [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) parties in Europe, including [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md), [National Rally](67119.md), [Sweden Democrats](65873.md),[^cite_note-413] [Brothers of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_of_Italy), and [Reform UK](59757202.md) were described as repulsed by Trump's threats against the sovereignty of a European country.[^cite_note-reuters_20260121-414][^cite_note-415][^cite_note-theguardian_20260127_trump-416] Italian prime minister [Giorgia Meloni](29228976.md) called Trump's statements "unacceptable" and said, "friendship requires respect." [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) called Trump's threats against Greenland a "very hostile act" against Europe.[^cite_note-cnn_20260130-417] According to a [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) poll from February 2026, the crisis also led to an increase of people in Western Europe with [an unfavourable opinion of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism#Europe).[^cite_note-i884-418]
