**Cyclonic Storm Ditwah**[^cite_note-1] was a weak but catastrophic [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) that brought heavy rain to [Sri Lanka](26750.md) and [Southern India](678583.md) in late November and early December 2025. The fourteenth [tropical depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_depression) and fourth cyclonic storm of the [2025 North Indian Ocean cyclone season](78605703.md), Ditwah originated from a well-marked low before steadily intensifying into a cyclonic storm and making landfall in Sri Lanka. Ditwah then moved off Sri Lanka and entered the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md), where it re-intensified and began to parallel the [Coromandel Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coromandel_Coast) of India. On 30 November, the system began to weaken due to increasingly unfavorable conditions. It lingered off to the coast of [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) and [Pondicherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pondicherry). It weakened into a deep depression later that day, and by 2 December, the system further weakened into a depression before becoming a remnant low on 3 December.

Most of the impact of Ditwah was concentrated in Sri Lanka. The storm caused heavy flooding and landslides, killing over 600 people and causing over [US\$](18717338.md)1.6 billion dollars of damage in the country. It was the deadliest natural disaster in the country since the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami).

At 18\:00 UTC on 26 November 2025, the [India Meteorological Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Meteorological_Department) (IMD) began to track a [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) formed from a well marked low just offshore the southeastern coast of Sri Lanka, due to favourable conditions such as high sea surface temperatures and mid-level vertical wind shear being low (10–15 knots).[^cite_note-2] The depression would intensify further, first into a deep depression at 00\:00 UTC on 27 November, then into a Cyclonic Storm at 06\:00 UTC, when it received the name Ditwah.[^cite_note-Regional_Specialised_Meteorological_Centre-3] Ditwah's clouds soon became increasingly organised into a curved band pattern.[^cite_note-Regional_Specialised_Meteorological_Centre-3]

Over the next few days, Ditwah crossed [Sri Lanka](26750.md) while maintaining cyclonic storm intensity, then moved into the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) on 29 November and headed northward. Ditwah slightly intensified once back over water, before beginning to weaken on 30 November due to increased [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear), inflow of dry air, and cooler sea-surface temperatures. Later that day, Ditwah weakened to a deep depression at 12\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] The system then continued to slowly parallel to the coast of Southern India as a Deep Depression into 1 December.[^cite_note-7] That evening at 18\:00 UTC, Ditwah made a turn and began to travel southwestwards. By 2 December, Ditwah had weakened to a depression.[^cite_note-8] Ditwah became a well-marked low pressure area on 3 December before making a landfall on the [North Tamil Nadu](29918.md) coast.[^cite_note-9]

### Effect of climate change

Further information: [Tropical cyclones and climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclones_and_climate_change)

In the aftermath of the storm, scientists at the World Weather Attribution group studied the storm to determine how much human-caused climate change influenced Cyclone Ditwah. They found that, around Sri Lanka, heavy precipitation events are "about 28% to 160% more intense" due to global warming. They further wrote that higher sea surface temperatures (SSTs) made more energy available for tropical storm development. "Without the trend related to the 1.3°C rise in global temperatures, the SSTs would have been about one degree colder and below the 1991-2020 normal."[^cite_note-10]

## Preparations

### Sri Lanka

The Sri Lankan government faced criticism over its response to advance warnings of Cyclone Ditwah. However, the exceptional scale of the disaster was also driven by extreme rainfall, widespread flooding and landslides. While the cyclone and its associated natural hazards could not have been prevented, subsequent assessments indicated that improved preparedness and early action could have mitigated the extent of the resulting damage and loss of life.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### India

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) issued red alerts for parts of north Tamil Nadu and Puducherry, warning of extremely heavy rainfall and strong winds as the system tracked along the coast.[^cite_note-13]

Chennai International Airport pre-emptively cancelled dozens of flights, and schools and colleges were closed in affected districts.[^cite_note-14]

The [National Disaster Response Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Disaster_Response_Force) (NDRF) deployed multiple teams across Tamil Nadu and issued port and marine advisories in coordination with state authorities.[^cite_note-15]

Flooding and landslides destroyed 6,200 homes and damaged 96,545 others nationwide.[^cite_note-Disaster_Management_Centre-2025-16] Military units rescued 69 people from a flooded bus in [Anuradhapura District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anuradhapura_District).[^cite_note-20] A landslide in [Gangoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangoda_\(Udunuwara_Divisional_Secretariat,_Kandy_District\)) buried 20 people.[^cite_note-adaderana-21] Three people were killed in [Sainthamaruthu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainthamaruthu), [Ampara District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampara_District) after their car was swept away by floodwaters.[^cite_note-22]

The Mavil Aru bund breach caused flooding across Trincomalee District, while train services in hill regions were suspended after mud and debris blocked tracks.[^cite_note-23] Cities along the [Kelani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelani_River) including [Kaduwela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaduwela,_Western_Province), [Kolonnawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolonnawa), and [Hanwella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwella) remained inundated for days after the storm.[^cite_note-Ada_Derana-2025-24]

Residents living near reservoirs were ordered to evacuate as water levels rose. The Disaster Management Centre issued advisories for communities downstream of the [Mavil Aru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavil_Aru) tank in [Trincomalee District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trincomalee_District), where the bund later breached in several places.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Power outages affected about 30 percent of the country, and major hydropower plants such as [Kotmale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotmale) and [Rantambe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rantembe_Dam) were temporarily shut down. An estimated 1.46 million people (407,594 families) were affected, with 59,000 families sheltered in 1,529 emergency centres.[^cite_note-Ada_Derana-2025-24]

Government officials estimated the economic loss to be [Rs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_rupee)1.03 trillion (US\$3.4 billion),[^cite_note-28] while the [World Bank](36752.md) estimated the cost to be slightly higher, amounted to Rs1.3 trillion (US\$4.1 billion).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### India

At least three people and 149 cattle were killed in rain-related incidents in Tamil Nadu, where Ditwah brought widespread flooding and agricultural losses. Two people died when walls collapsed in [Thoothukudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoothukudi) and [Thanjavur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanjavur), and one in [Mayiladuthurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayiladuthurai) from electrocution. About 57,000 hectares of farmland and 234 huts were damaged.[^cite_note-31] Preliminary losses in coastal Tamil Nadu were estimated at ₹3.5 billion (US\$40.6 million).[^cite_note-32] Heavy rain also affected parts of [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) and [Telangana](990267.md), the latter experiencing a brief cold spell as temperatures dropped after the storm.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

## Aftermath

Government offices and schools were closed as heavy rains intensified, with the Ministry of Education confirming temporary nationwide closures and authorising university administrations to decide on higher-education shutdowns.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The [Sri Lanka Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Armed_Forces) deployed more than 25,000 personnel for relief work. This included [SAR](172599.md) missions using the four operational [Bell 212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_212), [Bell 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_206) and [Mil Mi-17](1493374.md) helicopters in its fleet, resulting in the use of [Bell 412](3101264.md) used for VIP transport. The [Sri Lanka Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Air_Force) carried out surveillance operations using [KA-360ER](9023483.md) and airlifting dry rations, HADR equipments using [Y-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Y-12). [Sri Lanka Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Army) deployed its Unicorn/Uni Buffel vehicles, WMZ/BTR vehicles of the and the [Sri Lanka Navy](6186841.md) deployed boats.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

On 28 November, president [Anura Kumara Dissanayake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anura_Kumara_Dissanayake) declared a nationwide [state of emergency](298608.md) to expedite disaster-response coordination and resource mobilisation.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

While conducting disaster relief operations, a Sri Lanka Air Force Bell 212 helicopter crashed in the Wennappuwa area, killing the pilot and injuring four other Air Force personnel.[^cite_note-44] Five Sri Lanka Navy personnel died while conducting flood-mitigation operations at [Chalai Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalai_Lagoon) near Chundikkulam.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

[SpaceX](832774.md) satellite internet service [Starlink](45111627.md) announced that it would provide free satellite internet to new and existing customers in areas of Sri Lanka and Indonesia affected by flooding from Cyclone Ditwah through the end of December 2025, and stated that it was working with the Sri Lankan government to support restoration of connectivity in badly hit districts.[^cite_note-StarlinkFlood-47][^cite_note-DailyMirror-StarlinkDitwah-48]

### Operation Sagar Bandhu (India)









Indian Air Force Il-76MD and C-130J aircraft preparing for relief operations in Sri Lanka

On 28 November, India launched Operation Sagar Bandhu, a [Humanitarian Assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_Assistance) and [Disaster Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_Relief) (HADR) mission to support Sri Lanka following the devastating Cyclone Ditwah.[^cite_note-\:3-57]

- On the first day itself, the [Indian Navy](604522.md) mobilised its [aircraft carrier](2219.md), [INS *Vikrant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Vikrant_\(2013\)), and [frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_frigate), [INS *Udaygiri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Udaygiri_\(2022\)), which were already in [Port of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Colombo) for the International Fleet Review 2025.

  [^cite_note-86]

  [^cite_note-87]

  &#x20;Two ship-borne [Chetak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_Chetak) helicopters

  [^cite_note-\:3-57]

  &#x20;were also deployed for [aerial reconnaissance](20338498.md) during [search & rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_&_rescue).

  [^cite_note-\:0-88]

  &#x20;The ships delivered 9.5 tons of emergency dry rations.

  [^cite_note-\:3-57]

- On 29 November, at 10 am local time, a [C-130J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130J) aircraft of the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) supplied 12 tonnes of materials.

  [^cite_note-89]

  &#x20;Later, an [Il-76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-76) aircraft transported further 9 tonnes of relief material with a complement of over 80 personnel from the [National Disaster Response Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Disaster_Response_Force) (NDRF) with four search-and-rescue dogs, and 8 tonnes of specialised equipment.

  [^cite_note-90]

  [^cite_note-91]

## Records and statistics

United Nations agencies and the IFRC characterised the flooding and landslides associated with Ditwah as among the worst disasters to affect Sri Lanka in recent decades, with between one and 1.5 million people impacted across the country. The catastrophe prompted large-scale evacuations, the opening of more than 1,500 emergency shelters, and multiple rounds of international appeals for relief and early recovery support.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]
