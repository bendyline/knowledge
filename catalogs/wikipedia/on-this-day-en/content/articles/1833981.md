The **200 metres**, or **200-meter dash**, is a [sprint](89276.md) running event. On an outdoor 400-metre racetrack, the race begins on the curve and ends on the [home straight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_straight), so a combination of techniques is needed to successfully run the race. A slightly shorter race, called the *[stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_\(running_race\))* and run on a straight track, was the first recorded event at the [ancient Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Olympic_Games). The 200 m places more emphasis on speed endurance than shorter sprint distances as athletes predominantly rely on [anaerobic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_energy) system during the 200 m sprint. Similarly to other sprint distances, the 200 m begins from the [starting blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_blocks). When the sprinters adopt the 'set' position in the blocks they are able to adopt a more efficient starting posture and [isometrically preload](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_exercise#Isometric_presses_as_preparation_for_explosive_power_movements) their muscles. This enables them to stride forwards more powerfully when the race begins and start faster.

In the United States and elsewhere, athletes previously ran the 220-yard dash (201.168 m) instead of the 200 m (218.723 yards), though the distance is now obsolete. The standard adjustment used for the conversion from times recorded over 220 yards to 200 m times is to subtract 0.1 seconds,[^cite_note-1] but other conversion methods exist. Another obsolete version of this race is the [200 metres straight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/200_metres_straight), which was run on tracks that contained such a straight. Initially, when the International Amateur Athletic Association (now known as the [International Association of Athletics Federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Athletics_Federations)) started to ratify world records in 1912, only records set on a straight track were eligible for consideration. In 1951, the IAAF started to recognise records set on a curved track. In 1976, the straight record was discarded.

The event has been on the [Olympic](27566.md) [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_\(sport\)) programme since [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics) for men and since [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Summer_Olympics) for women. The race attracts runners from other events, primarily the [100 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_metres), wishing to double up and claim both titles. This feat has been achieved by men eleven times at the [Olympic Games](22576.md): by [Archie Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archie_Hahn_\(athlete\)) in 1904, [Ralph Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Craig) in 1912, [Percy Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Williams_\(sprinter\)) in 1928, [Eddie Tolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Tolan) in 1932, [Jesse Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens) in 1936, [Bobby Morrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Morrow) in 1956, [Valeriy Borzov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Borzov) in 1972, [Carl Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Lewis) in 1984, and most recently by Jamaica's [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) in 2008, 2012, and 2016. The double has been accomplished by women eight times: by [Fanny Blankers-Koen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Blankers-Koen) in 1948, [Marjorie Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Jackson) in 1952, [Betty Cuthbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Cuthbert) in 1956, [Wilma Rudolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilma_Rudolph) in 1960, [Renate Stecher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Stecher) in 1972, [Florence Griffith Joyner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Griffith_Joyner) in 1988, and [Elaine Thompson-Herah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Thompson-Herah) in 2016 and 2021. [Marion Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Jones) finished first in both races in 2000 but was later disqualified and stripped of her medals after admitting to taking [performance-enhancing drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance-enhancing_drugs). An Olympic double of 200 m and [400 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/400_meters) was first achieved by [Valerie Brisco-Hooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Brisco-Hooks) in 1984, and later by [Michael Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnson_\(sprinter\)) from the United States and [Marie-José Pérec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Jos%C3%A9_P%C3%A9rec) of France both in 1996. [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) is the only man to repeat as Olympic champion, [Bärbel Wöckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A4rbel_W%C3%B6ckel) (née Eckert), [Veronica Campbell-Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Campbell-Brown) and [Elaine Thompson-Herah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Thompson-Herah) are the three women who have repeated as Olympic champion.

The men's world record holder is [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) of Jamaica, who ran 19.19 seconds at the [2009 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_IAAF_World_Championships_in_Athletics). The women's world record holder is [Florence Griffith Joyner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Griffith_Joyner) of the United States, who ran 21.34 seconds at the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics). The reigning Olympic champions are [Letsile Tebogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letsile_Tebogo) (BOT) and [Gabby Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabby_Thomas) (USA). The reigning World Champions are [Noah Lyles](51135146.md) (USA) and [Melissa Jefferson-Wooden](71317804.md) (USA).

Races run with an aiding wind measured over 2.0 metres per second are not acceptable for record purposes.
