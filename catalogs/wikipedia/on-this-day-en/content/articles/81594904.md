The **Epstein Files Transparency Act** (**EFTA**) is a [law](27552742.md) passed by the [119th United States Congress](70645797.md) and signed by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) on November 19, 2025. It requires the [U.S. attorney general](44002.md) to "make publicly available in a searchable and downloadable format" all files (the "[Epstein files](80415168.md)") pertaining to the prosecution of the deceased child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) (if needed, declassifying them to the extent possible) within 30 days of passage, and then to give the Judiciary Committees in both the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md) and the [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) an unredacted "list of all government officials and politically exposed persons" named in the files.

In September 2025, Representative [Thomas Massie](28005891.md), a member of the [Republican Party](32070.md), filed a [discharge petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_petition) in support of the bill. On November 12, the discharge petition received the minimum-required 218 signatures needed, from 4 Republican[^cite_note-1] and 214 [Democratic Party](5043544.md) representatives, forcing a House vote on the bill. The House of Representatives voted 427–1 to pass the act on November 18, 2025, with Republican representative [Clay Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Higgins) of Louisiana casting the lone nay vote.[^cite_note-2] The next day, the Senate passed the bill via [unanimous consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unanimous_consent), and Trump signed the bill into law.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-presidentialdeskarrival-4][^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-Lebowitz-6] The law gave the attorney general 30 days to release the documents.[^cite_note-7]

On December 19, the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) released the first batch of Epstein files, violating U.S. law in failing to release all the files by that day.[^cite_note-Betts-8] This failure received bipartisan criticism.[^cite_note-Violation-9][^cite_note-10] Many documents contained extensive [redactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redaction), with hundreds of pages entirely blacked out.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Following a delayed and heavily criticized rollout,[^cite_note-Greene_182026-13] the Department of Justice released additional files in waves,[^cite_note-Betts-8] with a fifth release on January 30, 2026. Following this, the department claimed that it had fulfilled its legal obligations and released all available files, amounting to over 3.5 million pages.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-reportedlegalobligationcompliance2-15] This announcement received pushback, with some reports indicating that the full Epstein files consist of over 6 million pages.[^cite_note-epsteinfiles6millions2-16] A sixth release was published on March 5, 2026. Following this, about 50,000 previously removed files were restored, after review from the DOJ and [FBI](11127.md).[^cite_note-Epstein_NPR-17][^cite_note-18] In July 2026, Massie introduced the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, in response to the DOJ's actions.[^cite_note-Act_II-19]

The publication of the Epstein files had widespread political and cultural effects. In the months that followed, President Trump fired Attorney General [Pam Bondi](4550623.md)[^cite_note-Fired-20] and supported a successful [primary challenge against Massie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Kentucky#District_4).[^cite_note-Gallrein-21] Other effects included the opening of new political investigations, the resignations of over a dozen prominent public figures and politicians, reforms to the [House of Lords](13658.md), the arrests of [Peter Mandelson](216970.md) and [Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor](125267.md), and [increased backlash](83157483.md#Peter_Mandelson_appointment_scandal) against the [Starmer ministry](77280613.md) which later led to [Starmer's resignation](18681491.md#2026_leadership_crisis_and_resignation).[^cite_note-22] Among the American public, publication of the Epstein files fueled [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories), misinformation and disinformation,[^cite_note-23] [AI-generated](73291755.md) hoaxes,[^cite_note-24] [anti-elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-elitism) sentiment, [anti-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_the_United_States_government) sentiment,[^cite_note-25] [anti-Israel](30074296.md) sentiment,[^cite_note-Israel-26] [anti-Trump](49760486.md) sentiment, and [anti-Trump conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Trump).[^cite_note-27]

## Background

Main article: [Epstein files](80415168.md)



Mug shot of [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md), 2019

During the [2024 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_U.S._presidential_election), the [Republican](32070.md) nominee and former president [Donald Trump](4848272.md), when asked by [Rachel Campos-Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Campos-Duffy) on [Fox News](11121.md) in June 2024 whether he would release the Epstein files as president, answered "yeah, I would."[^cite_note-28] In September, while appearing on [Lex Fridman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Fridman)'s [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast), Trump promised to release the Epstein files if elected.[^cite_note-29] Trump won the 2024 election in November. In February 2025, the U.S. attorney general [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) stated in a Fox News interview that the Epstein client list was "sitting on my desk right now to review".[^cite_note-NPR-30] Later that month, the [White House](33057.md) gave binders to several prominent [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) and right-wing figures, including [Liz Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Wheeler) and [Scott Presler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Presler), reading "Epstein Files: Phase 1" and "Declassified". Later that day, [Representative](19468510.md) [Anna Paulina Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Paulina_Luna) criticized the event, stating that they released "old info".[^cite_note-NPR-30]

On July 7, 2025, Bondi released a statement saying that the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) (DOJ) found "no incriminating 'client list'", contradicting her February statement. That day, DOJ and the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI) announced that no further files from the Epstein investigation would be released.[^cite_note-NPR-30] Bondi's statement drew outrage from conservative figures; Wheeler called it "unforgivable behavior", while the [Hodgetwins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgetwins) called for Trump to fire Bondi.[^cite_note-31] On July 11, reports emerged that [Dan Bongino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bongino), the [deputy director of the FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_director_of_the_FBI), had a heated confrontation with Bondi over her handling of the files, and was considering resigning.[^cite_note-32] Bongino had reportedly yelled at Bondi, shouting "You fucked this thing up from the start"; Bongino and Patel both told the White House that Bondi needed to resign.[^cite_note-Freakout-33] Bongino reportedly meet with [Susie Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Wiles), and told her that the Epstein affair was about to become "Trump's [Iran–Contra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair)"; he was persuaded to halt his resignation by White House officials.[^cite_note-Freakout-33] Bongino was later demoted to Co-Deputy Director on September 15, 2025;[^cite_note-34] he later announced his upcoming resignation on December 17, 2025.[^cite_note-35] On July 12, Trump defended Bondi and referred to Epstein as "somebody that nobody cares about."

On July 16, 2025, Trump referred to the Epstein files as "a big hoax."[^cite_note-a920-36] On July 23, reports emerged that earlier in May, Bondi and her [deputy](2933681.md) [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) informed Trump that his name was "among many in the Epstein files." Trump denied this report.[^cite_note-NPR-30] Trump's change in position drew criticism from prominent podcasters close to Trump, as well as many conservatives. [Joe Rogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Rogan) accused the Trump administration of [gaslighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaslighting) the public, while [Andrew Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Schulz) said Trump was "insulting our intelligence".[^cite_note-37] In August 2025, Judge [Paul A. Engelmayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_A._Engelmayer) denied Bondi's request to unseal grand jury files from the [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md) prosecution case.[^cite_note-NPR-30]

### White House internal discussions



2003 [Birthday letter to Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book) attributed to Trump (text redacted due to potential copyright concerns)

According to *[The New York Times](30680.md)* (in content from *[Regime Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regime_Change_\(book\))*), on July 17, 2025, President Trump and his top officials met in the [Situation Room](925990.md) to discuss how to best handle the potential release of the Epstein files.[^cite_note-Freakout-33] With Trump's previous efforts to kill the release of the files failing, the administration now faced an impending article in *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, which would be published online during their meeting, which revealed the existence of [Jeffrey Epstein's birthday book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book) and Trump's letter in it.[^cite_note-38] Trump had attempted to kill the article by contacting [Robert Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Thomson_\(executive\)), [Rupert Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch), and [Emma Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Tucker), but was unsuccessful.[^cite_note-Freakout-33]

In attendance were President Trump, Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md), [Susie Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Wiles), [David Warrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Warrington), [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md), [Taylor Budowich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Budowich), [Steven Cheung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Cheung), [Todd Blanche](74006584.md), [Stanley Woodward Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Woodward_Jr.), and [James Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blair_\(political_advisor\)); while [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) and [Kash Patel](62202727.md) joined the meeting on speakerphone.[^cite_note-Freakout-33] According to *The New York Times*, Trump's team was divided on how to best handle the release of the files. Vice President Vance pushed for transparency, arguing for a quick and full release of the Epstein files, as well as a subsequent congressional investigation. Vance also suggested enlisting commentator [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson) to interview [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md), suggesting Maxwell could clear Trump's name. However, Vance's recommendations were opposed down by Wiles, who labeled him a [conspiracy theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory), as well as Blanche and Blair.[^cite_note-Freakout-33]

Blanche conversely suggested two possible courses of action. The first would be to petition to unseal grand jury testimonies, in hopes of shifting blame for concealing Epstein material onto the judges, who are very likely to deny such a petition. The second would be to publicly release a transcript of Maxwell's testimony.[^cite_note-Freakout-33] The idea of having Maxwell testify before Congress was also brought up, but Cheung and Blair noted that pardoning Maxwell was off the table, due to how it would be publicly perceived.[^cite_note-Freakout-33]

## Legislative history

### Discharge petition

On September 2, 2025, (the first day the House was back in session after the August recess) Representative [Thomas Massie](28005891.md) moved to force the House to vote to require the Justice Department to release the files, through a [discharge petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_petition).[^cite_note-39] Trump and other Republicans launched a pressure campaign to prevent the release of the files, with one anonymous official calling voting for Massie's discharge petition a "very hostile act to the administration".[^cite_note-40]

Within several days, [Nancy Mace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Mace), [Lauren Boebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Boebert), and [Marjorie Taylor Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Taylor_Greene) signed the discharge petition, alongside many Democrats.[^cite_note-41] Later that month, Greene [tweeted](68533868.md): "The Epstein rape and pedophile network must be exposed. ... Release all the Epstein information by any means possible." She added that "if something happens to me, I ask you all to find out" who might be trying "to stop the information from coming out."[^cite_note-42]

On September 9, [the House Rules Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Rules) rejected a motion 8–4, from Democratic representative [Jim McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_McGovern_\(American_politician\)), to request to vote on the Epstein Files Transparency Act in the House, along party lines.[^cite_note-43] The petition's final two signatures came from [Democrats](5043544.md): [James Walkinshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Walkinshaw), who won the [Virginia 11th District special election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Virginia's_11th_congressional_district_special_election) on September 9[^cite_note-44] and was sworn in the next day, and [Adelita Grijalva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelita_Grijalva), who won the [Arizona 7th congressional district special election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Arizona's_7th_congressional_district_special_election) on September 23[^cite_note-45] and was sworn in on November 12.[^cite_note-46]

In the hours before Grijalva was sworn in, Bondi, Blanche, and [FBI director](5861065.md) [Kash Patel](62202727.md) met with Boebert regarding the House effort. However, Boebert did not remove her name from the petition, and once Grijalva was sworn in and provided the 218th signature, it was no longer procedurally allowable for any signatories to remove their names.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

[Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson), the [speaker of the House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives), had delayed Grijalva's swearing-in, generally attributing his decision to the [2025 government shutdown](81225965.md), arguing that the House could not swear in new members during pro forma sessions or during a government shutdown; however, some critics noted that Johnson had sworn in two Republican members, [Jimmy Patronis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Patronis) and [Randy Fine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Fine), during a pro forma session in April.[^cite_note-50] On October 21, the [Arizona attorney general](4316853.md), [Kris Mayes](13233868.md), sued Johnson, seeking to force him to swear in Grijalva.[^cite_note-51] That day, Johnson told [Fox Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Business) that the delay "has zero to do with Epstein", given that "the Epstein files are being released" (seemingly referring to the [House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Oversight_Committee)'s releases).[^cite_note-52]

### Interval period

On November 12, 2025, Johnson stated that the House would vote on the bill the following week (November 16–22).[^cite_note-Grijalva-53] In the following days, Republican representatives [Don Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bacon),[^cite_note-Defect-54] [Andy Biggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Biggs),[^cite_note-Biggs-55] [Rob Bresnahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Bresnahan),[^cite_note-Defect-54] [Tim Burchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Burchett),[^cite_note-Defect-54] [Eli Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Crane),[^cite_note-56] [Warren Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Davidson),[^cite_note-Miller-57] [Carlos A. Giménez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_A._Gim%C3%A9nez),[^cite_note-Carlos-58] [Nicole Malliotakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Malliotakis),[^cite_note-Carlos-58] [Max Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Miller_\(politician\)),[^cite_note-Miller-57] and [Derrick Van Orden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Van_Orden),[^cite_note-Carlos-58] none of whom signed the discharge petition, stated that they will vote in favor of the legislation. Representative [María Elvira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Elvira_Salazar) stated when asked that she was still "thinking" over her vote.[^cite_note-59] *[Politico](9172097.md)* reported that, according to anonymous sources, over 100 Republicans were expected to defect from Johnson and vote for the bill.[^cite_note-Defect-54] Representative [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna) predicted that 40–50 Republicans might vote for release, while Massie similarly anticipated that Republican support could "snowball",[^cite_note-60] later predicting a deluge of "100 or more" Republican votes.[^cite_note-61]

On November 15, Trump ordered the DOJ to investigate Epstein's involvement with banks and prominent Democratic figures, including [Bill Clinton](3356.md), [Lawrence Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Summers), and [Reid Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reid_Hoffman). On [Truth Social](69077351.md), Trump wrote: "This is another Russia, Russia, Russia Scam, with all arrows pointing to the Democrats". Bondi subsequently assigned the investigation to U.S. attorney [Jay Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Clayton_\(attorney\)).[^cite_note-62] On November 16, Massie commented on the DOJ's new investigation ordered by Trump, saying:

> The president's been saying this is a hoax. He's been saying that for months. Well, he's just now decided to investigate a hoax, if it's a hoax ... I have another concern about these investigations ... If they have ongoing investigations in certain areas, those documents can't be released. So, this might be a big smoke screen, these investigations, to open a bunch of them ... as a last-ditch effort to prevent the release of the Epstein files.[^cite_note-Block-63]

Massie also added that he does not believe Trump himself is implicated in the files, but "instead is trying to protect a bunch of rich and powerful friends, billionaires, donors to his campaign, friends in his social circles".[^cite_note-Block-63] Several hours later,[^cite_note-Block-63] Trump posted on Truth Social, reversing his stance, writing that "House Republicans should vote to release the Epstein files ... it's time to move on from this Democrat Hoax".[^cite_note-64] Representative [Robert Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garcia_\(California_congressman\)) commented on Trump's reversal, arguing Trump was "panicking ... he is about to lose this Epstein vote to force the Department of Justice to release the files ... Trump has the power to release all the files today ... instead, he wants to continue this cover-up and launch bogus new investigations to deflect and slow down our investigation."[^cite_note-65] Massie also responded, commenting that Trump "got tired of me winning."[^cite_note-66]

On November 17, Trump said that he would sign the bill, but did not want it to "take it away from us". Massie responded to Trump on Twitter, writing: "Looking forward to attending this bill signing."[^cite_note-67] That day, Johnson also stated that he might support it, if it could be improved in the Senate to better protect victims' identities.[^cite_note-68] Additionally, on that day, [Mark Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Epstein_\(property_developer\)), the brother of Epstein, claimed there was an active coverup to "sanitize" the files by "scrubbing the files to take Republican names out." Mark Epstein claimed he had heard as such from a "pretty good source" and it was the reason for Trump's sudden shift on releasing the files.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] On November 18, Massie, Khanna, and Greene hosted a press conference at [Capitol Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Hill) alongside Epstein abuse survivors.[^cite_note-72]

### House vote



The House of Representatives voting 427–1 to approve the Epstein Files Transparency Act. [Clay Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Higgins) ([R](32070.md)-[LA](18130.md)-[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana's_3rd_congressional_district)) was the sole representative to vote against it.

The U.S. House of Representatives voted on the act on November 18, 2025, just before 3 p.m. [Eastern Time](496028.md). The bill was considered by the House "[under a suspension of rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_of_the_rules_in_the_United_States_Congress)", meaning it required a two-thirds majority (290) vote to succeed.[^cite_note-73] The vote passed 427–1. ### Senate vote

After passing the House, the bill would require 60 votes to pass in the [U.S. Senate](24909346.md).[^cite_note-77] That night, the Senate unanimously agreed to pass the bill as soon as it was received from the House.[^cite_note-Senate-78] The bill arrived in the Senate on November 19 and was formally transmitted from the Senate to the President's desk that morning.[^cite_note-79]

### Signing

Trump stated on November 17, 2025, that he would sign the Epstein Files Transparency Act into law if it reached his desk.[^cite_note-80] House speaker Mike Johnson, who had previously expressed staunch opposition to the release of the files, said on November 18: "I am deeply disappointed in this outcome. ... It needed amendments, I just spoke to the president about that. We'll see what happens."[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] A [presidential veto](74661.md) did not occur, but it could have been overridden had both chambers voted by a two-thirds majority, which they had easily reached in the previous votes on the act.[^cite_note-83] The act reached the presidential desk on the morning of November 19.[^cite_note-\:0-5] A senior White House official had told reporters that "the bill will be signed whenever it gets to the White House". In the evening, the White House told reporters that Trump would not appear on camera for the rest of the day,[^cite_note-84] after which Trump announced on [Truth Social](69077351.md) that he had signed the bill into law.[^cite_note-Lebowitz-6]

### FBI documents and redaction

As of November 2025, reports indicated that the Department of Justice's unreleased Epstein documents consisted of nearly 100,000 pages, as well as 40 computers, 70 CDs, 26 storage drives, and six recording devices, which contain a collective 300 [GB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabyte) of data.[^cite_note-Republic-91] According to *[The New Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Republic)*, physical evidence in the FBI's possession also includes "photographs, travel logs, employee lists...blueprints of [Epstein's island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Saint_James) and Manhattan home...a logbook of visitors to Epstein's private island, and a list \[of names]".[^cite_note-Republic-91]

On November 25, [Jason Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Leopold) of *[Bloomberg News](3057255.md)* began to report on the Epstein Files, after partially receiving redacted copies from the FBI; Leopold filed a [FOIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_\(United_States\)) request and a lawsuit against the FBI for the files. Leopold received a variety of files, mostly emails, which also revealed the existence of the "Special Redaction Project" (also internally referred to as the "Epstein Transparency Project").[^cite_note-Leopold-92] Leopold claimed that between January 2025 and July 2025, FBI personnel worked 4,737 overtime hours, costing the U.S. government over \$851,000, to redact files related to the Epstein investigation.[^cite_note-Leopold-92] On December 18, [CNN](62028.md) reported that the Department of Justice was racing to quickly redact documents over the past several weeks, ahead of the December 19 deadline. The article reported that each attorney associated with the case was processing up to 1,000+ documents per week, and that "Counterintelligence specialists were asked to drop nearly all of their other work to process the Epstein documents."[^cite_note-93]

Journalist [Katie Phang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Phang) also sued the attorney general over the redactions. A federal judge gave Todd Blanche until July 2, 2026 to "unredact certain emails, release additional FBI interviews and produce Justice Department documents with potential co-conspirator names unredacted, or show cause for why he cannot do so." He must also, as the law already requires, write in the Federal Register to justify each redaction, for which he was six months late.[^cite_note-94]

## First release – December 19, 2025

According to the text of the law, the Department of Justice was legally required to release the Epstein files by December 19, 2025. The law also provided that no record may be withheld, delayed, or redacted on the basis of embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity, including to any government official, public figure, or foreign dignitary.[^cite_note-95] However, the law did not establish a penalty for noncompliance. On December 19, Deputy Attorney General [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) told *[Fox & Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_&_Friends)* there would only be a partial release of files that day. Blanche said the Department of Justice would release "today, several hundred thousand \[files], and then over the next couple weeks, I expect several hundred thousand more."[^cite_note-96]

Blanche's announcement received bipartisan criticism from politicians, and was viewed as a violation of U.S. law.[^cite_note-Violation-9] Republican politicians including Massie and [Marjorie Taylor Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Taylor_Greene) attacked the staggered rollout of the files.[^cite_note-97] Massie responded to Blanche's remarks with a photograph of the law's text on [X](9988187.md), highlighting the requirement that all files be released within 30 days;[^cite_note-Betts-8] Senator [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul) described the partial release as a "big mistake."[^cite_note-98] Democratic politicians including [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer),[^cite_note-Betts-8] [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna),[^cite_note-Fox-99] [Jamie Raskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Raskin), and [Robert Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garcia_\(California_congressman\)) all condemned Blanche's announcement, and threatened legal action over the decision.[^cite_note-100] Legal scholar [Ryan Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Goodman) also responded, writing on X that "This is a violation of the Epstein Files Transparency Act."[^cite_note-Betts-8]

Democratic representative [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez) wrote on X that "Everyone involved will have to answer for this. Pam Bondi, Kash Patel, whole admin. Protecting a bunch of rapists and pedophiles because they have money, power, and connections. Bondi should resign tonight." Khanna stated that he was considering bringing articles of [impeachment](1795376.md) against Bondi,[^cite_note-101] while Massie also suggested that Bondi could be charged with [obstruction of justice](240718.md), over her failure to release every file by December 19, 2025.[^cite_note-102] According to [CBS News](359551.md), the Epstein files released on December 19 total 3,965 files, at 3 GB of data.[^cite_note-103] A website called [The Epstein Library](https://www.justice.gov/epstein) went live to the public, including thousands of PDFs. The site seemed to contain a search function, though *[HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)* noted it was not immediately working.[^cite_note-104]

Additionally, a released document contained an unverified FBI tip, from an unnamed woman in [New Mexico](21649.md). *Axios* reported on December 24 that there were 700,000 files remaining which were expected to be released by December 30.[^cite_note-134] Later that day, other outlets reported the Justice Department had yet to internally review and redact over a million files and said it "may take a few more weeks" to release them[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] as it sought U.S. attorneys to volunteer over Christmas to remotely do this work.[^cite_note-137] On December 30, *The New York Times* reported that the Department of Justice was reviewing as many as 5.2 million files.[^cite_note-138] On January 5, the Department of Justice said in a court filing that it had reviewed "12,285 documents (comprising 125,575 pages)" but had yet to review at least 2 million documents.[^cite_note-139] The letter also referenced an additional report, which separately suggested more than 5 million documents could be under review.[^cite_note-Sarah-140]

In early January 2026, less than 1% of the files were publicly released according to a DOJ letter sent to U.S. District Judge [Paul A. Engelmayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_A._Engelmayer).[^cite_note-Greene_182026-13] On January 8, 2026, Khanna and Massie wrote to Judge Engelmayer, "to suggest the appointment of a Special Master and Independent Monitor" so as to force the Justice Department to produce the remaining files.[^cite_note-Sarah-140][^cite_note-141] On January 16, Bondi and Blanche filed a six-page letter to Engelmayer, asking him to deny the Special Master request.[^cite_note-142] On January 21, Engelmayer denied the special master request.[^cite_note-143]

### Aftermath

#### Congressional access to unredacted files

On February 6, 2026, news outlets reported that Assistant Attorney General [Patrick Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Department_of_Justice_appointments_by_Donald_Trump#Appointments_\(second_administration\)) wrote a letter to all 535 members of Congress. Davis stated that beginning February 9, lawmakers would be allowed to view unredacted Epstein files, in a reading room at the Department of Justice. Congressional lawmakers are permitted to take notes on the files, but may not bring electronic devices with them.[^cite_note-188] On February 8, Massie asked users on X to identify important redacted documents that he should view first.[^cite_note-189]

On February 9, 2026, Massie and Khanna spoke to the press after their first reviewing of unredacted files at the Department of Justice. The pair criticized Bondi and the Department of Justice, accusing the latter of "breaking the law".[^cite_note-190] On February 10, 2026, Khanna read the names of six men on the House floor, who appeared in the unredacted Epstein files. Khanna said that the six men are "likely incriminated" by their inclusion. By reading the names during a House session, Khanna and Massie are offered some degree of protection against potential defamation lawsuits.[^cite_note-191] The six names[^cite_note-192] included [Leslie Wexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Wexner), [Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem](7424329.md), Salvatore Nuara, Zurab Mikeladze, Leonic Leonov, and [Nicola Caputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Caputo). On February 13, [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) claimed in response to Khanna that the latter named "completely random people" that "have NOTHING to do with Epstein or Maxwell". Blanche stated that only Wexner and bin Sulayem held ties to Epstein, while the other four were an "unmasking of completely random people selected years ago for an FBI lineup- men and women."[^cite_note-Lineup-193] Khanna subsequently admitted his mistake, while placing blame on the Department of Justice. Khanna stated, "I wish DoJ had provided that explanation earlier instead of redacting then unredacting their names. They have failed to protect survivors, created confusion for innocent men, and have protected rich and powerful abusers."[^cite_note-194] Massie also criticized Blanche, saying "Maybe you should have checked with your folks first, or provided some context, instead of trying to beat my TV appearance and then blaming us."[^cite_note-Lineup-193]

On February 9, 2026, Senator [Cynthia Lummis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Lummis) said that after reviewing unredacted files, "now I see what the big deal is. And the members of Congress that have been pushing this were not wrong."[^cite_note-195] That day, Representative [Jamie Raskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Raskin) also accused the Department of Justice of a coverup, after viewing some unredacted files.[^cite_note-196] Raskin also claimed "\[Trump’s] name, I think I put his name ... it appears more than a million times."[^cite_note-197] On February 10, 2026, Representative [Suhas Subramanyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhas_Subramanyam) gave an interview to [NPR](29697232.md), where he discussed viewing unredacted files.[^cite_note-198] That day, Representative [Jared Moskowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Moskowitz) described the content of the files as "just gross". Moskowitz also stated that a list of Epstein's co-conspirators "would surprise (the public), because a lot of them were women".[^cite_note-199] That day, House Speaker [Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson) stated that he intended to view unredacted files at a later date, while also defending the Department of Justice.[^cite_note-200] On February 11, 2026, Representative [Maxwell Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Frost) viewed unredacted documents, after asking [Reddit](3829005.md) users to help identify ones of interest. Frost stated that he only "scratched the tip of the iceberg", but that a "lot of these did relate to Donald Trump."[^cite_note-201]

#### Congressional hearings

Following the fifth file release, [Howard Lutnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Lutnick) and [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) both testified before U.S. congress. Lutnick was questioned on his ties to Epstein, which he defended as limited.[^cite_note-202] During Bondi's hearing, she faced bipartisan criticism over the Department of Justice's release of the files. Massie described the issue as "bigger than [Watergate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watergate_scandal)".[^cite_note-Fiery-203]

During the hearing, Bondi was also photographed with a document titled "[Jayapal Pramila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pramila_Jayapal) Search History". Congressional members expressed bipartisan outrage over the incident, accusing Bondi and the Department of Justice of spying on their search history of unredacted Epstein files. Representative Pramila Jayapal described it as "outrageous", while Representative Raskin called it an "outrageous abuse of power". Representative [Nancy Mace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Mace) called the document "disturbing...a form of intimidation, potentially", while House Speaker Johnson described it as inappropriate.[^cite_note-204] The department subsequently acknowledged the incident, stating that the "DOJ logs all searches made on its systems to protect against the release of victim information."[^cite_note-205] On February 13, 2026, House Democrats launched an investigation into the department, over the tracking of congressional file searches.[^cite_note-206]

#### Section 3 report to Congress

On February 14, 2026, Bondi sent a six-page letter to congress, outlining the department's justification for redactions made in the Epstein files (as legally required).[^cite_note-207] In the letter, Bondi also included a list of "all persons...\[named] in the files released under the Act at least once...in a wide variety of contexts."[^cite_note-Section_3-208] The list included both individuals with direct ties to Epstein, as well as individuals mentioned only in passing once.[^cite_note-Section_3-208] The list included approximately 340 names.

Following the release of Bondi's report, Representative Khanna accused Bondi of "purposefully muddying the waters on who was a predator and who was mentioned in an email." On [X](9988187.md), Khanna wrote, "To have [Janis Joplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janis_Joplin), who died when Epstein was 17, in the same list as [Larry Nassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Nassar), who went to prison for the sexual abuse of hundreds of young women and child pornography, with no clarification of how either was mentioned in the files is absurd."[^cite_note-Section_3-208] Representative Mace attacked the report for "missing names", and wrote on X that "This isn’t going away until people go to jail."[^cite_note-209]

#### Bondi subpoena

On March 4, 2026, the House Oversight Committee voted to subpoena Bondi to testify about how the Justice Department handled the investigation of Epstein and the release of the files.[^cite_note-210] The subpoena was issued on March 17; she was scheduled to sit for a deposition on April 14.[^cite_note-211] However, Trump announced on April 2 that he had fired Bondi,[^cite_note-212] after which the Justice Department said she would not appear for the deposition.[^cite_note-213] She was rescheduled for a transcribed interview on May 29 instead of a deposition.[^cite_note-214]

On May 29, 2026, Bondi appeared before the House Oversight Committee, and reportedly blamed [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) for the chaotic rollout of the Epstein files.[^cite_note-Bondi_May-215] Representative [Robert Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garcia_\(California_congressman\)) told reporters:

> "She continues to push all of the investigation and the blame on Acting AG Todd Blanche..It was Todd Blanche, the current acting AG, that was leading the Epstein investigation, and quite frankly, all of the mistakes that we saw — the redactions, not protecting survivors — \[Bondi] continues to push that back onto the acting AG Todd Blanche, who by the way was Donald Trump's personal lawyer."[^cite_note-Bondi_May-215]

Following the testimony, Bondi denied blaming Blanche, writing that "I praised Acting AG Blanche’s management of this [Herculean task](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labours_of_Hercules)".[^cite_note-Bondi_May-215] An 111-page transcript of Bondi's testimony was later released to the public on June 4, 2026.[^cite_note-216]

### United States

#### Anti-Israel sentiment

The release of the Epstein files led to Anti-Israel sentiment among the American public, due in part to Epstein's ties with [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) and pro-Israel groups. Within the Epstein files, an October 2020 Los Angeles-FBI report said that a bureau source had become “convinced that Epstein was a co-opted Mossad agent".[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243] Additionally, in 2025, media sources reported that Epstein also claimed to have influenced the [2019 Israeli election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election), writing to [Steve Bannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bannon) that he helped convince Barak to form the [Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Union_\(Israel\)) alliance.[^cite_note-244]

Shortly before the release of the files, a November 2025 poll from [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland)/[Ipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipsos) found that 27% of American respondents believed Epstein collaborated with the Israeli government.[^cite_note-245] Following the release of the files, Anti-Israel views become more prominent,[^cite_note-Israel-26] with *[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post)* arguing that the release of the Epstein files led to an increase in anti-Semitism.[^cite_note-246]

#### Bill and Hillary Clinton

Following the partial release of some Epstein files, the Republican-led [House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Oversight_Committee) summoned the Clintons to testify before the committee. Bill Clinton has appeared in several photos with Epstein released in the files, and maintained a [relationship with Epstein in the 1990s and early 2000s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relationship_of_Bill_Clinton_and_Jeffrey_Epstein).[^cite_note-Bill-247] Emails from 2011 and 2015 which were released in November 2025 revealed that Epstein personally denied that Bill Clinton ever visited his island.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249]

In January, the Clintons announced that they would refuse to comply with a [congressional subpoena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_subpoena), regarding an investigation into their ties to Jeffrey Epstein. In a statement, the Clintons called the committee's efforts "legally invalid". Following this, Oversight Chair Representative [James Comer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Comer) announced that he would begin contempt of Congress proceedings into the Clintons the following week. Comer added that "No one's accusing the Clintons of any wrongdoing. We just have questions."[^cite_note-250] On January 21, 2026, the House Oversight panel voted 34–8 to advance a motion to hold Bill Clinton in [contempt of congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt_of_congress); and 28–15 on the measure towards Hillary Clinton. Nine Democrats, including Lee, voted with Republicans to hold Bill Clinton in contempt, while three of the nine also voted in favor of the measure directed towards Hillary Clinton.[^cite_note-Bill-247]

Following the success of the Oversight panel's votes, a vote could be undertaken by the full House of Representatives. If a vote had passed in the full House, a referral would have then been sent to the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice).[^cite_note-Grayer-251] According to *[Politico](9172097.md)*, an estimated 12 to 15 Democrats intended to vote with Republicans, if a full House vote materialized.[^cite_note-252] Following this, the Department of Justice could have weighed prosecution against the Clintons.[^cite_note-Grayer-251]

Former U.S. Attorney [John P. Fishwick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Fishwick_Jr.) argued that the Clintons are "in a very difficult position, because these cases are very straightforward". On February 2, 2026, House Oversight Committee chair [James Comer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Comer) (R-Kentucky) declined the latest offer from the Clintons to testify.[^cite_note-259] Later in the day, it was agreed that Bill and Hillary Clinton would testify before the House Oversight Committee, thus putting an end to the threat of a contempt vote.[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] Hillary's deposition began on February 26, 2026.[^cite_note-263]

#### Firing of Pam Bondi

On January 21, 2026, Democratic Representative [Summer Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Lee) introduced a congressional amendment to hold Pam Bondi in [contempt of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt_of_Congress), accusing her of "failing to comply" with the Epstein Files Transparency Act. Lee's amendment ultimately failed along party lines, 19–24.[^cite_note-Bill-247]

In February 2026, Bondi testified before Congress over the files' release; her testimony drew bipartisan criticism.[^cite_note-Fiery-203] Alongside Democrats, many right-wing figures subsequently called on Bondi to resign or be impeached, including [Greg Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Kelly), [Carl Higbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Higbie),[^cite_note-264] [Erick Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erick_Erickson), [Kyle Rittenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Rittenhouse), and [Nick Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Fuentes).[^cite_note-265]

On April 2, 2026, Trump fired Bondi, reportedly due to her handling of the Epstein files, as well as her inability to secure federal indictments.[^cite_note-Fired-20] Following this, Deputy Attorney General [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) then became acting Attorney General on an interim basis.[^cite_note-Fired-20] According to *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, Trump told Bondi one day before her firing, "I think it's time."[^cite_note-266] Media sources subsequently speculated on who Trump would appoint as Attorney General, with [Lee Zeldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Zeldin) widely mentioned as a strong possibility.[^cite_note-Fox_Bondi-267] Other contenders included Blanche (as a permanent appointment), [Harmeet Dhillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmeet_Dhillon), [Jeanine Pirro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanine_Pirro), [Eric Schmitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Schmitt), [Ron DeSantis](36729527.md), [Ken Paxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Paxton), [Mike Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Lee), [Jay Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Clayton_\(attorney\)), and [Jeffrey Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Clark).[^cite_note-Fox_Bondi-267][^cite_note-268]

After Bondi's firing, Representative Massie praised Trump's decision. He also wrote to Blanche on X, saying "Congratulations AG Blanche. Now you have 30 days to release the rest of the files before becoming criminally liable for failure to comply with the Epstein Files Transparency Act."[^cite_note-269] That day, while on *Fox News*, Blanche was asked by host [Jesse Watters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Watters) if he would agree with Watters that the release of the Epstein files was not handled well. Blanche replied:

> "Look, the Epstein files has been a saga that’s lasted for the entire for the past year, and what happened when the president signed the transparency act is the Department of Justice has now released all the files with respect to the Epstein saga and the attorney general...And so I think that to the extent that the Epstein files was a part of the past year of this Justice Department, it should not be a part of anything going forward."[^cite_note-270]

On April 14, Blanche claimed the Justice Department had released everything the law required.[^cite_note-271]

*[Fox News](11121.md)* later reported that Blanche expressed his desire to Trump to serve as the permanent Attorney General; Trump reportedly told Blanche to treat his time in an acting status as an "audition".[^cite_note-272] On June 4, 2026, Trump announced at a closed-door meeting that he would nominate Blanche to serve as the permanent Attorney General.[^cite_note-273]

In May 2026, Representative [Thomas Massie](28005891.md) lost re-election in his Republican primary, against challenger [Ed Gallrein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Gallrein). Massie's accusation that Epstein held close ties to Israel had previously been shared by Representative [Marjorie Taylor Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Taylor_Greene)[^cite_note-280] and conservative commentator [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson).[^cite_note-281]

#### Proposed legislation

In February 2026, Congressional Democrats introduced Virginia’s Law, proposed legislation named in honor of Epstein victim [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre). The bill would end the [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations) and jurisdiction restrictions for civil sexual abuse cases.[^cite_note-286]

In March 2026, Democratic Representative [Debbie Wasserman Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Wasserman_Schultz) filed the No Escaping Justice Act. The proposed legislation would levy sanctions on foreign nationals who are implicated in the Epstein files.[^cite_note-287]

##### Epstein Files Transparency Act II

In July 2026, Massie introduced the Epstein Files Transparency Act II (Res. 9694) into the U.S. House of Representatives.[^cite_note-Act_II-19] [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna) and [Teresa Leger Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_Leger_Fern%C3%A1ndez) co-sponsored the bill and [Jeff Merkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Merkley) and [Ben Ray Luján](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Ray_Luj%C3%A1n) intend to introduce a Senate version of it. The bill aims to empower state attorneys general to challenge the DOJ in courts (for the latter's failure to comply with the original text of the law), allow Congressional members and victims to sue the DOJ, ban the invocation of the DOJ's common law privileges, and allow victims and prosecutors to obtain select unredacted files.[^cite_note-Act_II-19]

## Reactions

### Journalists

In an opinion article in *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(newspaper\))*, journalist and attorney Chris Traux argued that the Epstein files were "a political [albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albatross_\(metaphor\)) hanging around \[Trump's] neck", while being Trump's equivalent of the [Hillary Clinton email controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton_email_controversy). Traux also argued that the bill's overwhelming success in Congress showcased the weakened influence of Trump's political power.[^cite_note-319] Contributor [A. Scott Bolden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Scott_Bolden) similarly argued in *The Hill* that Trump's initial opposition to the bill proved to be a failure, while arguing that Bondi's new investigations were Trump's eleventh-hour attempt to withhold select files.[^cite_note-320] Contributor John Mac Ghlionn also argued in *The Hill* that Trump's initial failure to release the Epstein Files could "destroy whatever legacy he hoped to leave behind", and is partially responsible for the increasing popularity of far-right commentator [Nick Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Fuentes), a critic of Trump.[^cite_note-321]

Journalist Harrison Berger in *[The American Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Conservative)* argued that the bill granted too much power to Bondi to redact content while releasing the Epstein files. Berger argued that the Trump administration would likely abuse the bill's provision in order to hide Epstein's political and foreign ties. For example, Berger pointed to *[Drop Site News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_Site_News)*'s report that Epstein worked on Israel's behalf with [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) in order to help remove [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) from power.[^cite_note-322]

Following the files' release, contributor [Robby Soave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robby_Soave) argued in *The Hill* that the releases had led to [association fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_fallacy) among the general public. Soave praised Massie's "sincere effort to arrive at the truth", while also arguing that many of Epstein's minor associates likely had no knowledge of his crimes, and were being unfairly punished in the court of public opinion.[^cite_note-323]

Attorney [Daniel Richman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Richman) argued in *[The New York Times](30680.md)* that "The Epstein Files Should Never Have Been Released"; Richman argued that the files' release was ultimately a result of the justice system's failure to prosecute Epstein's associates.[^cite_note-324]

### Public opinion

A September 2025 [Marist Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marist_Poll), surveying 1,477 adult Americans, found that 90% of Americans answered that they wanted at least some of the Epstein files released, with the victims' names redacted. Among these surveyed, 77% stated that they wanted all of the Epstein files to be released, 13% wanted some of the files released, and 9% were opposed. Additionally, 84% of Democrats, 67% of Republicans, and 83% of independents answered that they wanted all of the Epstein files to be released.[^cite_note-325]

A December 2025 *[Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*/[YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) poll, surveying 1,591 Americans, found that 55% of Americans disapproved of Trump's handling of the Epstein investigation, while only 26% approved. Additionally, the poll found that 91% of Democrats, 78% of Independents, and 74% of Republicans supported releasing the Epstein files.[^cite_note-326]

A January 2026 *Economist*/YouGov poll, surveying 1,546 Americans, found that 56% of Americans disapproved of Trump's handling of the files, while 25% approved. Additionally, 49% of Americans answered that Trump is attempting to cover up Epstein's crimes, while 30% answered that he is not.[^cite_note-327] A January 2026 [CNN](62028.md) poll, surveying 1,209 adult Americans, found that only 6% said they're satisfied with what the federal government had so far released. Nearly half of Republicans, three-quarters of independents, and 9 in 10 Democrats said the government was withholding information.[^cite_note-328]
