**Homosexuality** is [romantic attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_attraction), [sexual attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_attraction), or [sexual behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexual_activity) between people of the same [sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sex) or [gender](38076.md).[^cite_note-AmPsycholAssn-definitions-1][^cite_note-Cross-CulturalPsyDefinition-2][^cite_note-EqualCurriculumDefinition-3] It also denotes [identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_identity) based on attraction, related behavior, and [community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community) affiliation.[^cite_note-apahelp-4][^cite_note-amici-5]

Along with [bisexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexuality) and [heterosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexuality), homosexuality is one of the three main categories of sexual orientation within the [heterosexual–homosexual continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexual%E2%80%93homosexual_continuum).[^cite_note-apahelp-4] Although no single theory on the cause of sexual orientation has yet gained widespread support, scientists favor [biological theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology_and_sexual_orientation).[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-Frankowski-7][^cite_note-\:2-8] There is considerably more evidence supporting nonsocial, biological causes of sexual orientation than social ones, especially for males.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-LeVay-9][^cite_note-Balthazart-10]

A major hypothesis involves the [prenatal environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_development), in particular the organizational effects of [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone) on the [fetal brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_and_perinatal_psychology#Psycho-physiological_aspects_of_the_prenatal_and_perinatal_phases).[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-\:1-11] There is no conclusive evidence which confirms that parenting or [early childhood experiences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_in_early_childhood) play a significant role in developing a sexual orientation.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-rcp2007-12] Scientific research shows that homosexuality is a natural and normal variation in [human sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexuality) and is not in and of itself a source of negative psychological effects.[^cite_note-apahelp-4][^cite_note-PAHO-13] Major mental health organizations overwhelmingly reject [conversion therapy](26410.md) as ineffective, [scientifically unsupported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience) and potentially harmful.[^cite_note-apa2009-14][^cite_note-15]

The most common terms for homosexual people are *[lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian)* for females and *[gay](5171128.md)* for [males](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_men), but the term *gay* also commonly refers to both homosexual females and males. The number of people who are gay or lesbian is difficult for researchers to estimate reliably, as many gay and lesbian people do not openly identify as such due to [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) or [prejudice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prejudice) such as [heterosexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexism) or [homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia).[^cite_note-levay-16] Homosexual behavior has also been documented [in many non-human animal species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual_behavior_in_animals),[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-Bagemihl-20][^cite_note-Biological_Exuberance:_Animal-21] though [domestic sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_sheep) are the only conclusively documented example of nonhuman animals exhibiting exclusive same-sex orientation.[^cite_note-Bailey-6]

Many gay and lesbian people are in committed same-sex relationships. These relationships are equivalent to heterosexual relationships in essential psychological respects.[^cite_note-amici-5] Homosexual relationships and acts have been admired as well as condemned throughout recorded history, depending on the form they took and the culture in which they occurred.[^cite_note-psych2010-22] Since the end of the 20th century, there has been a [global movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_social_movements) towards [freedom and equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights) for gay people, including the introduction of [anti-bullying legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-bullying_legislation) to protect gay teenagers at school, legislation ensuring non-discrimination, [equal ability to serve in the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation_and_military_service), [equal access to health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Montreal), equal ability to [adopt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_adoption) and [parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_parenting), and the establishment of [marriage equality](92656.md).

The word *homosexual* is a Greek and Latin [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_word), with the first element derived from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ὁμός *homos*, 'same' (not related to the Latin *homo*, 'man', as present in the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo)*, which includes *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)* and now [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct) [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species)), thus connoting sexual acts and affections between members of the same sex, including lesbianism.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The first known appearance of *homosexual* in print is found in an 1868 letter to [Karl Heinrich Ulrichs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Heinrich_Ulrichs) by the Austrian-born novelist [Karl-Maria Kertbeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Maria_Kertbeny)[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-glbtq-26] arguing against a [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) anti-[sodomy law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_law).[^cite_note-glbtq-26][^cite_note-27] In 1886, the psychiatrist [Richard von Krafft-Ebing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_von_Krafft-Ebing) used the terms *homosexual* and *heterosexual* in his book *[Psychopathia Sexualis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathia_Sexualis_\(Richard_von_Krafft-Ebing_book\))*. Krafft-Ebing's book was so popular among both laymen and doctors that the terms *heterosexual* and *homosexual* became the most widely accepted terms for sexual orientation.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-psexualis-29]

Many modern [style guides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_guide) in the U.S. recommend against using *homosexual* as a noun, instead using *gay man* or *lesbian*.[^cite_note-glaad-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Similarly, some recommend completely avoiding usage of *homosexual* as it has a negative, clinical history and because the word only refers to one's sexual behavior (as opposed to romantic feelings) and thus it has a negative [connotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connotation).[^cite_note-glaad-30] *Gay* and *lesbian* are the most common alternatives. The first letters are frequently combined to create the [initialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym_and_initialism) LGBT (sometimes written as GLBT), in which *B* and *T* refer to [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexuality) and [transgender](19904525.md) people.

*Gay* especially refers to male homosexuality,[^cite_note-36] but may be used in a broader sense to refer to all [LGBTQ](78174325.md) people. In the context of sexuality, *lesbian* refers only to female homosexuality. The word *lesbian* is derived from the name of the Greek island [Lesbos](2633994.md), where the poet [Sappho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sappho) wrote largely about her emotional relationships with young women.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

Although early writers also used the adjective *homosexual* to refer to any single-sex context (such as an all-girls school), today the term is usually used in reference to sexual attraction, activity, and orientation. The term *[homosocial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosociality)* is now used to describe single-sex contexts that are not specifically sexual. There is also a word referring to same-sex love, *[homophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophilia)*.

Some synonyms for same-sex attraction or sexual activity include *[men who have sex with men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_who_have_sex_with_men)* or *MSM* (used in the medical community when specifically discussing sexual activity) and *[homoerotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoeroticism)* (referring to works of art).[^cite_note-UNAIDS-39][^cite_note-40] [Pejorative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pejorative) terms in English include *[queer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer)*, *[faggot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faggot_\(slang\))*, *[fairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy)*, *poof*, *poofter*[^cite_note-41] and *homo*.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-terms-45] Beginning in the 1990s, some of these have been [reclaimed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reclaimed_word) as positive words by gay men and lesbians, as in the usage of [queer studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_studies), [queer theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_theory), and even the popular American television program *[Queer Eye for the Straight Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_Eye_for_the_Straight_Guy)*.[^cite_note-46] The word *[homo](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/homo)* occurs in many other languages without the pejorative connotations it has in English.[^cite_note-wolinski-47] As with [ethnic slurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_slur) and [racial slurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_slur), the use of these terms can still be considered offensive. The range of acceptable use for these terms depends on the context and speaker.[^cite_note-wolinski-47] Conversely, *gay*, a word originally embraced by homosexual men and women as a positive, affirmative term (as in [gay liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_liberation) and [gay rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_rights)),[^cite_note-48] came into widespread [pejorative use](5171128.md#Generalized_pejorative_use) among young people in the early 2000s.[^cite_note-bbcgay-49]

## History

Main article: [LGBT history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_history)

Further information: [Timeline of LGBT history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_LGBT_history)

&#x20;Same-sex relations are documented throughout history and varied in acceptance depending on time and place.[^cite_note-Hubbard-50] However, many historians and anthropologists warn against using terms like "homosexuality" to describe these practices, because that may draw false equivalencies between acts that, while technically similar, may have been viewed as completely different acts. In many cultures, same-sex relations were not always considered sexual in the same way that "reproductive" sex between men and women was—particularly lesbian sex.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-\:4-52] Modern-style homosexual partnerships, including marriages, are even more uncommon, probably because marriages have almost always been oriented around producing children.[^cite_note-\:4-52] Other scholars argue that there are significant continuities between historical and modern homosexuality.[^cite_note-Norton_2016-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Boswell_1989_17–36-55]



Burning of two homosexuals (the [knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight) [Richard von Hohenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Puller_von_Hohenburg) and his [squire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squire) Anton Mätzler) at the stake outside [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), 1482 (from the *[Spiezer Schilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiezer_Schilling)* chronicle)

In cultures influenced by [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions), the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_law#History) and the [church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_homosexuality) established [sodomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy) as a transgression against divine law or a [crime against nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_against_nature). The condemnation of [anal sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anal_sex) between males, however, predates Christian belief. Throughout the majority of [Christian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity), most [Christian theologians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_theology) and [denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denominations) have considered homosexual behavior as [immoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immoral) or [sinful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin_in_Christianity).[^cite_note-Gnuse_2015-56][^cite_note-57]

Many historical figures, including [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates), [Lord Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Byron), [Edward II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_II), and [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian),[^cite_note-58] have had terms such as *gay* or *[bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexuality)* applied to them. Some scholars have regarded uses of such modern terms on people from the past as an [anachronistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anachronism) introduction of a contemporary [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_construction) of sexuality that would have been foreign to their times.[^cite_note-foucault-59][^cite_note-buxton_myth-60] Other scholars see continuity instead.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-Boswell_1989_17–36-55][^cite_note-Norton_2016-53]

In social science, there has been a dispute between "essentialist" and "constructionist" views of homosexuality. The debate divides those who believe that terms such as "gay" and "straight" refer to objective, culturally invariant properties of persons from those who believe that the experiences they name are artifacts of unique cultural and social processes. "Essentialists" typically believe that sexual preferences are determined by biological forces, while "constructionists" assume that sexual desires are learned.[^cite_note-62] The philosopher of science [Michael Ruse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ruse) has stated that the social constructionist approach, which is influenced by Foucault, is based on a selective reading of the historical record that confuses the existence of homosexual people with the way in which they are labelled or treated.[^cite_note-63]

### Africa

The first record of a possible homosexual couple in history is commonly regarded as [Khnumhotep and Niankhkhnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khnumhotep_and_Niankhkhnum), an [ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) male couple, who lived around 2400 BCE. The pair are portrayed in a nose-kissing position, the most intimate pose in [Egyptian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_art), surrounded by what appear to be their heirs. The anthropologists [Stephen Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_O._Murray) and [Will Roscoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Roscoe) reported that women in [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho) engaged in socially sanctioned "long term, erotic relationships" called *[motsoalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motsoalle).*[^cite_note-murrayroscoe-64]

The anthropologist [E. E. Evans-Pritchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._E._Evans-Pritchard) also recorded that male [Azande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azande) warriors in the northern [Congo](76762.md) routinely took on young male lovers between the ages of twelve and twenty, who helped with household tasks and participated in [intercrural sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercrural_sex) with their older husbands.[^cite_note-evanspritchard-65] Some scholars have criticized labeling relationships like these "homosexual", because these relationships were temporary arrangements designed to prepare young men for full manhood, rather than the permanent sexual orientation implied by the word "homosexual".[^cite_note-\:4-52] Evans-Pritchard also recorded information about sexual relationships between women, based on reports from male Azande. According to male Azande, women would take female lovers and that partners would penetrate each other using bananas or other food items carved into the shape of a phallus. They also reported that the daughter of a ruler may be given a female slave as a sexual partner. Evans-Pritchard also recorded that the male Azande were fearful of women taking on female lovers, as they might view men as unnecessary.[^cite_note-66]

### Americas

#### Indigenous cultures



*Dance to the Berdache*\
[Sac and Fox Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sac_and_Fox_Nation) ceremonial dance to celebrate the two-spirit person. George Catlin (1796–1872); Smithsonian Institution, Washington, D.C.



Illustration of two [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs) homosexual men talking, from the [Florentine Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentine_Codex) (1577)

As is true of many other non-Western cultures, it is difficult to determine the extent to which Western notions of sexual orientation and gender identity apply to Pre-Columbian cultures. Evidence of homoerotic sexual acts and transvestism has been found in many pre-[conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) civilizations in [Latin America](18524.md), such as the [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec), [Mayas](18449273.md), [Quechuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_people), [Moches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)), [Zapotecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapotec_civilization), the [Incas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire), and the [Tupinambá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupinamb%C3%A1_people) of Brazil.[^cite_note-glbtqlatinamerica-67][^cite_note-glbtqmex-68][^cite_note-69]

The Spanish conquerors were horrified to discover sodomy openly practiced among native peoples, and attempted to crush it out by subjecting the *berdaches* (as the Spanish called them) under their rule to severe penalties, including public [execution](5902.md), burning and being torn to pieces by dogs.[^cite_note-coello-70] The Spanish conquerors talked extensively of sodomy among the natives to depict them as savages and hence justify their conquest and forceful conversion to Christianity. As a result of the growing influence and power of the conquerors, many native cultures started condemning homosexual acts themselves.

Among some of the [indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) in North America prior to European colonization, a relatively common form of same-sex sexuality centered around the figure of the [Two-Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-Spirit) individual (the term itself was coined only in 1990).[^cite_note-NovaTwoSpirit-71] Typically, this individual was recognized early in life, given a choice by the parents to follow the path and, if the child accepted the role, raised in the appropriate manner, learning the customs of the gender it had chosen.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Two-Spirit individuals were commonly [shamans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism) and were revered as having powers beyond those of ordinary shamans.[^cite_note-BrewminateTwoSpirit-74] Their sexual life was with the ordinary tribe members of the same sex.

During the colonial times following the European invasion, homosexuality was prosecuted by the [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisition), sometimes leading to death sentences on the charges of sodomy, and the practices became clandestine. Many homosexual individuals went into heterosexual marriages to maintain appearances, and many joined the (unmarried) Catholic clergy to escape public scrutiny of their lack of interest in the opposite sex.

#### Canada

During the colonial period, both the French and the British criminalised same-sex sexual relations. Anal sex between males was a capital offence.[^cite_note-75] Post-Confederation, anal sex and acts of "gross indecency" continued to be criminal offences, but were no longer capital offences.[^cite_note-76] Individuals were prosecuted for same-sex sexual activity as late as the 1960s, which led to the federal Parliament amending the *Criminal Code* in 1969 to provide that anal sex between consenting adults in private (defined as only two persons) was not a criminal offence. In advocating for the law, the then-Minister of Justice, [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau), said: "The state has no place in the bedrooms of the nation."[^cite_note-77]

In 1995, the [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md) held that sexual orientation is a protected personal characteristic under [the equality clause of the *Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_15_of_the_Canadian_Charter_of_Rights_and_Freedoms).[^cite_note-78] The federal Parliament and provincial legislatures began to amend their laws to treat same-sex relations in the same way as opposite-sex relations. Beginning in 2003, the courts in Canada began to rule that excluding same-sex couples from marriage violated the equality clause of the *Charter*. In 2005, the federal Parliament enacted the *[Civil Marriage Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Marriage_Act)*, which legalised same-sex marriage across Canada.[^cite_note-79]

Canada has been referred to as the most [gay-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay-friendly) country in the world, ranked first in the *[Gay Travel Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_tourism)* chart in 2018, and among the five safest in [*Forbes* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_magazine) in 2019.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] It was also ranked first in Asher & Lyric's LGBTQ+ Danger Index in a 2021 update.[^cite_note-82]

#### Mexico

Anthropologist Joseph Carrier found in the 1990s that same-gender sexual relations were relatively common in many neighborhoods in [Mexico City](18987.md). While long-term partnerships were not acceptable, and queer men were still expected to marry and have children, it was acceptable for men to have "discreet" relations with other men as long as they took on the active, penetrative role. Taking the passive role was considered detrimental to one's masculinity.[^cite_note-83]

#### United States

See also: [LGBT history in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_history_in_the_United_States)

During the Victorian era, same-sex desire was viewed as a common sin. Anyone was capable of "succumbing" to same-sex attraction, and various sexual and non-sexual acts—including masturbation—were considered possible ways that someone could be "turned" queer. Queer people were often viewed as predators, exclusively attracted to heterosexuals, and therefore a danger to society.[^cite_note-\:4-52]

Same-sex relationships were generally stigmatized throughout the 20th century, and hate crimes were commonly carried out, especially against men. Most queer people lived out these relationships in secret, often while still having opposite-sex marriages and families. Anti-gay sentiment surged [after the Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Scare), when homosexuals were seen as a threat to American society and were rooted out of organizations across the country.[^cite_note-\:4-52]

Homosexuality was declassified as a disease by the American Psychiatric Association in 1973.[^cite_note-\:4-52]

In 1986, the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) ruled in *[Bowers v. Hardwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowers_v._Hardwick)* that a state could criminalize [sodomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy), but, in 2003, overturned itself in *[Lawrence v. Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_v._Texas)* and thereby legalized homosexual activity throughout the [United States of America](3434750.md).

It is only since the 2010s that [census](6889.md) forms and political conditions have facilitated the visibility and enumeration of same-sex relationships.[^cite_note-Committed-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

[Same-sex marriage in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States) expanded from one state in 2004 to all 50 states in 2015, through various state court rulings, state legislation, direct popular votes ([referendums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendums) and [initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative)), and federal court rulings.

### Asia

#### East Asia



Anal sex between two males being watched, [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). Painting. 18th Century

In [East Asia](19605700.md), same-sex love has been referred to since the earliest recorded history.

[Homosexuality in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_in_China), known as the *passions of the cut peach* and various other euphemisms, has been recorded since approximately 600 BCE. Homosexuality was mentioned in many famous works of Chinese literature. The instances of same-sex affection and sexual interactions described in the classical novel *[Dream of the Red Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_of_the_Red_Chamber)* seem as familiar to observers in the present as do equivalent stories of romances between heterosexual people during the same period. Keith McMahon suggests that homosexual relationships between men in Ming stories such as Bian er chai was described as more harmonious and enjoyable than heterosexual relationships.[^cite_note-93] Writings from the [Liu Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Song_dynasty) by Wang Shunu claimed that homosexuality was as common as heterosexuality in the late 3rd century.[^cite_note-Geng-94]

Opposition to homosexuality in China originates in the medieval [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (618–907), attributed to the rising influence of Christian and Islamic values,[^cite_note-Hinsch,_Bret_1990_pp.77-78-95] but did not become fully established until the [Westernization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization) efforts of the late [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) and the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)).[^cite_note-Kang1-96]

#### North Asia

Early Russian ethnographers observed that [Chukchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_people) shamans in [Siberia](29639.md) were sometimes said to called by mystical forces to engage in a form of [ritualized homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritualized_homosexuality?action=edit\&redlink=1) relations with other men. This ritual typically involved a [gender change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_transition)—a religious ceremony that, it was believed, transformed his genitalia into that of a female. After the change, he might dress in women's clothing and behave in feminine ways. He was then believed to "lose" masculine traits like hunting skill, and instead take on "feminine" traits, like healing and nurturing. Some of these shamans would take male lovers, and could even marry other men, and the shaman would take on a "wifely" role. Homosexual relations outside of this specialized role were reportedly not tolerated.[^cite_note-\:4-52][^cite_note-97]

#### South Asia

See also: [Homosexuality in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_in_India), [Hinduism and LGBT topics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_and_LGBT_topics), and [Buddhism and sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_and_sexual_orientation)

South Asia has a recorded and verifiable history of homosexuality going back to at least 1200 BC. Hindu medical texts written in India from this period document homosexual acts and attempt to explain the cause in a neutral/scientific manner.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Numerous artworks and literary works from this period also describe homosexuality.[^cite_note-MitraVaruna-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

Several ancient Hindu texts have passages that condemn homosexuality. For example, Manu-smṛti (11.174) says that if a man has sex with another man, then he should take a shower with clothes on. The implication of the statement is that sex between two men is not considered normal.[^cite_note-105] Pali Cannon, written in Sri Lanka between 600 BC and 100 BC, states that sexual relations, whether of homosexual or of heterosexual nature, is forbidden in the monastic code, and states that any acts of soft homosexual sex (including masturbation and interfumeral sex) does not entail a punishment but must be confessed to the monastery. These codes apply to monks only and not to the general population.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] The Kama Sutra written in India around 200 AD also described numerous homosexual sex acts.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

Prior to early modern period and colonialism, there were no strict laws against homosexuality and transexuality. However certain dharmic moral codes forbade sexual misconduct (of both heterosexual and homosexual nature) among the upper class of persists and monks, and religious codes of foreign religions such as Christianity and Islam imposed homophobic rules on their populations.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

Hinduism describes a [third gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_gender) that is equal to other genders and documentation of the third gender are found in ancient Hindu and Buddhist medical texts.[^cite_note-112] There are certain characters in the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)* who, according to some versions of the epic, change genders, such as [Shikhandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikhandi), who is sometimes said to be born as a female but identifies as male and eventually marries a woman. [Bahuchara Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahuchara_Mata) is the goddess of fertility, worshipped by *hijras* as their patroness.

### Europe

#### Classical period

Further information: [Homosexuality in ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_in_ancient_Greece), [Homosexuality in ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_in_ancient_Rome), [Timeline of LGBT history in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_LGBT_history_in_the_United_Kingdom), and [Homosexuality in medieval Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_in_medieval_Europe)

The earliest Western documents (in the form of literary works, art objects, and [mythographic materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology)) concerning same-sex relationships are derived from [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pederasty_in_ancient_Greece).

The formal practice of Pederasty, an erotic yet often restrained relationship between a free adult male and a free adolescent, was valued for its [pedagogic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy) benefits and as a means of population control, though occasionally blamed for causing disorder. In his *[Symposium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_\(Plato\))*, [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato) has Phaedras praising its benefits whilst having Socrates encouraging platonic relationships between older and younger men[^cite_note-price-113] (the literal origin of the term, *platonic* love). In his later work, [Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_\(dialogue\)), he has an Athenian stranger propose prohibition of sexual relationships between men entirely saying that it is unnatural.[^cite_note-plato2-114] But Aristotle, in his Nichomachean ethics, states that homosexuality in men for some comes about by nature and others through habituation and further states that no one could be described as lacking in self control for whom nature is the cause[^cite_note-115]

Some scholars argue that there are examples of homosexual love in ancient literature, such as [Achilles and Patroclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_and_Patroclus) in the *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad)*.[^cite_note-116]



Female youths are depicted surrounding Sappho in this painting of [Lafond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafond) "Sappho sings for Homer", 1824.

Little is known of female homosexuality in antiquity. [Sappho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sappho), born on the island of [Lesbos](2633994.md), was included by later Greeks in the canonical list of [nine lyric poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_lyric_poets). The adjectives deriving from her name and place of birth ([Sapphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphic_love) and Lesbian) came to be applied to female homosexuality beginning in the 19th century.[^cite_note-lesbian-117][^cite_note-sapphic-118] Sappho's poetry centers on passion and love for various personages and both genders. The narrators of many of her poems speak of [infatuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infatuation) and [love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love) (sometimes requited, sometimes not) for various females, but descriptions of physical acts [between women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian) are few and subject to debate.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]



Sappho reading to her companions on an Attic vase constructed c. 435 BC

In [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), the kind of homosexuality seen took the form of pederastic relationships, where older free men took on an active role and slaves or freed youths took the receptive/passive role in sex. Romans saw passivity as generally unmanly, and as incompatible with the warrior ethos expected of men serving the expansionist Roman state.[^cite_note-121]

In his letter addressed to the Christian community in Rome, [Paul the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle) writes of people engaging in idolatry ("\[they] exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images made to look like a mortal human being and birds and animals and reptiles"[^cite_note-122]) who eventually become infatuated with a person of the same sex in a "suffering of dishonour".[^cite_note-123]

Pederastic attentions were legitimate only when directed toward current or former slaves. The [Hellenophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenophile) emperor [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian) (117–138) is renowned for his relationship with [Antinous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinous), a young man of low birth. Only the Christian emperor [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I) decreed a law on 6 August 390, condemning passive males to be burned at the stake. Notwithstanding these regulations taxes on [brothels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothel) with boys available for homosexual sex continued to be collected until the end of the reign of [Anastasius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasius_I_\(emperor\)) in 518. [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I), towards the end of his reign, expanded the proscription to the active partner as well (in 558), warning that such conduct can lead to the destruction of cities through the "wrath of God".[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]

#### Renaissance

During the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), wealthy cities in northern [Italy](14532.md)—[Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) in particular—were renowned for their widespread practice of same-sex love, engaged in by a considerable part of the male population and constructed along the classical pattern of Greece and Rome.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] But even as many of the male population were engaging in same-sex relationships, the authorities, under the [aegis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis) of the [Officers of the Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officers_of_the_Night) court, were prosecuting, fining, and imprisoning a good portion of that population.

From the second half of the 13th century, death was the punishment for male homosexuality in most of Europe.[^cite_note-128] The relationships of socially prominent figures, such as [King James I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_relationships_of_James_VI_and_I) and the [Duke of Buckingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Villiers,_1st_Duke_of_Buckingham), served to highlight the issue, including in anonymously authored street pamphlets: "The world is chang'd I know not how, For men Kiss Men, not Women now;...Of J. the First and Buckingham: He, true it is, his Wives Embraces fled, To slabber his lov'd Ganimede" (*Mundus Foppensis, or The Fop Display'd*, 1691).

*Love Letters Between a Certain Late Nobleman and the Famous Mr. Wilson* was published in 1723 in England, and is presumed by some modern scholars to be a novel. The 1749 edition of [John Cleland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cleland)'s popular novel *[Fanny Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Hill)* includes a homosexual scene, but this was removed in its 1750 edition. Also in 1749, the earliest extended and serious defense of homosexuality in English, *Ancient and Modern [Pederasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pederasty) Investigated and Exemplified*, written by [Thomas Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Cannon_\(author\)), was published, but was suppressed almost immediately. Executions for sodomy continued in the [Netherlands](21148.md) until 1803, and in [England](9316.md) until 1835, [James Pratt and John Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Pratt_and_John_Smith) being the last Englishmen to be so hanged.

Between 1864 and 1880 [Karl Heinrich Ulrichs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Heinrich_Ulrichs) published a series of 12 tracts, which he collectively titled *Research on the Riddle of Man-Manly Love.* In 1867, he became the first self-proclaimed homosexual person to speak out publicly in defense of homosexuality when he pleaded at the Congress of German Jurists in [Munich](19058.md) for a resolution urging the repeal of anti-homosexual laws.[^cite_note-levay-16] *Sexual Inversion* by [Havelock Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havelock_Ellis), published in 1896, challenged theories that homosexuality was abnormal, as well as [stereotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype), and insisted on the ubiquity of homosexuality and its association with intellectual and artistic achievement.[^cite_note-sexualinversion-131]

Although medical texts like these (written partly in Latin to obscure the sexual details) were not widely read by the general public, they did lead to the rise of [Magnus Hirschfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Hirschfeld)'s [Scientific-Humanitarian Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific-Humanitarian_Committee), which campaigned from 1897 to 1933 against [anti-sodomy laws in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragraph_175), as well as a much more informal, unpublicized movement among British intellectuals and writers, led by such figures as [Edward Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Carpenter) and [John Addington Symonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Addington_Symonds). Beginning in 1894 with *Homogenic Love*, Socialist activist and poet Edward Carpenter wrote a string of pro-homosexual articles and pamphlets, and "came out" in 1916 in his book *My Days and Dreams*. In 1900, [Elisar von Kupffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisar_von_Kupffer) published an anthology of homosexual literature from antiquity to his own time, *[Lieblingminne und Freundesliebe in der Weltliteratur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieblingminne_und_Freundesliebe_in_der_Weltliteratur)*.

Israel is considered the most tolerant country in the Middle East to homosexuals,[^cite_note-133] with [Tel Aviv](31453.md) being named "the gay capital of the Middle East"[^cite_note-134] and considered one of the most gay friendly cities in the world.[^cite_note-135] The annual [Pride Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Pride) in support of homosexuality takes place in Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-136]

It is thought that ancient [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) (2nd millennium BC to 1st millennium AD) viewed homosexuality as negative and at least criminal,[^cite_note-144] with the religious codes of [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism) forbidding homosexuality.[^cite_note-145] Some religious texts contain prayers for divine blessings on homosexual relationships. The *Almanac of Incantations* contained prayers favoring on an [equal basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_before_the_law) the love of a man for a woman, of a woman for a man, and of a man for man.[^cite_note-epistle-143]

### South Pacific

In some societies of [Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia), especially in [Papua New Guinea](22943.md), traditional practices required a prepubertal boy to be paired with an older adolescent who would become his mentor and who would "inseminate" him (orally, anally, or topically, depending on the tribe) over a number of years in order for the younger to also reach puberty.[^cite_note-melanesia-146] This practice among the [Simbari people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simbari_people) occurred due to local belief that semen was necessary for male growth. The practice did not appear to affect male sexual orientation; nearly all males were happy to move on to relationships with women once permitted.[^cite_note-LeVay_20162-147] A small minority of males remain bachelors and continue to engage in homosexual relations, and are considered unusual and ridiculed by other tribesmen.[^cite_note-Bailey_20032-148] Many Melanesian societies have abandoned this practice since the introduction of [Christianity](5211.md) via [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionaries).[^cite_note-melanesia-146]

## Sexuality and identity

### Behavior and desire

"Homosexual desire" redirects here. Not to be confused with the book, *[Homosexual Desire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual_Desire)*.

The [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), the [American Psychiatric Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychiatric_Association), and the [National Association of Social Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Social_Workers) identify sexual orientation as "not merely a personal characteristic that can be defined in isolation. Rather, one's sexual orientation defines the universe of persons with whom one is likely to find the satisfying and fulfilling relationships":[^cite_note-amici-5]

> Sexual orientation is commonly discussed as a characteristic of the individual, like biological sex, gender identity, or age. This perspective is incomplete because sexual orientation is always defined in relational terms and necessarily involves relationships with other individuals. Sexual acts and romantic attractions are categorized as homosexual or heterosexual according to the biological sex of the individuals involved in them, relative to each other. Indeed, it is by acting—or desiring to act—with another person that individuals express their heterosexuality, homosexuality, or bisexuality. This includes actions as simple as holding hands with or kissing another person. Thus, sexual orientation is integrally linked to the intimate personal relationships that human beings form with others to meet their deeply felt needs for love, attachment, and intimacy. In addition to sexual behavior, these bonds encompass nonsexual physical affection between partners, shared goals and values, mutual support, and ongoing commitment.[^cite_note-amici-5]

The [Kinsey scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_scale), also called the Heterosexual-Homosexual Rating Scale,[^cite_note-kinsey-149] attempts to describe a person's sexual history or episodes of their sexual activity at a given time. It uses a scale from 0, meaning exclusively [heterosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexuality), to 6, meaning exclusively homosexual. In both the Male and Female volumes of the [Kinsey Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_Reports), an additional grade, listed as "X", has been interpreted by scholars to indicate [asexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexuality).[^cite_note-Stange-150]

### Sexual identity

Main article: [Sexual identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_identity)

Often, sexual orientation and [sexual identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_identity) are not distinguished, which can impact accurately assessing sexual orientation. According to Bailey et al. "sexual identity, is one's self-conception (sometimes disclosed to others and sometimes not) as a homosexual, bisexual, or heterosexual person". One's sexual identity label may not align with one's sexual behavior or sexual orientation (e.g. in the case of homosexual persons who may present themselves as heterosexual).[^cite_note-Bailey-6]<sup>: 48, 89 </sup>

### Sexual fluidity

Main article: [Sexual fluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_fluidity)

While [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation) is a stable attribute that is an innate characteristic and an immutable trait, some research suggests that some people may experience change in their [sexual orientation identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation_identity) over the course of their lives, and this is more likely for [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female) than for [males](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male).[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] The American Psychological Association distinguishes between sexual orientation (an innate attraction) and sexual orientation identity (which may change at any point in a person's life).[^cite_note-apa2009-2-154]

### Same-sex relationships

Main article: [Same-sex relationship](19771642.md)



Male homosexuality symbol



Female homosexuality symbol

People with a homosexual orientation can express their sexuality in a variety of ways, and may or may not express it in their behaviors.[^cite_note-apahelp-4] Many have sexual relationships predominantly with people of their own [sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sex), though some have sexual relationships with those of the opposite sex, [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual) relationships, or none at all ([celibacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celibacy)).[^cite_note-apahelp-4] Studies have found same-sex and opposite-sex couples to be equivalent to each other in measures of satisfaction and commitment in relationships, that age and sex are more reliable than sexual orientation as a predictor of satisfaction and commitment to a relationship, and that people who are heterosexual or homosexual share comparable expectations and ideals with regard to romantic relationships.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-Duffy-156][^cite_note-Baccman-157]

### Coming out of the closet

Main article: [Coming out](71537.md)

*Coming out* (*of the closet*) is a phrase referring to one's disclosure of their sexual orientation or gender identity, and is described and experienced variously as a psychological process or journey.[^cite_note-Coming_Out:_A_Journey-158] Generally, coming out is described in three phases. The first phase is that of "knowing oneself", and the realization emerges that one is open to same-sex relations.[^cite_note-159] This is often described as an internal coming out. The second phase involves one's decision to come out to others, e.g. family, friends, or colleagues. The third phase more generally involves living openly as an LGBT person.[^cite_note-hrccontinuum-160] In the United States today, people often come out during high school or college age. At this age, they may not trust or ask for help from others, especially when their orientation is not accepted in society. Sometimes their own families are not even informed.

[Outing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outing) is the practice of publicly revealing the sexual orientation of a closeted person.[^cite_note-glbtqouting-161] Notable politicians, celebrities, military service people, and clergy members have been outed, with motives ranging from malice to political or moral beliefs. Many commentators oppose the practice altogether,[^cite_note-biasfreeusage-162] while some encourage outing public figures who use their positions of influence to harm other gay people.[^cite_note-statesmanouting-163]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_sexual_orientation)

Homosexuality occurs in most, and perhaps all, human cultures and societies.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-LeVay-9][^cite_note-Balthazart-10] In their 2016 literature review, Bailey *et al.* stated that they "expect that in all cultures ... a minority of individuals are sexually predisposed (whether exclusively or non-exclusively) to the same sex." They state that there is no persuasive evidence that the demographics of sexual orientation have varied much across time or place.[^cite_note-Bailey-6] Men are more likely to be exclusively homosexual than to be equally attracted to both sexes, while the opposite is true for women.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-LeVay-9][^cite_note-Balthazart-10]

Surveys in Western cultures find, on average, that about 93% of men and 87% of women identify as completely heterosexual, 4% of men and 10% of women as mostly heterosexual, 0.5% of men and 1% of women as evenly bisexual, 0.5% of men and 0.5% of women as mostly homosexual, and 2% of men and 0.5% of women as completely homosexual.[^cite_note-Bailey-6] An analysis of 67 studies found that the lifetime prevalence of sex between men (regardless of orientation) was 3–5% for East Asia, 6–12% for South and South East Asia, 6–15% for Eastern Europe, and 6–20% for Latin America.[^cite_note-164] The [International HIV/AIDS Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_HIV/AIDS_Alliance) estimates that worldwide between 3 and 16% of men have had some form of [sex with another man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_who_have_sex_with_men) at least once during their lifetime.[^cite_note-International_HIV/AIDS_Alliance_2003-165]

According to major studies, 2% to 11% of people have had some form of same-sex sexual contact within their lifetime;[^cite_note-Billy1993-166][^cite_note-Fay1989-167][^cite_note-Sell1995-168][^cite_note-SavinWilliams2009-169] this percentage rises to 16–21% when either or both same-sex attraction and behavior are reported.[^cite_note-SavinWilliams2009-169]

According to the 2021 [United States Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census), there were about 1.2 million same-sex couple households.[^cite_note-170] In the [United States](3434750.md), according to a report by [The Williams Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Williams_Institute) in April 2011, 3.5% or approximately 9 million of the adult population identify as lesbian, gay, bisexual or transgender.[^cite_note-171] A 2013 study by the CDC, in which over 34,000 Americans were interviewed, puts the percentage of self-identifying lesbians and gay men at 1.6%, and of bisexuals at 0.7%.[^cite_note-cdc.gov-172]

In October 2012, [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)) started conducting annual surveys to study the demographics of LGBT people, determining that 3.4% (±1%) of adults identified as LGBT in the United States.[^cite_note-gallup-173] It was the nation's largest poll on the issue at the time.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] In 2017, the percentage was estimated to have risen to 4.5% of adults, with the increase largely driven by [millennials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennials). The poll attributes the rise to greater willingness of younger people to reveal their sexual identity.[^cite_note-176]

Measuring the prevalence of homosexuality presents difficulties. It is necessary to consider the measuring criteria that are used, the cutoff point and the time span taken to define a sexual orientation.[^cite_note-levay-16] Many people, despite having same-sex attractions, may be reluctant to identify themselves as gay or bisexual. The research must measure some characteristic that may or may not be defining of sexual orientation. The number of people with same-sex desires may be larger than the number of people who act on those desires, which in turn may be larger than the number of people who self-identify as gay, lesbian, or bisexual.[^cite_note-black-177]

Reliable data as to the size of the gay and lesbian population are of value in informing public policy.[^cite_note-black-177] For example, demographics are of help in calculating the costs and benefits of [domestic partnership benefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_partnership_benefits), of the impact of legalizing [gay adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_adoption), and of the impact of the U.S. military's former [Don't Ask Don't Tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Ask_Don't_Tell) policy.[^cite_note-black-177] Further, knowledge of the size of the "gay and lesbian population holds promise for helping social scientists understand a wide array of important questions—questions about the general nature of labor market choices, accumulation of human capital, specialization within households, discrimination, and decisions about geographic location."[^cite_note-black-177]

The [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), the [American Psychiatric Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychiatric_Association), and the [National Association of Social Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Social_Workers) state:

> In 1952, when the American Psychiatric Association published its first [Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnostic_and_Statistical_Manual_of_Mental_Disorders), homosexuality was included as a disorder. Almost immediately, however, that classification began to be subjected to critical scrutiny in research funded by the [National Institute of Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Mental_Health). That study and subsequent research consistently failed to produce any empirical or scientific basis for regarding homosexuality as a disorder or abnormality, rather than a normal and healthy sexual orientation. As results from such research accumulated, professionals in medicine, mental health, and the behavioral and social sciences reached the conclusion that it was inaccurate to classify homosexuality as a mental disorder and that the DSM classification reflected untested assumptions based on once-prevalent social norms and clinical impressions from unrepresentative samples comprising patients seeking therapy and individuals whose conduct brought them into the criminal justice system.
>
> In recognition of the scientific evidence,[^cite_note-178] the American Psychiatric Association removed homosexuality from the DSM in 1973, stating that "homosexuality per se implies no impairment in judgment, stability, reliability, or general social or vocational capabilities." After thoroughly reviewing the scientific data, the American Psychological Association adopted the same position in 1975, and urged all mental health professionals "to take the lead in removing the stigma of mental illness that has long been associated with homosexual orientations." The National Association of Social Workers has adopted a similar policy.
>
> &#x20;Thus, mental health professionals and researchers have long recognized that being homosexual poses no inherent obstacle to leading a happy, healthy, and productive life, and that the vast majority of gay and lesbian people function well in the full array of social institutions and interpersonal relationships.[^cite_note-amici-5]

The consensus of research and clinical literature demonstrates that same-sex sexual and romantic attractions, feelings, and behaviors are normal and positive variations of human sexuality.[^cite_note-response-179] There is now a large body of research evidence that indicates that being gay, lesbian or bisexual is compatible with normal mental health and social adjustment.[^cite_note-rcp2007-12] The [World Health Organization](33583.md)'s [ICD-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICD-9) (1977) listed homosexuality as a mental illness; it was removed from the [ICD-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICD-10), endorsed by the Forty-third World Health Assembly on 17 May 1990.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-pinknewsstigma-182] Like the DSM-II, the ICD-10 added [ego-dystonic sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ego-dystonic_sexual_orientation) to the list, which refers to people who want to change their [gender identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity) or sexual orientation because of a psychological or behavioral disorder ([F66.1](https://icd.who.int/browse10/2019/en#/F66.1)). The [Chinese Society of Psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Society_of_Psychiatry) removed homosexuality from its [Chinese Classification of Mental Disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Classification_of_Mental_Disorders) in 2001 after five years of study by the association.[^cite_note-183]

According to the [Royal College of Psychiatrists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Psychiatrists), "\[t]his unfortunate history demonstrates how marginalisation of a group of people who have a particular personality feature (in this case homosexuality) can lead to harmful medical practice and a basis for discrimination in society."[^cite_note-rcp2007-12]

Most lesbian, gay, and bisexual people who seek psychotherapy do so for the same reasons as heterosexual people (stress, relationship difficulties, difficulty adjusting to social or work situations, etc.); their sexual orientation may be of primary, incidental, or no importance to their issues and treatment. Whatever the issue, there is a high risk for anti-gay bias in psychotherapy with lesbian, gay, and bisexual clients.[^cite_note-textbook-184] Psychological research in this area has been relevant to counteracting prejudicial ("[homophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia)") attitudes and actions, and to the [LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights) movement generally.[^cite_note-intro-185]

According to the American Psychological Association,[^cite_note-response-179] the appropriate application of psychotherapy is grounded in acceptance of evidence that: same-sex attractions and orientations are natural variations of human sexuality, not signs of mental or developmental disorders. While homosexuality and bisexuality have often been stigmatized, leading to social stress and other negative effects, these orientations are part of the normal range of human experience. People with same-sex orientations can and do lead fulfilling lives, forming stable, loving relationships and families. There is no scientific evidence supporting claims that same-sex orientation results from family dysfunction or trauma.[^cite_note-response-179]

### Sexual orientation change efforts

Main article: [Sexual orientation change efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation_change_efforts)

There are no studies of adequate scientific rigor that conclude that sexual orientation change efforts work to change a person's sexual orientation. Those efforts have been controversial due to tensions between the values held by some faith-based organizations, on the one hand, and those held by LGBT rights organizations and professional and scientific organizations and other faith-based organizations, on the other.[^cite_note-apa2009-14] The longstanding consensus of the behavioral and social sciences and the health and mental health professions is that homosexuality *per se* is a normal and positive variation of human sexual orientation, and therefore not a mental disorder.[^cite_note-apa2009-14] The American Psychological Association says that "most people experience little or no sense of choice about their sexual orientation".[^cite_note-answers-186] Some individuals and groups have promoted the idea of homosexuality as symptomatic of developmental defects or spiritual and moral failings and have argued that sexual orientation change efforts, including psychotherapy and religious efforts, could alter homosexual feelings and behaviors. Many of these individuals and groups appeared to be embedded within the larger context of conservative religious political movements that have supported the stigmatization of homosexuality on political or religious grounds.[^cite_note-apa2009-14]

No major mental health professional organization has sanctioned efforts to change sexual orientation and virtually all of them have adopted policy statements cautioning the profession and the public about treatments that purport to change sexual orientation. These include the American Psychiatric Association, American Psychological Association, American Counseling Association, National Association of Social Workers in the U.S.,[^cite_note-187] the Royal College of Psychiatrists,[^cite_note-royal2009-188] and the [Australian Psychological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Psychological_Society).[^cite_note-aps-189] The American Psychological Association and the Royal College of Psychiatrists expressed concerns that the positions espoused by [NARTH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_Research_&_Therapy_of_Homosexuality) are not supported by the science and create an environment in which prejudice and discrimination can flourish.[^cite_note-royal2009-188][^cite_note-apaexgay-190]

The American Psychiatric Association says "individuals maybe become aware at different points in their lives that they are heterosexual, gay, lesbian, or bisexual" and "opposes any psychiatric treatment, such as 'reparative' or ['conversion' therapy](26410.md), which is based upon the assumption that homosexuality *[per se](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_se_\(terminology\))* is a mental disorder, or based upon a prior assumption that the patient should change his/her homosexual orientation". They do, however, encourage [gay affirmative psychotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_affirmative_psychotherapy).[^cite_note-What_is-191] Similarly, the American Psychological Association is doubtful about the effectiveness and side-effect profile of sexual orientation change efforts, including conversion therapy.[^cite_note-192]

The American Psychological Association "encourages mental health professionals to avoid misrepresenting the efficacy of sexual orientation change efforts by promoting or promising change in sexual orientation when providing assistance to individuals distressed by their own or others' sexual orientation and concludes that the benefits reported by participants in sexual orientation change efforts can be gained through approaches that do not attempt to change sexual orientation".[^cite_note-apa2009-14]

## Causes

Main articles: [Biology and sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology_and_sexual_orientation) and [Environment and sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_and_sexual_orientation)

Scientists have come to favor biological causes of sexual orientation.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-Frankowski-7] There is considerably more evidence supporting nonsocial, biological causes of sexual orientation than social ones, especially for males.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-\:2-8] There is no substantive evidence which suggests parenting or early childhood experiences play a role with regard to sexual orientation.[^cite_note-rcp2007-12][^cite_note-Frankowski-7][^cite_note-Bailey-6]

The [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Pediatrics) stated in *[Pediatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatrics_\(journal\))* in 2004:

> There is no scientific evidence that abnormal parenting, sexual abuse, or other adverse life events influence sexual orientation. Current knowledge suggests that sexual orientation is usually established during early childhood.[^cite_note-Frankowski-7]

The [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), [American Psychiatric Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychiatric_Association), and [National Association of Social Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Social_Workers) stated in 2006:

> Currently, there is no scientific consensus about the specific factors that cause an individual to become heterosexual, homosexual, or bisexual—including possible biological, psychological, or social effects of the parents' sexual orientation. However, the available evidence indicates that the vast majority of lesbian and gay adults were raised by heterosexual parents and the vast majority of children raised by lesbian and gay parents eventually grow up to be heterosexual.[^cite_note-apahelp-4]

### Prenatal hormones

Main article: [Prenatal hormones and sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_hormones_and_sexual_orientation)

The prenatal hormonal theory of homosexuality posits that variations in exposure to sex hormones, particularly androgens and estrogens, during critical periods of early brain development play a central role in shaping sexual orientation. Evidence from natural experiments in humans and animal studies have found that elevated prenatal androgens are associated with increased attraction to females, while reduced androgen signaling or increased prenatal estrogen exposure may be linked to increased attraction to males.[^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-Bailey-6]

### Genes

Main article: [Biology and sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology_and_sexual_orientation)

Evidence from [twin studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_study) and molecular genetics studies indicate that genes likely play some role in sexual orientation.[^cite_note-Bailey-6]<sup>: 74–77 </sup>[^cite_note-\:5-194] Scientists caution that many people misconstrue the meanings of *genetic* and *environmental*. [Environmental influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_and_sexual_orientation) does not automatically imply that the social environment influences or contributes to the development of sexual orientation. Hypotheses for the impact of the post-natal social environment on sexual orientation are weak, especially for males.[^cite_note-Bailey-6]

### Maternal immune response

Main article: [Fraternal birth order and male sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternal_birth_order_and_male_sexual_orientation)

One environmental hypothesis of homosexuality is the maternal immune hypothesis, which proposes that male homosexuality might arise from a biological response by a mother's immune system to a male fetus during pregnancy.[^cite_note-Bailey-6] According to the hypothesis, when a woman is pregnant with a male fetus, her body is exposed to male-specific antigens linked to the Y chromosome. In some mothers, this exposure triggers an immune response that produces antibodies against these proteins. With each successive male pregnancy, the maternal immune response is thought to strengthen, and the resulting antibodies may cross the placenta to affect the sexual differentiation of the developing fetal brain. This process is hypothesized to underlie the [fraternal birth order effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternal_birth_order_effect), the observation that the likelihood of a man being homosexual increases with the number of older biological brothers he has.[^cite_note-Bailey-6][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-\:2-8]

### Epigenetic hypothesis

Main article: [Epigenetic theories of homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetic_theories_of_homosexuality)

A hypothesis developed by Rice et al. proposes that [epigenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) "epi‐marks" (non–DNA sequence modifications like [DNA methylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_methylation)) play a central buffering role in sexual development, by controlling how strongly fetal androgen signaling influences tissues.[^cite_note-\:6-196][^cite_note-\:5-194] Some of those epi‐marks are sex‐specific (i.e. different in XX vs. XY embryos) and help protect against atypical androgen exposure (too much in XX, too little in XY). Occasionally, some of these protective epi‐marks escape erasure across generations, and find themselves in an opposite‐sex individual, causing a gonad–trait discordance (a mismatch between gonadal sex and some sexually dimorphic traits).[^cite_note-\:6-196] Rice et al. propose that such a discordance may be causing same‐sex sexual orientations.[^cite_note-\:6-196][^cite_note-\:5-194]

### Evolution

Since homosexuality tends to lower [reproductive success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_success), it is unclear how it is maintained in the population at a relatively high frequency.[^cite_note-197] There are many proposed explanations, such as genes predisposing to homosexuality also conferring advantage in heterosexuals (sexual antagonism),[^cite_note-Bailey-6] a [kin selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kin_selection) effect,[^cite_note-198] [self-domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-domestication),[^cite_note-199] and more.

## Parenting

Main article: [Same-sex parenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_parenting)

Scientific research has been generally consistent in showing that lesbian and gay parents are as fit and capable as heterosexual parents, and their children are as psychologically healthy and well-adjusted as children reared by heterosexual parents.[^cite_note-cpa2006-200][^cite_note-apsp-201][^cite_note-amici2010-202][^cite_note-amici-5][^cite_note-pediatrics-203][^cite_note-herek2006-204][^cite_note-cpa2005-205]

Some research has examined the sexual orientation of children raised by same-sex couples. A 2005 review of studies by [Charlotte J. Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_J._Patterson) for the American Psychological Association did not find higher rates of homosexuality among the children of lesbian or gay parents.[^cite_note-APAreport-206] According to Bailey et al. 2016, available data do not suggest higher rates of non-heterosexuality among children of same-sex couples. However, they state that even given a modest heritability of sexual orientation, it would be expected that biological children of non-heterosexuals would be more likely to have a non-heterosexual orientation due to genes alone.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaileyVaseyDiamondBreedlove201684-207] According to a 2011 data, 80% of the children being raised by same-sex couples in the US are their own biological children.[^cite_note-ABCNews2-208] In addition, accepting social environments may facilitate the open expression of individuals same-sex attraction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaileyVaseyDiamondBreedlove201687-209] Thus, it is necessary to control for various confounding factors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaileyVaseyDiamondBreedlove201684-207] One study by Bailey et al. found that the sexual orientation of sons raised by gay men was not related to length of time they had lived with their fathers (social theories of homosexuality would predict sons who lived with a gay father the longest would be most likely to be gay).[^cite_note-\:3-210] The Bailey et al. review conclude that social environmental influence on male sexual orientation is not well supported, while it remains more plausible for female sexual orientation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaileyVaseyDiamondBreedlove201687-209]

The terms "[men who have sex with men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_who_have_sex_with_men)" (MSM) and "[women who have sex with women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_who_have_sex_with_women)" (WSW) refer to people who engage in sexual activity with others of the same sex regardless of how they identify themselves—as many choose not to accept [social identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_\(social_science\)) as lesbian, gay and bisexual.[^cite_note-msmafrica-211][^cite_note-UNAIDS1-212][^cite_note-TGHIVINF-213][^cite_note-MSTSW-214][^cite_note-TGMSMNET-215] These terms are often used in medical literature and [social research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_research) to describe such groups for study, without needing to consider the issues of sexual self-identity. The terms are seen as problematic by some, however, because they "obscure social dimensions of sexuality; undermine the self-labeling of lesbian, gay, and bisexual people; and do not sufficiently describe variations in sexual behavior".[^cite_note-216]

In contrast to its benefits, sexual behavior can be a [disease vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_\(epidemiology\)). [Safe sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_sex) is a relevant [harm reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harm_reduction) philosophy.[^cite_note-217] Many countries [currently prohibit men who have sex with men from donating blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_male_blood_donor_controversy); the policy of the [United States](3434750.md) [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) states that "they are, as a group, at increased risk for [HIV](14170.md), [hepatitis B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B) and certain other infections that can be transmitted by transfusion."[^cite_note-Blood-218]

### Mental

When it was first described in medical literature, homosexuality was often approached from a view that sought to find an inherent psychopathology as its root cause. Much literature on mental health and homosexual patients centered on their [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_depression), [substance abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse), and suicide. Although these issues exist among people who are [non-heterosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-heterosexual), discussion about their causes shifted after homosexuality was removed from the [Diagnostic and Statistical Manual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnostic_and_Statistical_Manual) (DSM) in 1973. Instead, social ostracism, legal discrimination, internalization of negative stereotypes, and limited support structures indicate factors homosexual people face in Western societies that often adversely affect their mental health.[^cite_note-219] Stigma, prejudice, and discrimination stemming from negative societal attitudes toward homosexuality lead to a higher prevalence of mental health disorders among lesbians, gay men, and bisexuals compared to their heterosexual peers.[^cite_note-220] Evidence indicates that the liberalization of these attitudes is associated with a decrease in such mental health risks among younger sexual minority people.[^cite_note-221]

### Gay and lesbian youth

See also: [Suicide among LGBT youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_among_LGBT_youth)

Gay and lesbian youth bear an increased risk of suicide, substance abuse, school problems, and isolation because of a "hostile and condemning environment, verbal and physical abuse, rejection and isolation from family and peers".[^cite_note-dhhs-222] Further, LGBT youths are more likely to report psychological and physical abuse by parents or caretakers, and more sexual abuse. Suggested reasons for this disparity are that (1) LGBT youths may be specifically targeted on the basis of their [perceived sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation_profiling) or gender non-conforming appearance, and (2) that "risk factors associated with sexual minority status, including discrimination, invisibility, and rejection by family members...may lead to an increase in behaviors that are associated with risk for victimization, such as substance abuse, sex with multiple partners, or running away from home as a teenager."[^cite_note-223]

Crisis centers in larger cities and information sites on the Internet have arisen to help youth and adults.[^cite_note-suicideorg-224] [The Trevor Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trevor_Project), a suicide prevention helpline for gay youth, was established following the 1998 airing on HBO of the [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award) winning short film *[Trevor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-225]

Main article: [Criminalization of homosexuality](69713049.md)

Most nations do not prohibit consensual sex between unrelated persons above the local [age of consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_consent). Some jurisdictions further recognize identical rights, protections, and privileges for the family structures of same-sex couples, including [marriage](92656.md). Some countries and jurisdictions mandate that all individuals restrict themselves to heterosexual activity and disallow homosexual activity via [sodomy laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_law). Offenders can face the death penalty in Islamic countries and jurisdictions ruled by [sharia](28840.md). There are, however, often significant differences between official policy and real-world enforcement.

Although homosexual acts were decriminalized in some parts of the [Western world](21208200.md), such as [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Poland) in 1932, [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Denmark) in 1933, [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Sweden) in 1944, and [England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_United_Kingdom) in 1967, it was not until the mid-1970s that the [gay community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_community) first began to achieve limited [civil rights](37071.md) in some [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country). A turning point was reached in 1973 when the [American Psychiatric Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychiatric_Association), which previously listed homosexuality in the [DSM-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSM-I) in 1952, removed homosexuality in the [DSM-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSM-II), in recognition of scientific evidence.[^cite_note-amici-5] In 1977, [Quebec](7954867.md) became the first state-level jurisdiction in the world to prohibit [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) on the grounds of sexual orientation. During the 1980s and 1990s, several developed countries enacted laws decriminalizing homosexual behavior and prohibiting discrimination against lesbian and gay people in employment, housing, and services. On the other hand, many countries today in the Middle East and Africa, as well as several countries in Asia, the Caribbean and the South Pacific, outlaw homosexuality. In 2013, the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) upheld Section 377 of the [Indian Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Penal_Code),[^cite_note-226] but in 2018, overturned its previous ruling and legalized homosexual activity in India.[^cite_note-227] Ten countries or jurisdictions, all of which are predominantly [Islamic](6037917.md) and governed according to [sharia](28840.md) law, have imposed the [death penalty for homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_for_homosexuality). These include Afghanistan, Iran, Brunei, Mauritania, Saudi Arabia, and several regions in Nigeria and Jubaland.[^cite_note-ILGA_2015-228][^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233]

Each of these statutes covers bias on the basis of race, religion, and ethnicity; 32 of them cover sexual orientation, 28 cover gender, and 11 cover transgender/gender-identity.

  &#x20;In October 2009, the [Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Shepard_and_James_Byrd_Jr._Hate_Crimes_Prevention_Act), which "...gives the Justice Department the power to investigate and prosecute bias-motivated violence where the perpetrator has selected the victim because of the person's actual or perceived race, color, religion, national origin, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity or disability", was signed into law and makes hate crime based on sexual orientation, amongst other offenses, a federal crime in the United States.

  [^cite_note-242]

#### European Union

In the [European Union](9317.md), discrimination of any type based on sexual orientation or gender identity is illegal under the [Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_of_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-243]

Since the 1960s, many LGBT people in the West, particularly those in major metropolitan areas, have developed a so-called [gay culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_culture). To many, gay culture is exemplified by the [gay pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_pride) movement, with annual parades and displays of rainbow flags. Yet not all LGBT people choose to participate in "queer culture", and many gay men and women specifically decline to do so. To some it seems to be a frivolous display, perpetuating gay stereotypes.



Original Rainbow Pride Flag in [San Francisco](49728.md), designed by [Gilbert Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Baker_\(artist\)) in 1978

With the outbreak of [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS) in the early 1980s, many LGBT groups and individuals organized campaigns to promote efforts in AIDS education, prevention, research, patient support, and community outreach, as well as to demand government support for these programs.

The death toll wrought by the AIDS epidemic at first seemed to slow the progress of the gay rights movement, but in time it galvanized some parts of the LGBT community into community service and political action, and challenged the heterosexual community to respond compassionately. Major American motion pictures from this period that dramatized the response of individuals and communities to the AIDS crisis include *[An Early Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Early_Frost)* (1985), *[Longtime Companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longtime_Companion)* (1990), *[And the Band Played On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_the_Band_Played_On)* (1993), *[Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_\(film\))* (1993), and *[Common Threads: Stories from the Quilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Threads:_Stories_from_the_Quilt)* (1989).

[Publicly gay politicians](71537.md) have attained numerous government posts, even in countries that had [sodomy laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_law) in their recent past. Examples include [Guido Westerwelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Westerwelle), Germany's [Vice-Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor_of_Germany); [Pete Buttigieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Buttigieg), the [United States Secretary of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Transportation), [Peter Mandelson](216970.md), a British [Labour Party](19279158.md) cabinet minister and [Per-Kristian Foss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per-Kristian_Foss), formerly [Norwegian](21241.md) [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance).

LGBT movements are opposed by a variety of individuals and organizations. Some [social conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism) believe that all sexual relationships with people other than an opposite-sex spouse undermine the traditional family[^cite_note-244] and that children should be reared in homes with both a father and a mother.[^cite_note-DMD-245][^cite_note-246] Some argue that gay rights may conflict with individuals' freedom of speech,[^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] religious freedoms in the workplace,[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250] the ability to run churches,[^cite_note-251] charitable organizations[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] and other religious organizations[^cite_note-254] in accordance with one's religious views, and that the acceptance of homosexual relationships by religious organizations might be forced through threatening to remove the tax-exempt status of churches whose views do not align with those of the government.[^cite_note-BannedBoston-255][^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258]

### Military service

Main article: [Sexual orientation and military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation_and_military_service)



U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) signs into law the [Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Ask,_Don't_Tell_Repeal_Act_of_2010).

Policies and attitudes toward gay and lesbian [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) personnel vary widely around the world. Some countries allow gay men, lesbians, and bisexual people to serve openly and have granted them the same rights and privileges as their heterosexual counterparts. Many countries neither ban nor support LGB service members. A few countries continue to ban homosexual personnel outright.

Most Western military forces have removed policies excluding sexual minority members. Of the 26 countries that participate militarily in [NATO](21133.md), more than 20 permit openly gay, lesbian and bisexual people to serve. Of the permanent members of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md), three ([United Kingdom](31717.md), [France](5843419.md) and [United States](3434750.md)) do so. The other two generally do not: [China](5405.md) bans gay and lesbian people outright, [Russia](25391.md) excludes all gay and lesbian people during peacetime but allows some gay men to serve in wartime (see below). [Israel](9282173.md) is the only country in the Middle East region that allows openly LGB people to serve in the military.

According to the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), [empirical evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_evidence) fails to show that sexual orientation is relevant to military effectiveness including [unit cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_cohesion), morale, recruitment and retention.[^cite_note-259]

Societal acceptance of non-heterosexual orientations such as homosexuality is lowest in Asian, African and Eastern European countries,[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261] and is highest in Western Europe, Australia, and the Americas. Western society has become increasingly accepting of homosexuality since the 1990s. In 2017, Professor Amy Adamczyk contended that these cross-national differences in acceptance can be largely explained by three factors: the relative strength of democratic institutions, the level of economic development, and the religious context of the places where people live.[^cite_note-Adamczyk-262]

These [stereotypical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype) beliefs of the people against the LGBTQ+ community have caused rejection and discriminatory behavior against them. Various researches have shown that LGBTQ+ people in societies that do not recognize homosexuality as a sexual identity of such group feel insecure, psychological pressure and isolated from the society.[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266] Legal restrictions such as imprisonment, fear of [execution](5902.md), not been to allowed employment in governmental jobs, along with informal restrictions such as sexual abuse in society, exclusion from family and social groups, verbal and public humiliation, etc., have all made life difficult for the LGBTQ+ groups.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269][^cite_note-\:0-270]

### Relationships

In 2006, the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), [American Psychiatric Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychiatric_Association) and [National Association of Social Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Social_Workers) stated in an [amicus brief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amicus_curiae) presented to the [Supreme Court of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_California): "Gay men and lesbians form stable, committed relationships that are equivalent to heterosexual relationships in essential respects. The institution of marriage offers social, psychological, and health benefits that are denied to same-sex couples. By denying same-sex couples the right to marry, the state reinforces and perpetuates the stigma historically associated with homosexuality. Homosexuality remains stigmatized, and this stigma has negative consequences. California's prohibition on marriage for same-sex couples reflects and reinforces this stigma". They concluded: "There is no scientific basis for distinguishing between same-sex couples and heterosexual couples with respect to the legal rights, obligations, benefits, and burdens conferred by civil marriage."[^cite_note-amici-5]

### Religion

Main article: [Homosexuality and religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_and_religion)

Though the relationship between [homosexuality and religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_and_religion) is complex, current authoritative bodies and doctrines of the world's largest religions view homosexual behaviour negatively. This can range from quietly discouraging homosexual activity, to explicitly forbidding same-sex sexual practices among adherents and actively opposing social acceptance of homosexuality. Some teach that homosexual desire itself is sinful,[^cite_note-OPCtrial-271] others state that only the sexual act is a sin,[^cite_note-272] while others are completely accepting of gays and [lesbians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian).[^cite_note-273] Some claim that homosexuality can be overcome through religious faith and practice. On the other hand, voices exist within many of these religions that view homosexuality more positively, and liberal [religious denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_denomination) may bless [same-sex marriages](92656.md). Some view same-sex love and sexuality as sacred, and a [mythology of same-sex love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_themes_in_mythology) can be found throughout the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECabezón1992vii-274] According to a 2025 poll conducted by [Pew Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), almost half of LGBT Americans identify as religious.[^cite_note-275]

## Discrimination



LGBT activists at [Cologne Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Pride) in 2015 carrying a banner with the flags of 70 countries where [homosexuality is illegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_by_country_or_territory)

### Gay bullying

Main article: [Gay bullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_bullying)

Gay bullying can be the [verbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbal_abuse) or [physical abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_abuse) against a person who is perceived by the aggressor to be [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian), [gay](5171128.md), [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual), [transgender](19904525.md), or generally queer, including persons who are actually [heterosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexual) or of non-specific or unknown [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation). In the US, teenage students heard anti-gay slurs such as "homo", "faggot" and "sissy" about 26 times a day on average, or once every 14 minutes, according to a 1998 study by Mental Health America (formerly National Mental Health Association).[^cite_note-276]

### Heterosexism and homophobia

Further information: [Heterosexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexism) and [Homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia)



Protests in New York City against [Uganda](31816.md)'s [Anti-Homosexuality Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Anti-Homosexuality_Bill)

In many cultures, homosexual people are frequently subject to prejudice and discrimination. A 2011 Dutch study concluded that 49% of Holland's youth and 58% of youth foreign to the country reject homosexuality.[^cite_note-277] Similar to other minority groups they can also be subject to [stereotyping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_stereotypes). These attitudes tend to be due to forms of homophobia and heterosexism (negative [attitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attitude_\(psychology\)), bias, and discrimination in favor of opposite-sex sexuality and relationships). Heterosexism can include the presumption that everyone is heterosexual or that opposite-sex attractions and relationships are the [norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_\(social\)) and therefore superior. [Homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia) is a fear of, aversion to, or discrimination against homosexual people. It manifests in different forms, and a number of different types have been postulated, among which are internalized homophobia, social homophobia, emotional homophobia, rationalized homophobia, and others.[^cite_note-278] Similar is [lesbophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbophobia) (specifically targeting lesbians) and [biphobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biphobia) (against bisexual people). When such attitudes manifest as crimes they are often called [hate crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_crimes) and [gay bashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_bashing).

Negative stereotypes characterize LGB people as less romantically stable and more likely to abuse children, but there is no scientific basis to such assertions. Gay men and lesbians form stable, committed relationships that are equivalent to heterosexual relationships in essential respects.[^cite_note-amici-5] Sexual orientation does not affect the likelihood that people will abuse children.[^cite_note-lamb-279][^cite_note-herekm-280][^cite_note-APAreport-206] Claims that there is scientific evidence to support [an association between being gay and being a pedophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedophilia_and_homosexuality) are based on misuses of those terms and misrepresentation of the actual evidence.[^cite_note-herekm-280]

### Violence against homosexuals

Main article: [Violence against LGBT people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_LGBT_people)

Further information: [Rape](7980471.md)

In the United States, the [FBI](11127.md) reported that 20.4% of hate crimes reported to law enforcement in 2011 were based on sexual orientation bias. 56.7% of these crimes were based on bias against homosexual men. 11.1% were based on bias against homosexual women. 29.6% were based on anti-homosexual bias without regard to gender.[^cite_note-fbicrime-281] Lesbians, may become the victims of "[corrective rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrective_rape)", a violent crime with the supposed aim of making them heterosexual. In certain parts of the world, LGBT people are also at risk of "[honor killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_killing)" perpetrated by their families or relatives,[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284] or violence perpetrated by the state.[^cite_note-285]

## Homosexual behavior in other animals

Main article: [Homosexual behavior in animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual_behavior_in_animals)



[Roy and Silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_and_Silo), two [New York](645042.md) [Central Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park_Zoo) male [chinstrap penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinstrap_penguin) similar to those pictured, became internationally known when they coupled and later were given an egg that needed hatching and care, which they successfully provided.

[^cite_note-Love_That_Dare_Not_Squeak-286]

Homosexual and bisexual behaviors occur in a number of other animal species. Such behaviors include [sexual activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_sexual_behaviour), [courtship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtship), [affection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affection), [pair bonding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_bond), and [parenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenting),[^cite_note-Bagemihl-20] and are widespread; a 1999 review by researcher [Bruce Bagemihl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Bagemihl) shows that homosexual behavior has been documented in about 500 species, ranging from [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate) to [gut worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acanthocephala).[^cite_note-Bagemihl-20][^cite_note-Biological_Exuberance:_Animal-21] Animal sexual behavior takes many different forms, even within the same species. The motivations for and implications of these behaviors have yet to be fully understood, since most species have yet to be fully studied.[^cite_note-287] According to Bagemihl, "the animal kingdom \[does] it with much greater sexual diversity—including homosexual, bisexual and nonreproductive sex—than the scientific community and society at large have previously been willing to accept".[^cite_note-288] According to [Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Michael_Bailey) et al., [domestic sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_sheep) are the only conclusively documented example of animals to exhibit an exclusively homosexual orientation, besides humans.[^cite_note-Bailey-6]

A review paper by N. W. Bailey and [Marlene Zuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlene_Zuk) looking into studies of same-sex sexual behaviour in animals challenges the view that such behaviour lowers reproductive success, citing several hypotheses about how same-sex sexual behavior might be adaptive; these hypotheses vary greatly among different species.[^cite_note-Bailey_et_al.-289]

In October 2023, biologists reported studies of animals (over 1,500 different species) that found [same-sex behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_behavior) (not necessarily related to human orientation) may help improve social stability by reducing conflict within the groups studied.[^cite_note-NAT-20231003-290][^cite_note-NYT-20231003cz-291]
