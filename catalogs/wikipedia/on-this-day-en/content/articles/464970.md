**Khorramshahr** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): خرمشهر; [\[xoræmˈʃæhɾ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian))[^cite_note-5] is a city in the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Khorramshahr_County\)) of [Khorramshahr County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorramshahr_County), Khuzestan province, Iran, serving as capital of both the county and the district.[^cite_note-Khuzestan_Province_Structure-6]

Khorramshahr is an [inland port](1341936.md) city located approximately 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) north of [Abadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadan,_Iran). The city extends to the right bank of the [Shatt Al Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatt_Al_Arab) waterway near its confluence with the [Haffar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haffar) arm of the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun) river. The city was destroyed in the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), with the 1986 census recording a population of zero. Khorramshahr was rebuilt after the war, and more recent censuses show that the population has returned to the pre-war level.

## History

The area where the city exists today was originally under the waters of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). It later became part of the vast marshlands and the tidal flats at the mouth of the [Karun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun_River). The small town known as *Piyan*, and later *Bayan* appeared in the area no sooner than the late [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) time in the first century AD. Whether or not this was located at the same spot where Khurramshahr is today, is highly debatable.

During the Islamic centuries, the [Daylamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylamite) [Buwayhid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buwayhid) king, Panah Khusraw [Adud ad-Dawlah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adud_ad-Dawlah) ordered the digging of a canal to join the Karun River (which at the time emptied independently into the Persian Gulf through the [Bahmanshir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahmanshir) channel) to the [Shatt al-Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatt_al-Arab) (the joint estuary of the [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris) and [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) rivers, known in Iran as *Arvand Rud*). The extra water made the joint estuary more reliably navigable. The channel thus created was known as the [Haffar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haffar), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) for "excavated," "dugout," which exactly described what the channel was. The Haffar soon became the main channel of the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun), as it is in the present day.

It was the capital of the [Sheikdom of Muhammara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Muhammara), and until 1847, at which time it became Persian territory (according to Article II of the [Treaty of Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Erzurum)), Khorramshahr was alternately claimed and occupied by [Persia](14653.md) and Turkey. Its [ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabir_Ibn_Merdaw) at the time was an Arab [sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh).[^cite_note-EB1911-7]

### Iran–Iraq War

Main articles: [Battle of Khorramshahr (1980)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khorramshahr_\(1980\)) and [Battle of Khorramshahr (1982)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khorramshahr_\(1982\))

Because of the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), the population of Khorramshahr dropped from 146,706 in the 1976 census to 0 in the 1986 census. During the first battle of Khorramshahr, the Iranian propaganda referred to the city as *Khuninshahr* ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): خونین شهر, lit. 'City of Blood'), because of the heavy casualties. The population reached 34,750 in the 1991 census and by the 2006 census it reached 123,866, and according to World Gazetteer its population as of 2012 is 138,398, making the population close to what it was before the war.[^cite_note-8]

### 2026 Iran war

Eyewitnesses reported that the city came under attack on the morning of April 6, 2026.[^cite_note-9]

## Demographics

### Mandaean community



[Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans) in Khorramshahr in 2015

Khorramshahr is home to a [Mandaean community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans). It is one of the last remaining locations in the world where [Neo-Mandaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Mandaic) is still spoken. There are only a few hundred speakers of the Khorramshahr dialect of Neo-Mandaic.[^cite_note-Häberl_2009-10]

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the city's population was 123,866 in 26,385 households.[^cite_note-2006_Khuzestan_Province-11] The following census in 2011 counted 129,418 people in 33,623 households.[^cite_note-2011_Khuzestan_Province-12] The 2016 census measured the population of the city as 133,097 people in 37,124 households.[^cite_note-2016_Khuzestan_Province-2]
