**Wayanad district** (Malayalam: [\[ʋɐjɐnaːɖɨ̆\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Malayalam)), or **Wynad**, is a [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India) in the north-east of the [Indian](14533.md) state of [Kerala](4349459.md),[^cite_note-7] with its administrative headquarters at the municipality of [Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta). It is the only [plateau](538005.md) in Kerala.[^cite_note-8] The Wayanad Plateau forms a continuation of the [Mysore Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Plateau), the southern portion of the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau). It is set high in the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats) with altitudes ranging from 700 to 2,100 meters and some areas resemble to [Eastern Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ghats) including Kolagapara, Kurumbalakotta and some parts of Vadakkanad and Chekadi.[^cite_note-9] Vellari Mala, a 2,240 m (7,349 ft) high peak situated on the trijunction of Wayanad, [Malappuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malappuram_district), and [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode_district) districts, is the highest point in Wayanad district.[^cite_note-10] The district was formed on 1 November 1980 as the 12th district in Kerala, by carving out areas from [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode_district) and [Kannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannur_district) districts. An area of 885.92 km<sup>2</sup> in the district is forested.[^cite_note-auto-11] Wayanad has three [municipal towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality)—[Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta), [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady) and [Sulthan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulthan_Bathery). There are many indigenous [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe) in this area.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The [Kabini River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabini_River), a tributary of the [Kaveri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaveri_River), originates at Wayanad. Wayanad district, along with the [Chaliyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaliyar) valley in the neighbouring [Nilambur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilambur) (Eastern [Eranad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eranad) region) in [Malappuram district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malappuram_district), is known for natural [gold](12240.md) fields,[^cite_note-14] which are also seen in other parts of the [Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiri_Biosphere_Reserve). The [Chaliyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaliyar) river, which is the fourth longest river of Kerala, originates on the Wayanad plateau. The historically important [Edakkal Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edakkal_Caves) are located in Wayanad district.

Wayanad district is bordered by [Karnataka](16880.md) ([Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu_district), [Chamarajanagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamarajanagar_district) and [Mysore](1702713.md) districts) to the north and north-east, [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) ([Nilgiris district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiris_district)) to the south-east (it is the only district that shares border with both the neighbouring states of Kerala), [Malappuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malappuram_district) to the south, [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode_district) to the south-west and [Kannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannur_district) to the north-west.[^cite_note-15] [Pulpally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulpally) in Wayanad boasts the only [Lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_\(Ramayana\))-[Kusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusha_\(Ramayana\)) temple in Kerala and [Vythiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vythiri) has the only mirror temple in Kerala, which is a [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) temple. Varambetta mosque is the oldest Muslim mosque of Wayanad.[^cite_note-16] Wayanad is famous for its role in the [Cotiote War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotiote_War),[^cite_note-17] where [Pazhassi Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazhassi_Raja) with the help of the [Kurichya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurichya) tribe in association with Hindus and Muslims of the Malabar region launched a revolt against the British. Kaniyambetta and Muttil Panchayaths are the centrally located [Panchayaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj) with the best access from all corners of Wayanad, while Tavinjal Panchayath is on the northeast border with Kannur district. The edicts found in the caves of [Ambukuthi Mala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambukuthi_Mala) are evidence that occupation dates from the beginning of the New Age Civilisation.[^cite_note-18]

## Etymology

The name 'Wayanad' is derived from 'vayal nāḍŭ' ([Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) / [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam)) which translates to 'the land of [paddy fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_fields)' in English.[^cite_note-auto-11]

## Formation



Topography of Wayanad on [Mysore Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Plateau)

Wayanad district lies in the [Bayalu Seeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayalu_Seeme) region (highland) of the [Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiri_Biosphere_Reserve). Geographically it is similar to the neighbouring districts of [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu_district) and [Mysore](1702713.md) of [Karnataka](16880.md), and [Nilgiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiris_district) of [Tamil Nadu](29918.md). Wayanad plateau forms a continuation of the [Mysore Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Plateau).[^cite_note-wnd-19]

During the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), Wayanad was a [taluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) in the erstwhile [Malabar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_District).[^cite_note-20] The regions included in the taluks of [Gudalur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudalur,_Nilgiris) and [Pandalur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandalur) in the present-day [Nilgiris district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiris_district), also known as Southeast Wayanad, formed part of the erstwhile Wayanad taluk.[^cite_note-Logan_2-21] Southeast Wayanad was part of Malabar District until 31 March 1877, when it was transferred to the neighbouring [Nilgiris district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiris_district) due to the heavy population of Malabar and the small area of Nilgiris.[^cite_note-Logan_2-21] Wayanad was a separate revenue division within the [Malabar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_District) until 1924.[^cite_note-Mbsp-22]

During the States Reorganisation of 1956 after the independence of India, the [Mysore state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_state) (present-day [Karnataka](16880.md)) claimed Wayanad due to its historical and geographical peculiarities. However the linguistic survey of the 1951 census of India found that 87.5% of the total population of Wayanad were native speakers of [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam) at that time, while just 6.2% of the total population spoke [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada_language).[^cite_note-census1951-23]

On 1 January 1957, the erstwhile Malabar District was divided into three: [Kannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannur), [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode), and [Palakkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad).[^cite_note-1961_kkd-24] On the same day Wayanad taluk was split up into North Wayanad and South Wayanad.[^cite_note-1961_cnr-25] Initially both of the taluks of Wayanad were included in newly formed Kannur district.[^cite_note-1961_cnr-25] However, two months later on 15 March 1957, South Wayanad taluk was transferred into [Kozhikode district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode_district).[^cite_note-1961_kkd-24] The North Wayanad Taluk was transferred to Kozhikode district on 1 January 1979.[^cite_note-w1981-26]

Wayanad district was formed by incorporating the taluks of North Wayanad and South Wayanad on 1 November 1980 as the 12th [district of Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Kerala).[^cite_note-27] On the same date, North Wayanad Taluk was renamed as [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), and South Wayanad was split to form the taluks of [Sultan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bathery) and [Vythiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vythiri).[^cite_note-w1981-26] [Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta) in [Vythiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vythiri) taluk became the headquarters of the new district.[^cite_note-w1981-26]

### Pre-history

Historians believe that human settlements have existed in these parts from at least 1,000 BCE. Much evidence of [New Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Stone_Age) civilisation can be seen in the hills throughout the present-day Wayanad district. The [Edakkal Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edakkal_Caves) have 6000-year-old rock engravings from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) age. The recorded history of this district is available only from the 18th century. In ancient times, this land was ruled by the Rajas of the Veda tribe.[^cite_note-28]

### Ezhimala kingdom

Main article: [Ezhimala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezhimala)

See also: [Mushika dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushika_dynasty)

In the earliest part of the recorded history of Wayanad District, Kasaragod-Kannur-Wayanad-Kozhikode Districts in the northern part of present-day Kerala were ruled by the Nannans ([Mushika dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushika_dynasty)) who later came to be known as the [Kolathiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathiri).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Politically the area was part of the Ezhimala Kingdom, with its capital at Ezhimala in present-day Kannur district. The most famous king of Ezhimala was Nannan, whose kingdom extended up to [Gudalur, Nilgiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudalur,_Nilgiris) and northern parts of [Coimbatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbatore). It is said that Nannan took refuge in the Wayanad hills in the 5th century CE when he was lost to [Cheras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chera_dynasty), just before his death in battle, according to the [Sangam works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangam_literature).[^cite_note-wnd-19] Wayanad was part of the Karkanad, which included the eastern regions of the Ezhimala kingdom (Wayanad-[Gudalur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudalur,_Nilgiris) areas including part of [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu) (Coorg)).[^cite_note-wnd-19] Karkanad along with Poozhinadu, which contained much of the coastal belt wedged between Mangalore and Kozhikode was under Ezhimala kingdom with a headquarters at Ezhimala.

Some linguists say that an inscription found in the [Edakkal Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edakkal_Caves) in Wayanad, from the 3rd century CE (approximately 1,800 years old), is the oldest known inscription in [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam), as it contains two modern Malayalam words, *Ee* (this) and *pazhama* (old). Historian [M. R. Raghava Varier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._R._Raghava_Varier), a specialist in Edakkal cave inscriptions, stated that this would be a 'dangerous interpretation of the script'.[^cite_note-anitquity-31] Varier, who discovered the inscription read it as 'Sri Vazhumi' meaning 'Sri Brahma' in Tamil and dated it to 3rd–4th century CE, whereas Vedachalam, read it as 'Vazhumi' and dated it to 5th–6th century CE.[^cite_note-32]

### Kolathunadu

Main article: [Kolathunadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathunadu)

The [Mooshaka kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushika_dynasty) were considered descendants of Nannan. By the 14th century, [Mooshaka Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mooshika_dynasty) was known as Kolathirinad and its rulers as [Kolathiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathiri). The Kolathunad Kingdom at the peak of its power reportedly extended from the [Netravati River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netravati_River) (Mangalore) in the north[^cite_note-Kola_Swaroopam-33] to Korapuzha (Kozhikode) in the south with Arabian Sea on the west and [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu) hills on the eastern boundary, also including the isolated islands of [Lakshadweep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep) in Arabian Sea.[^cite_note-Census_Handbook-34]

### Kingdom of Kottayam

See also: [Kingdom of Kottayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kottayam)

The [Kolathiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathiri) Dominion emerged into independent 10 principalities i.e., [Kadathanadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadathanadu) ([Vadakara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadakara)), [Randathara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmadom) or Poyanad ([Dharmadom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmadom)), [Kottayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kottayam) ([Thalassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassery)), [Nileshwaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nileshwaram), Iruvazhinadu ([Panoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panoor_\(Municipality\))), [Kurumbranad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurumbranad) etc., under separate royal chieftains due to the outcome of internal dissensions.[^cite_note-wnd-19][^cite_note-Logan-35] The [Nileshwaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nileshwaram) dynasty on the northernmost part of [Kolathiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathiri) dominion, were relatives to both [Kolathunadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathunadu) as well as [Zamorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamorin) of [Calicut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calicut), in the early medieval period.[^cite_note-Neeleswaram_fete-36]

The origin of [Kottayam royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kottayam) (the Kottayam referred here is [Kottayam-Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kottayam-Malabar) near [Thalassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassery), not to be confused with [Kottayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kottayam) in Southern Kerala) is lost in obscurity. It has been stated that the Raja of Kottayam set up a semi-independent principality of his own at the expense of Kolathiris. In the 10th century CE, the region comprised erstwhile Taluks of Kottayam, Wayanad and Gudallur was called *Puraikizhanad* and its feudal lord *Puraikizhars*. The [Thirunelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirunelly) inscriptions refer to the division of Puraikizhar family into two branches viz., Elder (*Muthukur*) and Younger (*Elamkur*) in the beginning of the 11th century. In the 17th century [Kottayam-Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kottayam-Malabar) was the capital of Puraikizhanad (Puranattukara) Rajas. It was divided into three branches i.e., Eastern, Western and Southern under separate dignitaries known as Mootha, Elaya and Munnarkur Rajas. The Kottayam Rajas extended their influence up to the border of [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu). By the end of the 17th century, they shared the area of [Thalassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassery) taluk with the Iruvazhinadu Nambiars and were in possession of North Wayanad and the small Village of [Thamarassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamarassery) which formed the Eastern portion of the present Vadakara, Quilandy and Thamarassery taluks.[^cite_note-wnd-19]

Thamarassery pass which connects Wayanad with the city of [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode) was laid in the 18th century by [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan), the ruler of [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore). In 930 AD, emperor Erayappa of [Ganga dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ganga_dynasty) led his troops to south west of Mysore and after conquering, called it *Bayalnad* meaning the land of swamps. After Erayappa, his sons Rachamalla and Battunga fought each other for the new kingdom of their father's legacy. Rachamalla was killed and Battunga became the undisputed ruler of Bayalnad. In the 12th century CE, Gangas were dethroned from Bayalnad by [Kadamba dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_dynasty)[^cite_note-37] of North Canara.[^cite_note-38] In 1104 CE, Vishnuvardhana of [Hoysala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala) invaded Bayalnad followed by [Vijayanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara) dynasty in the 16th century. In 1610 CE, Udaiyar Raja Wadiyar of Mysore drove out Vijayanagara General and became the ruler of Bayalnad and the Nilgiris. This Bayalnad is the native Kannada name from which Wayanad, its Malayali version, the present name of the district, is derived.

### The Early Kadambas

Main article: [Kadamba dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_dynasty)

Historian Sanu Kainikara states that with the end of the [Sangam period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangam_period), the 4th and 5th centuries brought trouble for the [Cheras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chera_dynasty) (who ruled entire Kerala and Kanyakumari District and adjoining areas), in that they lost control over some parts of Kerala which included Wayanad District due to the growing Kadamba power and superiority. This is indicated by the Kadamba inscriptions in [Edakal caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edakkal_Caves) of Wayanad.

A contemporary [Buddhist](3267529.md) work claims that the [Kalabhra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalabhra_dynasty) king Achuta Vikkanta defeated the 3 traditional southern dynasties – Pandya, Chera, and Chola, and even held all their three kings captive. For nearly five centuries, from 5th–10th century CE, the Cheras were reduced to the status of insignificant rulers due to their inability to avoid foreign invasions. They barely clung on to power with very minimal territory and had to survive at the mercy of their northern powerful imperial empires from Karnataka like the [Kadambas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_dynasty), the [Badami Chalukyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty), the [Rashtrakutas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty) and the [Kalyani Chalukyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_Empire), who invaded and moved through their realm as and when they pleased.[^cite_note-\:2-39]

### The Kutumbiyas (Kudumbiyas)



Thamarassery Churam

The two caves of Ampukuthimala (Edakal Caves) in [Sulthan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulthan_Bathery), with pictures on their walls and pictorial writings, speak volumes of a bygone civilisation. At the foot of the Edakal Male (hill) caves, [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) inscriptions belonging to Canarese chieftain Vishnu Varma of Kutumbiya (Kudumbiya) clan of Mysore dating to c. 5th century CE were discovered which read – *Palapulitaanamtakaari* (or *Pala pulinânam ta-kâri*), *Sri Vishnu Varma Kutumbiya Kulavardhanasya li..it..a..* As per Hultzch, a Chennai epigraphist, it speaks of the glorious descendant of Kutumbiya clan, Kannada chieftain, Vishnu Varma, as one who killed many tigers.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

### The Badami Chalukyas

Main article: [Chalukya dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty)

The inscriptions of the [Badami Chalukyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty) under their founding emperor [Pulakeshin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulakeshin_I) (reign c. 540-567 CE), claim to have defeated the Cheras and the [Ezhil Malai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezhimala_\(hill,_Kannur\)) rulers, which could be a [Pandya dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya_dynasty) reference, and also annexed the entire [Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_District) region to their empire. The [Pandya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya_dynasty), [Chera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chera_dynasty) and [Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty) powers strategised and combinedly attacked the Chalukyas as an alliance in order to avoid repeated reversals.

However, the confederacy was defeated and the Chera king was forced to pay a heavier price, in tribute and indemnity, than his two allies for their misadventure as the Chalukyas had gotten to know the identities of the persons responsible for forming the confederacy.

### The (Western) Gangas

Main article: [Western Ganga dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ganga_dynasty)

The recorded history of the Wayanad district exists only from the 10th century onward. In 930 CE, emperor Erayappa of Ganga dynasty led his troops to south west of Mysore and after conquering, called it Bayalnad meaning the land of swamps. After Erayappa, his sons Rachamalla and Battunga fought each other for the new kingdom of their father's legacy. Rachamalla was killed and Battunga became the undisputed ruler of Bayalnad.[^cite_note-\:1-43]

### The Later Kadambas

Main article: [Kadamba dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_dynasty)

In the 11th century AD, Gangas were dethroned from Bayalnad by [Kadamba dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_dynasty)[^cite_note-44] of North Canara.[^cite_note-45] Wayanad, called Bayalnad (Kannada) since beginning, was at that time divided into two portions – Bira Bayalnad and Chagi Bayalnad. One of the Mysore inscriptions (alluding perhaps to the treacherous beauty of the country, which attracted the stranger and then laid him low with malaria) says *"an adulteress with black waving curls, as adulteress with full-moon face, an adulteress with endless side-glances, an adulteress with adorned slim figure was this storeyed mansion, the double Bayalnad"*.[^cite_note-46]

Kadamba Bayalnad rule emerged in the 11th century under the chief Raviyammarasa with Kirttipura, Punnad, as its capital. Kanthirava (1090 CE) was described as ruling Chagi-Bayalnad. Iravi-Challamma (1108 CE) was the ruler of Bira-Bayalnad.[^cite_note-\:10-47]

### The Western Chalukyas (Kalyani Chalukyas)

Main article: [Western Chalukya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_Empire)

Under emperor [Tailapa II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailapa_II) (973–997 CE) many Jain Basthis were built in south India. The Jain centres and agricultural villages of Wayanad came in to existence during this time. There are many proofs, which justify the existence of Jainism in Wayanad.[^cite_note-48]

### The Hoysalas

Main article: [Hoysala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala_Empire)

In 1104 CE Vishnuvardhana of [Hoysala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala) invaded Bayalnad and Nilgiris and annexed them followed by [Vijayanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara) dynasty in the 14th century. A Kadamba king, Mukkanna-Kadamba ruled Bayal-nad in and around 1138 CE.[^cite_note-49]

### The Vijayanagara empire

Main article: [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire)

A feudatory chieftain of Sangama dynasty of Vijaynagar, Immadi Kadamba Raya Vodeyayya of Bayalnad Kadambas, is said to have ruled Bayalnad.[^cite_note-\:10-47]

### The Mysore Wodeyars and the Sultans

Main article: [Kingdom of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore)

In 1610 CE Udaiyar Raja Wadiyar of Mysore drove out Vijayanagara General and became the ruler of Bayalnad and the Nilgiris. Bayalnad is the present Wayanad.

An inscription discovered from a Jain Basti at Varadur near Panamaram dated to Saka era 1606 which is 1684 CE, shows that Jainism was still very powerful even in the 17th century. The inscription which was noticed by noted epigraphist and historian M. R. Raghava Varriar, has reference about the earliest Jain settlements and temples in Wayanad. The copper plate inscription which was placed under a water fountain at Varadur Ananthanatha Swami temple deals with the grant of various ritual materials to the Jain Basathis of Wayanad by Lalithappa, the younger son of Bommarasa of the Karkala Aremane Basathi.

The Kannada inscription dated Sakavarsha 1606 (i.e., 1684 CE) Rakthakshi Samvatsara Jeshtabahula Shukravara reads\:Karkala aremane basthiya bommarasanu mommaga lalithappanu devapooje chinna belli thamra kanchu upakaranagalu madisi kotta bibara.

The Jain Chaithyalayas or temples referred in the inscription are Arepathra, Bennegodu, Palagondu, Hanneradubeedhi, Puthangadi (Muthangadi) and Hosangadi. The Arepathra Chaityalaya is not yet identified. It is believed that it was at the hilltop near to the Panamaram river. The other Kannada touch place names are identified with their present Malayalam version names as Venniyode, Palukunnu, Sultan Batheri, Puthangadi and Mananthavady respectively.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Raghava Varriar says that there were seven Jain centres in Wayanad viz. Manikyapuri, Ksheerapuri, Kalpathi, Vennayode, Palagondu, Hosangadi and Hanneradubeedhi. It is believed that Manikyapuri was at the present day Manichira. The location of Ksheerapuri is not yet identified. Kalpathi may be Kalpetta, the present day Wayanad district headquarters, Anjukunnu was then Hanjugondu, etc.[^cite_note-52]

When Wayanad was under [Hyder Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyder_Ali)'s rule, the ghat road from Vythiri to Thamarassery was constructed.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-53] Then the British rulers developed this route to Carter road.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-54] When Wayanad was under Tipu Sultan's rule British invasion started. Tussle and turbulent times followed. The British claimed Wayanad under the 1792 treaty of [Srirangapatna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srirangapatna) citing it was part of Malabar. [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan) went in appeal before the [governor general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_general). Considering his arguments, relying on the successive Karnataka rule for centuries in Wayanad and its geographical detachment from Malabar, in 1798, Governor General [Lord Mornington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mornington) declared by proclamation[^cite_note-55] that Wayanad had not been ceded to the East India Company by the treaty of 1792. Consequently, the British troops withdrew from Wayanad conceding to Tipu's rule.

### Post-Independence

Wayanad eventually became part of Kerala despite its geographical delimitations and political dissent in 1956 on State's reorganisation.[^cite_note-57] Even now there is a considerable Kannada speaking population and the reminiscence of centuries old Karnataka rule is omnipresent in Wayanad. Agriculture Cultivation started broadly after 1900 A.D onwards. The British authorities opened up the plateau to cultivation of tea and other cash crops by constructing roads across the dangerous slopes of Wayanad, to [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode) and [Thalassery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassery). Later, they extended these new roads to the cities of [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore) and [Ooty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ooty) through Gudalur. Settlers emigrated from all parts of Kerala and the fecund lands proved a veritable goldmine with incredible yields of cash crops.

When the State of Kerala came into being in November 1956, Wayanad was part of [Kannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannur) district. Later, south Wayanad was added to Kozhikode district. To fulfil the aspirations of the people of Wayanad for development, North Wayanad and South Wayanad were carved out and joined to form the present district of Wayanad. This district came into being on 1 November 1980 as one of the twelve districts of Kerala,[^cite_note-wayanad-58] consisting of three [taluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taluk); [Vythiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vythiri), [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), and [Sulthan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulthan_Bathery).

In 2024, [landslides in Wayanad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wayanad_landslides) killed at least 336 people and 78 are missing.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-65] Many of the people killed were workers in the farms, or were members of their families.[^cite_note-66]

## Geography

Wayanad district stands on the southern tip of the Deccan Plateau and includes part of the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats). The western parts of the district bordering [Kozhikode district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode_district) consists of the Western Ghats covered with dense forest. The district forms a part of the south western [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau), and is sloped to the east. Quite a large area of the district is covered by forest but the continued and indiscriminate exploitation of the [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource) point towards an imminent[^cite_note-ingentaconnect-67] [environmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment) crisis. There are a lot of trekking points in this district. Chembra Peak (2,100m) is the highest peak in the Wayanad district. [Banasura Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banasura_Hill?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2,079m) in [Padinjarathara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padinjarathara) is also similar to height of [Chembra Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chembra_Peak). Brahmagiri Hill is also another trekking point in Wayanad.

Wayanad district is bounded by protected area network of different biological reserves such as [Malabar Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_Wildlife_Sanctuary) in the West, [Mudumalai National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudumalai_National_Park) in the South, [Bandipur National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandipur_National_Park) in the East, [Nagarhole National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarhole_National_Park) in the North East, [Brahmagiri Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmagiri_Wildlife_Sanctuary) in the North and [Aralam Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aralam_Wildlife_Sanctuary) in North West which is linked with [Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayanad_Wildlife_Sanctuary).

The district has rich water resources. There are east flowing and west flowing rivers in the region. One of the major rivers in the district is [Kabini River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabini_River), a tributary of River [Kaveri](177299.md); it is also one of the only three east flowing rivers in Kerala. Kabani has many tributaries including Thirunelli River, Panamaram River and Mananthavady River. All these rivulets help form a rich water resource as well as a distinct landscape for the district. Various streams flow into the Panamaram rivulet while it passes through the mountain gorges and finally the river falls down into Panamaram Valley. After flowing through the district for a certain distance, River Panamaram joins Mananthavady River, which originates from the lower regions of the peak called 'Thondarmudi'.[^cite_note-68]

### Banasura Sagar Dam

[Banasura Sagar Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banasura_Sagar_Dam) in [Padinjarathara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padinjarathara) across the Karamanathodu River, a tributary of River Kabini, m, is considered to be the largest earth dam in India and the second largest in Asia. The dam is ideally placed in the foothills of Banasura hills, which got its name from 'Banasura', the son of King Mahabali, the famous ruler of Kerala. The dam here was constructed on behalf of the Banasurasagar project in 1979, to support the Kakkayam Hydroelectric power project and to meet the water demand for irrigation and drinking purposes. The dam, located around 2 km away from Padinjarathara is a tourist destination in Wayanad. Banasura dam is made up of massive stacks of stones and boulders.[^cite_note-69]

[Karapuzha Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karapuzha_Dam) has been constructed on the Karapuzha River, a tributary of the Kabini River. Vazhavatta in Vythiri taluk of Wynad district for providing irrigation to an area of 5580 ha (CCA) say 5600 hectare in Vythiri and Sultan Bathery taluks of Wynad district of Kerala. The reservoir has a gross storage capacity of 76.50 M Cum and live storage capacity of 72.00 M Cum.[^cite_note-70]

### Flora and fauna

The soil and climate of Wayanad are suitable for horticulture on a commercial basis. For promoting the cultivation of vegetables and establishing orchards, the Kerala Agricultural University is running a regional Agricultural Research Station at Ambalavayal.

Elephant, bear and other wild animals from the neighbouring wild life sanctuaries of Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, stray into the Begur forest range and the forests around Muthanga, which is 20 kilometres away from the town of Sultan Bathery.

[Franky's narrow-mouthed frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franky's_narrow-mouthed_frog) was recently discovered in Wayanad district.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] The [Wayanad laughingthrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayanad_laughingthrush) is named after this region, but unlike other South Indian laughingthrushes, has a wide distribution through the Western Ghats.

### Tribes in Wayanad

The Wayanad have the largest tribal population in Kerala with 8 scheduled tribes including Adiyan, Paniyan, Mullukkurman, Kurichyan, Vettakkuruman, Wayanad Kadar, Kattuniakkan and Thachaanadan Mooppan. These communities have a number of symbolic oral narratives[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**[Adiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiyan)**: Adiyans are a matrilineal Tribal group, who were treated as bonded slave labourers by the landlords up to 1976. Now the majority of them are agricultural labourers and some of them are marginal agriculturalist. They spoke Adiya language as their mother tongue.[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Kattuniakkan**: A particularly Vulnerable Tribal group of Wayanad. Jenu Kurumban and Ten Kurumban are the synonyms used for the Kattunayakan community. They spoke Kattunaikka language a dialect of Kannada and Malayalam. They are patrilineal and a forest dwelling, hunting and gathering community. This is the largest population among PVTG in Kerala with a total population of 19995 (Male- 9953, Female-10042)[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Kurichyan**: They are the second largest community among Scheduled Tribes with a total population of 35909 (Male- 18129, Female-17780).[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Mullukkurman**: A patrilineal and patrilocal tribal agriculturalist community found in Wayanad. The community members are expert in hunting and their spoken language is Mullukkuruma language. The total population is 21375 (Male- 10625, female-10750).[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Paniyan**: A patrilineal slave tribe community until the 1976 Bonded Labour (Abolition) Act,[^cite_note-86] distributed in Wayanad, [Kannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannur), [Kozhikode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode) and [Malappuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malappuram). They are the largest single tribal community with a population of 92787 (Male-45112, female- 47675). Their language is a dialect known as Paniya Language and nowadays they are agricultural labourers.[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Thachaanadan Mooppan**: Thachaanadan Mooppan is a matrilineal community. In earlier days they were shifting cultivators and hunters. Nowadays they earn through agricultural labour work. Known for their expertise in carpentry and basket making, their total population is 1649, and consists of 814 males and 835 females.[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Vettakkuruman**: Vettakkuruman also a patrilineal tribal community. Their language is known as Bettakkuruma language. The population of Vettakuruman is 6482 consisting of 3193 males and 3289 females.[^cite_note-tribals-85]

**Wayanad [Kadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadar_people)**: They are found in Kozhikkode and Wayanad district and entirely a different generic stock from Kaders of Cochin. They are matrilineal marginal tribes with a population of 673, consisting of 348 males and 325 females.[^cite_note-tribals-85]

#### Revenue administration

District administration, Collectorate, is headquartered at [Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta). The district administration is headed by a [District Collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Collector), who is an [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) officer. For the purpose of revenue administration, Wayanad district is divided into revenue divisions, taluks, and revenue villages. The Revenue Divisional Office is at [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), which is headed by a [Sub Collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-divisional_magistrate) or Revenue Divisional Officer (RDO), who is also the [Sub-divisional magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-divisional_magistrate). There are 3 taluks in total: [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), [Vythiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vythiri), and [Sulthan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bathery). Each taluk office is headed by a [Tehsildar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsildar), who is also the executive magistrate of that [taluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil). These three taluks contain 49 revenue villages under their jurisdiction. These [revenue villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_Village) is further divided into amsoms and desams for revenue purpose.

#### Police administration

Wayanad District Police falls under Kannur Range which comes under North Zone.

The Wayanad district police is headquartered in [Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta). It is headed by a District Police Chief (DPC), an [IPS](451884.md) officer of the Kerala cadre, holding the rank of [Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_police_\(India\)) (SP). For the maintenance of law and order, the Wayanad police district is divided into 3 police subdivisions: [Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta), [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), and [Sultan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bathery), with 17 police stations in total. Each subdivision is headed by a [Deputy Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_superintendent_of_police) (DYSP) and consists of several police stations under its jurisdiction. Each police station is headed by an [Inspector of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector#India) or sub-inspector designated as the [station house officer](41402072.md) (SHO).[^cite_note-88]

Other special units/cells include the District Special Branch, District Crime Branch, District Crime Records Bureau, and Narcotic Cell, each headed by a Deputy Superintendent of Police (Dy.SP).

### Local self-governments

#### Panchayats

For the purpose of rural governance, the Wayanad District Panchayat functions at the [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District#India) level, headquartered in [Kalpatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta). Wayanad district panchayat is governed by elected council, headed by president and vice-president. The district panchayat consists of 16 divisions, each represented by an elected member. The [district panchayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_council_\(India\)) is at the helm for the development of rural areas in the district.

There are 4 [Block Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayat_samiti) for [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_development_block)-level governance and 23 [Gram Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Kerala) for village or group-of-village-level governance. These panchayats are governed by elected councils, headed by presidents and vice-presidents respectively. The block panchayats/block development offices are [Kalpatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta), [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), [Panamaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamaram), [Sulthan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bathery).[^cite_note-90]

#### Municipalities

For urban governance, there are three municipalities for the major towns: [Kalpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpetta), [Mananthavady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananthavady), and [Sultan Bathery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bathery). These municipalities are governed by the elected municipal councils, headed by a chairperson and vice chairperson.

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourist attractions in Wayanad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_attractions_in_Wayanad)

The District has more than 20 destinations. [District Tourism Promotion Councils (DTPC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Tourism_Promotion_Councils_\(DTPC\)) of Wayanad that functions under the Department of Tourism, Government of Kerala is responsible for all tourism related activities in the district.[^cite_note-92]

Wayanad is home to the [Wayanad Literature Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayanad_Literature_Festival), the largest rurally held literature festival in India. It sees a host of authors, journalists and activists in each edition, drawing people in from across the country.
