The **House of Assembly of Barbados** is the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) of the [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) [Parliament of Barbados](2555282.md). It has 30 [Members of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_Parliament) (MPs), who are directly elected in single member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) using the simple-majority (or [first-past-the-post](261709.md)) system for a term of five years. The House of Assembly sits roughly 40–45 days a year and is presided over by a [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_House_of_Assembly_of_Barbados). If the Speaker elected by the Assembly is not an MP currently in the House of Assembly, that Speaker becomes the 31st member of the Assembly, having a vote on motions that are tied.[^cite_note-HistoricSpeaker-1][^cite_note-HolderSpeaker-2]

The Barbadian House of Assembly chamber is located in the east-wing of The Public Buildings on Broad Street, in [Bridgetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgetown), Barbados.

## History

Main article: [Parliament of Barbados § History](2555282.md#History)

The genesis of a legislature in Barbados was introduced by Governor [Henry Hawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hawley), creating a structure of governance to Barbados, itself patterned after the [Parliament of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England)). The then unicameral Parliament originally was tasked with establishing a system of laws and was completely under the domination of the island's planter-class. The first meeting of the Barbados Assembly was held on 22 June 1639 making it the third oldest legislature in the [Americas](29833.md) (behind the [Virginia General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_General_Assembly) and [Bermuda House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_House_of_Assembly)), and is among the oldest in the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Oath of affirmation

Under section 59 of the constitution, before entering upon the functions of his office, the MPs must take the [oath of allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_allegiance) to Barbados.

## Next election

The [Constitution of Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Barbados) reads, in part:

- 61(3) "...Parliament, unless sooner dissolved, shall continue for five years from the date of its first sitting after any dissolution and shall then stand dissolved."

- 62(1) "After every dissolution of Parliament the Governor General \[since 30 November 2021: [President](68909900.md)] shall issue writs for a general election of members of appointment the House of Assembly returnable within ninety days from that dissolution."

  [^cite_note-6]

The next election is therefore due to be held in 2031, but can be held sooner if the [President of Barbados](68909900.md) so directs.

## Previous elections

Main article: [Elections in Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Barbados)

In previous elections the [National Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Party_\(Barbados\)) (NDP), the [Barbados National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados_National_Party) (BNP) (which previously was known as the Voter's Association, Barbados Electors Association and the Progressive Conservative Party in prior years), the [West Indian National Congress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_National_Congress_Party) (WINCP) and Independents also won seats besides the two big parties – the [Barbados Labour Party](408395.md) (BLP) (earlier known as the Barbados Progressive League (BPL)) and the [Democratic Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Labour_Party_\(Barbados\)) (DLP). The DLP had been in opposition since 6 September 1994. Fourteen years later when they won a surprise victory of 20 seats to 10 on 15 January 2008, DLP Leader [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_\(Barbadian_politician\)) was sworn in as the [6th Prime Minister of Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Barbados). Freundel Stuart was sworn in on 23 October 2010 because of the death of Prime Minister Thompson, who had been diagnosed with pancreatic cancer in March 2010. Two months later in May he became ill when the then acting prime minister Freundel Stuart had started, and five months later he was sworn in on 23 October as the 7th prime minister of Barbados. On 23 February 2013 he was sworn in as the 7th prime minister of Barbados by obtaining sixteen out of thirty seats. The general elections of 21 February were one of the closest elections Barbados has ever seen. On 24 May 2018, the BLP returned to power under [Mia Mottley](15337866.md) with a historic landslide victory that saw them win all 30 seats in parliament and Mottley becoming the first female prime minister and the 8th prime minister overall.[^cite_note-8] This occurred once again on the 19 January 2022 when the Mottley Administration won all 30 seats again in another landslide victory.[^cite_note-9] In February 2026, prime minister Mia Mottley won her third consecutive [election](71208378.md) victory by landslide, meaning her Barbados Labour party won again all the 30 seats in the House of Assembly.[^cite_note-10]
