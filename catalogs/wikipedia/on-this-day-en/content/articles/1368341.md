# Candidates Tournament

For the most recent Candidates Tournament, see [Candidates Tournament 2026](75933820.md).

The **Candidates Tournament** (or in some periods **Candidates Matches**) is a [chess](5134.md) tournament organized by [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE), chess's international governing body, since 1950, as the final contest to determine the challenger for the [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship). The winner of the Candidates earns the right to a match for the World Championship against the incumbent world champion.

Before 1993 it was contested triennially; almost always held every third year from 1950 to 1992 inclusive. After the split of the World Championship in the early 1990s, the cycles were disrupted, even after the reunification of the titles in 2006. Since 2013 it has settled into a 2-year cycle: qualification for Candidates during the odd-numbered year, Candidates played early in the even-numbered year, and the World Championship match played late in the even-numbered year. The latter half of the 2020 [Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2020) was suspended due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and was only played in April 2021.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-FIDEfeb16-2] The subsequent tournament, the [2022 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Candidates_Tournament), took place as scheduled in 2022.[^cite_note-3]

## Precursors

Before 1950, the champion had the right to handpick a challenger. However, a number of tournaments acted as de facto candidates tournaments:

- The [London 1883 chess tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_1883_chess_tournament) established [Johannes Zukertort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Zukertort) and [Wilhelm Steinitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Steinitz) as the best two players in the world, and was one of the important events leading to the [first official world championship match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1886) between the two, in 1886. Steinitz won, making him the first official world champion.

- The [Saint Petersburg 1895–96 chess tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_1895%E2%80%9396_chess_tournament), in which world champion [Emanuel Lasker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Lasker) finished first and Steinitz finished second, led to Steinitz gaining support for an [1897 rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1897),

  [^cite_note-4]

  &#x20;which Lasker won.

- The [AVRO 1938 chess tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVRO_1938_chess_tournament) was held partly to choose a challenger for [Alexander Alekhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Alekhine).

  [^cite_note-5]

  &#x20;[Paul Keres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Keres) won on tie-breaks, but [World War II](32927.md) prevented the match from happening.

## Organization



[Candidates Tournament 1956 Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_1956_chess_tournament): 10 players

The number of players in the tournament varied over the years, between eight and fifteen players. Most of these qualified from [Interzonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interzonal) tournaments, though some gained direct entry without having to play the Interzonal.

The first Interzonal/Candidates World Championship cycle began in 1948. Before 1965, the tournament was organized in a [round-robin](939742.md) format. From 1965 on, the tournament was played as [knockout matches](939680.md), spread over several months. In 1995–1996, the defending FIDE champion ([Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov)) also entered the Candidates, in the third round (Candidates final).

During its 1993 to 2006 split from FIDE, the "Classical" World Championship also held three Candidates Tournaments (in 1994–1995, 1998 and 2002) under a different sponsor and a different format each time. In one of these cases ([Alexei Shirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Shirov) in 1998) no title match eventuated, under disputed circumstances (see [Classical World Chess Championship 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_World_Chess_Championship_2000)).

After the [reunification of titles in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2006), FIDE tried different Candidates formats in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2007), [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2010) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2012), before settling on an 8 player, double round robin Candidates tournament from [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2013) onwards.

## Results of Candidates Tournaments

The tables below show the qualifiers and results for all interzonal, Candidates and world championship tournaments.

- Players shown bracketed in italics (*Bondarevsky, Euwe, Fine and Reshevsky* in 1950, *Botvinnik* in 1965, *Fischer* in 1977, *Carlsen* in 2011 and 2024, and *Radjabov* in 2020) qualified for the Candidates or were seeded in the Candidates, but did not play.

- Players shown in italics with an asterisk (*Stein\** in 1962 and again in 1965, and *Bronstein\** in 1965) were excluded from the Candidates by a rule limiting the number of players from one country.

- *Karjakin\** in 2022 was disqualified by FIDE after his qualification for the Candidates: the FIDE Ethics and Disciplinary Commission ruled that he breached Article 2.2.10 of the FIDE Code of Ethics after he made public comments approving of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). He is shown bracketed, in italics, and with an asterisk.

- Players listed *after* players in italics (Flohr in 1950, Benko in 1962, Geller, Ivkov and Portisch in 1965, Spassky in 1977, Grischuk in 2011, Vachier-Lagrave in 2020, Ding in 2022, and Abasov in 2024) only qualified due to the non-participation (withdrawal) of the bracketed players or players with an asterisk.

- Incumbent champions' names are struck through when they refused to defend their title (Fischer in 1975 and Carlsen in 2023).

Normally, the incumbent champion is seeded directly into the final against the challenger (who had to pass through the Candidates qualification), but there have been exceptions:

- The [World Chess Championship 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1948), in which five players were seeded into the championship tournament (the previous champion, [Alexander Alekhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Alekhine), having died in 1946). A sixth player, Fine, was also seeded into the championship tournament but chose not to play; he is shown in brackets.

- The [FIDE World Chess Championship 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_1996), in which the FIDE World Champion Anatoly Karpov was seeded in the Candidates final.

- The [Classical World Chess Championship 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_World_Chess_Championship_2000), in which two players were seeded into the championship final (one of them being incumbent champion Kasparov), and there were no previous qualifying stages. In this way, it resembled the pre-1946 events, in which the champion could handpick a challenger.

- The FIDE championships of 1999–2004 (during the split-title period), in which the incumbent champion had no special privileges.

- The [FIDE World Chess Championship 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_2005), in which eight players (including incumbent FIDE champion Kasimdzhanov) were seeded into the final championship tournament.

- The [FIDE World Chess Championship 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_2007), in which four players (including incumbent champion Kramnik) were seeded into the final championship tournament.

The incumbent champion [Bobby Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Fischer) refused to defend his title at the [World Chess Championship 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1975), and his challenger [Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov) won by forfeit. (At the time, the Candidates was a knock-out event, so the 1974 Karpov–Korchnoi Candidates final match – a best of 24 games, like world championships in the period 1951–1972 and 1985–1993 – arguably became a *de facto* world championship in retrospect.) [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen) refused to defend his title at the [World Chess Championship 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2023) and was replaced by the runner-up of the Candidates Tournament, [Ding Liren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding_Liren).

## Reunified title (since 2006)

After the reunification of the FIDE and "classical" titles, the [Chess World Cup](17269063.md) and [FIDE Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Grand_Prix) series were introduced as qualification for the Candidates Tournament. The [Swiss-system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss-system_tournament) FIDE Grand Swiss was introduced in the latter half of 2019, acting as another qualification path for the 2020 Candidates Tournament.[^cite_note-Grand_Swiss-39]
