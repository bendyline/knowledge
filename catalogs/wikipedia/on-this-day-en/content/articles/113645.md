**Guadalcanal** ([/ˌɡwɑːdəlkəˈnæl/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); indigenous name: *Isatabu*) is the principal island in the [Guadalcanal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_Province) of [Solomon Islands](265083.md), located in the southwestern [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), northeast of [Australia](4689264.md). It is the largest island in the nation of Solomon Islands by both area and population, followed by [Malaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaita). It is the second largest island in [the Solomon Islands archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands_\(archipelago\)) by area after [Bougainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bougainville_Island). The island is mainly covered in dense [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) and has a mountainous [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterland).



[Honiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honiara) is the largest city of Guadalcanal and the capital of [Solomon Islands](265083.md).



Detailed map of Guadalcanal

Archaeological sites show habitation on the island since 4500–2500 BC. The [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) [Lapita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapita_culture) peoples migrated to the island about 1200-800 BC. Guadalcanal was first charted by Europeans during the Spanish expedition of [Álvaro de Mendaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_de_Menda%C3%B1a_de_Neira) in 1568. The name comes from the village of [Guadalcanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal,_Seville), in the province of [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_\(province\)), in [Andalusia](2736.md), Spain, birthplace of Pedro de Ortega Valencia, a member of Mendaña's expedition.

During 1942 and 1943, it was the scene of the [Guadalcanal campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_campaign) and saw bitter fighting between Japanese and Allied troops. The Allied troops were ultimately victorious. At the end of [World War II](32927.md), [Honiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honiara), on the north coast of Guadalcanal, became the new capital of the [British Solomon Islands Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Solomon_Islands) and later the capital of the independent nation of Solomon Islands.

## Geography

Guadalcanal is the largest island in [Solomon Islands](265083.md), with a total land area of 5,302 km<sup>2</sup> (2,047 sq mi), and has a population of 155,605 people, making it the second-most populous island in the country after Malaita. [Mount Popomanaseu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Popomanaseu) is the island's highest point and the highest in Solomon Islands, with an elevation of 7,661 ft (2,335 m) above sea level. The Mbokokimbo River is the island's longest river, with a total length of 98.7 km (61.3 mi).

## History

### Early history

The island has been settled since at least 4500–2500 BC based on archaeological finds at Poha Cave and Vatuluma Posovi.[^cite_note-Review-2][^cite_note-3] During the period 1200-800 BC, Austronesian [Lapita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapita) peoples settled the islands.[^cite_note-Review-2]

A [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) expedition from [Peru](170691.md) in 1568 under the command of [Álvaro de Mendaña de Neira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_de_Menda%C3%B1a_de_Neira) were the first Europeans to see the island. Mendaña's subordinate, Pedro de Ortega Valencia, named the island after his home town [Guadalcanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal,_Spain) in Andalusia, Spain.[^cite_note-Guadalcanal-4] In the years that followed the discovery, the island was variously referred to as Guadarcana, Guarcana, Guadalcana, and Guadalcanar, which reflected different pronunciations of its name in Andalusian Spanish.

European settlers, whalers, and missionaries began to arrive in the 18th and 19th centuries. With these outsiders also arrived foreign institutions such as forced labour. Beginning during the 1860s, about 60,000 natives from many parts of the Solomon Islands were indentured and sent to Australia or Fiji by British authorities to work on plantations. This system continued into the 1890s.[^cite_note-\:0-5] In the 1880s, the Germans and the British vied for control of the Solomons. Germany established a protectorate over the northern Solomons in 1884, while in 1893, the [British Solomon Islands Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Solomon_Islands_Protectorate) was proclaimed, which included the island of Guadalcanal.[^cite_note-Guadalcanal-4][^cite_note-\:0-5] Germany eventually handed over most of their protectorate to Britain, though, in 1899. By the early 20th century, large agricultural plantations (specialising in [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra)), run mainly by Australians, were established in the region. Guadalcanal was not seriously affected by World War I.[^cite_note-\:0-5] In 1932, the British confirmed the name Guadalcanal in line with the town in Andalusia, Spain.

### Second World War

Main article: [Guadalcanal campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_campaign)



Japanese soldiers, killed while assaulting US Marine positions at the mouth of Alligator Creek

In the months following the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) in December 1941, the Japanese drove the Americans out of the [Philippines](23440.md), the British out of [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya), and the Dutch out of the [East Indies](23476997.md). The Japanese then began to expand into the western Pacific, occupying many islands in an attempt to build a defensive ring around their conquests and threaten the lines of communication from the United States to Australia and New Zealand. The Japanese reached Guadalcanal in May 1942.

When an American reconnaissance mission spotted construction of a Japanese airfield at [Lunga Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunga_Point) on the north coast of Guadalcanal, the situation became critical.[^cite_note-6] This new Japanese airfield represented a threat to Australia, so as a matter of urgency, despite not being adequately prepared, the United States conducted its first amphibious landing of the war on Guadalcanal. The initial landings of the [1st Marine Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Marine_Division) on 7 August 1942 secured the airfield with little difficulty, but holding the airfield for the next six months against combined Japanese ground assault, air attack and naval bombardment was one of the most hotly contested campaigns in the entire Pacific theatre of war.

Immediately after landing on the island, U.S. Navy [Seabees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabee) began finishing the airfield begun by the Japanese. It was then named [Henderson Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Field_\(Guadalcanal\)) after a Marine aviator killed in combat during the [Battle of Midway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Midway). Aircraft operating from Henderson Field during the campaign were a mix of U.S. Marine, Army, Navy, and other Allied aircraft that became known as the [Cactus Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus_Air_Force). They defended the airfield and threatened any Japanese ships that ventured too close to the island during daylight hours. At night, however, Japanese naval forces were frequently able to shell the airfield and deliver troops with supplies, retiring before daylight. The Japanese used fast ships, namely destroyers, to conduct this reinforcement and supply effort, which became known as the [Tokyo Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Express). So many ships from both sides were sunk in the many naval engagements in and around the Solomon Island chain that the nearby waters came to be referred to as [Ironbottom Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironbottom_Sound).



Guadalcanal American Memorial

The naval [Battle of Cape Esperance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Esperance) was fought on 11 October 1942 in the waters off the northwest coast of Guadalcanal. During the engagement, the [United States Navy](20518076.md) intercepted and defeated a Japanese formation of ships on their way down "[the Slot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Georgia_Sound)" to reinforce and resupply troops on the island, suffering losses of their own in the process. The multi-day [Naval Battle of Guadalcanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Battle_of_Guadalcanal) in early November marked a critical turning point in the campaign. Allied naval forces engaged a large, experienced Japanese surface force at night and forced it to withdraw. Notably, the [USS Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Washington_\(BB-56\)) sank the [Japanese battleship Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_battleship_Kirishima), becoming the first of only two occasions in the Pacific theatre that two battleships engaged each other in combat, the second being in 1944 during the [Battle of Leyte Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte_Gulf). Some Japanese viewpoints consider these naval engagements, which showcased the improving capability of Allied warships to challenge the Imperial Japanese Navy's significant advantage in night-fighting techniques, to be just as significant as the Battle of Midway in turning the tide against them.

After six months of hard combat in and around Guadalcanal, the Japanese forces on the island were critically undersupplied and no longer combat effective, while Allied troops were steadily increasing in quantity and quality. Remaining Japanese forces on the island were evacuated at [Cape Esperance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Esperance) on the northwest coast in February 1943.[^cite_note-7] American authorities declared Guadalcanal secure on 9 February 1943. The Guadalcanal campaign was a major turning point in the war, as it stopped further Japanese expansion.

Two U.S. Navy ships have been named for the campaign:

- [USS *Guadalcanal* (CVE-60)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Guadalcanal_\(CVE-60\)) was a World War II escort carrier.

- [USS *Guadalcanal* (LPH-7)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Guadalcanal_\(LPH-7\)) was an amphibious assault ship.

To date, the only [U.S. Coast Guardsman](32223.md) recipient of the [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor) is Signalman 1st Class [Douglas Albert Munro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Albert_Munro), awarded posthumously for his extraordinary heroism on 27 September 1942 at [Point Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Cruz). Munro provided covering fire and helped evacuate 500 besieged Marines from a beach at Point Cruz; he was killed during the evacuation. During the [Battle for Henderson Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Henderson_Field), the Medal of Honor was also awarded to [John Basilone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Basilone), who was later killed in action during the [Battle of Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima) in 1945.

After the war, American and Japanese groups repeatedly visited Guadalcanal to search for the remains of missing soldiers. The bodies of some 7,000 Japanese troops remain missing on the island, and bones of Japanese soldiers have been unearthed into the 2010s.[^cite_note-8]

### Postwar years

Immediately after the Second World War, the capital of the British Solomon Islands Protectorate was moved to Honiara on Guadalcanal from its previous location at [Tulagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulagi) in what were then the Florida Islands, now the [Nggela Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nggela_Islands). In 1952, the [high commissioner for the Western Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner_for_the_Western_Pacific) moved from [Fiji](10707.md) to Honiara, and the post was combined with that of the governor of the Solomon Islands Protectorate. The secondary airfield, known as "Fighter Two", is now the local golf course.[^cite_note-9]. On independence in 1978, the British Solomon Islands Protectorate became the nation of [Solomon Islands](265083.md), with its capital established in Honiara, Guadalcanal. The airfield long known as *Henderson Field* that caused the fighting in 1942 sits about eight kilometres east of Honiara and became the [Honiara International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honiara_International_Airport).&#x20;

### Civil war

See also: [History of Solomon Islands § Civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Solomon_Islands#Civil_war)

In early 1999, long-simmering tensions between the local Guale people on Guadalcanal and more recent migrants from the neighbouring island of [Malaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaita) erupted into violence. The Guadalcanal Revolutionary Army, later called [Isatabu Freedom Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isatabu_Freedom_Movement), began terrorising Malaitans in the rural areas of the island in an effort to force them out of their homes. About 20,000 Malaitans fled to the capital, and others returned to their home island; Guale residents of Honiara fled. The city became a Malaitan [enclave](4848945.md) and the [Malaita Eagle Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaita_Eagle_Force) took over government. The [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) and [Royal New Zealand Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy) deployed vessels to the area to protect the expatriate community resident mostly in Honiara. In 2003, the [Pacific Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum) negotiated the intervention of [RAMSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Assistance_Mission_to_Solomon_Islands) or Operation Helpem Fren involving Australia, New Zealand, and other Pacific island nations.

Aircraft in Vilu War Museum

About 25 km (15 mi) from Honiara to the west, Vilu War Museum houses an outdoor collection of remains of various parts of military equipment and of several aircraft. Several memorials for the American, Australian, Fijian, New Zealand, and Japanese soldiers who died were erected, as well.[^cite_note-10]

## Fauna

The island hosts a native [marsupial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsupial) known as the [phalanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanger) or grey cuscus, *[Phalanger orientalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanger_orientalis)*.[^cite_note-11] The only other mammals are bats and rodents.

Many species of colourful parrots are found there, and [estuarine crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile) inhabit the island's shores. In recent times, these crocodiles have been found only on the Weather Coast in the south of the island, but during World War II, they were found along the north coast in the vicinity of the airstrip where the fighting was taking place, as evidenced by names such as [Alligator Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Tenaru).

Venomous snakes are rare on the island and are not considered to be a serious threat, but a kind of [centipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede) there has a particularly nasty bite.[^cite_note-12] The Guadalcanal Watersheds form a site that has been identified by [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International) as an [important bird area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area), because it supports populations of [threatened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened) or [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) bird species. At 376,146 ha (1,452 sq mi), it covers some 70% of the island, extending along the southern coast inland to the central highlands, and contains riverine and lowland [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest), as well as the greatest contiguous area of [cloud forest](58903.md) in the Solomons. Although it also contains gardens and old village sites, most of it has never been permanently inhabited. Significant birds for which the site was identified include [chestnut-bellied imperial pigeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut-bellied_imperial_pigeon), [Woodford's rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodford's_rail), [Guadalcanal moustached kingfishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_moustached_kingfisher), [Meek's lorikeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meek's_lorikeet), [Guadalcanal honeyeaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_honeyeater), [Guadalcanal thicketbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_thicketbird), and [Guadalcanal thrushes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_thrush). Potential threats to the site include [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging) and [invasive species](67554.md).[^cite_note-bli-13]
