**Tehran province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان تهران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Ostān-e Tehrān*) is one of the 31 [provinces of Iran](448160.md). Its capital is the city of [Tehran](57654.md), the national capital of Iran.[^cite_note-Tehran_Province_Structure-7]

Tehran Province covers an area of 18,814 square kilometres (7,264 sq mi) and is located to the north of the [Central Plateau of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Plateau). It was made a part of the First Region with its secretariat located in Tehran, upon the division of the provinces into 5 [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran), solely for coordination and development purposes on June 22, 2014.[^cite_note-hamshahrionline.ir-3]

Tehran Province is situated in north-central Iran, lying on the southern slopes of the [Alborz Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_Mountains). The province’s location within the Alborz range gives it a diverse topography, with high mountains in the north and gradually descending plains toward the south. This geographical setting creates a varied climate, from cool alpine conditions to semi-arid lowlands.[^cite_note-8]

The province of Tehran has over 12 million inhabitants and is Iran's most [densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) region. Approximately 86.5 percent reside in urban areas and 13.5 percent in rural areas of the province.[^cite_note-9]

## History



The [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid) collection of the [National Museum of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Iran) in Tehran

Tehran province has several [archeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeological_site) indicating settlements dating back several thousand years. Until 300 years ago, [Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey,_Iran) was the most prominent of the cities of the province. However, the [city of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Tehran) rose to become the largest city and capital of Iran by 1796, and since then has been the political, cultural, economic, and commercial nucleus of Iran.

Tehran has over 1,500 historical sites of cultural significance registered with the [Cultural Heritage Organization of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Heritage_Organization_of_Iran). The oldest of these in Tehran Province is the remains of two sites in [Firuzkuh County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuzkuh_County) that date back to the fourth millennium BCE.

After the 2006 census, [Karaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_County), [Nazarabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazarabad_County), and [Savojbolagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savojbolagh_County) Counties were separated from the province in the establishment of [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_province) province.[^cite_note-Alborz_Province_Establishment_Law-10]

### Tehran province today

Tehran is the commercial heart of Iran. Tehran Province has over 17,000 industrial units employing 390,000 people, 26% of all units in [Iran](14653.md). The province contains 30% of Iran's economy, and comprises 40% of Iran's consumer market. The province has three hydrodams (Latiyan, Lar, and Amir Kabir)as well as two natural lakes, providing the water supply of Tehran and the province.

The province contains 170 mines, over 330 square kilometres of forests, and over 12800 square kilometres of pasture.

Generally speaking, year round, regions such as the southern slopes of the Alborz Mountains, especially in the mountains, valleys, and rivers and artificial lakes formed behind the great dams of Amir Kabir, Latiyan and Lar along with natural lakes of Jaban and Tarr provide considerable recreation for the province.

Moreover, due to excessive snowfall in the northern areas of the province during the winter season, the [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) mountains form an excellent environment for winter sports such as skiing. [Dizin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizin), [Shemshak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shemshak), and [Tochal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochal) are the most popular skiing resorts.

## Geography

Tehran province borders [Mazandaran Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran_Province) in the north, [Qom Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qom_Province) in the south, [Semnan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semnan_Province) in the east, [Alborz Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_Province) in the west and [Markazi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markazi_Province) in the southwest. The [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis) of Tehran is the [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) of the province and of Iran.

Tehran province is the richest in Iran, as it contributes approximately 29% of the country's gross domestic product (GDP). Furthermore, it houses approximately 18% of the country's population and is the most [industrialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialized) province in Iran, with nearly 94% of its residents living in the cities as of 2016.

The province gained importance when Tehran was proclaimed the capital by the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) in 1778. Today, Tehran, with a population of 8 million, [is ranked amongst the 40 most populous metropolitan cities of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_proper_by_population).

The largest rivers of this province are [Karaj River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_River) and [Jajrood River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jajrood_River).

Mountain ranges such as The [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) span the north; Savad Kooh and Firooz Kooh are located in the northeast; Lavasanat, Qarah Daq, Shemiranat, Hassan Abad and Namak Mountains are in the southern areas; Bibi Shahr Banoo and Alqadr are situated in the southeast and the heights of Qasr-e-Firoozeh been located to the east of the province.

Environmentally, the climate of Tehran province is stable and has four seasons, in winter its experiences cold and snowy conditions, in spring and autumn it experiences generally mild conditions with ample rain, and in summer it experiences warm to hot conditions, and is generally dry. In the mountains, however, it is cold and semi-humid all year round, and the higher regions are colder with long winters. The hottest months of the year are from mid-July to mid-September when temperatures range from 28 °C (82 °F) to 30 °C (86 °F) and the coldest months experience 1 °C (34 °F) around January–February, but at certain times in winter it can reach −20 °C (−4 °F). Tehran city has cold winters and warm to hot summers. Average annual rainfall is approximately 200 millimetres (7.9 in), the maximum being during the winter season mostly in the form of snow. On the whole, the province has a [cold semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Cold_semi-arid_climates), [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) climate in the south and an [alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) in the north.

## Demographics

### Ethnicity

#### Persians

The majority of people living in Tehran province are ethnically [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), and Tehran is the largest Persian-speaking city in the world.[^cite_note-britannica.com-11][^cite_note-12]

#### Armenians

A population of [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Armenians) mainly lives in the eastern areas of Tehran.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

#### Azerbaijanis

[Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis_in_Iran) are one of the Iranian ethnic groups living in Tehran province, who also form the largest group of immigrants in Tehran province. Azeris in Tehran speak the [Azeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) languages.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

#### Gilaks

A population of [Gilaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaks) also live in Tehran and speak the [Gilaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaki_language) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) languages.

#### Kurds

Many [Kurds](3821779.md) have migrated to Tehran province in recent years to continue their [studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) or to find [jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(human_activity\)). Also, the people of [Sarbandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbandan), [Jaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaban,_Tehran), [Sorkheh Deh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorkheh_Deh,_Tehran) and [Khosrovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrovan) districts of [Damavand county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damavand_County) in the east of Tehran province speak [Kurmanji Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurmanji) language. The people of these areas are Kurmanji Kurds who moved to this area from [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan_province) during the [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) period. Simultaneously with the migration of Kurmanji tribes from Khorasan to Damavand, another group of Kurds from Khorasan migrated from [Chenaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenaran) to [Piranshahr](30865712.md), one of the cities of Kurdistan south of [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

#### Lurs

Many [Lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurs) migrate to Tehran province every year, and in this way, Tehran has a minority of Lurs. Also, [Hadavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadavand_tribe) clan is one of the oldest and largest clans of Tehran province, which moved to Tehran province during the time of [Karim Khan Zand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Khan_Zand).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

#### Mazanderanis

[Mazanderanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazanderani_people) are the third largest tribe of Tehran province, who mostly migrated to Tehran from the cities of [Babol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babol), [Sari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari), [Amol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amol), [Qaem Shahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaem_Shahr) and [Savadkuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savadkuh_County). In addition to Tehran city, Mazanderani language is also spoken in [Firuzkuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuzkuh), [Damavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damavand,_Iran) and [Shemiranat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shemiranat_County) cities.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

#### Citizens of other countries

A population of [Iraqi](4184636.md) nationals who are of Iranian origin that live in [Dolat Abad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowlatabad,_Tehran) area of Tehran.

The unstable situation and the war in neighbouring [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md) prompted a rush of refugees into the country who arrived in their millions, with Tehran being a magnet for much seeking work, who subsequently helped the city to recover from war wounds, working for far less pay than local construction workers. Many of these refugees are being repatriated with the assistance of the UNHCR, but there are still sizable groups of Afghan and Iraqi refugees in Tehran who are reluctant to leave, being pessimistic about the situation in their own countries. Afghan refugees are mostly [Dari-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari) [Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks), [Parsiwans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsiwan) and [Hazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaras), speaking a variety of Persian, and Iraqi refugees are mainly [Mesopotamian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_Arabic)-speakers who are often of Iranian and [Persian ethnic heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranians_in_Iraq).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the province's population was 13,281,858 in 3,729,010 households.[^cite_note-2006_Tehran_Province-36] The following census in 2011 counted 12,183,391 people in 3,731,480 households.[^cite_note-2011_Tehran_Province-37] The 2016 census measured the population of the province as 13,267,637 in 4,288,563 households.[^cite_note-2016_Tehran_Province-4]

### Anthropology

Tehran Province is the most populous province of Iran.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The population density in this province is 969 people per square kilometer.[^cite_note-53] During a research that was commissioned by the General Culture Council in 2009 and based on a field survey and a statistical community among the residents of 288 cities and about 1400 villages across the country, the percentage of ethnic groups that were sampled in this survey in this province was as follows. [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) was 56.9%, [Azari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijanis) 56.9%, [Northern](8825402.md) ([Mazani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazanderani_people), [Gilak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaks) and [Talish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_people)) 30.3%, [Kurdish](61733686.md) 5.5%, [Lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurs) 2.8%, [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_of_Iran) 2.1%.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-britannica.com-11][^cite_note-59]

## Transportation

See also: [Transport in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Iran)

### Road transport



Freeways (in blue) and main roads (in green) in Tehran province

Tehran province is served by a large freeway and expressway network:

-  [Freeway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_2_\(Iran\)) (Tehran–Karaj Freeway): This freeway connects Tehran to the capital city of neighboring province of [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_Province), [Karaj](978698.md) and continues towards [Tabriz](439791.md) and [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E80).

-  [Freeway 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_5_\(Iran\)) (Tehran–[Saveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saveh) Freeway): This Freeway connects city of Tehran to its southern suburbs such as [Sabashahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabashahr), [Robat Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robat_Karim) and [Parand](1414890.md) and continues towards [Markazi province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markazi_province) to Saveh and [Salafchegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafchegan). There are plans to continue the freeway towards [Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan).

-  [Freeway 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_7_\(Iran\)) (Tehran–Qom Freeway/Khalij-e-Fars (Persian Gulf) Freeway): This Freeway connects Tehran City to its airport, [Imam Khomeini International Airport](716542.md) and continues towards [Qom](638742.md) and [Isfahan](223149.md).

-  [Tehran–Pardis Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran-Pardis_Freeway): This freeway connects Tehran City to its northeastern suburbs such as [Pardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardis), [Bumehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumehen) and [Rudehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudehen) and joins [Haraz Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_77_\(Iran\)) and [Firuzkuh Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_79_\(Iran\)).

-  [Tehran–Shomal Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran-Shomal_Freeway): This under construction freeway will connect Tehran to [Chalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalus,_Iran) and the touristic areas in [Shomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shomal).

-  [Makhsus Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_32_\(Iran\)): This road is the old road from Tehran to [Karaj](978698.md). Because trucks are not allowed on [the freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_2_\(Iran\)) this road has a high congestion of trucks.

-  [Road 38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_38_\(Iran\)): This road connects Tehran to [Shahriar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahriar,_Tehran_Province) and [Malard](17437690.md) in southwestern Tehran and continues towards [Buin-Zahra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buin-Zahra) in [Qazvin province](448087.md).

-  [Road 44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_44_\(Iran\)): This Expressway connects Tehran to [Mashhad](406512.md).

-  [Road 65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_65_\(Iran\)) ([Saidi Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saidi_Expressway)/Saveh Road): This road connects Tehran to its southern suburbs such as [Chahardangeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chahardangeh), [Eslamshahr](7031187.md) and [Golestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golestan,_Tehran_Province). It continues towards Saveh and further south towards [Isfahan](223149.md), [Abadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadeh), [Shiraz](39622.md) and finally the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) coastal industrial city of [Asaluyeh](3393482.md).

-  [Road 71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_71_\(Iran\)) ([Qom](638742.md) old Road): This road is the road that connected Tehran to Qom as a main road before the opening of [the freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_7_\(Iran\)) in 1980. It is still an important transit road because trucks are not allowed in the freeway.

-  (Damavand Road/Haraz Road): This road connects Tehran to cities like [Rudehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudehen), [Abali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abali) and [Damavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damavand) and continues towards [Amol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amol) in [Mazandaran province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran_province) in [Shomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shomal). It is the most congested road from Tehran to [Shomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shomal) after [Chalous Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_59_\(Iran\)).

-  [Road 79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_79_\(Iran\)) ([Firuzkuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuzkuh) Road): This road connects Tehran to [Firuzkuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuzkuh) and [Qaemshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaemshahr) and therefore [Sari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari).

### Rail network

#### National Rail Network

The city of Tehran is connected to the North, South, West and East with the railway. It has weekly trains for [Istanbul](3391396.md). Tehran is the headquarters of [RAJA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAJA) (Iran national railway). There may be plans to build high speed railway lines from Tehran to Mashhad and [Isfahan](223149.md).

#### Tehran Metro

Main article: [Tehran Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Metro)



Hassan Abad Sq. Metro station

Tehran is served by a system of metro of three urban lines (1, 2, 4) and one suburban line (5) serving Karaj and Tehran western suburbs. There are plans to extend the system to eight urban lines and express express suburban lines.

### Airports

Tehran province has two main passenger airports:

- [Mehrabad Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrabad_Airport)

- [Imam Khomeini Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Khomeini_Airport)

It also has a number of air force bases.

## Religious centers

### Mosques, shrines, mausoleums, and tombs

- Soltani Mosque, built by [Fath Ali Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fath_Ali_Shah)

- Atiq Mosque, built in 1663.

- Mo'ezz o-dowleh mosque, built by Fath Ali Shah

- Haj Seyd Azizollah mosque, built by Fath Ali Shah

- Al-javad mosque, Iran's first modernist design mosque.

- The Old Sepahsalar mosque, another prominent [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) era mosque.

- The new [Sepahsalar Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepahsalar_Mosque) (Madreseh e Motahari)

- Filsuf o-dowleh Mosque, Qajar era

- Moshir ol-Saltaneh Mosque, Qajar era

- Mo'ayyer ol-Mamalik Mosque, Qajar era

- [Shahr Banu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahr_Banu) Mausopleum

- *Javan-mard Qassab* Mausoleum, a pre-Islamic semi-mythical hero

- Dozens of Imam-zadeh shrines, hundreds of years old, including that of [Imam Zadeh Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Zadeh_Saleh).

- Dozens of [Saqa Khanehs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqa_Khaneh?action=edit\&redlink=1): traditional places of prayer

- Several [Tekyehs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekyeh): traditional places for mourning Muharram ceremonies for [Husayn ibn Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_ibn_Ali).

- *[Ebn-e Babooyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebn-e_Babooyeh)* cemetery, where numerous Iranian figures, such as [Takhti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gholamreza_Takhti) and [Ali Akbar Dehkhoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Dehkhoda), are buried.

- *Zahir o-dowleh* cemetery, housing the tombs of art and cultural figures such as [Iraj Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraj_Mirza), [Mohammad Taghi Bahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Taghi_Bahar), [Forough Farrokhzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forough_Farrokhzad), [Abolhasan Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolhasan_Saba), [Ruhollah Khaleghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhollah_Khaleghi), and [Darvish-khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darvish-khan) are buried.

- Kordan Tomb, [Seljuqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks) era, [Karaj](978698.md).

- Maydanak Tomb, 13th century, Karaj

- The Polish cemetery north of Tehran, where numerous [Western](21208200.md) [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) soldiers of [World War II](32927.md) are buried
