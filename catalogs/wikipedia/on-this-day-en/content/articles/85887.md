**Kuyavian–Pomeranian Voivodeship** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *województwo kujawsko-pomorskie* [\[vɔjɛˈvut͡stfɔ kuˈjafskɔ pɔˈmɔrskʲɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/72/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_kujawsko-pomorskie.wav/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_kujawsko-pomorskie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/72/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_kujawsko-pomorskie.wav/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_kujawsko-pomorskie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is one of [Poland](22936.md)'s 16 [voivodeships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland) (provinces).&#x20;

It was created on 1 January 1999 and is situated in mid-northern Poland, on the boundary between the two historic regions, from which it takes its name: [Kuyavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavia) (Polish: *Kujawy*) and [Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomerania) (Polish: *Pomorze*).&#x20;

Its two chief cities, serving as the province's joint capitals, are [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) and [Toruń](31258.md).&#x20;

## History

The Kuyavian–Pomeranian Voivodeship was created on 1 January 1999, as a result of the [Polish local government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_local_government_reforms) adopted in 1998. It consisted of territory from the former [Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Voivodeship), [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torun_Voivodeship) and [Włocławek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82oc%C5%82awek_Voivodeship) Voivodeships.&#x20;

The area now known as Kuyavia–Pomerania was previously divided between the region of [Kuyavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavia), [Dobrzyń Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrzy%C5%84_Land), [Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomerania) (including [Chełmno Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno_Land) and [Kociewie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kociewie)), and [Greater Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland) (including [Pałuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa%C5%82uki) and [Krajna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krajna)). Of the two principal cities of today's Kuyavian–Pomeranian voivodeship, one ([Bydgoszcz](74593.md)) was historically located in Kuyavia, while the other ([Toruń](31258.md)) was an important town of Chełmno Land.&#x20;

## Administration and territory

The functions of regional capital are split between [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) and [Toruń](31258.md). Bydgoszcz serves as the seat of the centrally appointed governor or [voivode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivode) ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *wojewoda*), while Toruń is the seat of the elected [Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Regional_Assembly) ([sejmik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_sejmik)), and of the executive elected by that assembly, headed by the voivodeship marshal (*marszałek województwa*).&#x20;

The Kuyavian–Pomeranian Voivodeship is bordered by five other voivodeships. These are [Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Voivodeship) to the north, [Warmian–Masurian Voivodeship](182402.md) to the north-east, [Masovian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masovian_Voivodeship) to the east, [Łódź Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA_Voivodeship) across a short boundary to the south, and [Greater Poland Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Voivodeship) to the south and west.&#x20;

## Economy

The Gross domestic product (GDP) of the province was 21.8 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 4.4% of Polish economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 17,300 euros or 57% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 64% of the EU average.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

In 2024 the Kuyavian–Pomeranian Voivodeship had over 228,000 registered business entities. More than 60% of firms were located in urban areas, particularly around Bydgoszcz and Toruń. The region ranked eighth in Poland in industrial output in 2024, with the food industry playing a leading role alongside wood and paper, electrical machinery, and chemical production. Agriculture remains a key sector, with the region ranking among the national leaders in livestock and crop production, especially potatoes and sugar beets.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

## Transportation

Transportation infrastructure is of critical importance to the voivodeship's economy. Kuyavia-Pomerania is a major node in the Polish transportation system. Railway lines from the South and East pass through [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) to connect to the major ports on the [Baltic Sea](3335.md). In addition to this, [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) is home to the rolling stock manufacturer [PESA SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PESA_SA), Poland's largest and most modern producer of railway and tram products. The province's sole international airport, [Ignacy Jan Paderewski Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski_Airport), is located in Bydgoszcz and has connections to a number of European destinations as well as [Warsaw](32908.md), which are all operated by either Irish carrier [Ryanair](262878.md) or [LOT Polish Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOT_Polish_Airlines).&#x20;

The main railway stations of the province are Bydgoszcz main station and Toruń main station; both stations are served by fast [PKP Intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Intercity) trains which connect them with the capital [Warsaw](32908.md), as well as other major Polish cities. In addition to these fast express services, iregional trains on electrified lines are operated by the [Polregio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polregio) , while regional rail transportation on unelectrified lines within the voivodeship are provided by [Arriva RP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva_RP), a private firm to which the provincial government subcontracted the provision of rail transport.&#x20;

The [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_autostrada_\(Poland\)) and [S5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressway_S5_\(Poland\)) highways pass through the province, with the [S10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressway_S10_\(Poland\)) also under construction.&#x20;

All major towns of the province have municipal transportation companies operating buses, while [Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramways_in_Bydgoszcz), [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramways_in_Toru%C5%84) and [Grudziądz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramways_in_Grudzi%C4%85dz) also have extensive tram systems.&#x20;

## Politics

Main article: [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Regional_Assembly)

The Kuyavian-Pomeranian voivodeship's government is headed by the province's voivode *(governor)* who is appointed by the [Polish Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Prime_Minister). The voivode is then assisted in performing his duties by the voivodeship's marshal, who is the appointed speaker for the voivodeship's executive and is elected by the sejmik *(provincial assembly)*. The current voivode of Kuyavia-Pomerania is [Ewa Monika Mes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewa_Monika_Mes), and the present marshal is [Piotr Całbecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Ca%C5%82becki).&#x20;

## Sights and tourism



[Medieval Town of Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Town_of_Toru%C5%84), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md)

There are eight [Historic Monuments of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Historic_Monuments_\(Poland\)) and one [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in the voivodeship:&#x20;

- [Medieval Town of Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Town_of_Toru%C5%84) (listed as both)

  [^cite_note-8]

- Old Town of [Chełmno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno)

  [^cite_note-9]

- [Biskupin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biskupin) archeological reserve

  [^cite_note-10]

- [Graduation towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciechocinek_graduation_towers), saline and spa parks of [Ciechocinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciechocinek)

  [^cite_note-11]

- [Grudziądz Granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudzi%C4%85dz_Granaries)

  [^cite_note-12]

- [Lubostroń Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubostro%C5%84_Palace) and Park Complex

  [^cite_note-13]

- Norbertine monastery complex with the Romanesque Saint Procopius Church and the Romanesque-Gothic-Baroque [Holy Trinity Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Church,_Strzelno) in [Strzelno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strzelno)

  [^cite_note-14]

- [Włocławek Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82oc%C5%82awek_Cathedral)

  [^cite_note-15]

The region is rich in historic architecture ranging from [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture_in_Poland) and [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) to [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture), [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) and [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau). Other preserved historic old towns include [Bydgoszcz](74593.md), [Grudziądz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudzi%C4%85dz) and [Brodnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brodnica). There are also numerous castles, including [Dybów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyb%C3%B3w_Castle), [Golub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golub_Castle), [Radzyń Chełmiński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radzy%C5%84_Che%C5%82mi%C5%84ski), [Świecie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiecie_Castle), and [Zamek Bierzgłowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamek_Bierzg%C5%82owski), preserved castle towers, including [Brodnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brodnica_Castle) and [Kruszwica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruszwica), and palaces, including [Jabłonowo-Zamek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jab%C5%82onowo-Zamek), [Ostromecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaces_and_park_ensemble_in_Ostromecko), [Wąpielsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C4%85pielsk), and [Żołędowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBo%C5%82%C4%99dowo,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship). The Dybów Castle was the place where in 1454 King [Casimir IV Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon) issued the famous [Statutes of Nieszawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutes_of_Nieszawa), covering a set of privileges for the [Polish nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta); an event that is regarded as the birth of the noble democracy in Poland, which lasted until the late-18th-century [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland). The manor in [Szafarnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szafarnia,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) was a place of stay for [Fryderyk Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin) during his 1824 and 1825 summer vacations, and contains a museum dedicated to the composer.&#x20;

Włocławek, Toruń and Bydgoszcz contain preserved Gothic cathedrals. Locations of historic monasteries include [Chełmno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno), [Grudziądz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudzi%C4%85dz), [Mogilno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogilno), [Rywałd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rywa%C5%82d,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) and [Skępe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C4%99pe). In [Gąsawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%85sawa), there is the 17th-century Saint Nicolas Church with a unique collection of multi-layered mural paintings, dating back several centuries.&#x20;



Graduation towers in [Ciechocinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciechocinek)

There are three [spa towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_town): [Ciechocinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciechocinek), [Inowrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inowroc%C5%82aw) and [Wieniec-Zdrój](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wieniec-Zdr%C3%B3j).&#x20;

Major museums and art galleries are located in Bydgoszcz, Toruń and Włocławek, including the [Leon Wyczółkowski Regional Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Museum,_Bydgoszcz) in Bydgoszcz, [District Museum in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Museum_in_Toru%C5%84) and Museum of Kuyavia and Dobrzyń Land in Włocławek. The more unique museums include:&#x20;

- [Copernicus House in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_House_in_Toru%C5%84), museum dedicated to [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus)

- European Museum of Money at the former [Polish Royal Mint in Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Mint)

- Museum of [Toruń Gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_gingerbread) in Toruń

- Museum of Far Eastern Art in the [Under the Star Tenement House in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_the_Star_Tenement_House_in_Toru%C5%84)

- Andrzej Szwalbe Collection of Historical Pianos at the palace in [Ostromecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostromecko), one of two largest such collections in Poland

- [Narrow Gauge Railway Museum in Wenecja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_Gauge_Railway_Museum_in_Wenecja)

- [Exploseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploseum) in Bydgoszcz, operated at the site of a former Nazi German arms factory.



Monument to the victims of Nazi Germany at the [Valley of Death (Bydgoszcz)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Death_\(Bydgoszcz\))

There are numerous World War II memorials in the province, including a memorial at the site of the former [Potulice concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potulice_concentration_camp), memorials at the sites of Nazi massacres of Poles, including the largest massacres at [Mniszek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mniszek,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship), [Gniewkowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gniewkowo), [Klamry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klamry), [Łopatki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81opatki,_W%C4%85brze%C5%BAno_County) and [Fordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Death_\(Bydgoszcz\)), and memorials to [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) prisoners of war held by Nazi Germany in the region at the sites of the former [Stalag XX-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_XX-A), [Oflag XXI-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_XXI-B) and [Oflag 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_64) [POW camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) in Toruń and [Szubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szubin).&#x20;

Other notable sights include the [Bydgoszcz Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Canal), connecting [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) and [Nakło nad Notecią](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nak%C5%82o_nad_Noteci%C4%85), the [Battle of Koronowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Koronowo) (1410) Monument, and monuments do distinguished people from the region, including cryptologist [Marian Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski) (birthplace and monument in Bydgoszcz), writer [Jan Kasprowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kasprowicz) (childhood home and monument in Inowrocław), and surgeon [Ludwik Rydygier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwik_Rydygier), the first surgeon in the world to carry out a peptic ulcer resection (former clinic and monument in Chełmno).&#x20;

## Curiosities

- [Brześć Kujawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brze%C5%9B%C4%87_Kujawski), [Bydgoszcz](74593.md), [Dobrzyń nad Wisłą](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrzy%C5%84_nad_Wis%C5%82%C4%85), [Gniewkowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gniewkowo) and [Inowrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inowroc%C5%82aw) were medieval ducal seats of the [Piast dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_dynasty).

- [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) and [Toruń](31258.md), although not capitals of Poland, hosted sessions of the Polish Parliament. Bydgoszcz in 1520, Toruń in 1576 and 1626.

  [^cite_note-16]

- Toruń served as the temporary Polish capital in 1809.

  [^cite_note-17]

- Medieval municipal rights modelled after [Chełmno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno), known as [Chełmno rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno_rights), became the basis of municipal form of government for various cities and towns of the region and beyond, including [Warsaw](32908.md).

- The Kuyavian–Pomeranian Voivodeship is one of four first-level administrative divisions containing the name of the historic region of Pomerania, the other being the [Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Voivodeship) and [West Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pomeranian_Voivodeship) in Poland, and [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) in Germany.
