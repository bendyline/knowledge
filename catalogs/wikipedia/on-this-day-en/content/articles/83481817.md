**Ali al-Taher** (Arabic: علي الطاهر) is a location in the [Nabatieh district](12169367.md) of the [Nabatieh Governorate](4981449.md), [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md).[^cite_note-2]

The name is used to refer to the hills on the northern outskirts of [Nabatieh al-Fawqa](67453293.md) and the village.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4]

## Geography

Ali al-Taher ridge is on the northern outskirts Nabatieh, in the Nabatieh district of the Nabatieh Governorate, rising to roughly 600 metres (2,000 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-5] The ridge lies north of the [Litani River](1920925.md) and its elevated position commands views over much of southern Lebanon, including [Beaufort Castle](6853436.md) and its surrounding valley to the south, the city of Nabatieh to the west and further southeast Iqlim al-Tuffah and the Rihan heights in the [Jezzine District](12169202.md).[^cite_note-6]

The area also contains a natural spring known locally as "Ein al-Tahara" and an old well, alongside remnants of ancient buildings, indicating earlier habitation of the site.[^cite_note-manateq2026-7] The hillside is covered in places by dense, old-growth [oak](39696.md) forest.[^cite_note-8] Its elevated position also offers views extending toward parts of the [Marjeyoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjeyoun_District) and [Hasbaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbaya_District) districts.[^cite_note-9]

## History

The site is named after a figure revered locally as a "[Wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali)" (a righteous person), believed to be a commander from early Islamic historical periods. A [Maqam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maqam_\(shrine\)) (shrine) was erected at the summit in his honor. This shrine was a place of local pilgrimage for residents of nearby villages, particularly during religious commemorations.[^cite_note-10]

The shrine was restored in 1180 [AH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) (1766–1767 CE) by Hajj Ali Hilal, and its religious endowments ([waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf)) have historically been administered by the Nour al-Din family of [Nabatieh al-Fawqa](67453293.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The site is historically described as lying within the district known as "Bilad al-Shaqif", named after the nearby Shaqif Arnoun (Beaufort) fortress.[^cite_note-13]

[Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) mentioned the shrine in the account of his second journey to Lebanon in 1852, locating it west of [Mount Jarmaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Jarmaq), although he did not personally visit the site.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-elnashra2024-15] The shrine is also cited in several works of local and regional history, including [Muhsin al-Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhsin_al-Amin?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s *Khitat Jabal Amil*, Sheikh Ibrahim Suleiman's *Buldan Jabal Amil*, and Sheikh Jaafar al-Muhajir's *Sittat Fuqaha Abtal* ("Six Hero Jurists").[^cite_note-elnashra2024-15] Pilgrimages to the site were reportedly most prominent during the commemoration of [Arba'in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arba'een) of [Imam Husayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_ibn_Ali).[^cite_note-elnashra2024-15]

During the [2026 Lebanon War](82549840.md) the hill was subjected by skirmishes and strikes between [Hezbollah](13919.md) and [Israel](9282173.md) due to its strategic position near Nabatieh al-Fawqa and the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) of [Nabatieh](43794798.md).[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-16] In June 2026, the Israel Defense Forces reported that the ridge contained a Hezbollah [underground facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_facility) built with Iranian assistance.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] On July 2, the [Egoz commando](4311894.md) unit of the [IDF](39237.md) claimed to have killed a militant exiting a tunnel in the area.[^cite_note-19]

### 2026 siege

Main article: [Siege of Ali al-Taher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ali_al-Taher)

It was reported in September 2026 that Israeli forces had surrounded approximately 200 Hezbollah fighters and Iranian military advisors from the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) who were sheltering in the complex.[^cite_note-20] By 4 September, the Israeli government said that it had gained "operational control" of the ridge, with Defense Minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) stating that the Israeli control of the ridge was part of a "security zone in southern Lebanon" that it was establishing.[^cite_note-21] On 10 September 2026, the IDF said that it had detonated more than 1,000 tons of TNT (4,200 GJ) of explosives, destroying the network of tunnels stretching 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) underneath the ridge and setting off tremors that measured 4.1 on the [Richter scale](19467352.md) in what Lebanon's [National News Agency](73575882.md) called a "massive explosion".[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]
