**Michaela "Michi" Benthaus** is a German [aerospace engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) who became the [first wheelchair user to travel to space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_space_travellers_by_first_flight).[^cite_note-hindu-2][^cite_note-bbc-bo-3] She works for the [European Space Agency](10363.md).

## Early life and education

Benthaus received her early education from the [Nymphenburg schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphenburg_schools?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Munich](19058.md), [Bavaria](3764.md). She then went on to study [mechatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechatronics) for her [Bachelor's degree](188874.md) at [Johannes Kepler University Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler_University_Linz), followed by a [Master's degree](170346.md) in aerospace engineering at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) with focus on space and [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics).[^cite_note-tum-4]

In 2018, Benthaus suffered a [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord) injury during a [mountain biking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_biking) accident, which left her with [paraplegia](410866.md) at the age of 26. Since then, she has been a full time wheelchair user.[^cite_note-nbcnews-5][^cite_note-dw-6]

## Career

Benthaus was enrolled in the graduate trainee program of the European Space Agency in the Netherlands.[^cite_note-nbcnews-5]

In 2022, Benthaus flew aboard a parabolic airplane flight named the [Vomit Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced-gravity_aircraft), which provides an environment of near-weightlessness for a brief period. In 2024, she took part in a two-week simulated astronaut mission in [Poland](22936.md).[^cite_note-spacedotcom-7][^cite_note-nbcnews-5][^cite_note-abcnews-8]

In 2025, Benthaus was accompanied by fellow German engineer [Hans Koenigsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Koenigsmann) in her mission to fly in [outer space](177602.md).[^cite_note-guardian-9] Benthaus herself reached out to Hans Koenigsmann to examine the possibility of whether she could realise her dream of becoming an astronaut.[^cite_note-bbc-bo-3][^cite_note-cbsnews-10]

Benthaus joined the [sub-orbital spaceflight](455295.md) [Blue Origin NS-37](81824603.md) as one of six crew members, with her trip financed by [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos)'s [Blue Origin](887418.md). On December 20, 2025, the 10 minute flight launched from [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas) at 9\:15 a.m. EST. and reached 65 miles in altitude, passing the [Kármán line](738149.md) which is some 62 miles far away above the Earth.[^cite_note-cnn-1][^cite_note-cbsnews-10] Benthaus became the first wheelchair user to reach space aboard a sub-orbital flight.[^cite_note-bbc-bo-3][^cite_note-guardian-9]

Benthaus also advocated for the equal rights and increased inclusion of individuals with physical disabilities. After successfully entering space, she emphasized that more initiatives had to be put in place to give more space for disabled people to shine in their preferred areas of interest. Benthaus is also known for her humanitarian efforts, as the event of her spaceflight raised money for "Wings for Life", a nonprofit organization focusing on spinal cord injury research.[^cite_note-cnn-1]

On the 6th of March 2026, Benthaus was invited to speak alongside [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA) astronaut [Sophie Adenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Adenot) at the [2026 Winter Paralympics opening ceremony](77701912.md).[^cite_note-paralympics-11]

## Personal life

Benthaus' interest in space began in childhood when she watched movies such as *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars)*.[^cite_note-noz-12] She plays [wheelchair tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair_tennis) during her leisure time.[^cite_note-abcnews-8]
