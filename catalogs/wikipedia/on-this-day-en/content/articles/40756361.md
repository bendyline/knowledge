**Terrorism in Mexico** is the phenomenon of organized violence against civilians. It appeared in the 1960s, committed by [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) guerrillas.[^cite_note-3]

## Far-left groups



Soldiers of the Mexican Army in the streets



TDR-EP guerrillas during a revolutionary meeting

Main articles: [Dirty War (Mexico)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_War_\(Mexico\)) and [Chiapas conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiapas_conflict)

From the late 1960s to the 1980s guerrilla movements operated in the country. The worst attacks were the assault of the Madera Cuartel and [Tlatelolco Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlatelolco_Massacre), the starting point for several guerrilla movements, specially in the states of [Guerrero](195816.md) and [Ciudad de Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_de_Mexico). Groups included [Partido de los Pobres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_the_Poor_\(Mexico\)), [People's Guerrilla Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Guerrilla_Group), or [Liga Comunista 23 de Septiembre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Comunista_23_de_Septiembre). These groups were demolished, amid allegations of extrajudicial executions or [forced disappearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Eventually in the 1990s the guerrilla activity it would focus in states of [Guerrero](195816.md), [Oaxaca](156896.md) (with bastions of the [Ejercito Popular Revolucionario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Revolutionary_Army), [Ejército Revolucionario del Pueblo Insurgente](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ej%C3%A9rcito%20Revolucionario%20del%20Pueblo%20Insurgente) and [Fuerzas Armadas Revolucionarias del Pueblo](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerzas%20Armadas%20Revolucionarias%20del%20Pueblo), [Tendencia Democrática Revolucionaria-Ejército del Pueblo](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendencia%20Democr%C3%A1tica%20Revolucionaria-Ej%C3%A9rcito%20del%20Pueblo), these last splits of the EPR)[^cite_note-6] and [Chiapas](6787.md) this one gaining relevance with the [Ejercito Zapatista De Liberacion Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejercito_Zapatista_De_Liberacion_Nacional)

## Narcoterrorism

Main article: [Mexican Drug War](8442323.md)

In 2012 United States politician [Sue Myrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sue_Myrick) claimed that mounting evidence of [Hezbollah](13919.md) presence in Mexico was ignored by the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] These groups became more visible by 2010, when the Tucson Police Department reported International Terrorism Situational Awareness for [Hezbollah](13919.md) in Mexico, noting the arrest of Jameel Nasar in [Tijuana](101739.md). Nasar had tried to form a Hezbollah network in Mexico and [South America](26769.md). A report from the US [House Homeland Security Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Homeland_Security) Subcommittee on Oversight, Investigations and Management tied Middle East terror organizations with Mexican drug cartels.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Currently the violence related with the [drug war](8442323.md) represents the conflict of greatest generation of violence in the country, causing some attacks that can be branded as narco-terrorism like [2008 Morelia grenade attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Morelia_grenade_attacks), [2009 Guanajuato and Hidalgo shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Guanajuato_and_Hidalgo_shootings) and the [2011 Monterrey casino attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Monterrey_casino_attack) or the recently 2019 [Minatitlán shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minatitl%C3%A1n_shooting).[^cite_note-11]

## Anarchist groups

Main article: [Anarchism in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Mexico)

Anarchist groups in México have been on the rise since the beginning of the 2000s, with a large number of attacks to banks, religious centers and government buildings, especially intensifying during the government of [Enrique Peña Nieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Pe%C3%B1a_Nieto).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Groups like [Células Autónomas de Revolución Inmediata Práxedis G. Guerrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9lulas_Aut%C3%B3nomas_de_Revoluci%C3%B3n_Inmediata_Pr%C3%A1xedis_G._Guerrero), [Célula Insurreccional Mariano Sanchez Añón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9lula_Insurreccional_Mariano_Sanchez_A%C3%B1%C3%B3n), Grupo de Ataque Insurrecto,[^cite_note-14] Brigada Informal Bruno Filippi, Caos Espontáneamente Anónimo, Salvaje,[^cite_note-15] Celula eco-anarquista por el Ataque Directo,[^cite_note-16] Frente Subversivo de Liberación Global,[^cite_note-17] [Frente de Liberación Animal-Comando Verde Negro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Liberation_Front),[^cite_note-18] [Individualistas Tendiendo a lo Salvaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualists_Tending_to_the_Wild), and others. The most notorious incident with this group was the [Monterrey Tech bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_Tech_bombing). One of the first important attacks the Brigada de Eco saboteadorxs por la Venganza Nunca Olvidada claimed a blast in a [Banamex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banamex) branch in the municipality of [Coacalco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coacalco), that do not leave injured[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] And two

In 2017 the [CISEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CISEN) (the Mexican civil intelligence institution at the service of the government of Mexico) said that these "direct violent actions" came from groups such as the [Earth Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Liberation_Front) (52 actions), the [Animal Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Liberation_Front) (44), Celulas Autónomas de Revolución Inmediata-Práxedis Guerrero (32), [Federazione Anarchia Informale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federazione_Anarchia_Informale) (30) and the Conspiracy of Fire Cells (12). In [Mexico City](18987.md), the Cisen documented the existence of the Campamento Revolución, Bloque Anarko Sur (responsible for assaulting a group of journalists during a demonstration in 2011),[^cite_note-21] the Anarko Norte Bloc, the Black Anarchist Bloc, [Chanti Ollin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanti_Ollin) (House in Motion), the Magonista Autonomous Collective.,[^cite_note-22] the Anarchist Student Coordinator, Cruz Negra Anarchist and [Okupa Che](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okupa_Che).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]
