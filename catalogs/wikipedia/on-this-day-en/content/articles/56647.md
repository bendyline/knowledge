**Cotonou** (French pronunciation: [\[kɔtɔnu\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Fon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fon_language): *Kútɔ̀nú*)[^cite_note-2] is the [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government) and largest city of [Benin](3459.md). The official city proper population was 679,012 in 2012; however, over two million people live in the larger urban area. The urban area continues to expand, notably toward the west. The city lies in the southeast of the country, between the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) and [Lake Nokoué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nokou%C3%A9).[^cite_note-3]

Cotonou is the [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government) of the Republic of Benin, where most of the government buildings are situated, government departments operate and foreign embassies are located, but the official capital, where the national legislature sits, is the smaller city of [Porto-Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto-Novo).

## History



Fort of Cotonou in 1900

See also: [Timeline of Cotonou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Cotonou)

The name "Cotonou" means "by the river of death" in the [Fon language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fon_language).[^cite_note-Bradt-4] At the beginning of the 19th century, Cotonou (then spelled "Kutonou") was a small fishing village, and is thought to have been formally founded by King [Ghezo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghezo) of [Dahomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahomey) in 1830.[^cite_note-Bradt-4] It grew as a centre for the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery), and later [palm oil](57561.md) and [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton).[^cite_note-Bradt-4] In 1851 the [French Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Second_Republic) made a treaty with King Ghezo that allowed them to establish a trading post at Cotonou.[^cite_note-5] During the reign of King [Glele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glele) (1858–89), the territory was ceded to the [Second French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire) by a treaty signed in 1878.[^cite_note-6] In 1883, the [French Navy](383256.md) occupied the city to prevent British conquest of the area.

After Glele's death in 1889, King [Béhanzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9hanzin) unsuccessfully tried to challenge the treaty. The town grew rapidly following the building of the harbour in 1908.[^cite_note-Bradt-4]

The population in 1960 was only 70,000.[^cite_note-7]

## Geography



The [Ouémé River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ou%C3%A9m%C3%A9_River) as it flows into the Atlantic Ocean at Cotonou

Cotonou is on the coastal strip between [Lake Nokoué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nokou%C3%A9) and the Atlantic Ocean. The city is cut in two by a canal, the lagoon of Cotonou, dug by the French in 1855. Three bridges are in this area. The [Ouémé River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ou%C3%A9m%C3%A9_River) flows into the Atlantic Ocean at Cotonou.



Bridge in Cotonou

The city has established transportation infrastructure including air, sea, river (to [Porto Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Novo)), and land routes that facilitate trade with its neighbors [Nigeria](21383.md), [Niger](21373.md), [Burkina Faso](3470.md) and [Togo](30138.md).

[Coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) has been noted for several decades. It worsened in 1961 following construction of the [Nangbeto Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangbeto_Dam) and deep-water port of Cotonou. A pilot project funded by the [United Nations Environment Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Program) (UNEP) revealed that in 40 years, the coast to the east of Cotonou had retreated by 400 meters.[^cite_note-8] This erosion has led many people to leave their homes along the coast.[^cite_note-9]

## Climate

Under [Köppen's climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Cotonou features a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate), alternating with two rainy seasons (April–July and September–October, totalling 1,000 to 1,300 mm (39 to 51 in) of rain per year) and two dry seasons. In December and January, the city is affected by [harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan) winds. Temperatures are relatively constant throughout the year, with the average high temperatures hovering around 30 °C (86 °F), and average low temperatures at around 25 °C (77 °F).[^cite_note-10]

## Transport

Further information: [Transport in Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Benin) and [Cotonou Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotonou_Lighthouse)



Autonomous Port of Cotonou

The Autonomous Port of Cotonou is one of the largest in West Africa. The city is connected to [Parakou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakou) in the north by the Benin-Niger railway. [Cotonou International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadjehoun_Airport) provides service to the capitals of the region and to France, as well as the major cities of Benin: Parakou, [Kandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandi,_Benin), [Natitingou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natitingou), [Djougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djougou), and [Savé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sav%C3%A9). There are road connections to neighboring countries: Nigeria, Burkina Faso, Niger, and [Togo](30138.md). A prevalent mode of transport in the city is the motorcycle-taxi, known locally as [zémidjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%A9midjan).[^cite_note-Bradt-4]

In 2015 a suburban passenger railway line was being developed between Cotonou and [Pahou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahou).[^cite_note-14]

## Economy



Downtown of Cotonou



Congress Palace of Cotonou



[BCEAO Tower Cotonou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCEAO_Tower_\(Cotonou\))

Important manufactured goods include [palm oil](57561.md), [brewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles), and [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement).[^cite_note-15] [Motor vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) and [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle) are assembled, and there are [sawmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawmill) in the city. [Petroleum](23195.md) products, [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) are major exports.[^cite_note-16] There are offshore platforms drilling for [oil](23195.md).[^cite_note-17] The city is a centre for the automotive trade, with European brands being sold from vast open-air parking lots. In the past, Citroën assembled cars (for instance, the [Citroën 2CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_2CV) and [Ami 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_Ami)) locally.[^cite_note-18]

By 2012, [piracy in the Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_Gulf_of_Guinea) had significantly decreased trade at the port of Cotonou.[^cite_note-ap1-19]

In the Missèbo area is a [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) market of African prints mainly handled by Indian wholesalers and retailers.[^cite_note-20]

The 12th arrondissement, between Boulevard de la Marina and the Atlantic Ocean, is the site of the second tallest [statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Monument_\(Benin\)) in Africa, honoring the [Dahomey Amazons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahomey_Amazons).[^cite_note-21]
