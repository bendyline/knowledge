# Aviation in India

&#x20;  **Aviation in India** can be broadly divided into [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) and [civil aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation_in_India). India has an extensive civilian [air transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_transportation) network and is amongst the fastest-growing aviation markets in the world according to the [International Air Transport Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association) (IATA).

The first commercial aviation flight in India took place on 18 February 1911. In March 1953, the [Indian Parliament](410238.md) passed the Air Corporations Act by which the eight domestic airlines operating independently at that time were merged into two government-owned entities. [Air India](26457880.md), tracing its origins to 1932, is the [flag carrier](465816.md) of India. [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](23160430.md) (DGCA), established in 1931, is the regulatory body responsible for civil aviation, working under the [Ministry of Civil Aviation](10257026.md). [Airports Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Authority_of_India) is responsible for the management of civil aviation infrastructure. As of 2025, there are 33 [international](1519705.md), 11 [limited international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_airport) airports and more than 100 domestic and private airports. De-regulation in the 1990s opened the industry to private players who cater to majority of the traffic as of 2025, handling more than 200 million air passengers annually.

The [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md) under the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) is responsible for the military operations. Royal Indian Air Force was established on 8 October 1932 with the first squadron commissioned in April 1933. It later became the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) in 1950 and is the major military air operator in India. Indian Air Force maintains a [fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_Indian_military_aircraft) of various aircraft and [air bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_Air_Force_stations) across India. [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), [Indian Navy](604522.md) and [Indian Coast Guard](786383.md) also maintain air infrastructure in a supporting role.

## History

Main articles: [History of Indian civil aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation_in_India#History) and [History of the Indian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Indian_Air_Force)



Tata Sons' Airline time-table, c. 1935

The first commercial aviation flight in India took place on 18 February 1911 from [Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad) to [Naini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naini,_Prayagraj), a distance of 9.7 kilometres (6 miles), flown by [Henri Pequet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Pequet) using a [Humber biplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber-Sommer_biplane) carrying 6,500 pieces of mail.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] In December 1912, the Indian State Air services along with the [Imperial Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Airways) introduced a [London](17867.md)-[Delhi](37756.md) flight, the first International flight to India.[^cite_note-PIB-3] In 1915, [Tata Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Sons) started a regular airmail service between [Karachi](17123.md) and [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras) marking the beginning of air transportation in the southern part of India.[^cite_note-PIB-3] Construction of the first civil airports at [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta), Allahabad and [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay) started in 1924. In April 1927, a department of civil aviation was set up with the [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](23160430.md) (DGCA) set-up in 1931.[^cite_note-PIB-3] The Aero Club of India was established in 1927 and the [Madras Flying Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_Flying_Club) in 1930.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] On 15 October 1932, Indian aviator [J. R. D. Tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._D._Tata) flew a [Puss Moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Puss_Moth) aircraft carrying mail from [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_airport) to [Juhu aerodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juhu_aerodrome), [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay); and the aircraft continued to [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras), marking the beginning of Tata Airlines which later became [Air India](26457880.md), the [flag carrier](465816.md) in 1946.[^cite_note-history-6][^cite_note-7]

The [Indian Air Force](403355.md) was established on 8 October 1932 as an auxiliary air force of the [Royal Air Force](25679.md).[^cite_note-clause4-8] The enactment of the Indian Air Force Act 1932 stipulated out their auxiliary status and enforced the adoption of the Royal Air Force uniforms, badges, brevets and insignia.[^cite_note-IAF-9][^cite_note-airpower3-10] On 1 April 1933, the IAF commissioned its first squadron, No.1 Squadron, with four [Westland Wapiti biplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Wapiti) and five Indian pilots.[^cite_note-11] In 1933–34, Indian Trans Continental Airways, Madras Air Taxi Services and Indian National Airways commenced operations.[^cite_note-PIB-3] The Indian Aircraft Act was propagated in 1934 and formulated in 1937.[^cite_note-PIB-3] In 1940, [Hindustan Aeronautics Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Aeronautics_Limited) (HAL) was set up at [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) and India's first aircraft rolled out for test flight in July 1941.[^cite_note-PIB-3] The Indian Air Force took part in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) and was christened as Royal Indian Air Force. In 1950, when India became a republic, the prefix was dropped and it reverted to being the Indian Air Force.[^cite_note-Ahluwalia2012-12] In March 1953, the [Indian Parliament](410238.md) passed the Air Corporations Act through which the eight independent domestic airlines in existence: Deccan Airways, Airways India, Bharat Airways, Himalayan Aviation, Kalinga Air Lines, Indian National Airways, Air India, Air Services of India were merged into two government owned entities of [Indian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Airlines) and Air India.[^cite_note-PIB-3] In 1960, the first [jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft) was introduced for commercial passenger traffic.[^cite_note-Jet-13]



A pair of [Indian Air Force](403355.md) [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) during the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971)

In 1961, the Indian Air Force provided air support in [Operation Vijay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vijay_\(1961\)) as a part of [liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Goa) of [Portuguese colony of Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India).[^cite_note-brgoa1-14] The Indian Air Force achieved air superiority during the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965).[^cite_note-15] In 1966, the IAF started inducting [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) and [Sukhoi Su-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-7) fighter jets.[^cite_note-16] Indian Air Force played a major part in the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971), carrying out strikes and providing air support for [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) and [Indian Navy](604522.md).[^cite_note-IAF71-17][^cite_note-18]



[Air India](26457880.md) the [flag carrier](465816.md) of India's [A350-900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350).

In 1972, the International Airports Authority of India (IAAI) was constituted with the National Airports Authority established in 1986. In 1995, both the agencies were merged to form [Airports Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Authority_of_India), responsible for the management of civil aviation infrastructure in India.[^cite_note-PIB-3] Until 1990, international aviation was restricted to the airports in the four major metros of India, viz., [Delhi](37756.md), Bombay, Calcutta and Madras. In 1991, the aviation industry was de-regulated to allow the entry of private airlines to provide [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_charter) services with East West Airlines becoming the first private airline to operate in the country after 37 years.[^cite_note-PIB-3] In 1994, private airlines were permitted to operate scheduled services and a number of private airlines commenced domestic operations.[^cite_note-PIB-3] On 11 May 1999, the Indian Air Force was called in to provide close air support to the Indian Army at the height of the ongoing [Kargil conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_conflict) with the use of helicopters.[^cite_note-iafo-19] In 2003, [low cost airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_cost_airline) entered service in the Indian market with [Air Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Deccan).[^cite_note-PIB-3] In 2004, [Government of India](553883.md) approved setting up of the first airports with private partnership at [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_India) and Bangalore.[^cite_note-PIB-3] In December 2004, other Indian carriers apart from Air India, with a minimum of 5 years of continuous operations and a fleet of 20 aircraft, were permitted to operate international services.[^cite_note-PIB-3] In 2007, the Regional Airlines policy was formulated to allow carriers to operate service within a particular region.[^cite_note-PIB-3] The Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA) was set up in 2009.[^cite_note-PIB-3]

## Civil aviation

Main article: [Civil aviation in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation_in_India)

The [Ministry of Civil Aviation](10257026.md) is responsible for civilian aviation, through regulatory oversight by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA).[^cite_note-20] The [National Civil Aviation Policy 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation_in_India#National_Civil_Aviation_Policy_2016) set the guidelines and goals for operations of civil aviation.[^cite_note-21] India is the third largest civilian aviation market with airlines carrying more than 200 million passengers in 2020.[^cite_note-22]

### Network and infrastructure

Main articles: [List of the busiest airports in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_India) and [List of airports in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_India)

India has various types of airports namely, designated international airports, customs airports with limited international flights, [civil enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-use_airport) within airbases, domestic airports and private airports.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Airports Authority of India is the nodal authority responsible for the development and maintenance of infrastructure.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] AAI administers the airports divided into four administrative regions.[^cite_note-28] As of December 2023, there are 33 [international](1519705.md) and 11 [limited international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_airport) airports apart from 100+ other domestic, private airports and civil enclaves.[^cite_note-29] The [UDAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDAN) regional connectivity scheme, launched in 2016, planned to develop a sustainable air network in over 400 tier-2 cities across India.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

## Military aviation

Further information: [List of active Indian Air Force aircraft squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_Indian_Air_Force_aircraft_squadrons), [List of Indian Air Force stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_Air_Force_stations), and [List of active Indian military aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_Indian_military_aircraft)

The [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) serves as the ex-officio [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_India) of the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md), with the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) responsible for policy. The air arms of the armed forces are the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) (IAF), commanded by the [Chief of Air Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Air_Staff_\(India\)), and the [Indian Naval Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Naval_Air_Arm), [Army Aviation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Aviation_Corps_\(India\)), and [Indian Coast Guard aviation](786383.md#Current_aircraft) are the agencies involved in the military aviation in India. The Indian Air Force is divided into five operational and two functional commands with [bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_air_base) or stations across various commands.[^cite_note-34] The armed forces operate a fleet of [fighter](10929.md), [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft), [helicopters](8286923.md), [tankers](237949.md), [AWAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control), [reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft) aircraft and [Unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md).[^cite_note-World_Air_Forces_2024-35]
