**Zambezia** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Zambézia* [\[zɐ̃ˈbɛzjɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)) is the second most-populous [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Mozambique) of [Mozambique](19301.md). It is located in the central coastal region, and it borders the provinces of [Niassa](448242.md) to the north, [Nampula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nampula_Province) to the northeast, [Sofala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofala_Province) to the southwest, [Tete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tete_Province) to the west and the country of [Malawi](19088.md) to the northwest. It has a population of 5.11 million, according to the 2017 census.[^cite_note-3] The provincial capital is [Quelimane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quelimane).

Zambezia has a total area of 103,478 km<sup>2</sup>. The 2,574 kilometre [Zambezi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambezi_River) runs through [Zambia](34415.md), [Angola](701.md), [Namibia](21292.md), [Botswana](3464.md), [Zimbabwe](34399.md), and the Tete, [Manica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manica_Province) and Sofala provinces of Mozambique before emptying into the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) off the coast of Zambezia, constituting its western border. [Mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) are predominant along the coast and considerable forests can be found inland, with a scattering of [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuaries) and rivers.

Zambezia's islands lie in the [Primeiras e Segundas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primeiras_and_Segundas_Archipelago) [Environmental Protection Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection_area). These islands split into the Primeiras Islands and Segundas Islands (First Islands and Second Islands in [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language)) and are largely uninhabited, aside from providing a stop-off for artisanal fishers.[^cite_note-4] At present, only [Ilha do Fogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilha_do_Fogo,_Mozambique), one of the Primeiras Islands, has infrastructure. It runs solely on clean energy, using [solar power](13690575.md), and offers [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) retreats.[^cite_note-5]

Agricultural products include [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [cashews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashew), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean), [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut), [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), and [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea). The country's largest tea estates are at [Gurúè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur%C3%BA%C3%A8), while [Lioma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lioma) is a centre of soybean production.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDi_MatteoOtsukiSchoneveld201668-6] Fishing is especially productive of [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp), and [gemstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone) are mined at several sites.

[Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama) landed at the site of Quelimane in 1498. Shortly after, the Portuguese established a permanent presence, and many moved up the Zambezi into the interior, for numerous years the furthest inland European presence (although over time there was much intermarrying, and few residents were of purely Portuguese descent).
