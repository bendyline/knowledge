**Yomif Kejelcha Atomsa** ([Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic_language): ዮሚፍ ቀጄልቻ; born 1 August 1997)[^cite_note-WAprofile-3] is an Ethiopian [long-distance runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_running). He is the current world record holder in the [half marathon](1019031.md) and [10,000 m road race.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10K_run) Kejelcha is also the former world record holder in the short track [mile](5227492.md), and he is the second athlete, after [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md), to break [two hours in the marathon](83044214.md) in a record-eligible race, finishing in 1\:59\:41.

Yomif won the silver medal in the [10,000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_metres) at the [2019 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Athletics_Championships) and gold medals for the [3000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_metres) at the [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_IAAF_World_Indoor_Championships) and [2018 World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_IAAF_World_Indoor_Championships). He was the [2013 World Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Youth_Championships_in_Athletics) 3000 m champion and [2014 World Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_World_Junior_Championships_in_Athletics) [5000 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres) gold medallist.

## Early life

Yomif was born on 1 August 1997 in [Showa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa), [Oromia](503738.md), Ethiopia.[^cite_note-4] His father is Kejelcha Atoma, and his mother is Biritu Negese. He is the fifth of nine children.[^cite_note-Paris_2024_KEJELCHA-2]

Yomif was introduced to athletics by his friend Abebe Dribsa. Against his father's wishes, Yomif dropped out of high school in the ninth grade to pursue running, leading to his expulsion from home. Yomif secured school clearance to apply for the police force, and returned home at the suggestion of his mother.[^cite_note-Paris_2024_KEJELCHA-2] Yomif joined the Burayu Kenema Club, and moved to the outskirts of Addis Ababa to train.[^cite_note-Paris_2024_KEJELCHA-2]

## Running career

### 2013

Yomif made his international debut at the [2013 World Youth Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Youth_Championships_in_Athletics) in [Donetsk](562951.md), winning gold in the boys' [3000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_metres) with a time of 7\:53.56.[^cite_note-tp-5][^cite_note-6]

### 2014

In June, Yomif placed second over 3000 m in 7\:36.28 at the [Ostrava Golden Spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrava_Golden_Spike) elite meeting, losing only to Kenya's [Caleb Ndiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Ndiku); he defeated 2008 Olympic medallist [Nick Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Willis) and former world champion [Bernard Lagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lagat).[^cite_note-tp-5][^cite_note-7] Yomif won the [5000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres) at the [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_World_Junior_Championships_in_Athletics) held in July in [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon) that year, running 13\:25.19. He also won gold in the [3000 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2014_Summer_Youth_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Boys'_3000_metres) at the [Youth Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Summer_Youth_Olympics) in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) later that summer.[^cite_note-tp-5] He finished 2014 as the world [youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under-18_athletics) leader at both the 3000 and 5000 metres, and world [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under-20_athletics) leader at 3000 metres.[^cite_note-tp-5]

### 2015

Yomif opened his 2015 season at the [African Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_African_Junior_Athletics_Championships) in [Addis Ababa](52593.md), winning the 5000 m in a time of 14\:31.03.[^cite_note-tp-5][^cite_note-8] He made his [IAAF Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_IAAF_Diamond_League) debut in [Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Athletic_Super_Grand_Prix), placing fifth in the 3000 m. He then won a non-Diamond Race 5000 m event at the [Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefontaine_Classic) in Eugene, improving his personal best to 13\:10.54 and outkicking [Edwin Soi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Soi) and [Galen Rupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen_Rupp).[^cite_note-9] Yomif scored his first Diamond League victory the following week at the [Golden Gala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gala) in [Rome](25458.md), winning 5000 m in a world-leading 12\:58.39 and breaking 13 minutes for the first time.[^cite_note-tp-5][^cite_note-10] On 11 September, Yomif competed in the event at the [Brussels](1111662.md) Diamond League, setting a new personal best and world-leading time of 12\:53.98.[^cite_note-11]

### 2016

He started 2016 by winning the gold in the 3000 metres at the [World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_IAAF_World_Indoor_Championships) in [Portland](23503.md). On 27 August, Yomif won the event at the [Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_IAAF_Diamond_League) [Paris meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meeting_de_Paris) in a new world U20 record of 7\:28.19.

### 2018

At the European Athletics Outdoor Classic Permit Meeting in Sweden on 18 August, Yomif won the 3000 m with a time of 7\:28.00.[^cite_note-12] On 31 August, he placed third in the 5000 m at the [Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_IAAF_Diamond_League) Brussels Final. He ran a personal best of 12\:46.79, making him the seventh-fastest runner ever in the event.

### 2019

Yomif came within a hundredth of a second of [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md)'s world indoor [mile](5227492.md) record on 9 February at the [Millrose Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millrose_Games).[^cite_note-13] He ran an Ethiopian national record and a Millrose meet record of 3\:48.46, making him second-fastest athlete of all time in the event.[^cite_note-14] On 3 March, Yomif broke this [record](854191.md#Men_indoor_IAAF_era) with a time of 3\:47.01, slicing 1.44 seconds off El Guerrouj's mark set in 1997 (this record was later broken by [Yared Nuguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared_Nuguse) of the United States in February 2025, running 3\:46.63). He came short of the [1500 metres](2707254.md) world indoor record with his 3\:31.25 split en route, making him the third-fastest indoor performer in history. He was paced by [Erik Sowinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Sowinski), [Christian Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_%22Cash%22_Harrison), and Harun Abda.[^cite_note-15]

Yomif won the silver medal for the [10,000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_metres) at the [2019 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Athletics_Championships) held in [Doha](26214389.md), Qatar, finished eighth in the event at the delayed [2020 Tokyo Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) in 2021, and placed also eighth in the 5000 m at the [2022 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_Championships) in [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon).[^cite_note-WAprofile-3]

Yomif was a member of the [Nike Oregon Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Oregon_Project) from 2018 to 2019, where he trained alongside [Galen Rupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen_Rupp) and [Sifan Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sifan_Hassan).[^cite_note-Paris_2024_KEJELCHA-2]

### 2023

On 19 March, he missed [Berihu Aregawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berihu_Aregawi)'s [5 kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5K_run) world record of 12\:49 by just one second in [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), France to move up to second on the world all-time list.[^cite_note-16]

On 2 June, at the [Golden Gala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gala) meeting in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), Yomif narrowly finished second to [Mohamed Katir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Katir) over the 5000 metres, in a time of 12\:52.12, losing to Katir by only 0.03 seconds. On June 15, Yomif won the 5000 metres at the [Bislett Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislett_Games) in [Oslo](22309.md) in a time of 12\:41.73, the fifth-fastest time in 5000 metre history. Finishing second to Yomif was [Jacob Kiplimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kiplimo), who lost by 0.003 seconds in one of the smallest margins in distance running history (12\:41.725 to 12\:41.728, making them the joint fifth fastest 5000 metre runners in history at the time).[^cite_note-17]

At the end of the 2023 outdoor season, on 17 September, Yomif competed in the 3000 metres at the Diamond League Final ([Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefontaine_Classic)) in [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon). He finished second to [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen), in a time of 7\:23.64, losing by only one hundredth of a second.[^cite_note-18] At the time, this was the fourth fastest men's 3000m performance in history, behind Ingebrigtsen's 7\:23.63, [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md)'s 7\:23.09, and [Daniel Komen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Komen)'s world record of 7\:20.67.[^cite_note-19]

### 2024

On 30 May, Yomif contested the [5000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5000_metres) at the [Oslo Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislett_Games), finishing second to compatriot [Hagos Gebrhiwet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagos_Gebrhiwet). Hagos ran 12\:36.73 to become the second fastest man in history at the 5000 metre distance, behind [Joshua Cheptegei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Cheptegei)'s world record of 12\:35.36, while Yomif finished in a time of 12\:38.95, becoming the fourth fastest 5000 metre runner in history, behind [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele) (12\:37.35), Hagos, and Cheptegei.[^cite_note-20]

After exchanging leads with his teammates for most of the race, Yomif finished sixth in the 2024 Olympic 10,000 m.[^cite_note-21]

On 27 October, in the [2024 Valencia Half Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Half_Marathon), Yomif set a new [half marathon](1019031.md) world record of 57\:30, breaking [Jacob Kiplimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kiplimo)'s former half marathon world record of 57\:31 by one second.[^cite_note-22] In 2025, Kiplimo broke Yomif's record by 10 seconds, running 57\:20.[^cite_note-23]

### 2025

On 16 February, Kejelcha ran 26\:31 at Spain's 10K Facsa Castellón. His time was later ratified as a world record after [Rhonex Kipruto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhonex_Kipruto) was stripped of his previous world record time set at the 2020 Valencia 10k due to doping[^cite_note-24].

### 2026

On 26 April, Yomif ran his first ever marathon in the [2026 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Marathon), setting a debut time of 1\:59\:41, which now stands as the fastest debut marathon ever run, the second fastest in marathon history and the second-ever [marathon under two hours](83044214.md) under record-eligible conditions. He finished 2nd behind [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md) (1\:59\:30) and ahead of [Jacob Kiplimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kiplimo) (2\:00\:28). All three men finished under [Kelvin Kiptum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_Kiptum)'s previous world record of 2\:00\:35. He was coached by Ethiopian distance coach Gemedu Dedefo for the race.[^cite_note-25]

On 23 August, Kejelcha set a new [half marathon](1019031.md) world record at the [Buenos Aires Half Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Half_Marathon), winning in 56\:51. His time improved [Jacob Kiplimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kiplimo)'s previous world record of 57\:20 by 29 seconds.[^cite_note-26]

## Personal life

Yomif married Yadu Birhanu Biru in 2022. Yomif is an avid fan of the English Premier League football team [Chelsea](7473.md).[^cite_note-Paris_2024_KEJELCHA-2]

Yomif's running hero is compatriot [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele), a four time Olympic medallist. He trains daily, running between 120 and 140 miles a week. He often trains alongside [Telahun Haile Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telahun_Haile_Bekele), [Hagos Gebrhiwet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagos_Gebrhiwet) and [Birhanu Balew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birhanu_Balew).[^cite_note-Paris_2024_KEJELCHA-2]
