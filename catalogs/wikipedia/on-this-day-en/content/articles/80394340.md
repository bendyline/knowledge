The **2026 Bangkok gubernatorial election** was held on 28 June 2026. It was the twelfth election for the [governorship](20769534.md) of [Bangkok](56615.md). The election was held concurrently with the [2026 Bangkok Metropolitan Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bangkok_Metropolitan_Council_election) and [local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Pattaya_City_local_elections) in [Pattaya](712796.md).[^cite_note-ThaiPBSWorld-20260410-1]

The election followed the resignation of [Chadchart Sittipunt](70784800.md), who was elected as an independent candidate in the [2022 Bangkok gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bangkok_gubernatorial_election). Chadchart resigned on 18 May 2026 and sought a second term.[^cite_note-Thairath-Chadchart-resign-2] The [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(Thailand,_2024\)), which won all 33 Bangkok constituency seats in the [2026 Thai general election](74706351.md), nominated [Chaiwat Sathawornwichit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaiwat_Sathawornwichit) as its candidate.[^cite_note-Thairath-General-20260208-3][^cite_note-Standard-Chaiwat-4] The election resulted in a landslide victory by Chadchart, surpassing his own record in the 2022 election.[^cite_note-5]

## Background

Chadchart Sittipunt took office in 2022 after winning more than 1.3 million votes as an independent candidate. His four-year term was originally due to end in May 2026. On 10 April 2026, the [Election Commission of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_Thailand) set 28 June 2026 as the polling date for the Bangkok gubernatorial election, the Bangkok Metropolitan Council election and the Pattaya local elections.[^cite_note-ThaiPBSWorld-20260410-1]

The election took place a few months after the [2026 Thai general election](74706351.md), in which the [Bhumjaithai Party](20737916.md) won the largest number of seats and later formed a coalition government with the [Pheu Thai Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheu_Thai_Party).[^cite_note-ThaiPBS-2026-results-6][^cite_note-Reuters-Bhumjaithai-coalition-7] In Bangkok, the [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(Thailand,_2024\)) won all 33 constituency seats, giving the party a strong parliamentary base in the capital ahead of the gubernatorial election.[^cite_note-Thairath-General-20260208-3][^cite_note-AP-2026-election-8]

The People's Party nominated [Chaiwat Sathawornwichit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaiwat_Sathawornwichit), while the [Democrat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Thailand\)) nominated [Anucha Burapachaisri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anucha_Burapachaisri).[^cite_note-Standard-Chaiwat-4][^cite_note-Nation-Anucha-9] [Bhumjaithai](20737916.md) and [Pheu Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheu_Thai_Party) did not field gubernatorial candidates.[^cite_note-Standard-Bhumjaithai-no-candidates-10][^cite_note-Standard-PheuThai-11]

## Campaign

On 28 May 2026, Chadchart launched his campaign platform at Stadium One, presenting more than 250 policies under the slogan "a city of opportunity and hope for everyone". His campaign grouped its proposals into four broad areas: [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life), [livability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livability), economic opportunity and administrative efficiency.[^cite_note-Thairath-Chadchart-policy-14][^cite_note-Nation-Chadchart-policy-15]

Chaiwat campaigned on the [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(Thailand,_2024\)) platform of making Bangkok easier to live in, with proposals covering [family welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_policy), [small businesses](276495.md), [transport links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) and [public services](1291782.md). The party also announced that it would not install gubernatorial [campaign posters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_advertising) on roads or [utility poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_pole), citing safety, waste and obstruction concerns, while continuing to use posters for its council candidates within legal limits.[^cite_note-Isranews-Chaiwat-policy-16][^cite_note-Thairath-Chaiwat-posters-17]

Anucha launched his campaign for the [Democrat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Thailand\)) with proposals focused on transport, cleanliness, living standards, incomes and transparency in city administration.[^cite_note-BangkokPost-16-candidates-18] Mallika campaigned on the use of [artificial intelligence](1164.md) in city administration and proposed reviving district councils in Bangkok.[^cite_note-NationTV-Mallika-19]
