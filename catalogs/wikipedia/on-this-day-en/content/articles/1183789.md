The **Pakistan Navy** (**PN**) ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): پاک بحریہ, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Urdu): *Pāk Bahrí'a*, pronounced [\[ˈpaːk baɦɽia\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)) is the [naval warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare) branch of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md). The [Chief of the Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)), a four-star [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(Pakistan\)), commands the navy. The Pakistan Navy operates on [the coastline of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan) in the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) and [Gulf of Oman](12896.md). It was established in August 1947 following the [independence of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-5]

The primary role of the Pakistan Navy is to defend Pakistan's sea frontiers from any external enemy attack.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy_Public_and_Military_Affairs-6][^cite_note-IDEAS_-Pakistan_Navy-7] In addition to its war services, the Navy has mobilised its [war assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics) to conduct [humanitarian rescue operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Madad_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) at home as well as participating in [multinational task forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Maritime_Forces) mandated by the [United Nations](31769.md) to prevent seaborne terrorism and piracy off the coasts.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

The Pakistan Navy is a [volunteer force](9354649.md) which has been in conflict with neighbouring [India](14533.md) [twice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts) on its sea borders. It has been [repeatedly deployed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_military_deployments) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) to act as a military advisor to Gulf Arab states and other [friendly nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_Pakistan) during the events of multinational conflict as part of its commitment to the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-Knowledge_World,_Roy-Chaudhury,_2000-10]<sup>: 88 </sup> The Pakistan Navy has several components including [Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Aviation), [Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines), and the [Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Maritime_Security_Agency) (a [coast guard](146790.md)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Since its commencement, the defensive role of the navy has expanded from securing the [sealines](1365004.md) and becoming the custodian of Pakistan's [second strike capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_strike_capability) with an ability to launch underwater missile system to target enemy positions.[^cite_note-Reuters,_Zahra-Malik_and_Macfie,_2017-14]

The Chief of the Naval Staff is nominated by the Prime Minister and appointed by the President of Pakistan. Admiral [Naveed Ashraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naveed_Ashraf) is the incumbent chief since 7 October 2023.[^cite_note-Naveed-4]

## History

### Division of the Royal Indian Navy in 1947

Main articles: [Royal Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy), [India in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_in_World_War_II), and [Partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India)

> Today is a historic day for Pakistan, doubly so for those of us in the Navy. The [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan) has come into being and with it a new Navy – the Royal Pakistan Navy – has been born. I am proud to have been appointed to command it and serve with you at this time. In the coming months, it will be my duty and yours to build up our Navy into a happy and efficient force

— [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah), the [founder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaid-e-Azam) of [Pakistan](23235.md), addressing the men and officers of [HMIS Godavari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMIS_Godavari) in March 1948[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15]



The [Royal Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy)'s [rating sailors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy_ratings_rank_insignia) breaching the [Gates of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_of_Delhi) during the [rebellion against the British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857) in India in 1857

The Pakistan Navy came into existence on 15 August 1947 with the [establishment of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_of_Pakistan) as an independent state from the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15] The Armed Forces Reconstitution Committee (AFRC), under [Field Marshal Auchinleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal_Auchinleck), the last British [Commander-in-Chief, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_India) (C-in-C, India), divided the shares and assets of the [Royal Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy) (RIN) between India and Pakistan in a ratio of 2\:1,[^cite_note-Notion_Press,_Col._Chandar,_2018-16]<sup>: conts. </sup> despite Pakistan having inherited the high percentage of [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) areas on its coast and the large maritime area covering the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) on the West and the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) on the East.[^cite_note-17]<sup>: 90 </sup> Pakistan received two [sloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop-of-war), two [frigates](11464.md), four minesweepers, two [naval trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_trawler) and four harbour launches.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 45–46 </sup> In addition, India also objected to transfer any machinery at the [Bombay Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Dockyard) to Pakistan and further refused to part the machinery that happened to be on its soil.[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 90 </sup>

The navy endured a difficult beginning—of only 200 officers and 3,000 sailors inherited, the most senior was [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(naval\)) [HMS Choudri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Choudri), who had little experience in [military staffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_staff).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 45 </sup> Of the 200 officers, twenty had come from the executive branch of the [Royal Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy),.[^cite_note-20] Only six officers were [mechanical engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineers). There were no [electrical engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineers) or specialists to care for the electrical systems used for weapons or other machinery.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 47 </sup> The navy suffered perennial problems with inadequate staff, lack of operational bases, lack of financial support, and poor technological and personnel resources.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 45 </sup> It was the smallest military branch, and lacked importance in federal budgeting.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 46 </sup>

Defence plans were based primarily from the point of view of the [army](1965925.md) and [air force](377433.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 46 </sup> The navy lacked facilities and maintenance machinery, as the only naval dockyard on the [subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Subcontinent) was located in [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Dockyard) in [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 46 </sup>

To overcome these difficulties, the navy launched a recruitment programme for the young nation, starting in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan). Sustaining the programme there proved difficult; therefore, it was moved to concentrate recruitment in [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 46 </sup> Furthermore, procurement was greatly determined by the navy's role in previous wars. Most functions were in coastal defense and monitoring sea lanes, not in combat, making it difficult to justify spending on major weapons systems.[^cite_note-Springer,_Siddiqa-Agha-21]<sup>: 66 </sup>

The Navy's combat actions largely remained in absence during the [first war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947) with [India](14533.md) in 1947–48 as all the fighting was restricted to [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Warfare) and [aerial combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_warfare) missions.[^cite_note-W\._W\._Norton_&_Company-23]<sup>: 474 </sup> On operational planning, Captain HMS Choudri had engaged on commanding a former [RIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy) [destroyer](8779.md) from [Karachi](17123.md) to [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay) to oversee the evacuation of [Indian emigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhajir_\(Urdu-speaking_people\)) to Pakistan.[^cite_note-W\._W\._Norton_&_Company-23]<sup>: 474 </sup> In 1948, the Royal Pakistan Navy had to engage in humanitarian missions to evacuate [Indian immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Pakistan) trapped in disputed and hostile areas, with its frigates operating continuously.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 48 </sup>

Command and control of the new Royal Pakistan Navy was extremely difficult as [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan)'s [administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Khan_administration) had to extend the employment of a large number of the [Royal Navy](26061.md) [officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy_officer_rank_insignia) from the [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty), with [Rear Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_admiral_\(Royal_Navy\)) [James Wilfred Jefford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wilfred_Jefford), [RN](26061.md), appointed as the [Flag Officer Commanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Chief_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) (FOC) who worked on creating the contingency plan, "Short-term Emergency Plan (STEP)", to work up the [frigates](11464.md) and naval defences in case of escalation of the war at sea.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15][^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 48 </sup> In 1948, the Directorate-General for the [Naval Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Intelligence_of_Pakistan) (DGNI), a staff corps, was established under [Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_\(naval\)) [S. M. Ahsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Mohammad_Ahsan), who served as its first Director-General, in Karachi.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15] When the first war came to an end in 1948, the Navy temporarily established its [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHQ_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) in Karachi and acquired its first [O-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_and_P-class_destroyer) from the transfer by the [Royal Navy](26061.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 49 </sup>

The Royal Pakistan Navy greatly depended on the generous donations from the British [Royal Navy](26061.md) with two [Battle-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle-class_destroyer) destroyers, [PNS *Tippu Sultan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Onslow_\(G17\)) and [PNS *Tariq*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Offa_\(G29\)).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium-24] *Tippu Sultan* was [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_commissioning) on 30 September 1949, under Commander P.S. Evans, whilst *Tariq* was placed under the command of [Lieutenant-Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-Commander) [A. R. Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afzal_Rahman_Khan).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium-24] The two destroyers formed the [25th Destroyer Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Destroyer_Squadron), as [PNS *Jhelum*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMIS_Narbada_\(U40\)) and [PNS *Tughril*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Onslaught_\(G04\)), under Commander [Muzaffar Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffar_Hassan), also joined the Royal Pakistan Navy.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium-24]

In 1950, the Navy's [nationalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization_in_Pakistan) took place when many officers from the air force and army volunteered to join the navy and [NCOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommissioned_officer) gaining commission as an officers.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 50–51 </sup> Support from the army and air force to the navy led to the establishment of logistics and maintenance machinery with vigorous efforts directed towards integrating the navy presence in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan), thereby creating opportunities for people in East Pakistan to participate in the build-up.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 51 </sup>

In 1951, the [Pakistan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Government) called for appointing native chiefs of the armed forces, but it was not until 1953 that a native navy chief was appointed.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 51–52 </sup> The [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty), however, maintained the command of the Navy through Rear-Admiral Jefford who had native deputy chiefs of staff including [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) HMS Choudhri, Commodore Khalid Jamil, and [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(rank\)) M.A. Alavi.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 51–52 </sup>



PNS *Badr*, a destroyer, visiting [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) on a goodwill mission in 1957

During this time, a number of goodwill missions were carried out by the navy's warships, and non-combat missions were conducted under the auspices of the Royal Navy.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium-24] In 1951, HMS Choudhri's promotion papers as naval chief were approved by [Prime Minister](461896.md) [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan) but it was not until 1953 when HMS Choudhri was promoted as [vice admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral) and commander with the support from [army commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief_of_the_Pakistan_Army) General [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(general\)).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 52 </sup> He handed over the command of 25th Destroyer squadron to a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_Poland) naval officer, Commander [Romuald Nalecz-Tyminski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romuald_Nalecz-Tyminski?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Polish_Spirit-25]

In the mid-1950s, the [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Pakistan\)) awarded contracts to the [Corps of Engineers (Pakistan Army)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_Engineers,_Pakistan_Army) for the construction of the [Karachi Naval Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard).[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15] In 1954, several efforts were made to procure a [Ch-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_H-class_submarine) from the Royal Navy but was rejected by [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty) which agreed to loan the [Ch-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch-class_destroyer), [HMS *Chivalrous*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Chivalrous), which was renamed PNS *Taimur*.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 51–52 </sup> From 1953 to 1956, HMS Choudri bitterly negotiated with the United States over the modernisation of the navy and convinced the [U.S. government](195149.md) to provide monetary support for modernisation of ageing O–class destroyers and [minesweepers](217118.md), while commissioning the Ch–class destroyers from the Royal Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 54 </sup> British naval tradition was disbanded and cancelled when the [United States Navy](20518076.md)'s [advisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Assistance_Advisory_Group) were dispatched to the [Pakistani military](23369.md) in 1955.[^cite_note-Hamid_Hussain,_Defence_Journal_of_Pakistan-26]

With the promulgation of the [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1956) that established the [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism) featuring the [federalised government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Pakistan), the prefix *Royal* was dropped, and the service was re-designated the Pakistan Navy ("**PN**") with the [Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_jack) replaced the [Queen's colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colours,_standards_and_guidons#United_Kingdom) and the [White Ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Ensign) respectively in 1956.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15] The order of precedence of the three services changed from Navy–Army–Air force to Army–Navy–Air Force.[^cite_note-AuthorHouse,_2013-27]

In February 1956, the British government announced the transfer of several major surface combat warships to Pakistan Navy, including a cruiser and four destroyers to be purchased with funds made available under the U.S. [Military Assistance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Assistance_Program).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 54 </sup> In 1957, the Navy finalised the purchase of a [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Diadem_\(84\)) from the United Kingdom and used the government's own funds for the purchase which caused a great ire against Admiral Choudhri in the Finance Ministry.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 55 </sup>

In 1958, the Navy made an unsuccessful attempt to obtain [*Neptun*-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptun-class_submarine) from Sweden using the American security funds; it was halted by the United States and Pakistan's Finance Ministry despite the fact that the idea had support from [Army GHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 57 </sup> In 1958–59, the [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHQ_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) staff began quarrelling with the Army GHQ staff and the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Pakistan\)) (MoD) over plans regarding the modernisation of the navy that resulted in bitter [interservice rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interservice_rivalry) between army and navy and ended with Admiral Choudri's resignation to the [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiwan-e-Sadr) in 1959.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 57 </sup>

Proposal of attaining the [aircraft carrier](2219.md) was deferred due to financial constraints, forcing Pakistan to move towards establishing the formidable submarine command.[^cite_note-Lancer_Publications_and_Distributors,_Hiranandani,_2000-28]<sup>: 108 </sup> From 1956 to 1963, two destroyers, eight coastal minesweepers, and an [oiler](1580755.md) were procured from the United States and United Kingdom as a direct result of Pakistan's participation in the anti-Communist defence pacts [SEATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEATO) and [CENTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CENTO).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]

### War with India and subsequent war deployments (1965–1970)

Main articles: [India–Pakistan war of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_war_of_1965), [Operation Somnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Somnath), and [1967 PNS Ghazi's circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Ghazi)

After the bitter resignation of Vice-Admiral HMS Choudri in 1959, Vice-Admiral [Afzal Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afzal_Rahman_Khan) was appointed as the [Commander in Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Chief_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) in Navy who worked towards building relations with President [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(general\)) in retaining hopes for procuring a submarine despite financial constraints.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 58–59 </sup> The Royal Navy accepted the long awaiting requests from the Pakistan Navy for a regular visit to [Karachi Naval Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard) to provide first hand experience in submarine operations in 1960–61.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 58 </sup> The [Ayub administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Ayub_Khan) did not increase the financial funding of the navy at the expense to army and air force but he did not object to American contributions to train the Pakistan Navy in submarine operations.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 59 </sup> It was the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) that provided an insightful and crucial training support to Pakistan Navy enabling it to conduct operations in long range in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and the proposal of procuring the submarine was met with favourable views in 1963 due to the prospect of the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy) leasing a submarine to the [Indian Navy](604522.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 58 </sup> After seeing the U.S. contribution, the [United Kingdom](31717.md) decided to provide training and education to Pakistan Navy on submarine operations, and in 1964, [PNS *Ghazi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Ghazi) was commissioned from the United States under the [Security Assistance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Assistance_Program) (SAP).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 58 </sup>



[PNS *Ghazi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Ghazi) in [war theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1965) in 1965. She executed a [circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumnavigation) of Africa and Southern Europe in order to be refit in Turkey in 1968. Sunk and later destroyed in 1971 due to [depth chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_charge) deployed by [INS Rajput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Rotherham) which triggered [torpedoes](31424.md) to fired on its own.

Even though, neither the Navy nor the Air Force was notified of the [Kashmir incursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gibraltar) in 1965, the Navy was well-prepared at the time when the [second war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_war_of_1965) broke out between Pakistan and India in 1965.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18] The [naval chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) Admiral [Afzal Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afzal_Rahman_Khan) ordered all war units of the Pakistan Navy to take up defensive positions off the coast, but did not order any offensive operations in the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 60–61 </sup> As the [Indian Air Force](403355.md)'s repeated sorties and raids disrupted [PAF](377433.md) operations, the Navy assumed a more aggressive role in the conflict.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 61 </sup> On 2 September, the Navy deployed its first long-range submarine, PNS *Ghazi* under [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(rank\)) [K. R. Niazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamat_Rahman_Niazi) which was charged with gathering intelligence on Indian naval movements that stalked the diverting threats posed by the aircraft carrier [INS *Vikrant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Vikrant_\(1961\)).[^cite_note-Usman_Tariq_Pakdef-29]

On the night of 7/8 September, a naval squadron comprising four destroyers, one frigate, one cruiser, and one submarine, under the command of Commodore [S. M. Anwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._M._Anwar), launched [artillery operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dwarka)— an attack on the radar facilities used by the Indian Air Force in the small coastal town of [Dwarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarka).[^cite_note-Usman_Tariq_Pakdef-29] The operation ended with limited damage to the area.[^cite_note-Usman_Tariq_Pakdef-29] After gunnery bombardment, [*Ghazi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Ghazi) was deployed against the Indian Navy's [Western Naval Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Naval_Command) at Bombay on 22 September and ended her operations and reported safely back to [Karachi Naval Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard) on 23 September 1965.[^cite_note-Usman_Tariq_Pakdef-29]



Small ship PNS *Alamgir* in 1947

The Pakistan Navy explored the idea of installing Russian [missile system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styx_missile) on former British frigates but Soviets refrained from doing so due to objections from India.[^cite_note-auto-30]

After the war, the United States imposed an arms embargo on Pakistan and Pakistani military began exploring options for military procurement from China, France, and Soviet Union.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 62 </sup> The United Kingdom offered the Navy to jointly built the [Type 21 frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_21_frigate) but was rejected by Ayub administration that would only allow the financial capital to be spent on submarine procurement.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup>

In 1966, the Pakistan Navy established its own [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations) force, the [Navy Special Service Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_Navy) (Navy SSG) after the recommendations from the [United States Navy](20518076.md).[^cite_note-Global_Security.org-31] In 1966–70, Pakistan Navy had been well aware of massive [procurement and acquisitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_Indian_Navy) of [weapon systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon_systems_of_the_Indian_Navy) being acquired by India from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the danger it will posed to Pakistan.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> In 1966–69, there were series of unsuccessful talks of acquiring the warships from the Soviet Navy which ended with no yielding results.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> The Soviet Union offered to sell their [Osa-class missile boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osa-class_missile_boat) but the Pakistan Navy wanted the [Styx missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styx_missile) to be installed in frigates in a belief that the missile boats were not big enough to meet the Pakistani requirements to operate in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> The Russians later determined to their strategic interests lay with India and allowed the developing relationship with Pakistan to wither.[^cite_note-auto-30]<sup>: 283–288 </sup>

Difficulties arose between and after the arms embargo was lifted by the United States which lifted based strictly on [cash-and-carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_and_carry_\(wholesale\)) basis.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> Pleas for strengthening the Navy in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) were ignored due to monetary issues and financial constraints restricted the Navy's capabilities to function more efficiently.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> In 1968, the [*Daphné*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphn%C3%A9-class_submarine) submarines were procured from France while operating a [*Tench*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tench-class_submarine) submarine that had been maintained with the help of the [Turkish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Navy).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 64 </sup> Due to the Egyptian blockade of the [Suez Canal](29323.md), the Navy had to execute a notable submerged [circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumnavigation) operation from the Indian Ocean through the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) in order to undergo a refit program at the [Gölcük Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6lc%C3%BCk_Naval_Shipyard) in Turkey which was the only facility to manage the [refitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refit) and [mid-life upgrades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-life_update) of [military computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_computers) of the *Tench* class.[^cite_note-DefenceJournal,_Ahmed-32] Despite reservations harbouring by the Navy NHQ about the ageing *Ghazi*, she was sailed under the command of Commander [Ahmed Tasnim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Tasnim) starting from the [Karachi coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_coast) in Indian Ocean to [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope), [South Africa](17416221.md), through the Atlantic Ocean and ended at the east coast of the [Sea of Marmara](28863.md) where the Gölcük Naval Shipyard was located.[^cite_note-DefenceJournal,_Ahmed-32]

In 1968–69, the Navy NHQ staff began its tussle with the [AHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AHQ_\(Pakistan_Air_Force\)) staff over the issue establishing the [naval aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Air_Arm) who feared the loss of fighter jets and their pilots in the sea and was hostile towards this idea.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> The United States entered in discussing the transfer of [P3B Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lockheed_P-3_Orion_variants) aircraft to the Navy in 1970 with [Yahya administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Yahya_Khan) but were not procured until the end of the 1970s.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> In 1970, the [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations) between Pakistan and East Pakistan further deteriorated and the Navy knew that it was impossible to defend East Pakistan from approaching Indian Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 63 </sup> Series of reforms were carried when Navy's serious reservations were considered by the Yahya administration and [East Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistani) were hastily recruited in what was known as [Eastern Naval Command (Pakistan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Command_\(Pakistan\)) but this proved to be disaster for Navy when majority of Bengali naval officers and ≈3,000 sailors [defected to India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Jackpot) to join the [Awami League](404861.md)'s military wing– the *[Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini)*.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 64–65 </sup> Such events had jeopardised the operational scope of the Navy and the Navy NHQ staffers and commanders knew very well that it (Navy) was ill-prepared for the war and Pakistan was about to learn the consequences of disconnecting strategy from reality.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 65 </sup>

### India–Pakistan war of 1971

Main articles: [Operation Barisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barisal), [Operation Jackpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Jackpot), and [Indo-Pakistani Naval War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_Naval_War_of_1971)

By 1971, the Navy NHQ staffers and their commanders knew very well that the Pakistan Navy was poorly represented in East Pakistan (now [Bangladesh](3454.md)) and there was no main infrastructure to conduct defensive operation against the [Eastern Naval Command](30139022.md) of Indian Navy in [Bay of Bengal](47892.md).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 64 </sup> The Navy was only able conducted the [riverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverine_Warfare)-based operations that was being undertaken by the [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines) with the assistance from the [Navy Special Service Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_Navy), code named, [Barisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barisal), in April 1971.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy,_Historical_reference-15] Although, the Governor of East Pakistan, Vice-Admiral [S.M. Ahsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Mohammad_Ahsan), made efforts to increase the naval presence and significance in 1969 but the Indian Navy's Eastern Naval Command continued to pose a significant threat since it had capability of conduct operations in long-range areas.[^cite_note-Inter_Services_Public_Relations-33]

Furthermore, the defections from Navy's Bengali officers and sailors had jeopardise the Navy's operational scope who went onto join the [Awami League](404861.md)'s militant wing, the *[Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini)* in a program known as *Jackpot*.[^cite_note-Inter_Services_Public_Relations-33] Though, the program was disrupted by the Navy from further annihilation but the naval facilities were severely damaged due to this operation on 15 March 1971.[^cite_note-Inter_Services_Public_Relations-33] East-Pakistan's geography was surrounded by India on all three landward sides by the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) as the Navy was in attempt to prevent India from blocking the coasts.[^cite_note-Inter_Services_Public_Relations-33]

During this time, the Navy NHQ was housed in Karachi that decided to deploy the newly [MLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-life_update) *Ghazi* submarine on East while [*Hangor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hangor_\(S131\)) in West for the intelligence gathering purposes.[^cite_note-Inter_Services_Public_Relations-33]

> At the end of East-Pakistan crisis.... We (Eastern Command) had no intelligence and hence, were both deaf and blind with the Indian Navy and Indian Air Force pounding us day and night....

— Admiral Mohammad Sharif, to U.S. Admiral [Zumwalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmo_Zumwalt) in 1971[^cite_note-roy1995-34]



PNS *Nazim*, took part in the [Vietnam](32611.md) and [Korean Wars](16772.md) in the U.S. Navy as [USS *Wiltsie*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wiltsie). She is now serving in the [Pakistan Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Maritime_Security_Agency) as its "on-sea" headquarters.

With no naval aviation branch to guard the [Karachi port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_port), the Indian Navy breached the seaborne borders of Pakistan and successfully launched the [first missile attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Trident_\(Indo-Pakistani_War\)), consisting of three Soviet-built [Osa-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osa-class_missile_boat) [missile boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_boat) escorted by two [anti-submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine) patrol vessels on 4 December 1971.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35] Nearing Karachi's port area, the Indian Navy's squadron launched [Styx missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS-N-2_Styx) anti-ship missiles, which the obsolescent Pakistani warships had no viable defence against.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35] Two of the warships, [PNS *Muhafiz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Muhafiz) and [PNS *Khaibar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Cadiz_\(D79\)), were sunk, while [PNS *Shahjahan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Charity_\(R29\)) was damaged beyond repair.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35] After the attacks, the Indian Navy's missile boat squadron safely returned to its home base without sustaining any damages.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35]

On 8 December 1971, [*Hangor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hangor_\(S131\)) commanded by its [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(rank\)) [Ahmed Tasnim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Tasnim), sank the Indian frigate [INS *Khukri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Khukri_\(F149\)) off the coast of Gujarat, India— this was the first sinking of a warship by a submarine since [World War II](32927.md), and resulted in the loss of eighteen officers and one-seventy six sailors of the Indian Navy while the inflicting severe damages to another warship, INS *Kirpan*, by the same submarine.[^cite_note-gs-36] The [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) now covering for Karachi made several of the unsuccessful attempts to engage the Indian Navy's missile boat squadron by carrying out the aerial bombing missions over the [Okha Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okha,_India)– the forward base of the Indian Navy's missile boat squadron.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35] The Indian Navy retaliated with a [second missile attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Python) on Pakistan's coast on the night of 8 December 1971 when a small [flotilla](981096.md) of Indian vessels, consisting of a missile boat and two frigates, approached Karachi and launched a missile attack that sank the Panamanian [cargo ship](786056.md) *Gulf Star*, PNS *Dacca* and the British merchant ship SS *Harmattan* were damaged.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35]

The missile-based attacks were the complete success for the Indian Navy, and a psychological trauma for Pakistan Navy, the human and material cost severely cutting into its combat capability, nearly 1,700 sailors perished at the barracks.[^cite_note-Trident,_Grandslam_and_Python:_Attacks_on_Karachi-37]



[PNS *Zulfiqar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Zulfiqar_\(K265\)) in 1947. She was subjected to a serious [friendly fire](202484.md) incident during the [Indo-Pakistani Naval War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_Naval_War_of_1971) when the senior naval observers misidentified their own ship as a [smaller missile boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osa-class_missile_boat), giving clearance to the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) to mount a missile attack.

[^cite_note-Trident,_Grandslam_and_Python:_Attacks_on_Karachi-37]

&#x20;She was scrapped in metal in 1983.

The [commercial pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_pilot_license) from the [Pakistan International Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines) volunteered to conduct air surveillance missions with the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md), but this proved less than helpful when the Pakistan Navy's forward observer team, led by [Cdre.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) [A. W. Bhombal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._W._Bhombal) misidentified their own larger frigate, [PNS *Zulfiqar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Zulfiqar_\(K265\)), as an Indian missile boat, giving clearance to the [F-86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-86) fighter jets of the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) which made several attack runs before finally identifying *Zulfiqar* by the Navy NHQ.[^cite_note-Trident,_Grandslam_and_Python:_Attacks_on_Karachi-37] This serious [friendly fire](202484.md) incident resulted in further loss of navy personnel, as well as the loss of the ship, which was severely damaged and the Pakistan Navy's operational capabilities were now virtually extinct, and morale plummeted.[^cite_note-Trident,_Grandslam_and_Python:_Attacks_on_Karachi-37] The Indian Navy observers who watched the raid nearby later wrote in their [war logs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_diary) that the "PAF pilots failed to recognize the difference between a large PNS *Zulfiqar* frigate and a relatively small Osa missile boat."[^cite_note-Trident,_Grandslam_and_Python:_Attacks_on_Karachi-37] The PAF, however, contested this claim by holding Cdre. Bhombal of the responsibility of misidentifying his own warship and giving clearance to the PAF to mount an attack on their own ship.[^cite_note-Trident,_Grandslam_and_Python:_Attacks_on_Karachi-37][^cite_note-archives.org._Haidar_and_Chopra,_2009-38]

The Navy's only long range submarine, *Ghazi*, was deployed to the area but, according to neutral sources, it sank en route under mysterious circumstances.[^cite_note-39] Pakistani authorities state that it sank either due to internal explosion or detonation of mines which it was laying at the time.[^cite_note-40] The Indian Navy claims to have sunk the submarine.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

The submarine's destruction enabled the Indian Navy to enforce a blockade on then East Pakistan.[^cite_note-Defencejournal-45] According to the defence magazine, *Pakistan Defence Journal*, the attack on Karachi, Dhaka, Chittagong and the loss of *Ghazi*, the Navy no longer was able to match the threat of Indian Navy as it was already outclassed by the Indian Navy after the 1965 war.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35]

The damage inflicted by the Indian Navy and [Indian Air Force](403355.md) on the Navy stood at seven [gunboats](274471.md), one minesweeper, two destroyers, three [patrol craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_craft), eighteen cargo, supply and communication vessels, and large-scale damage inflicted on the naval base and docks in the coastal town of Karachi.[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35] Three merchant navy ships; Anwar Baksh, Pasni and Madhumathi;[^cite_note-46] and ten smaller vessels were captured.[^cite_note-Orbat-47] Around 1,900 personnel were lost, while 1413 servicemen (mostly officers) were captured by Indian forces in [Dhaka](56656.md).[^cite_note-losses-48] The Indian Navy lost 18 officers and 176 sailors[^cite_note-gs-36][^cite_note-49] and a frigate, while another frigate was damaged and a [Breguet Alizé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breguet_Aliz%C3%A9) naval aircraft was shot down by the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md).[^cite_note-M.A._Hussain_Tiwana_Defence_Journal-35]

According to one Pakistan scholar, [Tariq Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Ali), the Pakistan Navy lost half its force in the war.[^cite_note-50] Despite the limited resources and manpower, the Navy performed its task diligently by providing support to inter-services (air force and army) until the end.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51]

According to the testimony provided by the [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [Mohammad Shariff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Shariff) in 2015, the primary reason for this loss has been attributed to the High Command's failure in defining a role for the Navy, or even considering Navy as military in general.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51] Since then the Navy has sought to improve the structure and fleet by putting special emphasis on sub-surface warfare capability as it allows for the most efficient way to deny the control of Pakistani sea lanes to an adversary.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51] In a thesis written by Dr. [P. I. Cheema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervaiz_Iqbal_Cheema) in 2002, [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(President_of_Pakistan\)), who had enjoyed considerable influence on Pakistan's national politicians, did not fully understood the Navy as a [military service](938728.md) or neither comprehend the importance of safeguarding the [sea lines of communication](1365004.md), which prevented the development of the Navy as a potent force as it should have in the 1970s.[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 93 </sup>

### Restructuring and building towards modern Navy: 1972–1989

Main article: [Pakistan-United States military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan-United_States_military_relations)



The [*Daphné*-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphn%C3%A9-class_submarine) [PNS *Ghazi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Ghazi_\(S134\)) deployed during the [Operation Restore Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Restore_Hope) in 1991. She was purchased from the [Portuguese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Navy) in 1975 and joined the Pakistan Navy in 1977.

[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52]

After [1971 war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Surrender_\(1971\)), steps were taken to modernise and increase the operational scope of the Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18][^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 103 </sup><sup>: 65 </sup> Unlike the army or the air force, the naval officers were able to continue their military service with the Navy, and their promotions were relatively quicker than other military branches in 1972–74.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 141 </sup>

In January 1972, the [Bhutto administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto#President_of_Pakistan) formed the [POW Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971_Prisoners_of_War_Investigation) to investigate the number of [war prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoners_of_War) held by the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) in East and submitted the request to the [Supreme Court of Pakistan](843857.md) to investigate the causes of the war failure with India in 1971.[^cite_note-Lancer_International,_Rai,_1987-53]<sup>: 28 </sup> After concluding a quick visit in the [United States](3434750.md) in 1972, President [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto) used his administrative powers to [dishonourably discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishonorably_discharge) the [commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer) of five senior admirals in the Navy, appointing the junior most [Hasan Hafeez Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Hafeez_Ahmed) as the first [Chief of Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) of the Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer_Publishers,_Singh,_2008-54]<sup>: 61 </sup> In 1973, the [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHQ_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) was permanently moved to [Islamabad](51153.md) to provide synergy with the [Army GHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHQ_\(Pakistan_Army\)) in Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52]<sup>: contents </sup>[^cite_note-Lancer_Publishers_LLC,_Singh,_1980-55]



The *Gearing*-class PNS *Taimur* (former [USS *Epperson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Epperson)) was acquired from the United States in 1977. She was sunk as a target during a naval exercise in 2000.

[^cite_note-56]

In 1974, the [Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Air_Arm) branch was established with the transfer of the [Westland Sea King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Sea_King) helicopters from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) in 1975, followed by test firing the [surface-to-ship](140725.md) [Exocet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet) missile as a [befitting response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible_response) to the Indian Navy in 1979.[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52] With the ability to fire the land-based Exocet missile from a reconnaissance aircraft, the Navy became the first of its kind in the [South Asia](21566765.md) to acquire land-based ballistics missile capable long range reconnaissance aircraft.[^cite_note-Navy-57]<sup>: 77 </sup>

In 1976, the Navy moved towards successfully acquiring the military computers from the British firm, the [Ferranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferranti), to increase its defence's for its coastlines.[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52] The [War Enquiry Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamoodur_Rahman_Commission) noted the lack of strategic communication and the [grand strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_strategy) between the four-branches of the military during the [conflict and wars with India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts), recommending the establishment of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) to maintain strategic military communication between the inter-services and the federal government, that is to be chaired by the appointed [Chairman joint chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) as the government's principal military adviser.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 140 </sup> In 1976, Navy saw its first [four-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_star_admiral) rank admiral when [Mohammad Shariff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Shariff) was promoted to this rank, and later becoming the first admiral to be appointed as the [Chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) of [Joint Chiefs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) in 1977.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51][^cite_note-58]<sup>: 372 </sup> In 1977, the United States reportedly transferred the two [refitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refit) [*Gearing*-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearing-class_destroyer) to the Pakistan Navy, which were much superior to the British frigates, followed by obtaining more destroyers from the U.S. Navy in 1982–83.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 142 </sup>

During this time, the Navy to diversify its procurement with defence deals made with [China](5405.md), [France](5843419.md), and the United Kingdom but the dependence grew on China when the Navy acquired the anti-submarine warships that gave the Navy credible sea-denial capability.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59] In 1979, the France offered to sell their [*Agosta-70A*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agosta-class_submarine)-class submarine and was immediately acquired which were commissioned as [*Hurmat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hurmat) and [*Hashmat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hashmat).[^cite_note-DefenceJournal,_Ahmed-32] Induction of the *Agosta-70A* class gave Pakistan Navy a [depth advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_depth_ratings) over the Indian Navy, and gave the Navy an ability to conduct operations in deeper [Indian Ocean](14580.md) at wider range.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59][^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18][^cite_note-ABC_Publishing_House,_Ravi,_1982-60]<sup>: 143 </sup>

In 1982, the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_administration) submitted the proposal of [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Dollar)3.2 billion aid for Pakistan that was aimed towards economic uplift and security assistance to the [United States Congress](31756.md) as the Navy entered in successful negotiation of obtaining the [Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_\(missile\)) system, despite the strong Indian lobby opposing and objecting of this deal.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 144 </sup> In 1985, the Navy bought the [Mirage 5V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_V) aircraft for the naval role and were equipped with the [Exocet A39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet) missile that gives the capability of sea denial to the Pakistan Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 144 </sup> With the induction of the missile systems, long-range and depth endurance submarines, missiles destroyers, fighter aircraft, and establishment of the [Pakistan Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Maritime_Security_Agency), the Pakistan Navy eventually ended the Indian Navy's control over the Indian Ocean, and the Indian Navy's confidence that it could contain the Pakistan Navy at shorelines.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 145 </sup>

Eventually, the Pakistan Navy began its wartime deployment in [Middle Eastern countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_countries) through the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and deployed its war assets in Saudi Arabia in support of the U.S. Navy's fleet in wake of the events involving the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) and [tensions with Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra_incident_\(1981\)).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 145 </sup> In 1982, the Reagan administration approved [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Dollar)3.2 billion military and economic aid to Pakistan with Pakistan acquiring eight [*Brooke*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke-class_frigate) and [*Garcia*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garcia-class_frigate) frigates from the United States Navy on a five-year lease in 1988.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51] A [depot](843566.md) for repairs, [USS *Hector*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Hector_\(AR-7\)) followed the lease of these ships in April 1989. This was done due to the [Zia administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_regime)'s [co-operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone) with the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_administration) against the Soviet Union's [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51]

### Self reliance, engagement and covert operations (1990–1999)

Main articles: [Karachi Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Affair), [Atlantique Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantique_Incident), [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War), and [Revolt of the Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Admiral)



The [*Garcia*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garcia-class_frigate) and [*Brooke*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke-class_frigate) [guided missile destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_destroyer) (FFG) being transferred to Pakistan Navy in 1986. All were returned to United States in 1993–95 when the renewing of lease with an option of purchase was denied by the United States.

After the Russian [troops withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_troop_withdrawal_from_Afghanistan) from [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Afghanistan) in 1989, the [Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_H._W._Bush) imposed the [arms embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressler_amendment) on Pakistan by uncovering the existence of the covert [atomic bomb program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_its_Nuclear_Deterrent_Program) to the United States Congress, which ultimately refrained the transfer of the maritime patrol aircraft, missile systems, and defence software on 1 October 1990.[^cite_note-The_Army_Press,_Islamabad-51] With the expiration of the lease of the [*Garcia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garcia-class_frigate) and [*Brooke*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke-class_frigate) guided missile frigates, the Navy had to return the frigates to the United States that were sold to India for scrapped metals, and Navy to faced the problems for adequate funding towards the modern Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 185 </sup> The embargo seriously impaired the Navy's operational scope and paralysed its ability to operate in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), since the Navy's [fleet was composed of entirely the former U.S.-built warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Ships_transferred_from_the_United_States_Navy_to_the_Pakistan_Navy).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 185 </sup>

Since 1987, the Pakistan Navy had been interested in acquiring the [Type 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_21_frigate) frigates from the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the Navy turned to the [Royal Navy](26061.md) for an immediate purchase which was approved in 1993 whose expensive refitting and technological upgrades had to carried out by Pakistan itself at their [Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard) in Karachi over the years.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 185 </sup> In 1994, the Pakistan Navy entered in [lengthy, complicated, and controversial negotiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Affair) with France to acquire the long-range submarine technology by dismissing the idea of procuring [nuclear-powered submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine) from China due to noise issue that the Indian Navy was quiet able to track.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 183–185 </sup>[^cite_note-NTI_1990-62] Despite embargo, the United States Navy maintained its relations with Pakistan Navy, inviting the Pakistanis to participate in the *Inspired Siren* in 1994, and gave the Pakistan Navy instructions and run down on the nuclear submarine and aircraft carrier operations.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 185 </sup> In an attempt to warm the political relations with the United States, the Pakistani military joined the [U.S. actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_Somalia_II) in the [Somali Civil War](3070332.md), conducting [wartime patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_United_Shield) in the Somali coast.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52]



The Navy's [P3C Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3C_Orion) taking a flight in 2010. One of its sister aircraft was involved in serious crash during its routine mission in 1999, claiming valuable lives.

In 1994, the Navy was [deployed in support of the U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Restore_Hope) and extended its support in 1995 to participate in [Operation United Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_United_Shield) to conclude its side of operation after evacuating personnel and equipment of the [army](1965925.md), [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines), and air force.[^cite_note-64] By 1996, the Brown amendment was introduced that allowed the uplifting of the embargo on Pakistan, allowing the transfer of the maritime patrol aircraft to the Navy.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 185 </sup>

By 1997, the [controversy over the technology transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Affair) from France had tarnished the public image of the Navy with the arrest of [naval chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansurul_Haq) when several [cases were levelled on political and military leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_charges_against_Benazir_Bhutto_and_Asif_Ali_Zardari) of the Navy.[^cite_note-NTI_1990-62] Despite India's strong objections in France, the [air-independent propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-independent_propulsion) was transferred to Pakistan which built the [*Agosta 90B*-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agosta_90B-class_submarine), capable to operating in Indian Ocean and at higher submarine depth.[^cite_note-NTI_1990-62] In 1999, the Navy saw the [public disagreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Admiral) with the [federal government](1897833.md) over the issue of [Pakistan Army's engagement with Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_war) in Kashmir and over the rightful appointment of the Admiral [Fasih Bokhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasih_Bokhari) as [Chairman joint chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee).[^cite_note-Repro_India_Ltd.-65] Pakistan Navy was forced to deploy its existing war assets when the Indian Navy deployed its warships near [Korangi Creek Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korangi_Creek_Cantonment) and [Port of Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Karachi) with their codename: *Operation Talwar*.[^cite_note-Lieutenant-General_F.S._Lodhi,_PA,-66]

On 10 August 1999, a serious incident took place in [Sir Creek region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Creek) when the Indian Air Force shot down the [Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Air_Arm) aircraft that resulted in deaths of 16 naval personnel, mostly officers.[^cite_note-AuthorHouse,_Baig-67]<sup>: 62 </sup> On 29 August 1999, another aircraft of the Navy, [P3C Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P3C_Orion), was lost due to an accident with the loss of twenty one lives.[^cite_note-68]<sup>: 537 </sup>[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

Over the issue of the Indian Air Force's shot down of the aircraft, the Navy filed a lawsuit against the Indian Air Force at the [International Court of Justice](14918.md), but the claim was later dismissed due to over-reaching of the court's mandate.[^cite_note-AuthorHouse,_Baig-67]<sup>: 62–63 </sup>[^cite_note-The_Independent-71]

#### War on terror in Afghanistan and operations in North-West (2001–present)

[PNS *Shah Jahan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Shah_Jahan_\(D-186\)) and [PNS *Tippu Sultan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Tippu_Sultan_\(D-185\)), the [*Tariq*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq-class_destroyer) [guided missile destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_destroyer), participating in *Exercise Inspired Siren* in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) in 2002.

After the [9/11 terrorist attacks](5058690.md) in the [United States](3434750.md), the sanctions on Pakistan were eventually uplifted, allowing the Navy to procure the U.S.-built weapon systems and warships to regain its ability to operate in the Indian Ocean as it became involved in war preparations during the [standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001-2002_India-Pakistan_standoff) with India in 2001–02.[^cite_note-Lieutenant-General_F.S._Lodhi,_PA,-66] In 2001, the Navy took serious consideration of deploying the [nuclear weapons](21785.md) on its submarines although none of the nuclear weapons were ever deployed in the submarines.[^cite_note-NTI_1990-62]

In 2003–04, there were several proposals made for acquiring the vintage [aircraft carriers](2219.md) but the Navy itself had dismissed the idea since the country has not aspired to have an aircraft capability.[^cite_note-Routledge_Dittmer-72]<sup>: 79 </sup> In 2002–03, the Pakistan Navy deployment took place in the Indian Ocean, participating in the naval drills to combat terrorism from seaborne platforms, and eventually entered in defence negotiations with China for acquiring the technology to designing and building the guided missile frigates— the [F-22P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-22P_Zulfiquar-class_frigate) guided missile frigates were eventually built it in 2006–15.[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52]



An operative of the [Navy Special Service Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_Navy) (SSGN) is silhouetted by the setting sun abroad PNS *Babur* while under way in the Indian Ocean in 2007.

Since 2004, the Navy's deployment took place in Indian Ocean, playing a crucial role in the multinational [NAVCENT](9880750.md) in [Bahrain](18933277.md), and took the leadership of the [CTF-150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Task_Force_150) and [CTF-151](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Task_Force_151) as well as taking active participation in the [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in 2006–10.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] In 2008, the task force group consisting of [PNS *Badr*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Badr_\(D-184\)), [PNS *Shah Jahan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Shah_Jahan_\(D-186\)), [PNS *Nasr*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Nasr), and the Pakistan Air Force's Explosive Ordnance Disposal participated in the *Exercise Inspired Union* with the U.S. Navy in the Indian Ocean to develop skills in a prevention of seaborne terrorism.[^cite_note-76]

Its deployment in the [war on terror](13425800.md) also included their actions in the [War in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) when the Navy's special forces were deployed to take participation in the Operations: [*Black Thunderstorm*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Black_Thunderstorm), [*Rah-i-Nijat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rah-e-Nijat), [*Mehran*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Mehran_Operation), and the [*Help*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Madad_\(Pakistan_Navy\)).[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52]

Despite its [seaborne mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Interdiction_Operations), the Navy had played an active role in controlling the insurgency in [former tribal belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_North-West_Pakistan) in [Western Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_North-West_Pakistan), mostly taking roles in managing [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics) and [intelligence gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_gathering) as well as conducting ground operations with the army in Western areas to track down the [al-Qaeda](1921.md) operatives.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-77] From 2010 to 2011, the Navy was in a brief direct conflict with the violent [TTP group](18953699.md) and [al-Qaeda](1921.md), and its Naval Intelligence was able to track down the infiltrated militants within the ranks of the Navy.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

In 2015, the Navy was deployed in support of the [Saudi-led blockade of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi-led_blockade_of_Yemen) after accepting the request from the [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-80] As of current, the Navy continues increase its operational scope in the Indian Ocean and reportedly successfully entering in defence talks with Turkey to jointly built the [MILGEM project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MILGEM_project) in Pakistan in 2018–2019 while it had earlier announced to start the building the program of the [nuclear submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine) for its current operational capabilities in 2013.[^cite_note-81]

### Principal Staff Commands and Principal Staff Officers

Main articles: [List of serving admirals of the Pakistan Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_serving_admirals_of_the_Pakistan_Navy) and [Chief of Naval Staff (Pakistan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\))

Leadership in the Navy is provided by the [Minister of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Minister_of_Pakistan), leading and controlling the direction of the department of navy from the Naval Secretariat-II at the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Pakistan\)), with the [Defense Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Secretary_of_Pakistan) who is responsible for the bureaucratic affairs of the army's department.[^cite_note-82] The [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) sets the role of the elected [President of Pakistan](419460.md) as the civilian [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Chief) of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md) while the [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md) served as the [Chief Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Executive) of the Pakistan Armed Forces, both the people-elected civilians, the President and Prime minister, maintains a [civilian control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military).[^cite_note-83]

The [Chief of Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) (CNS), an appointed four-star rank admiral, is a principal military adviser on the [naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare)/[maritime security](8111405.md) affairs to the [Federal government](1897833.md) and is a senior member of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) (JCSC)— a military body that advises and briefs the elected civilian [Prime Minister](461896.md) and its [executive cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Pakistan) on national security affairs and operational military matters under the [Chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee) of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84]

The war functions of the Navy is controlled from the single combat headquarters, the [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Headquarters_\(Pakistan\)), located in [Islamabad](51153.md) at vicinity of the [Joint Staff Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_HQ_\(Pakistan\)) and the [Army GHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHQ_\(Pakistan_Army\)) in [Rawalpindi Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Cantonment) in [Punjab](24751.md) in [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] The Chief of Naval Staff controls and commands the Navy at all levels of operational command, and is assisted by number of [Principal Staff Officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_officer) (PSOs) (Staff Commanders) who are commissioned at the three-star rank and two-star rank admirals. The Staff Appointments marked in the light goldish yellow colour are the most important seats at NHQ which play a very important administrative role for the proper functioning of the Pakistan Navy and its assets.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84]

Due to the influence from the [Royal Navy](26061.md) and later by the [United States Navy](20518076.md) since its earliest inception, the Pakistan Navy has a unique command structure and the navy's functionality is divided in various branches.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]

There are seven military staff commands in the navy that are in fact administrative, directed by the several appointed [Deputy Chief of the Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chief_of_the_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) (DCNS) and often assisted by the Assistant Chief of the Naval Staff (ACNS) holding the rank of commodore a one-star rank senior officer reporting directly to their respective [Deputy Chief of the Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chief_of_the_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) (DCNS).[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup> The Deputy Chiefs Of Naval Staff are usually holding either the [two-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-star_admiral) or [three-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-star_rank) ranks.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup>The each and appointed Deputy Chief of the Naval Staff head or commander of their respected branch reports directly to the [Chief of the Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) (CNS) at [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHQ_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) in Islamabad of their respected command.

### Naval commands in field and commanders

Since its restructuring and reorganisation over the several years, the Pakistan Navy now operates eight operational and tactical field commands and also two major type commands, two of the important commands of aviation and submarines are reporting directly to the senior Pakistan Fleet Command.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] Each command is headed by a senior flag officer who usually holds a ranks of [three-star rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-star_rank): [Vice-Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Admiral) and [two-star rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-star_rank): [Rear-Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear-Admiral).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] The appointment to the senior [fleet commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_commander) known as [Commander, Pakistan Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander,_Pakistan_Fleet) leads the navy's entire fleet with a responsibility of deploying the entire combat formations of the navy.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup>

Geographically, there are three administrative commanders, such as Commander Karachi (COMKAR), [Commander Central Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_Central_Punjab) (COMCEP), and Commander Northern (COMNOR), who administer the bulk of naval installations, offshore establishments, and training facilities besides the seven oceanic based commands.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup>

In 2012, the Pakistan Navy established the [Naval Strategic Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Strategic_Forces_Command_\(Pakistan\)) that has area responsibility of exercising the deployment of sea-borne [nuclear weapons](21785.md) and is described by the military as the "custodian of the nation's nuclear [second strike capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_strike_capability)."[^cite_note-88]

### Special operations forces

Main articles: [Special Service Group Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_Navy), [1st Marines Battalion (Pakistan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines#Marines_bases,_camps,_and_battalions), and [Naval Strategic Forces Command (Pakistan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Strategic_Forces_Command_\(Pakistan\))



The [Navy SSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_\(Navy\)) conducting the force-protection and underwater special forces training with their [United States Navy](20518076.md) counterparts, the [U.S. Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Navy_SEALs) in 2011.

The [Special Service Group Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_Navy) (SSG(N)), colloquially known as the **SSGN**[^cite_note-90] is an elite unit that conducts [unconventional warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_warfare), [combat diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving), [naval interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_interdiction), and [asymmetric warfare operations](46863.md), established under the guidance of the [United States Navy](20518076.md)'s [SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs) in 1966.[^cite_note-Navy_Special_Service_Group-91]



[Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines) dressed in operational camouflage uniforms, during training with the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) in 2009.

The Navy Special Service Group is headquartered at [PNS Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Iqbal) in Karachi where the physical conditioning and weapon tactics training take place.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_W\.S._Khan-92] The Navy Special Service Group's specialisation further includes training and mastery in the [visit, board, search, and seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visit,_board,_search,_and_seizure) methods, [naval interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_interdiction), and [security operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_security_operations) to prevent seaborne-based terrorism.[^cite_note-93]

The Navy Special Service Group is a tighter unit composed of highly qualified and selected personnel who are modelled on and inspired by the [U.S. Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs) training and tradition.[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_W\.S._Khan-92] The actual number of personnel of Navy Special Service Group is classified and their deployment are also subjected to [classified information](252857.md).[^cite_note-Dawn_Newspaper,_W\.S._Khan-92]

In 1970–71, the Navy established the [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines) to support the [amphibious warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) operations and were initially influenced by the [United States Marines Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marines_Corps) but the Marines component was decommissioned by the federal government in 1974.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Naval_Inter-Services_Public_Relations_(Naval_ISPR)-94] On 14 April 1990, the Pakistan Marines were again recommissioned in the Navy with about 2,000 personnel.[^cite_note-95] The advanced training of the Marines are often takes place with the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) at their School of Infantry and Tactics in Quetta in Balochistan.[^cite_note-ISPR_Marines-96]

The [1st Marines Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines#Marines_bases,_camps,_and_battalions), the special operation unit, of the Pakistan Marines is specifically trained by the Pakistan Army to conduct infiltration and [anti-aircraft warfare](146640.md) operations. The 1st Battalion is currently deployed in [Sir Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Creek).[^cite_note-Pakistan_Marines_Gharida_Farooqi-97]

## Military philosophy

### Combat doctrine



[PNS *Hamza*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hamza), the [air-independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-independent_propulsion) powered [*Agosta 90B*-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agosta_90B-class_submarine), being prepared to go through the [depth tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_depth_ratings) in 2007. The *Agosta 90B* submarines are [capable of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile_submarine) launching the [nuclear-based cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine-launched_cruise_missile) from an underwater platform.

[^cite_note-The_Diplomat,_Panda-98]

[^cite_note-99]

The military doctrine and philosophy of the Pakistan Navy is primarily directed towards preventing the [Indian Navy](604522.md) repeating the 1971 blockade of the Pakistani coasts.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] From 1947 until 1971, the Pakistan Navy was effectively little more than a [coast guard](146790.md) because the Government of Pakistan did not give importance to the strategy of protecting the [sea lines of communication](1365004.md).[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 68 </sup> In 1971, the [Indian Navy](604522.md) ultimately played a decisive role by enforcing a blockade of [Chittagong](380451.md) and [Karachi](17123.md), the only maritime outlets of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) and [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) respectively. The Navy was unable to break the blockade leading to Pakistan's economic and military resources being severely drained and communication was limited between the two wings of the country. Subsequently, the [federal government](1897833.md) increased the funding of the Navy.[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 97–98 </sup>[^cite_note-100]<sup>: 94 </sup>

Since 1971 the Navy tactical doctrine has included the acquisition, development, employment, and aggressive deployment of the long-range and [depth reaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_depth_ratings) submarines in an effort to target and destroy its adversaries by attacking surface warships before reaching the country's ports.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] The [mining](2206580.md) of the Karachi's harbour is also taken as a serious consideration of preventing the enemy from launching the missile attacks in the port city of Karachi.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84]

In 1983–85, the Navy commissioned the [Dassault Mirage 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_5) from [France](5843419.md) whose weapon system included the naval variant of the [Exocet missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet_missile) and are aimed towards engaging the Indian Navy's aircraft out to 500 kilometres (310 mi) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84][^cite_note-101]

The routine deployment of the surface fleet as part of the [Combined Task Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Task_Force_151) provides the opportunity to the safeguard the sealines of communications.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] Since 1999, the [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines)'s special reconnaissance forces has been deployed in the [Sir Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Creek) region are aimed towards offshore protection against the incursions from the Indian Army's [Para Commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_Commandos_\(India\)) from the sea while taking the initiatives of deployment of the special forces groups behind the enemy lines through insertion by the [HALO/HAHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HALO/HAHO) airdrop or by using the midget submarines.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84]

Responding to the development of the [INS Arihant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Arihant), the Pakistan Navy reportedly announced the launch of the nuclear powered submarine program to counter the submarine threat in 2012.[^cite_note-ARY_News,_Nuclear_submarine-102]

The Navy eventually pushed for attaining the naval-based nuclear [second-strike capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-strike_capability) in 2017 when the [ISPR](7718433.md) announced the Pakistan Navy's to have attained the sea-based second strike capability when it launched the nuclear [SLCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine-launched_cruise_missile) based on the [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)) cruise missile, though the range of the SLCM remains to be at the short range.[^cite_note-103]

The names of the commissioned warship and noncombat vessels of the Pakistan Navy are prefixed with the capital letters "**PNS**"— the Pakistan Navy Ship.[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] The naming convention of the ship is selected by the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Pakistan\)), often honouring important people or places in the history of Pakistan, and then commissioned by the [President of Pakistan](419460.md).[^cite_note-Official_Website_-_Frigates-104][^cite_note-Official_Website_-_Missile_Boats-105]

The Surface Fleet, established in 1947, is a pivotal component of the Navy with a crucial role in maintaining the military balance with the [Indian Navy](604522.md) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), taking part in multinational task forces to prevent seaborne terrorism and piracy.[^cite_note-106]

The Navy currently operates approximately 100 vessels including ones used by the Maritime Security Agency (MSA) and [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines).[^cite_note-PakDef_Military_Consortium,_Khan-84] In the current inventory, the Navy has a combination of Turkish, American, Chinese and locally produced ships including the American [*Oliver Hazard Perry*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hazard_Perry-class_frigate) frigate, Turkish-designed [Babur class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur-class_corvette), and locally-built [*Zulfiquar*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfiquar-class_frigate) frigate (built with Chinese assistance). Decommissioning of the ageing [*Tariq* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq-class_destroyer) destroyer has been completed after the construction of additional missile guided [*Zulfiquar*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfiquar-class_frigate) frigates in Pakistan by 2021 and the acquisition of the [Type 054A frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_054A_frigate) from China that started in 2017.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-informationxone-110]

The *Tariq* class were a class of [guided missile destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_destroyer) that were in service with the 25th Destroyer Squadron. In addition to the Navy's operations of warships, the Navy operates coast guard ships intended for the Maritime Security Agency – most are imported from China while others are locally built to guard Pakistan's seaborne borders from illegal activities, followed by ten of the locally designed and built [patrol boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Patrol_Boat) for the [Coast Guards](2014150.md) for the safety and policing of the [beaches in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_beaches_in_Pakistan).[^cite_note-116]

### Submarines

[PNS *Hashmat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hashmat) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) in 2014

Established in 1964, the Submarine Command is a major component of the Navy whose primary mission is to conduct clandestine military [reconnaissance](146911.md) for [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_cycle_management) and carry out [precision strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_Attack_Missile) on enemy positions from underwater during war.[^cite_note-Submarine_Force-122][^cite_note-NIT_Pakistan_Directorate-1][^cite_note-Defence_Industry_Daily-123]



[PNS *Hamza*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hamza), an [air-independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-independent_propulsion)-powered [*Agosta 90B*-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agosta_90B-class_submarine), being prepared to go through the [depth tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_depth_ratings) in 2007

[^cite_note-The_Diplomat,_Panda-98]

There are eight submarines in active service including the [*Hashmat*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashmat-class_submarine) submarines, based on the [*Agosta-70A* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agosta-class_submarine), and three [Italian](14532.md)–designed and locally–built [midget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midget_submarine) [*Cosmos*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos-class_submarine) (designated as X-Craft) submarines.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup> The submarines are powered with [diesel-electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel-electric_submarine) and [air-independent propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-independent_propulsion).[^cite_note-PN-124]

The *[Agosta-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agosta-class_submarine)* submarines are equipped with an air-independent propulsion system giving a capability of deeper dives and the ability to submerge for a longer period of time without detection.[^cite_note-125] They are armed with [Exocet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet) and [Babur-III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)) missiles, which can be launched from underwater.[^cite_note-The_Diplomat,_Gady,_2019-126] Two of the three *Agosta-90B* class have undergone refitting and modernisation by the Turkish firm, STM.[^cite_note-The_Diplomat,_Gady,_2019-126]



Yuan class diesel electric submarine

In 2014, Pakistan Navy entered in defence discussions with the [People's Liberation Army Navy](24918.md) for the procurement of the [Yuan-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_039A_submarine) [AIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-independent_propulsion) powered submarines, and eventually succeeded when the technology transfer agreement was signed between [two nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_China) in April 2015. This [national submarine program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy_SSP_programme) is known as [*Hangor*-class submarine](69950589.md) features air-independent propulsion is being constructed as a joint-venture with China with the expectation of being commissioned between 2023 and 2028.[^cite_note-127] In a direct response to [INS *Arihant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Arihant), the Pakistan Navy eventually succeeded getting the proposal approved for building the [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine) submarine whose delivery is expected to between 2028, according to the Pakistan Navy officials.[^cite_note-ARY_News,_Nuclear_submarine-102]

In April 2014, the Navy announced that submarine operations would move from [Naval Base Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard) to the new [Jinnah Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinnah_Naval_Base) in [Ormara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormara).[^cite_note-128]

Submarine training takes place at PNS *Abdoze* in Karachi. In May 2008, the Navy established the Fleet Acoustic Research and Classification Centre to validate submarine safety standards and to act as an underwater listening post to track unauthorised submarines.[^cite_note-129]

The Aircraft in the Pakistan Navy provides the logistical support to the navy's readiness at all level of commands and serves as the supply platform, through helicopters, to conduct the [search and rescue](172599.md), [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations), [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) (ASW), and the [anti-surface warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-surface_warfare) (ASuW).[^cite_note-Naval_Air_Arm,_Navy-144] Unlike the [Indian Navy](604522.md), the Pakistan Navy does not have the rapid [aircraft carrier](2219.md) based strike capability but relies its aerial strike operations from clear and traditionally long [landing platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_strip) built at the [Mehran Naval Air Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Mehran) in Karachi.[^cite_note-145]<sup>: 66 </sup> After realising the failure to protect the harbour from the attacks of the [Indian Navy](604522.md) in 1971, the Navy took the research on using the aircraft on sea in an attempt to lessen the dependence on the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md), which already covers the airspace of Pakistan, and established the naval aviation branch, the [Naval Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Air_Arm), in 1974.[^cite_note-Naval_Air_Arm,_Navy-144][^cite_note-146] whose initial pilot training takes place at the [PAF Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAF_Academy) in [Risalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risalpur).[^cite_note-Dunya_News,_Khan,_2018-147]

In addition, there are numbers of aircraft active in the [Pakistan Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Maritime_Security_Agency) (MSA).[^cite_note-148]

### Weapon systems and Air defence

See also: [List of missiles of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_missiles_of_Pakistan)



The [POF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Ordnance_Factories) [G3P4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G3P4) is a standard rifle issued by the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Ministry_of_Defence) as seen by the Navy's enlisted personnel in 2009.

[^cite_note-POF_Standard_rifle-149]

Current weapon systems in the Pakistan Navy is entirely composed and focused towards missiles, serving as both weapons or a defence from a threat.[^cite_note-DAWN.COM,_Air_Defense-150][^cite_note-Pakistan_Defense_Consortium,_SI_Shah,_2003-151]

In 1971 with the Indian Navy's introduction of [anti-ship missiles](140725.md), Navy had the strong emphasis on classically using the [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_artillery) and [ammunition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_ammunition) focusing towards the vintage tactics witnessed in the previous naval wars fought in the [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-News_International,_2012-152]

The Navy's primary air defence included the usage of the [CAMM-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAMM_\(missile_family\)), [LY-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-16), [FM-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-7#HQ-7B_\(FM-90\)), [FN-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN-6), [Anza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anza_\(missile\)) and the [Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(missile\)) system.[^cite_note-DAWN.COM,_Air_Defense-150]

The primary and standard rifle issued for the Navy is the [POF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Ordnance_Factories) [G3P4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G3P4), which is standard issue by the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Ministry_of_Defence), and is based on the German design of the [Heckler and Koch G3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_G3) rifle.[^cite_note-POF_Standard_rifle-149]

The Navy's ground based air defence is entrusted with the Pakistan Marines who received their weapons training at the School of Infantry and Tactics in Quetta with the Pakistan Army.[^cite_note-ISPR_Marines-96][^cite_note-Press_Release,_PN-153]

In 2016, the Navy inducted the [Harbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)#Harbah) [cruise missile](6590.md), based on the [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)) design, that was test fired from the PNS Himmat– the [*Azmat*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmat-class_fast_attack_craft) missile boat.[^cite_note-154] The Navy operates the [Zarb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarb_\(missile\)) cruise missile that was first test fired on 10 April 2016.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

The cruise missiles system in the Navy, the [Harbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)#Harbah) and [Babur–III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)) are the variants and derivatives of the improved version of the first cruise missile that entered in the service of the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md)— the [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_\(cruise_missile\)) cruise missile system.[^cite_note-157]

- [FN-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN-6), the [man-portable air-defense systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-portable_air-defense_systems), tested on 25 December 2010 by Pakistan Marines with a range of 6 km and altitude ≈3.5 km.

- [Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(missile\)) shoulder-fired surface-to-air missile, test fired on 25 December 2010 by Pakistan Marines.

The military uniform in the Pakistan Navy includes the full white-worn [service uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Service_Uniform) as seen in the footage, and is worn on regular basis by the senior ranking star officers in the Navy.[^cite_note-Diane_Publishing_Co.,_PR_Blood,_1996-158]<sup>: 295 </sup> In the past times of 1947–2012, the Navy's uniform had closely followed the uniforms issued in the British [Royal Navy](26061.md) with star officers often wearing the full white dress while the junior officers to enlisted members only wearing dressed-up blue working uniforms as their authorised working uniform in the vessels.[^cite_note-Diane_Publishing_Co.,_PR_Blood,_1996-158]<sup>: 295 </sup>

In 2014, the Navy working uniform pattern for all officials have been changed in favour of adopting the authorised digital camouflage pattern uniform which incorporates sparse black and medium grey shapes on a light grey background.[^cite_note-Navy_Working_Uniforms-159]

The [Navy Special Service Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_\(Navy\)) follows the [Army Special Service Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group)'s authorised uniform and wears the [U.S. Woodland (M81)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Woodland) uniform while the Pakistan Marines have their own woodland pattern featuring light brown, olive green and blue shapes on a tan or light olive background.[^cite_note-Navy_Working_Uniforms-159]

## Bases and facilities

Main articles: [List of Pakistan Navy bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pakistan_Navy_bases) and [Pakistan Armed Forces deployments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces_deployments)



Pakistan Navy personnel conducting a [naval interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_interdiction) exercise with the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) personnel in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) in 2004

From 1947 to 1991, the entire [naval infrastructure and bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_installations_in_Karachi) of the Pakistan Navy were primarily based in [Karachi](17123.md) with the exception of the [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Headquarters_\(Pakistan\)) in Islamabad.[^cite_note-Command_&_Structure_«_PakDef_Military_Consortium-85] In the 1950s, it was the crucial help from the [United States Navy](20518076.md) that the [Karachi Naval Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard) was built and constructed for wartime operations.[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press,_Harkavy-163]<sup>: 27 </sup> Besides the [Naval Base Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Naval_Dockyard), the PNS Dhaka in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) was the only naval base for the Pakistan Navy, dedicated for coastal operations only[^cite_note-164]<sup>: 24 </sup>

After the [Indian Navy's attack on Karachi in 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Trident_\(1971\)), the Navy concentrated on building and moving its operational assets in [Balochistan](4687.md), [Punjab](24751.md), and the [Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md).[^cite_note-Command_&_Structure_«_PakDef_Military_Consortium-85]

These naval bases are operationalised for various purposes including the logistics and maintenance support, armoury and ammunition support, air stations, military hospitals, [SEALs teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea,_Air,_and_Land_Teams), coastal and missile defences, missile boats and submarine bases, forward operating bases etc.[^cite_note-Command_&_Structure_«_PakDef_Military_Consortium-85] The [PNS Zafar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Zafar) serves as the major logistics naval base for the Pakistani military's operational capability in the western and northern Pakistan, followed by the naval forward operating base constructed at the vicinity of the [Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_War_College) in Lahore.[^cite_note-165]

The primary naval air station is [PNS Mehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Mehran), followed by the establishment of the naval air stations in [Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Makran), [Ormara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Ahsan), [Turbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Siddiq) and the [Manora Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Himalaya).[^cite_note-Turbat_Naval_Air_Station-166] In 2017, PNS Siddiq was commissioned to support the aerial missions for the [Pakistan Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Aviation) reconnaissance group to guard the safety of the [CPEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPEC).[^cite_note-Turbat_Naval_Air_Station-166]

The [PNS Hameed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Hameed), commissioned in 2017, is a [VLF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_low_frequency) facility near the Karachi coast, while the Karachi-based [PNS Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Iqbal) and the [PNS Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Qasim) serves for the operational activities dedicated for the [Navy Special Service Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group_\(Navy\)) and the [Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines). The [Jinnah Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinnah_Naval_Base) and proposed [Kalmat Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmat_Naval_Base) are dedicated towards maintaining and harbouring the country's strategic assets such as the nuclear-capable submarines.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Today,_Babar,_2016-167][^cite_note-168]

Besides deployment within Pakistan, the Pakistan Navy, along with the inter-services branches, are permanently based in different parts in the [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).[^cite_note-169]

## Personnel

### Officer Ranks

Main article: [Naval ranks and insignia of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_ranks_and_insignia_of_Pakistan)



[Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) Asif Khaliq (left) saluting with officers Cdre. [Keith Blount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Blount) (middle) of [RN](26061.md) Cdre. Daryl Bates (right) of [RNZN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy). The [Cdre.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) is a star officer rank equivalent to [One star rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_star_rank) ([Brig.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier)) in the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md).

[^cite_note-Officer_rank_system_in_Pakistan_Navy-174]

From its commencement in August 1947, the Pakistan Navy had traditionally followed the [ranks and insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy_officer_rank_insignia) of the [Royal Navy](26061.md) but disbanded in favour of adopting the [officer ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_officer_rank_insignia) system of the [United States Navy](20518076.md) as early as the 1950s.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59][^cite_note-Officer_rank_system_in_Pakistan_Navy-174]<sup>: 73 </sup>

Unlike the army or air force where there are several paths to become the officers, there is only one way of becoming the naval officer by must attending the [Pakistan Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Academy)—after passing out the [boot camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Himalaya) in [Manora Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manora_Island)— for one-and-half year for them to be able to [passed out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) from the academy.[^cite_note-Career_as_Professional_Officer-175]

The [passed out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) cadets gain [commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer) in the Navy as [midshipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midshipman), taking their first assignment in an open-sea ship that gives them the experience of life at sea while being trained in different careers on board.[^cite_note-Career_as_Professional_Officer-175] The training of the [passed out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) midshipman usually lasts till six months before rotating back to the naval academy to be promoted as the [Sub-lieutenants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-lieutenant).[^cite_note-Career_as_Professional_Officer-175] Their college education is provided at the [Pakistan Navy Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy_Engineering_College) in [Karachi](17123.md) for three years, pursuing a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in their choice of career.[^cite_note-Career_as_Professional_Officer-175]



US Navy and Pakistan Navy Sailors during a joint exercise

The Pakistan Navy has the same officer rank hierarchy as the [Royal Navy](26061.md); insignia [are similar to the Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy_officer_rank_insignia) except that commodore's and admiral's shoulder boards have a star and crescent instead of a crown.[^cite_note-176]

Besides the military officers, the Department of Navy also offers employments to civilians in financial management, accountancy, medical services, computing, and administration, and has currently employed ≈2,000 civilians that met the Navy's quota in 2018.[^cite_note-filectory.com-177]

### Enlisted personnel



Pakistan Naval Academy cadets at the [Jinnah's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-e-Quaid) in Karachi in 2007

The recruitment and the enlistment in the navy is nationwide and the recruitment in the Navy is carried out by the release of the employment tender in the [print newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspapers_in_Pakistan) and [televised commercials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Pakistan) twice a year– first group attending the boot camp in May and the second being directed on November.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179] The Directorate of Recruitment that is located in the [Navy NHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHQ_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) in Islamabad controls the recruiting offices and centers in all over the country— the recruiting offices are located in [Punjab](24751.md), [Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), [Sindh](28972.md), and [Balochistan](4687.md).[^cite_note-180] Before 1966, almost all the enlisted personnel and officers had to be sent to attend the military academies in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) to be educated and to be trained in technical branches for the Pakistan Navy.[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 90 </sup>

After passing out from the nine-month long boot camp, the enlisted personnel are directed for subsequent job training at the [PNS Karsaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Karsaz) in Karachi on the matters of technical subjects and assigned for different branches in the Navy.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179]

Promotion in the Navy from the enlistment to officers ranks are much quicker than the army or the air force, as the Department of Navy offers financial aid to those enlisted personnel successful in their profession to attend the [colleges and universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_In_Pakistan).[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179] Most of the enlisted personnel rarely stays in their enlisted ranks at the time of their retirement as most retires at junior officer ranks once reaching their retirement age of 62[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 90 </sup>

Their technical experiences in their fields is consolidated into the professional training that forms their basis to attend the respective university for them to earn the four-year college degree.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179]

The [noncommissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) (or enlists) wear respective anchors colour patches or badges chevrons on their shoulders.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179] Retirement age for the enlisted personnel varies and depends on the enlisted ranks that they have attained during their services.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179]

### Recruitment and training

Main article: [Quota system in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_in_Pakistan)



The [passing out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) (graduation) of cadets from the [Pakistan Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Academy) in [Karachi](17123.md) in 2008. The education and boot camp training last for two years before cadets becomes sailors.

[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179]

After the Navy was established in August 1947, the Navy had to send its officers and enlisted personnel to be trained at the [Britannia Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Royal_Naval_College) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) whose training and education by the British [Royal Navy](26061.md) was crucial at all levels of cadet's learning and schooling.[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19][^cite_note-Pakistan_Naval_Academy-181]<sup>: 91 </sup> During its earliest time in 1947, the Department of Navy had only 3,800 personnel (200 [officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_ranks_and_insignia_of_Pakistan), 3,000 Enlists, and 500 civilian employees) as the Navy faced the same problems as its [Department of Army](1965925.md) as the most technical enlisted personnel and skilled executive officers were [Punjabi Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Muslim) while others had [Urdu-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_speaker) background (i.e. [Indian immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people_in_Pakistan) as naturalised citizens of Pakistan).[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 47 </sup>

After 1971, the [Bhutto administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto) introduced the [quota system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_in_Pakistan) to give fair chance to the residents of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) and [Balochistan](4687.md) to enlist in the military.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 75 </sup> In 2012, Sanhia Karim became the first [Balochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_people) woman to be commissioned into the navy, she joined in a squad consisting of fifty-three female officers and seventy-two enlists from Balochistan.[^cite_note-Dawn_News,_19th_March_2012-182] In 2012, the Navy pushed its personnel strength to Balochistan after sending a large formation of Baloch university students to Navy Engineering Colleges and War College as well as staff schools to complete their officer training requirements.[^cite_note-Dawn_News,_10th_March_2012-183] The Navy established three additional facilities in Balochistan to supervise the training to its personnel.[^cite_note-Dawn_News,_10th_March_2012-183]

Recruitment in the Navy remains to be challenge for the naval recruiters to enlists citizens and their selfless commitment to the military from the [urbanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation_in_Pakistan) metropolitan cities where the preference of [college education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_education) (especially attending [post-graduate](153981.md) schooling in the [United States](3434750.md) and the [English-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) countries) is much higher and strongly desirable.[^cite_note-Lancer's_Publications_and_Distributors-18]<sup>: 80 </sup> Furthermore, the medical standards and education levels required by the Department of Navy to be able to perform technical jobs also poses significant challenges as the Navy requiring the significant percentage marks once the [matriculation examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation_in_Pakistan) are concluded.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179]

The Navy has only one [boot camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training), the [PNS *Himalaya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Himalaya) in [Manora Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manora_Island), where the [basic](183879.md) military training takes place.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179] The basic military training at the [PNS Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Himalaya) goes for nine-months where instructions on military life is given while the [physical conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_exercise) is strongly emphasised.[^cite_note-PNS_Himalaya-_the_boot_camp_of_Navy-184] The [officer cadets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_cadet) are sent to attend the [Pakistan Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Academy) where their training lasts for two years before they are able to [pass out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_\(military\)) from the Naval Academy.[^cite_note-NYU_Press,_PI_Cheema,_2002-19]<sup>: 93 </sup>[^cite_note-Career_as_Professional_Officer-175] Once passing out, the commissioned junior officers must spend six-month deployment in [Arabian Sea](2747.md) before being selected to attend the professional schools, such as the [Naval Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy_Engineering_College) in Karachi, to move towards attaining the bachelor's degree in a period of four-years.[^cite_note-Career_as_Professional_Officer-175]

As the estimates made in 2003 and 2009, the Navy had approximately ≈30,200 active duty personnel.[^cite_note-IISS2010-185] In 2014, the estimates established the Navy's manpower strength at 30,700 active duty personnel.[^cite_note-Penguin_UK,_Ahmed-186] but its combined manpower strength is increased and approximated at ≈40,500 personnel based on recent estimates in 2018.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup>

## Education and training

Main article: [Military academies in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_academies_in_Pakistan)

### Schooling, teaching, and institutions

The Pakistan Navy offers the wide range of lucrative careers to the high school graduates in the technical fields by issuing specialised diplomas and certifications at the [PNS Karsaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Karsaz) and the PNS Bahadur, which consists of the schools of operations, underwater, surface weapons, communications, and the naval police.[^cite_note-Career_as_a_Enlisted_Sailor-179][^cite_note-187] Instructions and technical education on technical fields and the engineering are primarily taught at the [Pakistan Navy Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy_Engineering_College) that is open for both military and public admission, and offers college degree programs at undergraduate and post-graduate level.[^cite_note-188]

When the Navy was established in 1947, there was no technical schools for the Navy to look after the ship maintenance and [power machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinist's_mate) that led to the establishment of the Pakistan Naval Polytechnic Institute (PNPI) in 1951 and the [Navy Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy_Engineering_College) in 1962 whose admissions are open to public besides the military personnel.[^cite_note-189] From 1947 to 1967, the Navy had to rely on the education and training provided by the [Royal Navy](26061.md) at all levels of schooling, and had to send most of its officers and enlisted men to be trained at the [Britannia Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Royal_Naval_College) at the [Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon) and the [Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College,_Greenwich) in Greenwich who were mostly trained in communication and navigation.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Naval_Academy,_intro-190] Training on the operations of warships and education on the [military staffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_\(military\)) was crucial for the Pakistan Navy in the 1960s under the [United States](3434750.md)-sponsored [International Military Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Education_and_Training) (IMET) arranged for Pakistan under the [Security Assistance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Assistance_Program) (SAP) as the U.S. Navy's officers served in the faculty of the engineering and technical schools of the Navy.[^cite_note-191]<sup>: 190 </sup>

In 1966, the [Pakistan Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Academy) was established under the guidance of the [United States Navy](20518076.md), and is a premier institution of higher learning whose alumni included the Commanders of the [Royal Qatari Navy](25194.md), [Royal Saudi Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Saudi_Navy), and the [Sri Lanka Navy](6186841.md) while other nations naval cadets have also attended the naval academy.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Naval_Academy,_intro-190]

In 1968, the [Pakistan Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_War_College) was established in [Lahore](125315.md), whose curriculum is very similar to the [Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_War_College) in the [United States](3434750.md), is a primary military staff college which offers critical thinking techniques and developing ideas for naval warfare to the officers in the army and the air force.[^cite_note-192] In 1970, the [School of Logistics and Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy_School_of_Logistics_and_Management) was established that conducts research on military logistics and management in imparting [naval warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Warfare) techniques to the military officers serving in the army, air force, and marines departments of the Pakistani military.[^cite_note-193]

After the [1971 war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971) with India, the Navy established several schools on strategy, naval warfare, and weapons tactics by commissioning the PNS Bahadur in 1981 as the navy established schools are listed below:

The [PNS Karsaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Karsaz)—the training facility on [heavy machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinist's_mate) was established in 1954.

Established in 1971, the [National Defense University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Pakistan) (NDU) in Islamabad is the most senior and premier institute of higher learning that provides the advance [critical thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thinking) level and research-based [strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_strategy) level education to the senior military officers in the Pakistani military.[^cite_note-auto2-194]<sup>: 9–10 </sup> The NDU is a significant institution of higher learning in understanding the institutional norms of military tutelage in Pakistan because it constitutes the "highest learning platform where the military leadership comes together for common instruction", according to thesis written by Pakistani author Aqil Shah.[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-195]<sup>: 8 </sup> Without securing their [graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) from their master's program at the NDU, no officer in the Pakistani military can be promoted as general in the army or air force, or admiral in the navy or marines as it is a prerequisite for their promotion to become a senior member at the [Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee).[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-195]<sup>: 8–9 </sup>

Additionally, the platform provided at the NDU represents a radical shift from the emphasis on operational and staff functions and the level of ranks are imposed as qualification to attend the [master's program](170346.md) at the NDU, usually [brigadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_\(United_Kingdom\)), [air commodores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Commodore), and [commodores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)), are invited to given admission in broad range of strategic, political, social, and economic factors as these factors affect the country's national security.[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-195]<sup>: 8–9 </sup> In this sense, the NDU becomes the critical thinking institution as its constitutes active-duty senior military officers corps' baptism into a shared ideological framework about the military's appropriate role, status, and behaviour in relation to state and society, and shared values affect how these officers perceive and respond to civilian governmental decisions, policies, and political crises.[^cite_note-auto2-194]<sup>: 9–10 </sup> Admission to the NDU is not restricted to military officials, but civilians can also attend and graduate, allowing them to explore the broader aspects of national security.[^cite_note-Harvard_University_Press,_Shah,_2014-195] <sup>: 8–9 </sup>

Established in 1991, the [National University of Sciences and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Sciences_and_Technology,_Pakistan) (NUST) has now absorbed and amalgamated the existing naval engineering college, and is a counterpart institution in [science and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_engineering) to that of the NDU in Islamabad.[^cite_note-196] Besides the strategic and military education, the Navy leads some marine scientific programs via the [Naval Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Naval_Observatory) while it leads the research on [hydrography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography) by conducting the [hydrographic survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrographic_survey) through the [PNS Behr Paima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Behr_Paima), and provides support to the oceanographic program led by the civilian [National Institute of Oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Oceanography_\(Pakistan\)) (NIO).[^cite_note-Pakistan_Navy_Hydrographic_Department-197]

## Naval jack

Main article: [Jack (flag)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_\(flag\))



Naval jack of the Pakistan Navy



Naval Flag of Pakistan Navy

From 1947 to 1956, the Pakistan Navy had stuck with the [Ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Naval_Ensign) of the [Royal Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy) that featured the British [Queen's colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_colours,_standards_and_guidons) and the white flag.[^cite_note-Lancer_International,_Singh.1992-198]<sup>: 264 </sup> The Navy continued the tradition that it inherited from the [Royal Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Navy) and British culture that was common with the [Royal Navy](26061.md) until the American military advisers was attached the guide the Navy on military arts and science under the [Military Advisory Assistance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Advisory_Assistance_Group) by the [Eisenhower administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower_administration) in 1956.[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup>

Since then, the Navy's tradition and culture is commonly and uniquely influenced from the [United States Navy](20518076.md).[^cite_note-Seaforth_Publishing,_Waters,_2011-59]<sup>: 73 </sup>

After the promulgation of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) in 1956, the Navy gained its independence from the British [Royal patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Organisations_based_in_the_United_Kingdom_with_royal_patronage) and became the federal institution of the armed forces commissioned by the elected [President of Pakistan](419460.md). The prefix *Royal* was permanently removed from the Navy as well as disbanding the British monarch culture and tradition in the Navy.

The [naval jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_jack) and the ensign flag of the Navy immediately replaced the [Queen's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Pakistan) [colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_colours,_standards_and_guidons) and the white ensign entirely, instead the dark blue colour with the anchor crest of the Navy was adopted while the blue anchor was added in the side of the corner white coloured section on the [national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Pakistan) of Pakistan. Since then, the naval jack has always flown in the warships of the Pakistan Navy while the naval ensign of the Navy is commonly used by the [Pakistan Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines) as their primary war flag.

Adm. The Pakistan Navy has played an integral part in the civil society of Pakistan, almost since its inception.[^cite_note-Taylor_and_Francis-e-Library-199] In 1996, General Jehangir Karamat described Pakistan armed forces' relations with the society:

> In my opinion, if we have to repeat of past events then we must understand that Military leaders can pressure only up to a point. Beyond that their own position starts getting undermined because the military is after all is a mirror image of the civil society from which it is drawn.

— General Jehangir Karamat on civil society–military relations[^cite_note-Taylor_and_Francis-e-Library-199]

In times of national calamities and emergencies, the Pakistan Navy has been deployed in relief operations and nation building programs in the country.[^cite_note-200] In 2004, a tactical task force under then-[Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) [Asif Sandila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Sandila) coordinated the peacetime relief operations in [Maldives](19117.md), [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Bangladesh](3454.md) when the [underwater earthquake caused a tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami) and struck the South Asian nations.[^cite_note-IRIN,_2004-201][^cite_note-auto1-202][^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] In 2005, the Navy deployed the [PNS *Badr (D-184)*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Badr_\(D-184\)) to help assists the relief efforts for the [earthquake that struck the northern part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake) of the country in October 2005.[^cite_note-Navy_ISPR,_History-52]

In 2010, the Navy coordinated one of its [largest relief operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Madad_\(Pakistan_Navy\)) during the [nationwide flash floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Pakistan_floods), with Navy divers rescuing and evacuating more than 352,291 people in August 2010.[^cite_note-207] In addition, the Navy and Marines personnel provided 43,850 kg of food and relief goods to flood victims; 5,700 kg of ready-to-cook food, 1,000 kg of dates and 5,000 kg of food has been dispatched to Sukkur.[^cite_note-208]As of January 2011, under the program *PN Model Village*, the Navy's civil engineering corps built the model houses in the affected areas for the [internally displaced person](427408.md) (IDPs).[^cite_note-209]

On 10 June 2018, Pakistan Navy and Maritime Security Agency rescued eleven [Iranian](14653.md) crew members on a sunken Iranian boat in the Northern [Arabian Sea](2747.md), about 230 kilometres (140 mi) away from [Karachi](17123.md).[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]

## Awards and honours

Main article: [Awards and decorations of the Pakistan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awards_and_decorations_of_the_Pakistan_Armed_Forces)

### Nishan-e-Haider

Main article: [Nishan-e-Haider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishan-e-Haider)



In [military awards hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Awards), the [Nishan-e-Haider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishan-e-Haider) (lit. 'Order of Lion Urdu نشان حیدر abbreviated as NH') is the highest and the most prestigious honour awarded posthumously for bravery and actions of valor in event of war.[^cite_note-216]<sup>: 220 </sup> Established in March 1956 by the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan), this award is an equivalent to the American [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor), British [Victoria Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Cross) (VC), Russian [Order of St. Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Andrew), or the French [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour).[^cite_note-217]<sup>: 87 </sup> Unlike the American [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor), the *[Nishan-e-Haider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishan-e-Haider)* (NH) has only been conferred to those who have been "martyred" and proved their distinguished valor of actions in an event of conflict or war.[^cite_note-218]<sup>: 5 </sup>[^cite_note-219]

Since the commencement of the Navy on 15 August 1947, no naval officer has been honoured or bestowed with the medal. After the [PNS Mehran attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Mehran_attack) on 22 May 2011, a recommendation was sent by the Prime Minister of Pakistan to the President of Pakistan to posthumously honour Lt. [Syed Yasir Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Yasir_Abbas)[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] for his heroic actions during the attack but the recommendations were rejected by the President of Pakistan.[^cite_note-Nishane-e-Haider,_2011-222][^cite_note-Trib-223]
