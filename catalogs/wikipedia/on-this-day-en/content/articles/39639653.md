# Gold laundering

**Gold laundering** is the process whereby illegally obtained [gold](12240.md) is [melted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting) and [recast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casting_\(metalworking\)) into another form. The recasting is performed to obscure or conceal the true origin of the gold. The recast gold is then sold, thus laundering it into [cash](55336.md).

It may also refer to a [money laundering](19390.md) transaction in which the exchanged good is gold.

## Process

There are several stages in the gold laundering process. The first is the acquisition of the gold, which may be from any combination of sources, at least one of which is illegal. This is then treated as [scrap metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrap_metal), melted,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERey_Mallén2013-1] then cast into a [mold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molding_\(process\)). This results in a transportable [ingot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingot), [gold bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_bar) or other bulk form [bullion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullion).

In some cases, there is no gold involved in the laundering scheme. [Colombia](5222.md) reported 70 tons of gold exports for 2012 despite production of only 15 tons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEColombia_faces_\$17bn_laundry_bill-2] This is a result of fictitious gold exports filed with the Colombian customs agency by [drug cartels](197015.md) to import "cash from international drug deals".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEColombia_faces_\$17bn_laundry_bill-2]

### Acquisition

Acquisition of gold occurs in many forms.

Small-scale [gold mining](1291393.md) operations, particularly those with no permits or licences,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEColombia_faces_\$17bn_laundry_bill-2] [extract gold](1686492.md) from areas unsuitable for large [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) operations. Gold obtained in this fashion that is exported may not be traced or authenticated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPresident_Mahama_charges_Inter-Ministerial_Task_Force_To_Tackle_Illegal_Mining_In_Ghana-3] This includes artisanal mines operated by a few individuals, who then sell the gold to brokers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERey_Mallén2013-1]



As Minister of Economics, [Walther Funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Funk) accelerated the pace of rearmament and as [Reichsbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsbank) president banked for the SS the [gold](12240.md) [rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_\(jewellery\)) of [Nazi concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camp) victims

Another form of acquisition is [theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_theft). In the 1940s, [Nazi Germany](21212.md) forcibly obtained the possessions of Jews, sometimes in collaboration with other parties. Most victims who died in [death camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_camp) and [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camp) were robbed of all valuable property by the state, which was then sold or, in the case of bullion, sent to the [Reichsbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsbank). For example, in 1945 the [Hungarian Gold Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Gold_Train) was established to transport the property of Hungarian Jews to [Berlin](3354.md). Once the Jews had been deported to German [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camp), the valuables were sorted into categories such that their owners could no longer be identified.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPresidential_Advisory_Commission_on_Holocaust_Assets_in_the_United_States1999-4] In the late 1930s and throughout [World War II](32927.md), Germany would increase its unofficial gold reserves by expropriating gold from foreign governments, including \$223m from [Belgium](3343.md) and \$193m from the [Netherlands](21148.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUK_Treasury-5] Some of the [Nazi gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_gold) was exchanged for cash,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimons1997-6] and some deposited in financial institutions, including \$316 million of the looted gold at the [Swiss National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Bank).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndependent_Commission_of_Experts_Switzerland-7] Of the nearly 100 tons of gold laundered through Swiss banks, only 4 tons were returned.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimons1997-6] The [Banco de Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_Portugal), the [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) of [Portugal](23033.md), would regularly purchase Nazi gold held at the Swiss National Bank, according to a 1944 document from the [American Overseas Special Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Overseas_Special_Services?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDePalma1997-8][^cite_note-9]

### Refining

See also: [Refining (metallurgy)](8921732.md) and [millesimal fineness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millesimal_fineness)

The scrap gold is then sold by brokers to smelters or [refiners](8921732.md), who heat the gold to at least 1,064 °C (1,947 °F), its [melting point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_point).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerry1995182-10] This may include gold from numerous sources, including recycled and newly mined gold. Once melted, it may be purified by [parting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_parting), which separates [silver](27119.md) impurities, and the [Miller process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_process) or [Wohlwill process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wohlwill_process), depending on the desired level of purity and scale of operation.

Once refined, it is cast into a transportable form for further processing. [Gold fingerprinting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_fingerprinting), that is identifying a particular source of gold based on its impurities or trace elements, may be possible if the impurities have not been removed via refining.

## Effects

To prevent the laundering of gold and other metals (such as [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum), [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin), and [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten)), some nations have established systems and regulations. For example, [Rwanda](25645.md) introduced regulations for mineral trade that require all ore extraction to be tagged, in part a response to the [Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodd%E2%80%93Frank_Wall_Street_Reform_and_Consumer_Protection_Act) in the [United States](3434750.md) that requires companies to disclose the use of [conflict minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_mineral) by publishing a [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain) audit.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEManson2012-11]

However, with only 100 government monitors overseeing 450 mining sites during typical business hours, smuggling still occurs, exacerbated by corrupt monitors illegally selling tags or failing to record transactions in logbooks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEManson2012-11]

In 2013, [Ghanaian](12067.md) President [John Dramani Mahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dramani_Mahama) announced that the [government of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ghana) would establish a task force to regulate the small-scale mining sector.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPresident_Mahama_charges_Inter-Ministerial_Task_Force_To_Tackle_Illegal_Mining_In_Ghana-3] This was to ensure that gold produced in small-scale mines could be authenticated, and also to eliminate the use of [heavy machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_equipment) that had become prevalent on such sites, which have caused [environmental damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_mining).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPresident_Mahama_charges_Inter-Ministerial_Task_Force_To_Tackle_Illegal_Mining_In_Ghana-3]

The [government of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uganda) obtained \$200 million in trade revenue from the sale of gold in 2012, but expects that to decrease in 2013 as a result of [illegal mining](49640132.md) operations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJaramogi2013-12]

The group [InSight Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InSight_Crime) claims that illegal mining represents up to 30% of the revenues of the [Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia](26100.md) (FARC).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERey_Mallén2013-1]

On 22 November 2000, the [World Jewish Congress lawsuit against Swiss banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Jewish_Congress_lawsuit_against_Swiss_banks) was settled, providing a fund of \$1.25 billion for restitution to individuals whose property was confiscated by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) during World War II.
