# National security

For other uses, see [National security (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_\(disambiguation\)).

"National defense" redirects here. For other uses, see [National defense (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_defense_\(disambiguation\)).

**National security**, or **national defense** (**national defence** in [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English)), is the [security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security) and [defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_\(military\)) of a [sovereign state](23604120.md), including its [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship), [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy), and [institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions), which is regarded as a duty of [government](12229.md). Originally conceived as protection against [military attack](15077993.md), national security is widely understood to include also non-military dimensions, such as the security from [terrorism](30636.md), minimization of [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime), [economic security](10211.md), [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security), [environmental security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_security), [food security](216361.md), and [cyber-security](7398.md). Similarly, national security risks include, in addition to the actions of other [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), action by [violent non-state actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_non-state_actor), by [narcotic cartels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotic_cartel), [organized crime](22625.md), by [multinational corporations](214491.md), and also the effects of [natural disasters](58921.md).

Governments rely on a range of measures, including [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\)), [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_power), and [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) power, as well as [diplomacy](28165911.md), to safeguard the security of a state. They may also act to build the conditions of security regionally and internationally by reducing [transnational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnationalism) causes of insecurity, such as [climate change](5042951.md), [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality), [political exclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_exclusion), and [nuclear proliferation](22158.md).

## Definitions

Many definitions of *national security* have been proposed, starting from simpler definitions which emphasised freedom from military threat and from political coercion.[^cite_note-cite_1993-1]<sup>: 1–6 </sup>[^cite_note-Paleri-2]<sup>: 52–54 </sup> Among the many definitions proposed to date are the following, which show how the concept has evolved to encompass non-military concerns:

- "A nation has security when it does not have to sacrifice its legitimate interests to avoid war, and is able, if challenged, to maintain them by war." ([Walter Lippmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Lippmann), 1943).

  [^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]

  : 5&#x20;

- "The distinctive meaning of national security means freedom from foreign dictation." ([Harold Lasswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Lasswell), 1950)

  [^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]

  : 79&#x20;

- "National security objectively means the absence of threats to acquired values and subjectively, the absence of fear that such values will be attacked." ([Arnold Wolfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Wolfers), 1960)

  [^cite_note-auto-4]

- "National security then is the ability to preserve the nation's physical integrity and territory; to maintain its economic relations with the rest of the world on reasonable terms; to preserve its nature, institution, and governance from disruption from outside; and to control its borders." ([Harold Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Brown_\(Secretary_of_Defense\)), [U.S. Secretary of Defense](43998.md), 1977–1981)

  [^cite_note-books.google.com-5]

- "National security... is best described as a capacity to control those domestic and foreign conditions that the public opinion of a given community believes necessary to enjoy its own self-determination or autonomy, prosperity, and wellbeing." ([Charles Maier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Maier), 1990)

  [^cite_note-MacArthur_Fellowship_Program_1990._p.5-6]

- "National security is an appropriate and aggressive blend of political resilience and maturity, human resources, economic structure and capacity, technological competence, industrial base and availability of natural resources and finally the military might." ([National Defence College of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_College,_India), 1996)

  [^cite_note-auto1-7]

- "\[National security is the] measurable state of the capability of a nation to overcome the multi-dimensional threats to the apparent well-being of its people and its survival as a nation-state at any given time, by balancing all instruments of state policy through governance... and is extendable to global security by variables external to it." ([Prabhakaran Paleri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabhakaran_Paleri?action=edit\&redlink=1), 2008)

  [^cite_note-Paleri-2]

  : 52–54&#x20;

## Dimensions

Potential causes of national insecurity include actions by other states (e.g. [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aggression) or [cyber attack](37925700.md)), [violent non-state actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_non-state_actor) (e.g. [terrorist attack](30636.md)), [organised criminal groups](22625.md) such as [narcotic cartels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotic_cartel), and also the effects of [natural disasters](58921.md) (e.g. flooding, earthquakes).[^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]<sup>: v, 1–8 </sup>[^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-\:0-9] Systemic drivers of insecurity, which may be [transnational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnationality), include [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming), [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) and [marginalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginalisation), [political exclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_exclusion), and [nuclear proliferation](22158.md).[^cite_note-\:2-8]<sup>: 3 </sup>[^cite_note-\:0-9]

In view of the wide range of risks, the security of a state has several dimensions, including [economic security](10211.md), [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security), [physical security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_security), [environmental security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_security), [food security](216361.md), [border security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_guard), and [cyber security](7398.md). These dimensions correlate closely with [elements of national power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elements_of_national_power).

Increasingly, governments organise their [security policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_policies) into a national security strategy (NSS);[^cite_note-10] as of 2017, Spain, Sweden, the United Kingdom, and the United States are among the states to have done so.[^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-\:7-12][^cite_note-\:3-13][^cite_note-\:4-14] Some states also appoint a [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_council) and/or a [National Security Advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_advisor) which is an executive government agency, it feeds the head of the state on topics concerning national security and strategic interest. The national security council/advisor strategies long term, short term, contingency national security plans. [India](14533.md) holds one such system in current, which was established on 19 November 1998.

Although states differ in their approach, various forms of coercive power predominate, particularly [military capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_level_of_military_equipment).[^cite_note-\:2-8] The scope of these capabilities has developed. Traditionally, military capabilities were mainly land- or sea-based, and in smaller countries, they still are. Elsewhere, the domains of potential warfare now include the [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force), [space](956982.md), [cyberspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare), and [psychological operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_warfare).[^cite_note-\:6-15] Military capabilities designed for these domains may be used for national security, or equally for offensive purposes, for example to conquer and annex territory and resources.

See also: [Elements of national security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elements_of_national_security) and [Elements of national power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elements_of_national_power)

### Physical

&#x20;In practice, national security is associated primarily with managing physical threats and with the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) capabilities used for doing so.[^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-\:3-13][^cite_note-\:4-14] That is, national security is often understood as the capacity of a nation to mobilise military forces to guarantee its borders and to deter or successfully defend against physical threats including [military aggression](15077993.md) and attacks by [non-state actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_non-state_actor), such as [terrorism](30636.md). Most states, such as South Africa and Sweden,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:7-12] configure their military forces mainly for territorial defence; others, such as France, Russia, the UK and the US,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-\:3-13][^cite_note-\:4-14] invest in higher-cost [expeditionary capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare), which allow their armed forces to [project power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) and sustain [military operations](666301.md) abroad.

See also: [Terrorism](30636.md), [Border guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_guard), and [Military aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aggression)

### Infrastructural

Main article: [Critical infrastructure](521286.md)

Infrastructure security is the [security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security) provided to protect [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), especially [critical infrastructure](521286.md), such as [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport), [highways](48519.md),[^cite_note-19] [rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport), [hospitals](18994221.md), [bridges](3397.md), [transport hubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub), network communications, [media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_\(communication\)), the [electricity grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_\(electricity\)), [dams](51518.md), [power plants](212141.md), [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport), [oil refineries](195137.md), and [water systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_system). Infrastructure security seeks to limit vulnerability of these structures and systems to [sabotage](29462.md), [terrorism](30636.md), and [contamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contamination).[^cite_note-20]

Many countries have established government agencies to directly manage the security of critical infrastructure, usually, through the Ministry of Interior/Home Affairs, dedicated security agencies to protect facilities such as United States [Federal Protective Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Protective_Service_\(United_States\)), and also dedicated transport police such as the [British Transport Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Transport_Police). There are also commercial transportation security units such as the [Amtrak Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Police) in the United States. Critical infrastructure is vital for the essential functioning of a country. Incidental or deliberate damage can have a serious impact on the economy and essential services. Some of the threats to infrastructure include:

- [Terrorism](30636.md): person or groups deliberately targeting critical infrastructure for political gain. In the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks), the Mumbai central station and hospital were deliberately targeted.

- [Sabotage](29462.md): person or groups such as ex-employees, anti-government groups, environmental groups. Refer to [Bangkok's International Airport Seized by Protestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Alliance_for_Democracy#Seizure_of_Suvarnabhumi_International_Airport).

- [Information warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_warfare): private person hacking for private gain or countries initiating attacks to glean information and damage a country's cyberinfrastructure. [Cyberattacks on Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_cyberattacks_on_Estonia) and [cyberattacks during the 2008 South Ossetia war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberattacks_during_the_2008_South_Ossetia_war) are examples.

- [Natural disaster](58921.md): hurricane or other natural events that damage critical infrastructures such as oil pipelines, water, and power grids. See [Hurricane Ike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ike#Oil_and_gas_spills) and [Economic effects of Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_effects_of_Hurricane_Katrina) for examples.

### Virtual

Main article: [Computer security](7398.md)

[Computer security](7398.md), also known as cybersecurity or IT security, refers to the security of computing devices such as [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) and smartphones, as well as [computer networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) such as private and public networks, and the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_access). It concerns the protection of hardware, software, data, people, and also the procedures by which systems are accessed, and the field has growing importance due to the increasing reliance on computer systems in most societies.[^cite_note-21] Since unauthorized access to critical civil and military infrastructure is now considered a major threat, cyberspace is now recognised as a domain of warfare. One such example is the use of [Stuxnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuxnet) by the US and Israel against the [Iranian nuclear programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_nuclear_programme).[^cite_note-\:6-15]

### Political

Main article: [Political security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_security)

[Barry Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Buzan), [Ole Wæver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_W%C3%A6ver), [Jaap de Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaap_de_Wilde) and others have argued that national security depends on [political security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_security): the stability of the social order.[^cite_note-\:5-22] Others, such as Paul Rogers, have added that the equitability of the international order is equally vital.[^cite_note-\:0-9] Hence, political security depends on the rule of [international law](8195726.md) (including the [laws of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_war)), the effectiveness of [international political institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_institutions), as well as [diplomacy](28165911.md) and [negotiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation) between nations and other security actors.[^cite_note-\:5-22] It also depends on, among other factors, effective political inclusion of disaffected groups and the [human security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_security) of the citizenry.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-23]

### Economic

Main article: [Economic security](10211.md)

Economic security, in the context of [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations), is the ability of a [nation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-state) to maintain and develop the national economy, without which other dimensions of national security cannot be managed. Economic capability largely determines the defence capability of a nation, and thus a sound economic security directly influences the national security of a nation. That is why we see countries with sound economy, happen to have sound security setup too, such as [The United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_United_States), [China](5405.md), [India](14533.md) among others. In larger countries, strategies for economic security expect to access resources and markets in other countries and to protect their own markets at home. [Developing countries](78449.md) may be less secure than economically advanced states due to high rates of unemployment and underpaid work.

### Environmental

Main article: [Environmental security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_security)

Environmental security, also known as ecological security, refers to the integrity of [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystems) and the [biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere), particularly in relation to their capacity to sustain a [diversity of life-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) (including human life). The security of ecosystems has attracted greater attention as the impact of ecological damage by humans has grown.[^cite_note-24] The degradation of ecosystems, including [topsoil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion), [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss), and [climate change](5042951.md), affect economic security and can precipitate [mass migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_migration), leading to increased pressure on resources elsewhere. Ecological security is also important since most of the countries in the world are developing and dependent on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and agriculture gets affected largely due to climate change. This effect affects the economy of the nation, which in turn affects national security.

The scope and nature of environmental threats to national security and strategies to engage them are a subject of debate.[^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]<sup>: 29–33 </sup> Romm (1993) classifies the major impacts of ecological changes on national security as:[^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]<sup>: 15 </sup>

- *Transnational environmental problems.* These include global environmental problems such as [climate change](5042951.md) due to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming), [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), and [loss of biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss).

  [^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]

  : 15&#x20;

- *Local environmental or resource pressures*. These include resource scarcities leading to local conflict, such as [disputes over water scarcity in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conflict_in_the_Middle_East_and_North_Africa); migration into the United States caused by the failure of agriculture in [Mexico](3966054.md);

  [^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]

  : 15&#x20;

  &#x20;and the impact on the conflict in Syria of erosion of productive land.

  [^cite_note-25]

  &#x20;Environmental insecurity in [Rwanda](25645.md) following a rise in population and dwindling availability of farmland, may also have contributed to the genocide there.

  [^cite_note-26]

- *[Environmentally threatening outcomes of warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_war).* These include acts of war that degrade or destroy ecosystems. Examples are the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) destruction of agriculture in [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage); Saddam Hussein's burning of oil wells in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War);

  [^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]

  : 15–16&#x20;

  &#x20;the use of [Agent Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange) by the UK in the [Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency) and the US in the [Vietnam War](32611.md) for [defoliating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defoliant) forests.

### Energy and natural resources

Main article: [Energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security)

Resources include water, sources of energy, land, and minerals. Availability of adequate natural resources is important for a nation to develop its industry and economic power. For example, in the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War) of 1991, [Iraq](7515928.md) captured [Kuwait](7515890.md) partly in order to secure access to its oil wells, and one reason for the US counter-invasion was the value of the same wells to its own economy. Water resources are subject to disputes between many nations, including [India](14533.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md), and in the [Middle East](19323.md).

The interrelations between security, energy, natural resources, and their sustainability is increasingly acknowledged in national security strategies and resource security is now included among the [UN Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals).[^cite_note-\:7-12][^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-\:8-27][^cite_note-\:4-14][^cite_note-28] In the US, for example, the military has installed [solar photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_photovoltaic) [microgrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microgrids) on their bases in case of [power outage](296636.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Issues

### Consistency of approach

The dimensions of national security outlined above are frequently in tension with one another. For example:

- The high cost of maintaining large [military forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) can place a burden on the economic security of a nation and annual defence spending as per cent of GDP varies significantly by country.

  [^cite_note-31]

  &#x20;Conversely, economic constraints can limit the scale of expenditure on [military capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military).

- Unilateral security action by states can undermine political security at an international level if it erodes the [rule of law](25166191.md) and undermines the authority of [international institutions](18201224.md). The [invasion of Iraq](201936.md) in 2003 and the [annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) in 2014 have been cited as examples.

  [^cite_note-32]

  [^cite_note-33]

- The pursuit of economic security in competition with other nation states can undermine the ecological security of all when the impact includes widespread [topsoil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion), [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss), and [climate change](5042951.md).

  [^cite_note-34]

  &#x20;Conversely, expenditure on mitigating or adapting to ecological change places a burden on the national economy.

If tensions such as these are mismanaged, national security policies and actions may be ineffective or counterproductive.

### Versus transnational security

&#x20;Increasingly, national security strategies have begun to recognise that nations cannot provide for their own security without also developing the security of their regional and international context.[^cite_note-\:4-14][^cite_note-\:8-27][^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-\:7-12] For example, Sweden's national security strategy of 2017 declared:

> "Wider security measures must also now encompass protection against epidemics and infectious diseases, combating terrorism and organised crime, ensuring safe transport and reliable food supplies, protecting against energy supply interruptions, countering devastating climate change, initiatives for peace and global development, and much more."[^cite_note-\:7-12]

The extent to which this matters, and how it should be done, is the subject of debate. Some argue that the principal beneficiary of national security policy should be the nation state itself, which should centre its strategy on protective and coercive capabilities in order to safeguard itself in a hostile environment (and potentially to project that power into its environment, and dominate it to the point of [strategic supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-spectrum_dominance)).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Others argue that security depends principally on building the conditions in which equitable relationships between nations can develop, partly by reducing antagonism between actors, ensuring that fundamental needs can be met, and also that differences of interest can be negotiated effectively.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-\:0-9] In the UK, for example, Malcolm Chalmers argued in 2015 that the heart of the UK's approach should be support for the Western strategic military alliance led through [NATO](21133.md) by the United States, as "the key anchor around which international order is maintained".[^cite_note-39]

### Civil liberties and human rights

See also: [Civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties), [Human rights](13831.md), [Mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance), [Preventive State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preventive_State), and [Police state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_state)

Approaches to national security can have a complex impact on [human rights](13831.md) and [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties). For example, the rights and liberties of citizens are affected by the use of [military personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_personnel) and [militarised police forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarization_of_police) to control public behaviour; the use of [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance), including [mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance) in [cyberspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberspace), which has implications for [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy); [military recruitment](3135672.md) and [conscription](5735.md) practices; and the effects of [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfare) on [civilians](145804.md) and [civil infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_infrastructure). This has led to a [dialectical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegelian_dialectic) struggle, particularly in [liberal democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy), between government [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority) and the rights and freedoms of the general public.

Even where the exercise of national security is subject to [good governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_governance), and the [rule of law](25166191.md), a risk remains that the term *national security* may become a [pretext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretext) for suppressing [unfavorable political and social views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_dissent). In the US, for example, the controversial [USA Patriot Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Patriot_Act) of 2001, and the revelation by [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) in 2013 that the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) [harvests the personal data of the general public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surveillance_disclosures_\(2013%E2%80%93present\)), brought these issues to wide public attention. Among the questions raised are whether and how national security considerations at times of war should lead to the suppression of individual rights and freedoms, and whether such restrictions are necessary when a state is at peace.

### Meaning

[Daniel W. Drezner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_W._Drezner) says, "the definition of national security has been stretched almost beyond recognition" with some seeing "everything as a national security threat". Drezner explains how policymakers overreacting can "explain that they were just being cautious or that [their very warnings helped neutralize the threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparedness_paradox)".[^cite_note-\:9-40] "The term has been described as invoking "an ambiguous foreign threat" and being "exploited to deflect public scrutiny and provide political cover for unpopular policies".[^cite_note-41] The extensive focus on national security can also lead the public to look to security rather than diplomacy.[^cite_note-\:9-40]

## By region

### Argentina and Brazil

National security ideology as taught by the [US Army School of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_School_of_the_Americas) to military personnel was vital in causing the [military coup of 1964 in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Brazilian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [the 1976 one in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Argentine_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). The military dictatorships were installed on the claim by the military that Leftists were an existential threat to the national interests.[^cite_note-42]

### China

Main article: [National security of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_of_China)

China's military is the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) (PLA). The military is the largest in the world, with 2.3 million active troops in 2005.

The [Ministry of State Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_State_Security_\(China\)) was established in 1983 to ensure "the security of the state through effective measures against enemy agents, spies, and counterrevolutionary activities designed to sabotage or overthrow China's socialist system."[^cite_note-43]

### European Union

Main articles: [Schengen area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_area), [Europol](525329.md), and [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy)

For [Schengen area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_area)[^cite_note-44] some parts of national security and external border control are enforced by [Frontex](3072700.md)[^cite_note-45] according to the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon). The [security policy of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy) is set by [High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy](750942.md) and assisted by [European External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service).[^cite_note-eeas-46] [Europol](525329.md) is one of the [agencies of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agencies_of_the_European_Union) responsible for combating various forms of [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime) in the European Union through coordinating law enforcement agencies of the EU member states.[^cite_note-47]

European Union national security has been accused of insufficiently preventing foreign threats.[^cite_note-48]

### India

Main articles: [National Security Council (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(India\)), [National Security Strategy (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Strategy_\(India\)), [Terrorism in India](1916193.md), and [Illegal immigration to India](48211514.md)

The state of the Republic of India's national security is determined by its internal stability and geopolitical interests. While Islamic upsurge in Indian State of Jammu and Kashmir demanding secession and far left-wing terrorism in India's [red corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_corridor) remain some key issues in India's internal security, [terrorism from Pakistan-based militant groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_state-sponsored_terrorism) has been emerging as a major concern for New Delhi.

The [National Security Advisor of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Advisor_\(India\)) heads the [National Security Council of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(India\)), receives all kinds of intelligence reports, and is chief advisor to the [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) over national and international security policy. The National Security Council has India's [defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(India\)), [foreign](812043.md), [home](1974473.md), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(India\)) ministers and deputy chairman of [NITI Aayog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NITI_Aayog) as its members and is responsible for shaping strategies for India's security in all aspects.[^cite_note-49]

A lawyer Ashwini Upadhyay filed a [Public interest litigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_interest_litigation_in_India) (PIL) in the "Supreme Court of India" (SC) to identify and deport illegal immigrants. Responding to this PIL, [Delhi Police](8808263.md) told the SC in July 2019 that nearly 500 illegal Bangladeshi immigrants have been deported in the preceding 28 months.[^cite_note-illegalbang3-50] There are estimated 600,000 to 700,000 illegal Bangladeshi and [Rohingya](1918632.md) immigrants in [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)) (NCR) region specially in the districts of [Gurugram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurugram), [Faridabad](1180794.md), and [Nuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuh_district) ([Mewat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewat) region), as well as interior villages of [Bhiwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhiwani_district) and [Hisar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisar_district). Most of them are Muslims who have acquired fake Hindu identity, and under questioning, they pretend to be from West Bengal. In September 2019, the [Chief Minister of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Haryana), [Manohar Lal Khattar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manohar_Lal_Khattar) announced the implementation of [NRC for Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Citizens_of_India) by setting up a legal framework under the former judge of the Punjab and Haryana High Court, Justice HS Bhalla for updating NRC which will help in weeding out these illegal immigrants.[^cite_note-illegalbang2-51]

### Iceland

In 2025, [Iceland](14531.md) became the first nation to formally integrate the [potential collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrupt_climate_change) of the [Atlantic meridional overturning circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_meridional_overturning_circulation) (AMOC) into its national security framework, classifying it as an existential threat due to its potential impact on food and energy security.[^cite_note-52]

### Russia

In the years 1997 and 2000, Russia adopted documents titled "National Security Concept" that described Russia's global position, the country's interests, listed threats to national security, and described the means to counter those threats. In 2009, these documents were superseded by the "*National Security Strategy to 2020*". The key body responsible for coordinating policies related to Russia's national security is the [Security Council of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council_of_Russia).

According to provision 6 of the *National Security Strategy to 2020*, national security is "the situation in which the individual, the society and the state enjoy protection from foreign and domestic threats to the degree that ensures constitutional rights and freedoms, decent quality of life for citizens, as well as sovereignty, territorial integrity and stable development of the Russian Federation, the defence and security of the state."

In 2024, Russian officials announced that the national security doctrine would be further modified to lower the threshold for the [use of nuclear weapons](588941.md).[^cite_note-53]

### Singapore

Main article: [Total Defence (Singapore)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Defence_\(Singapore\))

Total Defence is Singapore's [whole-of-society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole-of-society) national defence concept[^cite_note-54] based on the premise that the strongest defence of a nation is collective defence[^cite_note-\:10-55] – when every aspect of society stays united for the defence of the country.[^cite_note-56] Adopted from the national defence strategies of Sweden and Switzerland,[^cite_note-\:11-57] Total Defence was introduced in Singapore in 1984. Then, it was recognised that military threats to a nation can affect the psyche and social fabric of its people.[^cite_note-58] Therefore, the defence and progress of Singapore are dependent on all of its citizens' resolve, along with the government and armed forces.[^cite_note-\:12-59] Total Defence has since evolved to take into consideration threats and challenges outside of the conventional military domain.

### Ukraine

National security of Ukraine is defined in Ukrainian law as "a set of legislative and organisational measures aimed at permanent protection of vital interests of man and citizen, society and the state, which ensure sustainable development of society, timely detection, prevention and neutralisation of real and potential threats to national interests in areas of law enforcement, fight against corruption, border activities and defence, migration policy, health care, education and science, technology and innovation policy, cultural development of the population, freedom of speech and [information security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_security), social policy and pension provision, housing and communal services, financial services market, protection of property rights, stock markets and circulation of securities, fiscal and customs policy, trade and business, banking services, investment policy, auditing, monetary and exchange rate policy, information security, licensing, industry and agriculture, transport and communications, information technology, energy and energy saving, functioning of natural monopolies, use of [subsoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsoil), land and water resources, minerals, protection of ecology and environment and other areas of public administration, in the event of emergence of negative trends towards the creation of potential or real threats to national interests."[^cite_note-60]

The primary body responsible for coordinating national security policy in Ukraine is the [National Security and Defense Council of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_and_Defense_Council_of_Ukraine). It is an advisory state agency to the [President of Ukraine](20688479.md), tasked with developing a policy of national security on domestic and international matters. All sessions of the council take place in the [Presidential Administration Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Administration_Building_\(Kiev\)). The council was created by the provision of [Supreme Council of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_of_Ukraine) #1658-12 on 11 October 1991. It was defined as the highest state body of collegiate governing on matters of defence and security of Ukraine with the following goals:

- Protecting sovereignty

- Constitutional order

- Territorial integrity and inviolability of the republic

- Developing strategies and continuous improvement of policy in the sphere of defence and state security

- Comprehensive scientific assessment of the military threat nature

- Determining position toward modern warfare

- Effective control over the execution of the tasks of the state and its institutions keeping defence capabilities of Ukraine at the level of defence sufficiency

### United Kingdom

The primary body responsible for coordinating national security policy in the UK is the [National Security Council (United Kingdom)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)) which helps produce and enact the [UK's National Security Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Strategy_\(United_Kingdom\)). It was created in May 2010 by the new [coalition government](6036.md) of the [Conservative Party (UK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) and [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)). The National Security Council is a committee of the [Cabinet of the United Kingdom](162790.md) and was created as part of a wider reform of the national [security apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_apparatus). This reform also included the creation of a [National Security Adviser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Adviser_\(United_Kingdom\)) and a [National Security Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)#National_Security_Secretariat) to support the National Security Council.[^cite_note-61]

### United States

Main article: [National security of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_of_the_United_States)

#### National Security Act of 1947

Main articles: [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) and [United States National Security Council](44026.md)

The concept of national security became an official guiding principle of [U.S. foreign policy](7564.md) when the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) was signed on 26 July 1947, by [U.S. President](24113.md) [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman).[^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]<sup>: 3 </sup> As amended in 1949, this Act:

- created important components of American national security, such as the precursor to the [Department of Defense](7279897.md);

- subordinated the military branches to the new cabinet-level position of [Secretary of Defense](43998.md);

- established the [National Security Council](44026.md) and the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md);

  [^cite_note-Davis-62]

Notably, the Act did *not* define national security, which was conceivably advantageous, as its ambiguity made it a powerful phrase to invoke against diverse threats to interests of the state, such as domestic concerns.[^cite_note-Romm_1993-3]<sup>: 3–5 </sup>

The notion that national security encompasses more than just military security was present, though understated, from the beginning. The Act established the National Security Council so as to "advise the President on the integration of domestic, military and foreign policies relating to national security".[^cite_note-Paleri-2]<sup>: 52 </sup>

The act establishes, within the National Security Council, the Committee on Foreign Intelligence, whose duty is to conduct an annual review "identifying the intelligence required to address the national security interests of the United States *as specified by the President*" (emphasis added).[^cite_note-63]

In Gen. [Maxwell Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Taylor)'s 1974 essay "The Legitimate Claims of National Security", Taylor states:[^cite_note-64]

> The national valuables in this broad sense include current assets and national interests, as well as the sources of strength upon which our future as a nation depends. Some valuables are tangible and earthy; others are spiritual or intellectual. They range widely from political assets such as the Bill of Rights, our political institutions, and international friendships to many economic assets which radiate worldwide from a highly productive domestic economy supported by rich natural resources. It is the urgent need to protect valuables such as these which legitimizes and makes essential the role of national security.

#### National security state

To address the institutionalisation of new bureaucracies and government practices in the post–World War II period in the U.S., the culture of semi-permanent military mobilisation joined the National Security Council (NSC), the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), the Department of Defense (DoD), and the Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS) for the practical application of the concept of the *national security state*:[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

> During and after World War II, U.S. leaders expanded the concept of national security, and used its terminology for the first time to explain America's relationship to the world. For most of U.S. history, the continental United States was secure. But, by 1945, it had become rapidly vulnerable with the advent of long-range bombers, atom bombs, and ballistic missiles. A general perception grew that future mobilization would be insufficient and that preparation must be constant. For the first time, American leaders dealt with the essential paradox of national security faced by the Roman Empire and subsequent great powers: *Si vis pacem, para bellum* — "If you want peace, prepare for war."[^cite_note-68]

— David Jablonsky

[Jack Nelson-Pallmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nelson-Pallmeyer#Views) offers a seven-characteristic definition for 'national security state' as where the military and broader national security establishment, e.g., exert influence over political and economic affairs; hold ultimate power while maintaining an appearance of democracy; are preoccupied with external and/or internal enemies; define policies in secret and implement those policies through covert channels.[^cite_note-69]

#### Obama administration

The U.S. [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) defines national security of the United States in the following manner :[^cite_note-70]

> A collective term encompassing both national defense and foreign relations of the United States. Specifically, the condition provided by: a. a military or defense advantage over any foreign nation or group of nations; b. a favorable foreign relations position; or c. a defense posture capable of successfully resisting hostile or destructive action from within or without, overt or covert.

In 2010, the [White House](33057.md) included an all-encompassing world-view in a national security strategy which identified "security" as one of the country's "four enduring national interests" that were "inexorably intertwined":[^cite_note-71]

> "To achieve the world we seek, the United States must apply our strategic approach in pursuit of four enduring national interests:
>
> - *Security:*  The security of the United States, its citizens, and U.S. allies and partners.
>
> - *Prosperity:*  A strong, innovative, and growing U.S. economy in an open international economic system that promotes opportunity and prosperity.
>
> - *Values:* Respect for universal values at home and around the world.
>
> - *International Order:*  An international order advanced by U.S. leadership that promotes peace, security, and opportunity through stronger cooperation to meet global challenges.
>
> &#x20;Each of these interests is inextricably linked to the others: no single interest can be pursued in isolation, but at the same time, positive action in one area will help advance all four."

— *National Security Strategy*, [Executive Office of the President of the United States](44029.md) (May 2010)

#### Empowerment of women

Main article: [Hillary Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Doctrine)

U.S. Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) has said that, "The countries that threaten regional and global peace are the very places where women and girls are deprived of dignity and opportunity".[^cite_note-Lemmon_2013-72] She has noted that countries, where women are oppressed, are places where the "rule of law and democracy are struggling to take root",[^cite_note-Lemmon_2013-72] and that, when women's rights as equals in society are upheld, the society as a whole changes and improves, which in turn enhances stability in that society, which in turn contributes to global society.[^cite_note-Lemmon_2013-72]

#### Cyber

The Bush administration in January 2008 initiated the [Comprehensive National Cybersecurity Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_National_Cybersecurity_Initiative) (CNCI). It introduced a differentiated approach, such as identifying existing and emerging cybersecurity threats, finding and plugging existing cyber vulnerabilities and apprehending those trying to access federal information systems.[^cite_note-73]

President Obama said the "cyber threat is one of the most serious economic and national security challenges we face as a nation" and that "America's economic prosperity in the 21st century will depend on cybersecurity".[^cite_note-74]
