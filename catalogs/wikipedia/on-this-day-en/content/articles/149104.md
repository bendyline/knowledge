The **secretary of state for the Home Department**, more commonly known as the **home secretary**, is a senior [minister of the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Crown) in the [Government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md) and the head of the [Home Office](225695.md), equivalent to an [interior minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_minister).[^cite_note-gov.uk-3] The position is a [Great Office of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Office_of_State), making the home secretary one of the most senior and influential ministers in the government. The incumbent is a statutory member of the [British Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cabinet) and [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)).

The position was created in 1782,[^cite_note-4] though its responsibilities have [changed many times](225695.md#History).[^cite_note-5] Past office holders have included the prime ministers [Lord North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_North), [Robert Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Peel), the [Duke of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Wellington), [Lord Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Palmerston), [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan) and [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May). The longest-serving home secretary is [Henry Addington, 1st Viscount Sidmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Addington,_1st_Viscount_Sidmouth), who held the post continuously for 9 years, 221 days.[^cite_note-6] The shortest-serving home secretary is [Grant Shapps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Shapps), who served in the position for the final six days of the [premiership of Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Liz_Truss). In 2007, [Jacqui Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqui_Smith) became the first female home secretary.[^cite_note-7] The incumbent home secretary is [Shabana Mahmood](27266407.md), who has served since 5 September 2025 and was reappointed on the formation of the [Burnham ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_ministry) on 20 July 2026.[^cite_note-gov-mahmood-8][^cite_note-burnham-cabinet-9]

The office holder works alongside the other [Home Office ministers](225695.md#Home_Office_ministers) and the [permanent under-secretary of state of the Home Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_under-secretary_of_state_of_the_Home_Office). The corresponding [shadow minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_minister) is the [shadow home secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_home_secretary), and the performance of the home secretary is also scrutinized by the [Home Affairs Select Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Affairs_Select_Committee) in the House of Commons[^cite_note-10] and the Justice and Home Affairs Committee in the House of Lords.[^cite_note-11]

Historically, the role has been regarded as a political dead end for aspiring politicians, due to the numerous potential issues and controversies it tends to involve.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Responsibilities

Main article: [Powers of the home secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_the_home_secretary)

Corresponding to what is generally known as an [interior minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_minister) in many other countries, the home secretary's remit includes:

- Oversight of [Home Office ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_office#ministers)

* [Law enforcement](347149.md) in [England and Wales](275187.md) and UK wide elements of policing, that are not devolved.

  [^cite_note-15]

* Matters of [national security](240468.md) and [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism)

* Matters of [immigration](1247786.md) and border security

* Oversight of the [Security Service (MI5)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Service_\(MI5\)).

  [^cite_note-gov.uk-3]

Formerly, the home secretary was the minister responsible for prisons and probation in England and Wales; however in 2007 those responsibilities were transferred to the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(United_Kingdom\)) under the [lord chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_chancellor).

From the birth of [King Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VIII) in 1894 until the birth of [Princess Alexandra of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Alexandra_of_Kent) in 1936, the home secretary would be present at all royal births to verify that the baby and potential heir to the throne was a descendent of the monarch, and not an imposter. Prior to this, a wider range of [privy counsellors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)) would attend.[^cite_note-16] The practice was abolished by [King George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_VI) shortly before the birth of [King Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Charles_III).[^cite_note-17]

## History

The title [Secretary of State in the government of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_\(England\)) dates back to the early 17th century.[^cite_note-\:4-18] The position of Secretary of State for the Home Department was created in the [British governmental reorganisation of 1782](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_1782), in which the responsibilities of the [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Northern_Department) and [Southern Departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Southern_Department) were reformed into the [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office) and [Home Office](225695.md).[^cite_note-\:4-18]

In 2007, the new Ministry of Justice took on the criminal justice functions of the Home Office and its agencies.[^cite_note-19]
