[Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) in the [United States](3434750.md) has been active since the beginning of colonial times, but became a major industry in the 19th century with a number of new mineral discoveries causing a series of mining rushes. In 2015, the value of coal, metals, and industrial minerals mined in the United States was US\$109.6 billion. 158,000 workers were directly employed by the [mining industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_industry).[^cite_note-auto-1]&#x20;

The mining industry has a number of impacts on communities, individuals and the environment. Mine safety incidents have been important parts of American [occupational safety and health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_safety_and_health) history. Mining has a [number of environmental impacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_mining). In the United States, issues like [mountaintop removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaintop_removal_mining), and [acid mine drainage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_mine_drainage) have widespread impacts on all parts of the environment. As of January 2020, the EPA lists 142 mines in the [Superfund program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfund).[^cite_note-\:1mius-2]&#x20;

In 2019, the country was the 4th world producer of [gold](12240.md);[^cite_note-3] 5th largest world producer of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper);[^cite_note-4] 5th worldwide producer of [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum);[^cite_note-5] 10th worldwide producer of [silver](27119.md);[^cite_note-6] 2nd largest world producer of [rhenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenium);[^cite_note-7] 2nd largest world producer of [sulfur](27127.md);[^cite_note-8] 3rd largest world producer of [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate);[^cite_note-9] 3rd largest world producer of [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum);[^cite_note-10] 4th largest world producer of [lead](17747.md);[^cite_note-11] 4th largest world producer of [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc);[^cite_note-12] 5th worldwide producer of [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium);[^cite_note-13] 9th largest world producer of [iron ore](145352.md);[^cite_note-14] 9th largest world producer of [potash](56509.md);[^cite_note-15] 12th largest world producer of [cobalt](24580536.md);[^cite_note-16] 13th largest world producer of [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium);[^cite_note-17] world's largest producer of [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum);[^cite_note-18] 2nd largest world producer of [kyanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyanite);[^cite_note-19] 2nd largest world producer of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone);[^cite_note-20] in addition to being the 2nd largest world producer of [salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_salt).[^cite_note-21] It was the world's 10th largest producer of [uranium](31743.md) in 2018.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

## History

The extraction of minerals from the Earth dates back to the Ancient [Egyptians](31912046.md)' mining of copper in 3000 BCE.[^cite_note-\:3-23] Mining for ancient civilizations was often extremely dangerous involving the use of stone tools or hands for excavation.[^cite_note-\:3-23] By the Middle Ages, stone tools were replaced by explosives, black powder, to break-up larger rock formations.[^cite_note-\:3-23] [Colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler) in the United States found iron ore at the Jamestown settlement which resulted in a mining operation beginning in 1620; however, the operation was cut short due to the presence of [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) at the site.[^cite_note-24] Despite several small mining endeavors beginning in the 17th and 18th centuries, mining did not gain major traction in the United States until the 19th century.[^cite_note-\:3-23] In terms of technology, explosives such as black powder were phased out as dynamite increased in popularity as a new mining method in the 19th century.[^cite_note-\:3-23] The [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) began in 1848 after the discovery of gold at Sutter's Mill in 1848. By the year 1849, over 80,000 people had relocated to California in search of gold.[^cite_note-\:4-25] Eventually, the Gold Rush peaked in 1852 and concluded by the early 1900s.[^cite_note-\:4-25] During the rush [Chilean miners introduced technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chileans_in_the_California_gold_rush) such as the [Chilean mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_mill) and the *batea* [gold pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_pan) to the United States.[^cite_note-consulate-26][^cite_note-accul-27] Many Euramericans learned skills of the mining trade from the usually more experienced Chileans.[^cite_note-accul-27]&#x20;

## Mining accidents



Non-coal mining fatalities in the United States, 1911–2014 (data from US Department of Labor)

From 1880 to 1910, mine accidents claimed thousands of fatalities, with more than 3,000 in 1907 alone.[^cite_note-29] Where annual mining deaths had numbered more than 1,000 a year during the early part of the 20th century, they decreased to an average of about 500 during the late 1950s, and to 93 during the 1990s.[^cite_note-30] In addition to deaths, many thousands more are injured (an average of 21,351 injuries per year between 1991 and 1999), but overall there has been a downward trend of deaths and injuries.&#x20;

The [Monongah Mining Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongah_Mining_Disaster) was the worst mining accident of American history; 362 workers were killed in an underground explosion on December 6, 1907, in [Monongah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongah), [West Virginia](32905.md). The [U.S. Bureau of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bureau_of_Mines) was created in 1910 to investigate accidents, advise industry, conduct production and safety research, and teach courses in accident prevention, first aid, and mine rescue. The [Federal Coal Mine Health and Safety Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Coal_Mine_Health_and_Safety_Act) of 1969 and 1977 set further safety standards for the industry.&#x20;

In 1959, the [Knox Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knox_Mine_Disaster) occurred in [Port Griffith, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Griffith,_Pennsylvania). The swelling [Susquehanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susquehanna_River) river collapsed into a mine under it and resulted in 12 deaths. In [Plymouth, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth,_Pennsylvania), the [Avondale Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avondale_Mine_Disaster) resulted in the deaths of 108 miners and two rescue workers after a fire in the only shaft eliminated the oxygen in the mine. Federal laws for mining safety ensued this disaster. Pennsylvania suffered another disaster in 2002 at [Quecreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quecreek_Mine_Rescue), 9 miners were trapped underground and subsequently rescued after 78 hours. During 2006, 72 miners lost their lives at work, 47 by coal mining. The majority of these fatalities occurred in [Kentucky](16846.md) and [West Virginia](32905.md), including the [Sago Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sago_Mine_Disaster).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] On April 5, 2010, in the [Upper Big Branch Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Big_Branch_Mine_disaster) an underground explosion caused the deaths of 29 miners.&#x20;

## Environmental impact

Further information: [Environmental impact of mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_mining)

Mining has environmental impacts at many stages in the process and production of mining. In the United States, many different regions in the United States have environmental challenges caused by either historical or current mining.&#x20;

### Mountain top removal

This section is an excerpt from [Mountaintop removal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaintop_removal_mining).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mountaintop_removal_mining\&action=edit)]



Mountaintop removal site



Mountaintop removal site in [Pike County, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pike_County,_Kentucky).

[Mountaintop removal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaintop_removal_mining) (MTR), also known as mountaintop mining (MTM), is a form of [surface mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_mining) at the [summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_\(topography\)) or summit ridge of a mountain. [Coal seams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_seam) are extracted from a mountain by removing the land, or [overburden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overburden), above the seams. This process is considered to be safer compared to underground mining because the coal seams are accessed from above instead of underground. In the [United States](3434750.md), this method of [coal mining](208413.md) is conducted in the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) in the [eastern United States](509486.md). [Explosives](10192.md) are used to remove up to 400 vertical feet (120 m) of mountain to expose underlying coal seams. Excess rock and soil is dumped into nearby valleys, in what are called "holler fills" ("hollow fills") or "valley fills".[^cite_note-Mountaintop_removal_mining_ensnews-33][^cite_note-Mountaintop_removal_mining_epaPeis-34][^cite_note-Mountaintop_removal_mining_epa-35]&#x20;

### Abandoned mines

There are tens of thousands of abandoned mines in the United States. Many abandoned mines pose environmental challenges, such as [acid mine drainage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_mine_drainage). In Colorado alone, there are 18,382 abandoned mines.[^cite_note-\:0-36] The United States has had many different environmental disasters caused by these mines, such as the [2015 Gold King Mine waste water spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Gold_King_Mine_waste_water_spill). Many [Superfund sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Superfund_sites) are mines. As of January 2020. the EPA lists 142 mines in the Superfund program[^cite_note-\:1mius-2]&#x20;

### Water pollution

Fresh water is used in the mining process for the extraction of minerals like [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and the processing of ore.[^cite_note-37] After the water has cycled through the mining process, it is discharged as effluent. The effluent or drainage is acidic due to the presence of [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid). Acid-rich water drainage can lead to environmental implications including contaminated drinking water and disruption of [aquatic environments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_ecosystem) near the mining site.[^cite_note-38] In addition to the detrimental effects caused by mining drainage, the mining process is responsible for depleting groundwater supplies. The [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_California) River basin is being dried to support a copper mine in Arizona.[^cite_note-39]&#x20;

### Land degradation

44% of mining facilities in the United States are located in forests. Large-scale mining in areas of [Latin America](18524.md), like Brazil, release pollutants detrimental to neighboring [rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest).[^cite_note-\:1-40] Copper mining efforts in the Carajas Mineral Province in Brazil result in 6,100 km of deforestation every year.[^cite_note-\:1-40] Mining also results in a loss of [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity). Deforestation as well as the release of toxic chemicals to both the air and water can affect neighboring plant and animal life.[^cite_note-41] 40% of all global mines are located in areas of increased biodiversity that have experienced decline in species population due to surrounding industry.[^cite_note-\:2-42] Chemicals used in the mining process also result in [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion) that strips the land of essential nutrients affecting surrounding plant and animal life.[^cite_note-\:2-42]&#x20;

## Controversies

Mines are often controversial in their local areas, with local residents split by those in favor particularly due to the economic impact of new jobs and those concerned by the environmental impact and occupational hazards. In the case of the proposed [Crandon mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crandon_mine), the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) found that tribes have the right to regulate water and air, which destroyed the economic feasibility of the project.[^cite_note-43]&#x20;
