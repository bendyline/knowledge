The **Riau Islands** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Kepulauan Riau*; [Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_script): كڤولاوان رياو) is a [province of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) consisting of a group of islands located in the western part of the country. It was established in 2002 after being separated from the neighboring [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) Province. The capital of the province is [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang), while the largest city is [Batam](716783.md). It shares a maritime border with Riau and [Jambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambi) to the west, [Bangka Belitung Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Belitung_Islands) to the south, [Singapore](27318.md) to the northeast, [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [West Kalimantan](453636.md) to the east, and [Vietnam](202354.md) and [Cambodia](334751.md) to the north. It comprises a total of 2,408 islands (1,798 having names) scattered between [Sumatra](28679.md), [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula), and [Borneo](4518.md) including the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago).[^cite_note-6] The land area of these islands is 8,708.73 km<sup>2</sup>. Situated on one of the world's busiest shipping lanes along the [Malacca Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Strait) and the [Natuna Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Sea) ([South China Sea](74209.md)), the province shares water borders with neighboring countries such as Singapore, Malaysia, and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei). The Riau Islands also have relatively large potential mineral resources and energy, as well as marine resources.[^cite_note-7]

The Riau Islands have played a significant role in the maritime history and civilization of the [Nusantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara_\(term\)) region. Between the 7th and 13th centuries, the area was influenced by the powerful [Srivijaya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya_Empire), a major maritime kingdom that controlled trade routes along the [Malacca Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Strait).[^cite_note-8] Evidence of Srivijaya's influence in the Riau Islands can be seen from the discovery of the [Pasir Panjang Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prasasti_Pasir_Panjang?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Karimun Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimun_Regency), which shows the existence of religious practices through Buddha's footprints, indicating early settlement and the spread of Buddhism long before the arrival of Islam.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

After the decline of Srivijaya, the Riau Islands became part of the [Bentan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentan) around 1100 AD, with Raja Iskandar Syah as one of its most notable rulers.[^cite_note-11] In the next period, The Riau Islands became part of the [Johor Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Sultanate), which was later partitioned between the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) and [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya) after the [Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1824), in which the archipelago fell under Dutch influence.[^cite_note-12] A Dutch protectorate, the [Riau-Lingga Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau-Lingga_Sultanate), was established in the region between 1824 and 1911 before being directly ruled by the Dutch East Indies.[^cite_note-13] The archipelago became a part of [Indonesia](14579.md) following the [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies) of the [Japanese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) (1942–1945) and the [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) (1945–1949). The Riau Islands separated from the province of [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) in September 2002, becoming [Indonesia](14579.md)'s third-youngest province.

A [free trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_zone) of the [Indonesia–Malaysia–Singapore Growth Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia%E2%80%93Singapore_Growth_Triangle), the Riau Islands has experienced rapid industrialisation since the 1970s. The Riau Islands is one of the country's most prosperous provinces, having a [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) of [Rp](367435.md) 72,571,750 ([US\$](18717338.md)8,300.82) as of 2011, the fourth highest among all provinces in Indonesia after [East Kalimantan](453651.md), [Jakarta](16275.md) and [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau).[^cite_note-14] In addition, as of 2018, the Riau Islands has a [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of 0.748, also the fourth highest among all provinces in Indonesia after Jakarta, [Special Region of Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Region_of_Yogyakarta) and East Kalimantan.[^cite_note-15]

The population of the Riau Islands is heterogeneous and is highly diverse in ethnicity, culture, language and religion. The province is home to different ethnic groups such as the [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesians), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people) and others. Economic rise in the region has attracted many immigrants and workers from other parts of [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-sijgoog-16] The area around Batam is also home to many expatriates from different countries. Approximately 80% of these are from other [Asian](689.md) countries, with most of the westerners coming from the [United Kingdom](31717.md), rest of [Europe](9239.md), as well as [Australia](4689264.md) and the [United States](3434750.md). The province also has the second largest number of foreign tourist arrivals in [Indonesia](14579.md), after [Bali](4147.md).[^cite_note-17]

## Etymology

There are several possible origins of the word *riau.* The first possible origins is that *riau* is derived from the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) word *rio*, which means river.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Kondisisosbud-setneg-19] After the fall of the [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate) in the early 16th century, the remaining Malaccan nobles and subjects fled from [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca) to mainland Sumatra while being pursued by the Portuguese. The Portuguese is believed to have reached as far as the [Siak River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siak_River).[^cite_note-20]

The second theory of origins claims that *riau* comes from the word *riahi* which means sea water. The word is allegedly derived from the figure of [Sinbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinbad) al-Bahar in the book of the [One Thousand and One Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_and_One_Nights).[^cite_note-Kondisisosbud-setneg-19]

Another version is that *riau* is derived from the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) word *riuh,* which means crowded, frenzied working people. This word is believed used to reflect the nature of the [Malay people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) in present-day [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island). The name is likely to have become famous since [Raja Kecil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Kecil) moved the Malay kingdom center from Johor to Ulu Riau in 1719.[^cite_note-Kondisisosbud-setneg-19] This name was used as one of the four main sultanates that formed the kingdoms of Riau, Lingga, Johor and Pahang. However, as the consequences of the [Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1824) between the [Netherlands](21148.md) and United Kingdom, the Johor-Pahang sultanates fell under British influence, while the Riau-Lingga sultanates fell under Dutch influence.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Riau has for centuries been the home of [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) and [Orang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Laut) people (sea nomads). Later migrants came from southern China and [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia), today people from a large region of Asia has settled on the Islands. The Riau archipelago is located on the China–India maritime trading route. Chinese maritime records indicate that one of Riau archipelago's islands, [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island), and [Temasek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temasek), the old name of Singapore, were inhabited by Malay corsairs.[^cite_note-23]

As many as three hundred ships were used to force Chinese ships returning from the Indian Ocean into pirate harbors and to attack those who resisted. Large quantities of Chinese ceramics have been recovered on Bintan, some date back to the early [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) (960–1127).[^cite_note-24] An [Berber](2185.md) explorer, [Ibnu Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta), wrote about Riau in the 13th century: "*Here there are little islands, from which armed black pirates with poised arrows emerged, possessing armed warships; they plunder people but do not enslave them*."[^cite_note-25] Log records of Chinese ships testify these incidents in the 12th century. Even after several centuries, the Riau archipelago, especially Bintan, is still referred to as the "Pirate Island".[^cite_note-Hawk2-26]

During the 12th to 13th century, the Riau archipelago was a part of the [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya) Empire. Sri Tri Buana, a member of the royal family of [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang), visited Riau in 1290. The Queen of Bintan met him, and he combined flotilla of 800 vessels sailed for Bintan where he later became a king. It is also said that it was he who gave name to Singapore. Upon landing on Temasek, he spotted an animal, which he thought was a lion, and renamed Temasek as [Singapura](27318.md) (Lion City). He also proclaimed himself as a king of Singapore.[^cite_note-Hawk2-26][^cite_note-History-27]

According to the [Malay Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Annals), [Sri Tri Buana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sang_Nila_Utama), fleeing from the sacking of Palembang, stayed on Bintan for several years, gathering his strength before founding the [Kingdom of Singapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Singapura) (Singapore).[^cite_note-28]

### Sultanate era



[Pulau Penyengat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulau_Penyengat) (Lit: Wasp island), lithography of an original watercolour by J.C. Rappard. c. 1883–1889.

During the rule of Sultan [Mansur Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansur_Shah_of_Malacca) (1459–1466), the Riau archipelago became part of the [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate). The fall of Malacca Empire in 1511 when Malacca fell to the Portuguese. Sultan Mahmud Shah fled to [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang) and then to [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor) and Bintan in 1521.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] There, he held out against Portuguese attacks, he even laid siege on Malacca in 1524, before a Portuguese counterattack forced him to flee to Sumatra where he died in 1528.[^cite_note-31] Sultan [Alauddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Riayat_Shah_II_of_Johor) succeeded his father and set up a new capital in the south. The archipelago became the part of the "war triangle" between the Portuguese, the [Johor Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Sultanate) and the [Acheh Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_Sultanate) of northern Sumatra.

Bintan saw many rulers during the time, as the three countries grew and declined in power. Subsequently, the Portuguese attacked Johor in 1587.[^cite_note-pinto-32] The [Siege of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Johor_\(1587\)), resulted in a Portuguese victory. The forces of Johor were unable to prevent the heavy Portuguese infantry from landing and storming the city after a naval bombardment, its Sultan was forced to retreat into the jungle in a rout.[^cite_note-\:1-33] The Portuguese captured ample spoils, which included over 1000 cannon, the great majority of them of small caliber, 1500 firearms, and burned upwards to 2000 craft of many sizes.[^cite_note-34] Following the attack, Dom Pedro de Lima (brother of Dom Paulo de Lima) also sacked Bintan, then a vassal of Johor.[^cite_note-35]

After the fall of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_City) in 1511, the Riau islands became the centre of political power of the mighty Sultanate of Johor or Johor-Riau, based on Bintan Island, and were for long considered the centre of Malay culture.[^cite_note-36] Riau was a centre of Islamic studies and teaching. Many orthodox scholars from the Indian Subcontinent and Arabia were housed in special religious hostels, while devotees of [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) could seek initiation into one of the many [Tariqah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqah) (Sufi Brotherhood) which flourished in Riau.[^cite_note-37] On the northern part of Sumatra around the same period, the Aceh Sultanate was beginning to gain substantial influence over the [Straits of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Malacca). With the fall of Malacca [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) traders often skipped Malacca in favour of [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh) or Johor's capital [Johor Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Lama) (Kota Batu). Therefore, Malacca and Aceh became direct competitors. With the Portuguese and Johor frequently in conflict, Aceh launched multiple raids against both sides to tighten its grip over the straits.[^cite_note-38] The rise and expansion of Aceh encouraged the Portuguese and Johor to sign a truce and divert their attention to Aceh. The truce, however, was short-lived and with Aceh severely weakened, Johor and the Portuguese had each other in their sights again. During the rule of Sultan [Iskandar Muda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskandar_Muda), Aceh attacked Johor in 1613 and again in 1615.[^cite_note-pinto-32][^cite_note-39]

With the fall of [Portuguese Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Malacca) in 1641 and the decline of Aceh due to the growing power of the Dutch, Johor started to re-established itself as a power along the Straits of Malacca during the reign of Sultan [Abdul Jalil Shah III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Jalil_Shah_III) (1623–1677).[^cite_note-40] Its influence extended to [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang), [Sungei Ujong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungei_Ujong), Malacca, [Klang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klang_Valley) and the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago).[^cite_note-tan2-41]



The Tomb of Puteri Hamidah, Queen of Sultan Mahmud Shah III.

In the early 18th century, the descendants of the Sultan and the Regent of Johor were fighting for power. [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buginese_people) aristocrats from [Sulawesi](28740.md) was asked to assist the Regent of Johor, and managed to achieve control of Riau due to the internal struggle between the members of the Johor Sultanate. The Bugis made Bintan a major trading center. Riau and Bintan also attracted British, Chinese, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Arab](2185.md) and Indian traders. The Dutch however started to look upon Riau and the Bugis as a dangerous rival to Dutch trade in the region, drawing away trade from their ports in Malacca and [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies) (Jakarta). A Dutch fleet attacked Riau in 1784 but failed to hold the islands. Another attempt later the same year also failed before they managed to break the Bugis blockade of Malacca in June 1784.[^cite_note-42] The Bugis commander, Raja Haji was killed during battle and the Bugis units retreated, which opened the way for a Dutch counterattack on Riau. The Bugis was expelled from Bintan and Riau, and a treaty between the Dutch and the Malay sultans granted Dutch control over the area.[^cite_note-43] The treaty caused much anger among Malay rulers, and again in 1787 a force that was offered refuge by Sultan Mahmud drove out the Dutch. This also led to the return of the Bugis and the rivalry between the Bugis and Malay. Peace between the two parties was finally reached in 1803. Around this time Sultan Mahmud gave [Penyengat Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penyengat_Island) as a wedding gift to his bride, Raja Hamidah, [Raja Ali Haji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ali_Haji)'s daughter. Penyengat became for a period a center of government, Islamic religion and the Malay culture.[^cite_note-Matheson_1989_153–172-44]

In 1812, the Johor-Riau Sultanate experienced a succession crisis.[^cite_note-46] The death of the Mahmud Shah III in Lingga left no [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent). Royal custom required that the succeeding sultan must be present at his predecessor's deathbed. However at the time Mahmud Shah III died, the eldest prince, Tengku Hussein, was in [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang) to celebrate his marriage to the daughter of the [Bendahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendahara) (governor).[^cite_note-47] The other candidate was Tengku Hussein's half-brother, Tengku Abdul Rahman. To complicate matters, neither of the candidates was of full royal blood. The mother of Tengku Hussein, Cik Mariam, owed her origin to a [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people) slave lady and a [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis_people) commoner.[^cite_note-48] Tengku Abdul Rahman had a similarly lowborn mother, Cik Halimah. The only unquestionably royal wife and [consort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_consort) of Mahmud Shah was Engku Puteri Hamidah, whose only child had died an hour after birth.[^cite_note-49] In the following chaos, Engku Puteri was expected to install Tengku Hussein as the next sultan, because he had been preferred by the late Mahmud Shah. Based on the royal *[adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adat)* (customary observance), the consent of Engku Puteri was crucial as she was the holder of the *Cogan* ([Royal Regalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regalia)) of Johor-Riau, and the installation of a new sultan was only valid if it took place with the regalia. The regalia was fundamental to the installation of the sultan; it was a symbol of power, legitimacy and the sovereignty of the state.[^cite_note-50] [Yang di-Pertuan Muda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertuan_Muda_of_Riau) Raja Ja'afar (then-[viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceregal) of the sultanate) supported the reluctant Tengku Abdul Rahman, adhering to the rules of royal protocol, as he had been present at the late Sultan's deathbed.[^cite_note-51]

Rivalry between the British and the Dutch intensified. The British had earlier gained [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Malacca) from the Dutch under the [Treaty of The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_The_Hague_\(1795\)) in 1795 and saw an opportunity to increase their regional influence. They crowned Tengku Hussein in Singapore, and he took the title [Hussein Shah of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Shah_of_Johor).[^cite_note-sgmindef1997-52][^cite_note-Supreme_Court,_Singapore-53] The British were actively involved in the Johor-Riau administration between 1812 and 1818, and their intervention further strengthened their dominance in the Strait of Malacca. The British recognised Johor-Riau as a sovereign state and offered to pay Engku Puteri 50,000 Ringgits ([Spanish Coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_dollar)) for the royal regalia, which she refused.[^cite_note-54] Seeing the diplomatic advantage gained in the region by the British, the Dutch responded by crowning Tengku Abdul Rahman as sultan instead. They also obtained, at the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), a withdrawal of British recognition of Johor-Riau sovereignty.[^cite_note-55] To further curtail the British domination over the region, the Dutch entered into an agreement with the Johor-Riau Sultanate on 27 November 1818. The agreement stipulated that the Dutch were to be the paramount leaders of the Johor-Riau Sultanate and that only Dutch people could engage in trade with the kingdom. A Dutch garrison was then stationed in Riau.[^cite_note-56] The Dutch also secured an agreement that Dutch consent was required for all future appointments of Johor-Riau Sultans. This agreement was signed by Yang di-Pertuan Muda Raja Ja'afar representing Abdul Rahman, without the sultan's consent or knowledge.[^cite_note-Matheson_1989_153–172-44]

Just as the British, both the Dutch and Yang di-Pertuan Muda then desperately tried to win the royal regalia from Engku Puteri. The reluctant Abdul Rahman, believing he was not the rightful heir, decided to move from Lingga to [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu), claiming that he wanted to celebrate his marriage. The Dutch, who desired to control the Johor-Riau Empire, feared losing momentum because of the absence of mere regalia. They therefore ordered Timmerman Tyssen, the Dutch Governor of Malacca, to seize Penyengat in October 1822 and remove the royal regalia from Tengku Hamidah by force. The regalia was then stored in the crown prince's fort in Tanjung Pinang. Engku Puteri was reported to have written a letter to [Van Der Capellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godert_van_der_Capellen), the Dutch Governor in Batavia, about this issue. With the royal regalia in Dutch hands, Abdul Rahman was invited from Terengganu and proclaimed as the Sultan of Johor, Riau-Lingga and Pahang on 27 November 1822. Hence, the legitimate ruler of the Johor-Riau Empire was now Abdul Rahman, rather than the British-backed Hussein.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrcocki,_2007-57]

This led to the [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_\(politics\)) of Johor-Riau under the Anglo-Dutch treaty of 1824, by which the region north of the [Singapore Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Strait) including the island of Singapore and Johor were to be under British influence, while the south of the strait along with Riau and Lingga were to be controlled by the Dutch. By installing two sultans from the same kingdom, both the British and the Dutch effectively destroyed the Johor-Riau Sultanate.[^cite_note-Wins95-58][^cite_note-59] Under the treaty, [Tengku Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahman_Muazzam_Shah_of_Johor) was crowned as the [Sultan of Riau-Lingga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Riau-Lingga), bearing the name of Sultan Abdul Rahman, with the royal seat in Daik, Lingga.[^cite_note-60] [Tengku Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Shah_of_Johor), backed by the British, was installed as the Sultan of Johor and ruled over Singapore and Peninsular Johor.[^cite_note-sgmindef1997-52][^cite_note-Supreme_Court,_Singapore-53] He later ceded Singapore to the British in return for their support during the dispute. Both sultans of Johor and Riau acted mainly as [Puppet monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_monarch) under the colonial powers.



Ali Kelana, Crown Prince of Riau-Lingga, one of the founding fathers of the Roesidijah Club Riouw, the first modern organisation in the Netherlands East Indies. (taken in 1899)

The globalization of the 19th century opened new opportunities for the Riau-Lingga Sultanate. Proximity Singapore, 40 miles away, shaped the political climate of the kingdom, giving Riau Malays an opportunity to familiarize themselves with new ideas from the Middle East. The opening of [Suez Canal](29323.md) meant a journey to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) via [Port Said](205162.md), [Egypt](8087628.md) and Singapore could take no more than a fortnight; thus these cities became major ports for the [Hajj](23487999.md) pilgrimage.[^cite_note-61] Inspired by the experience and intellectual progress attained in the Middle East and influenced by the [Pan-Islamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism) brotherhood, the Riau Malay intelligentsia established the Roesidijah (Club) Riouw in 1895. The association was born as a literary circle to develop the religious, cultural and intellectual needs of the sultanate, but as it matured, it changed into a more critical organisation and began to address the fight against Dutch rule in the kingdom.[^cite_note-62] The late 19th century was marked by growing awareness among the elite and rulers of the importance of *watan* (homeland) and one's duty towards their native soil. It was also viewed that the penetration of the west in the state was slowly tearing apart the fabric of the [Malay-Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayness) identity.

By the dawn of the 20th century, the association had become a political tool against colonial power, with Raja Muhammad Thahir and Raja Ali Kelana acting as its backbone. Diplomatic missions were sent to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1883, 1895 and in 1905 to secure the liberation of the kingdom by Raja Ali Kelana, accompanied by a renowned [Pattani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patani_\(historical_region\))-born [Ulema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulema), Syeikh Wan Ahmad Fatani.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] The Dutch Colonial Office in Tanjung Pinang labelled the organisation as a left-leaning party. The organisation also won support from the *Mohakamah* (Malay Judiciary) and the *Dewan Kerajaan* (Sultanate Administrative Board). Its activities outraged the Dutch.

The movement was an early form of [Malay nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_nationalism). Non-violent and [passive resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolent_resistance) measures were adopted by the association. The main method of the movement was to hold symbolic boycotts. The Dutch then branded the movement as a passive resistance ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *leidelek verset*), and passive acts such as ignoring the raising of the Dutch flag were met with anger by the [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jakarta)-based *Raad van Indie* (Dutch East Indies Council) and the Advisor on Native Affairs, [Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Snouck_Hurgronje). Hurgronje wrote a secret letter to the *Raad van Indie*, advocating that the sultanate and association be crushed as resistance in the earlier [Aceh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_War) had been. Hurgronje justified this with several arguments, among which were that since 1902 the members of the Roesidijah Klub would gather around the royal court and refuse to raise the Dutch flag on government vessels. The Dutch Colonial Resident in Riau, A.L. Van Hasselt advised the [Governor-General of the Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies) that the Sultan was an opponent to the Dutch and immersed with a group of hardcore resistance. Later, on 1 January 1903, the Dutch Colonial Resident found that the [Dutch flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Netherlands) was not being raised during his visit to the royal palace.[^cite_note-65] In his report to the governor he wrote; "it seems that he (Sultan Abdul Rahman II) acted as if he was a sovereign king and he raises his own flag". Based on several records in the [Indonesian National Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Archives), there were some reports that the Sultan then apologized to the governor over the "flag incident".[^cite_note-66]



Abdul Rahman II of Riau Lingga, the last sultan of Riau-Lingga sultanate. He was subsequently deposed by the Dutch and went into exile

On 18 May 1905, the Dutch demanded a new agreement with the sultan, stipulating further limits on the powers of the Riau-Lingga Sultanate, requiring that the [Dutch flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_flag) must be raised higher than the flag of Riau, and specifying that Dutch officials should be given supreme honor in the land. The agreement further stipulated that the Riau-Lingga Sultanate was a mere loan from the Dutch Government. The agreement was drawn up due to the fact that the appointment of Sultan Abdul Rahman II had not been made with the consent of the Dutch and he was also clearly against colonial rule.[^cite_note-67]

The Dutch insisted that the sultan sign the agreement, but after consulting the fellow rulers of the state, Engku Kelana, Raja Ali, Raja Hitam and other members of the ruling elite, he refused, and decided to form a military regiment under the leadership of the prince regent, Tengku Umar. During a visit of the Dutch Resident's visit to Penyengat, the sultan then, on his own authority and without Dutch approval, summoned the rulers of Reteh, Gaung and Mandah, making the Resident feel as if he was being intimidated by the sultanate. The affiliates of the Roesidijah Klub, mainly the members of the administrative class, were thus able to slowly maneuver Abdul Rahman, once a supporter of Dutch rule, to act against the colonial power's wishes.[^cite_note-68]

On the morning of 11 February 1911, when the sultan and the court officials were in Daik to perform the *Mandi Safar* (a ritual purifying bath), ships of the [Royal Netherlands Navy](198478.md) anchored in [Penyengat Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penyengat_Island) and deployed hundreds of native soldiers ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *marechausse*) to lay siege to the royal court.[^cite_note-69] This was followed by the arrival of Dutch official K.M Voematra from Tanjung Pinang at the Roesidijah Club headquarters to announce the deposition of Abdul Rahman II.[^cite_note-70] The Dutch then seized the official coronation regalia,[^cite_note-71] and to prevent their seizure by the Dutch, many official buildings were deliberately razed by members of the court themselves. A mass exodus of civilians and officials to [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor) and Singapore then took place. To avoid violence and the death of civilians in Pulau Penyengat, the sultan and his officials decided not to fight the Dutch troops. The sultan and *Tengku Ampuan* (the Queen) left Pulau Penyengat and sailed to Singapore in the royal vessel *Sri Daik*, while Crown Prince Raja Ali Kelana, Khalid Hitam and the resistance movement in Bukit Bahjah followed a couple of days later. The deposed Abdul Rahman II was forced to live in exile in Singapore, where he died there in 1930 and was buried in Keramat Bukit Kasita, Kampung Bahru Road.

The Dutch officially annexed the sultanate to avoid future claims from the monarchy. Dutch direct rule over the Riau Archipelago began in 1913, and the province was administered as part of the Residency of Riau and Dependencies ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden*). The Dutch Residency consisted of Tanjung Pinang, Lingga, the [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) mainland and [Indragiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indragiri_River), while the [Tudjuh Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudjuh_Archipelago) was administered separately as part of the Pulau-Tujuh Division ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Afdeeling Poelau-Toedjoeh*).[^cite_note-hubert-herald1-72]

### Japanese occupation

Main article: [Fall of the Riau Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Riau_Islands)

See also: [Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies)

When [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Asian_theatre_of_World_War_II) broke out, the Dutch initially seemed reluctant to defend their territories in the [East Indies](23476997.md). This led the British to consider creating a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state) in Riau. They discussed prospects for a restoration with Tengku Omar and Tengku Besar, descendants of the sultans, who were then based in [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu). However, as the war approached Southeast Asia, the Dutch actively engaged in the defensive system alongside the British, and decided to shelve the restoration plan.

A day after the fall of Singapore on 15 February 1942, the 25th Army crossed the [Singapore Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Strait) and landed on [Batam Island](716783.md) without resistance. The Dutch [KNIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_East_Indies_Army) garrisons stationed on [Batam](716783.md) had already abandoned the island on 14 February 1942, after hearing reports of the impending total collapse of Singapore across the strait. Six days after the fall of Singapore, Japanese forces captured [Tanjungpinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjungpinang), marking the complete [fall of the Riau Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Riau_Islands) under Japanese control.[^cite_note-73]

### Independence struggle

Main article: [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution)



An article by H. M. Hassan entitled "The Singapore heir to the [Rhio Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago)" published in *[The Straits Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times)*.

In the aftermath of the war and the struggle against Dutch rule, several exile associations collectively known as the *Gerakan Kesultanan Riau* (Riau Sultanate Movement) emerged in Singapore, planning for a restoration. Some of the groups dated from as early as the dissolution of the sultanate, but started to gain momentum following the post-world war chaos. From the ashes of political uncertainty and fragility in the East Indies following the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies#End_of_the_occupation), a [royalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist) faction known as the Association of the Indigenous Riau Malays ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Persatoean Melayu Riouw Sedjati*) (PMRS) emerged to call for the restoration of the Riau-Lingga Sultanate. The council was financially backed by rich Riau Malay émigrés and Chinese merchants who hoped to obtain tin concession. Initially founded in High Street, Singapore, the association moved to Tanjung Pinang, Riau with the unprecedented approval by the Dutch administrators. Based in Tanjung Pinang, the group managed to gain the consent of the Dutch for self-governance in the region with the foundation of the Riouw Council ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Riouw Raad*, [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Dewan Riouw*). The Riouw Council was the devolved national unicameral legislature of Riau, a position equivalent to a Parliament.[^cite_note-74]

After establishing itself in Tanjung Pinang, the group formed a new organisation known as *Djawatan Koewasa Pengoeroes Rakjat Riow* (The Council of Riau People Administration), with the members hailing from Tudjuh Archipelago, [Great Karimun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Karimun), Lingga and [Singkep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singkep). The group strongly advocated the restoration of the Riau-Lingga Sultanate after the status of Indonesia became official. The leader of the council, Raja Abdullah, claimed that Riau Malays were neglected at the expense of the non-Riau Indonesians who dominated the upper ranks of the Riau civil administration. By restoring the monarch, they believed the position of Riau Malays would be guarded. The royalist association met with resistance from the republican group led by Dr. Iljas Datuk Batuah that sent delegates to Singapore to counter the propaganda of sultanate supporters. Based on Indonesian archival records, Dr. Iljas gained approvals from non-Malay newcomers to Riau, including [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minang_people), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang_people), and [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_people). He later formed a group known as the *Badan Kedaulatan Indonesia Riouw* (BKRI) (Indonesian Riau Sovereign Body) on 8 October 1945. The organisation sought to absorb the Riau Archipelago into the then-[newly independent Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution), as the archipelago was still retained by the Dutch. BKRI hoped that the new administration under [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) would give the [pribumis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pribumis) a fair chance to run the local government.

The royalist association would not give any public support to the Indonesian movement, as is evident in its refusal to display the *Bendera Merah-Putih* ([Indonesian Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Flag)) during the [Indonesian Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence) celebration on 17 August 1947 in Singapore. This led republicans to call the royalists 'pro-Dutch'. The royalists however, maintained that Riau was a Dutch territory and that only the Dutch could support it. The Dutch countered the claims of the BKRI by granting autonomous rule to the Riau Council, in which links with the Dutch would be maintained while a restored sultanate would play a secondary role. The council, created following the decree of the Governor General of the East Indies on 12 July 1947, was inaugurated on 4 August 1947, and represented a major step forward in the revival of the monarchy system. Several key members of the PMRS were elected to the Riau Council alongside their BKRI rivals, the [Chinese kapitans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitan_Cina) from Tanjung Pinang and Pulau Tujuh, local Malay leaders of Lingga and Dutch Officials in Tanjung Pinang. On 23 January 1948, the states of the Bangka Council, the Belitung Council, and the Riau Council merged to form the *Bangka Belitung and Riau Federation* which in the following year was admitted as part of the [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia) - with seats in the federal People's Representative Council and Senate.[^cite_note-75]

The call for revival of the sultanate continued throughout the period of autonomous rule under the Riau Council, although the influence of republicanism also continued to strengthen. The appeal of revival began to subside following the dissolution of the *Bangka Belitung and Riau Federation* on 4 April 1950. After the official withdrawal by the Dutch in 1950, the Riau Archipelago became *Keresidenan Riau* under [Central Sumatra Province](28679.md) under the United States of Indonesia. Being one of the last territories merged into Indonesia, Riau was known as the *daerah-daerah pulihan* (recovered regions), and the Riau area became a province in August 1957 - composed of both the islands and parts of the mainland, formerly part of the [Sultanate of Siak Sri Indrapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Siak_Sri_Indrapura).

The leader of Riau forces, Major Raja Muhammad Yunus, who led the bid to reestablish the sultanate apart from Indonesia fled into exile in Johor after his ill attempt.[^cite_note-76] The Geopolitical roots of the Riau Archipelago had molded her nationalist position to be sandwiched between the kindred monarchist Peninsular [Malay Nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Malay_nationalism) observed across the border in [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya) with the pro-republic and pan-ethnic [Indonesian Nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Awakening) manifested in her own Dutch East Indies domain.

### Contemporary era

Main article: [Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia_confrontation)

After the war, from 1950 the Riau archipelago was a duty-free zone until the [Indonesia-Malaysia confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konfrontasi) in 1963. During this period, the [Malaya and British Borneo dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaya_and_British_Borneo_dollar) used in [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya) was the principal currency in the region.[^cite_note-Nordholt,_p.447-448-77] To affirm its fiscal stake in the region, a decree was passed on 15 October 1963 to replace the Malaya and British Borneo dollar (the circulating currency) with an Indonesian-issued currency, the [Riau rupiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_rupiah), which replaced the dollar at par. Although the rupiah resembled the [Indonesian rupiah](367435.md) in appearance, having a much higher value. Malayan money was withdrawn from 1 November 1963. The Riau rupiah was exchangeable as a foreign currency with the Indonesian rupiah. Since 1 July 1964, the Riau rupiah is no longer valid, being replaced by the [Indonesian rupiah](367435.md) at a rate of 1 Riau rupiah = 14.7 Indonesian rupiah.[^cite_note-78]

Due to its proximity to Singapore and [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia), the Riau Islands area became the focus point during the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation. During this period, the territorial waters of the Riau Islands began to be visited by many Indonesian military forces who were members of the Indonesian Operations Command Corps or better known as the [Indonesian Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Marine_Corps). The city of [Batam](716783.md) became one of the staging point of Indonesian troops during the confrontation, with the construction of military bases around the city.[^cite_note-79] Anti-Malaysian guerrillas were gathered at Tanjung Balai Karimun to be trained and then sent to [Sambu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambu_Island) for briefing before operation.[^cite_note-80] These guerrillas aimed to sabotage and spread information to help Indonesia fight the British in Malaysia, disrupting the economy around the Riau Islands, especially Tanjung Balai Karimun.[^cite_note-81] At that time, [KOPASKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOPASKA) special forces of the [Indonesian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Navy) infiltrated Singapore from military bases in the Riau Islands, around Batam, Tanjung Balai Asahan and the surrounding area. They usually used small boats with outboard motors and disguise themselves as local residents who usually have relatives on the Malay Peninsula and Singapore. The coastline of Batam and the surrounding islands were heavily fortified to deter possible British and Malaysian invasion.[^cite_note-\:7-82]



In the 1970s, the city of Batam started an industrial boom, which continues to this day

The end of the Indonesia-Singapore trade during the confrontation had disrupted the economic conditions of the Riau Islands and Singapore. The inhabitants of Riau Islands could no longer barter or sell their produce to Singapore so they couldn't get basic necessities such as rice. Usually the basic needs of the Riau Islands population were brought in more from Singapore instead of the rest of Indonesia. Even some of the some needs of Singapore residents were usually imported from the Riau Islands has been disrupted.[^cite_note-\:7-82] Hostilities ceased in 1966 after the [30 September Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_September_Movement) attempted a coup d'état, resulting in the overthrow of the Sukarno regime which leads to a peace agreement between Indonesia and Malaysia that was signed on 28 May 1966 at [Bangkok](56615.md), [Thailand](30128.md).[^cite_note-83]

With the initial aim of making Batam an Indonesian version of Singapore, according to Presidential Decree number 41 of 1973, Batam Island was designated as an industrial working area. This was supported by the Batam Island Industrial Area Development Authority, better known as the Batam Authority Agency (BOB) which is now the Batam Development Board ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Badan Pengusahan Batam* or *BP Batam*). In the 1980s, based on Government Regulation No. 34 of 1983, the Batam District was upgraded to the Batam Municipality. This body runs the government and community administration and supports the development carried out by the Batam Authority (BP Batam).

Some level of unity returned in the Riau region for the first time after 150 years, with the creation of the [Sijori Growth Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sijori_Growth_Triangle) in 1989. But while bringing back some wealth to Riau, the Sijori Growth Triangle somewhat further broke the cultural unity within the islands. With [Batam](716783.md) island receiving most of the industrial investments and dramatically developing into a regional industrial centre, it attracted hundreds of thousands of non-Malay Indonesian migrants, changing the demographic balance in the archipelago.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] As the Malay, who were once the dominant ethnic group in the islands, have been reduced to about a third of the population, primarily as a result of immigration from elsewhere in Indonesia, they feel that their traditional rights are threatened. Similarly, the immigrants have felt politically and financially suppressed. Both of these causes have led to increased violence.[^cite_note-86] Piracy in the archipelago is also an issue.[^cite_note-87]

Dissatisfied with the provincial government's policy in [Pekanbaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekanbaru) and to avoid Riau mainland Minangkabau domination, the Riau islanders demanded to secede.[^cite_note-88] In 2002, after a short effort by the peoples of the archipelago, Riau Islands Province was created by an act of the [People's Consultative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Consultative_Assembly) with the capital of the former sultanate, Tanjung Pinang, as provincial capital.

## Politics

### Government

Main article: [Politics of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Indonesia)

The province of Riau Island is led by a governor (*gubernur*) who is elected directly by the people. The Governor also acts as a representative or extension of the central government in the province, whose authority is regulated in Law No. 32 of 2004 and Government Regulation number 19 of 2010. The legislative branch of the Riau Island government is the Riau Islands Regional People's Representatives Assembly ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Dewan Perwakilan Rakyat Daerah Kepulauan Riau – DPRD Kepri*), which is considered as a unicameral legislative body. Both governors and the members of the assembly are elected by citizens every five years by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage). Prior to this, the local executives had been elected by a vote of the assembly.[^cite_note-89] The governor office and the province assembly are located in Basuki Rahmat Road at Pulau Dompak, [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang).

The number of members of the assembly is at least thirty-five people and at most one hundred people with a term of office of five years and ends when the new assembly member takes an oath. Assembly membership was formalized by a decision of the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_\(Indonesia\)).[^cite_note-90] As of 2019, there are 45 seats in the assembly, scattered in several political parties. The majority (8 seats) are currently held by [Indonesian Democratic Party of Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Democratic_Party_of_Struggle) (PDIP) after the [2019 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indonesian_general_election).[^cite_note-91]

Prior to Dutch colonization, most of the modern-day province of Riau Islands is part of the [Riau-Lingga Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau-Lingga_Sultanate). The sultanate was headed by a [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan).The Sultan, who was a Malay, acted as [Head of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_State) while the *Dipertuan Muda/Yamtuan Muda* (deputy ruler or Viceroy), a position held by the [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis) ruling elite, served as the [Head of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_Government). The sultan's royal palace was located in [Penyengat Inderasakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penyengat_Island), while the *Yang di-Pertuan Muda* resided in [Daik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daik), Lingga. After becoming a Dutch protectorate, a Dutch resident was installed as an adviser to the sultan. For the hereditary position of the Sultan, the sultan was fully subjected under the influence of the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) authority. Albeit he is [de jure](43594.md) on the apex of the monarchy system, he is under the direct control of the Dutch Resident in [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang). All matters pertaining the administration of the sultanate including the appointment of the Sultan and the *Yang di-Pertuan Muda*, must be made within the knowledge and even the consent of the Dutch Resident.

After the Dutch fully annexed the sultanate, a Dutch resident was posted in Tanjung Pinang, heading the Residency of Riau and Dependencies. During the [3 years of Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies), Japanese military officers and local Indonesian nationalist leaders headed the region.

### Administrative divisions

Main article: [Subdivisions of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Indonesia)

This province is divided into five regencies (*kabupaten*) and two autonomous [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Indonesia) (*kota*). These are subdivided into 70 districts (*kecamatan*) and then into 256 villages (*desa* or *kelurahan*). Each regency is headed by a regent (*bupati*), while each district is headed by a district chief (*camat*) and each village headed by a village chief (known as a *kepala desa* or *[penghulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penghulu)*) for each village in the district.[^cite_note-indonesia-92]

During the Dutch East Indies era, the Riau Islands was included in the Residency of Riau and Dependencies ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): R*esidentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden*), which is also based in [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang). ## Environment

### Geography and climate



Tanjung Piayu, a scenic area in Batam

The total land area of the Riau Islands is 8,708.73 km<sup>2</sup> (3,362.46 sq mi) (3,362.46 sq miles), making it the 31st largest province in Indonesia, slightly smaller than the province of [Banten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten) on [Java](69336.md). 96% of the total area of the province is encompassed of ocean and only 4% of it is encompassed of land. The province is bordered by the South China Sea, the Strait of Malacca, and the [Karimata Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimata_Strait). It shares maritime borders with [Vietnam](202354.md) and [Cambodia](334751.md) to the north, the province of [Bangka Belitung Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Belitung_Islands) and [Jambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambi) in the south, [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) and the province of [West Kalimantan](453636.md) in the east and [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia), Singapore and mainland [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) in the west.

The Riau Islands consists of approximately 3,200 islands. The islands are grouped into several groups including the Riau Archipelago, the Lingga Islands, and the Tudjuh Archipelago. The Riau Archipelago is located in the northern part of the province and includes the island of [Batam](716783.md), which contains a majority of the province's population. Other islands in the Riau Archipelago include [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island), [Karimun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Karimun), [Bulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulan_Island) and [Kundur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundur_Island). The provincial capital of [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang) is located in the south of Bintan Island. The Riau Islands province also includes the [Lingga Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Islands) which lie south of the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago). To the northeast is the [Tudjuh Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudjuh_Archipelago), located between [Borneo](4518.md) and mainland [Malaysia](3607937.md). The archipelago consists of four distinct groups – the [Anambas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anambas_Islands), [Natuna Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Islands), [Tambelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambelan_Islands) islands and [Badas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badas_Islands) — which were attached to the new province, though not geographically part of the Riau Archipelago. The 2020 census count was 2,064,564, making it one of the fastest growing province in Indonesia; the official estimate as at mid 2025 was 2,233,973.[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1]

&#x20;The highest point in Riau Islands is Mount Daik with a height of 1,165 meters (3,822 ft), which is located on Lingga Island.[^cite_note-97] Most of the area on [Lingga Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Island) is hilly. The Lingga area is generally in the form of an area with a fairly high slope, where there are as many as 76.92 percent of the area which has a slope of more than 15%, while the plain area (slope less than 2%) only encompasses of 3.49 ha or 3.14 percent of the total area.



Tropical vegetation on Mount Ranai in eastern Great Natuna Island

As an archipelago, the climatic conditions in the province are affected by wind. Most of the province has wet tropical climate, there is rainy season and dry season interspersed with transition season with the lowest average temperature 20.4 °C. In November to February monsoon winds comes from the north and between June and December the monsoon winds comes from the south. During the northern monsoon the wind velocity at sea could range from 20 to 30 knots, on land the wind can range from 3–15 knots. This causes potentially extreme weather in the Riau Islands with rainfall of about 150–200 millimeters and wave height between 1.2 meters up to 3 meters. As in most other province of Indonesia, the Riau Islands has a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Af*) bordering on a tropical monsoon climate. The climate is very much dictated by the surrounding sea and the prevailing wind system. It has high average temperature and high average rainfall. According to Köppen and Geiger, this climate is classified as Af. In the Riau Islands, the average annual temperature is 25.2 °C. Precipitation here averages 2607 mm.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]



View of the [Anambas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anambas_Islands_Regency)

Oceanographic conditions, mainly wave and sea water current, in the study sites were influenced by wet monsoon period, i.e. from November to April. During the wet monsoon (wet season) the wind is characterized as very strong, blowing from North-west to South-east or from South-west to North-east, with velocity ranges 7–20 knot (≈ 13–37 km/hour). The strongest wind usually occurs in around December–February, with velocity is about 45 km/hour.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-\:8-101] During this season the sea wave may very high, sometime > 2 m especially during December–February. While the east monsoon (dry season), it usually occur in period of May to October, with wind velocity is relatively calm, that is about 7- 15 knot (≈ 13–28 km/hour). The sea wave was not very high reaching only around 0.8 m.[^cite_note-\:8-101][^cite_note-102] The sea water current in Riau islands follows the current pattern in [Malacca strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_strait). It is depends on the different between seawater level in northern part of [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea) and southern part of Malacca Strait. Since seawater level in Pacific Ocean is always higher than in Indian Oceans, the seawater level in [South China Sea](74209.md) is also higher than in Andaman Sea throughout the year resulted in Malacca Strait there is a current, which flow to Northwest direction throughout the year. In November to April, during the Northeast monsoon, Andaman Sea's current goes to the north. Therefore, majority of water masses are transported to the north. Consequent of this, during these months, Andaman Sea's water masses will be empty, and sea water level will be low or the different from South China Sea's sea water level will be higher. This sea water level reaches to maximum in February to March, while the different of sea water level between Northern and southern parts of Strait is about 50 cm, which resulted current velocity is about 12 cm/sec to Northwest.[^cite_note-\:8-101]

Conversely, during the southwest monsoon, May to October, most of the currents in western part of [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea) go to east and south, therefore water masses will be gathering in Andaman Sea. This resulted in that sea water level increases in northern part of [Malacca Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), and the different of sea water level to [South China Sea](74209.md)’s water level will be small, although in southern part of the Strait the sea water level is still higher. During this season, the water current in Malacca Strait flows to the north, with speed is little bit lower. Current velocity reaches to maximum in around July to August, with speed about 5 cm/sec. Oceanographic pattern is likely to affect the water quality and reef corals in the study sites. Strong wind during the wet monsoon period, for example, could cause water turbulence which affecting on high sediment, especially in coastal areas, and this might be worse with the high sediment run off from surrounding rivers and/or reclamation areas, such as happened in [Batam](716783.md). Water turbidity was recorded about 22 NTU during the wet season, and it is lower (about 7.4 NTU) in the dry season.[^cite_note-\:8-101][^cite_note-103]

### Biodiversity

Map of Riau Islands showing tree-cover loss years, with forest remaining since 2000 in green and loss years shaded yellow through purple.

Tree-cover loss year in Riau Islands, 2001-2024, from the [Global Forest Change dataset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Forest_Change_dataset).



Coral Reef at Anambas

Riau Islands became one of the coral reef conservation sites in Indonesia. Examples of coral reef conservation sites in the Riau Islands are Karang Alangkalam and Karang Bali. The area's marine life has made the [Natuna Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Sea) into a scuba diving destination. While there were once threats towards the coral reefs in the region, such as [blast fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_fishing), a survey by the [Indonesian Institute of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Institute_of_Sciences) found that most of the coral reef ecosystems and mangrove forests in a number of locations in the waters around Batam are in a good condition.[^cite_note-104] Nevertheless, there are some threats that might endanger the ecosystem in the province, such as [plastic waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_waste) which is mostly produced by households that are thrown into the sea.[^cite_note-105] [Illegal fishing](6346579.md) is also one of the factor that damages the ecosystem in the region.[^cite_note-106] The sea around the [Natuna Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Regency) in the previous years has been home to illegal fishing due to weak law enforcement in the region. Under the [Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) administration, the [Natuna Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Sea) has been frequently patrolled by the [Indonesian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Navy) and the [Indonesian Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Maritime_Security_Agency) to prevent illegal fishing. This has resulted in the arrest of hundreds of fishing boats and their crews from countries such as [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Vietnam](202354.md) and China.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

As most of the Riau Islands consists of islands, many beaches can be found in the province, such as Lagoi Beach, Impian Beach, Trikora Beach, Nongsa Beach, Sakerah Beach, Loola Beach, Padang Melang Beach, Nusantara Beach, Batu Leafy Beach, Indah Beach, Tanjung Siambang Beach, Tanjung Beach, Melur Beach, Melayu Beach, Pelawan Beach, Sisi Beach and Cemaga Beach.

The wealth of flora and fauna in the Riau Islands is also very diverse. The *[Lutjanus gibbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humpback_red_snapper)* and *[Piper betle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betel)* are the fauna and flora symbol of the Riau Islands province respectively.[^cite_note-Piper_betle-110] The [humphead wrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphead_wrasse) (*Cheilinus undulatus*), which is an [endangered species](20110668.md) of fish, inhabits the [Natuna Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Sea) in the northern part of the province. In addition, [Dugongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugong) also live in the waters around the island of [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island). Some other rare animals are the [Tarsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarsius), and the [Natuna Island surili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Island_surili) (*Presbytis natunae*), native to the island of [Natuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Regency). The Natuna Island surili is one of the 25 most endangered primates on Earth.[^cite_note-111] Wealth of flora in the province includes the *[Oncosperma tigillarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncosperma_tigillarium)*, [dragon fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_fruit), [areca nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areca_nut) and the rare udumbara plants that live on the Engku Puteri Plateau, Batam Center. The Riau Islands has two nature reserves, namely Pulau Burung Nature Reserve and Pulau Laut Nature Reserve.

The geographical position of the Riau Islands, which is close to neighboring countries located near the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), which is one of the busiest shipping lane in the world, is also a major factor in the need to increase competitiveness. The geographical location can be a leverage in attracting foreign and domestic investors. However, the strategic geographical location will not provide benefits if the Riau Islands do not have competitiveness. This is because skilled workers and investors are more interested in areas that have competitiveness than regions that are not competitive. Therefore, increasing competitiveness is a necessity to attract investors, both foreign and domestic, to invest in the economic development of the Riau Islands.[^cite_note-\:4-113]

The economic growth rate of Riau Islands Province in 2005 was 6.57%.[^cite_note-\:6-114] Sectors that grew well (faster than the total [GRDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) growth) in 2005 included the transportation and communication sector (8.51%), the manufacturing sector (7.41%), the financial sector, leasing, and corporate services (6.89%), the service sector (6.77%), and the trade, hotel and restaurant sector (6.69%). GRDP Per capita of the Riau Islands in the last five years (2001–2005) tends to increase. In 2001 the GRDP per capita (above the prevailing price – without oil and gas) was Rp. 22,808 million, and in 2005 it increased to Rp.29,348 million. But in real terms (without calculating inflation) the Per capita GRDP (without gas) in 2001 was only Rp.20,397 million, and in 2005 it increased to Rp. 22,418 million.[^cite_note-\:6-114]

&#x20;The Riau Islands have been identified as a potential center of economic growth in Indonesia. In some areas ([Batam](716783.md), [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Regency) and [Karimun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimun_Regency)), a pilot project for the development of a [special economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone) is being pursued in cooperation with the [Singaporean government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Singapore).[^cite_note-115] The implementation of the SEZ in Batam-Bintan-Karimun involves close cooperation between the central government, the local government, and the private sector.



The potential of natural wealth in the Riau Islands comes from mining and the [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_manufacturing) industries. The manufacturing sector contributes more than 35% and the processing industry accounts for 15.26% of the economy in Riau Islands with oil and gas commodities. In addition to the mining sector, community economic activities are dominant in the agriculture, plantation and forestry sectors. Riau Islands economic growth continued to decline in the period 2011–2014, then increased in 2014. During the period 2011–2014, the economic performance of the Riau Islands had an average growth rate of 6.31%.[^cite_note-\:4-113]



In 2017, the manufacturing sector formed the largest GDP per share in the province, forming around 36.8% of the total GDP per share of the province.[^cite_note-GDP_by_province_2017-116] Based on the main commodities, [mineral fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel), machinery and electrical equipment remain the mainstay trade of the Riau Islands. This type of commodity has not changed significantly compared to previous years. This needs to be considered given the expansion of the market must also be accompanied by an expansion of commodities both in quality and type.[^cite_note-\:6-114] Export-oriented electronics manufacturing in Batam, for example, made up half of Indonesian electronics exports in 1991 with foreign companies alone employing 45,000 workers in 2020.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

The second largest sector is the Construction industry, forming around 18% of the total GDP sector.[^cite_note-GDP_by_province_2017-116] The development of the number of construction businesses in the Riau Islands fluctuated considerably during the period 2008–2010. In 2010 the number of construction companies in the province was 1,570, with the value of construction completed (revenue) of Rp 5,483 billion. Number of National Labor Force Survey Results show that the number of residents working in the construction sector has fluctuated from time to time. As many as 43,232 people were recorded in February 2009, then decreased in February 2010 to 29,932 people, again increased in August 2010 to 50,833 people, then decreased in February 2011 to 58,211 and again increased in August 2011 to 59,755 people. Because the Riau Islands is a new developing province and almost half of the existing human resources come from outside the Riau Islands, this has caused housing needs in the Riau Islands to be out of proportion to their availability. This condition also seems to have caused housing costs to rise in the Riau Islands relatively faster than in other regions. The results of the National Socio-Economic Survey show that on average the expenditure per capita of the population for rental costs/housing contracts is the highest type of expenditure for non-food commodities, and the figure tends to increase every year. This fact is in line with the increasing frequency of commodity rental prices in the Riau Islands.[^cite_note-\:6-114]



Tin mining in [Singkep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singkep), Lingga Islands

The third largest sector is the mining industry.[^cite_note-GDP_by_province_2017-116] Riau Islands is the largest [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) producing region in Indonesia. This can be seen from the number of [bauxite mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite_mining) companies. Since 2008, the number of bauxite mining companies has always been the highest compared to other mining companies, although the number has always dropped from 2008 to 2010. In 2009 the number of bauxite companies fell by 37 companies to 32 in 2010. This decline also had an impact on the decline in the number of workers and output produced by this sector. The [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) company is the second largest company in the Riau Islands after bauxite. Tin companies also have the same situation as bauxite companies that are experiencing a decline. The tin company which initially had 25 companies in 2009 decreased to 22 companies in 2010.[^cite_note-\:6-114]

A small but significant sector in the province is the agriculture sector, forming around 3.5% of the total GDP sector.[^cite_note-GDP_by_province_2017-116] Despite having limited agricultural land, and production of food crops (mainly rice), the Riau Islands have not been able to meet the needs food for its population. This problem is caused by rice productivity that is still low. [Cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) plants have the highest productivity, then [sweet potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato) and rice plants are ranked third. In 2011, rice productivity in the Riau Islands reached 3.16 tons per hectare, far below the productivity of cassava and sweet potatoes, which amounted to 10.83 tons per hectare and 7.75 tons per hectare, respectively. When viewed from the harvested area and rice production in 2011, it experienced a very small increase as well as productivity. Rice productivity in 2010 was 3.15 tons per hectare, cassava was 10.82 tons per hectare and sweet potatoes was 7.72 tons per hectare. The increase in productivity of rice and cassava was only 0.01 tons per hectare and sweet potatoes was 0.03 tons per hectare. [Corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and peanut crops are food crops in Riau Islands with low productivity during 2010–2011. Whereas for corn productivity in 2010 amounted to 2.13 tons per hectare decreased to 2.12 tons per hectare in 2011. While for peanuts the productivity remained, at 0.92 tons per hectare. Decrease in corn productivity is due to the harvested area for maize also has decreased, besides it is also the effect of pests that cause disruption of corn pests.[^cite_note-\:6-114] Plantation and horticultural crops are types of plants that are quite potential to be cultivated in Riau Islands. This is based on the high price of agricultural products produced by these two types of businesses. As shown by the NTP (Farmer Exchange Rate) data, only estate and horticultural farmers have almost certainly an index value above 100. This condition is of course caused by the still higher price of goods produced by farmers compared to the price of goods needed, both for consumption and the cost of production. Production of [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rubber) plants in 2011 experienced a very high increase of 118 percent. While [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) plants experienced a very small increase of 2.4 percent. As for fruit production, in 2011, only [jackfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackfruit) and banana increased, where [jackfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackfruit) production increased by 5 percent and banana production increased by 50 percent, compared to production in 2010. As for the production of [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [durian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durian), orange and [rambutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambutan) decreased in 2011.[^cite_note-\:6-114]

The island of [Natuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Regency) located in the north-eastern part of the province is home to the largest [natural gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Natuna_gas_field) in Indonesia (estimated to 1.3 billion m<sup>3</sup>). The field was discovered in 1973 by [Agip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agip).[^cite_note-apec-119][^cite_note-dunn-120] In 1980, the Indonesian state-owned oil company [Pertamina](4042974.md) and [Exxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exxon) formed a joint venture to develop Natuna D-Alpha. However, due to the high CO<sub>2</sub> content the partnership was not able to start production.[^cite_note-offshore-natuna-121] In 1995, the Indonesian government signed a contract with Exxon but in 2007, the contract was terminated.[^cite_note-offshore-natuna-121][^cite_note-hiscock-122] In 2008, the block was awarded to Pertamina.[^cite_note-apec-119] The new agreement was signed between Pertamina and [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil) in 2010. Correspondingly, the field was renamed East Natuna to be geographically more precise.[^cite_note-antara061210-123] In 2011, the principal of agreement was signed between Pertamina, ExxonMobil, [TotalEnergies](804161.md) and [Petronas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronas).[^cite_note-jp130813-124][^cite_note-jp271115-125] In 2012, Petronas was replaced by [PTT Exploration and Production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PTT_Exploration_and_Production).[^cite_note-jp130813-124][^cite_note-2b1131112-126] As of 2016, negotiations about the new principal of agreement have not finalized and consequently, a production sharing contract is not signed.[^cite_note-jp070116-127] The area around the gas field is currently [disputed between Indonesia and China](32005536.md).[^cite_note-wsj171115-128]

## Infrastructure

From 2016 until 2020, the central government has outlined more development plan for the province. In 2017, the Indonesian Ministry of Public Works and People's Housing has allocated around Rp. 500 million (US\$36 million) for the development of infrastructure in the Riau Islands, which includes building new infrastructure as well as improving existing infrastructure in the province.[^cite_note-129] Meanwhile, in 2019, the Riau islands provincial government has also allocated a budget of around Rp. 15,5 trillion (US\$1 billion) that is allocated from the province's Regional Revenue and Expenditures Budget.[^cite_note-\:9-130] This is an increase from 2018's annual budget, which is around Rp. 13,9 trillion (US\$980 million).[^cite_note-\:9-130] The government continues to work towards accelerating maritime development (both economy and tourism) in Indonesia's foremost and outermost regions, one of which is in the Riau Islands province to support economic development, tourism, and marine resources in the Riau Islands.[^cite_note-131]

### Energy and water resources



PLTU Batam

Electricity distribution in the state are operated and managed by the [Perusahaan Listrik Negara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perusahaan_Listrik_Negara) (PLN). According to the Indonesian [Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy_and_Mineral_Resources_\(Indonesia\)), as of 2017, there are 240 power plants in the Riau Islands, consisting of 2 [steam power plant](2549543.md), 1 [Solar power](13690575.md) plant and 237 diesel power plant.[^cite_note-132] Currently, the largest power plant in the Riau Islands is the Tanjung Kasam power plant in [Batam](716783.md), which has a capacity of 430 [MW.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MegaWatt) It started operating in 2012.[^cite_note-133] The power plant supplies around 25% of the total electric needs in Batam and the surrounding region.[^cite_note-134] As of 2017, there are still around 104 villages in the province that is not fully electrified yet.[^cite_note-135] In response, PLN has allocated around Rp. 751 billion (US\$5.3 million) to build new power plants in the province with a total capacity of 45.500 KW. The location of the power plants will be scattered at several points such as in [Natuna Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Regency).[^cite_note-136] It also aimed that by 2020, all villages in the Riau Islands will be fully electrified.[^cite_note-137]

All pipes [water supply](684928.md) in the province is managed by the Riau Islands Municipal Waterworks ([Indonesian:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) *Perusahaan Daerah Air Minum Kepulauan Riau* – *PDAM Kepri*). Reliable [water supply](684928.md) is one of the main problem faced by the people of the Riau Islands. The province has often faced a shortage in clean water, mostly due to drought during the dry seasons.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] In response, the Indonesian government is currently constructing several dams across the province to anticipate water shortage in the future. Examples of dams that are being constructed are the Sei Gong Dam in [Galang Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galang_Island), [Batam](716783.md), which as a capacity of 11,8 million m<sup>3</sup> of water. Construction of the dam has finished in 2018 and is predicted to begin operation in 2019.[^cite_note-141] Another dam that is also under construction is the Sei Busung Dam in Bintan. It is slated to begin operation 2021 and would have a water capacity of 100 million m<sup>3</sup>.[^cite_note-142]

### Internet and telecommunication

In 2018, the household internet broadband penetration in the Riau Islands is 70%, which is above the Indonesian national figure around 54.86%, according to the survey results from the Indonesian Internet Service Providers Association ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Asosiasi Penyelenggara Jasa Internet Indonesia*) (APJII).[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] Nonetheless, there are still many regions in the Riau Islands that has limited or no internet access, especially around the outlying regions of Anambas and Natuna Islands.[^cite_note-145] Efforts have been taken by the government to improve internet access in the region, such as the construction of the Palapa ring, which is a massive, nationwide internet network using fiber-optic wires that will provide high-speed internet connection throughout the nation. The project aimed to make all the capital cities of regencies and municipalities in Indonesia connected with broadband or high-speed internet infrastructure. The idea was preceded by the difficulties of the outermost, rural, and disadvantaged regions in Indonesia in obtaining adequate telecommunications access.[^cite_note-146] The project work process began in 2017. Currently Palapa Ring construction has been completed and has also been inaugurated. A number of Indonesian telecommunications operators have begun cooperating with the [Indonesian Ministry of Communication and Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Communication_and_Information_Technology_\(Indonesia\)) so that the internet network can be accessed smoothly. The Palapa Ring network was completed in late 2019 and was inaugurated by the Indonesian president [Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) on 14 October 2019.[^cite_note-147]

Presently, the [mobile telecommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_telecommunication) penetration in the outlying regions of the Riau Islands such as Natuna and Anambas are increasing due to the improvement of telecommunication infrastructure compared to the previous years. Several Indonesian network providers such as [XL Axiata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XL_Axiata) and [Telkom Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telkom_Indonesia) has started building more [BTS tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTS_tower) around the region to increase network coverage around the region. XL Axiata has been present in Anambas and Natuna since 2008 with 2G services that use satellite connections and then upgraded to 3G services. In March 2019, XL Axiata inaugurated the 4G data network in the Anambas Islands, by using the West Palapa Ring backbone, in collaboration with the Telecommunications and Information Accessibility Agency (BAKTI) in using the West Palapa Ring network.[^cite_note-148] While in 2018, Telkomsel strengthens 4G services in Natuna Regency and Anambas Islands Regency by building two new 4G base transceiver stations. With the deployment of 4G network infrastructure in the country's border areas, Telkomsel now operates 89 BTS tower in Natuna and Anambas, including eight 4G BTS tower.[^cite_note-149]

### Transportation

#### Land



The cable-stayed Tengku Fisabilillah Bridge connecting Batam and Tonton, a part of the [Barelang Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelang_Bridge)

As most of the Riau Islands consists of oceans and outlying islands, land transportation is only limited to the larger islands in the province, such as [Batam](716783.md) and [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island). As of 2014, there are 4,954 km (3078 mi) of connected roadways in the Riau Islands, with 334 km (208 mi) being national highways, 512 km (318 mi) being provincial highways and the rest being city/regency highways.[^cite_note-150] Most of the main roads in the Riau Islands has been paved, but there are still road sections that remain unpaved, especially in the outlying regions. Despite this, efforts has been taken by the government to improve road infrastructure in the regions, such as building more roads and improve existing roads, as well as building new bridges to connect river and islands in the province.[^cite_note-humas.kepriprov.go.id-151]

As in other provinces in Indonesia, the Riau Islands uses a [dual carriageway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway) with the [left-hand traffic rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-_and_right-hand_traffic), and major cities in the province such as Batam and Tanjung Pinang provide public transportation services such as buses and taxis along with [Gojek](51272668.md) and [Grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grab_\(company\)) services. The Tengku Fisabilillah Bridge which connects Batam and Tonton Island is currently the longest bridge in the Riau Islands. It stretches for 642 meters and is a [cable-stayed bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable-stayed_bridge) with two 118 m high pylons and main span 350 m.[^cite_note-152] The bridge is part of the [Barelang Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelang_Bridge), a chain of 6 Bridges of various types that connect the islands of [**Ba**tam](716783.md), [**Re**mpang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rempang), and [Ga**lang**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galang_Island), Riau Islands that was constructed in 1992 and finished in 1997 and was inaugurated by former Indonesian president [B. J. Habibie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._J._Habibie), who oversaw the project in construction, aiming to transform the Rempang and Galang islands into [industrial sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_park) (resembling present-day Batam).[^cite_note-Enjoy_Batam-153][^cite_note-154] Travelling from the first bridge to the last is about 50 km and takes about 50 minutes. Over time the bridge sites have grown more into a [tourist attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_attraction) rather than a [transportation route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_hub).[^cite_note-155] The Tengku Fisabilillah Bridge will be surpassed as the longest bridge in the province by the [Batam-Bintan Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batam-Bintan_Bridge), which is still under the planning stage. Construction is expected to start in 2020 and finish in 3 to 4 years time. The Batam-Bintan bridge will have a length of around 7 km (4 mi).[^cite_note-humas.kepriprov.go.id-151][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

Presently, there is no toll road in the Riau Islands. However, the government has announced a plan to build a new toll road in Batam which would connect [Hang Nadim International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim_International_Airport) in the eastern part of the island and the Batu Ampar area in the northern part. The toll road will have a length of 19 km (12 mi) and will be constructed with various construction methods. About 5.2 kilometers of the toll road will elevated while the rest will be at grade.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

#### Air



[Hang Nadim International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim_International_Airport) in Batam, the busiest airport in the province

The province is served by several airports, the largest being [Hang Nadim International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim_International_Airport) in Batam. Originally developed to handle diversions of aircraft from [Singapore Changi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Changi_Airport) in case of an emergency, it has sufficient facilities for wide-body aircraft such as [Boeing 747s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747). The airport has the longest runway in Indonesia and the second longest runway in Southeast Asia, after [Kuala Lumpur International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur_International_Airport), at 4,214 metres (13,825 ft) long. The airport serve flights to major Indonesian cities such as [Jakarta](16275.md), [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan), [Surabaya](212635.md) and [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang), as well as some international destinations such as [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) in Malaysia and cities in China such as [Shenzhen](83368.md) and [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an).

The second-largest airport in the province is the [Raja Haji Fisabilillah Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Haji_Fisabilillah_Airport) in [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island). It mostly serves flight to Jakarta and destinations within the province such as Batam and Natuna. Other airports in the province are the [Dabo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabo_Airport) in [Singkep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singkep), [Raja Haji Abdullah Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Haji_Abdullah_Airport) in [Karimun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimun_Regency), [Letung Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letung_Airport) and [Matak Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matak_Airport) in the [Anambas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anambas_Islands_Regency) and [Raden Sadjad Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raden_Sadjad_Airport) in the [Natuna Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Islands). Raden Sadjad Airport is currently being developed as a military airbase due to territory dispute between Indonesia and China on the waters off the coast of the Natuna Islands. Additional facilities, such as aircraft hangars, [ILS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_Landing_System), refueling hangar are also currently being added.[^cite_note-160]

As the closest neighbour of Singapore and to realise that [Soekarno–Hatta International Airport](1203751.md) is fully used, [Lion Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Air) is developing hangars in [Batam Island](716783.md) and [Garuda Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Indonesia) is developing a new airport, with runway and maintenance facilities so as to make a new air hub in [Bintan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island).[^cite_note-161]

#### Sea



Tanjung Pinang Ferry Harbor

Due to the fact that the Riau Islands is an archipelago province located in an archipelago country, water transport becomes the main type of transportation. Inter-island connections are often contacted by water transport. This type of water transport includes ferries, canoes, speedboats, freight boats, etc. This ships consists of a ship operated by the state-owned [Pelni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelni) company as well as private vessels. The main ports are located in Batam and Bintan. There largest cargo port is the Port of Batu Ampar in Batam. It serves cargo ships to and from Batam and has a capacity of 1000 container.[^cite_note-162]

Major cities such as Batam and Tanjung Pinang serves international ferry routes to [Singapore](27318.md) and [Malaysia](3607937.md). Ferries connect Batam to Singapore, Bintan, and [Johor Bahru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Bahru) (Malaysia). Five ferry terminals are on Batam: [Batam Harbour Bay Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batam_Harbour_Bay_Ferry_Terminal), Nongsapura Ferry Terminal, Sekupang, Waterfront City, and [Batam Center Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batam_Center_Ferry_Terminal). Connections to Singapore are by way of Harbourfront and Tanah Merah Ferry Terminals run by [Singapore Cruise Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Cruise_Centre) (SCC). While in Tanjung Pinang, the Sri Bintan Pura Harbour is the largest harbour in Bintan. This port connects the city of Tanjung Pinang with ports to the north (Lobam port and Bulang Linggi port), with islands to the west, such as [Tanjung Balai Karimun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Balai_Karimun), Batam, and islands to the south such as [Lingga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Regency) and [Singkep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singkep) islands. For international ferry services, the port of Sri Bintan Pura also has transportation links to Singapore ([HarbourFront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HarbourFront_\(Singapore\)) and [Tanah Merah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanah_Merah_Ferry_Terminal)) and Malaysia ([Stulang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stulang_Laut_Ferry_Terminal)).

### Healthcare

Health-related matters in the Riau Islands is administered by the Riau Islands Health Agency ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Dinas Kesehatan Provinsi Kepulauan Riau*).[^cite_note-163] According to the Riau Islands branch of the Indonesian [Central Agency on Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Indonesia), as of 2016, there are around 28 hospitals in the Riau Islands which consists of 13 state-owned hospitals and 15 private hospitals.[^cite_note-164] According to the Riau Islands provincial government, there are 87 clinics in Riau Islands as of 2018, of which 70 of them are already accredited, while the remaining 17 clinics are not yet accredited.[^cite_note-165] The number of hospitals and clinics in the Riau Islands are still far below the ideal level, according to the [Ombudsman](26289198.md) of the Republic of Indonesia.[^cite_note-166]

The most prominent hospital is the Riau Islands Regional General Hospital ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Rumah Sakit Umum Daerah Kepulauan Riau*) in Tanjung Pinang, which is the largest state-owned hospital in the province.[^cite_note-167] Other prominent hospital in the province is the Santa Elisabeth Hospital in Batam and the Embung Fatimah Regional General Hospital which is also located in Batam. Due to lack of adequate health facilities in the province, many people usually travel to neighboring countries such as Malaysia and Singapore to get medical treatment as these countries has better medical infrastructure compared to the Riau Islands.

### Education

Main article: [List of universities in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Indonesia)

Education in the Riau Islands, as well as Indonesia in a whole, falls under the responsibility of the [Ministry of Education and Culture](44273441.md) (*Kementerian Pendidikan dan Kebudayaan* or *Kemdikbud*) and the [Ministry of Religious Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Religious_Affairs_\(Indonesia\)) (*Kementerian Agama* or *Kemenag*). In Indonesia, all citizens must undertake twelve years of [compulsory education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education) which consists of six years at elementary level and three each at middle and high school levels. [Islamic schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah) are under the responsibility of the Ministry of Religious Affairs. The [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Indonesia) also notes that there are two types of education in [Indonesia](14579.md): formal and non-formal. Formal education is further divided into three levels: primary, secondary and tertiary education. Indonesians are required to attend 12 years of school, which consists of three years of primary school, three years of secondary school and three years of high school.[^cite_note-168]

As of 2019, there are 1,621 schools in the Riau Islands, which consists of 1,051 government-owned schools and 570 private schools.[^cite_note-169] There are several international schools in the Riau Islands, which are mostly located in Batam and the surrounding area, due to its significant expatriate populations, such as Sekolah Global Indo-Asia,[^cite_note-170] The [Independent School Batam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_School_Batam),[^cite_note-171] Australian Intercultural School,[^cite_note-172] Sekolah Djuwita Batam,[^cite_note-173] etc. Most of the teachers that worked in these international schools are English-educated teachers from Singapore, [Malaysia](3607937.md), United Kingdom, Australia, United States and [Philippines](23440.md).

There are around 40 universities in the Riau islands, both public and private universities. Public universities in the Riau Islands falls under the responsibility of the [Ministry of Research and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Research_and_Technology/National_Research_and_Innovation_Agency_\(Indonesia\)) (*Kementerian Riset dan Teknolog*) or the [Ministry of Religious Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Religious_Affairs_\(Indonesia\)) for Islamic universities. Notable public universities in the province is the Raja Ali Haji Maritime University and the Sultan Abdurrahman Islamic College in Tanjung Pinang, and the Batam State Polytechnic in Batam.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] There are at least one international university in the Riau Islands, which is the Batam International University located in Batam.

## Demographics

According to the 2020 national census results (released in 2021 by the Indonesian [Central Agency on Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Indonesia)), the Riau Islands province had a population of 2,064,564 people, spread throughout five regencies and two cities, and consisting of 1,053,296 males and 1,011,268 females.[^cite_note-\:0-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178][^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-93] This makes the Riau Islands the 26th most populated province in Indonesia.[^cite_note-179] The population in the province was predicted to increase to 2,768,000 by 2030,[^cite_note-180] although this figure has since been revised downwards. The official estimate was 2,233,973 in mid 2025,[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] giving a population density in the Riau Islands Orovince of 256.52 people per km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-181] The city of [Batam](716783.md) is the most populated administrative division in the province, with 1,296,960 people in mid 2025, while the [Anambas Islands Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anambas_Islands_Regency) is the least populated in the province, with just 50,360 people.[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] Most of the population of Riau Island is concentrated on the islands nearest to Singapore, such as [Batam](716783.md) and [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island).

Riau Islands is a very diverse and multi-ethnic province. Owing to its proximity to Singapore, many people from other parts of Indonesia have migrated to the province. As of 2010, the ethnic groups in Riau Island consist of [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesian) (30.23%), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) (24.55%), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) (12.48%), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people) (9.72%), and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians) (7.70%).

The [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesian) are the largest ethnic group with a composition of 33.71% of the entire population of the Riau Islands. They are the native people of the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago). From the ancient times, the Riau Archipelago is considered the hub of Malay cultures. In the past, several Malay kingdoms and sultanates existed in the archipelago, the most notably being the [Riau-Lingga Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau-Lingga_Sultanate). A major sub-group of the Malay inhabiting the province is the [Orang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Laut) (lit. 'sea people'). The Orang Laut are Malays who are [Nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) in nature and have a rich marine culture. Presently, many Orang Laut have settled in coastal areas of the province. The Orang Laut are encompasses of numerous tribes and groups inhabiting the islands and estuaries in the Riau-Lingga Archipelagos, the [Tudjuh Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudjuh_Archipelago), the [Batam](716783.md) Archipelago, and the coasts and offshore islands of eastern [Sumatra](28679.md), southern [Malaysia Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and Singapore.[^cite_note-182] The geographical condition of the Riau Islands, which has almost 99% of its area as water, has made quite a number of Orang Laut groups living in this area. In the Riau Islands, the Orang Laut are usually found in islands and river mouths.[^cite_note-183] The Orang Laut are boat-dwelling tribe with main livelihoods as fish finders and other marine animals, such as *tripang* ([sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber)). This subsystem economic model is the characteristic of their culture. Since the implementation of the policy of relocation of settlements by the [Indonesian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Indonesia) in the late 1980s to the early 1990s, the customs of the Orang Laut are gradually disappearing.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] Then there is also a minority of [Orang Darat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Darat?action=edit\&redlink=1) (lit. 'land people') on the island of [Rempang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rempang) whose population is threatened with extinction. Previously, they were spread from the islands of [Batam](716783.md) to [Galang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galang_Island). Unlike the Orang Laut, who lived and depended on marine resources, the Orang Darat lived inland and relied on natural resources. Their residences are huts with coconut palm frond roofs, without walls and only on the ground. The Orang Darat lived nomadically and survived by hunting and bartering natural resources with [Chinese](372983.md) traders.[^cite_note-186]



Chinese junks *Sin Tong Heng* and *Tek Hwa Seng* in the [Sambu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambu_Island), [Singapore Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Strait), c. 1936. The Chinese have inhabited the Riau Islands since the late 18th century.

Other ethnic groups are mostly immigrants coming from different parts of Indonesia. Due to their proximity from [Singapore](27318.md), Riau Islands have become a melting pot for different ethnic groups coming from other parts of Indonesia. The [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) is the dominant ethnic group of the immigrants, forming 23.19% of the total population. The Javanese who stayed here are mostly due to the [Transmigration program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmigration_program) enacted by the Dutch during the colonial period to reduce the overpopulated Java and continued until the end of the [New Order](2864794.md). Another notable immigrants are the [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people) (Minang), who mostly originated from [West Sumatra](453590.md). Owing to its *rantau* (migration) culture, many Minangkabau people has migrated away from their homeland. The history of the Minangkabau migration in West Sumatra to the Riau mainland and the Riau Islands has been recorded to have lasted a very long time. When the means of transportation were still using the river, many Minang people migrated to various cities such as [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang) and [Batam](716783.md). Most of Minangkabau people work as traders, peddlers, and craftsmen, in addition to doctors, lawyers, and journalists.[^cite_note-187]

The [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese-Indonesian) are the fifth largest ethnic group in the province, forming 7.69% of the total population. Chinese traders have frequented the region since at least the 13th century, during the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty).[^cite_note-188] The mention of *[Longyamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Ya_Men)* ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 龙牙门; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 龍牙門; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): lóngyámé&#x6E;*)*, which means "Dragon's Tooth Strait" in Chinese, first appeared in the *Yüan shih* in 1320. In 1325, *Longyamen* sent a mission to China with a memorial and tribute.[^cite_note-189] The nearby island of [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island) had sent an earlier mission in 1323. Thus *Longyamen* was already known to the Yuan court before 1320. Another place named *Longyamen* appears in 1225 in [Zhao Rugua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Rukuo)’s text. It has been argued that this place was [Lingga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Regency), south of Riau, and that the 14th-century *Longyamen* was in that vicinity. It is more probable, however, that the Song text referred to a different place from the Yuan texts. There is overwhelming evidence to suggest that *Longyamen* was Singapore in the 14th and 15th centuries.[^cite_note-190] Presently, the Riau Island is one of the province in Indonesia that has the largest number of Chinese population. The capital of the Riau Islands, [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang), is one of the city that has the largest percentage of ethnic Chinese population among other cities in Indonesia, amounting to 13.5% of the total population. Until now some of Tanjung Pinang's economy is driven by the ethnic Chinese community. They not only controlled the small or medium-scale economy, but also developed into large-scale businesses such as shipbuilding, housing, restaurants and hospitality. In addition to moving in the economy, there are some who become politicians, people's representatives, political figures and even civil servants.[^cite_note-191] Most of the Chinese in Tanjung Pinang, as well as the Riau Islands in a whole trace their ancestors from [Fujian](88585.md), hence most of them are of [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoklo_people) origin, while significant population of [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_people) and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_people) origins also exist in the Riau Islands.

Other ethnic groups, such as the [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) from [North Sumatra](453595.md), the [Banjarese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjar_people) from [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan) and the [Buginese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buginese_people) from [South Sulawesi](389458.md) also inhabit the province, albeit to a lesser extent.

### Language

[Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is the official language of the state, as well as the province.[^cite_note-192] However, several [varieties of Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Malay?action=edit\&redlink=1) are spoken in the province. There are fifteen different varieties of Malay spoken in the Riau Islands, most of which are [mutually intelligible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility) with Indonesian, which is a standardized form of Malay.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-The_Indonesian_Language_2004._Page_14-194] The Riau Islands are considered the birthplace of the modern [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language), though it was the classical Malaccan Malay of the [Johor court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_court) rather than Riau Malay that formed the basis of the standard Indonesian language.[^cite_note-195] The common Riau Islands Malay dialect, which is spoken in major cities such as [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang) and [Batam](716783.md) is similar to the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malay) used in [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor), [Malaysia](3607937.md), although it is much more influenced by the Indonesian language. It served as the *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)* of the province.

The [Karimun Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimun_Regency) has several dialects namely the Kundur dialect and the Bintan-Karimun dialect. In general, it uses the Johor-Riau dialect. The main characteristic of this dialect is the suffix "A" which sounds as "E". The word /saya/ and /apa/ ("I" and "What" respectively) will be pronounced as /saye/ and /ape/ in the Johor-Riau dialect, which is similar to the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malay) used in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia). The dialects used in the [Bintan Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Regency) are the Bintan-Karimun dialect, the Malang Rapat-Kelong dialect which is used in Malang Rapat village and Kelong village and the Mantang Lama dialect in Mantang Lama Village in Kijang. The word pronunciation is similar to the Malay used in Tanjung Pinang and Karimun. The Malay used in the [Lingga Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Regency) is also similar to the Malay used in the Karimun and BIitan Regency. The variety of Malay spoken in the province's northernmost regencies, [Anambas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anambas_Islands_Regency) and [Natuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natuna_Regency), is not only mutually unintelligible to the standard Indonesian language, but also mutually unintelligible to the Malay varieties spoken in Bintan and Karimun. The Anambas Malay dialect slightly resembles the [Pontianak Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak_Malay) dialect spoken in [West Kalimantan](453636.md) and the [Belitung Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belitung_Malay) dialect spoken in [Bangka-Belitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Belitung_Islands), especially their vocabulary and phonology. For example, *kitak* or *ketak* which means you, *wan* which means grandma and *suduk* which means spoon are both used in Anambas and Pontianak. The Natuna Malay people spoke a dialect of Malay that is influenced by [Terengganu Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu_Malay) and [Pahang Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang_Malay) that are spoken in the Malaysian states of [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu) and [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang) respectively, as most of the people living there trace their origins from the eastern coast of the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula). Historically, the Anambas and Natuna Malays spoke the [Terengganu Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu_Malay), but its speakers (mostly elderly) are rapidly diminishing and replaced by the local Malay dialects on the islands (to a lesser extent, the usage of standard Indonesian was also the cause of the diminishment of native dialect speakers).[^cite_note-196] The *[Orang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Laut)* people also spoke their own variety of Malay which is still related to the Malay spoken in the Batam-Bintan area.[^cite_note-197]

Traditionally, the Malay language is written in [Jawi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_script) (locally known in Indonesia as "Arab-Melayu"), an Arabic-based writing.[^cite_note-198] However, the use of Jawi is diminishing rapidly, being replaced by the [Latin script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_script) introduced by the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) during the [colonial era](23476997.md). Today, the Jawi script are primarily used for religious and cultural activities.[^cite_note-199] Road signs and government buildings signs in the Riau Islands are also written in this script, usually written along the Latin script.

Most of the Chinese in the Riau Islands spoke a variety of [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien), a local variant of the [Min Nan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Nan) [Chinese variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Chinese), as most of the Riau Islands Chinese are of [Hoklo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoklo_people) descent. The Riau Islands Hokkien is related to the Hokkien spoken in mainland Riau, such as the town of [Bagansiapiapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagansiapiapi) and [Selat Panjang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selat_Panjang), as well as the Hokkien spoken in Central and Southern [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia) and [Singapore](27318.md). Together, they are known as the [Southern Malaysia Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Malaysia_Hokkien). This dialect is based on the [Quanzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou_dialect), which originated from the city of [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou) in [Fujian](88585.md), [China](5405.md). It is markedly distinct from [Penang Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang_Hokkien) and [Medan Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan_Hokkien), which are based on [Zhangzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou_dialect), another Hokkien dialect originated from the city of [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou), also in Fujian. Other varieties of Chinese are also spoken in the province, such as [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_dialect), [Hainanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainanese), [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), albeit to a lesser extent compared to Hokkien. Hokkien serves as the *lingua franca* of the Chinese people in the Riau Islands. Most of the Chinese varieties spoken in the region have been greatly influenced by [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) and [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language), especially their [vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_loanwords_in_Indonesian).

Other ethnic groups who migrated from other parts of Indonesia brought their own language, such as the [Javanese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) spoken by the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Minangkabau language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_language) spoken by the [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people) and different varieties of [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_languages) spoken by different sub-groups of the [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) people.

[Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is widely used as the national language. Most formal education, and nearly all national mass media, [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance), [administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration), [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), and other forms of communication in the Riau Islands, are conducted in Indonesian. It is also used for inter-ethnic communication. Indonesian is spoken as the first language in major cities such as [Batam](716783.md) and [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang), while it is spoken as a second language elsewhere in the province. The [Chinese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians) in the Riau Islands use Indonesian as their third language, with Hokkien or other [Chinese varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Chinese) being used as their first language and the local Riau Malay as their second language. Due to it close proximity from [Singapore](27318.md), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) or [Singlish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singlish) are sometimes used as another lingua franca among Riau Islands people to foreigners, especially in Batam.

The local Malay people of the Riau region since ancient times and even until the present-day is considered as a society that is accommodating, friendly, and welcome to immigrants who come to bring new cultures and religions, both from within and outside the country. The example are immigrants from [India](14533.md) with [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), from China with [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism), from Europe with [Christianity](5211.md) and [Catholicism](606848.md), as well as other migrants from various ethnic groups in the [Nusantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara_\(archipelago\)) archipelago. Based on the composition of the population of the Riau Islands which is full of diversity with different socio-cultural, linguistic and religious backgrounds, it is basically an asset for the Riau Archipelago itself. The religions embraced by the inhabitants of the province are very diverse, including [Islam](6037917.md), [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), [Catholicism](606848.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](3267529.md), and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism).

As of 2016, [Islam](6037917.md) is the dominant religion in the province, forming 78.29% of the total population. [Islam](6037917.md) is generally adhered by the ethnic [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people), [Banjars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjar_people), [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis), [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people) and some [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) sub-group. Islam has entered the region since at least the 12th century. Contact between the indigenous population and Muslim traders from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), [Persia](14653.md) and India did occur for the first time in the northern Sumatra region because of its strategic position to traders stopover at that time. This condition strengthens the indication that the [Islamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) process is expected to have taken place since the contact took place so that a community was formed where the Arabs who settled in the village were married to local residents thus forming Muslim communities.[^cite_note-201] Several Muslim organizations in Indonesia has branches in the Riau Islands, such as [Muhammadiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadiyah) and the [Nahdlatul Ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahdlatul_Ulama).

[Christianity](5211.md) forms the second-largest religion group in the province, forming around 14.51% of the total population. The two largest Christian domination in the Riau Islands are [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) and [Catholicism](606848.md), forming around 11.97% and 2.54% of the total population respectively. Most of the people who adhered Protestantism are from [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) sub-groups (specifically [Batak Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toba_Batak_people), [Karo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karo_people_\(Indonesia\)) and [Simalungun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simalungun_people)), [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias_people), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians), and migrants from [North Sulawesi](453688.md), [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands) and [Papua](1432822.md), while most of the people who adhered Catholicism are migrants from [East Nusa Tenggara](453728.md). The province does not have its own Catholic [Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese). Instead, it is served by the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Pangkal-Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Pangkal-Pinang), which is based in [Pangkal-Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangkal_Pinang), [Bangka Belitung Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Belitung_Islands).

[Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) forms a significant minority in the province, forming around 6.99% and 0.15% respectively. They are mostly adhered by the Chinese. Lastly, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) only forms around 0.05% of the total population, which is mostly adhered by [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people) migrants and [Indonesians of Indian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians).

## Culture

The culture of the Riau Islands is strongly influenced by the culture of the [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_culture) and the Chinese people. Some ethnic groups, for example [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people), [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak), and European culture also influenced the culture of the province. The culture of the Riau Islands is also influenced by the [culture of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_culture). The Malay people that inhabits Riau Islands is also overwhelmingly adhered to Islam. In addition to Islam, the culture of the Riau Islands has also been influenced by the other religions, such as [Buddhism](3267529.md), Christianity and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism). However, as the Riau Islands is considered as the home of the Malay people and part of the [Malay world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_world), the culture of the Riau Islands mostly relate to the culture of the Malay people themselves.

What is typical of the Malay people is the designation of a nickname for a specific person in a family. The first child in a Malay family is called *long* or *sulung*, the second child is called *ngah*/*ongah*, the third child is called *cik* and the youngest child is called *cu/ucu*. The nickname is usually followed by a word that describes the physical characteristics of the person concerned, for example *cik* *itam,* which means a third child with a dark/black characteristic. This culture can only be found in the Riau Malay community, both in the Riau Islands as well as mainland Riau.[^cite_note-202]

A typical Riau Malay *[kampung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong)* (village) are led by a village chief called the *penghulu*. Presently, the *penghulu* is a village administrator chosen based on government regulations. Besides the *penghulu* there is also a leader in the religious sector called *[imam](15240.md)*. This *imam* takes care of all matters relating to religion, such as religious studies and lessons, marriage/ divorce/reconciliation, the distribution of inheritance, collection of [zakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat) and others. Thus the *penghulu* accompanied by the *imam* are the leader of the *kampung*.[^cite_note-\:2-203]

In the *kampung*, most of the local people knew each other, because the villagers had a strong sense of attachment to each other. Harmony is a characteristic of the community of these *kampung*. The existence of harmony is not caused by external coercion in the form of harsh sanctions, but it does arise from a conscience that is influenced by the norms that live in the community. Starting from the movements, attitudes and traits are influenced by these factors. Avoiding things that can cause disgrace and shame is a motivating factor for continuing to act and be kind to others and such feelings are stronger than feelings of sin. All actions must be maintained so as not to cause "discordant eyes", "discordant ears", "discordant manners".[^cite_note-\:2-203]

### Arts and performance



A troupe of local Riau Malay dancers performing the [Joget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joget) Lambak. (taken in the late 19th century)

Various forms and types of arts are found in the Riau region, namely theater, dance, music, singing, and literature. Artists in urban areas generally feel alien to traditional arts. Therefore, an appreciative link is needed by introducing all types and forms of traditional arts in urban areas. Thus, contemporary art that grows, lives, and develops in urban areas will have a solid and tidy foundation in contributing to national art.

In the late 1890s, the Rusydiah Club was founded, an association for scholars, writers, and cultural figures.[^cite_note-204] This association was established in the Riau Islands, precisely on [Penyengat Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penyengat_Island) which at that time was the center of the government of the [Sultanate of Riau-Lingga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau-Lingga_Sultanate). In essence, this association is a cultural institution that includes art, performance and literature. Its activities began with the commemoration of Islamic holidays, such as [Maulud Nabi](1285650.md), [Isra-Mikraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isra_and_Mi'raj), [Nuzulul Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Quran), [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md), [Eid al-Adha](358226.md), and others which later developed to the publication of books by members of the association. All activities are supported by royal facilities in the form of the Marah Ahmadi Khanah Polar library and two [Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_alphabet) printing presses, namely Mathba‘at al Ahmadiyah and Mathba‘at al Riauwiyah.[^cite_note-205] The Rusydiah Klub is the first association of scholars in Indonesia. This association is not mentioned in national history, because of the lack of careful collection of historical material, or maybe because there is no input from those who know much about it. Rusydiah Club left behind a valuable heritage of creativity in the form of books of literature, religion, history and linguistics.[^cite_note-\:2-203]



[Mak Yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mak_Yong) dancers in Bintan

The Riau region has long been an area of trade traffic in neighboring countries, so Riau has given birth to diverse cultural figures and colors. This is a burden, as well as a historical-geographical blessing. Riau seems to be a field of association of various artistic potentials, in which there are cultural influences in neighboring countries and other Indonesian cultures. Riau Malay art is very diverse, because small groups that exist in the community are also developing.[^cite_note-\:2-203] The fact shows, the arts in Riau and the arts in Malay countries such as [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Singapore](27318.md) and [Brunei Darussalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei_Darussalam) mutually complement and influence each other. Likewise, Malay cultural regions such as [Deli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Deli), [Langkat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Langkat), [Serdang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serdang_Malays) and [Asahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahan_Sultanate) in [North Sumatra](453595.md), [Jambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambi), [West Kalimantan](453636.md), and others, were also affected by the [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people), [Mandailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandailing_people), [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buginese_people) and [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) cultures. Cultures influence that come from outside Indonesia such as [India](14533.md) ([Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus)-[Buddhist](3267529.md)), [Arabic](2185.md) ([Islam](6037917.md)), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture), and [Siamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_culture) influence. The flexibility of Malay culture is in line with the historical development and geographical location of Riau, making Riau very rich with a variety of artistic expressions. The development of Malay culture in Riau, in turn, can enrich national culture.[^cite_note-\:2-203]

*[Baju Kurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baju_Kurung)* is one of the traditional clothes of the Malay community in [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), [Indonesia](14579.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Singapore](27318.md), and [southern Thailand](1453076.md). *[Baju Kurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baju_Kurung)* is often associated with women. The hallmark of the clothes brackets is the loose design on the holes in the arms, abdomen, and chest. When worn, the bottom of the clothes bracket is parallel to the groin, but for rare cases there are also those that extend to knee level. Shirt brackets are not fitted with buttons, but are almost similar to t-shirts. Shirt brackets are also not collared, each end is crochet. Some parts are often decorated with gold embroidery. At first, the clothes of the brackets were usually worn for the great ceremony of Malay by women in the kingdom, used together with *[songket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songket)* cloth to be used as a scabbard, various gold jewelry, and a small bag or fan. Since the majority of Malay communities embraced [Islam](6037917.md), many women who wear clothes in brackets match them with the [hijab](68301.md), although there are also those who do not use them. Now the clothes of brackets are widely worn by ordinary people, used by children to read the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), or ladies to go to the market, without including accessories that seem luxurious.

However, the main traditional dress of the people of the Riau Islands is the *[Kebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebaya) Labuh* and the *Teluk Belanga.*[^cite_note-\:5-206] The *[Kebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebaya) Labuh a*re specifically worn by women. It is typically worn at the time of traditional ceremonies and official events of the local community. The hallmark of *Kebaya Labuh* and *Teluk Belangga* traditional clothing is the length of the kebaya to cover the knees with the shape of the kebaya looking wide and open. *Kebaya Labuh* is often combined with [batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik) cloth such as cual fabric. For the wedding, the groom wore a head covering called *[Tanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengkolok)*. The spindle is a rectangular [songket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songket) that is folded into a headband. A distinctive feature of the *Teluk Belanga* custom clothing is a plain color paired with shades of color, a sheath worn over the knee that acts as a scarf, a headband made of songket cloth called the *tanjak*. The custom attire of *Kebaya Labuh* and *Teluk Belangga* is complemented by a headdress called a peak which is a head covering made of square *songket* shaped like songkok or peci.[^cite_note-207] Typically, the skirt is only used for formal ceremonies or events such as feasts and custom ceremonies. However, these decorations are usually only worn on formal occasions such as death or marriage. Occasionally, *Teluk Belangga* clothing is usually accompanied by a *[peci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songkok)*. For women, the *Kebaya Labuh* is usually combined with batik and also with a scarf.



Malay men performing the *Silat Pengantin* traditional dance in a wedding ceremony at [Lingga Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingga_Regency)

The *[Mak yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mak_yong)* is one of the traditional dances that can be found in the Riau Islands. *Mak Yong* is a traditional theatrical art of the Malay community which is still sustainable today. Although it is not often performed, every service is always welcomed by enthusiastic residents, especially those who love Malay culture and native art. *Mak Yong* performances are usually performed by groups of dancers and musicians, who combine various religious elements, theatrical, dance, music, vocals and texts. The main characters are male and female, both are performed by female dancers. The *Mak yong* performance is accompanied by musical instruments such as fiddle and drums.[^cite_note-208] In the Riau Islands region, the *Mak Yong* oral tradition has been exhibited since several centuries ago and has spread to [Bangka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Island), Johor, [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca) and [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang). In the Riau Islands, this oral tradition developed rapidly during the sultanate era. *Mak yong* theater performing arts is played with dances, songs and jokes that are intertwined in a story line, the players are 20 people, women wearing glittering costumes.[^cite_note-209]

*[Zapin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapin)* is a Malay traditional dance that can be found in both the Riau Islands and mainland. This dance is one of several types of Malay dance that still exists today. According to history, in the beginning this dance was a dance of entertainment in the palace after being brought from [Hadramaut](325752.md) in Yemen by Arab traders in the early 16th century. At that time the country of [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor) became a substitute for the role of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca) as an international port in the 16th century. This can be seen from the development of *Zapin* dance creations that are identical with Malay culture and in terms of rhyming. Artists and culture are able to make traditional art, not stopping but full of dynamics that are always acceptable in every circumstance. This traditional dance is both educational and entertaining, and is used as an Islamic [da'wah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawah) medium through *Zapin* songs that are sung. The accompanying music consists of fiddles, tambourines, drums and small drum percussion instruments called [marwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirwas) or maracas. The *Zapin* dance focuses more on teamwork rather than individual efforts. Before the 1960s, *Zapin* was only performed by male dancers but now it is usually danced by female dancers or both male and female.[^cite_note-210]

The [Melemang Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melemang_Dance?action=edit\&redlink=1) is a traditional Riau dance by the people of [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island). It originated back in the 12th century in Tanjungpisau. Originally, it was only performed at the Royal Palace of the [Kingdom of Bentan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bentan) in Bukit Batu, Bintan. As the kingdom collapsed, this dance became a folk entertainment show. At present, *Malemang* Dance is displayed in various events, including festivals. In the show, the dancers demonstrate their ability to take something (handkerchief, change, etc.). They do this by flexing (standing up, bending backwards). From this skill then this dance is called *malemang* or *melemang*. In Tanjungpisau, this dance is better known as the *Melemang Bintan Penaga* Dance.[^cite_note-211]

### Architecture



A typical Malay traditional house in the [Taman Mini Indonesia Indah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Mini_Indonesia_Indah), Jakarta

In [Malay culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_culture), the art of development also has characteristics that are reflected in the symbols of traditional houses that are full of meaning. At present, Malay traditional houses can still be found in several regions in the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago), [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) and other [Malay-populated countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_world). In traditional Malay society, a house is an intact building that can be used as a family residence, meeting place and shelter for anyone in need. Therefore, traditional Malay houses are generally large, usually with six poles, six porch poles and twelve poles or porch houses. Of the several forms of the house, all are almost identical, both stairs, doors, walls and room layout are identical. In addition to being large, the Malay house is also always in the form of a stage or a house with a hole, facing towards the sunrise. In general, the types of Malay houses include dwellings, halls, houses of worship and storage houses. The naming is adjusted to the function of each building.[^cite_note-212]

Riau Malay traditional house buildings are generally given a variety of decorations, starting from the door, window, ventilation to the top of the roof of the building, the decoration is adjusted to the meaning of each carving. Additionally, these houses are also given a pattern. The pattern of a [Riau Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Malays) house is largely derived from nature, consisting of flora, fauna, and space objects. These things are designed in certain forms, either in their original form such as backdrops, forest flowers, or in abstracted or modified forms so that they no longer appear in their original form, but simply use their name.

The *Limas Potong* house is one of the traditional houses of the Malays inhabiting the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago).[^cite_note-\:3-213] *Limas Potong* is a stilt house, like a traditional house in [Sumatra](28679.md) in general. Height of about 1.5 meters from the ground surface. The walls of the house are made of brown planks, while the roof is red zinc. The doors, windows and pillars on the front of the house are painted in white oil. *Limas Potong* has five main parts, namely terrace, front room, center, back (bed), and kitchen. The front of the house is used to display historical photographs of this traditional house, the living room contains a typical Malay bridal diorama, and the back is a room that contains a mosquito net that integrates with the kitchen.[^cite_note-\:3-213]

One of the most famous traditional house of the people of the Riau Islands is the *Belah Bubung* traditional house. This house is also known as the *Rabung* house or the *Bumbung Melayu* house. The name *Belah Bubung* was given by the Malays because the shape of the roof was split. The size of the house built is determined by the ability of its owner, the richer a person is, the bigger the house and the more variety of decoration. However, wealth is not an absolute determinant. The most important consideration in making a home is harmony with the owner. To determine whether or not a house is compatible, the owner calculates the size of the house by cubits, from one to five. The veins are: snake swimming, pursuing ripples, ripples climbing beetles shelter, debt out of debt changed debt, and five debt has not been affected. The best measure is if it is right on the count of ripples to climb the shade beetle.[^cite_note-\:3-213]

### Traditional weapon

*Badik Tumbuk Lado* is a traditional weapon originating from the Riau Islands. *[Badik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badik)* itself is a term for traditional weapons which is known among the Bugis community and some regions in [Sumatra](28679.md). Meanwhile, Tumbuk Lada is a traditional weapon of the [Malay people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)). No wonder the *Badik Tumbuk Lado* has similarities with weapons from other Malay-populated region. The Riau Islands are inhabited by various races and ethnicity. However, the majority of indigenous people are Malay people. Therefore, the culture of the Riau region has a lot in common with other indigenous Malay regions. *Badik Tumbuk Lado* is a type of stabbing weapon measuring 27 to 29 cm and its width is around 3.5 to 4 cm. This weapon is not only used by the people of [Jambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambi), and also has similarities with the [Buginese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buginese_people) *Badik*, it only differs in the shape and motive of the holster. Not only domestically, Malaysia also has the same traditional weapons, both in name and form. This is inseparable from the background of Malay communities spread throughout [Indonesia](14579.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), [Vietnam](202354.md) and along the Malay peninsula. Similar to the *[keris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris)*, *badik* is also one of the identities that characterizes the Malay people. Until now the *Badik Tumbuk Lado* is still used by the people of the Riau islands to do production work such as farming or hunting. Some local customs also still maintain the *badik* as a complement to men's custom clothing.[^cite_note-214]

The *Jenawi* is a type of sword that became the most popular traditional weapon in the Riau area. This sword was often used by Malay warlords in the face of his enemies. The sword has a length of about 1 meter so it is often used for close combat. *Jenawi* sword is similar to a typical Japanese [katana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katana). Historians and cultural experts argue that this weapon originated from ancient Japan which experienced acculturation with Malay culture. Regardless of this opinion, the sword of genius remains a Malay people's identity on the national scene.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Malay cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_cuisine)



[Nasi lemak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_lemak) Sambal Cumi, a variety of Nasi Lemak that can be found in the Riau Islands

Cuisine in the Riau Islands are mostly similar to the one in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia), owing to its similar culture. There are also some dishes that is influenced by other parts of Indonesia, such as Java. Due to its cultural diversity, the people of the Riau Islands has developed a unique dishes that cannot be found in other parts of Indonesia. The Riau Islands cuisine is strongly influenced by the culture of the Malay, [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_India) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture). Some foods are also influenced by the culture of the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_culture), [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_cuisine), [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buginese_people), other ethnic groups in [Sumatra](28679.md) and [Borneo](4518.md), as well as European cultures, especially the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_cuisine) and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cuisine).

One of the most popular dish in the Riau Islands is its variety of the [Nasi lemak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_lemak), with some notable differences compared to the variety found in neighboring [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md). In archipelago region like Riau Islands, usually seafood are used to accompany nasi lemak, such as *ikan bilis* ([anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchovy)), *ikan tamban* (*[Sardinella longiceps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinella_longiceps)*), *ikan selar kuning* (*[Selaroides leptolepis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selaroides_leptolepis)*), *sotong* or *cumi-cumi* (squid) or small [Prawns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawn). The Riau islands traditional nasi lemak is quite similar to Malaysian version; it comes as a platter of coconut rice wrapped in banana leaf, with cucumber slices, small dried anchovies (*ikan bilis*), roasted peanuts, hard boiled egg, and hot spicy sauce (*sambal*).[^cite_note-Kepri-215] The Riau islands version however, comes with an addition of small fish locally known as *ikan tamban*, usually fried with *[sambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal)* chili paste and very crispy, the whole fish is edible.[^cite_note-216] [Prawns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawn) and [Squids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid) are also commonly stir-fried in chili paste as *sambal udang* or *sambal cumi*.

Another notable dish that can be found in the Riau Islands is the [laksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laksa). Laksa or Lakse in local dialect is a kind of noodle food mixed with typical Chinese and Malay spice blend. Lakse has a round white and slightly thick noodle shape. The name of lakse is taken from Sanskrit which has a lot meaning. It shows that lute noodle is made with various spices. Lakse in the Riau Islands presented in several kinds, one of which is a typical Anambas lakse which is included in the category of lakse curry with a fatty and spicy sauce and rich in spices. One of the most typical mackerel soups of Batam, popularly known as the Batam Fish Soup ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Sup Ikan Batam*). This Batam Fish soup is served with a slice of mackerel fish that had previously been separated from its bones. This piece of fish is smeared with egg whites to remove the fishy smell, then cooked with spices and mixed with starfruit and green tomatoes. Pieces of white or green mustard greens are also incorporated into this soup.

The [Bak kut teh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bak_kut_teh) (肉骨茶) is another dish found in the Riau Islands, usually consumed by the Chinese people in the province. the dish can only be found in the region as well as neighboring Singapore and Malaysia. Bak kut teh means meat tea bone. *Bak kut* itself is pork ribs, while the word *teh*, which means tea in [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien), comes from either the dish brown sauce which is similar to the color of a tea, or that it was originally served with tea to mollify the taste.. This gravy is a pork broth, with spices such as garlic and strong pepper.

Snacks and drinks such as the [teh tarik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik) and [curry puff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_puff) can also be found in the province. Teh Tarik is not originally an original beverage from the Riau Islands. This drink is familiar in [Southeast Asia](28741.md) especially easily found in [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md). However, in the Riau Islands, such as [Batam](716783.md), [Tanjung Pinang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Pinang) and Tanjung Balai Karimun, this drink is commonly available everywhere. The condition of the Riau Islands, which is close to [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md) has made Teh Tarik to spread around the Riau Islands.[^cite_note-217] Curry puff, also known as *epok-epok* in the local Riau Islands Malay, is a famous local snack that can be commonly found everywhere in the province. This pastry is made from flour and potatoes is very easy to find in the [Malay world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_world). In Malaysia or Singapore, for example. Epok-epok is better known as *karipap*. Whereas in [Java](69336.md), the pastry is usually called [pastel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastel_\(food\)). Epok-epok is a pastry made from flour dough which is printed with a special mold, with a filling typical for epok-epok. In the past, epok-epok are usually made of spicy potatoes, but now many other ingredients have been added to epok-epok as variations of flavor.[^cite_note-218]
