# Raymond Mboulou



Raymond Mboulou in July 2016

**Raymond Zéphirin Mboulou** (born 19 March 1956[^cite_note-Firma-1][^cite_note-Choc-2]) is a [Congolese](19599929.md) politician who has served in the government of Congo-Brazzaville as Minister of the Interior since December 2007. Previously, he was Secretary-General of the Presidency from May 2007 to December 2007.

## Political career

A member of the [Mbochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbochi) ethnic group,[^cite_note-Amis-3][^cite_note-4] Mboulou was born at [Mpouya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpouya), located in the [Plateaux Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateaux_Department_\(Congo\)) of Congo-Brazzaville.[^cite_note-Firma-1][^cite_note-Choc-2][^cite_note-Amis-3] He earned a degree from the [Center for Financial, Economic and Banking Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Financial,_Economic_and_Banking_Studies?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Paris[^cite_note-Firma-1][^cite_note-Choc-2] and became a state inspector in 1982;[^cite_note-Firma-1] afterwards he held various other positions in the civil service over the years. In the [1992 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Republic_of_the_Congo_parliamentary_election) and the [1993 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Republic_of_the_Congo_parliamentary_election), he was elected to the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Republic_of_the_Congo) as a candidate in Mpouya constituency.[^cite_note-Choc-2]

After [Denis Sassou Nguesso](158608.md) returned to power in October 1997, Mboulou served as Director-General of the Control of Markets and State Contracts from 1998 to 2002.[^cite_note-Firma-1] In the [May 2002 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Republic_of_the_Congo_parliamentary_election), he was again elected to the National Assembly as the candidate of the [Congolese Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_Labour_Party) (PCT) in Mpouya constituency; he won the seat in the first round with 80.15% of the vote.[^cite_note-5] After the election, he was Head of the Cabinet of [Aimé Emmanuel Yoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aim%C3%A9_Emmanuel_Yoka), the Director of the Presidential Cabinet, from 2002 to 2007.[^cite_note-Firma-1]

Mboulou was then appointed as Secretary-General of the Presidency of the Republic, with the rank of Minister, on 15 May 2007.[^cite_note-Firma-1] In the [June–August 2007 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Republic_of_the_Congo_parliamentary_election), Mboulou was again elected to the National Assembly as the PCT candidate in Mpouya; he won the seat in the first round with 96% of the vote.[^cite_note-6] After the election, he was appointed to the government as Minister of Territorial Administration and Decentralization on 30 December 2007.[^cite_note-7]

As Minister of Territorial Administration, Mboulou announced the provisional results of the [12 July 2009 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Republic_of_the_Congo_presidential_election) on 15 July 2009. Those results showed incumbent President [Denis Sassou Nguesso](158608.md) winning another term with 78.61% of the vote; Mboulou also placed the turnout rate at 66%, although that was strongly contested by the opposition, which had boycotted the election and claimed that the turnout rate was anemic.[^cite_note-8] Following the election, Sassou Nguesso retained Mboulou in the government as Minister of the Interior and Decentralization in a [cabinet reshuffle](845103.md) on 15 September 2009.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Mboulou stood again as the PCT candidate for Mpouya constituency in the [July–August 2012 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Republic_of_the_Congo_parliamentary_election); he again won the seat in the first round of voting.[^cite_note-11] Following the second round of voting, he announced the results of the election on 7 August 2012 in his capacity as Minister of the Interior; according to those results, the PCT won a large majority of the seats in the National Assembly.[^cite_note-12] Mboulou was retained in his post as Minister of the Interior and Decentralization in the post-election government named on 25 September 2012.[^cite_note-13]

On 11 September 2014, a day before the beginning of campaigning for the 28 September 2014 local elections, Mboulou called for the campaign to proceed peacefully in a tolerant and respectful atmosphere.[^cite_note-14]

Critics argued that Mboulou, as Minister of the Interior, played a key role in facilitating Sassou Nguesso's re-election in the [March 2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republic_of_the_Congo_presidential_election), restricting the activities of opposition candidates and ordering a communications blackout to prevent destabilization on election day and in the immediate aftermath of the vote.[^cite_note-15] He announced the official results of the election, which showed a first-round victory for Sassou Nguesso, on 24 March 2016.[^cite_note-16] Sassou Nguesso retained Mboulou in his post as Minister of the Interior, Decentralization and Local Development in the government named on 30 April 2016.[^cite_note-17]

In the [July 2017 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Republic_of_the_Congo_parliamentary_election), Mboulou stood unopposed as a candidate in Mpouya, with no other candidates standing in the constituency.[^cite_note-18]

## Titles and awards

Mboulou is a Grand Officer of the [Congolese Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_Order_of_Merit?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Firma-1]
