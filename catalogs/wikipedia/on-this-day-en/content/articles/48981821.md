**Vaishali Rameshbabu** (born 21 June 2001) is an Indian [chess grandmaster](12304.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-\:2-2] She is a two-time champion of the [FIDE Women's Grand Swiss Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Women's_Grand_Swiss_Tournament), winning consecutively in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Women's_Grand_Swiss_Tournament_2023) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Women's_Grand_Swiss_Tournament_2025). Having played in two editions of the [Women's Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Candidates_Tournament), she won the [2026 edition](79349652.md) to earn the right to challenge [Ju Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_Wenjun) for the title of [Women's World Chess Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Champion) in the upcoming [championship match held in 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Championship_2027).&#x20;

Representing India, she was part of the team that won the silver medal at the [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asian_Games) in the [women's team competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_team_standard), and was a member of the team that won the gold medal in the women's event of the [45th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Chess_Olympiad).

## Personal life



Vaishali pictured with her mother, Nagalakshmi *(left)*.

Vaishali was born in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), Tamil Nadu on 21 June 2001. Her father, Rameshbabu, works as a branch manager at [TNSC Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNSC_Bank),[^cite_note-3] and her mother, Nagalakshmi, is a [homemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homemaker).[^cite_note-4] Her younger brother [R Praggnanandhaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_Praggnanandhaa) is also a chess grandmaster; they are the first brother and sister duo to hold the title.[^cite_note-\:4-5]

## Career

### 2012-2019: Beginnings

Vaishali won the Girls' [World Youth Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Youth_Chess_Championship) for Under-12s in 2012 and Under-14s in 2015.[^cite_note-6] In 2013, at age 12, she defeated future World Chess Champion [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen) in a [simul competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_exhibition) that Carlsen held while in her hometown of Chennai for the [World Chess Championship 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2013).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

She won the [National Junior Girls Chess Championship (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Junior_Girls_Chess_Championship_\(India\)) consequently in 2015 and 2016.[^cite_note-9]

In 2016, she received the [Woman International Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman_International_Master) (WIM) title, and in October 2016, she was ranked second in India and World No.12 girl U16-player. At that time, she had an [Elo rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system) of 2300.

She became a [Woman Grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman_Grandmaster) (WGM) by completing her final norm in the [Riga Technical University Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_Technical_University_Open) chess tournament in Riga, Latvia on 12 August 2018.[^cite_note-10]

### 2020-2022: IM title and Olympiad

Vaishali was the part of the gold medal-winning team[^cite_note-11] at [Online Olympiad 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Online_Chess_Olympiad_2020), where India won its first ever medal.[^cite_note-12]

She received her [International Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Master) (IM) title in 2021. In 2022, Vaishali won the 8th Fischer Memorial, scoring 7/9 and winning her second [Grandmaster norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_norm).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

Vaishali was invited to participate in the FIDE Women's Speed Chess Championship 2022,[^cite_note-17] where she defeated the Women's World Blitz Chess Champion [Bibisara Assaubayeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibisara_Assaubayeva) in the round of 16,[^cite_note-18] and compatriot [Harika Dronavalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harika_Dronavalli) in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Vaishali played on Board 3 in the Women's section at the [44th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/44th_Chess_Olympiad) at Mamallapuram, Chennai, in July-Aug 2022. The India women's team won the team bronze medal, and Vaishali won individual bronze for board 3.

In December 2022, Vaishali won the [Tata Stel Chess India 2022 Blitz Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel_Chess_India#2022) in [Kolkata](47905.md), scoring 13½/18.[^cite_note-21]

### 2023-2025: Double Grand Swiss Champion and Olympiad Gold

Vaishali played in the [Tata Steel Challengers 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel_Chess_Tournament_2023), scoring 4.5/14 and beating two 2600-rated GMs, [Luis Paulo Supi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Paulo_Supi) and [Jerguš Pecháč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jergu%C5%A1_Pech%C3%A1%C4%8D). She finished twelfth in the standings overall.[^cite_note-22]

In the [Qatar Masters Open 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Masters_Open), Vaishali received her final GM norm after finishing with 5/9 and a performance rating of 2609.[^cite_note-\:0-23] She also won the top women's prize in the tournament, after finishing with better tiebreaks (performance rating) than compatriot [Divya Deshmukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Deshmukh).[^cite_note-\:0-23]

In November 2023, Vaishali won the [FIDE Women's Grand Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Women's_Grand_Swiss_Tournament_2023) held in the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man) going undefeated with the score 8.5/11, and qualified for the [2024 Women's Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Candidates_Tournament_2024).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-\:1-25] She and her younger brother Praggnanandhaa became the first sister-brother duo to qualify for the respective Candidates.[^cite_note-\:1-25]

In December, at the IV El Llobregat Open Tournament 2023 in Spain, Vaishali crossed the 2500 Elo rating threshold, thereby fulfilling all of the requirements for the [Grandmaster title](12304.md) and becoming the third woman and 84th person in India to do so.[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-\:3-26]

On January 31, 2024, the India Chess Federation submitted a Grandmaster Title Application[^cite_note-\:2-2] on behalf of Vaishali to the [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE) Qualification Committee, which administers [FIDE titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_titles).[^cite_note-27] She became the first female Grandmaster from [Tamilnadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamilnadu).[^cite_note-28]

Vaishali finished 4th at the [Women's Candidates Tournament 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Candidates_Tournament_2024) with the score 7½/14 (+6-5=3), the same score as that of 2nd place [Koneru Humpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koneru_Humpy) and 3rd place [Lei Tingjie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei_Tingjie) but with worse tiebreaks, she was tied for 3rd after 5 rounds but lost the next 4 games to reach last place and then made an incredible comeback with 5 consecutive wins to end the tournament on a positive score.[^cite_note-29]

In September 2024, Vaishali played Board 2 for the Indian team that won Gold in the [women's section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_event_at_the_45th_Chess_Olympiad) of the [45th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Chess_Olympiad). This marked India's first ever win at the [Women's Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad).[^cite_note-30]

In December 2024, Vaishali won bronze medal in the [Women's World Blitz Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Blitz_Chess_Championship_2024) she finished 1st in the round robin section and was defeated in the semifinals by eventual winner [Ju Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_Wenjun).[^cite_note-31]

In September 2025, Vaishali defended her Grand Swiss title at the [FIDE Women's Grand Swiss 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Women's_Grand_Swiss_Tournament_2025) held in [Samarkand](51491.md), Uzbekistan, scoring 8/11. Thus, she qualified for the [Women's Candidates Tournament 2026](79349652.md).[^cite_note-32]

### 2026: Candidates and World Championship

Vaishali won the [FIDE Women's Candidates Tournament 2026](79349652.md), scoring 8.5/14 (+5−2=7). She will play in the [Women's World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Championship_2027) against [Ju Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_Wenjun).[^cite_note-33]

In June 2026, she won the Rapid section of WR Women's Chess Tour 2026 Asia Leg, defeating [Irene Sukandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Sukandar) 1.5–0.5 in the quarterfinal, [Kateryna Lagno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kateryna_Lagno) 1.5–0.5 in the semifinal and [Alua Nurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alua_Nurman) 1.5–0.5 in the final.[^cite_note-34] She also participated in [World Rapid and Blitz Team Chess Championships 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rapid_and_Blitz_Team_Chess_Championships_2026) as part of the Chess Gurukul Team, They finished 5th in the Rapid section and Vaishali won Bronze for Board 6.[^cite_note-35] In July, Vaishali finished last of 6 players in the Challengers section of [Biel Chess Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biel_Chess_Festival#2026).[^cite_note-36] In August, Vaishali finished last of 10 players at [Cairns Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairns_Cup) scoring 2½/9 (+0-4=5).[^cite_note-37]

In September, Vaishali played on Board 2 for Indian Women's team that won bronze in the [Women's event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_event_at_the_46th_Chess_Olympiad) of the [46th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_Chess_Olympiad), she performed poorly scoring 2½/6 (+1-2=3) losing two critical games, she was rested after round 8.[^cite_note-38]
