**Commercial law**, also known as business law,[^cite_note-1] mercantile law or trade law depending on [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction), is the body of [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) or system of rules that govern the [rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rights), relations, [obligations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obligation), and conduct of [persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_person) and organizations engaged in [commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce) and [business](39206.md) activities.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-cornellcommerciallaw-4][^cite_note-5] It is often considered to be a branch of [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(common_law\)) and deals with issues of both [private law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) and [public law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_law).

Commerce is the system of activities and organizations involved in the [exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_\(economics\)) of [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(business\)), [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_\(economics\)), and other valuable [assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset).[^cite_note-6] These activities and the commercial entities—which are primarily engaged in [profit-driven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profit-driven) transactions—are subject to commercial law, which provides the legal framework for their formation, operation, and oversight. By establishing rules for [transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_transaction) and governing [business enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_enterprise), commercial law ensures that exchanges are orderly, enforceable, and predictable; promotes fair and efficient [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_Market); protects intellectual property and innovative [investments](63121.md); safeguards [consumer rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_rights) and [public trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_trust); and upholds [ethical standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical_standards) in business conduct.[^cite_note-7]

Commercial law is understood as the body of regulations that govern a broad range of matters involving [sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales), [lease](635681.md), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade) and [commercial entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_entity). This, non-exhaustively, includes [tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_law), [mergers and acquisitions](20769.md), [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_law), employment, [business-to-business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business-to-business) or [business-to-consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business-to-consumer) relations, [carriage of goods](28362.md), [guarantees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarantee) and [indemnities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indemnity), [partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership), and [principal-agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal-agent) relationships. Many of these categories further fall within [financial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_law), a subset of commercial law pertaining specifically to [financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financing) and the [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_market). Additionally, depending on the jurisdiction, commercial law legislation may be codified into either [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_code) or [commercial code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_code_\(law\)).

In the United States, commercial law is the province of both the [United States Congress](31756.md), under its power to regulate [interstate commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_commerce), and the states, under their police power. Efforts have been made to create a unified body of commercial law in the United States; the most successful of these attempts has resulted in the general [adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Commercial_Code_adoption) of the [Uniform Commercial Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Commercial_Code), which has been adopted in all 50 states (with some modification by state legislatures), the District of Columbia, and the U.S. territories.

Various regulatory frameworks govern the conduct of commerce, especially regarding [business-to-consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business-to-consumer) and employer-employee relations. Examples of these frameworks include privacy laws, [workplace safety laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workplace_Safety_Laws), and food and drug laws.

In some legal systems, business disputes are handled by specialized [commercial courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_court) which are judicial institutions responsible for enforcing and interpreting commercial laws to resolve disputes arising from business-related disagreements.[^cite_note-8]

## History

During the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Italy was the cradle of many modern institutions at the basis of commercial law. Around the 16th century, the trade of Italian [maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics) was the promoter of the birth of commercial law: the jurist [Benvenuto Stracca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benvenuto_Stracca), ([Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona), 1509–1579) published in 1553 the treatise *De mercatura seu mercatore tractats*; it was one of the first, if not the first, legal imprint dealing specifically with commercial law. This treatise focused on merchants and merchant contracts, practices and maritime rights, to which he soon added extensive discussions of [bankruptcy](4695.md), factors and commissions, third party transfers, and [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance). For this reason, Stracca is often considered the father of commercial law and author of the first Italian treaty about the insurance contract, beyond about the commerce. The legal work of Italian jurists had an impact on [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland), [Germany](11867.md), [England](9316.md) and [France](5843419.md).[^cite_note-10]
