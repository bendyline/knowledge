**Akane Yamaguchi** (山口茜, *Yamaguchi Akane*; born 6 June 1997) is a Japanese [badminton](3956.md) player who competes in women's singles. A former world number one, she became the first Japanese singles player to reach the top ranking on 19 April 2018. Yamaguchi is a three-time [World Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_World_Championships), winning the title in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BWF_World_Championships), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Championships) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BWF_World_Championships). This makes her the first Japanese player in any discipline to win three world titles. She also won the silver medal at the [2026 Asian Games](83296071.md). Her other major individual tournament victories include the [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_England_Open_Badminton_Championships), the [Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_Asia_Championships), and the season-ending [BWF World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_World_Tour_Finals).&#x20;

At the junior level, she won back-to-back [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_World_Junior_Championships) in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_BWF_World_Junior_Championships) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_BWF_World_Junior_Championships). While still a junior player, she also transitioned to the senior circuit and became the youngest player to win a [BWF Super Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Super_Series) tournament at the [2013 Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Japan_Super_Series).&#x20;

In team events, Yamaguchi was part of the squads that won gold at the [2018 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2018_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_team), the [2018 Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup), the [2017 Asia Mixed Team Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Badminton_Asia_Mixed_Team_Championships), and the [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Badminton_Asia_Team_Championships) and [2020 Asia Team Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Badminton_Asia_Team_Championships). Earlier in her career, she was named the [BWF Most Promising Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Awards#Rising_Star_of_the_Year) in both 2013 and 2014, and she later received the [BWF Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Awards#Players_of_the_Year) award in 2022.

## Early life and junior career

Akane Yamaguchi was born in Katsuyama, Fukui, Japan, on 6 June 1997.[^cite_note-profilesaishunkan-4] The youngest of three children, she was introduced to badminton by her two older brothers and first held a racquet at age three.[^cite_note-bwfinterview18-5][^cite_note-yomiurishimbun13-6] She began playing competitively at five, coached by her father at the local Heisenji Sports Club.[^cite_note-profilebadspi-7] Yamaguchi has stated that losses to her brothers motivated her to improve her skills.[^cite_note-coverage22-8] Diagnosed with asthma as a child, she took swimming lessons until the sixth grade to improve her health and body balance, focusing on backstroke to counter the one-sided bias of badminton.[^cite_note-profilebadspi-7] During her childhood, Yamaguchi had limited awareness of Japan's or the world's top players, instead developing her skills by studying examples from local players and coaches.[^cite_note-bwfinterview18-5]

While at [Heisenji Elementary School](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8B%9D%E5%B1%B1%E5%B8%82%E7%AB%8B%E5%B9%B3%E6%B3%89%E5%AF%BA%E5%B0%8F%E5%AD%A6%E6%A0%A1), she was a five-time champion at the National Elementary School ABC Tournament and won the National Elementary School Championship singles title for four consecutive years.[^cite_note-profilebadspi-7] At age nine, she was selected for the junior national team.[^cite_note-fukuishimbun13-9] During her time at Katsuyama Minami Junior High, she won both All Japan Junior Championships Newcomers' Division in her first year, followed by the National Junior High School Tournament title in her third year.[^cite_note-profilebadspi-7] At age 14, she became the youngest player to compete at the [All Japan Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Japan_Badminton_Championships), though she lost in the first round.[^cite_note-yomiurishimbun13-6] In December 2012, at age 15, she became the youngest player ever selected for Japanese national A team.[^cite_note-fukuishimbun13-9][^cite_note-profilebadspi-7]

Despite receiving offers from schools with elite badminton programs, Yamaguchi chose to attend her local [Katsuyama High School](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A6%8F%E4%BA%95%E7%9C%8C%E7%AB%8B%E5%8B%9D%E5%B1%B1%E9%AB%98%E7%AD%89%E5%AD%A6%E6%A0%A1) to play with childhood friends.[^cite_note-badmintonplanet-10] From 2013 to 2015, she became the first player in the tournament's history to win three consecutive singles titles at the [National High School Championships (Inter-High)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%85%A8%E5%9B%BD%E9%AB%98%E7%AD%89%E5%AD%A6%E6%A0%A1%E7%B7%8F%E5%90%88%E4%BD%93%E8%82%B2%E5%A4%A7%E4%BC%9A%E3%83%90%E3%83%89%E3%83%9F%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88%E3%83%B3%E7%AB%B6%E6%8A%80%E5%A4%A7%E4%BC%9A).[^cite_note-profilejsports-11] In 2014, at 17 years and 6 months old, she became the fourth high school student to win the All Japan Championships and the second-youngest winner in the tournament's history, after [Nozomi Okuhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nozomi_Okuhara).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Following her graduation in 2016, she joined the [Kumamoto Saishunkan badminton team](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%8F%E3%81%BE%E3%82%82%E3%81%A8%E5%86%8D%E6%98%A5%E9%A4%A8%E8%A3%BD%E8%96%AC%E6%89%80%E3%83%90%E3%83%89%E3%83%9F%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88%E3%83%B3%E3%83%81%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0).[^cite_note-15]

## Career

### 2012: International debut

Yamaguchi began her junior international career in 2012. At the [Asian Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Asian_Junior_Badminton_Championships), she won a bronze medal in [girls' singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Asian_Junior_Badminton_Championships_%E2%80%93_Girls_singles) and was part of the Japanese team that won the nation's first [mixed team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Asian_Junior_Badminton_Championships_%E2%80%93_Teams_event) title.[^cite_note-ajc12-16][^cite_note-ajt12-17] Later that year, she claimed a silver medal in [girls' singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_BWF_World_Junior_Championships_%E2%80%93_Girls_singles) and the [mixed team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_BWF_World_Junior_Championships_%E2%80%93_Teams_event) event at the [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_BWF_World_Junior_Championships).[^cite_note-wjc12-18] She also made her senior international debut that year at the [Osaka International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_International_\(badminton\)), where she upset the first seed [Bellaetrix Manuputty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellaetrix_Manuputty) in the first round, before losing to [Ayumi Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayumi_Mine) in the next round.[^cite_note-osa12-19]

### 2013: First Super Series title and World Junior champion

In 2013, Yamaguchi won her first [Super Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Super_Series) title at the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Japan_Super_Series). Advancing from the qualifying round, her victory at the age of 16 years, 3 months, and 16 days made her the youngest player ever to win a Super Series event.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-jpn13-21][^cite_note-profilejsports-11][^cite_note-profileIOC-22] This win also marked Japan's first title at the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Open_\(badminton\)) in any discipline in 32 years and only the second-ever Super Series women's singles title won by a Japanese player.[^cite_note-yomiurishimbun13-6] On the junior circuit, Yamaguchi won her first [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_BWF_World_Junior_Championships) title, defeating compatriot [Aya Ohori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aya_Ohori) in the final.[^cite_note-BWF13-23][^cite_note-wjc13-24] Her other results that year included a gold medal in mixed doubles at the Asian Youth Games, a bronze medal in women's singles at the East Asian Games, and runner-up finishes at both the [New Zealand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_Zealand_Open_Grand_Prix) and the Osaka International.[^cite_note-ayg13-25][^cite_note-nzl13-26][^cite_note-osa13-27] For her performance throughout the season, Yamaguchi was named the [BWF Most Promising Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Awards#Rising_Star_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-bwfawards13-28]

### 2014: Junior titles and senior breakthrough

In 2014, Yamaguchi successfully defended her [World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_BWF_World_Junior_Championships) title, becoming the first Japanese player in the tournament's history to win consecutive titles across all disciplines.[^cite_note-joc-wjc14-29][^cite_note-wjc14-30] That same year, she won the [Asian Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Asian_Junior_Badminton_Championships).[^cite_note-ajc14-31] She also contributed to Japan's mixed team bronze medals at both the World and Asian Junior Championships. At the [Youth Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2014_Summer_Youth_Olympics) in Nanjing, she served as the flag-bearer for the Japanese team and earned a silver medal in [girls' singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2014_Summer_Youth_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-yog14-33] On the senior circuit, she reached her first Super Series Premier final at the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_China_Open_Super_Series_Premier), finishing as runner-up after victories over [Wang Shixian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Shixian) and [Sun Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yu_\(badminton\)).[^cite_note-chn14-34] Her performance qualified her for the season-ending [BWF Super Series Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_BWF_Super_Series_Finals), where she reached the semifinals.[^cite_note-ssf14-35] She was also a member of the Japanese team that won the silver medal at the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup).[^cite_note-uber14-36] For the second consecutive year, she was named the [BWF Most Promising Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Awards#Rising_Star_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-BWFAwards14-37]

### 2015: First Grand Prix Gold title

In 2015, Yamaguchi won her first [BWF Grand Prix Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Grand_Prix_Gold_and_Grand_Prix) title at the [Bitburger Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Bitburger_Open_Grand_Prix_Gold), where she defeated [Busanan Ongbamrungphan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busanan_Ongbamrungphan) in the final.[^cite_note-bit15-38] She also finished as the runner-up at the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Japan_Super_Series) Super Series, losing the final to her compatriot Nozomi Okuhara.[^cite_note-jpn15-39] During the [Indonesian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Indonesia_Super_Series_Premier), she defeated the reigning Olympic champion, [Li Xuerui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Xuerui), in the second round.[^cite_note-ina15-40] In team competition, she helped Japan win the silver medal at the Sudirman Cup, which marked the country's first-ever silver medal at the event.[^cite_note-sudirman15-41]

### 2016: Olympic debut and two Super Series titles



Yamaguchi attending a prime ministerial reception for the Japanese Olympic delegation following the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics), August 2016.

Yamaguchi made her Olympic debut at the [2016 Rio Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics), where she was eliminated in the quarterfinals by her compatriot [Nozomi Okuhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nozomi_Okuhara).[^cite_note-og16-42] Following the Olympics, Yamaguchi won consecutive titles on the [Super Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Super_Series) circuit. In September, she captured her second career Super Series title at the [Korea Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Korea_Open_Super_Series), defeating [Sung Ji-hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sung_Ji-hyun) in the final.[^cite_note-kor16-43] The following month, she won the [Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Denmark_Super_Series_Premier) by defeating [Tai Tzu-ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tzu-ying) to claim her first Super Series Premier title.[^cite_note-olympic.org-44][^cite_note-den16-45] She concluded her season by qualifying for the year-end [Dubai Super Series Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_BWF_Super_Series_Finals), where she was eliminated in the round-robin stage. In team competition, she represented Japan at the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) in Kunshan, where the team finished with a bronze medal.[^cite_note-uber16-46]

### 2017: Season-ending title and Asian team gold

Yamaguchi won three titles in 2017: the [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_German_Open_Grand_Prix_Gold), the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_China_Open_Super_Series_Premier), and the season-ending [Dubai World Superseries Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BWF_Super_Series_Finals).[^cite_note-ger17-47][^cite_note-chn17-48] She also finished as the runner-up at the [Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Badminton_Asia_Championships), the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Australian_Super_Series), the [Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Denmark_Super_Series_Premier), and the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Super_Series).[^cite_note-bac17-49][^cite_note-aus17-50][^cite_note-den17-51][^cite_note-fra17-52] At the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BWF_World_Championships), she entered as the top seed but was eliminated in the third round.[^cite_note-bwc17-53] In team competition, she was part of the squad that won Japan's first [Asia Mixed Team Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Badminton_Asia_Mixed_Team_Championships) title.[^cite_note-batc17-54] She concluded the season as the year's highest-earning player, collecting US\$261,363 from 15 tournaments.[^cite_note-ssf17-55]

### 2018: World No. 1 and Uber Cup champion

On 19 April 2018, at the age of 20 years, 317 days, Yamaguchi became the first Japanese singles player to reach the world No. 1 ranking.[^cite_note-rank18-56] At major individual championships, she secured bronze medals at both the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_BWF_World_Championships) in Nanjing and the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2018_Asian_Games) in Jakarta, losing both semifinals to [P. V. Sindhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._V._Sindhu).[^cite_note-bwc18-57][^cite_note-ag18-58] On the [BWF World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_BWF_World_Tour), Yamaguchi won two titles and finished as a runner-up once. In March, she won the [German Open ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_German_Open) by defeating [Chen Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yufei) in the final.[^cite_note-ger18-59] She followed this by reaching her first [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_All_England_Open) final, finishing as the runner-up to [Tai Tzu-ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tzu-ying).[^cite_note-ae18-60] In October, she captured the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open_\(badminton\)) title, defeating Tai.[^cite_note-fra18-61] She concluded her season with a semifinal finish at the year-end [BWF World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_BWF_World_Tour_Finals).[^cite_note-wtfinals18-62]

In team competition, Yamaguchi won three gold medals with the Japanese national team. She was part of the squads that won the [Asia Team Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Badminton_Asia_Team_Championships) in Alor Setar and the Asian Games [women's team event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2018_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_team) in Jakarta.[^cite_note-agt18-63] She also contributed to Japan's gold medal at the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) in Bangkok, which marked the country's first title at the tournament in 37 years.[^cite_note-uber18-64]

### 2019: Asian Champion and return to World No. 1

In 2019, Yamaguchi became the first Japanese player to win the women's singles title at the [Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Badminton_Asia_Championships).[^cite_note-bac19-65][^cite_note-66] On the World Tour, she secured three titles: the [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_German_Open_\(badminton\)), her first Super 1000 title at the [Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indonesia_Open_\(badminton\)), and the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japan_Open).[^cite_note-ger19-67][^cite_note-ina19-68][^cite_note-jpn19-69] Her consecutive victories in Indonesia and Japan propelled her back to the World No. 1 ranking on 30 July.[^cite_note-70] She also finished as the runner-up at the [Malaysia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malaysia_Open) and earned a silver medal with the Japanese team at the [Sudirman Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sudirman_Cup).[^cite_note-mas19-71] The latter half of the season was disrupted by injuries; a lower back injury sustained after the Japan Open limited her preparation for the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BWF_World_Championships), resulting in a second-round exit.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-bwc19-73] She later suffered a right calf injury, forcing her withdrawal from the [Korea Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Korea_Open_\(badminton\)), before ending the season with a semifinal finish at the [BWF World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BWF_World_Tour_Finals).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-wtf19-75]

### 2020–2021: Olympic campaign and first World Championship title

In a 2020 season shortened by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Yamaguchi's primary achievement was winning the [Thailand Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Thailand_Masters_\(badminton\)).[^cite_note-tham20-76] In team competition, she was a member of the Japanese team that won the [Asian Women's team championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Badminton_Asia_Team_Championships).[^cite_note-batc20-77][^cite_note-78]

The following year, in 2021, Yamaguchi competed as the fourth seed at the [2020 Tokyo Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics), where she lost in the quarterfinals to P. V. Sindhu.[^cite_note-og20-79] On the BWF World Tour, she won two consecutive titles at the [Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Denmark_Open) and the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open), and finished as the runner-up at the [Indonesia Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Indonesia_Masters).[^cite_note-den21-80][^cite_note-fra21-81][^cite_note-inam21-82] In team competitions, she was a member of the Japanese squad that earned silver medals at both the [Sudirman Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Sudirman_Cup) and the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup). Yamaguchi ended the year by capturing her first world title at the [2021 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BWF_World_Championships) in Huelva, after defeating Tai Tzu-ying in the final. With this victory, she became the second Japanese women's singles player to win the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_World_Championships) after Nozomi Okuhara.[^cite_note-bwc21-83]

### 2022: Second World Championship title and All England champion

Yamaguchi's 2022 season was highlighted by defending her world title and winning her first [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_England_Open_Badminton_Championships) title. At major individual championships, she successfully defended her crown at the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in Tokyo, defeating [Chen Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yufei). Her victory at the World Championships made her the first Japanese women's singles player to win consecutive world titles.[^cite_note-bwc22-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Earlier in the season, she won a silver medal at the [Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Badminton_Asia_Championships) in Manila after losing to China's [Wang Zhiyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhiyi).[^cite_note-ac22-87] On the [World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Tour) Yamaguchi won three titles. In March, she captured her maiden [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_All_England_Open) title by defeating [An Se-young](60664879.md).[^cite_note-ae22-88] In September, she won the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Japan_Open), once again defeating An in the final.[^cite_note-jpn22-89] She concluded the season by winning the year-end [World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Tour_Finals) in Bangkok, defeating [Tai Tzu-ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tzu-ying).[^cite_note-wtf22-90] In team competition, she represented Japan at the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) in Bangkok, contributing to the squad's bronze medal finish. In recognition of her achievements, Yamaguchi was named the [BWF Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_Awards#Players_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-91]

### 2023: Five World Tour titles and season-ending injury

In 2023, Yamaguchi won five World Tour titles: the [Malaysia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Malaysia_Open_\(badminton\)), [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_German_Open_\(badminton\)), [Malaysia Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Malaysia_Masters), [Canada Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Canada_Open), and [Hong Kong Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hong_Kong_Open_\(badminton\)).[^cite_note-maso23-92][^cite_note-ger23-93][^cite_note-masm23-94][^cite_note-hkg23-95] She also finished as runner-up at the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_\(badminton\)), [Singapore Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Singapore_Open_\(badminton\)), and [India Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_India_Open).[^cite_note-chn23-96] Notably, five of her eight World Tour finals that season were played against [An Se-young](60664879.md); Yamaguchi defeated An to win the Malaysia and German Opens, but lost to her in the finals of the India, Singapore, and China Opens. At major individual championships, Yamaguchi secured bronze medals at both the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BWF_World_Championships) in Copenhagen and the [Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Badminton_Asia_Championships) in Dubai.[^cite_note-bwc23-97][^cite_note-ac23-98] In team events, she contributed to Japan's bronze medal finishes at the [Sudirman Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sudirman_Cup) and the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2022_Asian_Games). However, her season was curtailed by a right peroneal tendon injury sustained during the Asian Games in September, forcing her to retire from the match.[^cite_note-99] This injury subsequently led to her withdrawal from the rest of her scheduled tournaments, including the season-ending [World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BWF_World_Tour_Finals) in Hangzhou.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

### 2024: Olympic run and two home titles

After a three-and-a-half-month recovery from a right foot injury sustained in late 2023, Yamaguchi began her 2024 season in January.[^cite_note-102] She finished as the runner-up at consecutive tournaments, losing to [An Se-young](60664879.md) at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_\(badminton\)) and retiring against [Carolina Marín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Mar%C3%ADn) in the [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_All_England_Open) final due to a right hip injury.[^cite_note-fra24-103][^cite_note-ae24-104] This hip injury recurred at the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) in May, where she contributed to Japan's bronze medal. The injury continued to disrupt her Olympic preparations, leading to a withdrawal from the [Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indonesia_Open) in June and delaying her return to full-scale training until July, mere weeks before the Olympics.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

At the [2024 Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics), she reached the quarterfinals before losing to the eventual gold medalist, An Se-young. Following the Olympics, Yamaguchi secured two titles on home soil, defeating [Busanan Ongbamrungphan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busanan_Ongbamrungphan) to win the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Japan_Open) and [Gregoria Mariska Tunjung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregoria_Mariska_Tunjung) to claim the [Japan Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Japan_Masters).[^cite_note-jpn24-107][^cite_note-jpnm24-108] Her season concluded with another injury-forced retirement, this time due to a right calf injury, at the All Japan Championships in December.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

### 2025: Third World Championship title



Yamaguchi on the podium after winning her third women's singles title at the [2025 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BWF_World_Championships).

Returning from a calf injury that concluded her 2024 season, Yamaguchi's 2025 campaign was highlighted by a historic victory at the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BWF_World_Championships) in Paris. She secured her third women's singles world title by defeating [Chen Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yufei) in the final (21–9, 21–13).[^cite_note-bwc25-111] This victory made her the first Japanese player in any discipline to win three world championships titles.[^cite_note-worldchampionship252-112]

On the [World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BWF_World_Tour) Yamaguchi captured two Super 500 titles. She won her first tour title of the year at the [Korea Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Korea_Open_\(badminton\)) by defeating [An Se-young](60664879.md).[^cite_note-kor25-113] She followed this by winning the [Arctic Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Arctic_Open) with a straight-games victory over [Busanan Ongbamrungphan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busanan_Ongbamrungphan).[^cite_note-arc25-114] She displayed consistent form throughout the season, reaching the semifinals of six tournaments she entered. This run included the season-ending [World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BWF_World_Tour_Finals), as well as three Super 1000 events: the [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_All_England_Open), the [Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indonesia_Open), and the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China_Open_\(badminton\)). In team events, she represented Japan at the [Sudirman Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Sudirman_Cup) in Xiamen, contributing to the squad's bronze medal finish.[^cite_note-sudirman25-115]

### 2026: Three titles in six consecutive finals and silver at the World Championship

Yamaguchi began her 2026 season in January at the [Malaysia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Malaysia_Open_\(badminton\)), reaching the quarterfinals before retiring from her match against India's [P. V. Sindhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._V._Sindhu) due to a right knee injury.[^cite_note-mas26-116][^cite_note-ae26intv-117] In March, despite limited practice time and the lingering effects of her knee injury, she advanced to the semifinals of the [All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_All_England_Open), losing to the eventual champion, [Wang Zhiyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhiyi).[^cite_note-ae26intv-117][^cite_note-ae26-118] The following month, Yamaguchi won a bronze medal at the [Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Badminton_Asia_Championships) in [Ningbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningbo) after losing the semifinal to Wang.[^cite_note-ac26-119][^cite_note-ac26intv-120] Later that month, she represented Japan at the [Uber Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Thomas_&_Uber_Cup) in [Horsens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsens), contributing to the team's bronze medal finish.[^cite_note-uber26-121]

Between May and July, Yamaguchi reached six consecutive tournament finals.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-chnop26f-123] She won her first title of the year at the Super 500 [Thailand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Thailand_Open_\(badminton\)) by defeating [Chen Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yufei).[^cite_note-tha26-124] This victory was followed by consecutive runner-up finishes at the [Singapore Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Singapore_Open_\(badminton\)) and the [Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Indonesia_Open), where she lost both finals to [An Se-young](60664879.md).[^cite_note-sgp26-125][^cite_note-ina26-126] She then secured her second title of the season at the Super 500 [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_\(badminton\)), defeating [Pornpawee Chochuwong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornpawee_Chochuwong).[^cite_note-aus26-127] After finishing as the runner-up to Sindhu at the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Japan_Open), Yamaguchi won the Super 1000 title at the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_China_Open_\(badminton\)) with another victory over Chen.[^cite_note-jpn26-128][^cite_note-chnop26-129] At the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BWF_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in New Delhi, Yamaguchi won the silver medal after losing the final to An.[^cite_note-wc26-130] In September, she helped Japan earn a silver medal in the [women's team event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2026_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_team) at the [2026 Asian She ultimately won silver after losing to An in the final.[^cite_note-ag26-133]

## Rivalries

### An Se-young

Yamaguchi maintains a prominent rivalry with South Korea's [An Se-young](60664879.md), with An leading their head-to-head record 23–15 across 38 encounters. Yamaguchi's victories over An in tournament finals include the [2020 Thailand Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Thailand_Masters_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-tham20-76], [2021 Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Denmark_Open)[^cite_note-den21-80], [2022 All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_All_England_Open)[^cite_note-ae22-88], [2022 Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Japan_Open)[^cite_note-jpn22-89], [2023 Malaysia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Malaysia_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-maso23-92], [2023 German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_German_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-ger23-93], and the [2025 Korea Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Korea_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-kor25-113]. Conversely, An has won several finals against Yamaguchi, capturing titles at the [2021 Indonesia Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Indonesia_Masters)[^cite_note-inam21-82], [2023 India Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_India_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-india23-134], [2023 Singapore Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Singapore_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-sgp23-135], [2023 China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-chn23-96], [2024 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-fra24-103], [2026 Singapore Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Singapore_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-sgp26-125], and [2026 Indonesia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Indonesia_Open)[^cite_note-ina26-126]. Their encounters at major championships include the quarterfinals of the [2024 Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics), where An defeated Yamaguchi en route to winning the gold medal. Yamaguchi has defeated An in two [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_World_Championships) matches: in the [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BWF_World_Championships) quarterfinals and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Championships) semifinals, en route to her world titles.

### Chen Yufei

Yamaguchi has a long-standing rivalry with China's [Chen Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Yufei), leading their head-to-head record 24–14 across 38 encounters. Their most significant matches include two [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWF_World_Championships) finals, where Yamaguchi defeated Chen to secure the gold medal in both the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Championships) Tokyo[^cite_note-bwc22-84] and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BWF_World_Championships) Paris editions[^cite_note-bwc25-111]. Yamaguchi has also defeated Chen in the finals of the [2018 German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_German_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-ger18-59], the [2026 Thailand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Thailand_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-tha26-124], and the [2026 China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_China_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-chnop26-129]. Conversely, Chen has secured several victories against Yamaguchi, including an upset win over the top-seeded Yamaguchi in the third round of the [2017 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BWF_World_Championships), as well as semifinal victories at the [2019 World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BWF_World_Tour_Finals), [2023 All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_All_England_Open)[^cite_note-ae23-136], and [2023 China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_\(badminton\)). They have also frequently represented Japan and China as top singles players in major team events; notably, Chen defeated Yamaguchi in the final of the [2019 Sudirman Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sudirman_Cup) to help China claim the title.[^cite_note-sud19ws-137]

### Nozomi Okuhara

Yamaguchi shares a domestic rivalry with compatriot [Nozomi Okuhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nozomi_Okuhara), with Okuhara leading their head-to-head record 12–9 across 21 encounters. Their rivalry began in their junior careers, with Okuhara defeating Yamaguchi in the final of the [2012 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_BWF_World_Junior_Championships)[^cite_note-wjc12-18]. In their senior careers, Okuhara achieved key victories, including the quarterfinal of the [2016 Rio Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics)[^cite_note-og16-42] and the finals of the [2015 Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Japan_Super_Series)[^cite_note-jpn15-39] and [2017 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Australian_Super_Series)[^cite_note-aus17-50]. Yamaguchi's significant wins include defeating Okuhara in the [2019 Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japan_Open_\(badminton\)) final[^cite_note-jpn19-69]. Their matches have frequently determined the top position in Japanese women's singles.

### Tai Tzu-ying

Yamaguchi has a closely contested rivalry with Chinese Taipei's [Tai Tzu-ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tzu-ying), with Tai leading their head-to-head record 13–11 in 24 encounters. Yamaguchi's notable victories against Tai in finals include the [2021 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BWF_World_Championships)[^cite_note-bwc21-83], the [2022 World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BWF_World_Tour_Finals)[^cite_note-wtf22-90], the [2016 Denmark Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Denmark_Super_Series_Premier)[^cite_note-den16-45], and the [2018 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-fra18-61]. Conversely, Tai has defeated Yamaguchi in the finals of the [2017 Asian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Badminton_Asia_Championships)[^cite_note-bac17-49], the [2017 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Super_Series)[^cite_note-fra17-52], the [2018 All England Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_All_England_Open)[^cite_note-ae18-60], and the [2019 Malaysia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malaysia_Open_\(badminton\))[^cite_note-mas19-71].

## Playing style

Chinese player [Sun Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yu_\(badminton\)) described Yamaguchi's style, noting that despite her small stature, she is diligent, swift, and adept at retrieving difficult shots that others typically cannot save. Sun Yu characterized her play as based on patience and consistent performance,  observing that she makes few mistakes and capitalizes on opponents' rushed errors. Another Chinese player, [Wang Shixian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Shixian), concurred that Yamaguchi is quick and nimble, suggesting challengers must match her speed.[^cite_note-138]

In 2014, Chinese team head coach [Li Yongbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Yongbo) commented that Yamaguchi plays with patience and quality. Regarding her short stature and potential for further development, he stated that badminton talent is not limited by height restrictions, and her skill is sufficient to compensate for her height. He added that her future would depend on her performance in the coming year.[^cite_note-139]
