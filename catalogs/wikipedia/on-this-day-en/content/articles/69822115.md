An [indirect election](652583.md) took place in [Estonia](28222445.md) on 2 September 2026 to elect the [president of Estonia](1581712.md), who is the country's [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state).[^cite_note-1] The [Riigikogu](794927.md) elected [Ülle Madise](55279031.md) to serve in the office and she will be sworn in as the 7th president on 12 October 2026.[^cite_note-2]

The incumbent president, [Alar Karis](67085807.md), had served one term and was eligible to seek a second consecutive term, but announced in June 2026 that he would not stand for re-election.[^cite_note-3]

## Process

By law, the president of Estonia is indirectly elected. The [Riigikogu](794927.md) has the task of electing the president in the first instance, in up to three rounds. If no candidate receives the required supermajority of two-thirds (68 votes out of 101), the election for president then moves to an electoral college consisting of 101 MPs and 107 representatives of local (municipal) governments. If the electoral college fails to achieve a majority in two rounds, the process is to return to the parliament yet again to restart.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

An incumbent president who has served one term is eligible to seek a second consecutive term in office, while a former president who has completed two consecutive terms may seek non-consecutive election after a break.[^cite_note-6]

## Candidates

Individuals must be nominated by one-fifth of the members of Riigikogu (21 MPs) in order to officially stand as candidates.[^cite_note-7]

As part of changes adopted before the election, candidates were to be set up 12 to nine days before voting instead of four to two as before. Nominated candidates are also now expected to give a speech of up to 10 minutes detailing their positions.[^cite_note-8]
