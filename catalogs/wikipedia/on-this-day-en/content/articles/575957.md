The **National Intelligence Organization** ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Millî İstihbarat Teşkilatı*), also known by its Turkish initials **MİT** or colloquially as **the Organization** (*Teşkilat*), is a national civilian intelligence and security agency of the Turkish government tasked with advancing national security through collecting and analyzing intelligence from around the world. This is done through conducting analysis development, intelligence gathering, and counterintelligence systems to create national security. MIT is involved in clandestine and covert operations, countering hybrid threats, counterintelligence, counterterrorism, creating a civilian security network intelligence, supporting hybrid warfare operations, and assessing threats to national security.&#x20;

MIT gathers information for the [Presidency](3680197.md) and the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md) (TSK) about current and potential threats from inside and outside against all elements that make up Turkiye’s integrity, constitutional order, existence, independence, security and national power and takes precautions when necessary.

&#x20;MIT is under the provision of the Presidency. It works closely with the [Turkish diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Turkey) overseas.

## History

### Special Organization

Further information: [Special Organization (Ottoman Empire)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Organization_\(Ottoman_Empire\))

Originally organized under the [Ministry of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_War_\(Ottoman_Empire\)), the organization was shifted to answer directly to the ruling party [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) (CUP) in February 1915.[^cite_note-6] It was led by [Bahaeddin Şakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahaeddin_%C5%9Eakir) and [Nazım Bey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naz%C4%B1m_Bey). The exact date of establishment is unclear or disputed. Its establishment date is rather vague since it was really a continuation of various smaller groups established by Enver Pasha and friends in the aftermath of 1908 [Young Turk Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Turk_Revolution).[^cite_note-7] Some provincial bands in Eastern Anatolia directly integrated Kurdish [Hamidiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamidiye_\(cavalry\)) units.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAkçam2007136-8]

During [World War I](4764461.md) Eşref Sencer Kuşçubaşı was allegedly the director of operations in Arabia, the Sinai, and North Africa.[^cite_note-9] He was captured in Yemen in early 1917 by the British military and was a [POW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW) in Malta until 1920 and subsequently released in exchange for British POWs.[^cite_note-vatanhaini-10] In Libya, [Nuri Killigil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuri_Killigil) organized operations involving propaganda, subversion, terrorism and sabotage; he coordinated these operations with the [Senussi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senussi).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Karakol Society

Further information: [Karakol society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakol_society)

The Karakol Society was founded in November 1918 as the first clandestine organization fighting against the [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) [Occupation of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Constantinople). It served as a continuation of the [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress)' intelligence agency, the [Special Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Organization_\(Ottoman_Empire\)), with the majority of its members coming from the latter.

Karakol created a line of communication and transportation between Constantinople and Anatolia, smuggling volunteers, weapons, and armaments into the latter. Karakol representatives took part in the [Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum_Congress) and [Sivas Congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sivas_Congress), where they supported the unification of various resistance organizations under the banner of the [Association for the Defense of the Rights of Anatolia and Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_the_Defense_of_the_Rights_of_Anatolia_and_Rumelia) (ADRAR), and Karakol went on to publish the [Amasya Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasya_Protocol).&#x20;

### National Security Service

Further information: [National Security Service (Turkey)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Service_\(Turkey\))

It was established at a time when [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) was purging [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) elements, including the [Karakol society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakol_society) and [Teşkilât-ı Mahsûsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Organization_\(Ottoman_Empire\)) ("Special Organization") intelligence organizations.[^cite_note-tarihce-13][^cite_note-yanlis-14] The first director of the MAH was [Şükrü Âli Ögel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9E%C3%BCkr%C3%BC_%C3%82li_%C3%96gel) (1886–1973). During [World War II](32927.md), Turkey saw increased [espionage](10501.md) by [British](31717.md), [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and [German](11867.md) operatives and sympathizers. The MAH learned that [Nazi Germany](21212.md) would not attempt to invade Turkey, allowing the [İnönü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) administration to resist mounting [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) pressure to declare war on Germany. The MAH would go on to receive significant financial support from the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) during the [Cold war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_war).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] One of the MAH directors was [Celal Tevfik Karasapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celal_Tevfik_Karasapan) who held the post between 1959 and 1960.[^cite_note-17]

## Organization

### Organizational structure

The Organization is headed by a Director-General, who is appointed by the [President of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkey).[^cite_note-18] The Director-General is typically a career intelligence officer with past experience in intelligence and security matters.[^cite_note-19]

The Organization is divided into various departments and units, each specializing in specific areas of intelligence, counterterrorism, and analysis. These units include the Counterterrorism Department, Foreign Relations Department, Signals Intelligence Department, and more.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

On 9 February 2023 the National Intelligence Academy (NIA), in Turkish *Milli İstihbarat Akademisi*, was founded and began its activities on 6 January 2024. It is an institution within MIT with the purpose "to provide postgraduate education and conduct scientific research in intelligence, security and strategy."[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Connections to the Turkish military

According to the former director of Foreign Operations, Yavuz Ataç, the military presence in the organization is negligible,[^cite_note-24] although the organization has a military heritage. In 1990, the percentage of military personnel was 35%, while in 2007 the lower echelons were 4.5% military.[^cite_note-entrika-25] While the organization has historically recruited from relatives of existing employees,[^cite_note-26] the former director, [Emre Taner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emre_Taner), says that this is no longer the case.[^cite_note-27]

### Duties

MIT is in charge of collecting intelligence on existing and potential threats from internal and external sources posed against the territory, people and integrity, the existence, independence, security, and all the other elements that compose the constitutional order and the national power of the [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey). The MİT is in charge of communicating this intelligence to the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkey), the [Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chiefs_of_the_Turkish_General_Staff), the Secretary General of the [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(Turkey\)) and other relevant state organizations as necessary.

MIT is in charge of counterintelligence activities in Turkey.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Legally, it cannot be given any other duty and cannot be led to any other field of activity than collecting intelligence concerning the security of the Republic. The MİT engages in [cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare). The [Turkish Ministry of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defence_\(Turkey\)) considers [cybersecurity](7398.md) as the country's "fifth frontier" after land, air, sea and space. The MİT uses local cybersecurity solutions mostly developed by companies like [HAVELSAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAVELSAN) and the [Scientific and Technological Research Council of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_and_Technological_Research_Council_of_Turkey).

## Activities

In 2014, it was revealed in the Turkey's Parliament's Internal Affairs Commission that the MİT has units working abroad and was wiretapping the telephones of 2,473 people, mostly related to "terrorism and spying activities."[^cite_note-31]

On 5 April 2018, Turkish Deputy Prime Minister [Bekir Bozdağ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bekir_Bozda%C4%9F) said that MIT has captured 80 Turkish citizens, suspected of links to [FETÖ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fethullah%C3%A7%C4%B1_Ter%C3%B6r_%C3%96rg%C3%BCt%C3%BC), from 18 different countries so far.[^cite_note-32]

On 16 October 2020, according to Reuters, Turkey's intelligence officials arrested a man, suspected of spying on foreign Arab nationals on behalf of the United Arab Emirates (UAE). The officials said that the man entered Turkey using a non-UAE passport. The detainee confessed to the charges and Turkish authorities obtained "a trove of documents" from him showing UAE affiliation, but there were no reactions received from the UAE.[^cite_note-33] In 2019, Turkey arrested two men suspected of spying on Arab nationals for the UAE. One of the men was connected to the murder of Saudi journalist [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi).[^cite_note-34]

In 2022, MIT participated and helped in the [prisoner swap](17209653.md) between Konstantin Yaroshenko (Russia) and Trevor Reed (USA) which took place in Istanbul.[^cite_note-35]

On 25 December 2025, the MIT arrested 115 Islamic State suspects 'planning New Year's attacks'. The arrests come 2 days after the Turkish intelligence agents carried out a raid against IS on the Pakistan-Afghanistan border.[^cite_note-36]

### Africa

#### Angola

Turkish judicial documents revealed that the National Intelligence Organization spied on Turkish nationals in Angola. They were supporters of the [Gülen movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BClen_movement).[^cite_note-37]

#### Egypt

On 22 November 2017, Egypt's public prosecutor ordered the detention of 29 people suspected of espionage on behalf of Turkey against [Egypt](8087628.md)'s national interests and joining a terrorist organization. They were also accused of [money laundering](19390.md), conducting overseas calls without a license and trading currency without a license. According to the results of an investigation by the [General Intelligence Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Intelligence_Service_\(Egypt\)), the group had been recording phone calls and passing information to Turkish intelligence as part of a plan to bring the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) back to power in Egypt.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

#### Gabon

In March 2018, MIT abducted three suspected members of the Gülen Movement from Gabon and transferred them from [Libreville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libreville) to [Ankara](802.md) on a private jet.[^cite_note-40]

#### Kenya

MIT captured [Abdullah Öcalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_%C3%96calan) in Kenya on 15 February 1999, while being transferred from the Greek embassy to [Jomo Kenyatta International Airport](142547.md) in [Nairobi](21482.md), reportedly with the help of the CIA.[^cite_note-nytimes-capture-41] He was then forcibly transferred to Turkey and [imprisoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprisonment_of_Abdullah_%C3%96calan) on [Imrali island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0mral%C4%B1) where he faced [trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_Abdullah_%C3%96calan). Also, MIT has rescued an Italian citizen, [Silvia Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvia_Romano?action=edit\&redlink=1), who has been kidnapped in Kenya on 20 November 2018, and taken to Somalia.[^cite_note-42] On 31 May 2021, the MIT arrested in Kenya Selahaddin Gülen, who is a nephew of Fetullah Gülen, and brought him to Turkey.[^cite_note-43]

#### Libya

Further information: [Turkish military intervention in the Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_military_intervention_in_the_Second_Libyan_Civil_War)

In August 2019, a report was published, which stated that MIT was operating within the capital Tripoli in order to support the [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (which is UN recognized).[^cite_note-44] MIT personnel arrived in Libya before any Turkish military unit was sent.[^cite_note-45]

In September 2020, five Turkish journalists were jailed in Turkey after revealing documents about the Turkish intelligence activities in Libya. Three of them released in February 2022.[^cite_note-46]

Even after the 2020 ceasefire agreement, Turkish intelligence held multiple meetings with authorities in both eastern and western Libya.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Starting in 2024, Turkey and eastern Libya began to shift their positions and move closer together.[^cite_note-52] During that period, the head of MIT held meetings with Eastern Libyan leaders, including [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) and his sons.[^cite_note-53]

#### Sudan

In a joint operation of MIT and [National Intelligence and Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Intelligence_Service_\(Sudan\)) (NISS), a man believed to be a chief financier for the FETÖ, was captured and transported to Turkey.[^cite_note-54]

### Americas

#### United States

In 2018, the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) charged [Kamil Ekim Alptekin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamil_Ekim_Alptekin) and Bijan Rafiekian with acting as illegal agents of Turkey in the United States.[^cite_note-55]

In July 2019, it was reported that Turkish diplomats with the support of MIT, had extensively spied on critics of the government of Turkey. Among the organizations that were spied on, were schools, companies, non-governmental and not-for-profit organizations and foundations located in [New York](8210131.md), [Washington D.C.](108956.md), [Georgia](48830.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [Texas](29810.md) and [Chicago](6886.md).[^cite_note-56]

### Asia

#### Armenia

In 2002, Armenian special services arrested an Armenian government official on charges of spying for Turkish national intelligence.[^cite_note-57] He was sentenced to 10 years in prison after being convicted of spying for Turkey.[^cite_note-58]

#### Azerbaijan

In 2018, MIT arrested a FETÖ member in Azerbaijan and brought him to Turkey. He was a teacher working for a school in Azerbaijan which was operated by FETÖ .[^cite_note-59]

#### Gaza

MIT played an active role in addressing the situation during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), engaging with all relevant actors. According to the head of the agency, its efforts focused on securing a permanent ceasefire, ensuring the delivery of humanitarian aid, facilitating prisoner exchanges, supporting Palestinian internal reconciliation and advancing the two-state solution.[^cite_note-60]

#### Georgia

According to a published news report, operatives of Turkish counterterrorism unit and MIT assigned to the Turkish Embassy had engaged in a large-scale spying campaign on FETÖ-linked organisations and foundations.[^cite_note-61]

#### Iraq

In August 2017, the [Kurdistan Communities Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Communities_Union) said that had captured two Turkish nationals in [Sulaymaniyah](653537.md), Iraq; the Kurdish group said both individuals captured were working for the MIT. The plan of the two captured Turks, according to [NRT News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRT_News), was to assassinate a senior [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK) figure.[^cite_note-62]

In August 2018, it was announced that the PKK leader [Ismail Özden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smail_%C3%96zden) and 4 other militants of the [Sinjar Resistance Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjar_Resistance_Units) (YBŞ) were killed in Turkish airstrikes in [Sinjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjar). The [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md) carried out the joint operation with the MIT.[^cite_note-63]

In April 2019, 4 PKK militants were nabbed at Iraq's [Mount Sinjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjar_Mountains) and were brought to Turkey as part of a joint operation by the MIT and Turkish Armed forces.[^cite_note-64]

In June 2019, Diyar Gharib Muhammed — one of seven members of the PKK's Central Committee was Killed. A Turkish F-16 struck his vehicle with the assistance of human intelligence provided by National Intelligence Organization (MIT) in the Kortek Bend area of Qandil in northern Iraq.[^cite_note-65]

In August 2019, 4 PKK militants were killed in an airstrike by Turkish warplane in a joint operation with MIT in the Qandil area of northern Iraq.[^cite_note-66]

In September 2019, 3 PKK militants were killed in an airstrike by Turkish warplane in a joint operation with MIT in the Gara region of northern Iraq.[^cite_note-67] The same month the MIT in a joint operation, in northern Iraq's Qandil region, with the Turkish Armed Forces neutralized a senior female PKK member together with her driver.[^cite_note-68]

In January 2025, MIT neutralized Islam Dotkanlou, a top figure in the PKK's Iranian branch, along with his bodyguard during an operation carried out in Iraq.[^cite_note-69]

#### Malaysia

A Turkish teacher in Malaysia was abducted in 2016 from Kuala Lumpur over alleged links to the Gülen movement. According to reports he has been subjected to beating, torture, death threats and staged executions during his pretrial detention in Ankara.[^cite_note-70]

In August 2019, MIT arrested the Malaysia chief of the FETÖ in an operation and brought him back to Turkey. The person was wanted by Turkey on charges of being involved in the activities of terrorist organization in multiple countries.[^cite_note-71]

#### Mongolia

In July 2018, a Turkish teacher with links to the Gulenist movement was allegedly kidnapped in the Mongolian capital and taken to the city's airport. He was released after authorities temporarily grounded the airplane, which was operated by the Turkish Air Force.[^cite_note-72]

#### Myanmar

Turkish judicial documents revealed that Turkish nationals at Myanmar, who were Gülen supporters, were spied.[^cite_note-73]

#### Pakistan

In September 2017, in a joint operation of MIT, [ISI](2159021.md) and [Punjab Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Police_\(Pakistan\)), A prominent Turkish teacher and his family were arrested and brought to Turkey. He was vice president of FETÖ-affiliated educational foundation operating in Pakistan.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

#### Syria

See also: [Turkish involvement in the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_involvement_in_the_Syrian_Civil_War)

After the outbreak of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) in 2011, the intelligence services of several NATO countries began operating near the [Syria-Turkey border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria-Turkey_border), coordinated by MIT. While operating on the Turkish side, all initiatives had to be cleared first with MIT.[^cite_note-76]

The [2014 National Intelligence Organisation scandal in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_National_Intelligence_Organisation_scandal_in_Turkey) revealed MIT's role in supplying arms to Syrian rebels in Syria.[^cite_note-reuters_exclusive-77] The Turkish journalists who exposed this arrangement were charged with espionage and "divulging state secrets" by the Turkish courts.[^cite_note-78] One of the journalists claimed:'Those who sent the convoy from Turkey knew that the weapons were "heading to end \[up] in [ISIS](9087364.md) hands"'. [Turkish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Army) officers who intercepted some of the intelligence agency's weapons-filled trucks also faced espionage charges.[^cite_note-reuters_exclusive-77] In June 2019, a Turkish court sentenced the group of officers and prosecutors who stopped the MIT trucks to at least two decades behind bars for obtaining and disclosing confidential state documents. They were also accused of being [FETÖ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fethullah%C3%A7%C4%B1_Ter%C3%B6r_%C3%96rg%C3%BCt%C3%BC) members.[^cite_note-79] The Turkish newspaper *[Cumhuriyet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumhuriyet)* published video footage which it said showed security forces discovering weapons parts being sent to Syria on trucks belonging to MIT.[^cite_note-80] In December 2020, Turkish court sentenced to prison 27 people because they stopped the MIT trucks in 2014.[^cite_note-81]

In October 2014, an Egyptian official said that MIT was helping the ISIS with satellite images and other critical data.[^cite_note-82]

In October 2014, [Press TV](10945963.md) journalist [Serena Shim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Shim) was killed when the car she was travelling in collided with a heavy vehicle in [Suruç](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suru%C3%A7) in what are claimed, by her employer and parents, to be suspicious circumstances. The car crash happened just days after Shim reported that the MIT had threatened her and accused her of spying because of her reporting on Turkey's stance on [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) militants during the [siege of Kobane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kobane). She also claimed that she had received images of ISIL militants crossing the Turkish border into Syria in [World Food Programme](33582.md) and [NGO](46539.md) trucks.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

On 18 March 2016, Russia's UN Ambassador [Vitaly Churkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Churkin) sent a letter to the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) saying that three Turkish humanitarian organizations sent weapons and supplies to extremists in Syria on behalf of MIT. The three NGOs were the [Besar Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besar_Foundation?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Iyilikder Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyilikder_Foundation?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Foundation for Human Rights and Freedoms (IHH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHH_\(Turkish_NGO\)).[^cite_note-86] In a 2018 interview, former [Turkish National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Police) official [Ahmet Yayla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmet_Yayla?action=edit\&redlink=1) said that the MIT has used Turkey's IHH as an intermediary to arm Islamist terrorists.[^cite_note-87]

In 2016, one of the commanders of the Syrian Turkmen rebel group [Seljuk Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Brigade) in Syria, Hani al-Mulla, was assassinated. Suspicion ranged from the involvement of Turkish intelligence and Turkish-backed rebels to [ISIS](9087364.md).[^cite_note-88]

Since at least 2016 MIT played a role in helping what is now [Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham](53015755.md) separate from its ISIS and [al-Qaeda](1921.md) past. MIT continued to liaison with HTS as the group consolidated control in [Idlib](7445043.md) province, a significant Syrian city and province that borders Turkey.[^cite_note-89]

MİT played an active role in the [Turkish military operation in Afrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_military_operation_in_Afrin), including the coordination and direction of the [Syrian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Army), as well as intelligence support in the identification of targets for [Turkish Air Force](1799517.md) airstrikes, and post-destruction evaluation.[^cite_note-90]

According to documents revealed in 2019, the MIT was secretly transported ammunition and fighters into Syria with buses in 2015.[^cite_note-91]

In May 2019, Syria's [Military Intelligence Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Directorate_\(Syria\)) officials met with Turkish MIT intelligence officials including MIT's head [Hakan Fidan](39996069.md), despite hostilities between the Syrian and Turkish governments. Turkey's ruling [Justice and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_\(Turkey\)) (AKP) released a statement saying the meeting was held to prevent a conflict between Syria and Turkey. AKP spokesperson Omer Celik defended the meeting, saying "Our intelligence agencies and our elements in the field can hold any meeting they like at any time they like to prevent some humanitarian tragedies or in line with some needs."[^cite_note-92]

In 2020, [European Union Agency for Asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_Asylum) reported that a lot of the armed opposition groups in Syria are under the near-total control of MIT and the Turkish Ministry of Defence.[^cite_note-93]

[Ibrahim Kalın](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Kal%C4%B1n), the head of MIT, stated in the 2025 annual report that MIT was actively involved in the Syrian crisis from its beginning to its resolution and during the process of building a new state, had taken proactive measures to counter potential threats to Turkey that might emerge in Syria.[^cite_note-94]

### Europe

#### Austria

In 2017, [Peter Pilz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pilz) released a report about the activities of Turkish agents operating through ATIB (Avusturya Türkiye İslam Birliği – Austria Turkey Islamic Foundation), the Diyanet's arm responsible for administering religious affairs across 63 mosques in the country, and other Turkish organizations. Pilz's website faced a [DDoS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDoS) attack by Turkish hacktivists and heavy security was provided when he presented the report publicly. Per the report, Turkey operates a clandestine network of 200 informants targeting opposition as well as Gülen supporters inside Austria.[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-95]

In September 2020, a Turkish spy was arrested and confessed. In addition, the Austria's interior minister said that there are indications that Turkey's secret service tried to recruit Austrian citizens and that [Europol](525329.md) and the European Council had been informed. Turkey rejected the accusations.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

In October 2020, a Turkish spy turned himself in to Austrian police and claimed that MIT had plans to attack the Austrian politicians, [Aygül Berivan Aslan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayg%C3%BCl_Berivan_Aslan) and Peter Pilz. In addition, he said that at the past he had been forced to give a false testimony which convicted Metin Topuz, an employee at the American Consulate in Istanbul. Turkey denied accusations.[^cite_note-98]

#### Belgium

In 2017, the [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people) interior minister, [Liesbeth Homans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liesbeth_Homans), started the process of withdrawing recognition of the Turkish-sponsored and country's second largest mosque, Fatih mosque in [Beringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringen,_Belgium) accusing the mosque of spying in favor of Turkey.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

**Cyprus**

In 1994, a prominent [Greek Cypriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Cypriots) supporter of [Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan), Theophilos Georgiadis, was murdered outside his house in [Aglandjia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aglandjia), [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia) by a Greek Cypriot who was likely paid off by Turkish intelligence, using a Turkish Army officers gun.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

According to reports, partially confirmed by [Cyprus Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Police), there have been migrants crossing into the government controlled side of [Cyprus](5593.md) with links to terrorist organisations and Turkish Intelligence with a Police spokesperson saying that the suspected individuals are under constant surveillance by the intelligence apparatus.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

#### Czech Republic

Judicial documents revealed that critics of the Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan were spied upon in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-105]

#### France

MIT was blamed for the [2013 murders of three PKK activists in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_murder_of_Kurds_activists_in_Paris).[^cite_note-independent.co.uk-106]

#### Germany

In July 2015 The [Tagesspiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagesspiegel) newspaper reported that German federal prosecutors were looking into claims that three men - two Turks and a German national - were instructed by MIT to spy on Erdogan critics in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), particularly [Kurds](17068.md) and members of the Muslim minority [Alevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alevi) community.[^cite_note-auto-107]

In 2016, Bundestag intelligence oversight committee members demanded answer from German government about the reports that Germans of Turkish origin are being pressured in Germany by informers and officers of Turkey's MIT spy agency. According to reports Turkey had 6,000 informants plus MIT officers in Germany who were putting pressure on "German Turks". [Hans-Christian Ströbele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Christian_Str%C3%B6bele) told that there was an "unbelievable" level of "secret activities" in Germany by Turkey's MIT agency. According to Erich Schmidt-Eenboom, not even the former communist [East German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German) [Stasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stasi) secret police had managed to run such a large "army of agents" in the former [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany): "Here, it's not just about intelligence gathering, but increasingly about intelligence service repression."[^cite_note-auto-107] German lawmakers have called for an investigation, charging that Turkey is spying on suspected Gulen followers in Germany.[^cite_note-auto2-108]

In December 2016, a 31-year-old Turkish citizen who had resided in Germany for a decade was arrested in Hamburg on suspicion of espionage and plotting the assassination of two prominent Kurds on behalf of Turkish security services.[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-95]

In March 2017 the Turkish secret intelligence service was accused of conducting espionage of more than 300 people and 200 associations and schools linked to supporters of exiled [Fethullah Gülen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fethullah_G%C3%BClen). [Boris Pistorius](69097811.md), interior minister for [Lower Saxony](18435.md) State, called this "intolerable and unacceptable", stating that "the intensity and ruthlessness with which people abroad are being investigated is remarkable". A German security official said that "we are horrified at how openly Turkey reveals that it is spying on Turks living here".[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] On 30 March 2017 Interior Minister [Thomas de Maiziere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_de_Maiziere) expresses suspicions that the move may have been intended to weigh on Turkish-German relations − "to provoke us in some way".[^cite_note-112] The appallment was deepened when it was revealed that the 300 persons included politicians, including [Michelle Müntefering](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle%20M%C3%BCntefering).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

In June 2017, according to news reports, Turkish Intelligence had reportedly been collecting an increasing amount of information on [Bundestag](3768.md) members who are involved in defense as well as domestic and foreign policy. A spokeswoman for [Bundeskriminalamt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Criminal_Police_Office_\(Germany\)) confirmed the report that the agency met with German MPs "in recent weeks" to discuss "safety" concerns, since "protecting members of the Bundestag is a legal task of the BKA".[^cite_note-116]

In October 2017, according to German press reports officials working in Germany's immigration authorities pass on information about Turkish asylum seekers to Turkey. In many cases, even their locations were also revealed, that even their families did not know for security reasons. These incidents showed that Turkish spies may have infiltrated German authorities.[^cite_note-117] In addition, [Herbert Reul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Reul), the interior minister for the German state of the [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), submitted a report to the state parliament, alleging that the Turkish-German organisation *[Osmanen Germania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmanen_Germania)* works with MIT. The organisation denied the accusations.[^cite_note-118] In July 2018, Germany banned the organisation on allegations it is involved in organized crime and represents a threat to the general public.[^cite_note-119]

In October 2021, German authorities arrested a Turk in a hotel at [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) for spying on behalf of Turkey. Police found a pistol, ammunition and a list with the names Gulen supporters.[^cite_note-120]

The 2024 report from the [Germany’s federal domestic intelligence agency (BfV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Office_for_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution) revealed that Turkish intelligence services continue to operate extensively in Germany, primarily engaging in transnational repression. Their main targets include followers of the Gulen movement, supporters of the PKK and individuals or organizations labeled as “enemies of the state” by Turkey. In addition, Turkish organizations in Germany are involved in influence operations aimed at Turkish communities, with the goal of affecting political decision-making processes in Germany. The report highlights the Union of International Democrats (UID), based in Cologne and founded in 2004, as the most prominent state-affiliated organization carrying out such influence activities on behalf of Turkey.[^cite_note-121]

According to the 2026 report of the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution, the Turkish National Intelligence Organization was one of the four most active foreign intelligence services operating in Berlin, the others being those of Russia, China and Iran.[^cite_note-122]

#### Greece

In December 2011, the Turkish newspaper Birgun reported on an interview with former Turkish prime minister [Mesut Yilmaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesut_Yilmaz) saying that Turkey was behind a number of large [forest fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_fires) in Greece in the 1990s. Yilmaz later denied the statements, saying he had been misquoted by the newspaper and that he had been actually referring to unsubstantiated reports of Greek involvement in Turkish forest fires.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] In addition, former head of Greek intelligence service said they had intelligence that Turkish agencies were involved in the arsons in the 1990s but had no proof. He said they had received information from their agents in Turkey that Turkish agents or others were involved in the forest fires on Greek islands.[^cite_note-125]

In August 2013, Greek police arrested a German citizen on the island of [Chios](17067.md) on suspicion of spying for Turkey. Police said they had found in the man's possession cameras, laptops, maps and glasses with an embedded camera, and an email he had sent to an unidentified recipient with details on Greek warships and army vehicles on the island. The man confessed he had photographed barracks and other military-related buildings on the island for five people he believed were Turkish nationals who paid him up to 1,500 euros (\$2,000) for each assignment. Greek authorities suspected that the individuals worked for the Turkish secret services.[^cite_note-126]

In October 2014, Greek police arrested a German citizen on the island of [Kos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kos) because he photographed sensitive locations on the island. He admitted that was spying for MIT[^cite_note-Ιστορίες_με_κατασκόπους_στο_Αιγαίο-127][^cite_note-128] and he was recruited in 2011. He was paid 2,000 euros every month and the money were deposited to his bank account in Germany. Furthermore, he said that he was one of the many European retirees living in Greece who have been recruited by Turkey in order to spy against Greece.[^cite_note-129]

In June 2016, a Turkish intelligence officer who worked in the [Trabzon Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabzon_Province) admitted that his agency ran surveillance on Greek tourists who visited the province. Many Greeks visit the Trabzon and the historic Greek Orthodox [Sumela Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumela_Monastery) there.[^cite_note-130]

In February 2017, the newspaper [Kathimerini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathimerini) reported that the Turkish National Intelligence Organisation recruits EU retirees to spy on a variety of Greek sensitive locations, including military bases, airports and power plants.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-Ιστορίες_με_κατασκόπους_στο_Αιγαίο-127]

In March 2017, the former editor in chief of the English version of Turkish *[Zaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaman_\(newspaper\))* newspaper, Abdullah Bozkurt, posted a tweet on his account warning of increased [clandestine operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clandestine_operations) of Turkish intelligence agents in Greece.[^cite_note-132]

In April 2020, a document published by the Gülen-aligned Nordic Monitor, claims that Turkish intelligence was spying on refugees and migrants in Greece in order to find details of those who were persecuted by the Erdogan administration.[^cite_note-133]

In August 2020, more documents revealed the illegal information gathering and surveillance activities in Greece by the MIT, the Turkish Embassy and the Turkish consulates against Turkish asylum seekers.[^cite_note-134]

In December 2020, Greek authorities arrested two Greek Muslims in [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes) for espionage. One was working at the Turkish Consular Authority in Rhodes and the other on a passengers ship which connects the Rhodes with [Kastelorizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastelorizo).[^cite_note-135]

#### Kosovo

In March 2018, six Turkish nationals from [Kosovo](17391.md) had been captured by Turkish intelligence and brought to Turkey over alleged links to schools financed by the Gulen movement. Turkish President Tayyip Erdoğan said, speaking to supporters and party members in Istanbul: "Our National Intelligence Agency captured six of the highest ranking members \[of Gulen's network] in the Balkans in the operation it conducted in Kosovo."[^cite_note-136]

#### Lithuania

The cyber team of the MİT hacked the server of the [ByLock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ByLock_\(Application\)) in Lithuania and transferred all signed-up IP's to the headquarters in Ankara. The MİT had finalized its operation in December 2015 and January 2016, before ByLock has ceased its operation. Bylock was a secure communication app and Turkish authorities believe that it was exclusively allocated for the members of the Gülen Movement.[^cite_note-137]

#### Moldova

In 2018, Turkish together with Moldovan intelligence services detained at least six Turkish nationals employed at a private chain of high schools in Moldova. All were teachers or students at the Horizont Turkish high-school private chain, which is seen as close to the Fethullah Gulen.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

#### Norway

The Gülen-aligned [Stockholm Center for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Center_for_Freedom) accused the Norwegian Islamist religious organizations that are affiliated with Turkish government that were illegally profiling and intelligence gathering activities on Turks who are believed to be affiliated with Gülen movement in Norway.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

#### Serbia

In 2015, [Matthew Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Aid) wrote that according to reports spies from Turkey are among the most active in Serbia. Turkey organize and finance the movement of [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniak) for the secession of the [Raška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C5%A1ka_\(region\)) region.[^cite_note-142]

#### Sweden

According to the Gülen-aligned [Stockholm Center for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Center_for_Freedom), an investigation was launched on suspicion of unlawful intelligence gathering and illegal "mapping" against opposition circles in Sweden.[^cite_note-143]

#### Switzerland

Parliamentarian Alex Kuprecht announced that the government was considering opening a criminal case against regarding espionage and other illegal activities performed by Turkish agents against dissidents. Also, there were espionage against academics who were critical of Turkey.[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-95] On 16 March 2017, the Office of the Attorney General launched a criminal inquiry into possible foreign spying on Switzerland's Turkish community. Investigation also concerned an attempt to kidnap a Swiss-Turkish in Zurich to take him abroad.[^cite_note-144] That same month, Switzerland's foreign minister warned Turkey against illegal spying on expatriate Turks in Switzerland.[^cite_note-145] In April 2017, a Swiss policeman of Turkish origin was arrested on the accusation that he was spying for the Turkish government.[^cite_note-146]

#### Turkey

At the time of the [1971 Turkish military memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Turkish_military_memorandum), the MIT did not inform Prime Minister [Süleyman Demirel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCleyman_Demirel) about the coup plans, and asked for the PM's resignation on the day of the coup.[^cite_note-ABDULKADİR_SELVİ-147]

During the [1980 Turkish coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), the plans for "Operation Flag" (Bayrak Harekâtı) were conveyed to the military units by an MİT airplane. Once again, the agency did not notify the prime minister, even though legally it was under his authority, as it was part of the coup.[^cite_note-ABDULKADİR_SELVİ-147]

During the [2016 Turkish coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt), the MİT fought against the coup plotters.[^cite_note-ABDULKADİR_SELVİ-147]

According to Turkish media, the Turkish National Intelligence Organization prevented a second attack days after the initial [2024 Lebanon electronic device attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Lebanon_electronic_device_attacks). They intercepted a shipment at [Istanbul Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Airport) containing 1,300 pagers and 700 chargers destined for Lebanon.[^cite_note-148]

#### Ukraine

In July 2018, a gulen-linked suspect was brought to the Turkey from Ukraine following operations conducted by the MİT.[^cite_note-149]

In February 2022, MIT captured and abducted the Turkish arm dealer and former special forces captain in the Turkish armed forces, Nuri Bozkir, who exposed the Turkish arms transfers to militant groups in Syria and Libya. He applied for political asylum in Ukraine and Turkey issued an Interpol red notice calling for his arrest for the 2002 murder of Turkish academic Necip Hablemitoglu while Bozkir denied any involvement.[^cite_note-150]

#### United Kingdom

In 1994, Mehmet Kaygisiz, a [Kurdish](17068.md) man with links to the PKK and the north London underworld, was shot dead at a café in [Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islington). His murder remained unsolved and at the time his murder was thought to be drug-related, but in 2016 new documents suggest that MIT ordered his murder.[^cite_note-independent.co.uk-106]

In 2018, Turkish [NBA](22093.md) player [Enes Kanter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enes_Kanter), who was against Turkey's president [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan), informed the [New York Knicks](72855.md) he will not be traveling with the team to London because he believed that a trip there would be life-threatening. When a reporter asked him if he really believes a trip to London would be dangerous, he replied: "They've got a lot of spies there. I can get killed very easy. That will be a very ugly situation."[^cite_note-151]

## Permission for testimony

Testimony in court may only be made with and by the permission of the Director of the MİT. According to Article 29 of Law No. 2937, MİT agents must not give their testimony if it pertains to state secrets without further permission from the MİT.
