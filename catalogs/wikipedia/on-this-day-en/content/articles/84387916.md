The **2026 French high school protests** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *blocage des lycées*, also called the *fronde lycéenne*) are a series of blockades and demonstrations by [lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_France) students that began in the [Créteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9teil) academy on 21 September 2026 and have since spread across France.[^cite_note-reuters-fr-1] Students cited missing teachers, classes of 32 to 35 pupils, long timetables, run-down buildings, and stress linked to the application website [Parcoursup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parcoursup),[^cite_note-guardian-29-2][^cite_note-franceinfo-how-3] as well temperatures up to 35 °C (95 °F) during the [2026 European heatwaves](83266395.md).[^cite_note-4] By 1 October, parts of the movement included arson, attacks on staff, and clashes with police. The Interior Ministry said 1,949 people were arrested that day.[^cite_note-bbc-1-5][^cite_note-ici-1949-6]

### Study conditions and the budget

The protests came at the start of the school year and at the same time as a public-sector strike over the government's budget plans.

The conditions at issue were not new. At [Saint-Exupéry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Saint-Exup%C3%A9ry_\(Marseille\)) students pointed to unfilled teaching posts, no deputy head, no director of vocational and technological training, no school nurse, and no *Accompagnant des élèves en situation de handicap* (AESH)[^cite_note-7] staff to support pupils with disabilities.[^cite_note-guardian-29-2] A pupil told [CNN](62028.md) that the toilets in the prefabs had no water, that there were cockroaches and rats in the school, and that teachers were not replaced when they left.[^cite_note-cnn-8] [^cite_note-9]

Another told [franceinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franceinfo) that some rooms had too few tables, and that teachers had explained their own discontent before the pupils decided to join them.[^cite_note-franceinfo-how-3] Ryad Rani, president of the [Union Syndicale Lycéenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Syndicale_Lyc%C3%A9enne) (USL), later cited a pupil who had gone seven months without a French teacher in a year with a French exam, and classes of 32 to 36.[^cite_note-afp-principals-10] Pupils elsewhere told ICI of classes of 35.[^cite_note-ici-1949-6]

The [Créteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9teil) *rectorat* told [Agence France-Presse](471981.md) that demands still varied by school: teachers not replaced, class size, heavy timetables, shortages of education assistants, nurses, social workers and managers, incidents between pupils and teachers left without follow-up, and deficient undergraduate admissions web portal "[Parcoursup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parcoursup)".[^cite_note-ici-1949-6] franceinfo summarised what it was hearing from pupils as more resources, smaller classes, buildings in better condition, enough teachers and staff, and less stress from Parcoursup.[^cite_note-franceinfo-how-3]

### A recurring form

Blocking the gate at the start of the day is an established lycée tactic. Writing on 30 September, while this movement was spreading, the historian Claude Lelièvre recalled three earlier waves and distinguished them from 2026.

In autumn 1990, lycée students, many from the Paris [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banlieue), protested over safety, cleanliness, staffing and materials. Lelièvre wrote that nearly 10,000 marched in Paris on 24 October, and that by mid-November the protests had reached on the order of 100,000 people in Paris and about 200,000 in large provincial cities. Education minister [Lionel Jospin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Jospin) answered with an emergency plan and an increase in the education budget.[^cite_note-auto4-11]

In early 2005, lycée students mobilised against the Fillon orientation law, and in particular against a reform of the *[baccalauréat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalaur%C3%A9at)*. About 100,000 marched on 10 February, by Lelièvre's account. On 14 February, [François Fillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Fillon) announced a government amendment removing the *baccalauréat* from the bill. He later said he had decided, on the evening of the first demonstration, that the exam would not be reformed unless the fears could be calmed.

In spring 2018, opposition to the ORE law and to the new Parcoursup platform was carried mainly by university occupations. Lycée blockades were scattered. In the autumn, lycée students mobilised again, against Parcoursup and against [Jean-Michel Blanquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Michel_Blanquer)'s *baccalauréat* reform. On 11 December, hundreds of establishments were blocked or badly disrupted.[^cite_note-monde-diplo-12] *[Le Monde diplomatique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde_diplomatique)* later wrote that this map could not be explained only by the knock-on effect of the [Yellow vests protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_vests_protests), then at their height, but that the Yellow Vest movement was part of the context. (By 2 October [The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist) was drawing comparisons with [May 68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_68).)[^cite_note-13] Parcoursup was kept, with some adjustments, and the *baccalauréat* reform went ahead.[^cite_note-monde-diplo-12] In 2025 the USL had already called a national lycée blockade over the same kinds of grievance. The union said 170 establishments took part.2026.

On 1 October 2026, Rabah Safsaf, a mathematics teacher at the Lycée Gutenberg in [Créteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9teil), told *[Politis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politis_\(magazine\))* that the scene reminded him of the 2006 movement against the *[contrat première embauche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrat_premi%C3%A8re_embauche)*. That mobilisation ended when the government dropped the contract.2026. *Politis* noted the difference: in 2026 there was no single bill the government could withdraw.[^cite_note-14]

Lelièvre's conclusion was that the 2026 movement, because it began in the suburbs over day-to-day conditions rather than against a named reform, resembled 1990 more than 2005 or 2018.[^cite_note-auto4-11] He added that a suggestion, made in the Senate and by the higher-education minister, that the *baccalauréat* might cease to be the key to university could still become a further cause, as in 2005 and 2018.[^cite_note-auto4-11]

### Saint-Exupéry, 17–21 September

The mobilisation that spread is generally traced to the Lycée Antoine-de-Saint-Exupéry in [Créteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9teil).  [Franceinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franceinfo) reported that, the day after a teachers' strike on 17 September against lack of resources and staff, pupils there organised a blockade. Cheick Sanogo, a *première* pupil aged 17, told franceinfo: "The teachers explained the reasons for their discontent and we decided to join them." Pupils said they felt neglected by the Ministry of National Education and cited lack of resources and staff, overcrowded classes, and rooms short of tables. An academic source and the USL later presented that mobilisation as the start of the national movement.[^cite_note-auto2-15]

Contemporaneous reports, including *Le Monde* and a 24 September account by actu.fr, dated the spreading movement to Monday 21 September at the same lycée, where several teaching posts were unfilled.[^cite_note-auto2-15]

### The first week

The Créteil rectorat told [AFP](471981.md) that demands still varied by school. A *terminale* pupil at the Lycée Guillaume-Budé in Limeil-Brévannes told AFP: "It was peaceful. We didn't do anything stupid." A *terminale* pupil at the Lycée Jean-Moulin in [Le Blanc-Mesnil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Blanc-Mesnil) said she was outside from 7 a.m., that "it's been going badly for three years," and that some pupils were sitting the *baccalauréat* without a form teacher.

Through 25 September the spread stayed local. [Actu.fr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actu.fr), citing the Créteil academy, said about 10 lycées were affected by 24 September, mainly in [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne), and described a copycat effect on social networks. That morning, pupils tried to block the entrance of the Lycée Auguste-Blanqui in [Saint-Ouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Ouen) until late morning. An academy spokesperson told AFP there had been a gathering and material damage but no injuries. Police dispersed the crowd, and the school was closed for the afternoon at police request.

On 25 September the Créteil rectorat said the movement was "rather gaining momentum" and counted "a good dozen" lycées blocked, above all in Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-de-Marne. It said some mobilisations had produced crowds and material damage. The Versailles rectorat reported disruption at three of 62 lycées in [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne) and mobilisations at five of 65 in [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine). The [Île-de-France](1716427.md) authority counted about 20 establishments affected, about 10 of them with damage, and about 10 lycées still mobilised out of 468 public lycées in the region. *Le Monde* later wrote that about 60 Paris-region lycées were disrupted on 24 and 25 September, that about 12 percent of the 468 public lycées saw attempted blockades, and that around 10 were actually blocked. It described the movement as sporadic until it intensified.

The Paris police prefecture announced 31 arrests in the Paris agglomeration by 17\:00 on 25 September. It did not name the schools or say whether anyone was held in custody. Rani told AFP that it had started at Saint-Exupéry and "spread very quickly," and cited shortages of education assistants, nurses and social workers.[^cite_note-auto3-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-auto3-16]

Banner at a protest: "Sorry to bother you, we just want a future"

The USL's national call, for 29 September, named four grievances: lack of teachers and staff, "dilapidated lycées," overloaded timetables and classes, and the stress caused by [Parcoursup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parcoursup). Rani said classes sometimes held 32 to 35 pupils and asked protesters to remain peaceful. The union later called a second day on 1 October, an "act two," and a further day of action on 6 October, and asked the ministry to receive its representatives. On RTL on 30 September, Rani said dialogue bodies run by the ministry had been "abandoned," and that it was for the minister to take the first step.

## Timeline

### 28–30 September

On 28 September, Lecornu said escalation would be "irresponsible" and wrote that fireworks, fires, and violence near high schools endangered pupils, staff, and police.[^cite_note-bbc-28-18] Interior Minister [Laurent Nuñez](60527935.md) said 164 people had been arrested that day, 180 schools were affected, and 12 security officers, 12 students, and three staff were injured.[^cite_note-bbc-28-18] In [Saint-Ouen-l'Aumône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Ouen-l'Aum%C3%B4ne), [Val-d'Oise](90616.md), a 14-year-old boy suffered a double fracture of the jaw and broken teeth.[^cite_note-lemonde-ddd-19]

On 29 September the Education Ministry said about 400 of roughly 3,700 lycées were disrupted. Nuñez told [BFM TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFM_TV) that 440 people had been detained, most of them minors.[^cite_note-guardian-29-2] CNN published photographs of police firing tear gas at the Lycée César-Baggio in [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille).[^cite_note-cnn-8] In [Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis), [La France Insoumise](52520313.md) (LFI) mayor Bally Bagayoko stood with pupils outside the Lycée Paul-Éluard. On BFM on 29 September he said that violence could, at a given moment, be legitimate. On 30 September Paris police prefect [Patrice Faure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Faure) referred him to the Bobigny prosecutor, under article 40 of the [code of criminal procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_criminal_procedure?action=edit\&redlink=1), for suspected provocation to commit an offence. On 1 October Bagayoko told franceinfo that he stood by the remarks and called for de-escalation.[^cite_note-franceinfo-bagayoko-20][^cite_note-figaro-bagayoko-21] [Jean-Luc Mélenchon](20298042.md) wrote that the pupils were harmless, that the crackdown had a racist undertone, and that LFI activists should "step in" between pupils and police.[^cite_note-guardian-29-2]

On 30 September Nuñez said 625 people were detained, 83 police and gendarmes were wounded, and 356 schools were affected. He described projectiles, attempted arson, and what he called urban violence.[^cite_note-euronews-22] Education Minister [Édouard Geffray](81324689.md) said 119 pupils and staff had been injured since the protests began.[^cite_note-euronews-22] In [Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tours), a young person was hospitalised with head injuries.[^cite_note-lemonde-ddd-19]

### 1 October

On 1 October university students joined the blockades. The Union étudiante reported about 30 campus blockades, including [Paris Cité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Cit%C3%A9_University), [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University), [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), [Montpellier](73369.md), [Lyon](8638634.md), and [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes).[^cite_note-reuters-fr-1] franceinfo added [Sciences Po Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po_Rennes), Rennes 1, and the [Université de Bretagne-Occidentale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_de_Bretagne-Occidentale), and said students at Paul-Valéry in Montpellier and at [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University) were mobilised.[^cite_note-franceinfo-regions-23] Police counts that morning were lower than the evening total: 340 actions and 169 blockades by mid-morning, according to franceinfo.[^cite_note-franceinfo-images-24] The evening Interior Ministry figure was 1,027 lycées affected, 222 blockaded, and 1,949 arrests.[^cite_note-bbc-1-5][^cite_note-ici-1949-6] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported nearly 3,000 lycée students detained since the start, including the Thursday figure.[^cite_note-guardian-1-25]

Lecornu held an interministerial crisis meeting and told ministers to cancel travel.[^cite_note-guardian-1-25] His office said the violence made it nearly impossible to keep schools open safely, and that intelligence services described the movement as "an operation organised by LFI and its proxies on the far left."[^cite_note-reuters-lfi-26][^cite_note-guardian-1-25] LFI coordinator [Manuel Bompard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Bompard) said the government was "sinking into conspiracism" and called the mobilisation spontaneous.[^cite_note-figaro-lfi-27]

Geffray ordered remote classes where damage or blockades made buildings unsafe, and said a little more than 400 establishments would be closed on 2 October.[^cite_note-ici-1949-6][^cite_note-lemonde-direct-28] That evening the Interior Ministry said prefects had banned demonstrations outside the closed lycées. Paris police prefect Patrice Faure banned gatherings at 13 closed Paris schools.[^cite_note-franceinfo-direct-29]

### 2 October



Police at a protest in [Perpignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan), 2 October

About 400 of France's roughly 3,700 lycées were closed on the orders of the prefects, with many switching to [distance learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_learning). Every prefect banned gatherings outside lycées closed that day. In Paris, prefect of police Patrice Faure banned gatherings around 13 named closed lycées.[^cite_note-figaro2oct-30][^cite_note-franceinfo2oct-31][^cite_note-reuters2oct-32]

By midday the Ministry of National Education said 564 establishments "have been or are the subject of actions or disruptions since the start of the day". The Île-de-France region said 55 lycées were "the scene of violence, clashes and damage". Mobilisation was widely described as lower than on 1 October, but fires, mortar fire and clashes continued.[^cite_note-franceinfo2oct-31][^cite_note-obs2oct-33][^cite_note-lunion2oct-34]

On BFM TV that morning, Education Minister Édouard Geffray said 1,207 lycées had been disrupted or blockaded on Thursday, that more than 100 had suffered "severe damage" since the start of the week, and that 65 education staff had been injured, including 40 headteachers. He said a staff flat in a Lyon lycée had been ransacked by people from outside the school, repeated that the movement had been "taken hostage by ultra-violent groups", and told parents that "in the street, children are in danger". From 09\:00 he met education unions, the Union syndicale lycéenne, parents' groups and elected members of the Conseil national de la vie lycéenne. Services at Matignon described the mobilisation as a "manoeuvre organised by La France Insoumise and its ultra-left proxies"; LFI coordinator Manuel Bompard accused Matignon of "sinking into conspiracy thinking". After the meeting, SNES-FSU general secretary Sophie Vénétitay said there were still "no guarantees" and called a strike for the following Tuesday in support of the lycée demands. Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin told prosecutors to seek parental civil liability so families would pay for damage caused by their children.[^cite_note-bfm2oct-35][^cite_note-figaro2oct-30][^cite_note-franceinfo2oct-31][^cite_note-parisien2oct-36]

A bin fire spread to the façade of the lycée professionnel Gaston-Bachelard in the [13th arrondissement of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_arrondissement_of_Paris) early in the morning; the entrance windows blew out and the Brigade de sapeurs-pompiers de Paris extinguished the blaze at about 07\:55. Firefighters also put out a fire at the gate of the lycée Léonard-de-Vinci in the 15th. BFM TV filmed the charred entrance door of the lycée Roger-Verlomme, also in the 15th; *Le Figaro* reported that the fire followed mortar fire aimed at the door, that several bicycles burned, and that firefighters arrived within about two minutes. *Le Parisien* reported fireworks outside the lycée professionnel Beaugrenelle, on rue Saint-Charles, and a pallet-and-bin fire that firefighters put out shortly before 09\:00. An Île-de-France regional employee was injured and treated by firefighters at the lycée Molière in the 16th. Around midday a barricade of bins was burning at Place de la Nation and police used tear gas. The Île-de-France region also listed a serious fire at the lycée Henri-Moissan in Meaux. RTL quoted the headteacher as saying the façade, entrance hall, part of the canteen and some boarding rooms had burned, and that the damage ran into millions of euros. France Télévisions authenticated images of a fire outside a school in Montigny-lès-Metz.[^cite_note-figaro2oct-30][^cite_note-obs2oct-33][^cite_note-parisien2oct-36][^cite_note-franceinfo2oct-31][^cite_note-bfmverlomme-37][^cite_note-figarobeaugrenelle-38][^cite_note-bbc2oct-39][^cite_note-20minutes2oct-40][^cite_note-rtlmeaux-41]



High school student mobilization day in Perpignan

At the lycée Bougainville in Nantes, mortar fire and burning bins met the morning opening; police and CRS cleared several blockade attempts around 08\:00, and *Le Figaro* reported 10 arrests nearby, one of a youth carrying a metal bar. In Rennes, bin-and-barrier barricades blocked traffic near the lycée Jean-Macé from about 08\:30, with further tension at Émile-Zola and Joliot-Curie; Agence France-Presse reported a barricade and a Palestinian flag on a main road. In Quimper, police used tear gas to disperse a blockade of several hundred people at the lycée Chaptal.[^cite_note-ofnantes-42][^cite_note-figaro2oct-30][^cite_note-afp2oct-43]

In Lyon, mortar fire around 10\:00 led to tear gas in the Foch district beside the lycée Édouard-Herriot; 44 establishments in the Rhône were closed, and a crowd remained outside the still-open lycée Ampère. In the Bordeaux academy, 18 lycées were closed and about 6,000 pupils were reported mobilised in Bordeaux, Mérignac, Bègles, Langon and Libourne; the authorities reported five police officers, one firefighter and four pupils injured, and two teachers confronted at their schools. In the Drôme, Agence France-Presse reported nine arrests and a policewoman injured in the knee during an intervention at a Montélimar lycée and taken to hospital. In Metz, ICI Lorraine reported mortar fire and tear gas at the lycée Fabert early in the morning, one arrest outside the school, and a bin fire at the lycée professionnel Léonard-de-Vinci in Montigny-lès-Metz that licked the building before firefighters put it out. Further clashes and at least one arrest were reported at midday at the entrance of the university campus.[^cite_note-figaro2oct-30][^cite_note-afp2oct-43][^cite_note-franceinfo2oct-31][^cite_note-icimetz-44]

In Évreux, several hundred people gathered from 08\:00 outside the lycée Aristide-Briand. *Paris-Normandie* reported a washing machine set alight, gates forced, windows broken and repeated mortar fire, and quoted prefect Xavier Delarue denouncing "urban violence". France 3 Normandie reported that Delarue said groups had tried to force the entrance in order to burn the lycée, with Molotov cocktails and projectiles, and that the headteacher of another Évreux lycée said Aristide-Briand had suffered mortar fire and an intrusion that succeeded.[^cite_note-pnevreux-45][^cite_note-f3evreux-46]

In La Ciotat, the local site Var Actu reported that a physics teacher at the lycée de la Méditerranée was hit in the face with the contents of a bottle described as containing petrol, briefly blinded, and then kicked, after he tried to put out a fire lit near a tree at about 08\:25. It said the suspected teenager was caught and arrested. A live blog citing *Le Figaro* described the victim as the headteacher, "doused with petrol and beaten" while trying to calm a tense blockade, and said one person was arrested and then released because of the crowd facing the police. The two accounts name the same lycée and the same morning, but not the same victim.[^cite_note-varactu-47][^cite_note-morandini-48]

A Friday claim that staff at the lycée Victor-Hugo in Marseille, beside Gare Saint-Charles, had been doused with petrol refers to Thursday 1 October, not a new attack. Franceinfo reported on 2 October that the lycée was closed after Thursday's clashes, that the entrance door was charred, and that the management team had been splashed with petrol. *La Provence* reported the rectorat's correction: the headteachers "were not directly targeted by youths who doused them" but were splashed while people poured petrol into bins. The Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region had said a headteacher, a deputy head, a general secretary and a team leader were attacked and doused. BFM Marseille reported that the prefecture and the rectorat said they would file a complaint, and that the damaged door had to be made safe.[^cite_note-finfohugo-49][^cite_note-provencehugo-50][^cite_note-bfmhugo-51]

Separately on 2 October, Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said images of "rare violence" showed a police officer in Belfort deliberately targeted, thrown to the ground and beaten, including to the head, by masked people in black on the margins of the lycée blockades. The officer lost consciousness and was hospitalised; Nuñez said his condition was no longer a major concern. The Belfort prosecutor opened an investigation for attempted murder of a person holding public authority. Franceinfo's photograph of the intervention is dated 1 October, so the beating itself may predate the Friday announcement.[^cite_note-belfortinfo-52][^cite_note-belfortfig-53]

No national arrest total for 2 October had been published by early afternoon. The latest Interior Ministry count remained Thursday's: 1,949 arrests, with 305 police and gendarmes injured that day, and 1,793 people placed in custody since Monday, about 90 per cent of them minors.[^cite_note-franceinfo2oct-31][^cite_note-figaro1793-54]

### South-west, Grand Est, and other cities

Reuters reported that riot police clashed with youths in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) and that at least one car was burned in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg).[^cite_note-reuters-lfi-26][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] In [Tournefeuille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournefeuille), west of Toulouse, *[La Dépêche du Midi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_D%C3%A9p%C3%AAche_du_Midi)* reported fires and projectiles at the Lycée Françoise from about 9\:30 a.m., smashed windows at the entrance, and staff moved to the back of the building. A resident told the paper there was smoke everywhere and that police were firing smoke grenades.[^cite_note-depeche-francoise-132] At the Lycée Stéphane-Hessel, in the Roseraie district, *La Dépêche* reported that bins were set alight and then the lodge was burned. Teachers and pupils told the paper that many people from outside the school had joined the blockade.[^cite_note-depeche-toulouse-133] The same paper reported a large fire that reached the lodge of the Lycée Roland-Garros.[^cite_note-depeche-toulouse-133] Toulouse FM reported bins, a bicycle, and a Tisséo bus shelter set on fire or smashed on route d'Espagne during the blockade of the Lycée Gallieni, and a CRS charge to clear the entrance for firefighters. It listed further clashes at Déodat-de-Séverac, Les Arènes, Bellevue, Ozenne, Saint-Sernin, Henri-de-Toulouse-Lautrec, Urbain-Vitry, and Rive-Gauche.[^cite_note-toulousefm-134] ICI, citing the authorities, reported 57 arrests in Haute-Garonne, 13 pupils slightly injured, including several attacked while trying to put out a fire, damaged gates, lodges partly destroyed at two lycées, about 30 vandalised bus shelters, and fires of bins and tyres. Hessel and Françoise were among 18 schools closed the next day.[^cite_note-ici-31-135] On 29 September, Toulouse FM reported that a member of the management at Stéphane-Hessel was charged, knocked down, and beaten by a crowd of minors while trying to put out a bin fire.[^cite_note-toulousefm-29-136] In Strasbourg, authorities halted trams. [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) reported that barriers were burned on the tracks.[^cite_note-aj-137] franceinfo's photograph caption showed a street blocked at the Lycée Camille-Jullian in [Bordeaux](4097.md).[^cite_note-franceinfo-images-24] franceinfo also reported mortar fire in Bordeaux, then a fire on a lycée facade.[^cite_note-franceinfo-deg-138] The same report said a pupil was burned in front of classmates at another Nantes lycée.[^cite_note-franceinfo-deg-138] RMC reported that a deputy principal in Nîmes was slightly injured.[^cite_note-rmc-59]

### Attacks not tied to a named school

CNN reported that videos showed two teachers being attacked, and that the presidency, officials, and teaching unions condemned the footage. It did not name the schools. It also reported a government figure of one pupil who partly lost the use of a hand after setting off a firework.[^cite_note-cnn-8] Al Jazeera, citing regional education authorities, prefectures, and unions, reported that several principals were injured, harassed, or assaulted during Thursday's blockades.[^cite_note-aj-137] RMC reported that at least 10 head teachers and deputy heads had been injured since the protests began.[^cite_note-rmc-59]

### Belgium

On 1 October the mobilisation was echoed in [Liège](28341707.md). The Liège police said a call on social media brought pupils, at about 8 a.m., to five schools: the Athénée de Fragnée, Saint-Laurent, Saint-Sépulcre, Marie-Thérèse, and Liège-Atlas. The police said eggs, apples, barriers, and firecrackers were thrown at the schools and at officers, and that one member of a school's management was physically attacked.[^cite_note-auto-139] [RTBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTBF) reported a fire near the Institut Marie-Thérèse, on rue Hors-Château, into which scooters and barriers were thrown, road signs and street furniture torn up, and some vehicles damaged. It reported that a glass door at the Collège Saint-Barthélemy was smashed with an iron bar, and that the Carrefour Express on rue Saint-Léonard was vandalised. The police told RTBF of five judicial arrests and one administrative arrest, and said there had been no further incident after 1 p.m. Rue Hors-Château, closed during the clashes, reopened at 2 p.m. after the city cleared the fire damage.[^cite_note-rtbf-140] Sudinfo reported schools vandalised and scooters set on fire.[^cite_note-sudinfo-141] RTBF said the French mobilisation appeared to be spreading to Belgium. Pupils were filmed chanting "Glatigny, casse-toi", aimed at the Walloon education minister.[^cite_note-rtbf-140][^cite_note-bruxelles-today-142] [VRT NWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VRT_NWS) reported that free-network and municipal secondary schools in Liège would stay closed on 2 October for safety.[^cite_note-vrt-143] At the Athénée Paul-Brusson in [Montegnée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montegn%C3%A9e), RTBF reported a quiet blockade in support of teachers, with no clash.[^cite_note-rtbf-140][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-auto-139]

## Casualties, arrests and damage

On 1 October the Interior Ministry's arrest figure was 1,949.[^cite_note-bbc-1-5][^cite_note-ici-1949-6] *Le Figaro* reported 1,793 people placed in custody, 90 percent of them minors. It said about one third of the cases concerned attacks on public officers, one third unlawful assembly, and one third property damage. Bobigny prosecutor Alexandre de Bosschère described the local pattern as a pre-riot, citing group possession of incendiary products or weapons, damage, and violence.[^cite_note-figaro-gav-146] ICI attributed the 1,793 custody figure to the Justice Ministry and said it covered the period since Monday. An arrest is not the same thing as being placed in custody.[^cite_note-ici-1949-6]

Officer injuries were reported differently on the same day. The franceinfo live blog, citing the Interior Ministry, reported 84 police and gendarmes injured.[^cite_note-franceinfo-direct-29] Later, ICI and the BBC, also citing the Interior Ministry, reported 305.[^cite_note-ici-1949-6][^cite_note-bbc-1-5] On Wednesday, Nuñez had said 83 officers were injured.[^cite_note-euronews-22]

Geffray said on Thursday evening that 235 people had been hurt on the education side since Monday: 170 pupils and 65 staff.[^cite_note-ici-1949-6] Earlier on Wednesday he had given a cumulative figure of 119.[^cite_note-euronews-22] CNN reported a government figure of 159 students and staff injured over several days.[^cite_note-cnn-8]

## Responses

### Public opinion

A poll conducted for *[Le Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Figaro)* indicates that 62% of French people believe that the students’ movement is justified.[^cite_note-147] The same poll found that 61% believed that LFI was too involved, 74% felt that the party was putting students in danger, and 78% felt that the party was exploiting the movement for political gain.[^cite_note-148]

### Government

Lecornu said the difficulties of the lycées "must be heard" and that violence had to be condemned.[^cite_note-bbc-28-18] After the 1 October crisis meeting, his office called the unrest urban violence and cited the intelligence assessment naming LFI.[^cite_note-reuters-lfi-26] *Le Figaro* reported that it had seen a note from the Paris police prefecture whose authors wrote that the origins of the movement "plainly reside in an instrumentalisation of student-union structures and of political movements close to the far left." Matignon told the paper that about 20 national LFI office-holders were involved on 29 September, from Lille to Rennes and Toulouse.[^cite_note-figaro-note-149] Geffray said the lycée movement had been "taken hostage by ultra-violent groups", told parents not to let their children join, and said no demand could justify the acts.[^cite_note-franceinfo-remote-150][^cite_note-ici-1949-6] Justice Minister [Gérald Darmanin](54070416.md) called the 36 prosecutors general to a video meeting and asked for a firm penal response.[^cite_note-figaro-pg-151] Nuñez said police had opened internal inquiries into allegations of brutality against three students.[^cite_note-aj-137]

### Political parties

[Bruno Retailleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Retailleau), a Republican presidential candidate, told BFM-RMC that LFI was "fascinated by violence", was calling for sedition, and was trying to organise an insurrection. He said the lycée clashes were a "dress rehearsal" for 2027 if [Marine Le Pen](572897.md) won, and raised the question of banning LFI.[^cite_note-auto1-152] Mélenchon had already called the pupils harmless and the police response racist in tone.[^cite_note-guardian-29-2][^cite_note-auto1-152]

[Gabriel Attal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Attal), a Renaissance presidential candidate, told Agence France-Presse that LFI's use of the movement was unbearable and asked Mélenchon and LFI deputies to stop using young people as "political cannon fodder". BFM reported that Attal had first repeated the regional council's claim that a Marseille management team had been doused with petrol, and that the *rectorat* then described them as splashed.[^cite_note-afp-attal-114][^cite_note-bfm-direct-115]

Bompard rejected Matignon's account of the origin of the movement.[^cite_note-figaro-lfi-27] [Valérie Pécresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val%C3%A9rie_P%C3%A9cresse), president of Île-de-France, gave the damage estimate above €1 million.[^cite_note-politico-153] *Le Figaro* reported that Pécresse said she would send a €100,000 bill to the mayor of Saint-Denis, Bally Bagayoko, for the Paul-Éluard gate, guarding, and cleaning, and that she accused him of having encouraged the people who damaged it.[^cite_note-figaro-bill-154] Europe 1 quoted the region's vice-president, James Chéron, saying those responsible and their legal guardians would be pursued, and that the region counted more than 20 burned gates and about 10 ransacked schools.[^cite_note-europe1-pecresse-155]
