The **United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York** (in [case citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_citation), **E.D.N.Y.**) is the [federal district court](70235.md) whose territorial jurisdiction spans five [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_New_York) in [New York State](8210131.md): the four [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island) counties of [Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York), [Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_County,_New_York), [Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_County,_New_York) (Brooklyn), and [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), as well as [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_County,_New_York) (Staten Island), the latter three being among [New York City](645042.md)'s five [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_New_York_City). The court also has [concurrent jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_jurisdiction) with the [Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York) over the waters of [New York](45470.md) (Manhattan) and [Bronx](3338.md) Counties (including [New York Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor) and the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River)).[^cite_note-1] Its courthouses are located in Brooklyn and [Central Islip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Islip,_New_York).

Appeals from the Eastern District of New York are taken to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Second_Circuit) (except for [patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent) claims and claims against the U.S. government under the [Tucker Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Act), which are appealed to the [Federal Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Federal_Circuit)).

The United States Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York represents the United States in civil and criminal litigation in the court. The [United States Attorney for the Eastern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_for_the_Eastern_District_of_New_York) since May 2025 is Joseph Nocella Jr. The [U.S. Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marshals_Service) for the court is Vincent F. DeMarco.

## Courthouses



[Theodore Roosevelt United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_United_States_Courthouse)



[Alfonse M. D'Amato United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonse_M._D'Amato_United_States_Courthouse)

The main location is the [Theodore Roosevelt United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_United_States_Courthouse) at 225 [Cadman Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadman_Plaza) East in the civic center of Brooklyn. The 15-story building was designed by [Cesar Pelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Pelli). The courthouse was designed in 1995 but did not open until 2006 following redesign requirements in the wake of the [Oklahoma City bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City_bombing) and the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md). It replaced the six story [Emanuel Celler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Celler) Federal Building (built in 1962 and located next door and connected via glass atrium). In 2008 it was renamed for [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt).[^cite_note-2] The building was originally to be renamed in honor of former [New York Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Governor) [Hugh Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Carey) but politicians backed off because Carey was alive at the time. The associated prison is the [Metropolitan Detention Center, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Detention_Center,_Brooklyn).

The Divisional office is in the [Alfonse M. D'Amato United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonse_M._D'Amato_United_States_Courthouse) in [Central Islip, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Islip,_New_York). The courthouse designed by [Richard Meier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Meier) opened in 2000 and is the largest building on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island).[^cite_note-3] The 12-story building has 870,000 square feet (81,000 m<sup>2</sup>), 23 courtrooms and 24 judges' chambers.[^cite_note-4]

It is the third largest federal courthouse in the United States (after the [Daniel Patrick Moynihan United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Patrick_Moynihan_United_States_Courthouse) and [Thomas F. Eagleton United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_F._Eagleton_United_States_Courthouse)).

Chief judges have administrative responsibilities with respect to their district court. Unlike the Supreme Court, where one justice is specifically nominated to be chief, the office of chief judge rotates among the district court judges. To be chief, a judge must have been in active service on the court for at least one year, be under the age of 65, and have not previously served as chief judge.&#x20;

A vacancy is filled by the judge highest in seniority among the group of qualified judges. The chief judge serves for a term of seven years, or until age 70, whichever occurs first. The age restrictions are waived if no members of the court would otherwise be qualified for the position.

When the office was created in 1948, the chief judge was the longest-serving judge who had not elected to retire, on what has since 1958 been known as [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status), or declined to serve as chief judge. After August 6, 1959, judges could not become or remain chief after turning 70 years old. The current rules have been in operation since October 1, 1982.
