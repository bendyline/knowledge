**Maskanah** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مَسْكَنَة), also spelled **Meskene**, is a town in northern [Syria](7515849.md), administratively part of the [Manbij District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij_District) of the [Aleppo Governorate](7444618.md). The town is located 100 kilometres (62 mi) southeast of [Aleppo](159244.md) on the [Lake Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Assad) part of the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates). Nearby localities include [Dayr Hafir](35490900.md), [Humaymah Kabirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humaymah_Kabirah) and [Tell Ayoub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Ayoub) to the northwest and [Tabqa](10513014.md) to the southeast. According to the [Syria Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Statistics_\(Syria\)) (CBS), Maskanah had a population of 15,477 in the 2004 census.[^cite_note-CBS-1]

## History

The [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) city of [Emar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emar) lies a few kilometres north of the present town.[^cite_note-brill-2]

In 1210, during [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) rule, the al-Adil Minaret was constructed. The brick structure was built in the simple, [Persian architectural style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_architecture). It was further developed during the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) period. It was restored during [Hafez al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez_al-Assad)'s rule (1970–2000) and is one last historic architectural remains in the town.[^cite_note-3]

During [Ottoman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria), the area was mostly inhabited by nomadic [Bedouin](61770.md) tribes.[^cite_note-brill-2] In 1838, Maskanah was classified as a ruined village ("*khirba*") by Biblical scholar [Eli Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Smith).[^cite_note-4] In 1915 the town was visited by orientalist [Alois Musil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Musil) who mentions the town having barracks, a large *[khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai)*, and the residence of the head of the telegraph service.[^cite_note-brill-2] A year later, Maskanah became a major deportation route during the [Armenian genocide](140376.md) where an estimated 80,000 Armenians died.[^cite_note-5]

Under the [French mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_of_Syria), the town was the center of a *[qadaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadaa)*, and served as a center for milk production and cattle merchants. In 1945 the village had 430 inhabitants.[^cite_note-brill-2]



*Maskanah* is the administrative center of [Nahiya Maskanah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya_Maskanah?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Manbij District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij_District).

On 3 June 2017, the city was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maskanah_Plains_offensive) by the [Syrian Army](78567599.md) from the [Islamic State](9087364.md).[^cite_note-6] The [SDF](48205248.md) took control of the town on 1 December 2024 following the withdrawal of the Syrian Army. They later withdrew east of the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) on 17 January 2026 under pressure from advancing [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) forces during the [northeastern Syria offensive](82106178.md).[^cite_note-7]
