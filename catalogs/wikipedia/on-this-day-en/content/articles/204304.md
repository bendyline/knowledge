**Unicameralism** (from *uni*- "one" & Latin *camera* "chamber") is a type of [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) consisting of one house or assembly that legislates and votes as one.[^cite_note-1] Unicameralism makes up nearly 60% of all national legislatures[^cite_note-2] and an even greater share of subnational legislatures.

Sometimes, as in [New Zealand](4913064.md) and [Denmark](76972.md), unicameralism comes from the abolition of one of two [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) chambers, or, as in [Sweden](5058739.md), through the merger of the two chambers into a single one, while in others a second chamber has never existed from the beginning.

## Rationale for unicameralism and criticism

The principal advantage of a unicameral system is more efficient lawmaking, as the legislative process is simpler and there is no possibility of [deadlock](3786577.md) between two chambers. Proponents of unicameralism have also argued that it reduces costs, even if the number of legislators stays the same, since there are fewer institutions to maintain and support financially. Popular among 20th-century democratic countries, unicameral, proportional legislatures were seen by nascent nations as both more democratic and effective.[^cite_note-3] However, at the tail end of the 20th century, the trend in favour of unicameral legislatures over bicameral legislatures started to reverse,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-opinion-1128-6] with a trend of countries starting to either restore or create Upper houses with the objectives of achieving greater checks and balances, representation, and/or legislative oversight,[^cite_note-7] causing the number of bicameral parliaments to rise from some 45 countries at the beginning of the 1970s to more than 80 in 2024.[^cite_note-opinion-1128-6]

Proponents of [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) legislatures say that having two legislative chambers offers an additional restraint on the [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority), though critics note that there are other ways to restrain majorities, such as through non-partisan courts and a robust constitution.[^cite_note-8]

Approximately half of the world's [sovereign states](23604120.md) are currently unicameral. The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) is somewhat in-between, with a legislature and a formal advisory body. China has a [Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_People's_Political_Consultative_Conference) which meets alongside the [National People's Congress](38878740.md), in many respects an advisory "upper house".

Many [subnational entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) have unicameral legislatures. These include the state of [Nebraska](21647.md) and territories of [Guam](11974.md) and the [Virgin Islands](32135.md) in the [United States](3434750.md), the Chinese [special administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China) of [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macao](19068.md), the [Australian](4689264.md) state of [Queensland](59051.md) as well as the [Northern Territory](21638.md) and the [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory), a majority of the [provinces of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina), all of the [provinces and territories](75763.md) in [Canada](5042916.md), all of the Communities, Regions and Community Commissions of Belgium, all of the regions of [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) and [France](58846.md), all of the [provinces of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Nepal), all of the [Spanish autonomous communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_autonomous_communities), both of the [autonomous regions of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_Portugal), most of the [states and union territories of India](375986.md), and all of the states of [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federative_units_of_Brazil), [Mexico](8196151.md), [Nigeria](226734.md), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Austria), and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany). In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the devolved [Scottish Parliament](61188.md), the Welsh [Senedd](238480.md), the [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly), and the [London Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Assembly) are also unicameral.

## Unicameralism in the Philippines

Though the current [Congress of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) is bicameral, the country experienced unicameralism in 1898 and 1899 (during the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic)), from 1935 to 1941 (the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) era) and from 1943 to 1944 (during the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines)). Under the 1973 Constitution, the legislative body was called [Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa), which functioned also a unicameral legislature within a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) (1973–1981) and a [semi-presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) (1981–1986) form of government.

The ongoing process of amending or revising the [current Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Constitution) and form of government is popularly known as [Charter Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_Change). A shift to a unicameral parliament was included in the proposals of the constitutional commission created by President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo).[^cite_note-14] Unlike in the United States, senators in the [Senate of the Philippines](299521.md) are elected not per district and state but nationally; the [Philippines](23440.md) is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state).[^cite_note-15] The Philippine government's decision-making process, relative to the United States, is more rigid, highly centralised, much slower and susceptible to [political gridlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_gridlock). As a result, the trend for unicameralism as well as other political system reforms are more contentious in the Philippines.[^cite_note-16]

While Congress is bicameral, all local legislatures are unicameral: the [Bangsamoro Parliament](42303279.md), the [Sangguniang Panlalawigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlalawigan) (Provincial Boards), [Sangguniang Panlungsod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlungsod) (City Councils), [Sangguniang Bayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Bayan) (Municipal Councils), [Sangguniang Barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Barangay) ([Barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) Councils), and the [Sangguniang Kabataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Kabataan) (Youth Councils).

## Unicameralism in the United States

Local government legislatures of counties, cities, or other political subdivisions within states are usually unicameral. Three U.S. states and territories have a unicameral legislature: the state of [Nebraska](21647.md), and the territories of [Guam](11974.md) and the [Virgin Islands](32135.md).

The [Nebraska Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Legislature) (also called the Unicameral) is the supreme legislative body of the state of Nebraska and the only unicameral state legislature in the United States. Its members are called "senators", as it was originally the upper house of a bicameral legislature before the Nebraska House of Representatives dissolved in 1937. The legislature is also notable for being nonpartisan and officially recognizes no party affiliation, making Nebraska unique among US states. With 49 members, it is also the smallest legislature of any US state.

### Early history

The [history of unicameralism in the United States](#Unicameralism_in_the_United_States) began with the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution): The provisional government of the early United States was unicameral from 1775–1788.

Three revolutionary states had unicameral legislatures: [Georgia](48830.md) (1777–1789), [Pennsylvania](23332.md) (1776–1790), and [Vermont](32578.md) (1776–1836). These constitutions introduced a strong unicameral legislature and weak governor with no veto power. In Pennsylvania, the 1776 Constitution was based substantially on [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine)'s *[Common Sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Sense)*.[^cite_note-Williams1988-17] In 1790, moderates gained power in the Pennsyvlania state legislature, called a new constitutional convention, and rewrote the constitution with a strong executive with veto power and created a new upper house.[^cite_note-Wood1992-18] Thomas Paine strongly opposed the new Constitution, calling it "a departure from the principles" of representative government and a mere "copy in miniature of the government of England".[^cite_note-Wood1992-18][^cite_note-19]

Since the creation of the [United States Senate](24909346.md) at the [Constitutional Convention of 1787](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(United_States\)) under the [Connecticut Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Compromise), reformers have proposed [reforms to the United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_of_the_Senate) or [its outright abolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_the_Senate), which would make the United States federal government a unicameral entity.

### Later proposals

In 1944, [Missouri](19571.md) held a vote on changing the [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_General_Assembly) to a unicameral one, which was narrowly rejected by the voters 52.42–47.58. Only the city of [St. Louis](27687.md) and the [St. Louis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_County,_Missouri) voted in favor, whilst [Jackson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_County,_Missouri) (containing the bulk of [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri)) narrowly voted against, and all other counties voted against the change to unicameralism.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

In 1970, [North Dakota](21651.md) voters voted to call a constitutional convention. In 1972, a change to a unicameral legislature was approved by 69.36–30.64;[^cite_note-22] however, since the voters rejected the new constitution at the same referendum, it never took effect.[^cite_note-23]

In 1999, Governor [Jesse Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Ventura) proposed converting the [Minnesota Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Legislature) into a single chamber.[^cite_note-24] Although debated, the idea was never adopted.

In 2005, the [US territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) of [Puerto Rico](23041.md) held a [non-binding unicameralism referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_unicameralism_referendum). Voters approved changing its [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Puerto_Rico) to a unicameral body by 456,267 votes in favor (83.7%) versus 88,720 against (16.3%).[^cite_note-25] If both the territory's [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_House_of_Representatives) and [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Senate) had approved by a 2⁄3 vote the specific amendments to the [Puerto Rico Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Constitution) that are required for the change to a unicameral legislature, another referendum would have been held in the territory to approve such amendments. If those constitutional changes had been approved, Puerto Rico could have switched to a unicameral legislature as early as 2015.

On June 9, 2009, the [Maine House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_House_of_Representatives) voted to form a unicameral legislature, but the measure did not pass the Senate.[^cite_note-26]

During the [2009 New York State Senate leadership crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_New_York_State_Senate_leadership_crisis), which yielded [legislative gridlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_gridlock), former Congressman [Rick Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Lazio), a prospective candidate for governor, proposed that [New York](8210131.md) adopt unicameralism.[^cite_note-27]

A 2018 study found that efforts to adopt unicameralism in Ohio and Missouri failed due to rural opposition.[^cite_note-Myers2018-28] There was a fear in rural communities that unicameralism would diminish their influence in state government.[^cite_note-Myers2018-28]

Für Bundestag und Bundesrat ist dagegen eine gemeinsame Bezeichnung nicht allgemein üblich, und es ist sogar umstritten, ob der Bundesrat eine Zweite Kammer ist. (English: Abroad, such a parliamentary system is in general called a bicameral one. For Bundestag and Bundesrat such a common designation is not usual and it is even contentious whether the Bundesrat is a second chamber at all.)
