**Caracas**,[^cite_note-3] officially **Santiago de León de Caracas** (**CCS**), is the capital and largest city of [Venezuela](32374.md), and the center of the [Metropolitan Region of Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Region_of_Caracas) (or Greater Caracas).[^cite_note-\:122-4] It is located along the [Guaire River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaire_River) in the northern part of Venezuela, within the Caracas Valley of the [Venezuelan coastal mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Range,_Venezuela). The valley is close to the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md), separated from the shore by a steep 2,200-meter-high (7,200-foot) mountain range, [Cerro El Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_%C3%81vila_National_Park). To the south there are more hills and mountains that form the valley. The Metropolitan Region of Caracas has an estimated population of over 5 million inhabitants.

The historic center of the city is the [Caracas Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Cathedral), located on [Bolívar Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar_Square_\(Caracas\)),[^cite_note-\:322-5] though some consider the center to be [Plaza Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Venezuela), located in the Los Caobos area.[^cite_note-\:122-4][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:42-7] Businesses in the city include service companies, banks, and malls. Caracas has a largely service-based economy, apart from some industrial activity in its metropolitan area.[^cite_note-Caracas-8] The [Caracas Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Stock_Exchange) and [Petróleos de Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr%C3%B3leos_de_Venezuela) (PDVSA) are headquartered in Caracas. [Empresas Polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empresas_Polar), the largest private company in Venezuela, is also headquartered here. Caracas is Venezuela's cultural capital, with many restaurants, [shopping centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_center), theaters, and museums found in the city, including the [Museum of Contemporary Art of Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art_of_Caracas).[^cite_note-9] Caracas is home to some of the tallest skyscrapers in Latin America,[^cite_note-\:2-10] such as the [Parque Central Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_Central_Complex).[^cite_note-11]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Caracas).



*Diego de Losada* by [Antonio Herrera Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Herrera_Toro)

Before the city was founded in 1567,[^cite_note-\:0-12] the valley of Caracas was populated by [indigenous peoples](32374.md#Pre-Columbian_history). [Francisco Fajardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Fajardo), the son of a Spanish [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(armed_forces\)) and a [Waikeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikeri) *[cacique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacique)*, who came from [Margarita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Island), began establishing settlements in the area of [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira) and the Caracas valley between 1555 and 1560. Fajardo attempted to establish a plantation in the valley in 1562 after these coastal towns proved unsuccessful, but it did not last long, being destroyed by natives of the region led by [Terepaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terepaima_National_Park) and [Guaicaipuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaicaipuro).[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-14] Fajardo's 1560 settlement was known as Hato de San Francisco, and another attempt in 1561 by Juan Rodríguez de Suárez was called Villa de San Francisco, and was also destroyed by the same native people.[^cite_note-\:3-15] The eventual settlers of Caracas came from [Coro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coro,_Venezuela), the capital of the German [Klein-Venedig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein-Venedig) colony located around the present-day coastal [Colombia–Venezuela border](47414233.md). From the 1540s, the colony had been *de facto* controlled by Spaniards. Moving eastward from Coro, groups of Spanish settlers founded inland towns including [Barquisimeto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquisimeto) and [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Carabobo), before reaching the Caracas valley.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

On 25 July 1567, Captain [Diego de Losada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Losada) laid the foundations of the city of *Santiago de León de Caracas*.[^cite_note-\:0-12] De Losada had been commissioned to capture the valley, and was successful by splitting the natives into smaller groups and defeating each of them individually.[^cite_note-\:3-15] Out of the newly established settlements, Caracas was the closest to the coast. The Spanish colonists retained a native workforce, which allowed them to develop a trade network between Caracas, the interior of Venezuela, and Margarita. The towns further inland traded cotton and beeswax, while Margarita was a rich source of pearls. The Caracas valley had a good environment for both agricultural and arable farming, which contributed to the system of commerce. However, the town's population was initially sparse, as it was only large enough to support a few farms.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

In 1577, Caracas became the capital of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire)'s [Venezuela Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Province),[^cite_note-16] under the province's new governor [Juan de Pimentel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Pimentel), leader from 1576–1583.[^cite_note-\:3-15] In the 1580s, residents of Caracas started selling food via ship to Spanish soldiers stationed in [Cartagena](63927.md), who docked there to load goods from other parts of South America. Wheat was becoming increasingly expensive in the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), and the Spanish profited from buying it from farmers in Caracas. This cemented the city in the empire's trade circuit.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

During the 16th and 17th centuries, the coast of Venezuela was frequently raided by pirates. With the coastal mountains of the Central Range acting as a barrier, Caracas was relatively immune to such attacks, compared to other Caribbean coastal settlements,[^cite_note-\:1-13] but in 1595 the [Preston–Somers expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston%E2%80%93Somers_expedition) landed and around 200 English [Privateers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer), including [George Somers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Somers) and [Amyas Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyas_Preston), crossed the mountains through a little-used pass while the town's defenders were guarding the more frequently used one. Encountering little resistance, the invaders sacked and set fire to the town after a failed ransom negotiation.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The city managed to rebuild, using wheat profits and "a lot of sacrifice".[^cite_note-\:3-15] In the 1620s, farmers in Caracas discovered that [cacao beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacao_bean) could be sold, first selling them to native people of Mexico and quickly growing across the [Caribbean](18956035.md). The city became important in the [Viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty) of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), and also moved from using largely native slave labor to African slaves, the first of the Spanish colonies to become part of the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery). The city was successful and operated on cacao and slave labor until the 1650s, when an *alhorra* blight, the [Mexican Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Inquisition) of many of their Portuguese traders, and increased cacao production in [Guayaquil](215586.md) greatly affected the market. This and the destructive [1641 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1641_Caracas_earthquake) put the city into decline, and they likely began illegally trading with the [Dutch Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Empire), which Caraqueños later proved sympathetic to; by the 1670s, Caracas had a trading route through [Curaçao](65205.md).[^cite_note-\:1-13]



Caracas in 1839

In 1728, the [Guipuzcoan Company of Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guipuzcoan_Company_of_Caracas) was founded by the king, and the cacao business grew in importance. Caracas was made one of the three provinces of [Nueva Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Granada), corresponding to Venezuela, in 1739. Over the next three decades the Viceroyalty was variously split, with Caracas province becoming the Venezuela province. [Luis de Unzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_Unzaga) created the [Captaincy General of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Venezuela) in the summer of 1777, with Caracas as the capital.[^cite_note-\:3-15] Venezuela then attempted to become independent, first with the 1797 [Gual and España conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gual_and_Espa%C3%B1a_conspiracy), based in Caracas,[^cite_note-The_connection-19] and then the successful 1811 [Venezuelan Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Declaration_of_Independence).[^cite_note-\:3-15] Caracas then came under worse luck: in 1812, an [earthquake destroyed Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1812_Caracas_earthquake), a quarter of its population migrated in 1814, and the [Venezuelan War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_War_of_Independence) continued until 24 June 1821, when [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) defeated [royalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist_\(Hispanic_American_Revolution\)) in the [Battle of Carabobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Carabobo).[^cite_note-\:3-15][^cite_note-HT-20] Urban reforms only took place towards the end of the 19th century, under [Antonio Guzmán Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Guzm%C3%A1n_Blanco): some landmarks were built, but the city remained distinctly [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonial_architecture) until the 1930s.[^cite_note-\:3-15]



Caracas in 1950

Caracas grew in size, population, and economic importance during Venezuela's oil boom in the early 20th century. In the 1950s, the metropolitan area of Gran Caracas was developed, and the city began an intensive modernization program, funding public buildings, which continued throughout the 1960s and early 1970s.[^cite_note-\:3-15] Cultural landmarks, like the [University City of Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City_of_Caracas), designed by modernist architect [Carlos Raúl Villanueva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ra%C3%BAl_Villanueva) and declared a World Heritage Site by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 2000;[^cite_note-\:23-21] the [Caracas Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Museum_of_Contemporary_Art); and the [Teresa Carreño Cultural Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_Carre%C3%B1o_Cultural_Complex) were built, as well as the [Caracas Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Metro) and a developed downtown area. Urban development was rapid, leading to the growth of slums on the hillsides surrounding the new city. Much of the city development also fell into disrepair come the end of the 20th century, with the [1980s oil glut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_oil_glut) and political instability like the [Caracazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracazo), meaning maintenance can not be sustained. The economic and social problems persist throughout the capital and country, characterized as the [crisis in Venezuela](52966258.md). By 2017, Caracas was the most violent city in the world.[^cite_note-\:3-15] On 3 January 2026, the [United States launched strikes on Caracas](82021123.md) and captured President [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md), and his wife, First Lady [Cilia Flores](9530834.md), and removed them from the country to New York City for criminal prosecution on [narcoterrorism](221350.md) charges.[^cite_note-22]

On 24 June 2026, the city was struck by a powerful [earthquake doublet](83547391.md) causing widespread damage, killing and injuring many.[^cite_note-23]

The previous coat of arms was adopted in 1591. Simón de Bolívar, an ancestor of Venezuelan liberator Simón Bolívar,[^cite_note-24] had been named the first prosecutor general of the Venezuelan province in 1589. He served as the representative of Venezuela to the Spanish Crown, and vice versa.[^cite_note-25] In 1591, de Bolívar introduced a petition to King [Philip II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) for a coat of arms, which he granted by [Royal Cedula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Cedula) on 4 September that year in San Lorenzo. The coat of arms represents the city's name with the red [Santiago (St. James') cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_cross). It originally depicted "a brown bear rampant on a field of silver, holding between its paws a golden shell with the red cross of Santiago; and its seal is a crown with five golden points".[^cite_note-26] In the same act, the king declared Caracas as "The Most Noble and Very Loyal City of Santiago de León de Caracas".[^cite_note-27]

Until 2022, the anthem of the city was *Marcha a Caracas*, written by the composer Tiero Pezzuti de Matteis with the lyrics by [José Enrique Sarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Enrique_Sarabia) and approved in 1984.[^cite_note-28]

## Geography



Ávila national park, seen from [Parque del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_del_Este)

Caracas is contained entirely within a valley of the Venezuelan Central Range, and is separated from the Caribbean coast by a roughly 15-kilometer (9 mi) expanse of [El Ávila National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_%C3%81vila_National_Park). The valley is relatively small and quite irregular. The altitude varies between 870 and 1,043 meters (2,854 and 3,422 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level); the historic center lies at about 900 meters (3,000 feet) above sea level.[^cite_note-mountains-29] This, along with the rapid population growth, has profoundly influenced the [urban development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) of the city.[^cite_note-30] The most elevated point of the Capital District, wherein the city is located, is the *Pico El Ávila*, which rises to 2,159 meters (7,083 feet).[^cite_note-mountains-29]

The main body of water in Caracas is the [Guaire River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaire_River), which flows across the city and empties into the [Tuy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuy_River), which is also fed by the El Valle and [San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_de_los_Altos) rivers, in addition to numerous streams which descend from El Ávila. The La Mariposa and [Camatagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camatagua_reservoir?action=edit\&redlink=1) reservoirs provide water to the city.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The city is occasionally subject to earthquakes – notably in [1641](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1641_Caracas_earthquake), [1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1812_Caracas_earthquake), 1967 and [2026](83547391.md).

Geologically, Caracas was formed in the [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous) period, with much of the rest of the Caribbean, and sits on what is mostly [metamorphic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock). Deformation of the land in this period formed the region.[^cite_note-35]

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Caracas has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) (*Aw*), milder due to the elevation and within the [Venezuelan Coastal Range (Maritime Andes)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Coastal_Range). Caracas precipitation varies between 900 and 1,300 millimeters (35 and 51 inches) (annual), in the city proper to 2,000 millimeters (79 inches) in some parts of the Mountain range. While Caracas is within the tropics, due to its altitude, temperatures are cooler compared to other locations with a typical tropical savanna climate. The annual average temperature is approximately 23.4 °C (74 °F). The average of the coldest month, January, is 21.7 °C (71 °F). The average of the warmest month, May, is 24.5 °C (76 °F), which gives a small annual thermal amplitude of 2.8 °C (5.0 °F).[^cite_note-climate-38]

In December and January abundant fog may appear, in addition to a sudden nightly drop in temperature, until reaching 8 °C (46 °F).[^cite_note-climate-38] This peculiar weather is known by the natives of Caracas as the *Pacheco*. Nightly temperatures year round are much (7 to 11 °C) lower than daytime highs and usually do not remain above 24 °C (75 °F), resulting in very pleasant evening temperatures. Hailstorms appear in Caracas, although only on rare occasions. [Electrical storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_storm) are much more frequent, especially between June and October, due to the city being in a closed valley and the orographic action of Cerro El Ávila.[^cite_note-39]

### Hydrography

The hydrographic network of the city of Caracas is made up of the Guaire river basin which is a sub-basin of the Tuy river. This basin crosses the valley where the city is located from West to East (Las Adjuntas – [Petare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petare)). It covers about 655 square kilometers, about 45 km. long and about 15 km. wide, has a rectangular shape. The basin is formed by the Caracas Valley, which is relatively high, narrow and long, surrounded by the high and steep Cordillera de la Costa, which runs parallel to the Litoral.

Among the characteristics of the hydrographic network associated with the city of Caracas is the high degree of contamination present in the lower parts of the [Guaire River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaire_River) due to its tributaries having been used for sewage collection since 1874, during the government of Antonio Guzmán Blanco, until today. The level of contamination is such that it does not allow the maintenance of the life of the species that once populated the basin, and it has become extinct or has been restricted to the highest areas of the basin, mainly within the boundaries of [El Ávila National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_%C3%81vila_National_Park). Awareness for the recovery of the basin has recently begun, however, much remains to be done before results can be observed.

Among the main rivers and streams that make up the basin are in its northern slope: San Pedro River, Macarao River, Quebrada Caroata, Catuche River, Anauco River, Chacaito River, Tocome River, Caurimare River; on its southern slope we find that among the main [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributaries) are: El Valle river, Quebrada Baruta and Quebrada La Guairita. In the upper part of the basin there are two reservoirs with the purpose of supplying water to the western part of the city; These are the Macarao Dam and the La Mariposa Reservoir.

## Urbanism

| --- | --- |



Caracas seen from the Avila National Park

Caracas shares commonalities with many [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American) cities: densely populated and with limited space because it is surrounded by mountains. Because of this, the city has grown vertically. A very striking aspect is the number of people living in substandard housing built on the mountain slopes surrounding the city. This type of housing is called ranchos, built improvised, without any official planning, with deficiencies and inadequate materials, marking a difference to those who live in the valley proper; 45% of the population lives in these settlements, which comprise in 25% of the urban area.

The city center, developed around a small historic center, represents less than a quarter of the total area of the city, which has spread along the valley and has also been connected in recent years with satellite cities in the states of Miranda and La Guaira, creating a major metropolitan area known as Gran Caracas. The city's rapid population growth has resulted in increasing [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion). To this end, the subway transportation system (Metro de Caracas) has been progressively expanded and is currently linked to the [Los Teques Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Teques_Metro) and, in the future, to the Guarenas-Guatire Metro system. The "Ezequiel Zamora" Central Railway System also links the communities of Charallave and Cúa de los Valles del Tuy with the subway transportation system of the capital city.

Some areas of the city have a grid layout, either inherited from the [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) or developed during the urban projects of the 20th century. Other areas, built on the mountain slopes, do not follow this pattern, but adapt to the irregularities of the terrain. These elevated areas enjoy a temperate temperature throughout the year.

## Demographics



Bolivar Avenue

In 2011, the city proper of Caracas (Distrito Capital) had over 1.9 million inhabitants.[^cite_note-ine.gov.ve-43] The Metropolitan District of Caracas had an estimated 2.9 million in 2011. In 2025, the population of Caracas proper is over 3 million.[^cite_note-44] The majority of the population is [mixed-race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_Race), typically with varying degrees of [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe), [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_of_Africa) and occasionally [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Asia) ancestry. There is also a notable [Afro-Venezuelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Venezuelan) community.[^cite_note-ine.gov.ve-43][^cite_note-ReferenceA-45] The city has been shaped by a rich mix of ethnic, racial, and cultural groups throughout its history.

### Immigration

See also: [Immigration to Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Venezuela)

During the 1940s and 1950s, after the Second World War, a growing wave of [European immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_immigrants) began to settle in the country, primarily Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese people, with smaller communities of Germans ([Colonia Tovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonia_Tovar)), French, English, [Serbs](59512.md) and [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Venezuela). Because of this, Caracas has a large number of [European Venezuelans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Venezuelans) who descend from this influx of immigration. In particular, there are descendants of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_immigration_to_Venezuela), Portuguese, [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Venezuelans), Serbian, Chinese, Colombian, German, Syrian, and Lebanese people in Caracas.[^cite_note-ine.gov.ve-43][^cite_note-ReferenceA-45]

During the early 1960s, President [Rómulo Betancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B3mulo_Betancourt) followed the same policy as the [Marcos Pérez Jiménez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_P%C3%A9rez_Jim%C3%A9nez) administration: promoting immigration, especially from Latin American countries. These policies were maintained until the late 1980s, with a notable influx of Argentines, Uruguayans, Chileans, Cubans, Peruvians, Ecuadorians, Chinese, and Arabs. Towards the beginning of the 1980s, immigration was marked by a strong influx of Colombians.

### Growth

Caracas has exceeded the administrative limits of its perimeter due to accelerated population growth, so that its most suitable demographic study territory is the Metropolitan District or AMC. According to 2011 calculations by the National Institute of Statistics, the metropolitan city had a population for the 2011 census of 2,923,959 inhabitants. The Metropolitan District represents less than 1% of the national territory and is home to one-fifteenth of Venezuela's population. In percentage numbers, 9.2% of Venezuela's population lives in the five capital municipalities, out of the 335 municipalities that comprise Venezuela.

The region is called Greater Caracas or the Metropolitan Region of Caracas, and includes satellite cities and areas like [Altos Mirandinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altos_Mirandinos), the central coast of La Guaira, [Guarenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarenas), [Guatire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatire) and Valles del Tuy. This agglomeration had an estimated population of 4.3 million inhabitants in 2011.[^cite_note-46]

In the 20th century, an exodus of the peasantry to the capital and other cities intensified, motivated by a search for improvements in their quality of life. This led to depopulation of the rural areas of the country and demographic crowding of the centers. This caused more impoverished settlements in the outskirts of the city to expand; however Caracas still had the lowest unemployment rates in the entire country. In 1936, the total population of Venezuela had been the same as the estimated population of Greater Caracas in 2000: almost 4 million inhabitants. From 1936 to 1990, Caracas multiplied its population, although far less than other major cities such as Valencia, which in the same period multiplied its population by almost 25 times.

### Crime

Main article: [Crime in Venezuela](37476256.md)

Venezuela and its capital, Caracas, have been reported to both have among the highest per capita murder rates in the world.[^cite_note-seguridadjusticiaypaz-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-Consejo_Ciudadano_para_la_Seguridad_Pública_y_la_Justicia_Penal-52] In 2010 Caracas had the highest murder rate in the world,[^cite_note-53] having more deaths than [Baghdad](4492.md) during the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-54]

In 2019, Caracas was the [city with the sixth highest homicide rate in the world outside of a warzone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_murder_rate), with a rate of around 76 murders per 100,000 people. In 2023 it was the [nineteenth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_murder_rate), with a rate of around 52 murders per 100,000 people.[^cite_note-55] Most murders and other violent crimes go unsolved, with estimates of the number of unresolved crimes as high as 98%.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

## Economy



The Sabana Grande area

Businesses that are located in Caracas include service companies, banks, and malls, among others. It has a largely service-based economy, apart from some industrial activity in its metropolitan area.[^cite_note-Caracas-8] The [Caracas Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Stock_Exchange) and [Petróleos de Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr%C3%B3leos_de_Venezuela) (PDVSA) are headquartered here. PDVSA, a state-run organization, is the largest company in Venezuela,[^cite_note-59] and negotiates all the international agreements for the distribution and export of petroleum.[^cite_note-60] When it existed, the airline [Viasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasa) had its headquarters in the Torre Viasa.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

Several international companies and embassies are located in [El Rosal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Rosal,_Caracas) and [Las Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Mercedes_\(district_in_Caracas,_Venezuela\)), in the Caracas area. The city also serves as a hub for communication and transportation infrastructure between the metropolitan area and the rest of the country. Important industries in Caracas include chemicals, textiles, leather, [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry), iron, and wood products. There are also rubber and cement factories.[^cite_note-63] Its nominal GDP is US\$70 billion and the GDP (PPP) per capita is US\$24,000.[^cite_note-64]

A 2009 United Nations survey reported that the [cost of living](522934.md) in Caracas was 89% of that of the survey's baseline city, New York.[^cite_note-65]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Venezuela)



The historic center of Caracas

In 2013, the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) evaluated countries for Travel and Tourism competitiveness. Out of the 140 countries evaluated, Venezuela came 113th. Factors that contributed to the ranking included safety and security concerns and the low prioritisation of the tourism industry.[^cite_note-tourism2013-66]

In an attempt to attract more foreign visitors, the Venezuelan Ministry of Tourism invested in multiple hotel infrastructures. The largest hotel investment has been in the Hotel Alba Caracas. The cost for the general maintenance of the north and south towers of the hotel is approximately 231.5 million Venezuelan bolivars. Although the Venezuelan Ministry of Tourism has taken the initiative to recognize the importance of the tourism industry, the Venezuelan government has not placed the tourism industry as an economic priority. In 2013, the budget for the Ministry of Tourism was only 173.8 million bolivars, while the Ministry of the Youth received approximately 724.6 million bolivars.[^cite_note-tourism2013-66] The tourism industry in Venezuela contributes approximately 3.8 percent of the country GDP. The World Economic Forum predicts Venezuela's GDP to rise to 4.2 percent by 2022.[^cite_note-67]

## Government

On 8 March 2000, the year after a new constitution was introduced in Venezuela, it was decreed in *Gaceta Official* N° 36,906 that the Metropolitan District of Caracas would be created and that some of the powers of the [Libertador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertador_Bolivarian_Municipality), [Chacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chacao_Municipality), [Baruta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruta_Municipality), [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre_Municipality,_Miranda), and [El Hatillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hatillo_Municipality,_Miranda) municipalities would be delegated to the *Alcaldía Mayor*, physically located in the large Libertador municipality, in the center of the city.[^cite_note-68] The Metropolitan District of Caracas was suppressed on 20 December 2017 by the [Constituent National Assembly of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Constituent_National_Assembly).[^cite_note-69]

The [Simón Bolívar Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar_Symphony_Orchestra) is based in Caracas. ### Sports

Professional sports teams in the city include the [football](10568.md) clubs [Caracas Fútbol Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_F%C3%BAtbol_Club), [Deportivo Petare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_Petare), [Atlético Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Venezuela), [SD Centro Italo Venezolano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Centro_Italo_Venezolano), [Estrella Roja FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrella_Roja_FC) and [Deportivo La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_La_Guaira). ## Transportation



Railway Caracas – Cúa

The [Caracas Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Metro) has been in operation since 27 March 1983, with four lines, 47 stations and about 10 more to be constructed. It covers a great part of the city and also has an integrated ticket system that combines the route of the *Metro* with those offered by the *Metrobús*, a bus service of the Caracas Metro. In 2010, the first segment of a new aerial cable car system opened, [Metrocable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrocable_\(Caracas\)),[^cite_note-80] which feeds into the larger metro system.

Buses are the main means of [mass transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport). There are two bus systems: the traditional system and the *Metrobús*. Other transportation services include the [IFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Aut%C3%B3nomo_de_Ferrocarriles_del_Estado) train to and from the Tuy Valley cities of [Charallave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charallave) and [Cúa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%BAa); the [Simón Bolívar International Airport](8277069.md), the biggest and most important in the country; the metro additional services [Caracas Aerial Tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Aerial_Tramway) and [Los Teques Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Teques_Metro) (connecting Caracas with the suburban city of [Los Teques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Teques)); and the [Generalissimo Francisco de Miranda Air Base](6558466.md) used by military aviation and government airplanes.

### Roads and highways

The largest concentration of road networks in Venezuela is in the Caracas region and its surroundings, with a large network of highways and avenues in the Metropolitan District, as well as other urban, suburban and interurban roads. The city's road network has become a major crossroads between the western, eastern, and central regions of the country, causing traffic congestion problems as the city is already saturated with vehicles from both within the city and from its immediate area of influence in La Guaira and Miranda, as well as from more distant parts of Venezuela.

Currently, a link is being built that will connect the Central Regional highway (at km 31) with the Gran Mariscal de Ayacucho highway (Kempis sector), in order to serve as a spillway to the city of Caracas and neighboring Guarenas and Guatire, so that vehicles that go from east to west or center, and vice versa, do not have to enter Caracas. The route of this highway would extend from the vicinity of the Charallave airport, passing through Santa Lucía and going up to the Kempis area (between Guatire and [Caucagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucagua)).

Traffic in Caracas is heavily congested due to the high population density and large number of vehicles, causing traffic jams at any time in the day. Peak rush hour traffic is particularly bad, where vehicles can take up to 3 hours to get out of the congestion.

Since 1986, a mysterious black substance referred to as [La Mancha Negra (The Black Stain)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mancha_Negra) has oozed from roads has caused many car accidents killing multiple people.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

Coche Station, Caracas Metro

The Caracas Metro is one of the city's main forms of [public transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transportation), with 47 operating stations transporting about 2 million passengers, according to official figures. The Caracas Metro system runs across the central part of the city from east to west, with additional lines that connect the southwest with other [surface transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_transportation) systems of the Metro system (BusCaracas, MetroCable San Agustín). To the southeast, the network is connected to Cabletrén de Petare and Metrocable Mariche. Most stations also have Metrobús feeder routes.&#x20;

The system is expanding, with projects like the extension of Line 3 to the [La Rinconada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rinconada_\(Caracas_Metro\)) station, creation of other intermediate stations along that route, and an interconnection between Plaza Venezuela and the [Capuchinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capuchinos_\(Caracas_Metro\)) station. There is also a planned connection to the Guarenas-Guatire Metro system, beginning with the Bello Monte station. An extension also reaches the city of [Los Teques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Teques), the capital of Miranda. The three stations that comprise this last line make up the Los Teques Metro System. Both systems are operated by the company C. A. Metro de Caracas (Cametro).

The system inaugurated in 1983 is 71 km long and has five lines, being one of the longest in Latin America. Expansion plans also include an extension to the Baruta and El Hatillo municipalities, as well as to other metrocable systems in the city center.

Caracas Metrocable

The Caracas Metrocable is a [cable car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_lift) system integrated into the Caracas Metro, conceived in such a way that residents of Caracas who are located in mountainous areas can travel in a faster and safer way to the city center. It functions as a feeder route in the style of the metrobus.

In 2011, a new metrocable system was built in the parish of San Agustín del Sur, where people can freely enjoy a view of a large part of Caracas.

There is a national railway project, which aims to connect Caracas with the central area of the country. Currently, the Caracas-[Cúa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%BAa) train service is operating, belonging to the Central Railway System of Venezuela "Ezequiel Zamora I". Inaugurated in 2006, it is the only railway section currently operating in Venezuela, according to the Instituto de Ferrocarriles del Estado (IFE). This commuter train line connects the city of Caracas with the towns of the [Valles del Tuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valles_del_Tuy) (Charallave, Santa Lucía, Ocumare, Santa Teresa, Yare and Cúa) and runs for 41.4 kilometers.

The network begins its journey at the Libertador Simón Bolivar station in Caracas, located in La Rinconada and connected to line 3 of the Caracas metro. From the city it communicates with three other stations: Charallave Norte Francisco de Miranda and Charallave Sur Don Simón Rodríguez; both in the town of Charallave and ends at the Cúa General Ezequiel Zamora station (Cúa, [Miranda](556284.md)).
