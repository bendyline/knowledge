The **Embassy of the United States to Bahrain** is the [diplomatic mission](8970.md) of the [United States](3434750.md) in [Bahrain](18933277.md). The building is located in [Zinj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinj,_Bahrain), a district of the capital, [Manama](20481.md).[^cite_note-Location-1] The post of [U.S. Ambassador to Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Ambassador_to_Bahrain) is currently held by [Stephanie L. Hallett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Hallett).

The embassy is one of two major American installations in Bahrain, the other being [Naval Support Activity Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain), a base which houses the main [United States Navy](20518076.md) operations in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).[^cite_note-Navy-2]

## History

The United States formally acknowledged the sovereignty of the State of Bahrain on August 15, 1971 coinciding with its independence from the [British Empire](31717.md).[^cite_note-history.state.gov-3][^cite_note-policy-history-4]

Embassy Manama was officially inaugurated on September 21, 1971, with John N. Gatch, Jr. assuming the role of Chargé d'Affaires ad interim. The first official ambassador, [William Stoltzfus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Stoltzfus), presented his credentials to the Bahraini government on February 17, 1972, while maintaining residence in [Kuwait](7515890.md). It was not until 1974 that Ambassador [Joseph W. Twinam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_W._Twinam) submitted his credentials and became the first American ambassador to take up residence at Embassy Manama.[^cite_note-policy-history-4]

The embassy was dedicated on 4 July 1990 and occupied in December that year. The building was constructed in accordance with [Department of State](31975.md) security requirements.[^cite_note-5]

## Protests

Bahraini citizens and activists have protested outside of the embassy for various reasons, including anti-Iraq War demonstrations, the presence of the [U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Fleet_\(United_States_Navy\)) in Bahrain, and to protest Israeli actions in the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-6] The U.S. had seen by some as a proxy for the Israeli government when [Israel and Bahrain did not have diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain%E2%80%93Israel_relations).[^cite_note-7]
