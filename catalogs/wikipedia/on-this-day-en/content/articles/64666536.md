The **Alliance for the Union of Romanians** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Alianța pentru Unirea Românilor*, **AUR**) is a [right-wing populist](8354382.md) and [far-right](221220.md) political party active in [Romania](25445.md), with a branch in the neighbouring [Republic of Moldova](19260.md) too. It was founded in 2019 by [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion) and [Claudiu Târziu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudiu_T%C3%A2rziu) and as of 2026, it is the second largest party in both chambers of the [Parliament of Romania](4462971.md), namely the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) and the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Romania\)).

The party was founded in order to take part in the 2020 [local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_local_elections) and [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_parliamentary_election) elections in Romania, its main leader Simion formerly candidating in the [2019 European election in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_Romania) as an independent candidate, failing to obtain a place as he did not receive the required number of votes. Despite almost no success localy, the party unexpectedly obtained 9% of the votes in the parliamentary elections, making it the fourth largest party in the Parliament. Four years later, it obtained 18% of the votes, making it the second largest party.[^cite_note-o750-23]

AUR's manifesto aims for the unification of all [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians), particularly focusing on the [union of Moldova and Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Moldova_and_Romania). The AUR logo itself represents a map of Romania composed of a line depicting the actual border of the country and [eight stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Europe) that are overextended in the right part as to include Moldova within that border too. George Simion has also been regularly seen in public wearing a pin with the map of the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) at its 1918–1940 territorial apogee. Due to the promoted actions and speech from its members, the party has been described as [conservative](6675.md), far-right, [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) or [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascism) (particularly [neo-Legionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Legionarism)), [pro-Russian](2541825.md), [pro-Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism), and as promoting [anti-Hungarian sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hungarian_sentiment).[^cite_note-romania.europalibera.org-18][^cite_note-Pîrvulescu:_Ce_propune_AUR-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-RP-27]

The party further states its four pillars are [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_values), [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_nationalism), [Christian faith](384458.md), and [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty) and has close ties with the [Romanian Orthodox Church](384458.md).[^cite_note-28]

## History

### Background and founding

The Alliance for the Union of Romanians was formally established on 19 September 2019.[^cite_note-29] Later, during the [Great Union Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Union_Day) of [Romania](25445.md) on 1 December 2019, its leader, [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion), said the party's aims were to participate in the [2020 Romanian local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_local_elections) and [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_legislative_election) elections of the country.[^cite_note-30] Simion had up to this point been a campaigner for the [unification of Romania and the Republic of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Moldova_and_Romania).[^cite_note-g4-6] The party began as an anti-vaxx movement during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-31] [Claudiu Târziu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudiu_T%C3%A2rziu), who was co-president of the party along Simion until 27 March 2022,[^cite_note-GS-32] was a member of the [Coalition for Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coali%C8%9Bia_pentru_Familie) which unsuccessfully campaigned to ban gay marriage through constitutional change in a [2018 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Romanian_constitutional_referendum).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-FR-34]

On 26 June 2020, AUR condemned the disinterest of the Romanian authorities regarding the [minority rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_rights) of the [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) in [Serbia](29265.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md) and declared that it would fully support them once it entered the [Romanian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Parliament).[^cite_note-35] Two days later, AUR also condemned the 80th anniversary of the [annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_northern_Bukovina) of [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia), [Northern Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Bukovina), and the [Hertsa region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertsa_region) by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), declaring that "it is our obligation to regain our state".[^cite_note-36] By July 2020, AUR counted 22 branches in Europe and North America for the [Romanian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_diaspora).[^cite_note-37] The first of these was established in [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton), in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-38] AUR was the only party in Romania that expressed support for [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in the lead-up to the [2020 United States presidential election](48410011.md).[^cite_note-europa_liberă-39]

During the [2020 Romanian local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_local_elections) on 27 September, AUR won the [mayoralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoralty) in three towns: [Amara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amara,_Romania), [Pufești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pufe%C8%99ti), and [Valea Lungă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valea_Lung%C4%83,_Alba).[^cite_note-40]

### Election to Parliament and first legislature (2020–2024)

In the 2020 Romanian legislative election, AUR obtained a high percentage of the votes, being called as the "surprise" of Romania. The results also increased the popularity of the party on the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet).[^cite_note-g4-6][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Many members of the [Romanian Orthodox Church](384458.md) campaigned for the AUR during the 2020 Romanian legislative election.[^cite_note-43] The party came first among [Romanians in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians_in_Italy), the largest group of the Romanian diaspora, and ran a close second among [Romanians in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians_in_France) and [Romanians in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians_in_Spain). It also scored first in [Cyprus](5593.md).[^cite_note-44] AUR's candidate for prime minister was [Călin Georgescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Georgescu), who worked for the [United Nations](31769.md) for 17 years.[^cite_note-45] According to a statement released by AUR on 8 December 2020, 15,000 Romanians joined the party in just 24 hours. The party got 47 MPs in the 2020–2024 Romanian legislature.[^cite_note-46]

The party achieved good results in rural areas of [Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Moldavia) and [Dobruja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobruja), areas traditionally dominated by the other big parties. Its most significant percentages were in the counties where the [Romanian Orthodox Church](384458.md) has a strong influence and a large number of practicing believers. These are [Suceava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suceava_County) (14.72%), [Botoșani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boto%C8%99ani_County) (14.62%), [Neamț](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neam%C8%9B_County) (14.4%), [Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba_County) (14.2%), and [Vrancea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrancea_County) (13.43%).[^cite_note-DR-47] The party speculated the new communication channels (social networks) in a similar way to the [Greater Romania Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Romania_Party) (PRM) of the late 1990s - early 2000s, which used the newspaper "România Mare" (*Greater Romania*) as a communication channel, reaching high electoral scores. Another example is the [People's Party – Dan Diaconescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_%E2%80%93_Dan_Diaconescu) (PP-DD), which was propelled with the help of the [OTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTV_\(Romanian_TV_channel\)) television channel.[^cite_note-DR-47]

Recorder, a Romanian online publisher, argues that the election campaign of AUR has adapted to the rural environment, which lacks modern technology, relying more on messages desired by the masses than on a coherent ideology. In this way, they argue, in addition to a core of supporters who voted for radical messages, there is also the wider category of electorate strictly attracted by populist messages.[^cite_note-DR-47]

On 22 January 2021, Simion announced that the party would officially adhere at European level to the "[European Conservatives and Reformists Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Conservatives_and_Reformists_Party)" after going on visits in Poland and [Brussels](3708.md), Belgium.[^cite_note-48] Simion announced on 15 March 2021 that the AUR had intentions to start operating in the [Republic of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Moldova) on the occasion of the [Day of the Union of Bessarabia with Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Union_of_Bessarabia_with_Romania) celebrated every 27 March.[^cite_note-49] The party was officially launched, as previously stated, on 27 March 2021, and the elected president of the party was Vlad Bilețchi, a renowned Moldovan unionist.[^cite_note-50] This new section of the AUR in Moldova later participated in the [Moldovan snap parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Moldovan_parliamentary_election) of 11 July 2021.[^cite_note-51]

On 2 October 2021, AUR organised a 15,000–20,000 people-strong protest against [COVID-19](63030231.md) restrictions at the [Victory Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Square,_Bucharest) in Bucharest, drawing both national and international attention and being the most attended protest in Romania since the start of the pandemic.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] On 5 October 2021, a [motion of no confidence](208101.md) initiated by AUR, but legally proposed by PSD, was passed with 281 votes, thus dismissing the [Cîțu Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%AE%C8%9Bu_Cabinet).[^cite_note-54]

On 27 March 2022, AUR held its first party congress at the [Palace of the Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Parliament). On it, it was intended to elect the party's president. There were two candidates, Simion and Dănuț Aelenei, AUR deputy in the Constanța County. Aelenei claimed to have nominated himself with the simple intention of showing that AUR was a democratic party and that he did not intend to "expel" Simion from the party, admitting that he was less well-known compared to him. 784 voted for Simion and 38 for Aelenei, making Simion the party's sole president after having previously shared leadership with Târziu, who became president of the party's CNC.[^cite_note-GS-32] In November 2022, Simion met with Israel's ambassador to Romania, Reuven Azar. The encounter drew outrage from some Israelis and diaspora Jews, as AUR is officially boycotted by Israel due to its history of judeophobia.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] On 29 January 2023, [Ramona-Ioana Bruynseels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramona-Ioana_Bruynseels), a former candidate for the [Humanist Power Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Liberal_Humanist_Party) in the [2019 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Romanian_presidential_election), joined AUR.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### Presidential elections and second legislature (2024–present)

In the first round of the [2024 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_presidential_election) on 24 November, Simion received 13.9% of the vote, not preceding to the planned runoff on 8 December. However, the runoff was never held as due to [accusations of Russian interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusations_of_Russian_interference_in_the_2024_Romanian_presidential_election) in favour of first round winner [Călin Georgescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Georgescu). In the [2024 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_parliamentary_election) a week later, AUR received 18% of the vote, becoming the second largest party in both houses of the Romanian Parliament.[^cite_note-67]

The aftermath of the first presidential vote was controversial and led [Romania](25445.md) to the brink of a political crisis,[^cite_note-68] with AUR aligning itself with Georgescu, arranging [protests in his favour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Romanian_election_annulment_protests).[^cite_note-69]

Simion won the first round of the [2025 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Romanian_presidential_election) on 4 May with 41% of the vote, losing the runoff on 18 May to Independent candidate [Nicușor Dan](35543359.md) with 46.4% against Dan's 53.6%.[^cite_note-70] In early June, geopolitician [Dan Dungaciu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Dungaciu), lawyer [Silvia Uscov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvia_Uscov?action=edit\&redlink=1) and former [SOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.O.S._Romania) member [Andrei Gușă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Gu%C8%99%C4%83?action=edit\&redlink=1) joined AUR.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

On 22 January 2026, Petru Gabriel Negrea, an AUR deputy and a bussinessman who had offered support to [Ukrainian refugees](70199806.md), stated he had been banned from entering Ukraine. He stated that Ukrainian border authorities had verbally informed him that he was considered an "enemy of the Ukrainian people", with him having been detained "in degrading conditions and physically pushed across the border", adding he had been forced to fully undress for a body search, which he described as deliberate humiliation. The deputy attributed this treatment to his public defense of the rights of the ethnic [Romanians in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians_in_Ukraine), and pointed out double standards as Romanian support for Ukraine in the context of the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) took place while the Romanian minority in Ukraine was at the same time being "abandoned" as Negrea described it.[^cite_note-73] AUR stated on 25 January it would submit official requests regarding the case to the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Romania\)) and the Ukrainian embassy in Romania.[^cite_note-74]

## Ideology

The party claims it is a [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right),[^cite_note-75] [patriotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic),[^cite_note-76] and [Christian democratic](328969.md) party.[^cite_note-77] However, it is described as [far-right](221220.md)[^cite_note-85] by third-party sources.

> AUR is young as a party, but it didn't come out of nowhere. It brings together some people whose history is linked to the [late period of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_communism_in_Romania). Its candidates are experts in [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda), intellectuals with more or less open sympathy for [legionnaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Guard) and legionary or pro-legionary intellectuals, businessmen and itinerant politicians who wander from one radical party to another.

— [Oliver Jens Schmitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Jens_Schmitt)[^cite_note-86]

According to the party's website, AUR's ultimate goal is to achieve the unification of all Romanians "wherever they are located, in [Bucharest](36877.md), [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ia%C8%99i), [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara), [Cernăuți](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi), [Timoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timok_Valley), [Voivodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodina), Italy, or Spain", while wanting to unite Romania and [Moldova](19260.md) together, as well as land with Romanian speakers in neighboring countries.[^cite_note-87] The party has been called [irredentist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irredentism).[^cite_note-n673-88]

The party's website names four pillars for the party: [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_values), [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_nationalism), [Christian faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_faith), and [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty).[^cite_note-rfed8-89][^cite_note-FR-34] The party characterises its members as "the defenders of the Church".[^cite_note-europa_liberă-39] It is opposed to "[gender ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_ideology)" and believes that a nation has no chance of surviving "unless it cultivates the original pattern of the classic family".[^cite_note-adevărul-90] The party opposes same-sex marriage, [euthanasia](9587.md) and [medically-assisted suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_suicide).[^cite_note-91] The AUR wants to introduce an [anti-LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_LGBTQ_people) law based on the [Hungarian anti-LGBTQ law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_anti-LGBTQ_law).[^cite_note-DW,_2021-84] The party also opposes abortion.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

The party and some of its members have been linked to the [Legionary Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Guard) or intellectuals and people who support this ideology and [neo-Legionarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Legionarism).[^cite_note-98]

The party's representatives became popular on [social media](5897742.md) as a result of their positioning against measures taken by the government during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Romania). Leading members, such as Șoșoacă (later expelled), gained thousands of followers. AUR has been described as supporting "anti-medicine, anti-vaccination" rhetoric.[^cite_note-FR-34] This accusation was rejected by George Simion, president of AUR, claiming that the party supports the "freedom of choice".[^cite_note-99] The party's manifesto opposes [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) and condemns [atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and claims that [Christians](18337522.md) are persecuted in Romania.[^cite_note-digi-81] The party has been critical of the impact of the [local autonomy of Hungarians in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9kely_autonomy_movement) on the rights of ethnic Romanians in the centre of the country (where the [Hungarians](17106358.md) are the majority),[^cite_note-adevărul-90] leading to accusations of being [Magyarophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hungarian_sentiment).[^cite_note-g4-6] The latter accusation was rejected by the president of AUR.[^cite_note-100]

Simion has cited [Law and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_Justice_\(Poland\)) and [Fidesz](404647.md), the ruling parties in Poland and Hungary respectively, as some of his models.[^cite_note-NP-101][^cite_note-102]

Despite this, AUR has also expressed deep criticism of Fidesz, stating that it would not join the same group in the [European Parliament](9581.md) as Fidesz, due to its claims on Romanian [territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory).[^cite_note-103] However, AUR later reversed its stance, expressing openness to Fidesz joining the [European Conservatives and Reformists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Conservatives_and_Reformists_Group).[^cite_note-104]

The AUR is [Eurosceptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosceptic).[^cite_note-mei-2][^cite_note-romania.europalibera.org-18][^cite_note-Belton-19][^cite_note-Griera-20] Dan Tapalaga, the editor of the independent news portal *[G4 Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G4_Media_\(website\))*, described the AUR in 2023 as, "extremist and anti-Semitic … based on isolationist nationalism, anti-Europeanism, economic nationalism, traditionalism and \[Christian] Orthodoxy".[^cite_note-105]

AUR has addressed three issues in [Serbia](29265.md) of public interest: Romanian-language education for the [Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs_of_Serbia) of the Timok Valley, which the party refers to as Romanians, the church and their right to [church services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_service) in Romanian, and pollution in the area of the town of [Bor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bor,_Serbia).[^cite_note-106] On 24 March 2021, Târziu declared that AUR would oppose Serbia's [accession to the European Union](10038148.md) over the Vlachs' situation, as "no sort of their rights are respected", "in line with the rights that [Romanian citizens of Serbian ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Romania) in Romania have". Târziu also stated he had notified the [European Commission](9974.md) about this matter.[^cite_note-107] With regards to [Kosovo](17391.md), the party supports the [Serbian](29265.md) stance on its [political status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Kosovo), considering it to be part of Serbia.[^cite_note-108]

By 2023, the party had become critical of Romanian military support for Ukraine in the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), suggesting that the war is "not ours". AUR also criticised the transit of Ukrainian agricultural products through Romania, and Simion has been banned from entering Ukraine.[^cite_note-109] Nevertheless, the party leadership is critical of relations with Russia, with Simion calling for the expulsion of the Russian Ambassador and closing Russian consulates in Romania following Russian threats against Romania in December 2023.[^cite_note-110]

AUR takes a strongly pro-Israel stance, supporting the expansion of [Israeli settlements](15123.md) in the Palestinian [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-RP-27] Simion has called for Western nations to "stop exporting wars", suggesting that the downfall of the "strong Syrian state" during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) had increased illegal immigration.[^cite_note-111]

AUR wishes to ensure Romania's self-sufficiency in energy, the prosecution of those deemed responsible for mismanaged post-communist [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) projects, and a fight against [illegal logging](465780.md) by banning the export of non-processed wood.[^cite_note-NP-101] The party has a senate seat, which is equivalent to the National Executive Committee of other Romanian parties such as the PSD, the [National Liberal Party](863906.md) (PNL), and the [Save Romania Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Romania_Union) (USR).[^cite_note-112]
