**North India** is a [geographical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_region), loosely defined as a [cultural region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_region) comprising the northern part of [India](14533.md) wherein [Indo-Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryans) (speaking Indo-Aryan languages) form the prominent majority population, while [Tibeto-Burmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages) (speaking Tibeto-Burman languages) form the significant minority population. It extends from the [Himalayan mountain range](37966.md) in the north to the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain), the [Thar Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert), till [Central Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Highlands_\(India\)). It occupies nearly two-quarters of the area and population of India and includes one of the three [mega cities of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_cities_by_population#List): [Delhi](37756.md). In a more specific and administrative sense, North India can also be used to represent the northern Indo-Gangetic Plain within this broader expanse, to the Thar Desert.[^cite_note-Frykenberg2008-2]

Several major rivers flow through the region including the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus), the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges), the [Yamuna](354286.md) and the [Narmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada) rivers. North India includes the states of [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), [Uttarakhand](1429154.md), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India), [Haryana](14189.md), [Rajasthan](26291.md), [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), and the [union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) of [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh), [Delhi](37756.md), [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and [Ladakh](303611.md). Occasionally, states of [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_India), [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India) and [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India) India are referred as "North Indian" in a broader term.[^cite_note-States-1] [^cite_note-Indian_Zonal_Council-16][^cite_note-isc.nic-17][^cite_note-MoM-GSI-18]

The majority of people in North India speak [Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages). The region was the historical centre of the ancient [Vedic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_culture), the [Mahajanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapada), the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire); the medieval [Pratihara dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratihara_dynasty) and the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate); and the modern [Mughal India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_India) and [Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire), among many others. . It has a diverse culture, and includes the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) pilgrimage centres of [Char Dham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Char_Dham), [Haridwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridwar), [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), [Vindhyachal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhyachal), [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya), [Mathura](283812.md), [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj), [Vaishno Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishno_Devi) and [Pushkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkar), the [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) pilgrimage centres of [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath) and [Kushinagar](33686220.md), the [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) pilgrimage centre of [Golden Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Temple), [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar), as well as world heritage sites such as the [Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi_Biosphere_Reserve), [Khajuraho Temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajuraho), [Hill Forts of Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Forts_of_Rajasthan), [Jantar Mantar (Jaipur)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar_\(Jaipur\)), [Qutb Minar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_Minar), [Red Fort](1681410.md), [Agra Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra_Fort), [Fatehpur Sikri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri) and the [Taj Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_Mahal). North India's culture developed as a result of [interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga-Jamuni_tehzeeb) between Hindu and Muslim religious traditions.[^cite_note-Dhulipala2000-19]

## Northern Region/Zone



States under Northern Zonal Council in *orange*

The terms 'North Zone,' 'North Region,' or 'Northern Cultural Zone' are used by various ministries of the [Government of India](553883.md) to refer to the northernmost administrative division of the country, whether one of four or six. These terms are distinct from 'North India,' which refers to a much larger geo-cultural region.

### Government of India definitions

The [Northern Zonal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Zonal_Council) is one of the advisory councils, created in 1956 by the [States Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act) to foster interstate co-operation under the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_\(India\)), which included the states of [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh), [Delhi](37756.md), [Haryana](14189.md), [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md), [Ladakh](303611.md), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) and [Rajasthan](26291.md).[^cite_note-Indian_Zonal_Council-16][^cite_note-isc.nic-17]

The [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(India\)) established the [North Culture Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Zones_of_India) in [Patiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patiala), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) on 23 March 1985. It differs from the North Zonal Council in its inclusion of [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) and the omission of [Delhi](37756.md).[^cite_note-MoC-North-20]

The [Geological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Survey_of_India) (part of the [Ministry of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Mines_\(India\))) in its Northern Region, included [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) and [Delhi](37756.md), but excluded [Rajasthan](26291.md) and [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh), with a regional headquarters in [Lucknow](392095.md).[^cite_note-MoM-GSI-18]

### Colloquial definitions of Northern Region/Zone

#### Indian press definition

*[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu)* newspaper puts Bihar, Delhi and Uttar Pradesh related articles on its *North* pages.[^cite_note-TheHindu-Apr2017-3] Articles in the Indian press have included the states of Bihar,[^cite_note-TNN-marriages-4] Gujarat,[^cite_note-The_Hindu,_May_22,_2016-7][^cite_note-The_Hindu,_July_27,_2016-6] Jharkhand,[^cite_note-Jharkhand-10] Madhya Pradesh,[^cite_note-FP-overtake-5] and West Bengal[^cite_note-Daily_Bhaskar,_May_12,_2015-8][^cite_note-The_Hindu,_January_26,_2016-9] in North as well.

#### Latitude-based definition

The [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer), which divides the [temperate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_zone) from the [tropical zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_zone) in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), runs through India, and could theoretically be regarded as a geographical dividing line in the country.[^cite_note-dubey1961-21] Indian states that are entirely above the Tropic of Cancer are [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India), [Haryana](14189.md), [Delhi](37756.md), [Uttarakhand](1429154.md), [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) and [Bihar](442482.md) and most of [North East Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_India) states. However that definition would also include major parts of [Rajasthan](26291.md), [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), [Jharkhand](197225.md) and [West Bengal](34040.md) and minor regions of [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) and [Gujarat](53707.md).

#### Anecdotal usage

In [Maharashtra](20629.md), the term "North Indian" is sometimes used to describe migrants from Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, often using the term *bhaiya* (which literally means 'elder brother') along with it in a derogatory sense.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Deccan2012-23] However within Uttar Pradesh (literally meaning "North Province" in Hindi) itself, "the cultural divide between the [east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Uttar_Pradesh) and the [west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Uttar_Pradesh) is considerable, with the *purabiyas* (easterners) often being clubbed with Biharis in the perception of the westerners."[^cite_note-mazumdar1988-24][^cite_note-raghavan2007-25] The [Government of Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Bihar) official site places the state in the eastern part of India.[^cite_note-26] Uttar Pradesh and Bihar are often considered as being a part of north India, however, within most modern definitions of North India, Bihar is not included.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Deccan2012-23][^cite_note-28]

## History

Main article: [India § History](14533.md#History)

### Ancient era

By 55,000 years ago, the first modern humans, or *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)*, had arrived on the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) from [Africa](5334607.md), where they had earlier evolved.[^cite_note-PetragliaAllchin-29][^cite_note-Dyson2018p1-30][^cite_note-Fisher2018p23-31] The earliest known modern human remains in [South Asia](21566765.md) date to about 30,000 years ago.[^cite_note-PetragliaAllchin-29] After 6500 BC, evidence for domestication of food crops and animals, construction of permanent structures, and storage of agricultural surplus appeared in [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh) and other sites in [Balochistan, Pakistan](4687.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConinghamYoung2015104–105-32] These gradually developed into the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulkeRothermund200421–23-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConinghamYoung2015104–105-32] the first urban culture in South Asia,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009181-34] which flourished during 2500–1900 BC northwestern Indian subcontinent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPossehl20032-35] Among its urban centres, [Kalibangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibangan) (in Rajasthan) and [Rakhigarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhigarhi) (in Haryana) were prominent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009181-34] These cities participated in a complex system of urban planning, long-distance trade, and craft production, sustained through a variety of subsistence strategies.



Urban planning and fire altars at [Kalibangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibangan) reflect Harappan-era religious and civic life in northwestern India.

Between 2000 BC and 1500 BC, several waves of Indo-Aryan migrations from Central Asia occurred and these migrants settled in the Indo-Gangetic Plain. The [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas), the oldest scriptures associated with [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009186–187-36] were composed during this period,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWitzel200368–69-37] and historians have analysed these to posit a [Vedic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period) in the [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region) and the upper [Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009255-38] During the period 2000–500 BCE, many regions of the subcontinent transitioned from the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) cultures to the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) ones.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009255-38] The [caste system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_system), which created a hierarchy of priests ([Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmins)), warriors [Kshatriyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kshatriyas), and commoners and peasants ([Vaishyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishyas) and [Shudras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shudras)), and but which excluded certain peoples whose occupations were considered impure, arose during this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulkeRothermund200441–43-39]

In the late Vedic period, around the 6th century BCE, the small states and chiefdoms of the Ganges Plain and the north-western regions had consolidated into 16 major oligarchies and monarchies that were known as the *[mahajanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapadas)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009260–265-40][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulkeRothermund200453–54-41] Among these, [Kuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_kingdom), [Panchala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchala), [Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosala_\(Mahajanapada\)), and [Kashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81%C5%9B%C4%AB_\(kingdom\)), all located in present-day [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) and Haryana, figured prominently. The emerging urbanisation gave rise to non-Vedic religious movements, two of which became independent religions. [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) came into prominence during the life of its exemplar, [Mahavira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavira).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009312–313-42] [Buddhism](3267529.md), based on the teachings of [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha), attracted followers from all social classes excepting the middle class; chronicling the life of the Buddha was central to the beginnings of recorded history in India.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulkeRothermund200454–56-43][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein199821-44][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein199867–68-45] In an age of increasing urban wealth, both religions held up [renunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nekkhamma) as an ideal,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009300-46] and both established long-lasting monastic traditions. Notably, cities like [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath), [Shravasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravasti), and [Kushinagar](33686220.md), became closely associated with the life of the Buddha and the early Buddhist sangha.



The [Ashoka Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_Pillar) at [Prayagraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj) also contains inscriptions attributed to the [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) emperor [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta).

Although the Mauryan capital was located in [Pataliputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataliputra), cities such as [Kaushambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosambi) and [Prayag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj) in Uttar Pradesh gained prominence as administrative and religious centres. The Magadhan Mauryan emperors are known as much for their empire-building and determined management of public life as for [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka)'s renunciation of militarism and far-flung advocacy of the Buddhist *[dhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma_\(Buddhism\))*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009367-47][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulkeRothermund200463-48] Under Ashoka, the spread of Buddhism was institutionalised, and pillar inscriptions appeared across northern India, including sites in Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan.

By the 4th and 5th centuries, the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) had created a complex system of administration and taxation in the greater Ganges Plain; this system became a model for later Indian kingdoms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulkeRothermund200489–91-49][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009545-50] Under the Guptas, a renewed Hinduism based on devotion, rather than the management of ritual, began to assert itself.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein199898–99-51] This renewal was reflected in a flowering of [sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture_in_the_Indian_subcontinent) and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_India), which found patrons among an urban elite.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009545-50] [Classical Sanskrit literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_literature#Classical_Sanskrit_literature) flowered as well, and [Indian science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science_and_technology_in_the_Indian_subcontinent), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_astronomy), [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda), and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_mathematics) made significant advances.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2009545-50]

Following the decline of the Guptas, the Indian early medieval age, from 600 to 1200 AD, is defined by regional kingdoms and cultural diversity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998132-52] When [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha) of [Kannauj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannauj), who ruled much of the Indo-Gangetic Plain from 606 to 647 CE, attempted to expand southwards, he was defeated by the [Chalukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty) ruler of the Deccan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998119–120-53] When his successor attempted to expand eastwards, he was defeated by the [Pala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) king of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998119–120-53] In Rajasthan, a constellation of Rajput clans rose to power, forging new dynasties and asserting martial values through courtly traditions and fortified architecture.

At the same time, the hill regions of [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) and [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) remained under smaller principalities, shaped by geographic isolation and local cultural traditions. In the plains, cities such as [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer), [Kannauj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannauj), and [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) emerged as religious and commercial centres, supported by an expanding temple economy and patronage networks. During this time, pastoral peoples, whose land had been cleared to make way for the growing agricultural economy, were accommodated within caste society, as were new non-traditional ruling classes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998121–122-54] The caste system consequently began to show regional differences.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998121–122-54]

### Indo-Muslim era

After the 10th century, Muslim Central Asian nomadic clans, using [swift-horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courser_\(horse\)) cavalry and raising vast armies united by ethnicity and religion, repeatedly overran South Asia's north-western plains. A general [Qutub-ud-din Aibak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutub-ud-din_Aibak) declared his independence and established the [Sultanate of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Delhi) in 1206.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELudden200268-55] The sultanate was to control much of North India and to make many forays into South India. Although at first disruptive for the Indian elites, the sultanate largely left its vast non-Muslim subject population to its own laws and customs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot200847-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf20066-57] By repeatedly repulsing [Mongol raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) in the 13th century, the sultanate saved India from the devastation visited on West and Central Asia, setting the scene for centuries of [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_India#Medieval_era) of fleeing soldiers, learned men, mystics, traders, artists, and artisans from that region into the subcontinent, thereby creating a syncretic Indo-Islamic culture in the north.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELudden200267-58][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot200850–51-59]



The three Battles of [Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipat) fought in [1526](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Panipat), [1556](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Panipat), and [1761](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Panipat) were pivotal in shaping the political landscape of North India.

In the early 16th century, northern India, then under mainly Muslim rulers,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobb200180-60] fell again to the superior mobility and firepower of a new generation of Central Asian warriors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998164-61] A Turco-Mongol emir, [Zahir-ud-din Mohammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahir-ud-din_Mohammad) "Babur", after defeating the Delhi Sultanate, upgraded himself from [Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) and proclaimed himself as the [Padishah of Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padishah_of_Hindustan). His successors were called [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughals) or Moguls by European historians owing to the dynasty's Mongol origins. They did not stamp out the local societies it came to rule. Instead, it balanced and pacified them through new administrative practices[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008115-62][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobb200190–91-63] and diverse and inclusive ruling elites,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200617-64] leading to more systematic, centralised, and uniform rule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008152-65] Eschewing tribal bonds and Islamic identity, especially under [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar), the Mughals united their far-flung realms through loyalty, expressed through a Persianised culture, to an emperor who had near-divine status.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200617-64]

The State's economic policies, deriving most revenues from agriculture[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008158-66] and mandating that taxes be paid in the well-regulated silver currency,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998169-67] caused peasants and artisans to enter larger markets.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008152-65] The relative peace maintained by the empire during much of the 17th century was a factor in the economic expansion of the region,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008152-65] resulting in greater patronage of [painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_painting), literary forms, textiles, and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008186-68] During this time, religious movements such as [Sikhism](27964.md) grew in strength in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), particularly under later Gurus, who adopted a militarised posture in response to growing Mughal intolerance.

### Modern era

By the early 18th century, Mughal authority had weakened. In its place, regional powers in northern and western India, such as the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire), the [Rajputs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajput), and the [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire) asserted political control.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200623–24-69] This fragmentation, coupled with growing European trading influence,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008286-70][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200644–49-71] created conditions that favoured British expansion. The [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company), leveraging military superiority and access to local revenue systems, took control of much of North India by the early 19th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobb200198–100-72][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008286-70][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELudden2002128–132-73][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200651–55-74] The annexation of [Delhi](37756.md) (1803) and [Oudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudh_State) (1856) consolidated British political authority across the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200668–71-75] By this time, with its economic power severely curtailed by the [British Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Parliament) and having effectively been made an arm of British administration, the East India Company began more consciously to enter non-economic arenas, including education, social reform, and culture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsherTalbot2008289-76]



A 1912 map of Northern India, showing the major centres of the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857)

The appointment in 1848 of [Lord Dalhousie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Broun-Ramsay,_1st_Marquess_of_Dalhousie) as [Governor General of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General_of_India) set the stage for changes essential to a modern state. These included the consolidation and demarcation of sovereignty, the surveillance of the population, and the education of citizens. Technological changes—among them, railways, canals, and the telegraph—were introduced not long after their introduction in [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobb2001151–152-77][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200694–99-78][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown199483-79][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeers200650-80] However, disaffection with the company also grew during this time and set off the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). Fed by diverse resentments and perceptions, including invasive British-style social reforms, harsh land taxes, and summary treatment of some rich landowners and princes, the rebellion rocked many regions of northern and central India and shook the foundations of Company rule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006100–103-81][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown199485–86-82] This widespread revolt saw intense fighting and significant uprisings in key northern cities like [Delhi](37756.md), [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur), [Lucknow](392095.md), [Jhansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhansi), and [Meerut](413695.md), which became focal points of resistance against British control.

Although the rebellion was suppressed by 1858, it led to the dissolution of the East India Company and the [direct administration of British territories in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) by the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Crown). Proclaiming a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) and a gradual but limited British-style parliamentary system, the new rulers also protected princes and landed gentry as a feudal safeguard against future unrest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998239-83][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006103–108-84] In 1861, a supreme legislature for India was established — the [Imperial Legislative Council of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Legislative_Council_of_India). Further reforms also created a unified bank — the [Imperial Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Bank_of_India), a police force — the [Indian Imperial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Imperial_Police) and a unified army — the [Imperial Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army). In 1876, the Crown-ruled India and the numerous Indian states under the Crown's suzerainty formed a loose political union called the [Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), and [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) was crowned the [Empress of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_of_India) in 1877. In the decades following, public life gradually emerged all over India, leading eventually to the founding of the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) in 1885.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobb2001183-85][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESarkar19831–4-86][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECopland2001ix–x-87][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006123-88]

The rush of technology and the commercialisation of agriculture in the second half of the 19th century was marked by economic setbacks, and many small farmers became dependent on the whims of far-away markets.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998260-89] There was an increase in the number of large-scale [famines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine_in_India),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein2010245-90] and, despite the risks of infrastructure development borne by Indian taxpayers, little industrial employment was generated for Indians.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStein1998258-91] There were also salutary effects: commercial cropping, especially in the newly canalled Punjab, led to increased food production for internal consumption.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006126-92] The railway network provided critical famine relief,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200697-93] notably reduced the cost of moving goods,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf200697-93] and helped nascent Indian-owned industry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006126-92] The imperial capital was shifted from [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta) to [Delhi](37756.md) in 1911.



The [India Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Gate) stands as a memorial to 74,187 soldiers of the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) who died between 1914 and 1921 in the [First World War](4764461.md).

After World War I, in which approximately [one million Indians served](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army_during_World_War_I) in the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006163-94] a new period began. It was marked by the enactment of the [Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montagu%E2%80%93Chelmsford_Reforms) as the Government of India Act 1919 but also [repressive legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowlatt_act), by more strident Indian calls for self-rule, and by the beginnings of a [nonviolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolent) movement of non-co-operation, of which [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) would become the leader and enduring symbol.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006167-95] During the 1930s, slow legislative reform was enacted; the Indian National Congress won victories in the resulting elections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006195–197-96] The next decade was beset with crises: [Indian participation in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_in_World_War_II), the Congress's final push for non-co-operation, and an upsurge of [Muslim nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_nationalism_in_South_Asia). All were capped by the advent of independence in 1947, but tempered by the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) into two states: India and Pakistan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2006203-97]

### Contemporary era

The partition of India caused significant upheaval, especially in Punjab and Delhi, with widespread violence and population displacement.[^cite_note-98] [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)), which acceded to India amid conflict, became a central point of territorial disputes involving India, Pakistan, and China.[^cite_note-americana-99] In the decades after independence, North India underwent major administrative changes. The princely states of the [Rajputana Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajputana_Agency) were merged to form Rajasthan in 1949.[^cite_note-GuptaBakshi2008PA143-100] In 1966, Punjab was [reorganised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Reorganisation_Act,_1966) along linguistic lines, creating Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, and the present-day Punjab.[^cite_note-paul_brass_north_india-101] Punjab also experienced a separatist [insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Punjab) in the 1980s.[^cite_note-Cynthia_Taksal77-102] The Union Territory of Delhi was renamed the National Capital Territory of Delhi in 1991, gaining a legislative assembly with limited powers.[^cite_note-NCTact-103] The [Uttarakhand movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttarakhand_movement), which gained traction in 1994, culminated with Uttarakhand being carved out of Uttar Pradesh as a separate hill state in 2000.[^cite_note-104]

In 2019, the revocation of [Article 370 of the Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_370_of_the_Constitution_of_India) changed Jammu and Kashmir's status, dividing it into two union territories [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and [Ladakh](303611.md).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

## Geography

### Topography



Map of the Himalayas (including the [Karakoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram) and [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush))

North India exhibits diverse topography consisting of [high mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya), [fertile plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangetic_plains), [deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts), and [plateau](538005.md) regions. The northern boundary is dominated by the [Himalayas](37966.md), which extend across Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand. The Himalayan system includes the [Trans-Himalayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Himalayan) ranges such as the [Karakoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram) Range, [Ladakh](303611.md) Range, and [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar) Range, characterized by rugged terrain and cold desert conditions. South of these ranges lie the Greater Himalayas or Himadri, containing permanently snow-covered peaks and glaciers, including [Nanda Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi) and the [Gangotri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangotri) Glacier. The Lesser Himalayas and the [Shiwalik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiwalik) ranges further south consist of forested hills, valleys, and sedimentary foothills prone to erosion and landslides.

The Indo-Gangetic Plain, also known as the Northern Plain or North Indian River Plain

A part of the Gangetic Plain

South of the Himalayas lies the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain), one of the world's largest alluvial plains, formed by the deposition of sediments carried by the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus) and [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) river systems and their tributaries. The plains extend across Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and parts of Delhi, and are among the most fertile and densely populated regions in the country. The [Punjab Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Plains) are traversed by tributaries of the Indus system, while the Ganga Plains form the agricultural core of northern India.

Western North India is dominated by the [Thar Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert) in Rajasthan, characterized by sandy plains, dunes, low rainfall, and sparse vegetation. The region contains seasonal rivers such as the [Luni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luni_River) River and several saline depressions. Along the eastern margin of the desert lies the [Aravalli Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravalli_Range), one of the world's oldest fold mountain systems, extending from Delhi to Gujarat. The range consists of heavily eroded hills rich in mineral resources, with Guru Shikhar as its highest peak. Parts of the Peninsular Plateau also extend into North India through the [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) and [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand) plateaus, which are characterized by rocky uplands, lava formations, and seasonal river systems.

### Climate



Onset dates and prevailing wind currents of the southwest summer monsoons in India

North India is one of the most climatically diverse regions on Earth. According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), the region lies mainly in the north [temperate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_zone) of the [Earth](9228.md) The climate varies significantly due to differences in altitude, topography, and distance from the sea. The higher Himalayan regions experience alpine and [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) climatic conditions, with heavy snowfall during winter and cool summers, while the Trans-Himalayan areas exhibit cold desert conditions with low [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation). [Dras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dras) is claimed to be the second-coldest inhabited place on the planet (after [Siberia](29639.md)), with a recorded low of −60 °C. The Indo-Gangetic Plains experience a [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) climate characterized by hot summers, cool winters, and seasonal monsoon rainfall. Summers are generally marked by high temperatures and dry winds known locally as [loo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loo_\(wind\)). During summer, temperatures often rise above 35 °C across much of the Indo-Gangetic plain, reaching as high as 50 °C in the Thar Desert, and up to 49 °C in Delhi. Winters are often accompanied by dense fog, particularly in Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh. The western desert regions of Rajasthan experience an [arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid_climate) with extreme temperatures, low humidity, and limited rainfall.



Sand dunes in the Thar Desert

The annual rainfall pattern is dominated by the [southwest monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_monsoon), which generally arrives in northern India between late June and early July and lasts until September. The lofty Himalayas play a major role in shaping the climate of the region by acting as a barrier against cold Central Asian winds and intercepting the southwest monsoon winds. As a result, the Himalayan foothills and adjoining plains receive substantial rainfall during the monsoon season. During this period, moisture-laden winds from the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) move inland and bring the majority of the region's annual precipitation.

Northern Indian tradition recognises six distinct seasons in the region: summer (*grishma* or *garmi* (jyesth- ashadh), May–June), rainy (*varsha* (shravan-bhadra), July–August), post-monsoon (*sharad* (ashivan-kartik), September–October, sometimes thought of as 'early autumn'), autumn (*hemant* (margh-paush), November–December, also called *patjhar*, lit. *leaf-fall*), winter (*shishir* or *sardi* (magh-phagun),January–February) and spring (*vasant* (chaitra-baishakh), March–April).[^cite_note-season-108] The literature, poetry and folklore of the region uses references to these six seasons quite extensively and has done so since ancient times when [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) was prevalent.[^cite_note-ref38bujab-109][^cite_note-ref72yuvur-110][^cite_note-ref08loqih-111] In the mountainous areas, sometimes the winter is further divided into "big winter" (e.g. [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_language) *chillai kalaan*) and "little winter" (*chillai khurd*).[^cite_note-ref52dapeb-112]

### Flora and fauna

Further information: [Wildlife of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_India)



[Valley of Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Flowers), [Chamoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamoli)



Male [Bharal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharal) in [Spiti Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiti_Valley)

The varied topography and climate of North India support diverse vegetation and wildlife.[^cite_note-Vegetation-113] [Alpine vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_vegetation) is found in the higher Himalayan regions, where low temperatures permit the growth of [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasses), [mosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosses), and [dwarf shrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subshrub). [Coniferous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coniferous_forests) consisting of [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), [deodar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus_deodara), [fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir), and [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce) dominate the middle Himalayan altitudes, while temperate broadleaf forests occur in lower elevations. [Montane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane) ecosystems are found in the colder regions of Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and Jammu and Kashmir. [Cold desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_desert) ecosystems occur in Ladakh and the [Spiti Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiti_Valley) of Himachal Pradesh. The Western Himalayan region abounds in [chir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chir_pine), pine, deodar (Himalayan cedar), [blue pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_pine), spruce, firs, [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch) and [junipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniper). The Himalayan region is also home to wildlife species such as the [snow leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_leopard), [Himalayan black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_black_bear), [musk deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_deer), [red fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_fox), and [Himalayan monal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_monal). The Indo-Gangetic Plains historically supported [subtropical moist deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests) forests, although much of the natural vegetation has been cleared for agriculture and urbanization. Common tree species in the plains include [sal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal_\(tree\)), [teak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teak), [neem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neem), [peepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peepal), and [banyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyan). [Subtropical dry deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_dry_broadleaf_forests) forests are found in eastern Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, and parts of Delhi along the [Aravalli Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravalli_Range). The region also contains extensive grasslands, including [dry rocky shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravalli_West_Thorn_Scrub_Forests) along the Thar Desert in western Rajasthan and areas bordering the Central Highlands. Thorn forest vegetation includes [babul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vachellia_nilotica), [dhok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminalia_pendula), [palash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butea_monosperma), [khejri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosopis_cineraria), and [khair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalia_catechu). The thorn woodland region mainly contains shrubs and trees like babul, khejri, [ronjh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vachellia_leucophloea), and khair along with dhok and palash in upland regions. Seasonal riverine grasslands occur alongside rivers in the Punjab Plain and the [Upper Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Doab). The primary fauna of this forest includes the [Indian leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_leopard), [golden jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_jackal), [jungle cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_cat), and [striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena). [Wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands) and riverine ecosystems in the plains support species such as [gharials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharials), freshwater dolphins, migratory birds, and numerous fish species. In the arid regions of the Thar Desert, thorn forests and scrub vegetation predominate, with species adapted to dry conditions, including acacia and khejri trees. Wildlife in the desert includes camels, desert foxes, chinkara, and the great Indian bustard.[^cite_note-Wildlife-114] The strikingly coloured [bir bahuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trombidiidae) is also found in this region.[^cite_note-shakespear1817-115]

Important ecological regions of North India include the [Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi_Biosphere_Reserve) in the Himalayas, [Jim Corbett National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Corbett_National_Park) is a protected forest area and a major component of the Tiger Reserve under [Project Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Tiger), [Keoladeo National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keoladeo_National_Park), a UNESCO World Heritage wetland famous for [Siberian crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_crane) migratory birds, and [Valley of Flowers National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Flowers_National_Park), known for its alpine flora and endemic species.[^cite_note-Corbett-116][^cite_note-ValleyOfFlowers-117] [Great Himalayan National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Himalayan_National_Park) is located in Himachal Pradesh and ranges in altitude from 5,000 to 17,500 feet, host the [Himalayan brown bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_brown_bear) and the [musk deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_deer). Located in Rajasthan, [Desert National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_National_Park) features extensive sand dunes and dry salt lakes.

## Politics

Politics in North India is marked by a blend of regional and national political parties, with a strong presence of the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) (INC) and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), alongside significant regional players. The region played a crucial role in the Indian freedom movement, and post-independence, political dynamics were initially dominated by the INC. The Congress established a firm grip over states like [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), [Maharashtra](20629.md) and southern states, with prominent leaders such as [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi), [Shri Krishna Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Krishna_Sinha), [K. Kamaraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Kamaraj) and [Zail Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zail_Singh) leading national politics.

The post-independence period also saw the emergence of regional political movements. In Punjab, the Shiromani Akali Dal became a major force representing Sikh political interests. However, the political landscape of North India took a significant turn in the 1990s with the rise of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP), which came to prominence following the [Ram Janmabhoomi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Janmabhoomi_movement) and the subsequent [demolition of the Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_of_the_Babri_Masjid) in 1992. The BJP's rise was further fueled by its strong stance on [Hindu nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalism) and its appeal to the urban and rural electorate in the Hindi heartland. Subsequently, in Uttar Pradesh, parties such as the Samajwadi Party and the Bahujan Samaj Party gained prominence by mobilizing support among Other Backward Classes (OBCs), Dalits, and other marginalized groups. Caste and religious identities have remained significant factors influencing electoral politics across the region.

Uttar Pradesh, the most populous state in India, has long been a battleground for the INC, BJP, SP, and BSP. The BSP, under the leadership of [Mayawati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayawati), has emerged as a dominant force in representing Dalit and backward communities, while the SP, led by the Yadav family, continues to assert its influence over the OBC vote base. The Congress, once the dominant force in the state, has lost its foothold in recent decades, but it continues to hold sway in pockets, particularly in urban centers. In [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), the political scene has been dominated by the SAD, which has a strong following among the Sikh community, and the INC, which has had a fluctuating relationship with the state due to the legacy of the Punjab insurgency in the 1980s. The state witnessed significant political change after the 2017 elections, with the emergence of the [Aam Aadmi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aam_Aadmi_Party) (AAP) as a major contender, offering an alternative to traditional parties. [Rajasthan](26291.md) and [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) have also experienced alternating political control between the INC and BJP, with regional leaders like [Ashok Gehlot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashok_Gehlot) in Rajasthan and [Shivraj Singh Chouhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivraj_Singh_Chouhan) in Madhya Pradesh asserting strong regional leadership. In Delhi, the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), under the leadership of [Arvind Kejriwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvind_Kejriwal), has rapidly risen to prominence, challenging the traditional dominance of the Congress and BJP.

The northern hill and border states and union territories—[Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md), [Ladakh](303611.md), Uttarakhand, and Himachal Pradesh have distinct political dynamics shaped by geography, security concerns, and regional aspirations. Politics in Jammu and Kashmir has historically revolved around issues of autonomy, governance, and relations with the Union government, with parties like the Jammu & Kashmir National Conference and Jammu and Kashmir Peoples Democratic Party playing key roles. The reorganization of the state following the Revocation of [Article 370](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_370) marked a major shift, bringing it and Ladakh under direct union territory administration. In Ladakh, politics is more localized, focusing on development, tribal rights, and representation, with organizations like the [Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Autonomous_Hill_Development_Council). In [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) and [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), politics is largely dominated by the Bharatiya Janata Party and the Indian National Congress, with power frequently alternating between the two. Key issues in these Himalayan states include infrastructure development, tourism, environmental conservation, and disaster management due to their fragile ecology. Meanwhile, [Haryana](14189.md) has a politically competitive landscape influenced by agrarian issues, caste dynamics, and proximity to the national capital [New Delhi](51585.md). Regional parties such as the [Indian National Lok Dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Lok_Dal) and newer formations like the [Jannayak Janta Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannayak_Janta_Party) have played significant roles alongside national parties.

North India—particularly Uttar Pradesh—has produced the highest number of Prime Ministers of India, either by birthplace or political base, including leaders like [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi), [Vishwanath Pratap Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishwanath_Pratap_Singh) and [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee). The region has produced several prominent national leaders, including [Govind Ballabh Pant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govind_Ballabh_Pant), [Jayaprakash Narayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayaprakash_Narayan), and [Kanshi Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanshi_Ram). North India has also contributed to the country's presidency, with notable figures like [Rajendra Prasad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Prasad), [Zakir Husain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakir_Husain), [Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhruddin_Ali_Ahmed), [Giani Zail Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giani_Zail_Singh) and [Ram Nath Kovind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Nath_Kovind) all hailing from this region.

### Administration

North India consists of several northern states including Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan, along with the union territories of Chandigarh, Delhi, Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh. These states and union territories have varying administrative structures: the states and Delhi have elected legislatures, while Jammu and Kashmir (with provisions for a legislature) and Ladakh are administered under the framework of the Union government.

A [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor), appointed by the Government of India through the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India), serves as the de jure head of each state, while the leader of the majority party or [coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition) in the legislative assembly is appointed as the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister), the de facto head of the government. In union territories like Delhi, a Lieutenant Governor represents the central government, alongside an elected Chief Minister, whereas Ladakh is directly governed by a [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor) without a legislature.

Administratively, the states and union territories of North India are divided into [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts), each headed by a [District Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Magistrate) or Deputy Commissioner responsible for law and order, revenue collection, and general administration. These districts are further subdivided into [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) or [taluqs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taluk), overseen by [tehsildars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsildar), and into blocks for developmental administration. At the grassroots level, [local governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_governance) is carried out through urban bodies such as [municipal corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporations), [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities), and [cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonment) boards, and rural bodies including [zila parishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_council_\(India\)), [panchayat samitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayat_samiti), and [gram panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_panchayat), forming a multi-tiered system of governance across the region.

North India elects 150 members to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md), accounting for roughly one-fourth of the total strength of 543. The region is allocated 66 seats in the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), out of the total 245 members.

The [state legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislatures) of Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and Delhi are [unicameral](204304.md), while Uttar Pradesh has a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) legislature. State legislatures elect members for terms of five years. [Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(India\)) may suspend or dissolve assemblies and can administer the state when no party is able to form a government.

### Religion

[Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) is the dominant religion in North India. Other religions practised by various ethnic communities include [Islam](6037917.md), [Sikhism](27964.md), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism), [Judaism](15624.md), [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), [Christianity](5211.md), and [Buddhism](3267529.md). Hindus constitute more than 80 per cent of the North India's population. The national capital of India, [New Delhi](51585.md), is overwhelmingly Hindu-majority with Hindus constituting nearly 90% of the capital city's population. The states of [Rajasthan](26291.md), [Haryana](14189.md), [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) are overwhelmingly Hindu-majority. [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) and [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) are also Hindu majority states, but have a large Muslim minority (14% In Uttarakhand, 19% in Uttar Pradesh). [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh) is also a Hindu-majority union territory, though also having a quarter (25%) of Sikh presence as well. The union territory of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) has a Muslim majority, while [Ladakh](303611.md) has a Muslim plurality with minority Hindus and Buddhists. The state of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) has a [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) majority of 60% and is the homeland of Sikh religion.

### Languages

Further information: [Languages of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India)



Distribution of [Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages)

Linguistically, North India is dominated by [Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages). It is in this region, or its proximity, that [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) and the various [Prakrits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakrit) are thought to have evolved. [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) is spoken in Western Uttar Pradesh and Delhi and by a large number of people in many urban centres across North India. Many other languages of the Central Indo-Aryan languages such as Awadhi, Braj, Haryanvi, Chhattisgarhi, Bundeli and Bagheli are spoken in Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh. Marwari, Harauti, Malvi, Gujarati, Khandeshi, Marathi and Konkani are spoken in Rajasthan, extreme eastern Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra and Goa. Towards the far north, languages of Dardic (such as Kashmiri) and Pahari (such as Dogri, Kumaoni and Garhwali) groups are spoken in Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal and Uttarakhand. [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) is spoken in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India). Bengali is spoken in West Bengal. Languages of Bihari group, such as Maithili, Magahi and Bhojpuri are spoken in Bihar and Jharkhand.

A number of aboriginal languages of Austroasiatic and Dravidian origin are spoken in some regions.[^cite_note-languages-129] Several [Sino-Tibetan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages) are spoken in the Himalayan region like [Kinnauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinnauri),[^cite_note-languages-129] [Ladakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakhi_language), [Balti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balti_language), and [Lahuli–Spiti languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahuli%E2%80%93Spiti_languages).

## Culture



North Indian Hindu bride in [Lehenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehenga)

The composite [culture of North India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian_culture) is known as [Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga-Jamuni_tehzeeb), a result of the amicable interaction of Hindus and Muslims there.[^cite_note-Dhulipala2000-19]

### Dance

See also: [List of Indian folk dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_folk_dances)

Dance of North India too has diverse *folk* and *classical* forms. Among the well-known [folk dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_folk_dance) are the *[bhangra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhangra_\(dance\))* of the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region), Ghoomar of Rajasthan, [Nati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nati_\(dance\)) of [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) and *rouf* and *[bhand pather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhand)* of Kashmir. Main dance forms, many with narrative forms and [mythological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_mythology) elements, have been accorded [classical dance status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_dance) by India's *[National Academy of Music, Dance, and Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangeet_Natak_Academi)* such as *[Kathak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathak)*.[^cite_note-130]

### Clothing

Each state of North India has its own regional forms of clothing:

3. [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir): [Pheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheran), [pulhoer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulhoer) and poots.

4. [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md): [Shalwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalwar_kameez), [Kurta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurta), [Churidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churidar), [Dhoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhoti), [Himachali cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himachali_cap) and angarkha.

### Nature

The Indian [Himalayas](37966.md), the [Thar desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_desert) and the [Indo-Gangetic plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_plain) dominate the natural scenery of North India. The region encompasses several of the most highly-regarded hill destinations of India such as [Srinagar](294148.md), [Shimla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimla), [Manali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manali,_Himachal_Pradesh), [Nainital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nainital), [Mussoorie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussoorie), [Kausani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kausani) and [Mount Abu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Abu). Several spots in the states of Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh provide panoramic views of the snow-clad Himalayan range. The Himalayan region also provides ample opportunity for adventure sports such as mountaineering, trekking, river rafting and skiing. Camel or jeep safaris of the Thar desert are also popular in the state of Rajasthan. North India includes several national parks and reserve area such as the [Pilibhit Tiger Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilibhit_Tiger_Reserve), [Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi_Biosphere_Reserve), [Jim Corbett National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Corbett_National_Park), [Keoladeo National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keoladeo_National_Park) [Ranthambore National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranthambore_National_Park), [Sundarbans National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_National_Park) and the [Kutch Desert Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch_Desert_Wildlife_Sanctuary).

### Pilgrimage

North India encompasses several pilgrimage centres of Hinduism, some considered the holiest in Hindiuism, including [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), [Haridwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridwar), [Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad), [Char Dham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Char_Dham), [Vaishno Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishno_Devi), [Rishikesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishikesh), [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya), [Mathura](283812.md)-[Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan), [Pushkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkar), [Prayag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad), and two of the twelve [Jyotirlinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyotirlinga) sites. It also contains what is considered the most sacred destinations of Buddhism ([Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath) and [Kushinagar](33686220.md)), the most regarded pilgrimage centres of Sikhism ([Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar) and [Hemkund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemkund)), and some highly regarded destinations in [Sufi Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) ([Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer) and [Delhi](37756.md)). The largest Hindu temple, [Akshardham Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akshardham_\(Delhi\)), the largest Buddhist temple in India, [Mahabodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabodhi_Temple), the largest mosque in India, [Jama Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jama_Masjid,_Delhi), and the largest Sikh shrine, [Golden Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmandir_Sahib), are all in this region.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

### Historical



[Amer Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amer_Fort) in [Rajasthan](26291.md)

North India includes some highly regarded historical, architectural and archaeological treasures of India. The [Taj Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_Mahal), an immense mausoleum of white marble in [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra), is one of the universally admired buildings of world heritage.[^cite_note-134] Besides Agra, [Fatehpur Sikri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri) and [Delhi](37756.md) also carry some great exhibits from the Mughal architecture. In Punjab, [Patiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patiala) is known for being the city of royalty while [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar) is a city known for its [Sikh architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_architecture) and the [Golden Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Temple). [Lucknow](392095.md) has the famous Awadhi [Nawab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab) culture while [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur) reflects Anglo-Indian architecture with monuments like [All Souls Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur_Memorial_Church), [King Edward Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur_Sangrahalaya), Police Quarters, [Cawnpore Woollen Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India_Corporation), [Cutchery Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Cemetery,_Kanpur) etc. [Khajuraho temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajuraho) constitute another famous world heritage site. The state of Rajasthan is known for exquisite [palaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_palaces_in_Rajasthan) and [forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Forts_of_Rajasthan) of the [Rajput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajput) clans. Historical sites and architecture from the ancient and medieval [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hinduism) and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Buddhism) periods of Indian history, such as [Jageshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jageshwar), [Deogarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deogarh,_Uttar_Pradesh) and [Sanchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanchi), as well as sites from the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) [Indus Valley civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation), such as [Manda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manda,_Jammu) and [Alamgirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamgirpur), can be found scattered throughout northern India. [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), on the banks of the [River Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ganga), is considered one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world and the second oldest in India after [Nalanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda). [Bhimbetka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimbetka) is an archaeological site of the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) era, exhibiting the earliest traces of human life on the Indian subcontinent.

## Economy

Further information: [Economy of India](1472206.md) and [BIMARU states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIMARU_states)

The economy of North India varies from agrarian in the northern plains to very industrialised in the [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)). Northwest Indian plains have prospered as a consequence of the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution_in_India) in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh, and have experienced both economic and social development.[^cite_note-ghonemy1986-135][^cite_note-rastyannikov1981-136][^cite_note-bhatia1985-137] The eastern areas of East Uttar Pradesh, however, have lagged[^cite_note-lucas1988-138][^cite_note-etienne1985-139] and the resulting disparity has contributed to a demand for separate statehood in West Uttar Pradesh (the [Harit Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harit_Pradesh) movement).[^cite_note-kudaisya2006-140][^cite_note-141]

The major industrial regions in North India are the Gurugram-Delhi-Meerut Belt ([NCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\))). North Indian state with highest GDP per capita in the Indian Union was [Haryana](14189.md) in 2021. Other North Indian states which follow are [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) and [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md).[^cite_note-142] Delhi has the highest per-capita State Domestic Product (SDP) of any Indian union territory.[^cite_note-mosapi-143] The National Capital Region of Delhi has emerged as an economic power house with rapid industrial growth.

According to a 2009–10 report, a large number of unskilled and skilled workers have moved to [southern India](678583.md) and other nations because of the unavailability of jobs locally.[^cite_note-144] The technology boom that occurred in the past three decades in southern India has helped many Indians from the northern region to find jobs and live prosperous lives in southern cities. An analysis by Multidimensional Poverty Index creators reveals that acute poverty prevails in eight Indian states including the northern states of Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-145]
