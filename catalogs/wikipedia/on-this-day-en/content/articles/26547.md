The **Six Nations Championship** (known as the **Six Nations**, branded as Guinness M6N)[^cite_note-1] is an annual international [rugby union](25405.md) competition by the men's teams of [England](691024.md), [France](955656.md), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_rugby_union_team), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_union_team). It is the oldest sports tournament contested by the [Home Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Nations). The championship holders are France, who won the [2026 tournament](80005683.md).

The tournament is organised by the unions of the six participating nations under the banner of Six Nations Rugby, which is responsible for the promotion and operation of the men's, [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Six_Nations_Championship) and [under-20s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Nations_Under_20s_Championship) tournaments, and the Autumn International Series, as well as the negotiation and management of their centralised commercial rights.

The Six Nations is the successor to the Home Nations Championship (1883–1909 and 1932–39), played by teams from England, Ireland, Scotland, and Wales, which was the first international rugby union tournament.[^cite_note-Godwin1-2] With the addition of France, this became the Five Nations Championship (1910–31 and 1947–99), and the Six Nations Championship with Italy joining in 2000.

England and Wales have won the championship the most times, both with 39 titles, but England have won the most outright titles with 29 (28 for Wales). Since the Six Nations era started in 2000, only Italy and Scotland have failed to win the Six Nations title.

The [women's tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Six_Nations_Championship) started as the [Women's Home Nations in the 1996 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Women's_Home_Nations_Championship). The men's [Six Nations Under 20s Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Nations_Under_20s_Championship) is the successor to the Under 21s tournament which began in 2004.

## History and expansion

The tournament was first played in 1883 as the Home Nations Championship among the then four [Home Nations of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Nations) – England, Ireland, Scotland, and Wales. However, England was excluded from the 1888 and 1889 tournaments due to their refusal to join the [International Rugby Football Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rugby_Football_Board). The tournament then became the Five Nations Championship in 1910 with the addition of France. The tournament was expanded in 2000 to become the Six Nations Championship with the addition of Italy.

Following the relative success of the Tier 2 nations in the [2015 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup), there were calls by [Octavian Morariu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian_Morariu), the president of [Rugby Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Europe), to let [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_rugby_union_team) and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_rugby_union_team) join the Six Nations due to their consistent success in the [European Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Europe_International_Championships) and ability to compete in the [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_World_Cup).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

From 2024 the series was styled as "M6N" (referring to "Men's 6 Nations"), as a [rebranding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebranding) consistent with the women's competition as "W6N", and the under-20s as "U6N".[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## Format



The locations of the Six Nations participants

The tournament begins on the first weekend in February and culminates on the second Saturday in March. Each team plays every other team once (a total of 15 matches), with home ground advantage alternating from one year to the next. Before the 2017 tournament, two points were awarded for a win, one for a draw and none for a loss. Unlike many other rugby union competitions, a [bonus point system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_bonus_points_system) had not previously been used.

A bonus point system was first used in the 2017 Championship. The system is similar to the one used in most rugby championships (0 points for a loss, 2 for a draw, 4 for a win, 1 for scoring four or more tries in a match, and 1 for losing by 7 points or fewer). The only difference is that a team that wins all their games (a Grand Slam) are automatically awarded 3 extra points - to ensure they cannot be overtaken by a defeated team on bonus points.

Before 1994, teams equal on match points shared the championship. Since then, ties have been broken by considering the points difference (total points scored minus total points conceded) of the teams. The rules of the championship further provide that if teams tie on both match points and points difference, the team that scored the most tries wins the championship. Were this decider to be a tie, the tying teams would share the championship.[^cite_note-rules-7] To date, however, match points and points difference have been sufficient to decide the championship.

The [Wooden Spoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_Spoon_\(rugby_union\)) is a metaphorical award given to the team that finishes in last place; a team which loses all their matches is said to have been "whitewashed".[^cite_note-8] Since the inaugural Six Nations tournament in 2000, only England and Ireland have avoided finishing last. Italy have finished last 18 times in the Six Nations era, and have lost all their matches in 12 tournaments.

### Championship Trophy

The winners of the Six Nations are presented with the Championship Trophy.[^cite_note-trophy-9] This was originally conceived by the [Earl of Westmorland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Anthony_Thomas_Fane,_15th_Earl_of_Westmorland), and was first presented to the winners of the 1993 Five Nations Championship; France. It was a [sterling silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_silver) trophy, designed by James Brent-Ward and made by a team of eight silversmiths from the London firm William Comyns. The current championship trophy for the Six Nations Championship was designed and made in 2015 by [Thomas Lyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Lyte), London silversmiths.[^cite_note-10]

The old trophy had 15 side panels representing the 15 members of the team and with three handles to represent the three officials (referee and two touch judges). The cup has a capacity of 3.75 litres (0.8 imp gal; 1.0 US gal) – sufficient for five bottles of champagne. Within the mahogany base is a concealed drawer which contains six alternative [finials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finial), each a silver replica of one of the team emblems, which can be screwed on the detachable lid.

A new trophy was introduced for the [2015 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Six_Nations_Championship).[^cite_note-11] The trophy was sterling silver, containing over fifty individual pieces, standing at a height of 75 cm (30 in) and had a distinctive six-sided design representing the six nations that compete.[^cite_note-12] In 2026, after the third round of the championship, the trophy was damaged by fire and retired from ceremonial use. A replica was presented to the 2026 Six Nations winners, and a new trophy will be commissioned for the 2027 Championship.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### Grand Slam and Triple Crown

A team that wins all its games wins the '[Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_\(rugby_union\))'.

The [Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_\(rugby_union\)) may only be won by one of the [Home Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Nations) of England, Ireland, Scotland or Wales, when one nation wins all three of their matches against the others. The Triple Crown dates back to the original Home Nations Championship, but the physical Triple Crown Trophy has been awarded only since 2006, when the Royal Bank of Scotland (the primary sponsor of the competition) commissioned [Hamilton & Inches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_&_Inches) to design and create a dedicated Triple Crown Trophy. As of 2026, the trophy has been won six times by Ireland, four times by Wales, three times by England, and is yet to be won by Scotland.

### Rivalry trophies

Several individual competitions take place under the umbrella of the tournament. Some of these trophies are also awarded for other matches between the two teams outside the Six Nations. Only Scotland play for a 'rivalry' or challenge trophy in every Six Nations match, as well as for the oldest such trophy, the [Calcutta Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Cup). Wales became the last nation to contest such a trophy, the [Doddie Weir Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doddie_Weir_Cup) in 2018, while the newest such trophy is the [Solidarity Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_Trophy) introduced between Ireland and France in 2026.

Games which form part of the Triple Crown are marked <sup>TC</sup>.

The opening of Aviva Stadium in May 2010 ended the arrangement with the [Gaelic Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Athletic_Association) (GAA) that allowed the all-Ireland governing body for rugby union, the [Irish Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rugby_Football_Union), to use the GAA's flagship stadium, [Croke Park](322713.md), for its international matches. This arrangement was made necessary by the 2007 closure and subsequent demolition of Ireland's traditional home at [Lansdowne Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansdowne_Road); Aviva Stadium was built on the former Lansdowne Road site. During this construction, Croke Park was the largest of the Six Nations grounds, with a capacity of 82,300.

In 2012, Italy moved their home games from the 32,000 seat [Stadio Flaminio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Flaminio), to Stadio Olimpico, also in Rome, with a capacity of 72,000.

The [French Rugby Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Rugby_Federation) (FFR) had planned to build a new stadium of its own, seating 82,000 in the southern suburbs of Paris,[^cite_note-23] because of frustrations with their tenancy of Stade de France.[^cite_note-BBC_FFR_stadium-24] However the project was cancelled in December 2016.[^cite_note-25] France played their [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Six_Nations_Championship#Round_3) and [2026](80005683.md#Round_3) matches against Italy at [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome) in [Marseille](40888948.md) and [Stade Pierre-Mauroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Pierre-Mauroy) in [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille) respectively.[^cite_note-26]

In 2020, Wales played their [final game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Six_Nations_Championship#Round_5) at [Parc y Scarlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_y_Scarlets) in [Llanelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanelli) due to the Principality Stadium being used as [Dragon's Heart Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon's_Heart_Hospital) in response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-27]

In 2024, France was unable to use the Stade de France for their Six Nations home games due to ongoing preparations for its use in the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-28] Instead they played their three home matches at the Stade Vélodrome, Stade Pierre-Mauroy, and [Parc Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_Olympique_Lyonnais) in [Lyon](8638634.md).

## Records

Main article: [List of Six Nations Championship records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Six_Nations_Championship_records)

Ireland's [Johnny Sexton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Sexton) holds the record for most points in the competition, with 566. England's [Jonny Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Wilkinson) holds the records for individual points in one match (35 points against Italy in 2001) and one season with 89 (scored in 2001). Wales’s Dan Biggar holds the record for highest successful kick percentage at 85% (minimum of 50 kicks attempted).

The record for tries in a match is held by [George Campbell Lindsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Campbell_Lindsay), who scored five tries for Scotland against Wales in 1887.[^cite_note-SNrecords-39]France's [Louis Bielle-Biarrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bielle-Biarrey) holds the record for tries in one tournament with 9 in 2026.[^cite_note-BBC25-40] Ireland's [Brian O'Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_O'Driscoll) has the Championship record for tries with 26.

The record for appearances is held by [Sergio Parisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Parisse) of Italy, with 69 appearances,[^cite_note-SNRrecords-41] between 2004 and 2019.

The most points scored by a team in one match was 80 points, scored by England against Italy in 2001. England also scored the most points in a season in 2001 with 229.[^cite_note-SNrecords-39] France scored the most tries in a season, with 30 both in 2025 and 2026.[^cite_note-BBC25-40] Wales hold the record for fewest tries conceded during a season in the Six Nations era, conceding only 2 in 5 games in 2008, but the 1977 Grand Slam-winning France team did not concede a try in their four matches. Wales hold the record for the longest time without conceding a try, at 358 minutes in the 2013 tournament.

## Administration

The Championship is run from headquarters in [Dublin](8504.md), [Ireland](14560.md) by Six Nations Rugby Ltd.[^cite_note-42]

Former [England and Wales Cricket Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_and_Wales_Cricket_Board) (ECB) CEO, [Tom Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Harrison_\(cricketer\)),[^cite_note-\:0-43] was appointed the CEO of Six Nations Rugby in January 2023 following the resignation of Benjamin Morel in November 2022.[^cite_note-44] Morel had held the position of CEO since November 2018,[^cite_note-45] replacing John Feehan, who stepped down after sixteen years as CEO in April 2018.[^cite_note-46] Harrison's tenure as CEO commenced from April 2023.[^cite_note-\:0-43][^cite_note-\:1-47]

[Ronan Dunne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_Dunne) was appointed as the Chairman for Six Nations Rugby in November 2021 with his tenure commencing from January 2022. Dunne has responsibility for the commercial and marketing operations for both the men's and [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Six_Nations_Championship) Six Nations tournaments.[^cite_note-48]

## Marketing

### Broadcasting rights

One of the most important rugby union tournaments in the world, the Six Nations Championship is broadcast in various countries in addition to the six participating nations.[^cite_note-49]

In the United Kingdom, the [BBC](19344654.md) has long covered the tournament, broadcasting all matches (apart from England home matches between 1997 and 2002, which were shown live by [Sky Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sports) with highlights on the BBC) until 2015. In addition, [Welsh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) coverage of broadcasts matches featuring the Welsh team shown by the BBC are shown on [S4C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S4C) in [Wales](69894.md) in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-50] Between 2003 and 2015, the BBC covered every match live on BBC Sport either on BBC One or BBC Two with highlights also on the BBC Sport website and either on the BBC Red Button or late at night on BBC Two.

On 9 July 2015, in reaction to bids by Sky for the rights beginning in 2018, the BBC ended its contract two seasons early, and renegotiated a joint contract with [ITV Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Sport) for rights to the Six Nations from 2016 to 2021. [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_\(TV_network\)) acquired rights to England, Ireland and Italy home matches, while the BBC retained rights to France, Scotland and Wales home matches. By ending its contract early, the BBC saved around £30 million, while the new contract generated £20 million in additional revenue for the Six Nations.[^cite_note-51]

With the end of the contract nearing, speculation once again emerged in 2020 that Sky was pursuing rights to the Six Nations from 2022 onward; under the Ofcom ["listed events"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofcom_Code_on_Sports_and_Other_Listed_and_Designated_Events) rules, rights to the tournament can be held by a pay television channel if delayed broadcasts or highlights are made available on free-to-air television.[^cite_note-52] It was reported that the bid for [CVC Equity Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CVC_Equity_Partners) to purchase a stake in the Six Nations was being hindered by a desire for a more lucrative broadcast contract; a call for the Six Nations to be moved to Category A (which requires live coverage to air free-to-air) was rejected.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] In May 2021, the BBC and ITV renewed their contracts through 2025. The BBC continued to broadcast home matches from Scotland and Wales and all women's and under-20s matches, with ITV airing England, France, Ireland and Italy home matches.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] This new deal would see the BBC retain 5 live matches with ITV showing the other 10. In 2025, the deal was further extended until the 2029 tournament in a deal which would see BBC retain 5 matches (all featuring Scotland or Wales) and ITV retain 10 matches whilst also gaining exclusivity of all matches featuring England.[^cite_note-57]

France, Ireland, and Italy listed the Six Nations as a major event with cultural significance and enacted national and [EU](9317.md) laws to ensure coverage would be available on free-to-air channels.[^cite_note-58]

In Ireland, each of Ireland's games in the Six Nations may be held by a pay television channel, if the match is delayed broadcast and in full on free-to-air television.[^cite_note-59] [RTÉ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89) have broadcast the championship since RTÉ's inception and continued to do so until 2017, while [TG4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TG4) televised highlights. However, in late 2015 RTÉ's free-to-air rival TV3 was awarded the rights for every game from the Six Nations on Irish television from 2018 to 2021.[^cite_note-60] In 2022, it was announced that RTÉ and [Virgin Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Television_\(Ireland\)) would share broadcasting rights.[^cite_note-61]

In France, the entire Six Nations rugby tournament must appear on free-to-air television.[^cite_note-62] [France Télévisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9visions) has covered the competition in France.

In Italy, Six Nations rugby matches involving the Italian national team must be broadcast on free-to-air television.[^cite_note-63] [Sky Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Italia) broadcasts all matches while free-to-air [TV8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV8_\(Italian_TV_channel\)) only covers [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team) fixtures.

In the United States, [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports) broadcasts matches in English.[^cite_note-64] The tournament is also broadcast on [DAZN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAZN) in Canada, [Premier Sports Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Sports_Asia) in [East](19605700.md) and [Southeast Asia](28741.md), [Sky Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sport_\(New_Zealand\)) in New Zealand, [Stan Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_\(service\)) in Australia and [SuperSport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperSport_\(South_African_broadcaster\)) in South Africa.[^cite_note-65]

In 2024, the Six Nations teams featured in a Netflix documentary Six Nations: Full Contact.[^cite_note-66] In February 2024, the show was green lit for a second season.[^cite_note-67] In January 2025, the tournament organisers confirmed that Netflix would not be taking up the option to produce a third series.[^cite_note-68]

### Sponsorship

Until 1998, the competition had no [title sponsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_sponsor). Sponsorship rights were sold to [Lloyds TSB Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyds_Banking_Group) for the 1999 tournament and the competition was titled the **Lloyds TSB 5 Nations** and **Lloyds TSB 6 Nations** until 2002.[^cite_note-69]

The [Royal Bank of Scotland Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NatWest_Group) took over sponsorship from 2003 until 2017, with the competition being branded the **RBS 6 Nations**. A new title sponsor was sought for the 2018 tournament and beyond.[^cite_note-70] However, after struggling to find a new sponsor, organisers agreed a one-year extension at a reduced rate. As the RBS brand was being phased out, the tournament was named after the [NatWest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NatWest) banking subsidiary, becoming the **NatWest 6 Nations**.[^cite_note-71]

On 7 December 2018, [Guinness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness) was announced as the Championship's new title sponsor, with the competition to be named the **Guinness Six Nations** from 2019 to 2024.[^cite_note-72] Due to the *[Loi Évin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loi_%C3%89vin)* laws which prohibit [alcohol sponsorship in sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_advertising#Sponsorship_in_sport), "Guinness" cannot be used as part of the branding of the tournament in France. The French-language logo for the tournament replaces the Guinness logo with the word "Greatness" in the same colour and typeface as the Guinness wordmark.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] [^cite_note-75]
