# Vaccination in Bangladesh

&#x20;**Vaccination in Bangladesh** includes all aspects of [vaccination](32473.md) in [Bangladesh](3454.md).

A 2020 study reported that the cost of a malaria vaccination program in Bangladesh would be cost effective in terms of increasing the people's [disability-adjusted life years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability-adjusted_life_years).[^cite_note-1]

Bangladesh had its first outbreak of [avian influenza](442916.md) in 2007 and the disease continues to be a national problem.[^cite_note-2] Part of the response that scientists recommend is the development of vaccination programs, but this has been difficult.[^cite_note-3]

A 2016 program to provide [HPV vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPV_vaccines) to girls created a range of ethical issues for communities.[^cite_note-Salwa_2018-4] The source of all the problems was that the design of the vaccination program came from foreign people outside the country who had no understanding of local cultural norms.[^cite_note-Salwa_2018-4] There was an attempt at local consultation, but unexpected problems happened anyway.[^cite_note-Salwa_2018-4] Problems included lack of public health education for the communities receiving the vaccine, forcefulness and lack of consent in arranging for girls to take the vaccine, a lack of planning to treat adverse side effects of vaccination, and a lack of female leadership and empowerment in running a health program for females.[^cite_note-Salwa_2018-4]

There are multiple [cholera vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholera_vaccines) available in Bangladesh as well as multiple strategies for making them available to people who need them.[^cite_note-Islam_2020-5] While there is major government support for vaccination, there is debate and research about how to manage the vaccination program to make it more efficient.[^cite_note-Islam_2020-5]

Bangladesh has experienced outbreaks of the [Nipah virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipah_virus) and although a vaccine exists, the vaccine option is not well developed and preventing outbreaks without vaccines is a better option in this case.[^cite_note-6]

Bangladesh began a vaccination program for [congenital rubella syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congenital_rubella_syndrome) in 2012 and since then, cases have gone down greatly.[^cite_note-7]

## [EPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_Program_on_Immunization) (Expanded program on immunization) in Bangladesh

On 7th April 1979, about 5 years after EPI was launched globally by WHO, EPI was formally launched in Bangladesh as a pilot project in eight thanas.[^cite_note-10] In 1985, the People's Republic of Bangladesh committed to the Global Universal Child Immunization Initiative (UCI), and began a phase-wise process of EPI intensification from 1985 to 1990.[^cite_note-11]

Bangladesh has made significant progress in the elimination and control of [Vaccine-preventable disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine-preventable_disease) (VPDs). The last case of wild [Poliovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliovirus) was detected in 2006 and maintains [Polio](25107.md) free since then. Maternal and neonatal [tetanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetanus) was eliminated in 2008. Bangladesh also achieved [rubella](172323.md) control goal in 2018. Surveillance for [AFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_flaccid_paralysis) and [measles](58911.md) is maintained at standard level. Bangladesh has also introduced several new vaccines since last decades. [HepB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B) vaccine was introduced in 2003, [Hib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemophilus_influenzae) in 2009, [rubella](172323.md) in 2012, [PCV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumococcal_conjugate_vaccine) and IPV in 2015, [MR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MR_vaccine) second dose in 2015 and fIPV in 2017.[^cite_note-12]
