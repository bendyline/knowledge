# Air traffic control

"Air traffic" redirects here. For the Canadian band, see [Air Traffic Control (band)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Traffic_Control_\(band\)). For the Owl City song, see [Maybe I'm Dreaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maybe_I'm_Dreaming).

This article is about civilian direction of aircraft. For other types of aircraft control, see [Air control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_control_\(disambiguation\)).



The air traffic control tower of [Mumbai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_International_Airport) in India

**Air traffic control** (**ATC**) is a service provided by ground-based [air traffic controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_traffic_controller) who direct aircraft on the ground and through controlled [airspace](353557.md). The primary purpose of ATC is to prevent collisions, organise and expedite the flow of air traffic, and provide information and other support for pilots.[^cite_note-1] In some countries, ATC can also provide advisory services to aircraft in non-controlled airspace. Controllers monitor the location of aircraft in their assigned airspace using [radar](25676.md) and communicate with [pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot) by [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio).[^cite_note-2] To prevent collisions, ATC enforces [traffic separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_\(air_traffic_control\)) rules, which ensure each aircraft maintains a minimum amount of empty space around it. ATC services are provided to all types of aircraft, including [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation), [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aviation), and commercial flights.[^cite_note-3] Depending on the type of flight and the class of airspace, ATC may issue mandatory *instructions* or non-binding *advisories* (known as *flight information* in some countries). While pilots are required to obey all ATC instructions, the [pilot in command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_in_command) of an aircraft always retains final authority for its safe operation. In an emergency, the pilot may deviate from ATC instructions to the extent required to maintain the safety of the aircraft.[^cite_note-4] Weather conditions, such as thunderstorms, strong winds, and low visibility, can significantly affect air traffic control operations, leading to delays, diversions, and the need for alternate routing.[^cite_note-5]

## Language

According to requirements of the [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md) (ICAO), ATC operations are conducted either in English, or the local language used by the station on the ground.[^cite_note-IDAO-FAQ-6] In practice, the native language for a region is used; however, English must be used upon request.[^cite_note-IDAO-FAQ-6]

See also: [Aeronautical phraseology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_phraseology) and [Aviation English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_English)

## History



ATC staff at [Washington National Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_National_Airport)'s tower, 1958

In 1920, [Croydon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon_Airport) near London, England, was the first [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) in the world to introduce air traffic control.[^cite_note-7] The 'aerodrome control tower' was a wooden hut 15 feet (5 metres) high with windows on all four sides. It was commissioned on 25 February 1920, and provided basic traffic, weather, and location information to pilots.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In the United States, air traffic control developed three divisions. The first of several air mail radio stations (AMRS) was created in 1922, after World War I, when the U.S. Post Office began using techniques developed by the [U.S. Army](32087.md) to direct and track the movements of [reconnaissance aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft). Over time, the AMRS morphed into [flight service stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_service_station). Today's flight service stations do not issue control instructions, but provide pilots with many other flight related informational services. They do relay control instructions from ATC in areas where flight service is the only facility with radio or phone coverage. The first airport traffic control tower, regulating arrivals, departures, and surface movement of aircraft in the US at a specific airport, opened in Cleveland in 1930. Approach- and departure-control facilities were created after adoption of radar in the 1950s to monitor and control the busy airspace around larger airports. The first air route traffic control center (ARTCC), which directs the movement of aircraft between departure and destination, was opened in Newark in 1935, followed in 1936 by Chicago and Cleveland.[^cite_note-10]

After the [1956 Grand Canyon mid-air collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Grand_Canyon_mid-air_collision), killing all 128 on board, the FAA was given the air-traffic responsibility in the United States in 1958, and this was followed by other countries. In 1960, Britain, France, Germany, and the [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux) countries set up [Eurocontrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocontrol), intending to merge their airspaces. The first and only attempt to pool controllers between countries is the [Maastricht Upper Area Control Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Upper_Area_Control_Centre) (MUAC), founded in 1972 by Eurocontrol, and covering Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and north-western Germany. In 2001, the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) aimed to create a '[Single European Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_European_Sky)', hoping to boost efficiency and gain economies of scale.[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11]

In the USSR, the first air traffic control service was organized in 1929 on the [Moscow](19004.md) - [Irkutsk](56872.md) air route; in 1930, control areas were defined along all existing air routes.[^cite_note-12]

## Airport traffic control tower



Control tower at [São Paulo–Guarulhos International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo%E2%80%93Guarulhos_International_Airport)



Control tower at [Birmingham Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Airport), England



Small control tower at [Räyskälä Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A4ysk%C3%A4l%C3%A4_Airfield) in [Loppi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loppi), Finland

The primary method of controlling the immediate airport environment is visual observation from the airport control tower. The tower is typically a tall, windowed structure, located within the airport grounds. The [air traffic controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_traffic_controller), usually abbreviated 'controller', are responsible for separation and efficient movement of aircraft and vehicles operating on the taxiways and runways of the airport itself, and aircraft in the air near the airport, generally 5 to 10 [nautical miles](21289.md) (9 to 19 [kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre); 6 to 12 [miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile)), depending on the airport procedures. A controller must carry out the job using the precise and effective application of rules and procedures; however, they need flexible adjustments according to differing circumstances, often under time pressure.[^cite_note-13] In a study which compared stress in the general population and among staff working in this kind of system, there was markedly showed more stress level for controllers. This variation can be explained, at least in part, by the characteristics of the job.[^cite_note-14]

[Remote and virtual tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_and_virtual_tower) (RVT) is a system based on air traffic controllers being located somewhere other than at the local airport tower, and still able to provide air traffic control services.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Ground control



Inside [Pope Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Field) air traffic control tower

Ground control (sometimes known as *ground movement control*, GMC) is responsible for the airport *movement* areas.[^cite_note-Smith_2021-18]

Some busier airports have surface movement radar (SMR).[^cite_note-Smith_2021-18]

### Air control or local control

Air control (known to pilots as *tower* or *tower control*) is responsible for the active runway surfaces.[^cite_note-Smith_2021-18]

### Flight data and clearance delivery

Clearance delivery is the position that issues route clearances to aircraft, typically before they commence taxiing. These clearances contain details of the route that the aircraft is expected to fly after departure.[^cite_note-Smith_2021-18]

### Analogous use

In the field of [supply chain management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain_management), the concept of a "supply chain control tower" reflects the "end-to-end visibility" provided by an air traffic control tower.[^cite_note-19] Ashutoch Gupta, writing for [Gartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gartner), observes that a supply chain control tower can be established "by combining people, process, data, organization and technology to improve visibility, control and decision making".[^cite_note-20]

## Airports without a control tower (mandatory frequency airport)

An airport with a mandatory frequency (MF)[^cite_note-21], mandatory traffic advisory frequency (MTAF), mandatory broadcast zone (MBZ)[^cite_note-\:0-22] or air ground communications service (AGCS)[^cite_note-\:1-23] is an airport which [does not have a control tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-towered_airport) but still requires arriving and departing aircraft to communicate with other aircraft or a radio operator on a published frequency.

Mandatory frequency airports are rare in the [United States](3434750.md), one example being [Ketchikan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchikan_International_Airport) ([14 CFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_Code_of_Federal_Regulations) [93.151](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-93.151)), but they are common in other countries such as [Canada](5042916.md), [Australia](4689264.md),[^cite_note-\:0-22] the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Norway](21241.md); often, an MF or MTAF airport is one with [scheduled passenger service](1942.md) but insufficient traffic to support a [control tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_tower).&#x20;

If there is a flight service specialist monitoring the frequency, the specialist will give pilots advisories about traffic, weather, and surface conditions, and may relay [IFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules) clearances from en route [controllers](48563.md), but do not give clearances.[^cite_note-24] In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), weather and traffic information can be provided by radio stations named Air Ground Communications Services (AGCS). These similarly inform pilots who are then responsible for decisions made.[^cite_note-\:1-23]

Some examples of MF airports in Canada include [Kingston/Norman Rogers Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston/Norman_Rogers_Airport) and [Kuujjuaq Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuujjuaq_Airport).

## Area control centre/en-route centre

Main article: [Area control center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_control_center)



The training department at the Washington Air Route Traffic Control Center, [Leesburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leesburg,_Virginia), United States

An [area control center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_control_center), as opposed to a terminal control center, is a specific type of air traffic control center that primarily controls [IFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules) traffic, or aircraft at high altitude flying by instruments, instead of aircraft that are landing or taking off.[^cite_note-FAAglossary-26] Each area control center is assigned a [flight information region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_region), and certain area control centers near the coast are also tasked with monitoring aircraft flying over the ocean.[^cite_note-FAAglossary-26] Area control centers perform similar duties to other types of air traffic control, such as rerouting aircraft to deal with adverse conditions and directing the course of aircraft to ensure separation.

## Radar coverage



Unmanned en-route radar on a remote mountain

The position of aircraft is mainly determined by the following types of radars:

- *En-route radar* with a coverage up to 240 NM (460 km)

- [Airport surveillance radar](4456695.md), with a coverage up to of 52 NM (96 km) of the airport below an elevation of 25,000 feet

- [Surface movement radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_movement_radar) with a coverage of the airport surface, within 2 NM (3.7 km)

- [Secondary surveillance radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_surveillance_radar) with a coverage within line-of-sight range



En-route radar with primary and secondary radar antennas



Airport surveillance radar with primary and secondary radar antennas



Stand-alone secondary surveillance radar

In order to extend their surveillance capabilities, some air navigation service providers (e.g., Airservices Australia, the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration, [Nav Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nav_Canada), etc.) have implemented [automatic dependent surveillance – broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_dependent_surveillance_%E2%80%93_broadcast) (ADS-B). This newer technology reverses the radar concept. Instead of radar 'finding' a target by interrogating the transponder, the ADS-B equipped aircraft 'broadcasts' a position report as determined by the [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation) equipment on board the aircraft. ADS-C is another mode of automatic dependent surveillance, however ADS-C operates in the 'contract' mode, where the aircraft reports a position, automatically or initiated by the pilot, based on a predetermined time interval. It is also possible for controllers to request more frequent reports to more quickly establish aircraft position for specific reasons.[^cite_note-27] However, since the cost for each report is charged by the ADS service providers to the company operating the aircraft, more frequent reports are not commonly requested, except in emergency situations. ADS-C is significant, because it can be used where it is not possible to locate the infrastructure for a radar system (e.g., over water). [Operational display systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_display_system) are now deployed and accept ADS-C inputs as part of their display.[^cite_note-28]

A radar archive system (RAS) keeps an electronic record of all radar information, preserving it for a few weeks. This information can be useful for [search and rescue](172599.md). When an aircraft has 'disappeared' from radar screens, a controller can review the last radar returns from the aircraft to determine its likely position. For an example, see the crash report in the following citation.[^cite_note-29]

## Problems

### Traffic

Further information: [Air traffic flow management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_traffic_flow_management)



Intersecting [contrails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrail) of aircraft over London, an area of high air traffic

Air traffic control errors occur when the separation (either vertical or horizontal) between airborne aircraft falls below the minimum prescribed separation set (for the domestic United States) by the US Federal Aviation Administration. Separation minimums for terminal control areas (TCAs) around airports are lower than en-route standards. Errors generally occur during periods following times of intense activity, when controllers tend to relax and overlook the presence of traffic and conditions that lead to loss of minimum separation.[^cite_note-30]

### Weather

### Congestion

Constrained control capacity and growing traffic lead to [flight cancellation and delays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_cancellation_and_delay).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In America, delays caused by ATC grew by 69% between 2012 and 2017.[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11] ATC staffing issues were a major factor in congestion.[^cite_note-33]Estimates suggest that more efficient ATC could save 5-10% of aviation fuel by avoiding [holding patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_pattern) and indirect [airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_\(aviation\)).[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11]

Accordingly, the explanation of delays as resulting from ATC restrictions has become a common justification used by airlines to deny compensation claims.[^cite_note-34] However, several European courts have determined that this rationale is sometimes applied incorrectly, with airlines attributing delays to ATC although all measures haven't been taken by the air carrier concerned to avoid the delays or cancellations.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Call signs

A prerequisite to safe air traffic separation is the assignment and use of distinctive [call signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_sign). These are permanently allocated by [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO) on request, usually to [scheduled flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_air_transport), and some air forces and other military services for [military flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aviation). There are written call signs with a two or three letter combination followed by the flight number such as AAL872 or VLG1011. As such, they appear on flight plans and ATC radar labels. There are also the *audio* or *radio-telephony* call signs used on the radio contact between pilots and air traffic control. These are not always identical to their written counterparts. An example of an audio call sign would be 'Speedbird 832', instead of the written 'BAW832'. This is used to reduce the chance of confusion between ATC and the aircraft. By default, the call sign for any other flight is the [registration number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) (or tail number in US parlance) of the aircraft, such as 'N12345', 'C-GABC', or 'EC-IZD'. The short *radio-telephony* call signs for these tail numbers is the last three letters using the [NATO phonetic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_phonetic_alphabet) (e.g. ABC, spoken *alpha-bravo-charlie* for C-GABC), or the last three numbers (e.g. *three-four-five* for N12345). In the United States, the prefix may be an aircraft type, model, or manufacturer in place of the first registration character, for example, 'N11842' could become 'Cessna 842'.[^cite_note-37]

## Technology

The [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) has spent over US \$3 billion on software, but a fully automated system is still yet to be achieved. In 2002, the United Kingdom commissioned a new [area control center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_control_center) into service at the [London Area Control Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Area_Control_Centre) (LACC) at [Swanwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanwick,_Hampshire) in Hampshire, relieving a busy suburban centre at [West Drayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Drayton) in Middlesex, north of [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport). Software from [Lockheed-Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed-Martin) predominates at the London Area Control Centre. However, the centre was initially troubled by software and communications problems causing delays and occasional shutdowns.[^cite_note-38]

- Flight data processing systems: this is the system (usually one per centre) that processes all the information related to the flight (the flight plan), typically in the time horizon from gate to gate (airport departure / arrival gates). It uses such processed information to invoke other flight plan related tools (such as e.g. [Medium Term Conflict Detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_Term_Conflict_Detection?action=edit\&redlink=1) (MTCD)).

## Proposed changes

In the United States, [free flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_flight_\(air_traffic_control\)) is a developing air traffic control method that uses no centralised control (e.g. air traffic controllers). Instead, parts of airspace are reserved dynamically and automatically in a distributed way using computer communication to ensure the required separation between aircraft.[^cite_note-46]

In Europe, the [Single European Sky ATM Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_European_Sky_ATM_Research) (SESAR)[^cite_note-SESARref-42] programme plans to develop new methods, technologies, procedures, and systems to accommodate future (2020 and beyond) air traffic needs. In October 2018, European controller unions dismissed setting targets to improve ATC as "a waste of time and effort", as new technology could cut costs for users but threaten their jobs. In April 2019, the EU called for a 'Digital European Sky', focusing on cutting costs by including a common digitisation standard, and allowing controllers to move to where they are needed instead of merging national ATCs, as it would not solve all problems. Single air-traffic control services in continent-sized America and China does not alleviate congestion. Eurocontrol tries to reduce delays by diverting flights to less busy routes: flight paths across Europe were redesigned to accommodate the new airport in Istanbul, which opened in April, but the extra capacity will be absorbed by rising demand for air travel.[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11]

Well-paid jobs in western Europe could move east with cheaper labour. The average Spanish controller earn over €200,000 a year, over seven times the country average salary, more than pilots, and at least ten controllers were paid over €810,000 (\$1.1m) a year in 2010. French controllers spent a cumulative nine months on strike between 2004 and 2016.[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11]

### Privatisation

Many countries have also privatised or corporatised their air navigation service providers.[^cite_note-47] There are several models that can be used for ATC service providers. The first is to have the ATC services be part of a government agency as is currently the case in the United States. The problem with this model is that funding can be inconsistent, and can disrupt the development and operation of services. Sometimes funding can disappear when lawmakers cannot approve budgets in time. Both proponents and opponents of privatisation recognise that stable funding is one of the major factors for successful upgrades of ATC infrastructure. Some of the funding issues include sequestration and politicisation of projects.[^cite_note-48] Proponents argue that moving ATC services to a private corporation could stabilise funding over the long term which will result in more predictable planning and rollout of new technology as well as training of personnel. As of November 2024, The United States had 265 contractor towers that are staffed by private companies but administered by FAA through its FAA Contract Tower Program, which was established in 1982. These contract control towers cover 51% of all the Federal air traffic control towers in the U.S.[^cite_note-49]

Another model is to have ATC services provided by a government corporation. This model is used in Germany, where funding is obtained through user fees. Yet another model is to have a for-profit corporation operate ATC services. This is the model used in the United Kingdom, but there have been several issues with the system there, including a large-scale failure in December 2014 which caused delays and cancellations and has been attributed to cost-cutting measures put in place by this corporation. In fact, earlier that year, the corporation owned by the German government won the bid to provide ATC services for Gatwick Airport in the United Kingdom. The last model, which is often the suggested model for the United States to transition to is to have a non-profit organisation that would handle ATC services as is used in Canada.[^cite_note-50]

The Canadian system is the one most often used as a model by proponents of privatisation. Air traffic control privatisation has been successful in Canada with the creation of Nav Canada, a private non-profit organisation which has reduced costs, and has allowed new technologies to be deployed faster due to the elimination of much of the bureaucratic [red tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_tape). This has resulted in shorter flights and less fuel usage. It has also resulted in flights being safer due to new technology. Nav Canada is funded from fees that are collected from the airlines based on the weight of the aircraft and the distance flown.[^cite_note-51]

Air traffic control is operated by national governments with few exceptions: in the [European Union](9317.md), only Italy has private shareholders.[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11] Privatisation does not guarantee lower prices: the profit margin of MUAC was 70% in 2017, as there is no competition, but governments could offer fixed terms [concessions](3261664.md).[^cite_note-Economist15jun2019-11]

## ATC regulations in the United States

The United States airspace is divided into 21 zones (centres), and each zone is divided into sectors. Also within each zone are portions of airspace, about 50 miles (80 kilometres) in diameter, called TRACON (Terminal Radar Approach Control) airspaces. Within each TRACON airspace are a number of airports, each of which has its own airspace with a 5 miles (8.0 kilometres) radius. [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA) [control tower operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_tower_operator?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CTO) / [air traffic controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_traffic_controller) use [FAA Order 7110.65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA_Order_7110.65) as the authority for all procedures regarding air traffic.[^cite_note-52]
