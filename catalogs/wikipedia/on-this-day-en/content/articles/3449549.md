A U.S. [color guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_guard) bears the American and Israeli flags during a visit by [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz) to [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), 2015

Since the 1960s, the **relationship between Israel and the United States** has grown into a close alliance in economic, strategic and military aspects. The U.S. has provided strong support for Israel; it has played a key role in the promotion of good relations between Israel and its neighbouring [Arab states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world). In turn, Israel provides a strategic American foothold in the region as well as intelligence and advanced technological partnerships. Relations with Israel are an important factor in the [U.S. foreign policy in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy_in_the_Middle_East).

Israel is the largest cumulative recipient of [U.S. foreign aid](22557286.md): up to February 2022, the U.S. had provided Israel US\$150 billion (non-inflation-adjusted) in assistance.[^cite_note-SharpAid2Isr-1] The United States' first [free trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreements_of_the_United_States) was with Israel, in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_Free_Trade_Agreement). In 1999, the U.S. government signed a commitment to provide Israel with at least US\$2.7 billion in [military aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_aid) annually for ten years; in 2009 it was raised to \$3 billion; and in 2019 raised to a minimum of US\$3.8 billion.[^cite_note-SharpAid2Isr-1] The bulk of the U.S. money that Israel receives is used to buy military equipment from American companies.[^cite_note-2] Since 1972, the U.S. has also extended [loan guarantees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_guarantee) to Israel to assist with housing shortages, absorption of new Jewish immigrants and economic recovery.[^cite_note-SharpAid2Isr-1]

In addition to financial and military aid, the U.S. provides large-scale political support, having used its [United Nations Security Council veto power](1900272.md) 42 times against [resolutions condemning Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_Nations_resolutions_concerning_Israel), out of 83 times in which its [veto](74661.md) has been used. Between 1991 and 2011, out of the 24 vetoes invoked by the U.S., 15 were used to protect Israel.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] As of 2021, the United States remains the only [permanent member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) to have recognized the [Golan Heights](7515865.md) as non-occupied Israeli sovereign territory, [recognized Jerusalem as the capital of Israel](4024423.md), and [moved its embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_recognition_of_Jerusalem_as_capital_of_Israel) there from [Tel Aviv](31453.md) in 2018.[^cite_note-5] Israel is designated by the United States as a [major non-NATO ally](3812445.md).

Bilateral relations have developed from an early American policy of sympathy and support for the establishment of a [Jewish homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_homeland) in 1948 to a partnership that connects the United States – a [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower) seeking to balance competing interests in the Middle East – with Israel, a small but militarily powerful nation.[^cite_note-MarkIsrUSrel2-6] Late U.S. senator [Jesse Helms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Helms) argued that the military foothold offered by Israel justifies the expense of American military aid, referring to Israel as "America's [aircraft carrier](2219.md) in the Middle East".[^cite_note-7]

## History



First American Consulate in Jerusalem, Ottoman Empire (1857)

Support for Zionism among [American Jews](1131183.md) was minimal, until the involvement of [Louis Brandeis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Brandeis) in the [Federation of American Zionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_American_Zionists),[^cite_note-8] starting in 1912 and the establishment of the [Provisional Executive Committee for General Zionist Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Executive_Committee_for_General_Zionist_Affairs?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1914; it was empowered by the [Zionist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Zionist_Organization) "to deal with all Zionist matters, until better times come".[^cite_note-9]

[Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson), who was sympathetic to the plight of Jews in Europe and favorable to Zionist objectives (giving his assent to the text of the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration) shortly before its release) stated on March 2, 1919, "I am persuaded that the Allied nations with the fullest concurrence of our own Government and people are agreed that in Palestine shall be laid the foundation of a future Jewish commonwealth" and on April 16, 1919, corroborated the U.S. government's "expressed acquiescence" in the Balfour Declaration.[^cite_note-10] Wilson's statements did not result in a change in policy of the U.S. State Department in favor of Zionist aims. However, the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) passed the [Lodge–Fish Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodge%E2%80%93Fish_Resolution),[^cite_note-11] the first [joint resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_resolution) stating its support for "the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people" on September 21, 1922.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The same day, the [Mandate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_of_Palestine) was approved by the Council of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations).

During [World War II](32927.md), while U.S. foreign policy decisions were often *ad hoc* moves and solutions dictated by the demands of the war, the Zionist movement made a fundamental departure from traditional Zionist policy and its stated goals, at the [Biltmore Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biltmore_Conference) in May 1942.[^cite_note-14] Previous stated policy towards establishing a Jewish "national home" in Palestine were gone; these were replaced with its new policy "that Palestine be established as a Jewish Commonwealth" like other nations, in cooperation with the United States, not Britain.[^cite_note-15] Two attempts by Congress in 1944 to pass resolutions declaring U.S. government support for the establishment of a Jewish state in Palestine were objected to by the Departments of War and State, because of wartime considerations and Arab opposition to the creation of a Jewish state. The resolutions were permanently dropped.[^cite_note-16]

Following the war, the "new postwar era witnessed an intensive involvement of the United States in the political and economic affairs of the [Middle East](19323.md), in contrast to the hands-off attitude characteristic of the prewar period. In [Truman's administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Harry_S._Truman_administration) the United States had to face and define its policy in all three sectors that provided the root causes of American interests in the region: the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) threat, the [birth of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine), and [petroleum](23195.md)."[^cite_note-17]



Israeli prime minister David Ben-Gurion meeting with U.S. representatives [Franklin D. Roosevelt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt_Jr.) and [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) in 1951

The [United States](3434750.md) was the first country to recognize the [State of Israel](9282173.md) on May 14, 1948.[^cite_note-\:10-18]

## Recognition of the State of Israel



Telegram on recognition of the State of Israel, May 1948

Previous American presidents, although encouraged by active support from members of the American and world Jewish communities, as well as domestic civic groups, labor unions, and political parties, supported the Jewish homeland concept, alluded to in [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland)'s 1917 [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration), they officially continued to "acquiesce". Throughout the Roosevelt and Truman administrations, the Departments of War and State recognized the possibility of a Soviet-Arab connection and the potential Arab restriction on oil supplies to the U.S., and advised against U.S. intervention on behalf of the Jews.[^cite_note-19] With continuing conflict in the area and worsening humanitarian conditions among [Holocaust survivors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_survivors) in Europe, on November 29, 1947, and with U.S. support, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) adopted as Resolution 181, the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine), which recommended the adoption and implementation of a *Plan of Partition with Economic Union*.[^cite_note-20] The voting was heavily lobbied by Zionist supporters, which President [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman) later noted,[^cite_note-21] and rejected by the Arabs.



President Truman meeting on May 8, 1951, with Prime Minister [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) (right) and [Abba Eban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abba_Eban) (center)

As the end of the mandate approached, the decision to recognize the Jewish state remained contentious, with significant disagreement between Truman, his domestic and campaign adviser, [Clark Clifford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Clifford), and both the [State Department](31975.md) and [Defense Department](7279897.md). Truman, while sympathetic to the [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) cause—and personally lobbied for recognition by old friend [Eddie Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Jacobson)—was most concerned about relieving the plight of the [displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_persons_camps_in_post%E2%80%93World_War_II_Europe); Secretary of State [George Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marshall) feared U.S. backing of a Jewish state would harm relations with the [Muslim](19541.md) world, limit access to Middle Eastern oil, and destabilize the region. On May 12, 1948, Truman met with Secretary of State Marshall, Under Secretary of State [Robert A. Lovett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Lovett), Counsel to the President Clark Clifford, and others to discuss the Palestine situation. Clifford argued in favor of recognizing the new Jewish state in accordance with the partition resolution. Marshall opposed Clifford's arguments, contending that they were based on domestic political considerations in the election year. The Secretary of State said that, if Truman followed Clifford's advice and recognized the Jewish state, then Marshall would vote against Truman in the election. Truman did not clearly state his views in the meeting.[^cite_note-TruLibChrono-22]

Two days later, on May 14, 1948, the United States, under Truman, became the first country to extend any form of recognition. This happened within hours of the [Jewish People's Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_People's_Council) gathering at the Tel Aviv Museum and [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) declaring "the establishment of a [Jewish state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_state) in [Eretz Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretz_Israel), to be known as the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel)". The phrase "in Eretz Israel" is the only place in the [Declaration of the Establishment of the State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_the_Establishment_of_the_State_of_Israel) containing any reference to the location of the new State.[^cite_note-23]

The text of the communication from the [provisional government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_government_of_Israel) to Truman was as follows:

> MY DEAR MR. PRESIDENT: I have the honor to notify you that the state of Israel has been proclaimed as an independent republic within frontiers approved by the General Assembly of the United Nations in its Resolution of 29 November 1947, and that a provisional government has been charged to assume the rights and duties of government for preserving law and order within the boundaries of Israel, for defending the state against external aggression, and for discharging the obligations of Israel to the other nations of the world in accordance with international law. The Act of Independence will become effective at one minute after six o'clock on the evening of 14 May 1948, Washington time.
>
> With full knowledge of the deep bond of sympathy which has existed and has been strengthened over the past thirty years between the Government of the United States and the Jewish people of Palestine, I have been authorized by the provisional government of the new state to tender this message and to express the hope that your government will recognize and will welcome Israel into the community of nations.
>
> Very respectfully yours,
>
> ELIAHU EPSTEIN
>
> &#x20;Agent, [Provisional government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_government_of_Israel)[^cite_note-24]

The text of the United States recognition was as follows:

> This Government has been informed that a Jewish state has been proclaimed in Palestine, and recognition has been requested by the provisional Government thereof.
>
> The United States recognizes the provisional government as the de facto authority of the new State of Israel.
>
> (sgn.) *Harry Truman*
>
> Approved,
>
> 14 May 1948
>
> &#x20;6.11[^cite_note-25]

With this unexpected decision, U.S. representative to the United Nations [Warren Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Austin), whose team had been working on [an alternative trusteeship proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_trusteeship_proposal), shortly thereafter left his office at the UN and went home. Secretary of State Marshall sent a State Department official to the United Nations to prevent the entire United States delegation from resigning.[^cite_note-TruLibChrono-22] [De jure](43594.md) recognition came on January 31, 1949.

Following UN mediation by American [Ralph Bunche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Bunche), the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) ended the [1948 Arab Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab_Israeli_War). Related to enforcement of the armistice, the United States signed the [Tripartite Declaration of 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Declaration_of_1950) with Britain and France. In it, they pledged to take action within and outside the United Nations to prevent violations of the frontiers or armistice lines; outlined their commitment to peace and stability in the area and their opposition to the use or threat of force; and reiterated their opposition to the development of an arms race in the region.

Under rapidly changing geopolitical circumstances, U.S. policy in the Middle East was generally geared toward supporting Arab states' independence; aiding the development of oil-producing countries; preventing [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) influence from gaining a foothold in [Greece](12108.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), and [Iran](14653.md); and preventing an arms race and maintaining a neutral stance in the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md). U.S. policymakers initially used [foreign aid](22557286.md) to support these objectives.

## Foreign policy of the U.S. government

### Eisenhower administration (1953–1961)



Prime Minister Ben Gurion with Vice President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) in 1960

During these years of [austerity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austerity_in_Israel), the United States provided Israel moderate amounts of economic aid, mostly as loans for basic foodstuffs; a far greater share of state income derived from [German war reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reparations_Agreement_between_Israel_and_West_Germany) (86% of Israeli [GDP](12594.md) in 1956) which were used for domestic development.

[France](5843419.md) became Israel's main arms supplier at this time and provided Israel with advanced military equipment and technology. This support was seen by Israel to counter the perceived threat from [Egypt](8087628.md) under President [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser) with respect to the "[Czech arms deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_arms_deal)" of September 1955. During the 1956 [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) invaded Egypt and were soon followed by French and British forces. For differing reasons, France, Israel and Britain signed a [secret agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_of_S%C3%A8vres) to topple Nasser by regaining control of the Suez Canal, following its nationalization, and to occupy parts of western Sinai assuring free passage of shipping (for Israel) in the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba).[^cite_note-26] In response, the U.S., with support from the Soviet Union at the UN intervened on behalf of Egypt to force a withdrawal. Afterward, Nasser expressed a desire to establish closer relations with the United States. Eager to increase its influence in the region, and prevent Nasser from going over to the Soviet Bloc, U.S. policy was to remain neutral and not become too closely allied with Israel. At this time, the only assistance the U.S. provided Israel was food aid. In the early 1960s, the U.S. would begin to sell advanced, but defensive, weapons to Israel, Egypt, and [Jordan](7515964.md), including [Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIM-23_Hawk) anti-aircraft missiles.

### Kennedy and Johnson administrations (1961–1969)



[Yad Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yad_Kennedy), Memorial to [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy), located in the [Jerusalem Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Forest)

Main article: [Presidency of John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_John_F._Kennedy)

As president, Kennedy initiated the creation of security ties with Israel, and he was the founder of the [U.S.–Israeli military alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_military_relations). Kennedy, basing his policy decision on his White House advisors, avoided the State Department with its greater interest in the Arab world. A central issue was the status of Palestinians, split among Israel, [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Gaza_Strip_by_Egypt) and [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank). By 1961 there were 1.2 million Palestinian refugees living in Jordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Egypt. The Soviet Union, although it initially supported the creation of Israel, was now an opponent, and looking to the Arab world to build support. The United Nations General assembly was generally [anti-Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_Nations_resolutions_concerning_Israel), but all decisions were subject to American veto power in the Security Council. According to international law, UNGA resolutions are not legally binding while UNSC resolutions are. Kennedy tried to be evenhanded, but domestic political pressures pushed him to support Israel.[^cite_note-27]

> I had forecast that once the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War) was over, [France would drop Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France-Israel_relations) like a hot coal and renew its ties with the Arab world. And that, of course, is exactly what happened – Israel adopted the US instead.

— [Uri Avnery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Avnery)

[^cite_note-uri-28]

Kennedy ended the arms embargo that the Eisenhower and Truman administrations had enforced on Israel. Describing the protection of Israel as a moral and national commitment, he was the first to introduce the concept of a 'special relationship' (as he described it to [Golda Meir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golda_Meir)) between the U.S. and Israel.[^cite_note-29]

President John F. Kennedy in 1962 sold Israel a major weapon system, the [Hawk antiaircraft missile.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIM-23_Hawk) Professor Abraham Ben-Zvi of Tel Aviv University argues that the sale resulted from Kennedy's "need to maintain – and preferably broaden and solidify – the base of Jewish support of the administration on the eve of the November 1962 congressional elections." As soon as the decision was made White House officials told American Jewish leaders about it. However, historian Zachary Wallace argues that the new policy was driven primarily by Kennedy's admiration of the Jewish state. It deserved American support in order to achieve stability in the Middle East.[^cite_note-30]



Israeli prime minister [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol) meeting Attorney General [Robert F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy), 1964

Kennedy warned the Israeli government against the production of nuclear materials in [Dimona](9103.md), which he believed could instigate a nuclear arms-race in the Middle East. After the existence of a nuclear plant was initially denied by the Israeli government, [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) stated in a speech to the Israeli [Knesset](74656.md) on December 21, 1960, that the purpose of the nuclear plant at [Beersheba](312755.md) was for "research in problems of arid zones and desert flora and fauna."[^cite_note-Salt201203-31] When Ben-Gurion met with Kennedy in New York, he claimed that Dimona was, for the time being, being developed to provide nuclear power for desalinization and other peaceful purposes. In 1962, the Israeli and U.S. governments agreed to an annual inspection regime. Despite this inspection policy \[agreement], [Rodger Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodger_Davies), the director of the State Department's Office of Near Eastern Affairs, concluded in March 1965 that Israel was developing [nuclear weapons](21785.md). He reported that Israel's target date for achieving nuclear capability was 1968–1969.[^cite_note-Salt201203-31] In 1966, when defecting [Iraqi](7515928.md) pilot [Munir Redfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munir_Redfa) landed in Israel flying a Soviet-built [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) fighter jet, information on the plane was immediately shared with the United States.

During [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Lyndon_B._Johnson)'s presidency, U.S. policy shifted to a whole-hearted, but not unquestioning, support for Israel. In the lead up to the [Six-Day War](29328.md) of 1967, while the Johnson Administration was sympathetic to Israel's need to defend itself against foreign attack, the U.S. worried that Israel's response would be disproportionate and potentially destabilizing. Israel's raid into [Jordan](7515964.md) after the 1966 [Samu Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samu_Incident) was very troubling to the U.S. because Jordan was also an ally and had received over \$500 million in aid for construction of the [East Ghor Main Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Canal), which was virtually destroyed in subsequent raids.

The primary concern of the Johnson Administration was that should war break out in the region, the United States and the Soviet Union would be drawn into it. Intense diplomatic negotiations with the nations in the region and the Soviets, including the first use of the [Hotline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow%E2%80%93Washington_hotline), failed to prevent war. When Israel launched preemptive strikes against the Egyptian Air force, Secretary of State [Dean Rusk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Rusk) was disappointed as he felt a diplomatic solution could have been possible.

During the Six-Day War on June 8, 1967, Israeli jets and torpedo boats [attacked USS *Liberty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Liberty_incident), a [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) [spy ship](2521578.md) in [international waters](394357.md) north of the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), about 25.5 [nautical miles](21289.md) (47.2 km; 29.3 mi) northwest from the Egyptian city of [Arish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arish),[^cite_note-NSAhistory26-32] killing 34 crewmen and wounding 171 more. Following a strong protest by the U.S. government, Israel, which stated the *Liberty* had been mistaken as the Egyptian vessel *El Quseir*, claimed it was an instance of [friendly fire](202484.md) and issued a formal apology. The U.S. government accepted the apology as such, although the incident raised much controversy, and some still believe it to be deliberate:[^cite_note-trib-33][^cite_note-34] [Thomas Hinman Moorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hinman_Moorer), 7th [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md), famously accused President Johnson of having covered up that the attack was a deliberate act, calling it "one of the classic all-American cover-ups".[^cite_note-ap2003-10-23-35]

Prior to the Six-Day War, U.S. administrations had taken considerable care to avoid giving the appearance of favoritism. Writing in *American Presidents and the Middle East*, [George Lenczowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lenczowski) notes, "Johnson's was an unhappy, virtually tragic presidency", regarding "America's standing and posture in the Middle East", and marked a turning point in both U.S.–Israeli and U.S.–Arab relations.[^cite_note-LenczAmPresLBJ-36] He characterizes the Middle Eastern perception of the U.S. as moving from "the most popular of Western countries" before 1948, to having "its glamour diminished, but Eisenhower's standing during the Arab–Israeli Suez Crisis convinced many Middle Eastern moderates that, if not actually lovable, the United States was at least a fair country to deal with; this view of U.S. fairness and impartiality still prevailed during Kennedy's presidency; but during Lyndon B. Johnson's presidency America's policy took a definite turn in the pro-Israeli direction". He added: "The June war of 1967 confirmed this impression, and from 1967 on \[writing in 1990] the United States emerged as the most distrusted if not actually hated country in the Middle East."

Following the war, the perception in Washington was that many Arab states (notably Egypt) had permanently drifted toward the Soviets. In 1968, with strong support from Congress, Johnson approved the sale of [Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-4_Phantom_II) fighters to Israel, establishing the precedent for U.S. support for Israel's qualitative military edge over its neighbors. However, the U.S. continued to provide military equipment to Arab states such as [Lebanon](17771.md) and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), to counter Soviet arms sales in the region.

During the Israeli–Egyptian [War of Attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Attrition), Israeli commandos captured a Soviet-built [P-12 radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-12_radar) station in an operation code-named [Rooster 53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rooster_53). Previously unknown information was subsequently shared with the U.S.[^cite_note-38]

When the French government imposed an [arms embargo](5974443.md) on Israel in 1967 shortly after the Six-Day War, Israeli spies procured designs of the [Dassault Mirage 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_5) from a Swiss Jewish engineer in order to build the [IAI Kfir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Kfir). These designs were also shared with the United States.

#### Qualitative Military Edge

According to the pro-Israel American [think tank](37101.md)[^cite_note-proI-39] [Washington Institute for Near East Policy (WINEP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Institute_for_Near_East_Policy), Qualitative Military Edge (QME) is a concept in [U.S. foreign policy](7564.md). The U.S. commits itself to maintain [Israel](9282173.md)'s qualitative military edge (QME) – that is, the [technological](90815.md), [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics), and other advantages that allow it to deter numerically superior adversaries according to the [WINEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WINEP).[^cite_note-40] This policy is defined in current [U.S. law](27552742.md).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

#### 1963 standoff between Israel and United States



[Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) and [Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) in 1961

Israeli newspaper *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* reported in 2019 that, throughout the spring and summer of 1963, the leaders of the United States and Israel – President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) and prime ministers [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) and [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol) – were engaged in a high-stakes battle of wills over [Israel's nuclear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_Israel). The tensions were invisible to the publics of both countries, and only a few senior officials, on both sides, were aware of the severity of the situation. According to [Yuval Ne'eman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuval_Ne'eman), [Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol), Ben-Gurion's successor, and his associates saw Kennedy as presenting Israel with a real ultimatum. According to Ne’eman, the former Israel Air Force commander Maj. Gen. (res.) [Dan Tolkowsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Tolkowsky), seriously entertained the fear that Kennedy might send U.S. airborne troops to [Dimona](9103.md), the home of [Israel's nuclear complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Nuclear_Research_Center).[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

On March 25, 1963, President Kennedy and CIA Director [John A. McCone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._McCone) discussed the Israeli nuclear program. According to McCone, Kennedy raised the "question of Israel acquiring nuclear capability," and McCone provided Kennedy with [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Kent)'s estimate of the anticipated negative consequences of Israeli nuclearization. According to McCone, Kennedy then instructed National Security Adviser [McGeorge Bundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGeorge_Bundy) to guide Secretary of State [Dean Rusk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Rusk), in collaboration with the CIA director and the AEC chairman, to submit a proposal "as to how some form of international or bilateral U.S. safeguards could be instituted to protect against the contingency mentioned." That also meant that the "next informal inspection of the Israeli reactor complex \[must] ... be undertaken promptly and ... be as thorough as possible."[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

This presidential request was translated into diplomatic action, on April 2, 1963, Ambassador Barbour met Prime Minister Ben-Gurion and presented the American request for his "assent to semi-annual visits to Dimona perhaps in May and November, with full access to all parts and instruments in the facility, by qualified U.S. scientists." Ben-Gurion, apparently taken by surprise, responded by saying the issue would have to be postponed until after [Passover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover), which that year ended on April 15. To highlight the point further, two days later, Assistant Secretary [Talbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Talbot) summoned Israeli ambassador [Harman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avraham_Harman) to the State Department and presented him with a diplomatic démarche on the inspections. This message to Ben-Gurion was the first salvo in what would become "the toughest American-Israeli confrontation over the Israeli nuclear program".[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

On April 26, 1963, more than three weeks after the original U.S. demand concerning Dimona, Ben-Gurion responded to Kennedy with a seven-page letter that focused on broad issues of Israeli security and regional stability. Claiming that Israel faced an unprecedented threat, Ben-Gurion invoked the specter of "another Holocaust," and insisted that Israel's security should be protected by joint external security guarantees, to be extended by the U.S. and the Soviet Union. Kennedy, however, was determined not to let Ben-Gurion change the subject. On May 4, 1963, he replied to the prime minister, assuring him that "we are watching closely current developments in the Arab world". As to Ben-Gurion's proposal for a joint superpower declaration, Kennedy dismissed both its practicality and its political wisdom. Kennedy was much less worried about an "early Arab attack" than he was by "a successful development of advanced offensive systems which, as you say, could not be dealt with by presently available means."[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

Kennedy, would not budge on Dimona, and the disagreements became a "pain in the neck" for him, as [Robert Komer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Komer) later wrote. The confrontation with Israel escalated when the State Department transmitted Kennedy's latest letter to the Tel Aviv embassy on June 15 for immediate delivery to Ben-Gurion by Ambassador Barbour. In the letter Kennedy fleshed out his insistence on biannual visits with a set of detailed technical conditions. The letter was akin to an ultimatum: If the U.S. government could not obtain "reliable information" on the state of the Dimona project, Washington's "commitment to and support of Israel" could be "seriously jeopardized." But the letter was never presented to Ben-Gurion. The telegram with Kennedy's letter arrived in Tel Aviv on Saturday, June 15, the day before Ben-Gurion's announcement of his resignation, a decision that stunned his country and the world. Ben-Gurion never explained, in writing or orally, what led him to resign, beyond citing "personal reasons." It is widely believed that the [Lavon Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavon_Affair), a botched Israeli spy mission in Egypt, was the impetus for his resignation. He denied that his move was related to any specific policy issues, but the question of the extent to which Kennedy's Dimona pressure played a role remains open to discussion to the present day.[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

On July 5, less than 10 days after Levi Eshkol succeeded Ben-Gurion as prime minister, Ambassador Barbour delivered to him a first letter from President Kennedy. The letter was virtually a copy of the undelivered letter of June 15 to Ben-Gurion.[^cite_note-45] As Yuval Ne’eman stated, it was immediately apparent to Eshkol and his advisers that Kennedy's demands were akin to an ultimatum, and thus constituted a crisis in the making. A stunned Eshkol, in his first and interim response, on July 17, requested more time to study the subject and for consultations. The premier noted that while he hoped that U.S-Israeli friendship would grow under his watch, "Israel would do what it had to do for its national security and to safeguard its sovereign rights." Barbour, apparently wanting to mitigate the bluntness of the letter, assured Eshkol that Kennedy's statement was "factual": Critics of strong U.S.-Israel relations might complicate the diplomatic relationship if Dimona was left uninspected.[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

On August 19, after six weeks of consultations that generated at least eight different drafts, Eshkol handed Barbour his written reply to Kennedy's demands. It began by reiterating Ben-Gurion's past assurances that Dimona's purpose was peaceful. As to Kennedy's request, Eshkol wrote that given the special relationship between the two countries, he had decided to allow regular visits of U.S. representatives to the Dimona site. On the specific issue of the schedule, Eshkol suggested – as Ben-Gurion had in his last letter to Kennedy – that late 1963 would be the time for the first visit: By then, he wrote, "the French group will have handed the reactor over to us and it will be undertaking general tests and measurements of its physical parameters at zero power."[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

Eshkol was vague on the proposed frequency of visits. Eshkol disregarded Kennedy's demand for biannual tours, while avoiding a frontal challenge to Kennedy's request. "Having considered this request, I believe we shall be able to reach agreement on the future schedule of visits," Eshkol wrote. In sum, the prime minister split the difference: To end the confrontation, he assented to "regular visits" by U.S. scientists, but he did not accept the idea of the prompt visit that Kennedy wanted and avoided making an explicit commitment to biannual inspections. Kennedy's appreciative reply did not mention these divergences, but assumed a basic agreement on "regular visits."[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

In the wake of Eshkol's letter, the first of the long-sought regular inspection visits to Dimona took place in mid-January 1964, two months after [Kennedy's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy). The Israelis told the American visitors that the reactor had gone critical only a few weeks earlier, but that claim was not accurate. Israel acknowledged years later that the Dimona reactor became operational in mid-1963, as the Kennedy administration had originally assumed.[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

It turned out that Kennedy's insistence on biannual visits to Dimona was not implemented after his death. U.S. government officials remained interested in such a schedule, and President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) did raise the issue with Eshkol, but he never pressed hard on the subject the way that Kennedy had.[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

In the end, the confrontation between President Kennedy and two Israeli prime ministers resulted in a series of six American inspections of the Dimona nuclear complex, once a year between 1964 and 1969. They were never conducted under the strict conditions Kennedy laid out in his letters. While Kennedy's successor remained committed to the cause of nuclear nonproliferation and supported American inspection visits at Dimona, he was much less concerned about holding the Israelis to Kennedy's terms. In retrospect, this change of attitude may have saved the Israeli nuclear program.[^cite_note-Haaretz2019-44]

### Nixon and Ford administrations (1969–1977)

On June 19, 1970, Secretary of State [William P. Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Rogers) formally proposed the [Rogers Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Plan), which called for a 90-day cease-fire and a military standstill zone on each side of the Suez Canal, to calm the ongoing [War of Attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Attrition). It was an effort to reach agreement specifically on the framework of [UN Resolution 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Resolution_242), which called for Israeli withdrawal from territories occupied in 1967 and mutual recognition of each state's sovereignty and independence.[^cite_note-46] The Egyptians accepted the Rogers Plan, but the Israelis were split and did not; they failed to get sufficient support within the "unity government". Despite the Labor-dominant [Alignments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alignment_\(political_party\)), formal acceptance of UN 242 and "peace for withdrawal" earlier that year, [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin) and the [right wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_wing) [Gahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gahal) alliance were adamantly opposed to withdraw from the [Palestinian Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Territories); the second-largest party in the government resigned on August 5, 1970.[^cite_note-47] Ultimately, the plan also failed due to insufficient support from Nixon for his secretary of state's plan, preferring instead the position of his [National Security Advisor](44028.md), [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger), not to pursue the initiative.

No breakthrough occurred even after President Sadat of Egypt in 1972 unexpectedly expelled Soviet advisers from Egypt, and again signaled to Washington his willingness to negotiate.[^cite_note-48]

On February 28, 1973, during a visit in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), the then Israeli prime minister [Golda Meir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golda_Meir) agreed with the then U.S. National Security Advisor [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger)'s peace proposal based on "security versus sovereignty": Israel would accept Egyptian sovereignty over all [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), while Egypt would accept Israeli presence in some of Sinai strategic positions.[^cite_note-Rabin1996p215-49][^cite_note-Kissinger2011p252-50][^cite_note-Kumaraswamy2013p105-51][^cite_note-Parker2001p64-52][^cite_note-Spiegel1986p237-53]

Faced with this lack of progress on the diplomatic front, and hoping to force the Nixon administration to become more involved, Egypt prepared for military conflict. In October 1973, Egypt and Syria, simultaneously attacked Israel, thus starting the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War).



U.S. president [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) and Israeli prime minister [Golda Meir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golda_Meir) meeting on March 1, 1973, in the Oval Office. Nixon's National Security Advisor [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger) is to the right of Nixon.

Despite intelligence indicating an attack from Egypt and Syria, Prime Minister [Golda Meir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golda_Meir) made the controversial decision not to launch a pre-emptive strike. Meir, among other concerns, feared alienating the United States, if Israel was seen as starting another war, as Israel only trusted the United States to come to its aid. In retrospect, the decision not to strike was probably a sound one, though it is vigorously debated in Israel to this day. Later, according to Secretary of State [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger), had Israel struck first, they would not have received "so much as a nail". On October 6, 1973, during the Jewish holiday of [Yom Kippur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur), Egypt and Syria, with the support of Arab expeditionary forces and with backing from the Soviet Union, launched simultaneous attacks against Israel. The resulting conflict is known as the Yom Kippur War. The Egyptian Army was initially able to breach Israeli defenses, advance into the Sinai, and establish defensive positions along the east bank of the [Suez Canal](29323.md), but they were later repulsed in a massive tank battle when they tried to advance further to draw pressure away from Syria. The Israelis then crossed the Suez Canal. Major battles with heavy losses for both sides took place. At the same time, the Syrians almost broke through Israel's thin defenses in the Golan Heights, but were eventually stopped by reinforcements and pushed back, followed by a successful Israeli advance into Syria. Israel also gained the upper hand in the air and at sea early in the war. Days into the war, it has been suggested that Meir authorized the assembly of Israeli nuclear bombs. This was done openly, perhaps in order to draw American attention, but Meir authorized their use against Egyptian and Syrian targets only if Arab forces managed to advance too far.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The Soviets began to resupply Arab forces, predominantly Syria. Meir asked Nixon for help with military supply. After Israel went on full nuclear alert and loaded their warheads into waiting planes, Nixon ordered the full scale commencement of [a strategic airlift operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Nickel_Grass) to deliver weapons and supplies to Israel; this last move is sometimes called "the airlift that saved Israel". However, by the time the supplies arrived, Israel was gaining the upper hand.

Again, the U.S. and Soviets feared that they would be drawn into a Middle East conflict. After the Soviets threatened intervention on the behalf of Egypt, following Israeli advances beyond the cease-fire lines, the U.S. increased the [Defense Condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Condition) (DEFCON) from four to three, the highest peacetime level. This was prompted after Israel trapped Egypt's Third Army east of the Suez canal.

Kissinger realized the situation presented the United States with a tremendous opportunity—Egypt was totally dependent on the U.S. to prevent Israel from destroying the army, which now had no access to food or water. The position could be parlayed later into allowing the United States to mediate the dispute, and push Egypt out of Soviet influences. As a result, the United States exerted tremendous pressure on the Israelis to refrain from destroying the trapped army. In a phone call with Israeli ambassador [Simcha Dinitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simcha_Dinitz), Kissinger told the ambassador that the destruction of the Egyptian Third Army "is an option that does not exist". The Egyptians later withdrew their request for support and the Soviets complied.

After the war, Kissinger pressured the Israelis to withdraw from Arab lands; this contributed to the first phases of a lasting Israeli-Egyptian peace. American support of Israel during the war contributed to the [1973 OPEC embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) against the United States, which was lifted in March 1974.

#### The Reassessment Crisis

In early 1975, the Israeli government turned down a U.S. initiative for further redeployment in Sinai. President Ford responded on March 21, 1975, by sending Prime Minister Rabin a letter stating that Israeli intransigence has complicated U.S. worldwide interests, and therefore the administration will "reassess" its relations with the Israeli government. In addition, arms shipments to Israel halted. The reassessment crisis came to an end with the Israeli–Egyptian disengagement of forces agreement of September 4, 1975.[^cite_note-56]

### Carter administration (1977–1981)



President Carter, Prime Minister Menachem Begin and [Zbigniew Brzezinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zbigniew_Brzezinski) in 1978

The [Carter administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_administration) was characterized by very active U.S. involvement in the Middle East peace process. With the May 1977 [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Israeli_legislative_election) of [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud)'s [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin) as prime minister, after 29 years of leading the Israeli government opposition, major changes took place regarding Israeli withdrawal from the [occupied territories](2781576.md).[^cite_note-MarkIsrUSrel-57] This led to friction in U.S.–Israeli bilateral relations. The two frameworks included in the Carter-initiated [Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David_Accords) process were viewed by right-wing elements in Israel as creating U.S. pressures on Israel to withdraw from the captured [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories), as well as forcing it to take risks for the sake of peace with Egypt. The Israeli-Egyptian peace treaty was signed at the White House on March 26, 1979. It led to Israeli withdrawal from Sinai by 1982. [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) governments have since argued that their acceptance of full withdrawal from the Sinai as part of these accords and the eventual [Egypt–Israel peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_peace_treaty) fulfilled the Israeli pledge to withdraw from Sinai.[^cite_note-MarkIsrUSrel-57] President Carter's support for a [Palestinian homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_homeland) and for Palestinian political rights particularly created tensions with the Likud government, and little progress was achieved on that front.

### Reagan administration (1981–1989)



President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) and Israeli prime minister [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin) on the [South Lawn](15728461.md) in 1981



President Ronald Reagan meeting Israeli ambassador to the United States [Ephraim Evron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephraim_Evron), 1982

Israeli supporters expressed concerns early in the first [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) term about potential difficulties in U.S.–Israeli relations, in part because several Presidential appointees had ties or past business associations with key Arab countries (for example, Secretaries [Caspar Weinberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_Weinberger) and [George P. Shultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_P._Shultz) were officers in the [Bechtel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechtel_Corporation), which has strong links to the Arab world; see [Arab lobby in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_lobby_in_the_United_States).) However, President Reagan's personal support for Israel, and the compatibility between Israeli and Reagan perspectives on [terrorism](30636.md), security cooperation, and the Soviet threat, led to considerable strengthening in bilateral relations.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

In 1981, Weinberger and Israeli minister of defense [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) signed the [Strategic Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Cooperation_Agreement), establishing a framework for continued consultation and cooperation to enhance the national security of both countries. In November 1983, the two sides formed a [Joint Political Military Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Political_Military_Group), which meets twice a year, to implement most provisions of that agreement. Joint air and sea military exercises began in June 1984, and the United States constructed two [War Reserve Stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Reserve_Stock) facilities in Israel to stockpile military equipment. Although intended for American forces in the Middle East, the equipment can be transferred to Israeli use if necessary.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

U.S.–Israeli ties strengthened during the second Reagan term. Israel was granted "[major non-NATO ally](3812445.md)" status in 1989, giving it access to expanded weapons systems and opportunities to bid on U.S. defense contracts. The United States maintained grant aid to Israel at \$3 billion annually and implemented a free trade agreement in 1985. Since then all customs duties between the two trading partners have been eliminated. However, relations soured when Israel carried out [Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera), an Israeli airstrike on the [Osirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osirak) nuclear reactor in [Baghdad](4492.md). Reagan suspended a shipment of military aircraft to Israel, and harshly criticized the action. Relations also soured during the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War), when the United States even contemplated sanctions to stop the Israeli [siege of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Beirut). The U.S. reminded Israel that weaponry provided by the U.S. was to be used for defensive purposes only, and suspended shipments of cluster munitions to Israel. Although the war exposed some serious differences between Israeli and U.S. policies, such as Israel's rejection of the Reagan peace plan of September 1, 1982, it did not alter the Administration's favoritism for Israel and the emphasis it placed on Israel's importance to the United States. Although critical of Israeli actions, the United States vetoed a Soviet-proposed [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) resolution to impose an arms embargo on Israel.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

In 1985, the U.S. supported Israel's economic stabilization through roughly \$1.5 billion in two-year loan guarantees the creation of a U.S.–Israel bilateral economic forum called the [U.S.–Israel Joint Economic Development Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Israel_Joint_Economic_Development_Group) (JEDG).[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

The second Reagan term ended on what many Israelis considered to be a sour note when the United States opened a dialogue with the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) in December 1988. But, despite the U.S.–PLO dialogue, the [Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Pollard) spy case, and the Israeli rejection of the Shultz peace initiative in the spring of 1988, pro-Israeli organizations in the United States characterized the Reagan Administration (and the 100th Congress) as the "most pro-Israel ever", and praised the positive overall tone of bilateral relations.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

### George H. W. Bush administration (1989–1993)



[Yitzhak Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Shamir) and [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) meeting at the White House in 1990

In the midst of the [first Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), Secretary of State [James Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baker) told an [American Israel Public Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC) (AIPAC, a [pro-Israel lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States) group) audience on May 22, 1989, that Israel should abandon its "expansionist policies". President [Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) raised the ire of the [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) government when he told a press conference on March 3, 1991, that [East Jerusalem](343508.md) was occupied territory and not a sovereign part of Israel as Israel says. Israel had [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Law) East Jerusalem in 1980, an action which did not gain international recognition. The United States and Israel disagreed over the Israeli interpretation of the Israeli plan to hold elections for a Palestinian peace conference delegation in the summer of 1989, and also disagreed over the need for an investigation of the Jerusalem incident of October 8, 1990, in which Israeli police killed 17 Palestinians.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

Amid the [Iraq–Kuwait crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and [Iraqi](7515928.md) threats against Israel generated by it, former president Bush repeated the U.S. commitment to Israel's security. Israeli–U.S. tension eased after the start of the Persian [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) on January 16, 1991, when Israel became a target of Iraqi [Scud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud) missiles, suffering over 30 strikes during the war. The United States urged Israel not to retaliate against Iraq for the attacks because it was believed that Iraq wanted to draw Israel into the conflict and force other coalition members, Egypt and [Syria](7515849.md) in particular, to quit the coalition and join Iraq in a war against Israel. Israel did not retaliate, and gained praise for its restraint.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

Following the Gulf War, the administration immediately returned to Arab-Israeli peacemaking, believing there was a window of opportunity to use the political capital generated by the U.S. victory to revitalize the Arab-Israeli peace process. On March 6, 1991, President Bush addressed Congress in a speech often cited as the administration's principal policy statement on the new order in relation to the Middle East, following the expulsion of Iraqi forces from Kuwait.[^cite_note-OrenPFnF-59][^cite_note-60] [Michael Oren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Oren) summarizes the speech, saying: "The president proceeded to outline his plan for maintaining a permanent U.S. naval presence in the Gulf, for providing funds for Middle East development, and for instituting safeguards against the spread of unconventional weapons. The centerpiece of his program, however, was the achievement of an Arab–Israeli treaty based on the territory-for-peace principle and the fulfillment of Palestinian rights." As a first step, Bush announced his intention to reconvene the international peace [conference in Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Conference_of_1991).[^cite_note-OrenPFnF-59]

However, unlike earlier American peace efforts, no new aid commitments would be used. This was both because President Bush and Secretary Baker felt the coalition victory and increased U.S. prestige would itself induce a new Arab–Israeli dialogue, and because their diplomatic initiative focused on process and procedure rather than on agreements and concessions. From Washington's perspective, economic inducements would not be necessary, although these did enter the process when Israel injected them in May. Israeli prime minister [Yitzhak Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Shamir)'s request for \$10 billion in U.S. [loan guarantees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_guarantees) added a new dimension to U.S. diplomacy and sparked a political showdown between his government and the Bush administration.[^cite_note-LasenskyUnderwrite-61]

Bush and Baker were thus instrumental in convening the Madrid peace conference in October 1991 and in persuading all the parties to engage in the subsequent peace negotiations. It was reported widely that the Bush Administration did not share an amicable relationship with the [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) government of [Yitzhak Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Shamir). However, the Israeli government did win the repeal of [United Nations General Assembly Resolution 3379](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_3379), which equated Zionism with racism. After the conference, in December 1991, the UN passed [United Nations General Assembly Resolution 46/86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_46/86); Israel had made revocation of resolution 3379 a condition of its participation in the Madrid peace conference.[^cite_note-62] After the [Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_\(Israel\)) won the 1992 election, U.S.–Israel relations appeared to improve. The Labor coalition approved a partial housing construction freeze in the occupied territories on July 19, something the Shamir government had not done despite Bush Administration appeals for a freeze as a condition for the loan guarantees.

### Clinton administration (1993–2001)



[Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin), [Bill Clinton](3356.md), and [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) during the Oslo Accords on September 13, 1993.

Israel and the PLO exchanged letters of mutual recognition on September 10, and signed the Declaration of Principles on September 13, 1993. President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) announced on September 10 that the United States and the PLO would reestablish their dialogue. On October 26, 1994, President Clinton witnessed the [Jordan–Israeli peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Jordan_peace_treaty) signing, and President Clinton, Egyptian president [Mubarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosni_Mubarak), and [King Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein) of [Jordan](7515964.md) witnessed the White House signing of the September 28, 1995, [Interim Agreement between Israel and the Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord).[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]



[Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) and Reuma Weizman, wife of [Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman) at [Beit HaNassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_HaNassi), [Jerusalem](16043.md), 1998

President Clinton attended the funeral of assassinated Prime Minister [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin) in Jerusalem in November 1995. Following a March 1996 visit to Israel, President Clinton offered \$100 million in aid for Israel's anti-terror activities, another \$200 million for [Arrow anti-missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(Israeli_missile\)) deployment, and about \$50 million for an anti-missile laser weapon.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

President Clinton disagreed with Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md)'s policy of expanding Jewish settlements in the Palestinian territories, and it was reported that the president believed that the prime minister delayed the peace process. President Clinton hosted negotiations at the [Wye River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wye_River_Memorandum) Conference Center in Maryland, ending with the signing of an agreement on October 23, 1998. Israel suspended implementation of the Wye agreement in early December 1998, when the Palestinians violated the Wye Agreement by threatening to declare a state (Palestinian statehood was not mentioned in Wye). In January 1999, the Wye Agreement was delayed until the Israeli elections in May.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

[Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) was elected prime minister on May 17, 1999, and won a vote of confidence for his government on July 6, 1999. President Clinton and Prime Minister Barak appeared to establish close personal relations during four days of meetings between July 15 and 20. President Clinton mediated meetings between Prime Minister Barak and Chairman [Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) at the White House, [Oslo](22309.md), [Shepherdstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherdstown,_West_Virginia), Camp David, and [Sharm al-Shaykh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharm_el-Sheikh_Memorandum) in the search for peace.[^cite_note-JohnLang-58]

### George W. Bush administration (2001–2009)



[Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md), [George W. Bush](3414021.md), and [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) after reading statement to the press during the closing moments of the Red Sea Summit in [Aqaba](328399.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), June 4, 2003.

President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and Prime Minister [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) established good relations in their March and June 2001 meetings. On October 4, 2001, shortly after the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), Sharon accused the Bush Administration of appeasing the Palestinians at Israel's expense in a bid for Arab support for the U.S. anti-terror campaign.[^cite_note-63] The White House said that the remark was unacceptable. Rather than apologize for the remark, Sharon said that the United States failed to understand him. Also, the United States criticized the Israeli practice of [assassinating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_killings_by_Israel_Defense_Forces) Palestinians believed to be engaged in terrorism, which appeared to some Israelis to be inconsistent with the U.S. policy of pursuing [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) "dead or alive".[^cite_note-64] Nonetheless, it was later revealed that Sharon obtained an understanding from the Bush administration that the U.S. government would provide support for Israel while it undertook an extensive campaign of targeted assassinations against [Palestinian militants](2646043.md), in exchange for an Israeli undertaking to desist from continuing with the creation of further [settlements](15123.md) in the [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-65]

In 2003, in the middle of the [Second Intifada](71717.md) and a sharp economic downturn in Israel, the U.S. provided Israel with \$9 billion in conditional loan guarantees made available through 2011 and negotiated each year at the U.S.–Israel Joint Economic Development Group.[^cite_note-66]

All recent U.S. administrations have disapproved of Israel's settlement activity as prejudging final status and possibly preventing the emergence of a contiguous Palestinian state. However, President Bush noted in an April 14, 2002, Memorandum which came to be called "the Bush [Roadmap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_map_for_peace)" (and which established the parameters for subsequent Israel-Palestinian negotiations) the need to take into account changed "realities on the ground, including already existing major Israeli population centers", as well as Israel's security concerns, asserting that "It is unrealistic to expect that the outcome of final status negotiations will be full and complete return to the [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) lines of 1949."[^cite_note-67] He later emphasized that, within these parameters, details of the borders were subjects for negotiations between the parties.

At times of violence, U.S. officials have urged Israel to withdraw as rapidly as possible from Palestinian areas retaken in security operations. The Bush Administration insisted that [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) resolutions be "balanced" by criticizing Palestinian as well as Israeli violence, and it vetoed resolutions which did not meet that standard.[^cite_note-68]

Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) did not name a Special Middle East Envoy and did not say that she would not get involved in direct Israeli-Palestinian negotiations of issues. She said that she preferred to have the Israelis and Palestinians work together, traveling to the region several times in 2005. The Administration supported Israel's [disengagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_disengagement_from_Gaza) from [Gaza](12047.md) as a way to return to the Road Map process to achieve a solution based on two states, Israel and Palestine, living side by side in peace and security.[^cite_note-69] The evacuation of settlers from the Gaza Strip and four small settlements in the northern West Bank was completed on August 23, 2005.

On July 14, 2006, as the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) broke out, the U.S. Congress was notified of a potential sale of \$210 million worth of jet fuel to Israel. The Defense Security Cooperation Agency noted that the sale of the JP-8 fuel, should it be completed, will "enable Israel to maintain the operational capability of its aircraft inventory", and that "The jet fuel will be consumed while the aircraft is in use to keep peace and security in the region".[^cite_note-70] It was reported on July 24 that the United States was in the process of providing Israel with "[bunker buster](167751.md)" bombs, which would allegedly be used to target the leader of Lebanon's [Hezbollah](13919.md) guerrilla group and destroy its trenches.[^cite_note-71]



A protest against military aid near the [U.S. Capitol](31979.md) in July 2007.

American media also questioned whether Israel violated an agreement not to use cluster bombs on civilian targets. Although many of the cluster bombs used were advanced M-85 munitions developed by [Israel Military Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Military_Industries), Israel also used older munitions purchased from the U.S. Evidence during the conflict, hitting civilian areas, although the civilian population had mostly fled. Israel asserts that civilian damage was unavoidable, as Hezbollah ensconced itself in highly populated areas. Simultaneously, indiscriminate Hezbollah rocket fire turned many of its northern towns into virtual ghost towns, in violation of international law. Many bomblets remained undetonated after the war, causing hazard for Lebanese civilians. Israel said that it had not violated any international law because cluster bombs are not illegal and were used only on military targets.[^cite_note-72]

##### Opposing immediate unconditional ceasefire

On July 15, the United Nations Security Council again rejected pleas from Lebanon that it call for an immediate ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon. The Israeli newspaper *Haaretz* reported that the U.S. was the only member of out the 15-nation UN body to oppose any council action at all.[^cite_note-DN_2006-07-17-73]

On July 19, the Bush administration rejected calls for an immediate ceasefire.[^cite_note-2006-07-19-74] Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) said that certain conditions had to be met, not specifying what they were. [John Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Bolton), U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations, rejected the call for a ceasefire, on the grounds that such an action addressed the conflict only superficially: "The notion that you just declare a ceasefire and act as if that is going to solve the problem, I think is simplistic."[^cite_note-DN_2006-07-20-75]

On July 26, foreign ministers from the U.S., Europe, and the Middle East that met in Rome vowed "to work immediately to reach with the utmost urgency a ceasefire that puts an end to the current violence and hostilities". However, the U.S. maintained strong support for the Israeli campaign, and the conference's results were reported to have fallen short of Arab and European leaders' expectations.[^cite_note-76]

### Obama administration (2009–2017)



[Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) in 2009

Israeli–U.S. relations came under increased strain during Prime Minister Netanyahu's [second administration](56472.md#Second_term) and the new [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_administration). After he took office, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) made achieving a peace deal between Israel and the Palestinians a major goal, and pressured Prime Minister Netanyahu into accepting a Palestinian state and entering negotiations. Netanyahu eventually conceded on July 14, 2009. In accordance with U.S. wishes, Israel imposed a ten-month freeze on settlement construction in the West Bank. As the freeze did not include [East Jerusalem](343508.md), which Israel regards as its sovereign territory, or 3,000 pre-approved housing units already under construction, as well as the failure to dismantle already-built [Israeli outposts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_outpost), the Palestinians rejected the freeze as inadequate, and refused to enter negotiations for nine months. Palestinian negotiators signaled a willingness to enter into negotiations weeks before the end of the construction freeze if they were to be extended, but this was rejected by the Israelis.

In 2009, Obama became the first U.S. president to authorize the sale of bunker buster bombs to Israel. The transfer was kept secret to avoid the impression that the United States was arming Israel for an attack on Iran.[^cite_note-78]

In February 2011, the Obama administration vetoed a UN resolution declaring Israeli settlements in the West Bank illegal.[^cite_note-79] In 2011, the Obama administration paved the way for the development and production of the [Iron Dome](18342427.md) missile defense system for Israel, contributing \$235 million to its funding.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

In March 2010, Israel announced that it would continue to build 1,600 new homes that were already under construction in the eastern [Jerusalem](16043.md) neighborhood of [Ramat Shlomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Shlomo), during Vice President [Joe Biden](145422.md)'s visit to Israel. The incident was described as "one of the most serious rows between the two allies in recent decades".[^cite_note-newsbbc-82] Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) said that Israel's move was "deeply negative" for US–Israeli relations.[^cite_note-83] East Jerusalem is widely considered by the international community to be occupied territory, while Israel disputes this, as it annexed the territory in 1980.[^cite_note-newsbbc-82] Obama was reported to be "livid" over the announcement.[^cite_note-fox-84]



U.S. President Barack Obama meeting Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu shortly after arriving on a visit to Israel in March 2013

Shortly afterward, President Obama instructed Secretary of State Hillary Clinton to present Netanyahu with a four-part ultimatum: that Israel cancel the approval of the housing units, freeze all Jewish construction in [East Jerusalem](343508.md), make a gesture to the Palestinians that it wants peace with a recommendation on releasing hundreds of Palestinian prisoners, and agree to discuss a partition of Jerusalem and a solution to the [Palestinian refugee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee) problem during the negotiations. Obama threatened that neither he nor any senior administration official would meet Netanyahu and his senior ministers during their upcoming visit to Washington.[^cite_note-85]

On March 26, 2010, Netanyahu and Obama met in the [White House](33057.md). The meeting was conducted without photographers or any press statements. During the meeting, Obama demanded that Israel extend the settlement freeze after its expiration, impose a freeze on Jewish construction in East Jerusalem, and withdraw troops to positions held before the start of the [Second Intifada](71717.md). Netanyahu did not give written concessions on these issues, and presented Obama with a flowchart on how permission for building is granted in the Jerusalem Municipality to reiterate that he had no prior knowledge of the plans. Obama then suggested that Netanyahu and his staff stay at the White House to consider his proposals so that he could inform Obama right away if he changed his mind, and was quoted as saying: "I'm still around, let me know if there is anything new". Netanyahu and his aides went to the [Roosevelt Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Room), spent a further half-hour with Obama, and extended his stay for a day of emergency talks to restart peace negotiations, but left without any official statement from either side.[^cite_note-fox-84][^cite_note-times-86]

In July 2010, a 2001 video of citizen Netanyahu surfaced; he was speaking to a group of bereaved families in [Ofra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofra) about relations with the United States and the peace process, and reportedly unaware he was being recorded. He said: "I know what America is; America is a thing you can move very easily, move it in the right direction. They won't get in their way." He also bragged how he undercut the peace process when he was prime minister during the Clinton administration. "They asked me before the election if I'd honor \[the [Oslo accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_accords)]," he said. "I said I would, but ... I'm going to interpret the accords in such a way that would allow me to put an end to this galloping forward to the '67 borders."[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-87][^cite_note-washingtonpost-88] While it created little stir in the press, it was heavily criticized among the Left in Israel.[^cite_note-89]



Obama called for a return to the [pre-1967 Israeli borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)) with mutually agreed land swaps.

On May 19, 2011, Obama made a foreign policy speech in which he called for a return to the [pre-1967 Israeli borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)) with mutually agreed land swaps, to which Netanyahu objected.[^cite_note-90] The [Republicans](32070.md) criticized Obama for the speech.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] The speech came a day before Obama and Netanyahu were scheduled to meet.[^cite_note-93] In an address to the [American Israel Public Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Israel_Public_Affairs_Committee) on May 22, Obama elaborated on his May 19 speech:

> It was my reference to the 1967 lines—with mutually agreed swaps—that received the lion's share of the attention, including just now. And since my position has been misrepresented several times, let me reaffirm what "1967 lines with mutually agreed swaps" means.
>
> By definition, it means that the parties themselves—Israelis and Palestinians—will negotiate a border that is different than the one that existed on 4 June 1967. That's what mutually agreed-upon swaps means. It is a well-known formula to all who have worked on this issue for a generation. It allows the parties themselves to account for the changes that have taken place over the last 44 years.
>
> &#x20;It allows the parties themselves to take account of those changes, including the new demographic realities on the ground, and the needs of both sides. The ultimate goal is two states for two people: Israel as a Jewish state and the homeland for the Jewish people and the state of Palestine as the homeland for the Palestinian people—each state in joined self-determination, mutual recognition and peace.[^cite_note-Kessler-94]

In his speech to a joint session of congress on May 24, Netanyahu adopted some of Obama's earlier language:

> Now the precise delineation of those borders must be negotiated. We'll be generous about the size of the future Palestinian state. But as President Obama said, the border will be different than the one that existed on 4 June 1967. Israel will not return to the indefensible boundaries of 1967.[^cite_note-Kessler-94]

On September 20, 2011, President Obama declared that the U.S. would veto any Palestinian application for statehood at the United Nations, asserting that "there can be no shortcut to peace".[^cite_note-95]

In October 2011, the new American defense secretary, [Leon Panetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Panetta), suggested that Israeli policies were partly responsible for its diplomatic isolation in the Middle East. The Israeli government responded that the problem was the growing radicalism in the region, rather than their own policies.[^cite_note-96]

In 2012, President Obama signed into law a bill that would extend by another three years the program of United States guarantees for Israeli government debt.[^cite_note-97]

[Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken), National Security Advisor to then-U.S. vice president [Joe Biden](145422.md), lamented in 2012 a tendency by U.S. politicians to use the debate over policy toward Israel for political purposes. Until then, Israel had been a bastion of [bipartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisanship) consensus in the U.S.[^cite_note-98]

In 2010 and again in July–August 2012, Israeli exports to the United States surpassed those to the [European Union](9317.md), usually the top destination for Israeli exports.[^cite_note-99]

Reaction in Israel was mixed to the [Geneva interim agreement on the Iranian nuclear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_interim_agreement_on_the_Iranian_nuclear_program). Prime Minister Netanyahu strongly criticized it as a "historic mistake",[^cite_note-100] and finance minister [Naftali Bennett](39754840.md) called it a "very bad deal".[^cite_note-101] However, [Kadima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadima) Party leader [Shaul Mofaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaul_Mofaz),[^cite_note-102] opposition leader [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md),[^cite_note-103] and former [Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Directorate_\(Israel\)) chief [Amos Yadlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Yadlin) voiced some measure of support for the agreement and suggested that it was more important to maintain good ties to Washington than to publicly rebuke the agreement.[^cite_note-104] According to CNN reporter, Stephen Collinson, Netanyahu's years of meddling in U.S. politics on Iran nuclear program, have alienated many Democrats, and their party's shift to the left has further eroded support for Israel.[^cite_note-cnn.com-105]



Secretary of State [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) with Prime Minister Netanyahu, at the latter's residence in Jerusalem, November 24, 2015

On April 2, 2014, U.S. ambassador to the UN [Samantha Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Power) reaffirmed the administration's stand that the U.S. opposes all unilateral Palestinian moves to statehood.[^cite_note-Riechmann-106]

During the [2014 Israel–Gaza conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Israel%E2%80%93Gaza_conflict), United States temporarily halted a supply of [Hellfire missiles](38151.md) to Israel, sparking tensions between the two countries.[^cite_note-107]

In December 2014, Congress passed the [United States–Israel Strategic Partnership Act of 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Israel_Strategic_Partnership_Act_of_2013).[^cite_note-108] This new category is one notch above the Major Non-NATO Ally classification and adds additional support for defense, energy, and strengthen cooperation business and academics.[^cite_note-109] The bill additionally calls for the U.S. to increase their [war reserve stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reserve_stock) in Israel \$1.8 billion.[^cite_note-110]

Bar Ilan's Begin-Sadat Center for Strategic Studies conducted a study in November 2014 which showed that 96% of the Israeli public feels that the country's relations with the United States are important or very important. It was also felt that Washington is a loyal ally and that America will come to Israel's aid against existential threats. On the other hand, only 37% believe that President Obama has a positive attitude towards Israel (with 24% saying that his attitude is neutral).[^cite_note-111]

In September 2016, following ten months of negotiations, the United States and Israel agreed to [a 10-year Memorandum of Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_U.S.-Israel_Memorandum_of_Understanding), providing \$38 billion in military aid to Israel across the period of FY2019-FY2028 (\$3.8 billion per year), making it the largest aid package in US history.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

On December 23, 2016, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) passed [a resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_2334) calling for an end to [Israeli settlements](15123.md); the Obama administration's UN ambassador, [Samantha Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Power), was instructed to abstain—although the U.S. had previously [vetoed](1900272.md) a comparable resolution in 2011. President-elect [Donald Trump](4848272.md) attempted to intercede by publicly advocating the resolution be vetoed and successfully persuading Egypt's [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](36703624.md) to temporarily withdraw it from consideration. The resolution was then "proposed again by [Malaysia](3607937.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Senegal](27278.md) and [Venezuela](32374.md)"—and passed 14 to 0. Netanyahu's office alleged that "the Obama administration not only failed to protect Israel against this gang-up at the UN, it colluded with it behind the scenes," adding: "Israel looks forward to working with President-elect Trump and with all our friends in Congress, Republicans and Democrats alike, to negate the harmful effects of this absurd resolution."[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

On December 28, 2016, U.S. secretary of state [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) strongly criticized Israel and its settlement policies in a speech.[^cite_note-117] Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) strongly criticized the UN Resolution[^cite_note-118] and Kerry's speech.[^cite_note-119] On January 6, 2017, the Israeli government withdrew its annual dues from the organization, which totaled \$6 million in [United States dollars](18717338.md).[^cite_note-120] On January 5, 2017, the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md) voted 342–80 to condemn the UN Resolution.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

According to Army Radio, the U.S. has reportedly pledged to sell Israel materials used to produce electricity, nuclear technology, and other supplies.[^cite_note-123]

[Christian Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Zionism) Pastor [John Hagee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hagee) with Rabbi [Shlomo Riskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Riskin) and Israel's deputy foreign minister [Tzipi Hotovely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzipi_Hotovely) in November 2018

In August 2020, Trump, Netanyahu and [Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Zayed_Al_Nahyan) jointly announced the establishment of formal [Israel–United Arab Emirates relations](26287631.md).[^cite_note-133] This was followed by [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain%E2%80%93Israel_normalization_agreement), [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Sudan_normalization_agreement) and [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Morocco_normalization_agreement) establishing relations with Israel through U.S. mediation.[^cite_note-134]

### Biden administration (2021–2025)



President Biden views [missile defense technology](1099196.md) during an official visit to Israel in July 2022

## Economic relations

The United States and Israel's economic relations are currently centered around technology, with aircraft, machinery, diamonds, optic and medical instruments and agricultural products being the main sectors of American-Israeli trade.[^cite_note-202] The United States has provided economic aid to Israel from its founding, until the maturing and success of the Israeli economy with the hightech boom in the 1990s with the U.S. providing \$30 billion until 2008 when the last batch of economic aid was provided.[^cite_note-203] Israel is the United States's 23rd largest trade partner as of 2015.[^cite_note-\:8-204] Bilateral trade volume in goods amounted to around \$35 billion in 2023[^cite_note-\:3-205] and the U.S. is Israel's most important trading partner. Israel, accounting for 2.5% of the Middle East's population, imports 20% of American exports in the region according to the [WINEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WINEP).[^cite_note-\:6-206] Israel accounts for 50% of the Middle East's investment in the United States.[^cite_note-\:7-207] As of 2013, Israel's cumulative FDI in the United States was greater than China's and India's.[^cite_note-\:8-204]

The United States signed its first free trade agreement with Israel in 1985.[^cite_note-\:8-204] The free trade agreement with Israel supports the most American jobs relative to export dollars of all free trade agreements of the United States.[^cite_note-\:8-204] There are several regional American-Israeli chambers of commerce that make it easier for Israeli and American companies to expand into each other's markets. There is [U.S.–Israel Joint Economic Development Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Israel_Joint_Economic_Development_Group), founded in 1985. In the same year, the [Israel–United States Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_Free_Trade_Agreement) was signed.

Since the 1970s, Israel has been one of the top recipients of [United States foreign aid](22557286.md). In the past, a portion was dedicated to economic assistance, but all economic aid to Israel ended in 2007 due to Israel's growing economy.[^cite_note-forbes_aid-217][^cite_note-nyt1-218] Currently, Israel receives \$3 billion annually in U.S. assistance through [Foreign Military Financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Foreign_Military_Financing) (FMF).[^cite_note-state.gov-219] Seventy-four percent of these funds must be spent on the acquisition of U.S. defense equipment, services, and training.[^cite_note-jpost.com-220] Thus, "[United States military aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_aid) to Israel is seen by many as a subsidy for U.S. industries", according to [Kenneth M. Pollack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_M._Pollack).[^cite_note-pollak2003-221]

FMF is intended to promote U.S. national security by contributing to global stability, strengthening military support for democratically elected governments and containing transnational threats, including terrorism and trafficking of weapons.[^cite_note-state.gov-219] According to the [United States Department of State](31975.md), these grants enable U.S. allies to improve their defense capabilities and foster closer military relationships between the U.S. and recipient nations. Meanwhile, [U.S. Senator](24909346.md) [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul) (R-[KY](16846.md)) has stated, in regards to U.S. foreign military financing to Israel, that "aid hampers Israel's ability to make its own decisions as it sees fit".[^cite_note-222]

In 1998, Israeli, congressional, and Administration officials agreed to reduce US\$1.2 billion in Economic Support Funds (ESF) to zero over ten years, while increasing FMF from \$1.8 billion to \$2.4 billion. Separate from the scheduled cuts, there was an extra \$200 million in anti-terror assistance, \$1.2 billion to implement the Wye agreement, and the supplemental appropriations bill assisted for another \$1 billion in FMF for the 2003 fiscal year. For the 2005 fiscal year, Israel received \$2.202 billion in FMF, \$357 million in ESF, and migration settlement assistance of \$50 million. For 2006, the Administration has requested \$240 million in ESF and \$2.28 billion in FMF. H.R. 3057, passed in the House on June 28, 2005, and in the Senate on July 20, approved these amounts. House and Senate measures also supported \$40 million for the settlement of immigrants from the former Soviet Union and plans to bring the remaining [Ethiopian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Jews) to Israel.

Obama's Fiscal Year 2010 budget proposed \$53.8 billion for appropriated international affairs' programs. From that budget, \$5.7 billion was appropriated for foreign military financing, military education, and peacekeeping operations. From that \$5.7 billion, \$2.8 billion, almost 50%, was appropriated for Israel.[^cite_note-223] Israel also has available roughly \$3 billion of conditional loan guarantees, with additional funds coming available if Israel meets conditions negotiated at the U.S.–Israel Joint Economic Development Group.

But Eli Lake, the national security correspondent of *The Washington Times*, reported on September 23, 2011, that Obama had authorized at the beginning of his presidency "significant new aid to the Israeli military that includes the sale of 55 deep-penetrating bombs known as [bunker busters](167751.md)".[^cite_note-224]

Former head of the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md), retired major general Eitan Ben Eliyahu, has called the American sale of [Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II](11812.md) nuclear capable stealth fighter bombers to Israel a key test of the relationship.[^cite_note-225]

While United States law forbids the use of [offset agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offset_agreement) on FMF sales, Israel's Industrial Cooperation Authority attempts to secure industrial participation contracts of around 35 percent of such sales.[^cite_note-226]



Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) addresses a [joint session of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_session_of_the_United_States_Congress) in Washington, D.C., July 24, 2024

In fiscal year 2013, the automatic U.S. budget sequestration process took effect as required by the [Budget Control Act of 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_Control_Act_of_2011). The process cut appropriations for certain discretionary spending, which reduced foreign military aid to Israel by \$157 million, and also reduced funding for Israeli and Israeli–U.S. missile defense programs by \$32.7 million.[^cite_note-227]

In November 2013, Steven Strauss (a faculty member at the [Harvard Kennedy School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Kennedy_School)) published an editorial calling for the United States to phase out all grant aid to Israel. Strauss argues that the United States should retain a close relationship with Israel, but that Israel is affluent enough to pay for the military equipment it needs.[^cite_note-228]

In 2023, citing "the alarming actions of the new extreme right-wing Israeli government" against Palestinians, [U.S. Representative](19468510.md) [Jamaal Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaal_Bowman) ([NY-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York's_16th_congressional_district)) and Senator [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) ([I](2172378.md)-[VT](32578.md))[^cite_note-229] released a letter signed by a dozen of congressional colleagues and supported by numerous advocacy organizations demanding the Biden administration to review the billions of dollars in largely unconditional arms support that the United States provides Israel annually.[^cite_note-230]

The Republican plan, approved by the United States House of Representatives, [allocates \$14.5 billion in military aid for Israel](65284610.md). Also, Israel has received the highest amount of military assistance from the U.S. compared to any other nation since World War II, with aid exceeding \$124 billion.[^cite_note-231]

Section 219 of the 2027 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA)—passed by the House of Representatives and awaiting Senate approval—seeks to permanently institutionalize U.S.-Israeli military technology cooperation through joint ventures, licensing agreements, and co-production partnerships on American soil, mandating integration across counter-drone systems, missile defense, quantum computing, artificial intelligence, and cyber warfare, while the Senate version (Section 1217) further requires ongoing consultation with the Israeli Minister of Defense; Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has hailed the measure as transforming Israel from a foreign aid recipient into a "full-scale partner," and future presidents would be unable to unilaterally withdraw from the arrangement, all against the backdrop of an expiring 2028 memorandum of understanding that guarantees \$3.8 billion in annual U.S. military aid. However, a coalition of 56 civil rights and advocacy organizations—including Amnesty International, the National Lawyers Guild, and Arab and Jewish groups—has urged Congress to scrap the provision, arguing that at a time of diverging strategic interests and shifting American public opinion against unconditional support, granting Israel new influence within the U.S. defense-technology ecosystem is "exceptionally dangerous," while explicitly citing Israel's military campaign in Gaza as genocide and condemning settler violence in the occupied West Bank as violations of international humanitarian law that should preclude any military cooperation; additionally, the lobbying group New Policy has warned that the approach exposes sensitive U.S. technologies to counterintelligence risks, normalizes systems developed under occupation, disadvantages American defense companies, and obscures ongoing military assistance from congressional and public transparency, though an amendment to strike the provision introduced by Representatives Thomas Massie and Ro Khanna failed to advance in the House.[^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233]

### Settlements

### Washington pressure towards peace talks with Syria

Syria has repeatedly requested that Israel re-commence peace negotiations with the Syrian government.[^cite_note-timesonline2006-244] There is an ongoing internal debate within the Israeli government regarding the seriousness of this Syrian invitation for negotiations. ### Washington brokers "peace process"

### Military sales to China

Over the years, the United States and Israel have regularly discussed Israel's sale of sensitive security equipment and technology to various countries, especially the [People's Republic of China](5405.md). U.S. administrations believe that such sales are potentially harmful to the security of U.S. forces in Asia. China has looked to Israel to obtain technology it could not acquire from elsewhere, and has purchased a wide array of military equipment and technology, including [communications satellites](45207.md), and Harpy Killer [unmanned aerial vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicles) in 1999, and which China tested over the [Taiwan Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Strait) in 2004. In 2000, the United States persuaded Israel to cancel the sale of the [Phalcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EL/M-2075). The U.S. was also said to have demanded that Israel provide information on 60 recent arms deals with China, agree to U.S. supervision of arms deals which could be seen as "sensitive" to the U.S.[^cite_note-250]

### Jerusalem

See also: [Positions on Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positions_on_Jerusalem)



[Western Wall](75225.md) in Jerusalem, nearby the Temple Mount, which is the most important Jewish religious site



The old United States consulate in Jerusalem



Netanyahu, [Jared Kushner](6191053.md) and [Ivanka Trump](1861441.md) attended the opening of the U.S. Embassy in Jerusalem

After capturing East Jerusalem in the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md), Israel annexed it and incorporated it into the Jerusalem Municipality, and has built neighborhoods as well as homes in Arab neighborhoods there, along with government offices. Israel has insisted that [Jerusalem](16043.md) is its eternal and indivisible capital. The United States does not agree with this position and believes the permanent [status of Jerusalem](4024423.md) is still subject to negotiations. According to the [WINEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WINEP), this is based on the UN's [1947 Partition plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Partition_plan_for_Palestine), which called for separate international administration of Jerusalem which was accepted at the time by most [UN countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_countries) and the [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist) leadership, but rejected by the Palestinian Arab leadership as well as Arab countries.[^cite_note-254] Most countries had located their embassies in [Tel Aviv](31453.md) before 1967; Jerusalem was also located on the contested border. The [Declaration of Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_Liberation_Organization_letters_of_recognition) and subsequent [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) signed between Israel and the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) in September 1993 similarly state that it is a subject for permanent status negotiations. U.S. administrations have consistently indicated, by keeping the [Embassy of the United States in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States_in_Israel) in Tel Aviv, that Jerusalem's status is unresolved. In 1999 both chambers of Congress recognized Jerusalem as the capital and in 2017, the United States president recognized Jerusalem as the capital of Israel and the embassy was moved to Jerusalem.[^cite_note-\:13-255]

In 1995, however, both houses of Congress overwhelmingly passed the [Jerusalem Embassy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Embassy_Act) to move the embassy to Jerusalem, no later than May 31, 1999, and suggested funding penalties on the State Department for non-compliance. [Executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch_of_the_United_States) opposition to such a move, on constitutional questions of Congressional interference in foreign policy, as well as a series of presidential waivers, based on national security interests, have delayed from the [Clinton Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bill_Clinton) until 2017.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-\:13-255]



U.S. embassy, Jerusalem

The U.S. Consulate General in Jerusalem was first established in 1844, just inside the [Jaffa Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_Gate). A permanent consular office was established in 1856 in this same building. The mission moved to [Street of the Prophets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_of_the_Prophets) in the late 19th century, and to its present location on Agron Street in 1912. The consulate general on [Nablus Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Road) in East Jerusalem was built in 1868 by the Vester family, the owners of the [American Colony Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Colony_Hotel). In 2006, the U.S. Consulate General on Agron Road leased an adjacent building, a [Lazarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarist) monastery built in the 1860s, to provide more office space.[^cite_note-257]

In March 2010, General [David Petraeus](3223944.md) was quoted by [Max Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Boot) saying the lack of progress in the Middle East peace process has "fomented [anti-Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism), undermined moderate Arab regimes, limited the strength and depth of U.S. partnerships, increased the influence of Iran, projected an image of U.S. weakness, and served as a potent recruiting tool for [Al Qaeda](1921.md)".[^cite_note-258] When questioned by journalist [Philip Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Klein_\(editor\)), Petraeus said Boot "picked apart" and "spun" his speech. He believes there are many important factors standing in the way of peace, including "a whole bunch of extremist organizations, some of which by the way deny Israel's right to exist". He continued: "There's a country that has a nuclear program who denies that the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) took place. So again we have all these factors in there. This \[Israel] is just one."[^cite_note-259] U.S.–Israel relations came under strain in March 2010, as Israel announced it was building 1,600 new homes in the eastern Jerusalem neighborhood of [Ramat Shlomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Shlomo) as Vice President [Joe Biden](145422.md) was visiting.[^cite_note-BidenClintonRemarks-260] Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) described the move as "insulting".[^cite_note-BidenClintonRemarks-260] Israel apologized for the timing of the announcement.

On December 6, 2017 [U.S. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._President) [Donald Trump](4848272.md) officially [recognized Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_recognition_of_Jerusalem) as Israel's capital and announced his intention to move the [American embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Tel_Aviv) to Jerusalem.[^cite_note-\:13-255] On January 22, 2018 [Vice President](32759.md) [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence) in an address to the Israeli [Knesset](74656.md) announced that the embassy would be moved before the end of 2019.[^cite_note-261] On October 18, 2018, [Secretary of State](32293.md) [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) announced that the [U.S. Consulate-General in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_the_United_States,_Jerusalem) would be merged into the [U.S. Embassy in Jerusalem](57417950.md). Hitherto, the consulate general had been responsible for conducting U.S. relations with the Palestinians.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263] In early March 2019, the consulate general was formally merged into the U.S. Embassy, ending the U.S. practice of assigning separate missions to the Israelis and Palestinians. The consulate general's former Agron Street site will be repurposed as the embassy's new Palestinian Affairs Unit.[^cite_note-US_Embassy_Merger-264][^cite_note-TOI_4_Mar-265][^cite_note-CNN_4_Mar_2019-266][^cite_note-JPost_4_Mar_2019-267] In October 2024, the Knesset passed a bill banning the creation of new consulates in Jerusalem, in a move interpreted as blocking the creation of consular services for Palestinians in the city.[^cite_note-268]

### Immigration

Further information: [Immigration to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Israel) and [Israeli Americans](19995589.md)

Soon after Israel's declaration of independence, President Truman offered \$135 million in loans to help Israel cope with the arrival of thousands of Holocaust refugees. Within three years of Israel's establishment, the Jewish population more than doubled on account of immigration. Mass immigration continued throughout Israeli history. Since 1989, Israel absorbed approximately one million Jews from the former Soviet Union. The United States worked with Israel to bring Jews from Arab countries, Ethiopia and the former Soviet Union to Israel, and has assisted in their absorption into Israeli society. America itself remains a major source of Jewish immigrants to Israel, while the United States is the top destination for Israelis emigrating abroad (*[yerida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerida)*) permanently or for an extended stay.

### Strategic cooperation

Further information: [Israel–United States military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_military_relations)



Israeli (left) and American (right) generals meet in Tel Aviv in 2019

The U.S. and Israel are engaged in extensive strategic, political and military cooperation. This cooperation is broad and includes American aid, intelligence sharing, and joint military exercises. American military aid to Israel comes in different forms, including grants, special project allocations, and loans.

President Obama pledged to maintain Israel's "QME" (Qualitative Military Edge) over the other countries in the region.[^cite_note-269]

#### Missile defense program

One facet of the U.S.–Israel strategic relationship is the joint development of the [Arrow Anti-Ballistic Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(Israeli_missile\)) Program, designed to intercept and destroy ballistic missiles. This development is funded by both Israel and the United States. The Arrow has also provided the U.S. with the research and experience necessary to develop additional weapons systems. So far, the development cost has been between \$2.4 and \$3.6 billion, with the United States picking up 50 percent of the final costs. The U.S. has notably provided funding for Israel's [Iron Dome](18342427.md) short-range missile defense system: from 2011 until 2022, the [United States](3434750.md) contributed a total of US\$2.6 billion to the Iron Dome defense system.[^cite_note-\:0-270][^cite_note-\:1-271]

#### Counter-terrorism



U.S. secretary of state [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) and Israeli president [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) in Tel Aviv, Israel, January 2024

In April 1996, President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) and Prime Minister [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres) signed the US–Israel Counter-terrorism Accord. At the federal, state and local levels there is close Israeli–American cooperation on [homeland security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_security). Israel was one of the first countries to cooperate with the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Homeland_Security) in developing initiatives to enhance homeland security. In this framework, there are many areas of partnership, including preparedness and protection of travel and trade. American and Israeli law enforcement officers and Homeland Security officials regularly meet in both countries to study counter-terrorism techniques and new ideas regarding intelligence gathering and threat prevention.

In December 2005, the U.S. and Israel signed an agreement to begin a joint effort to detect the smuggling of nuclear and other radioactive material by installing special equipment in Haifa, Israel's busiest seaport. This effort is part of a nonproliferation program of the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Energy)'s National Nuclear Security Administration that works with foreign partners to detect, deter, and interdict illicit trafficking in nuclear and other radioactive materials.

#### Military bases

See also: [Civil-Military Coordination Center](81409826.md)

The United States maintains six [War Reserve Stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Reserve_Stock) inside Israel, at Airwing 7 air base and maintains some \$300 million in military equipment at these sites. The equipment is owned by the United States and is for use by American forces in the Middle East, but can also be transferred to Israeli use during a time of crisis. The United States is also alleged to keep fighter and bomber aircraft at these sites, and one of the bases is thought to contain a 500-bed hospital for [U.S. Marines](17349325.md) and [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Special_Forces).[^cite_note-275] According to the American military journalist and commentator [William Arkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Arkin) in his book *Code Names*, the U.S. has prepositioned in at least six sites in Israel, munitions, vehicles, and military equipment, and even a 500-bed hospital, for use by U.S. Marines, Special Forces, and Air Force fighter and bomber aircraft in a wartime contingency in the Middle East.[^cite_note-jonathanpollard.org-276] Arkin in his book writes that some of the sites are located at [Ben Gurion Airport](113149.md), Nevatim, Ovda air base, and in [Herzliya Pituah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Pituah). The sites are numbered as "site 51," "site 53," "site 54," "site 55" and "site 56." Some of the depots are underground, others were built as open hangars. According to Arkin, site 51 holds ammunition and equipment in underground depots. Site 53 is munitions storage and war reserve vehicles at [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) bases, site 54 is an emergency military hospital near [Tel Aviv](31453.md) with 500 beds, and sites 55 and 56 are ammunition depots.[^cite_note-277] However, Israel is not the only country in the Middle East to host U.S. military bases; there are American facilities in Turkey, Egypt, Jordan, Saudi Arabia (mostly withdrawn from in 2003), Oman, and the Persian Gulf states of Kuwait, Bahrain (headquarters of the [United States Fifth Fleet](418867.md)), Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates. The Bahrain headquarters of the United States Fifth Fleet is meant to act as a watchdog and deterrent to potential Iranian aggression in the Persian Gulf region.[^cite_note-jonathanpollard.org-276]

The Israeli Mediterranean [Port of Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Haifa) hosts regular visits by navy vessels of the [United States Sixth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Sixth_Fleet), which is headquartered in [Naples](55880.md), Italy.[^cite_note-278]

The [Dimona Radar Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimona_Radar_Facility) is an American radar facility in the [Negev](142273.md) desert of southern Israel, located near [Dimona](9103.md).[^cite_note-279] The facility has two 120-metre (400-foot) radar towers designed to track ballistic missiles through space and provide ground-based missiles with the targeting data needed to intercept them. It can detect missiles up to 2,400 kilometres (1,500 miles) away. The facility is owned and operated by the U.S. military, and provides only second-hand intelligence to Israel. The towers of the facility are the tallest radar towers in the world, and the tallest towers in Israel.

#### Intelligence relations

The United States and Israel have [cooperated on intelligence](46556073.md) matters since the 1950s. Israel was behind the disclosure of [Nikita Khrushchev's secret speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Cult_of_Personality_and_Its_Consequences) denouncing Stalin in 1956.[^cite_note-280] Throughout the [Cold War](325329.md), Israel provided the U.S. with information on [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-built weapons systems captured from the Arabs. Israel also provides the U.S. with much of its Middle Eastern [human intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence_\(espionage\)). The CIA became more reliant on [Israeli intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Intelligence_Community) following the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) and the [1983 Beirut barracks bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Beirut_barracks_bombing).[^cite_note-281] Meanwhile, the U.S. provided Israel with satellite imagery, and in the early 1980s, the CIA reportedly began giving Israel intelligence that it denied its closest [NATO](21133.md) allies. In particular, Israel received almost unlimited access to intelligence from the [KH-11 Kennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_Kennan) military satellite, though Israeli access was more restricted following [Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera).

The National Security Agency has acknowledged that it provides to Israel raw unfiltered information intercepts that include private details and messages of American citizens.[^cite_note-282]

##### American espionage against Israel

Despite intense intelligence cooperation, both countries have been heavily engaged in [espionage](10501.md) operations against one another. The United States has mainly tried to penetrate Israel's political, military and intelligence circles and gather information on Israel's alleged nuclear and non-conventional capabilities, while Israel has also penetrated the U.S. government, and has engaged in [industrial espionage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_espionage) in the United States in an attempt to boost its military and alleged nuclear capabilities.[^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285]

In 1996, two espionage scandals broke. It was revealed that the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) wiretapped the phone lines to Israel's embassy in [Washington](108956.md) and broke the Israeli security code, exposing Israel's deepest policy secrets to the United States. The wiretapping was discovered following the widely publicized "Mega Scandal", when a phone call intercepted by the NSA became public. Due to Israel's expertise in computers and electronics and the sophistication of its electronic code system, it was widely believed that the NSA used an Israeli mole to obtain the security code. The resulting "Mega Scandal" was the allegation that Israeli intelligence had a highly placed mole within the U.S. government.[^cite_note-286]

On November 10, 2004, a U.S. submarine entered Israeli territorial waters eighteen kilometers off the coast of [Haifa](55606.md). The submarine's mission was never revealed. It was thought to have been trying to gather intelligence on the city's naval base and headquarters and other vital infrastructure, and was also suspected of intending to intercept Israeli naval electronic signals and test Israel's response to an intrusion. It also may have been trying to install sensors near Israeli naval headquarters and other vital installations. Minutes after it entered Israeli waters, the submarine was detected and tracked by the [Israeli Navy](1370788.md). The submarine was initially identified as belonging to a [NATO](21133.md) power, and later confirmed to be American. The Israeli General Staff refrained from ordering an attack on what was considered the asset of a friendly nation. After several hours, the submarine submerged and fled, presumably determining that it was under surveillance. The Israeli Navy then sent fast patrol craft, missile boats, and helicopters in pursuit. The submarine was not found, but military sources maintained that the submarine had failed to complete its mission.[^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288] According to Israeli officials, such spy missions were common, and Western spy submarines had been intercepted by Israel before.[^cite_note-289]

In December 2013, documents released by whistle-blower [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) revealed that in January 2009 the NSA and its British counterpart [GCHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCHQ) had spied on an email address belonging to Israeli prime minister [Ehud Olmert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Olmert), and had monitored email traffic between then-Israeli defense minister [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) and his chief of staff, Yoni Koren.[^cite_note-290]

##### Israeli espionage against the United States

Main article: [Israeli espionage in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_espionage_in_the_United_States)

Shortly after [Rafael Eitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Eitan) visited [Inslaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inslaw) in February 1983, the software PROMIS was allegedly stolen and copied by the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md), which triggered years of litigation.[^cite_note-291] Earl W. Brian and [Edwin Meese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Meese), two men with close ties to [Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan), are said to have sold or given the software to over 80 countries, with a “back door” built into the program, which allowed for espionage.[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-293] Two versions of the software are said to have been sold: an American one for the CIA and an Israeli one for the Mossad. The software with the built-in back door is said to have reached the [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) via the British publisher [Robert Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Maxwell), a Mossad agent. Maxwell was also able to sell the bugged Israeli version of PROMIS to [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories) and [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory), two of the most important nuclear research and national security facilities in the United States. He is said to have been assisted by [John Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tower).[^cite_note-294]

[Jonathan Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Pollard), a civilian analyst working for U.S. naval intelligence, was arrested in 1985 and charged with conveying highly classified documents to Israeli agents after shopping it around to a host of other countries. He pleaded guilty to one count of conspiracy to deliver national defense information to a foreign government, and was sentenced to life imprisonment. Israel later granted him citizenship, and he was released in 2015.

According to journalist [Daniel Halper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Halper), Israel blackmailed US President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) in 1998 with the release of incriminating conversations with [Monica Lewinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Lewinsky) in order to secure the release of Pollard. Israel is said to have obtained the Lewinsky recordings by monitoring White House phone lines.[^cite_note-295]

"The Israelis are pretty aggressive" when it comes to espionage, including against the United States. "They're all about protecting the security of the Israeli state and they do whatever they feel they have to achieve that objective," according to a former senior U.S. intelligence official.[^cite_note-politico.com-296] In May 2014, a U.S. National Security Agency (NSA) document leaked by [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) and published by journalist [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald) revealed the [CIA](5183633.md) was concerned that Israel had set up an extensive spying network in the United States. Defense secretaries from both countries denied the claim with [Chuck Hagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Hagel) saying he had no facts to substantiate the report, while [Moshe Ya'alon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Ya'alon) said he was never allowed to spy on the United States while he was head of the Israeli intelligence services, "and as defense minister I don't allow spying on the United States whatsoever."[^cite_note-IsraelSpying-297]

After a careful study over a two-year period ending in September 2019, the United States intelligence community and FBI concluded that it "was pretty clear that the Israelis were responsible" for cellphone surveillance devices near the White House and other sensitive [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)-area locations, according to several former senior U.S. officials.[^cite_note-298][^cite_note-politico.com-296] The Israeli agents placed [Stingray phone trackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingray_phone_tracker), which are miniature surveillance devices that act like ordinary [cellphone towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_site), fooling cellphones in the area into providing their identity and location data and capturing the contents of telephone calls and other data. The devices were configured to gather information on the American president and his top aides (including [Rudy Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Giuliani)), an operation made easier by President Trump's failure to observe White House telecommunications security protocols. Publicly unknown is whether or to what extent Israel succeeded in gathering such information. In response, Israeli officials categorically strongly denied the allegation that Israel conducts espionage against the United States. President Trump stated he finds the story "hard to believe." However, U.S. officials with extensive intelligence experience derided the perfunctory Israeli denials.[^cite_note-politico.com-296]

The U.S. journalists [Dylan Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Howard), Melissa Cronin and James Robertson linked the Mossad to American sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) in their book *Epstein: Dead Men Tell No Tales*. They relied for the most part on the former Israeli intelligence officer [Ari Ben-Menashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari_Ben-Menashe).[^cite_note-299] According to him, Epstein's activities as a spy served to gather compromising material on powerful people in order to [blackmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackmail) them.[^cite_note-300] Epstein's victim [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre) also alleged Epstein to be an intelligence asset, linking on [Twitter](9988187.md) to a [Reddit](3829005.md) page, that alleged Epstein being a spy, running a blackmail operation.[^cite_note-301]

#### Visa Waiver Program

Israel applied to join the U.S. government's [Visa Waiver Program](421825.md) in 2005. Under this program, citizens of selected countries can enter the United States for up to 90 days for tourism and business purposes without having to apply for an entry visa. The [House of Representatives](19468510.md) approved the bid, but the [Senate](24909346.md) rejected it. Israel failed to fulfill two basic requirements; not all citizens owning a [biometric passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passport), and the entry visa rejection rate for Israelis exceeded 3%. In addition, the United States insisted that [Palestinian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Americans) entering Israel not be subjected to any more security checks than other U.S. citizens.[^cite_note-305] In January 2013, a new bill was submitted to the House calling for Israel's inclusion, with its supporters saying Israel now meets the program's current criteria.[^cite_note-306] As of 2014, Israel regularly bars the entry of American citizens.[^cite_note-307]

See also: [Palestinian freedom of movement § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_freedom_of_movement#United_States)

In 2023, Israel joined the Visa Waiver Program.[^cite_note-308]

### In the United States

#### Since 1948



[Kippah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kippah) with Israeli and American flags

From the creation of Israel in 1948 until the 2020s, American public opinion had consistently favoured Israel over the Palestinians.[^cite_note-\:17-326][^cite_note-\:18-327] Support for Israel has been decreasing since around 2017, driven mostly by a decline among Democrats and Independents, with Republican support consistently higher, though favorability among Republicans has also been trending downwards.[^cite_note-\:18-327] In the 2020s, unfavorable views of Israel among Democrats, Independents and Republicans all reached record highs.[^cite_note-\:9-328] Support has been higher at times when Israel has been under threat, and lower when Israel is perceived as the aggressor, such as after the [1982 Israeli invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War).[^cite_note-\:17-326] Support for Israel among the American public is distinctly higher than in other nations, including countries in Europe and Asia.[^cite_note-\:17-326] According to the Washington Post, about 23,380 American citizens serve in the Israeli armed forces.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-243]

Views on the United States in Israel

[^cite_note-pewusfavor-330]

As of July 2006, a poll stated that 44% of Americans thought that the "United States supports Israel about the right amount", 11% thought "too little", and 38% thought "too much". The same poll asked "In general, do you favor or oppose the establishment of a Palestinian state that is recognized by the United Nations?" with 42% responding in the affirmative with 34% opposed.[^cite_note-331][^cite_note-332][^cite_note-333] Many in the United States question the levels of aid and general commitment to Israel, and argue that a U.S. bias operates at the expense of improved relations with various [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) states. Others maintain that democratic Israel is a helpful and strategic ally, and believe that U.S. relations with Israel strengthen the U.S. presence in the Middle East.[^cite_note-334] A 2002–2006 [Gallup Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_Poll) of Americans by party affiliation (Republican/Democratic) and ideology (conservative/moderate/liberal) found that, although sympathy for Israel is strongest amongst the right (conservative Republicans), the group most on the left (liberal Democrats) also have a greater percentage sympathizing with Israel. Although proportions are different, each group has most sympathizing more with Israel, followed by both/neither, and lastly more with the Palestinians.[^cite_note-335] These findings support the view that support for Israel in the U.S. is [bipartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisanship). A 2007 Gallup World Affairs poll included the annual update on Americans' ratings of various countries around the world, and asked Americans to rate the overall importance to the United States of what happens in most of these nations, according to that poll, Israel was the only country that a majority of Americans felt both favorably toward (63%) and said that what happens there is vitally important to the United States (55%).[^cite_note-336]

#### 2010s

A 2013 Gallup poll finds 64% of Americans sympathize with Israelis and 12% with Palestinians. Analysis of the poll data showed that Republicans, conservatives and older Americans were more likely to be partial to Israel. Republicans (78%) were much more likely to sympathize with Israel than Democrats (55%). Democratic support for Israel has increased by four percent since 2001, while Republican support for the Jewish state has jumped 18 percentage points in the same period. The percentage of respondents favoring the Palestinians increases with formal education, ranging from 8% of those with no college experience to 20% of postgraduates. According to Gallup, Palestinians receive the highest sympathy from Democrats, liberals, and postgraduates, but even among these, support tops off at 24%.[^cite_note-337][^cite_note-338] According to a 2013 [BBC](19344654.md) World Service Poll, the United States is the only Western country surveyed holding favorable views of Israel, and the only country in the survey with a majority of positive ratings, with 51% of Americans viewing Israel's influence positively and 32% expressing a negative view.[^cite_note-339]



Map showing U.S. states where [anti-BDS legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-BDS_laws) has passed, is pending, or has failed as of November 2023

A 2012 report from [The David Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_David_Project), an Israel advocacy organization in the U.S., found that the strongest anti-Israel behaviour in America is found in universities. Quoting the experience of Jewish students who felt largely comfortable in American universities, the report denied that anti-Israeli feelings were based on [antisemitism](1078.md), as commonly believed. Instead the problem was said to lie in a "drip-drip negativity" about Israel that threatened to erode support over the long term, and might eventually spread from campuses to the population at large.[^cite_note-340] Amongst ethnic groups, the [Hispanic and Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) population is believed to be the most hostile towards Israel, according to the [Israel Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Project) (TIP), a U.S. nonprofit organization active in Israel advocacy. According to TIP, Israel is more popular among older Americans, [Republicans](32070.md), conservatives and Evangelicals and less popular among "liberal elites", [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) and [Democrats](5043544.md).[^cite_note-341]

Mark Heller, the lead research associate at Tel Aviv's [Institute for National Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_National_Security_Studies_\(Israel\)) believes that the American public opinion has shifted over time against Israel and predicts that the relations between the country with the U.S. will deteriorate in the future. To compensate for this loss, he suggests that Israel should strengthen its ties with key Asian countries instead, because, in his view, the major Asian countries "don't seem to indicate much interest about how Israel gets along with the Palestinians, Arabs, or anyone else." He believes that countries like China, India and Singapore would be less committed to the types of liberal and humane concerns that occasionally affect Western policy and are less inclined to protest Israel's settlement construction and its policies towards Palestinians.[^cite_note-342]

In 2012 tensions emerged between the [Emergency Committee for Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Committee_for_Israel) and other Jewish charities it argued are hostile to Israel.[^cite_note-343] According to Paul Berger, The group's advertisements against Jewish charities it accuses of supporting anti-Israel organizations seemed unsuccessful. Several people quoted in the Emergency Committee for Israel's [New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times) advertisement immediately distanced themselves from the publicity campaign. The Jewish groups the ads targeted reported little change in donor support.[^cite_note-344]

A 2015 [Bloomberg Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Politics) poll of Americans asked "When it comes to relations between the U.S. and Israel, which of the following do you agree with more?" 47% of respondents chose "Israel is an ally but we should pursue America's interests when we disagree with them." 45% of respondents chose "Israel is an important ally, the only democracy in the region, and we should support it even if our interests diverge." 8% were unsure.[^cite_note-BloombergPoliticsPollApr2015-345]

#### 2020s



[Gaza Solidarity Encampment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Solidarity_Encampment) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), March 2024.

According to a 2023 *[Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution)* report, released prior to the [Gaza war](74998519.md), American popular support for Israel has declined in recent years. A March 27 – April 5, 2023 [Ipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipsos)/[University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) poll found that Americans most commonly describe Israel as either "a [flawed democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiberal_democracy)" or "a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity) with [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) similar to [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid)."[^cite_note-\:2-346]

&#x20;In November 2023, a [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup,_Inc.) poll found that 50% of respondents in the United States supported Israel's [actions in Gaza](74998519.md), while 45% disapproved.[^cite_note-347] The [Marist Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marist_Poll) published on November 15 found that American respondents were more likely to sympathize with Israelis (61%) than Palestinians (30%).[^cite_note-348] According to the poll conducted by the Harvard CAPS–[Harris Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Harris_Poll) on January 17–18, 2024, 67% of American respondents said that a ceasefire in Gaza should only happen after all Israeli hostages are released and Hamas is removed from power. 74% of American respondents believed that the Hamas attack was [genocidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegations_of_genocide_in_the_October_7_Hamas-led_attack_on_Israel), while 34% of respondents believed that Israel was committing [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_genocide_accusation).[^cite_note-349]



Pro-Israel rally in [Stanford, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California), October 10, 2023



[Assyrian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Americans) in the annual [Assyrian New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kha_b-Nisan) march in [Chicago](6886.md), where they express support for Israel (April 2025)

According to a November 2023 poll from The Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research, Americans are divided over whether Israel has gone too far in its response to last month's attack.[^cite_note-350]

American public opinion has grown more negative towards Israel since the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) and the subsequent [Israeli invasion of Gaza](74998519.md).[^cite_note-\:16-351][^cite_note-\:18-327]

In a May 2024 survey by the [Jerusalem Center for Security and Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Center_for_Security_and_Foreign_Affairs), 30% of Jewish Americans agreed that Israel has committed [genocide in Gaza](75668427.md) (including 38% under the age of 44, and 22% over), while 51% disagreed and 19% neither agreed nor disagreed. 34% characterised the [2024 pro-Palestinian protests on university campuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_pro-Palestinian_protests_on_university_campuses) as "anti-war, pro-peace", 28% as "anti-Israel", 25% as both, and 12% as neither. 52% agreed with Biden's stated position of withholding arms shipments if Israel [invaded Rafah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafah_offensive), 22% disagreed and 25% neither agreed nor disagreed.[^cite_note-352]

In a March 2025 survey by Pew Research Center, 53% of Americans held a negative opinion of Israel, increasing from 42% in March 2022.[^cite_note-\:16-351] Negative opinion among Republicans increased from 27% to 37% over this period, while among Democrats increased from 53% to 69%. Older voters in both parties were less likely to view Israel negatively: just 23% of Republicans did aged 50 and over, versus 50% aged 18–50, and among Democrats 66% and 71%, respectively. White evangelical Christians held a positive view of Israel by 72% versus 26%, significantly more than white non-evangelical Protestants (47% versus 50%) and Catholics (47% versus 53%). By one percentage point, Jewish Americans are the most likely to view Israel positively (73% versus 27%), and Muslim Americans the most negatively (at 81% versus 19%). The religiously unaffiliated view Israel negatively by 69% to 28%.

Peaceful coexistence between Israel and an independent Palestine is seen as possible by more Democrats (56%) than Republicans (46%), with younger voters in both parties more likely to say so, and by more Muslim Americans (56%) than Jewish Americans (47%), according to an April 2025 [Pew Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research) poll.[^cite_note-\:16-351]

In the March 2025 Pew survey, a majority of Americans expressed low confidence in Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) "to do the right thing regarding world affairs", at 52% versus 32%, and Republicans over 50 years were the only political grouping with a positive view (at 70% versus 24%), with little or no confidence expressed by Republicans under 50 (45% versus 32%) and Democrats of all ages (70% versus 15%). Whereas Jewish Americans had the most positive view of Israel, 53% versus 45% expressed low confidence in Netanyahu. White evangelical Christians were the only religious grouping with a positive view (58% versus 28%).[^cite_note-\:16-351]

A [Quinnipiac University poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinnipiac_University_Polling_Institute) in August 2025 found that 60 percent of respondents oppose more military support for Israel, the highest number since the October 7 attacks.[^cite_note-353]

A February 2026 [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup,_Inc.) poll found that 41% of Americans say they sympathize more with the Palestinians in the Middle East situation, while 36% sympathize more with the Israelis. A similar poll conduct the year before (February 2025), showed that 46% sympathized more with the Israelis and 33% with the Palestinians. Gallup stated: "For the first time in Gallup’s annual measurement since 2001, Americans’ sympathies no longer lie more with the Israelis than the Palestinians." Additional findings include: 46% of Americans viewing Israel favorably and 37% viewing the Palestinian Territories favorably (a new high for the latter), and 57% of Americans favor an independent Palestinian state.[^cite_note-gallup-354][^cite_note-355]

In March 2026, a [Manhattan Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Institute_for_Policy_Research) survey about Democratic voters in the United States found that attitudes vary across age groups, political activists, and general voters. According to the survey, while most Democrats continue to support the U.S.–Israel relationship overall, many of them expressed mixed opinions on Israeli government policies.[^cite_note-356][^cite_note-357]

According to a 2026 Gallup poll, 46% of Americans have favorable opinions of Israel, while 48% have an unfavorable opinion.[^cite_note-358]



[Pro-Palestine protesters](75347645.md) in [Times Square](31389.md) in front of a state sponsored pro-Israel ad on the day of Netanyahu's speech at the [81st UNGA meeting](83334052.md), September 2026

According to a March 2026 survey by Pew Research Center, 60% of Americans had an unfavorable opinion of Israel, while 37% had a favorable opinion. The survey also found that 80% of Democrats and 70% of Americans aged 18-49 had an unfavorable view of Israel. 44% of Republicans had a negative opinion of Israel, while 45% had a positive opinion; in addition, 58% of Republicans aged 18-49 had an unfavorable opinion of Israel. 59% of Americans did not have confidence in Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, while 27% of Americans did.[^cite_note-\:9-328]

According to a 2026 [Harvard CAPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_CAPS)/Harris Poll, differences between age groups were found in views of the [Israel–Hamas war](74998519.md). Younger respondents were less likely compared with older age groups to express support for Israel, with respondents aged 18–24 showing lower levels of support for Israel when asked if they supported Israel or [Hamas](13913.md) in the conflict.[^cite_note-359][^cite_note-360]

### In Israel

Israeli attitudes toward the U.S. are largely positive. In several ways of measuring a country's view of America (American ideas about democracy; ways of doing business; music, movies, and television; science and technology; and spread of U.S. ideas), Israel came on top as the [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) who viewed it most positively.[^cite_note-361]

In December 2014, a public opinion poll of Israelis showed a majority of Israelis believe Israel's relationship with the U.S. is "in crisis". The survey found that 61.7 percent of respondents said there was a crisis in U.S.–Israel relations. Less than one quarter of respondents said the relations were "stable and good." A majority of people polled said that Netanyahu's government had "harmed" the relationship.[^cite_note-362] Despite the positive attitudes towards the U.S., the poll found that Israelis are generally mistrustful of the U.S. president, with only 37 percent of respondents calling Obama's views of Israel "positive," while 61% characterized his attitude towards Israel as "negative" or "neutral."[^cite_note-363] According to a 2026 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) survey, 81% of Israelis had a favorable view of the United States, while 19% had a negative view.[^cite_note-\:62-364]
