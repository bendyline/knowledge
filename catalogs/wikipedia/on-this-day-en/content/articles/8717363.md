# Ibrahim Iskandar of Johor

In this [Malay name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_names#Malay_names), there is no [surname or family name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname). The name *Iskandar* is a [patronymic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronymic), and the person should be referred to by their [given name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_name), *Ibrahim*. The word "ibni" means 'son of'.

**Ibrahim ibni Iskandar**[^cite_note-1] (born **Tunku Ibrahim Ismail ibni Tunku Mahmood Iskandar**, 22 November 1958) is [King of Malaysia](376412.md) and [Sultan of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Johor).

Ibrahim was born during the reign of his great-grandfather, [Sultan Sir Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_of_Johor), and became heir apparent when his father, [Sultan Iskandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskandar_of_Johor), acceded to the throne of [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor) in 1981. He was named Crown Prince of Johor on 3 July 1981. He was educated at [Trinity Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Grammar_School_\(New_South_Wales\)), in [Sydney](27862.md), Australia. In 1982, he married [Raja Zarith Sofiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Zarith_Sofiah). They had six children, including [Ismail Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Ismail_Idris) and [Aminah Maimunah Iskandariah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Tun_Aminah_Maimunah_Iskandariah).

Ibrahim became [Sultan of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Johor) upon his father's death in 2010. On 31 January 2024, Ibrahim was sworn in as the King of Malaysia,[^cite_note-swearing-in-2] having been elected to a five-year term on 27 October 2023.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-cna_yda-4] Significant events in his reign have included [his installation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Installation_of_Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong_XVII) in July 2024.[^cite_note-5]

## Early life and education

Tunku Ibrahim was born on 22 November 1958 at the [Sultanah Aminah Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanah_Aminah_Hospital), [Johor Bahru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Bahru), during the reign of his great-grandfather, Sultan [Ibrahim of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_of_Johor). He was the third child and eldest son of Sultan [Iskandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskandar_of_Johor) by his first wife, [Josephine Ruby Trevorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephine_Ruby_Trevorrow), an English woman from [Torquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquay).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Trevorrow temporarily took on the name *Khalsom binti Abdullah* following her marriage to Iskandar. Ibrahim's mother later remarried and returned to England.[^cite_note-Morais(1967)-10] Ibrahim's great-grandfather died in London on 8 May 1959. Thus, Ibrahim's grandfather, [Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_of_Johor), became [Sultan of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Johor).

Ibrahim completed his secondary education at [Trinity Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Grammar_School_\(New_South_Wales\)) in [Sydney](27862.md), [Australia](4689264.md) from 1968 until 1970. After finishing high school, he was sent to the Army Training Centre (PULADA) in [Kota Tinggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Tinggi) for basic military training. He also received military training in the United States, at [Fort Benning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benning), [Georgia](48830.md), and later at [Fort Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bragg), [North Carolina](21650.md).[^cite_note-Info1989-11]

## Crown Prince of Johor (1981–2010)

Ibrahim was appointed as the Crown Prince of Johor on 3 July 1981,[^cite_note-Info1989-11] and has primarily resided at the [Istana Pasir Pelangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istana_Pasir_Pelangi) since then.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] He served as the regent of Johor between 26 April 1984 and 25 April 1989 when his father served his term as the [Yang di-Pertuan Agong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong).[^cite_note-Info1989-11] In recent years, Ibrahim gradually took over some state duties and functions from his aging father.[^cite_note-Thanam_Visvanathan-14] These included attending the 211th Conference of Rulers, where Ibrahim and [Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_of_Pahang), the Crown Prince of Pahang, represented their fathers in the meetings,[^cite_note-15] among other state functions.[^cite_note-16]

Shortly before Filipino opposition politician [Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino) was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ninoy_Aquino) at [Manila International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_International_Airport) on 21 August 1983, Ibrahim met Aquino upon his arrival in Singapore and later brought him to meet other Malaysian leaders across the [Johor–Singapore Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor%E2%80%93Singapore_Causeway).[^cite_note-17] Once in Johor, Aquino also met with Ibrahim's father, Iskandar, who was a close friend.[^cite_note-18]

## Sultan of Johor (2010–present)

A few hours before his father's death on 22 January 2010, Tunku Ibrahim was appointed as the [Regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent) of Johor, following medical reports that suggested Sultan Iskandar's impending death.[^cite_note-19] Sultan Iskandar died on the same night, and Tunku Ibrahim was installed as the Sultan of Johor the following morning.[^cite_note-20] The Menteri Besar (Chief Minister) of Johor, [Abdul Ghani Othman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Ghani_Othman) cited that Sultan Ibrahim and immediate members of the royal family would undertake a mourning period of 40 days.[^cite_note-21] During the mourning season, Sultan Ibrahim made his inaugural presence at the [Conference of Rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_of_Rulers) in February 2010 as the Sultan of Johor.[^cite_note-22]

On 30 June 2011, Sultan Ibrahim drove the last train from [Tanjong Pagar railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjong_Pagar_railway_station), having received tuition from Chief Locomotive Inspector Hasnol Azahari Aman of [Keretapi Tanah Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keretapi_Tanah_Melayu) to enable him to do this. He stated that he wished to do this as his grandfather, Sultan Ismail, had opened the causeway between Singapore and Malaya in 1923, and that it was appropriate for him to drive the last train out of the station.[^cite_note-ST010711-23]

Sultan Ibrahim also declared [Muar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muar_\(town\)) as Johor's new royal capital, replacing [Johor Bahru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Bahru), on 5 February 2012. This coincided with the [Mawlid](1285650.md) celebrations.[^cite_note-24]

Sultan Ibrahim became the first ruler of [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor) to celebrate his birthday celebration in Muar on 22 November 2012. He picked the town because it was "rich in history and tradition besides being peaceful, beautiful and progressive". He wanted the state government to gazette all the old buildings in the town as a state heritage. He also wants the local authorities to preserve the cleanliness of the [Muar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muar_River) which could be done by relocating the bus and taxi terminals elsewhere.[^cite_note-25]

Johor, as with the former [Unfederated Malay States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfederated_Malay_States) of [Perlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlis), Kedah, [Kelantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelantan) and [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu), used to rest on Fridays and Saturdays, in contrast with the former [Federated Malay States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_Malay_States) as well as [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca), Penang, [Sabah](28678.md) and [Sarawak](28258.md) and the [Federal Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Territories_\(Malaysia\)) which observe their weekends on Saturdays and Sundays. Johor, together with Perlis, moved their weekend to Saturday and Sunday on 1 January 1994 to synchronise with neighboring Singapore as well as with [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md), as Johor by then was a quickly industrialising state.

However, on 22 November 2013, on Sultan Ibrahim's birthday celebrations, he decreed that Johor would move its weekend back to Friday and Saturday, to enable [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) in the state to attend their Friday prayers, effective 1 January 2014. This move was not without controversy due to the disruptions it caused, particularly for private businesses dealing with Singapore. The private sector nevertheless continues to rest on Saturdays and Sundays.[^cite_note-26]



Monument in [Mersing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersing) commemorating Ibrahim's coronation as Sultan of Johor

Sultan Ibrahim was crowned as the Sultan of [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor) on 23 March 2015.[^cite_note-27] The last coronation was that of his grandfather Sultan [Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_of_Johor) on 10 February 1960.[^cite_note-28] From 2015 onwards, 23 March was made an annual state public holiday as the Sultan's official birthday, replacing the public holiday on 22 November, the Sultan's actual birthday.[^cite_note-29]

In August 2015, Sultan Ibrahim decreed that the name of [Kulaijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulaijaya) district be reverted to its former name of Kulai.[^cite_note-thestar.com.my-30] He also decreed [Nusajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusajaya) to be renamed to Iskandar Puteri, as well as renaming [Ledang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledang_District) to Tangkak District.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Sultan Ibrahim issued a decree banning [vaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaping) in Johor effective 1 January 2016. The purposes of the ban was for the sake of the health of the residents of Johor. [Kelantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelantan) and [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu) followed soon after.[^cite_note-34]

Ibrahim is noted for advocating religious moderation and has criticised the creeping [Arabisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabisation) of Malaysian Muslim culture.[^cite_note-35] Despite being the only sultan in Malaysia without tertiary (post-secondary) education, he has promoted quality education for Johor.

## King of Malaysia (2024–present)



Sultan Ibrahim with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), 6 August 2025.

On 27 October 2023, the [Conference of Rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_of_Rulers) elected Sultan Ibrahim as the 17th [Yang di-Pertuan Agong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong) (King of Malaysia), replacing Sultan [Abdullah of Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_of_Pahang).[^cite_note-cna_yda-4][^cite_note-36] During this time, the Conference of Rulers also elected Sultan [Nazrin Shah of Perak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazrin_Shah_of_Perak) to a third term as the [Deputy Yang di-Pertuan Agong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong).[^cite_note-37]

His official swearing in and possession of the Istana Negara occurred on 31 January 2024.[^cite_note-swearing-in-2]

The [Ceremonial Installation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Installation_of_Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong_XVII) of Sultan Ibrahim as the 17th Yang di-Pertuan Agong took place at the Throne Hall of the Istana Negara on 20 July 2024, a few months after his assumption of the throne.[^cite_note-38] The installation featured traditional Malay rites, ceremonial cannon fire, and was attended by various national dignitaries.[^cite_note-39]

## Controversies and issues

### Allegations of criminal misconduct

Occasional reports of alleged criminal misconduct from the 1980s onwards marred Tunku Ibrahim's reputation somewhat, albeit to a much lesser extent than his father, Sultan Iskandar, whose past crimes had received considerable attention from mainstream media.[^cite_note-40] In the 1980s, he was convicted of shooting a man dead in a nightclub during a feud, but was quickly pardoned.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

In late 1992 to early 1993, Tunku Ibrahim also experienced fallout from the [Gomez Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Iskandar_of_Johor#Gomez_Incident)– in which his father and younger brother, [Tunku Abdul Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Abdul_Majid) were accused of two separate but related incidences of assault which provoked nationwide moral outrage and ultimately resulted in constitutional amendments to allow members of royalty to be prosecuted for criminal wrongdoing. During that period of time, the press, which was supported by the Malaysian government, launched a series of press reports on the history of alleged incidents of royal wrongdoings,[^cite_note-43] of which parliamentarians highlighted that Tunku Ibrahim had been accused in at least two cases of assault in the 1980s.[^cite_note-Mahkota_assault-44] This included Rahim Mohd Nor, who was allegedly assaulted by Tunku Ibrahim and who described his assault experience as an act of sadism by Tunku Ibrahim.[^cite_note-45]

In March 2005, a member of the Malaysian royalty allegedly assaulted a young woman by the name of Yasmin whom he accused of two-timing him with another policeman.[^cite_note-46] The victim's father, Mohd Yasin, later lodged a police report which alleged that the assault culprit was Tunku Ibrahim, the then Tunku Mahkota of Johor.[^cite_note-47]

### Other incidents

In October 2005, a brawl occurred on [Pulau Rawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulau_Rawa) after a Johor prince allegedly gatecrashed a wedding party. The prince ordered some guests off the island after a fight broke out when a woman refused to dance with one of the gatecrashers. The locals, who felt offended by the woman's attitude, went off but soon returned with golf clubs and weapons and started a fight. In the process, several people were injured and sent to hospital, while five others were arrested, including a 20-year-old prince from the Johor royal family. The names of the culprits were not released by the police, who chose to retain the confidentiality of the attackers.[^cite_note-48] The Tunku Mahkota issued a press statement to urge the culprits to apologise to the affected guests.[^cite_note-49]

The following June, press reports leaked that Tunku Ibrahim had chalked RM26,700 worth of unpaid traffic fines since 2000, causing much embarrassment to the crown prince.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] A later report quoted him settling all the past fines with the traffic police.[^cite_note-52]

Tunku Ibrahim also spent lavishly on an unusual car plate numbers. For example, in May 2012, he spent RM520,000 in a successful bid for the car number plate WWW 1[^cite_note-53] for his red orange [Satria Neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satria_Neo).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] In January 2014, he spent a record breaking RM748,000 on the car plate number W1N for[^cite_note-56] his blue [Suprima S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Suprima_S).[^cite_note-57] In July 2016, he also spent a record breaking RM836,660 on the car plate number F1 for his ruby red [Proton Perdana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Perdana).[^cite_note-58] He continued to break records by securing the FF1 number plate for RM1.2 million and GOLD1 number plate for RM1.5 million.[^cite_note-59]

He asserted that it is legitimate for royalty to be involved in business and that he has been transparent about his ventures, stating, "I must earn a living, like ordinary Malaysians." The palace emphasized that the Johor sultans have historically donated their entire state government allocations to benefit the people of Johor.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

### Sports

Sultan Ibrahim has keen interest in sports, especially polo, which has led him to participate in various tournaments up to the international level. He is also interested in tennis, water surfing, shooting, driving sports cars, and parachuting.[^cite_note-73]

A motorcycle enthusiast, Sultan Ibrahim is the founder of the annual motorcycling tour event [Kembara Mahkota Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kembara_Mahkota_Johor).[^cite_note-74]

(Brunei), S.P.M.J, S.S.I.J, S.P.M.T, S.M.N, S.P.M.K, S.P.M.P, P.I.S, Sultan Yang Dipertuan Bagi Negeri dan Jajahan Takluk Johor Darul Ta’zim*.[^cite_note-75]

while in English: *His Majesty Sultan Ibrahim Ibni Almarhum Sultan Iskandar, D.K. (Johor), D.K. (Perak), D.K. (Kelantan), D.K. (Perlis), D.K. (Selangor), D.K. (Negeri Sembilan), D.K. (Terengganu), D.K.M.B. (Brunei), S.P.M.J, S.S.I.J, S.P.M.T, S.M.N, S.P.M.K, S.P.M.P, P.I.S, The Sultan and Sovereign Ruler of the State of Johor, The Abode of Dignity and its dependencies*.[^cite_note-76]

During his tenure as [Yang di-Pertuan Agong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong) (2024–present), Sultan Ibrahim's full title and style in Malay is: *Kebawah Duli Yang Maha Mulia Seri Paduka Baginda Yang di-Pertuan Agong Sultan Ibrahim*[^cite_note-77] while in English: *His Majesty Sultan Ibrahim, King of Malaysia*[^cite_note-78] or simply *His Majesty The King of Malaysia*.[^cite_note-79]
