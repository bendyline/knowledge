The **Let L-410 Turbolet** is a twin-engine short-range [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) transport aircraft designed and produced by the [Czech](5321.md) aircraft manufacturer [Let Kunovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Industries) (named Aircraft Industries since 2005).

It was developed as the *L-400* during the 1960s in response to an [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot) requirement for an [Antonov An-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-2) replacement and performed its [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) on 16 April 1969. Since 1970, the L‑410 has been in operation with a variety of customers, having been typically used as an [airliner](51215.md) and a [utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility) transport aircraft, numerous military air services have also adopted the type. The aircraft is capable of landing on short and unpaved [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runways) and operating under extreme conditions from −50 to +50 °C (−58 to 122 °F).

Various models of the L-410 have been produced over the type’s production run of over fifty years; while initial aircraft were powered by imported [Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6)-27 engines, most models have been powered by domestically built [Walter M601](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_M601). Both the size and capabilities of the aircraft differ across the family; during the 1990s, the company pivoted towards the Western market and pursued [type certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certification) by the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) and [European Union Aviation Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency) (EASA) of its later models. Perhaps the most substantial variant to date is the [L 410 NG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Industries_L_410_NG), which has double the range of the original model, as well as other improvements such as a [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit). By 2016, in excess of 1,200 L-410s had been constructed while in excess of 350 aircraft were reportedly in service with operators across more than 50 countries.[^cite_note-UVP-E20brochure-1]

## Development

Development of the L-410 was started during the mid-1960s by the [Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) aircraft manufacturer Let Kunovice.[^cite_note-4] The company had decided to launch the project with awareness that the Soviet airline [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot) was actively seeking a [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop)-powered replacement for the [Antonov An-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-2) biplane, which was viewed as a potentially lucrative opportunity for a suitable aircraft.[^cite_note-airwaysmag2023-5] Preliminary studies performed by Let centred around a design referred to as the *L-400*. In addition to its use as an airliner, equal attention was paid towards its suitability for the role of a [cargo aircraft](2375692.md).[^cite_note-janes725-3]

Following considerable revision of the original concept, a new version was produced, which the company called the *L-410 Turbolet*. On 16 April 1969, the first prototype, designated *XL-410*, performed its [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight), piloted by Vladimir Vik and Frantisek Svinka.[^cite_note-janes725-3][^cite_note-airwaysmag2023-5] The prototype was not only used for test flights but also to promote the aircraft to potential customers, appearing at various air shows, including the 1969 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show).[^cite_note-airwaysmag2023-5] As a consequence of the delayed development of a suitable Czech engine, the [Walter M601](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_M601), both the prototype and the initial production model were powered by [Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6)-27 engines and [Hartzell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartzell_Propeller) HC-B-3TN-3D three-blade propellers, both of which were imported from [North America](21139.md).[^cite_note-cecrdle267-6]

Once development of the M601 engine had been completed, it was quickly adopted and coupled with [Avia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avia_Motors)-built V 508 three-blade propellers to replace the PT6 engine on the second production model, designated *L-410M*.[^cite_note-cecrdle267-6] A further version, the *L-410 UVP*, was specifically developed for Aeroflot, who had expressed dissatisfaction in the performance of the initial model.[^cite_note-airwaysmag2023-5] In comparison with earlier models, the revised aircraft provided improved performance in take-off and landing, which are largely due to its increased wing and tail area, making it a [Short take-off/landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOL) (STOL) aircraft. As a consequence of its increased empty weight and a shift in the center of gravity, the L-410 UVP has a decreased [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of 15 passengers.[^cite_note-Aerospace2017-7]

The most common variant of the type, the *L-410 UVP-E*, possesses an increased maximum take-off weight of 6,400 kg (14,100 lb), is equipped with more powerful M601E engines that drive new five-blade [propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller_\(aircraft\)) designated V 510, and is provisioned for equipping [wing tip tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_tip) that increase the aircraft's overall fuel capacity.[^cite_note-cecrdle267-6] The L-410 UVP-E performed its first flight in 1984 while quantity production of the model commenced during 1986. By 1990, in excess of 1,000 L-410s had been constructed.[^cite_note-8]

The *L-410 UVP-E9* and *L-410 UVP-E20* are versions which vary from each other only minorly, these were produced to satisfy the differing regulations of various certifying authorities of different regions. Later production L-420s have been outfitted with a newer Walter engine variant, the M601F. Production of the L-410 UVP-E20 was underway in the early twenty-first century, being powered by the latest derivative of the M601 engine, designated *GE H80-200*, and outfitted new Avia Propeller *AV-725* five blade propellers.[^cite_note-cecrdle2678-9]

Following the end of the [Cold War](325329.md), company officials opted to pursue opportunities for the aircraft in Western markets. On 11 March 1998, the L‑410 first received approval for its use in [North America](21139.md) by the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA); the improved L-410 UVP-E20 was similarly certified on 30 June 2015.[^cite_note-FAAtcds-10][^cite_note-AINrollout2015-2] On 4 February 2005, this same model of the L-410 had been certified by the [European Union Aviation Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency) (EASA) while all other production variants followed on 28 March 2007.[^cite_note-EASAtcds-11] In the mid-2010s, a typical production rate of 15 L-410 UVPs were being completed each year.[^cite_note-12]

On 3 September 2013, the Russian company [UGMK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mining_and_Metallurgical_Company) (Iskander Machmudov) became the majority owner of LET Kunovice Aircraft Industries. At the time of the acquisition, it was announced that a new production line for the L-410 would be established within Russia within one year.[^cite_note-13] As part of this strategy, the company developed the most substantial variant of the aircraft to date, the [L 410 NG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Industries_L_410_NG), which can be visually distinguished from its predecessors by its considerably longer nose as well its enlarged rear area, the latter change being made to accommodate the carriage of double the luggage. It is also furnished a new wing design and has been fitted with a modernised [Garmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmin) G3000 [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit). The propulsion system has also been redesigned, featuring more powerful and quieter GE H85 engines that drive Avia-725 propellers. The L 410 NG can fly for double the range of the original model of the aircraft.[^cite_note-airliner_NG2015-14][^cite_note-airwaysmag2023-5]

On 7 July 2015, the company displayed the first L 410 NG, which had been manufactured in Russia;[^cite_note-15] it performed its first flight on 29 July 2015.[^cite_note-airliner_NG2015-14][^cite_note-16] Power grew up to 850 shp (630 kW) instead of the previous 800 shp (600 kW) [GE H80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_H80)-200, speed increased to 223 kn (413 km/h). [Maximum take-off weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_take-off_weight) rose 500 kg (1,100 lb) to 7,000 kg (15,000 lb) and [range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_\(aeronautics\)) to 1,350 nmi (2,500 km) up from the original 820 nmi (1,520 km). [Fuel](642298.md) capacity rose from 1,300 to 2,450 kg (2,870 to 5,400 lb) and [endurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endurance_\(aeronautics\)) from five hours to nine hours. [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA), [EASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EASA) and Russian [certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) took place in late 2017.[^cite_note-17] Serial production of the L 410 NG commenced in March 2018.[^cite_note-18]

Due to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, which led to sanctions against the Russian Federation by the European Union and other Western countries, Aircraft Industries lost its main customer for its passenger aircraft – Russia. Due to the subsequent financial difficulties, the Kunovice based aircraft factory was acquired by the [OMNIPOL Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnipol) in 2022.[^cite_note-19]

## Design



Head-on view of a L-410 landing upon snow

The Let L-410 Turbolet is an [unpressurized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization) all-metal high-wing commuter aircraft. It is characterised by its relatively low maintenance and operating costs, as well as its adaptable design, which includes special interchangeable configurations.[^cite_note-20] All models are equipped with a retractable [undercarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear). The L‑410 UVP-E features two [hydraulic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic) circuits, one being the primary and the other intended for emergencies; the main electrical system operates with 28V DC. The [de-icing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_protection_system) is [leading edge pneumatic deicers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deicing_boot) and electrical heating of propellers, [cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit) [windshields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windshield) and [pitot-static system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitot-static_system) heads. The maximum take-off weight of the L-410 UVP-E is 6400 kg with the possibility of an increase to 6,600 kg (14,600 lb) for the E9 and E20 variants, possessing a maximum seating capacity of between 17 and 19 passengers. The L‑410 UVP-E is equipped with [Avia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avia_Motors) V 510 five-blade propellers.

The L 410 UVP-E20 is certified on the basis of [FAR 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAR_23) either Amendment 34 or Amendment 41. It is certified by the European Union, the Russian Federation, the United States, [Brazil](3383.md), [Argentina](18951905.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Venezuela](32374.md), [Cuba](5042481.md), [India](14533.md), [Nepal](171166.md), [Philippines](23440.md), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), [Indonesia](14579.md), Republic of [South Africa](17416221.md), [Algeria](358.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), and many other countries accepting some of the previous certificates.[^cite_note-certifications-21] The aircraft has also been approved for operation in a number of other countries, such as [Kenya](188171.md), [Tanzania](30118.md), [Uganda](31816.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), [Colombia](5222.md), Venezuela, [South Korea](27019.md) and others. The airplane is certified for [IFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules) operation, CAT I [ILS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_landing_system) approaches, and flights in icing conditions.

- **L-410UVP:** (*Ukorochennaya vzlot-posadka*, "short take-off and landing") Third series, fundamentally modified. The principal changes are a trunk, an extended wingspan by 0.80 m (2.6 ft), M601Bs (M601Ds after 1983) with Avia V508B (V508D after 1983) propellers, and a higher horizontal stabilizer. The UVP variants possesses [STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOL) characteristics. Passenger capacity fell to 15 due to increased weight.&#x20;

## Operators



The L-410 has been a popular aircraft for [skydivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydiver)

Main article: [List of Let L-410 Turbolet operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Let_L-410_Turbolet_operators)

Large numbers of L-410s were delivered to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union); by 1985, 500 aircraft had reportedly been obtained by various operators within the USSR alone.[^cite_note-22] Accordingly, the type has been heavily operated by various ex-Soviet states, in particular [Russia](25391.md). Furthermore, numerous L-410s have been sold to airlines across [Asia](689.md), [Africa](5334607.md), Central America, and [South America](26769.md). Forty aircraft are in use throughout [Europe](9239.md) for commercial operations, including [skydiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydiving).

### Military

The [Myanmar Air Force](9484818.md) uses at least [3 confirmed L-410](9484818.md#Aircraft), delivered in violation of EU sanctions against the [Myanmar junta](19457.md#Coup_d'%C3%A9tat_and_civil_war)[^cite_note-23].
