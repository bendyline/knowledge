**Słubice** (Polish: [\[swuˈbʲit͡sɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish)) is a [border town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_town) in the [Lubusz Voivodeship](75149.md), in western [Poland](22936.md).[^cite_note-TERYT-2] It is the capital of [Słubice County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82ubice_County) and the administrative seat of the [Gmina Słubice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_S%C5%82ubice,_Lubusz_Voivodeship). As of 2019, the town had a population of 16,705, with an urban agglomeration of Słubice-[Frankfurt (Oder)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Oder\)) counting 85,000 inhabitants. It is part of the historical region of [Lubusz Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Land).

Located on the [Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder) river, it lies directly opposite the city of [Frankfurt (Oder)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Oder\)) in [Germany](11867.md), which it was a part of as *Dammvorstadt*[^cite_note-3] until 1945. It is the seat of the [Collegium Polonicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Polonicum).

## History



Frankfurt with eastern bridgehead (above), 1701

The name is a modern [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) version of *Zliwitz*, a [West Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polabian_Slavs) settlement east of the Brandendamm causeway across the [Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder), mentioned in Frankfurt's city charter of 1253.[^cite_note-4] Until 1249 it was part of the Polish [Lubusz Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Land), which since 1138 in different periods formed part of the [Greater Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Greater_Poland) or [Silesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Silesia) provinces of then fragmented [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland). In 1225 *Zliwitz* was granted [staple rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_right) by [Henry the Bearded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Bearded). The [Ascanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Ascania) margraves of [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg) had purchased the Lubusz Land from the [Silesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Silesia) Duke [Bolesław II the Bald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_II_the_Horned) in 1249. After a war broke out over control of the region in 1319, the area came under the control of the [Duchy of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Pomerania). In 1319 [Wartislaw IV, Duke of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartislaw_IV,_Duke_of_Pomerania) granted new [privileges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privilege_\(law\)) to the town of [Frankfurt (Oder)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Oder\)), which today's Słubice was already part of.[^cite_note-5] The area fell again to Brandenburg in 1324. Between 1373 and 1415 it was part of the [Lands of the Bohemian Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lands_of_the_Bohemian_Crown) (or the *Czech Lands*), ruled by the [Luxembourg dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg-Luxemburg_dynasty).

In early 1945, a German-perpetrated [death march](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_marches_during_the_Holocaust) of prisoners of various nationalities from the dissolved camp in [Świecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awiecko) to the [Sachsenhausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp) passed through the town.[^cite_note-6]

Słubice is closely linked to its German sister city Frankfurt (Oder), of which it was a part until 1945. The two cities have been forced apart by the drawing of the [Oder–Neisse line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder%E2%80%93Neisse_line), which drew the Polish-German border through the city. The two cities share many urban amenities and collaborate on various projects, such as a [wastewater treatment plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) in Słubice that serves both towns, as well as the [Collegium Polonicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Polonicum) extension of some of the [Viadrina European University's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_University_Viadrina) departments on the Polish side of the border. Furthermore, Słubice is part of a special Słubice-Kostrzyn Economic Zone.

## Climate

Słubice has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*).[^cite_note-kottek2006-7][^cite_note-Peel-8]

Słubice set an all time record for the highest temperature ever recorded in Poland, reaching 40.5 °C (104.9 °F) on June 28th, 2026. The previous record was on July 29th, 1921 in [Prószków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pr%C3%B3szk%C3%B3w), where the temperature was 40.2 °C (104.4 °F).[^cite_note-9]

## Culture



*[Wikipedia Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_Monument)*, 2014, by [Mihran Hakobyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihran_Hakobyan)

Słubice was the setting for the 2003 film *[Distant Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distant_Lights_\(2003_film\))* (*Lichter*) as well as for scenes in the 2002 film *[Grill Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grill_Point)*.

On October 22, 2014, [a monument to Wikipedia editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_Monument) by [Mihran Hakobyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihran_Hakobyan) was unveiled in the town, the first such honoring of the worldwide [Wikipedian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedian).[^cite_note-thenews-21]

## Transport

The [National roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_roads_in_Poland) 29 and 31, and the [Voivodeship road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_road) 137 run through the town, and the [A2 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_autostrada_\(Poland\)), part of the [European route E30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E30), runs nearby, south of the town.

## Cuisine

The officially protected [traditional food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_food) of Słubice and the county is *schab tradycyjny słubicki*, a local type of [smoked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_\(cooking\)) *schab* ([pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork) [loin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loin)), a popular traditional [Christmas](6237.md) dish in the area (as designated by the [Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development_\(Poland\))).[^cite_note-22]

## Sports

[Polonia Słubice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonia_S%C5%82ubice) football club is based in Słubice. Polonia's home ground is the [OSiR Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSiR_Stadium_in_S%C5%82ubice).
