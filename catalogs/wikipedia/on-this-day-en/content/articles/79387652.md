The **2025–26 Texas's 18th congressional district [special election](155434.md)** to determine the member of the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md) for [Texas's 18th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas's_18th_congressional_district) was held on November 4, 2025. The seat became vacant following the death of Democrat [Sylvester Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Turner) on March 5, 2025. It is considered a [safely Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_seat) district. This was the second special election to this seat in almost one year, the first being held in [November 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Texas's_18th_congressional_district_special_election) after the July 2024 death of Representative [Sheila Jackson Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Jackson_Lee).

No candidate received a majority of the vote in the first round, leading to a runoff between the top two candidates: county attorney [Christian Menefee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Menefee) and former city councilwoman [Amanda Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Edwards). The runoff election was held on January 31, 2026[^cite_note-1] and Menefee was selected to succeed Turner to finish the term.[^cite_note-RunoffDate-2]

## Background

Incumbent [Sylvester Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Turner) died in office on March 5, 2025 from health complications, according to a statement released by his family.[^cite_note-hchron-dies-3][^cite_note-Lozano_2025-4] Turner was [elected in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Texas#District_18) to Congress after serving as a two-term [mayor of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Houston).[^cite_note-Lozano_2025-4] District 18 is a [deeply blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_seat) urban district, with Turner winning the district with 69.4% of the vote.[^cite_note-HPVT-5] More than a month later, Governor [Greg Abbott](1492735.md) called for the special election to take place on November 4.[^cite_note-6] Texas has no legal deadline for when special elections must take place, and many Democrats criticized Abbott, alleging he intentionally delayed the election to aid congressional Republicans, who hold a narrow majority in the House of Representatives.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The special election was conducted with all candidates on the ballot and was followed by a runoff because no candidate received a majority of the vote in the first round.
