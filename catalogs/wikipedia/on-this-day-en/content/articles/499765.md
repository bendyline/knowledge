The **Saeima** (Latvian pronunciation: [\[ˈsai.ma\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latvian)) is the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) of the [Republic of Latvia](17514.md). It is a [unicameral](204304.md) parliament consisting of 100 members who are elected by [proportional representation](23298.md), with seats allocated to [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties) which gain at least 5% of the popular vote. Elections are scheduled to be held once every four years, normally on the first Saturday of October. The most recent elections were held in [October 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Latvian_parliamentary_election).

The [President of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia) can dismiss the Saeima and request early elections. The [procedure for dismissing it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_dissolution_power_of_Latvian_President) involves substantial political risk to the president, including a risk of loss of office. On 28 May 2011 president [Valdis Zatlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Zatlers) decided to initiate the dissolution of the Saeima, which was approved in a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Latvian_parliamentary_dissolution_referendum), and the Saeima was dissolved on 23 July 2011.[^cite_note-6]

The current [Speaker of the Saeima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Saeima) is [Daiga Mieriņa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daiga_Mieri%C5%86a) of the [Union of Greens and Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Greens_and_Farmers) party. The basic document that regulates the proceedings of the parliament is the Rules of Order of *Saeima* (*Saeimas kārtības rullis*, also Rules of Procedure), adopted 23 March 1923 with amendments in 1929 and 1994.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## History and etymology

The Saeima traces its origins to the [Sejm of the Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland), which led to the creation of the [Sejm (Seimas) of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seimas_of_the_Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) and later to the creation of the [Sejm of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth). [Polish Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflanty_Voivodeship), a part of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth), was exposed to the Polish mode of administration and introduced to the Sejm-system. The Warsaw Sejm of 1677 settled the case of remaining part of Polish Livonia or [Latgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgale) ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Inflanty*), naming it a voivodeship and a duchy, with the right to name three senators: the Bishop, the Voivode and the Castellan of Inflanty (...) Local sejmiks took place at [Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugavpils), while starostas resided at Daugavpils, [Ludza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludza), [Rēzekne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%93zekne) and [Viļaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C4%BCaka). The voivodeship had six deputies to the Sejm, but only two of them came from Inflanty, the other four were symbolically named by the king, to remember the lost part of Livonia[^cite_note-9] ([Swedish Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Livonia)). However, the rest of Latvia belonged to the [Duchy of Courland and Semigallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Courland_and_Semigallia), which was governed by the Dukes and the [Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag) of Courland.

The word *sejm* derives from the verb "sjąć się" meaning "to get together", with similar words in some other Slavic languages,[^cite_note-10] of [proto-Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Slavic) origin \*sъjęti < \*sъjemti[^cite_note-11] Sejm, then, as a noun meant "a gathering, a meeting, a council."



Representatives of the 1st Saeima of the Republic of Latvia in 1922

In the 19th century, as the concept of nations began to emerge, [Juris Alunāns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Alun%C4%81ns), a member of a Latvian nationalist group called the [Young Latvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Latvians), claimed ownership to the word "saeims". Despite the similar sound, similar semantic structure and clear historical connotations, he claimed that it was a purely Latvian word that he had invented. As mentioned earlier, the word bears a similar meaning: "a gathering, a meeting, a council".  He claimed that the word he constructed stemmed from the archaic [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language) word *eima* instead, meaning "to go" (derived from the [PIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) *\*ei* "to go" and also a cognate with the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) *eimi*, [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulish_language) *_eimu_*, among others).[^cite_note-12]

He could not explain, however, how the s- prefix got added to the word, and what sense this addition made within the limits of the Latvian language. Nevertheless, according to Alunāns, the word is purely Latvian and completely independent of the aforementioned historical context. However, the prefix sa- to a verb in modern Latvian language usually stands for a complete action and the word "Saeima" can stand for a meaning "let's gather together completely".

&#x20;In the pre-war Latvia, the Saeima was elected for three-year terms. The [1st Saeima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Saeima) met from 7 November 1922 to 2 November 1925, the [2nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Saeima) from 3 November 1925 to 5 November 1928, the [3rd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Saeima) from 6 November 1928 to 2 November 1931, and the [4th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Saeima) from 3 November 1931 to 15 May 1934 (date of the [Latvian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Latvian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)).

## Elections

The Saeima is an entirely elected body. All Latvian citizens (including naturalized citizens) over the age of 18 are eligible to vote. Candidates must be qualified to vote, but must also be over 21, must not be former employees of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Latvian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) State security services, intelligence or counter-intelligence services or any other foreign affiliated organizations, must not have been convicted of a criminal offence or deemed to be of diminished mental capacity.[^cite_note-13]

The term of the Saeima is four years. An election may be called early, but doing so is more complicated than in other parliamentary democracies. If the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia) proposes that the Saeima be dissolved, a national referendum must be held to confirm the dissolution. If the dissolution is not approved, the President is removed from office. If one-tenth of the electorate signs a petition demanding a dissolution, a referendum can be held without the involvement of the President.

There are five constituencies, [Kurzeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courland_\(Saeima_constituency\)) (12 deputies), [Latgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgallia_\(Saeima_constituency\)) (14), [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_\(Saeima_constituency\)) (35), [Vidzeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonia_\(Saeima_constituency\)) (25), and [Zemgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigallia_\(Saeima_constituency\)) (14). Overseas votes are counted for the Riga constituency.

Seats are distributed in each constituency by [open list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) [proportional representation](23298.md) among the parties that overcome a 5% national [election threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_threshold) using an unmodified version of the [Webster/Sainte-Laguë method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster/Sainte-Lagu%C3%AB_method).

Voters cast a vote for a party list, which consists of the candidates that the party has submitted in that constituency. Although a specific ordering is listed for each candidate, which is determined by the party, this has no effect on the actual chances of each candidate. Instead, voters cast "specific votes" for candidates. These votes can be either positive votes or negative votes. The number of votes for each candidate is determined by taking the number of votes for the respective list, and adding it to the candidate's positive votes, before subtracting the number of negative votes for that candidate. The candidates with the highest number of votes fill the party's seats. A positive vote is indicated by drawing a plus sign (+) next to the candidate's name on the ballot paper. A negative vote is indicated by crossing out the candidate's name. Voters may only cast specific votes for the candidates on the list that they voted for.

It is uncommon for any party to achieve more than 30% of the vote in an election. The record is 32.4% for the [Latvian Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Way) party in the [1993 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Latvian_parliamentary_election). This means that a coalition has always been necessary.

If a seat falls vacant during a term of the Saeima, it is filled by the next candidate on the appropriate list.

The [Communist Party of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Latvia) is the only political party that is banned.
