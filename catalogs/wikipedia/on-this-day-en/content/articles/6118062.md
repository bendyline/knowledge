# States parties to the Rome Statute



&#x20; State party

&#x20; Signatory that has not ratified

&#x20; State party that subsequently withdrew its membership

&#x20; Signatory that subsequently withdrew its signature

&#x20; Non-state party, non-signatory

The **states parties to the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court** are those [sovereign states](23604120.md) that have [ratified](429650.md), or have otherwise become party to, the [Rome Statute](68762.md). The Rome Statute is the [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) that established the [International Criminal Court](14880.md), an international court that has [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) over certain international crimes, including [genocide](12441.md), [crimes against humanity](61099.md), and [war crimes](48626.md) that are committed by nationals of states parties or within the territory of states parties. States parties are legally obligated to co-operate with the Court when it requires, such as in arresting and transferring [indicted persons](18248714.md) or providing access to evidence and witnesses. States parties are entitled to participate and vote in proceedings of the Assembly of States Parties, which is the Court's governing body. Such proceedings include the election of such officials as [judges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judges_of_the_International_Criminal_Court) and the [prosecutor](33903993.md), the approval of the Court's budget, and the adoption of [amendments to the Rome Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court).

### Implementing legislation

The Rome Statute obliges states parties to cooperate with the Court in the investigation and prosecution of crimes, including the arrest and surrender of suspects.[^cite_note-37] Part 9 of the Statute requires all states parties to “ensure that there are procedures available under their national law for all of the forms of cooperation which are specified under this Part”.[^cite_note-38]

Under the Rome Statute's [complementarity principle](14880.md#Complementarity), the Court only has jurisdiction over cases where the relevant state is unwilling or unable to investigate and, if appropriate, prosecute the case itself. Therefore, many states parties have implemented national legislation to provide for the investigation and prosecution of crimes that fall under the jurisdiction of the Court.[^cite_note-39]

As of October 2024, 68 states have adopted at least one piece of complementarity legislation, 59 have adopted at least one cooperation law, 52 have adopted neither, and 55 have adopted both, whereas 13 have implemented complementarity only and 4 have implemented cooperation only.[^cite_note-list_of_implementation_complementarity_cooperation-40]

### Regional groups and minimum voting requirements

The number of states parties from the several [United Nations regional groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Regional_Groups) has an influence on the regional minimum voting requirements during elections of judges. Paragraph 20(b) of the procedure for the nomination and election of judges of the Court[^cite_note-41] states that each Party must vote for at least as many candidates from a regional group as would be required to bring the number of judges from that group to two. If, however, more than 16 states parties belong to the group, this target is increased to three.

The following table lists how many states parties there are from each regional group as of August 2024. After the accession of the Maldives on 1 December 2011, the Asia–Pacific Group became the last regional group to reach the threshold size of 17 members, just in time to increase the minimum voting requirement for this group in the [2011 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_International_Criminal_Court_judges_election).[^cite_note-42] Since then, the target for the regional minimum voting requirement has remained at three for all regional groups. This has resulted in at least three judges from each regional group sitting on the bench except during the term from 2020 to 2023, when there were only two judges from the Asia–Pacific group. Because only a single candidate from that group was nominated in the [2020 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_International_Criminal_Court_judges_election), no minimum voting requirement was applied for the group, and the one nominee was not elected.

## Withdrawal

Article 127 of the Rome Statute allows for states to withdraw from the ICC. Withdrawal takes effect one year after notification of the [depositary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depositary), and has no effect on prosecution that has already started. As of September 2026, 11 states have given formal notice of their intention to withdraw from the statute, although three rescinded their notification before it came into effect.[^cite_note-untreaty-1]

### Burundi, South Africa, and the Gambia

Several states have argued that the ICC is a tool of Western [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism), only punishing leaders from small, weak states while ignoring crimes committed by richer and more powerful states.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] This sentiment has been expressed particularly by African states, 34 of which are members of the ICC, due to a perceived disproportionate focus of the Court on Africa. Nine out of the ten situations which the ICC has investigated were in African countries.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-france24.com-50]

In June 2009, several African states, including Comoros, Djibouti, and Senegal, called on African states parties to withdraw *en masse* from the statute in protest against the [indictment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigations#Darfur,_Sudan) of [Sudanese](27421.md) President [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md).[^cite_note-51] In September 2013, [Kenya](188171.md)'s [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Kenya\)) passed a motion to withdraw from the ICC in protest against the [ICC prosecution of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Kenya) Kenyan Deputy President [William Ruto](2577967.md) and President [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta) (both charged before coming into office).[^cite_note-52] A mass withdrawal from the ICC by African member states in response to the trial of Kenyan authorities was discussed at a special summit of the [African Union](10634933.md) in October.[^cite_note-53] The summit concluded that serving heads of state should not be put on trial, and that the Kenyan cases should be deferred.[^cite_note-54] However, the summit did not endorse the proposal for a mass withdrawal due to lack of support for the idea.[^cite_note-55] In November the ICC's Assembly of State Parties responded by agreeing to consider proposed [amendments to the Rome Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Rome_Statute) to address the AU's concerns.[^cite_note-56]

In October–November 2016, Burundi, South Africa, and The Gambia all notified the UNSG of their intention to withdraw from the ICC. Burundi was the subject of an ongoing preliminary investigation by the ICC at the time.[^cite_note-57] South Africa's exit followed its refusal to execute an ICC warrant for Sudan's al-Bashir when he was in the country. Following [The Gambia's presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Gambian_presidential_election) later that year, which ended the long rule of [Yahya Jammeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Jammeh), The Gambia rescinded its withdrawal notification.[^cite_note-untreaty-1] The constitutionality of South Africa's notice was challenged by the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) opposition party, which argued that the approval of parliament was required and not sought. The [High Court of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_South_Africa) ruled in February 2017 that the government's notification was not legal, and it was required to revoke the notice effective 7 March 2017.[^cite_note-untreaty-1] A parliamentary bill on ICC withdrawal was subsequently withdrawn by the government.[^cite_note-58] The governing [African National Congress](2503.md) party continued to support withdrawing,[^cite_note-59] and in 2019 a new bill was put before Parliament to withdraw from the Statute,[^cite_note-60] though this was also withdrawn in March 2023.[^cite_note-61] Following the issuance of [ICC arrest warrants for Vladimir Putin and Maria Lvova-Belova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_arrest_warrants_for_Vladimir_Putin_and_Maria_Lvova-Belova) of Russia in March 2023, due to the deportation of children from Ukraine to Russia during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine),[^cite_note-khankc-62] South African president [Cyril Ramaphosa](1089194.md) declared that his country had decided to withdraw from the treaty to allow Putin to visit their country without risk of arrest for the upcoming [2023 BRICS summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_BRICS_summit) hosted in South Africa. However, it was subsequently clarified that such a decision had not been made.[^cite_note-aljsaf-63][^cite_note-voakb-64][^cite_note-auto-65]

### Philippines

On 14 March 2018, [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md), the Philippine president who was under preliminary examination by the ICC for his [controversial war on drugs campaign](51032591.md), announced that the country would withdraw from the Rome Statute.[^cite_note-66] He argued that while the Statute was ratified by the [Senate of the Philippines](299521.md) in 2011, it was never published in the [Official Gazette of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Gazette_\(Philippines\)), a requirement for penal laws (of which the Rome Statute subscribes as such) to take effect. Hence, he claimed that the Philippines was never a State Party *ab initio*. Additionally, he stated that the ICC was being utilized as a political tool against weak targets such as the Philippines. The United Nations received the official notification of withdrawal on 17 March 2018;[^cite_note-67] one year later (March 17, 2019), by rule, the Philippines' withdrawal became official. The legal validity of the withdrawal was challenged at the [Supreme Court of the Philippines](1335534.md),[^cite_note-philSC-43] which dismissed the case in 2021 in a unanimous decision for being "moot and academic".[^cite_note-GR238875-44] Nevertheless, the Supreme Court affirmed that the Philippines was obliged to cooperate with the ICC regarding acts committed while the Rome Statute was still in effect.[^cite_note-68]

While [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md), Duterte's successor as President, initially stated that the country "has no intention of rejoining the ICC",[^cite_note-69] in 2023 he stated that he was studying the possibility of rejoining.[^cite_note-70] The Philippine government also confirmed that it would extradite Duterte to the ICC if the matter were transferred to Interpol.[^cite_note-71] When an arrest warrant was issued in March 2025, [Duterte was arrested](79430656.md) by Philippine police[^cite_note-72] and surrendered to the custody of the ICC shortly thereafter.[^cite_note-73] In July 2025 the Secretary of Foreign Affairs stated that while no decision to rejoin the ICC had been made, the possibility was under discussion by relevant agencies.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] In February 2026, opposition parliamentarians introduced a resolution to calling for the government to rejoin the ICC.[^cite_note-76] On 6 November 2025, the ICC issued a secret arrest warrant against Duterte's erstwhile police chief, now Senator [Ronald dela Rosa](50966529.md), charging him with the crime against humanity of repeated murder during the period between 1 November 2011, and 16 March 2019.[^cite_note-77] On 11 May 2026, the arrest warrant was made public, and dela Rosa was placed on the international wanted list. The Senate refused to authorize his arrest, and an attempt to capture him in the Senate building itself on 13 May resulted in the [Senate lockdown](83172418.md) and a shootout, during which dela Rosa fled.[^cite_note-78] President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md)'s spokeswoman, while stating that there are "no plans yet" underway for the country's return within the Court, had communicated the previous day that the president still intended to apply national and international laws and that parliamentary guarantees are subject to limits.[^cite_note-79]

### Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan

In February 2025, following the ICC prosecutor's [request for arrest warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Afghanistan#Arrest_warrants) against Supreme Leader [Hibatullah Akhundzada](50633776.md) and Chief Justice [Abdul Hakim Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hakim_Haqqani), the [Taliban](30635.md) government of Afghanistan—which is [not recognized by the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_the_Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan)—stated that it did not view itself as bound to the Rome Statute, and declared that the country's accession in 2003 was "devoid of legal validity".[^cite_note-80] However, the Taliban has not formally initiated its withdrawal from the Rome Statute.[^cite_note-81]

### Hungary

On 3 April 2025, Hungary announced that it would withdraw from the ICC, shortly after [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), who is [wanted by the court for alleged war crimes in Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Israeli_leaders), arrived to the country for an official visit.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] Prime Minister [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) stated that the court's decision to prosecute Netanyahu showed it had become a "political court".[^cite_note-84] On 20 May 2025, with 134 votes in favour, 37 against and 7 abstentions, the [Hungarian National Assembly](585416.md) passed a bill approving withdrawal.[^cite_note-85] According to article 127 of the Rome Statute, withdrawal would not discharge Hungary from its obligations under the Statute until it had officially become effective[^cite_note-86] (1 year after its formal notification, i.e. on 2 June 2026).[^cite_note-87] Following the [2026 Hungarian parliamentary election](79337461.md), newly elected Prime Minister [Péter Magyar](76453946.md)'s government announced on 25 May 2026 that it would revoke the withdrawal.[^cite_note-88] A bill to revoke withdrawal was passed by an overwhelming majority in the National Assembly on 27 May.[^cite_note-89] President [Tamás Sulyok](76167806.md) and Parliamentary Speaker [Ágnes Forsthoffer](82940266.md) signed the bill into law the next day, and it officially took effect on 29 May.[^cite_note-90] The proposed withdrawal, had it succeeded, would have made Hungary the only nation in the [European Union](9317.md) not to recognise the ICC.[^cite_note-91]

### Alliance of Sahel States

In September 2025, [Burkina Faso](3470.md), [Niger](21373.md) and [Mali](19127.md), who form the [Alliance of Sahel States](74840743.md) announced their plans to withdraw from the ICC, labeling it as an "instrument of neo-colonialist repression". The three leaders ([Ibrahim Traoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Traor%C3%A9), [Abdourahamane Tchiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdourahamane_Tchiani) and [Assimi Goïta](65000306.md)) accused the ICC of targeting less privileged countries and stated that the ICC "has proven itself incapable of handling and prosecuting proven war crimes, crimes against humanity, crimes of genocide, and crimes of aggression". The three leaders further stated that they were in favor of creating "indigenous mechanisms for the consolidation of peace and justice" as an alternative.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] On 18 June 2026, nine months after the announcement, Niger submitted its instrument of withdrawal to the ICC,[^cite_note-94] as did Burkina Faso and Mali on 24 June.[^cite_note-95]

### U.S. campaign for state withdrawals (2026–present)

In July 2026, the United States announced that it was launching a campaign to encourage ICC member states to withdraw, citing threats to American sovereignty.[^cite_note-96]

In July 2026, Venezuelan foreign minister [Félix Plasencia](81332725.md) announced that Venezuela had informed the UN of its "irrevocable" decision to withdraw from the ICC under the orders of interim president [Delcy Rodríguez](44199228.md), accusing the court of disproportionately focusing on African and Latin American nations in the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South).[^cite_note-97] The [National Assembly of Venezuela](510768.md) had voted in December 2025 to revoke the law it had previously passed to ratify the Rome Statute.[^cite_note-98] The United States praised Rodríguez for joining its campaign to dismantle the ICC.[^cite_note-VenezuelaExplainer-99] On 24 July 2026 the acting government of Venezuela formally notified the UN of its decision, with the withdrawal thus becoming effective on 24 July 2027,[^cite_note-100] but Venezuela's exit will not affect the ongoing [ICC investigation](62526848.md) in the country.[^cite_note-VenezuelaExplainer-99]

On 27 July 2026 Chad announced its intention to withdraw from the ICC, citing limited effectiveness and disproportionate attention to African cases; it was directly asked to withdraw by the United States.[^cite_note-101] According to the Chadian authorities, the instrument of withdrawal was duly notified to the Secretary-General of the United Nations on the same day; consequently, the withdrawal will become effective on 27 July 2027.[^cite_note-Chad-12] On 7 August 2026, the Presidency of the Assembly of States Parties officially expressed its regret for the decisions taken by Chad and Venezuela.[^cite_note-102]

In September 2026, Naoero (formerly known as Nauru) announced its intention to withdraw from the International Criminal Court. President David Adeang stated during his address to the United Nations General Assembly that the country would deposit its instruments of withdrawal from the Rome Statute, describing the ICC as a multilateral institution of “increasing irrelevance” to Naoero. The announcement followed a call by U.S. President Donald Trump earlier the same day for states to leave the court. In the same speech, Adeang also criticised the International Seabed Authority for prolonged delays in adopting a mining code for deep seabed mineral extraction, an issue of particular importance to Naoero as one of the strongest Pacific proponents of such activities.[^cite_note-103]

In September 2026, Tajikistan’s lower house of parliament considered a draft law providing for the country’s withdrawal from the International Criminal Court. The initiative followed the ICC’s formal finding of non-compliance after Tajikistan hosted Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in October 2025 without executing the court’s outstanding arrest warrant against him, an action that had earlier drawn criticism from the [European Union](9317.md). [Tajikistan](30108.md), which ratified the Rome Statute in 2000 and became a state party in 2002, cited the obligations arising from membership as incompatible with its national interests and close partnership with Russia. As of late September 2026, however, the country had not yet submitted a formal instrument of withdrawal to the United Nations Secretary-General.[^cite_note-104]

## Acceptance of jurisdiction

Pursuant to article 12(3) of the [Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court), a state that is not a party to the Statute may, "by declaration lodged with the Registrar, accept the exercise of jurisdiction by the Court with respect to the crime in question." Even if the state that does so is not a State Party to the Statute, the relevant provisions of the statute would still be applicable on the accepting state, but only on an *[ad hoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_hoc)* basis.

To date, the Court has received seven article 12(3) declarations. Additionally, a declaration was submitted in December 2013 by the [Freedom and Justice Party of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_and_Justice_Party_\(Egypt\)) seeking to accept jurisdiction on behalf of Egypt. However, the Office of the Prosecutor found that as the party had lost power following the [2013 Egyptian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Egyptian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) that July, it did not have the authority to make the declaration on behalf of Egypt.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

According to the [Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Convention_on_the_Law_of_Treaties), a state that has signed but not ratified a treaty is obliged to refrain from "acts which would defeat the object and purpose" of the treaty. However, these obligations do not continue if the state has "made its intention clear not to become a party to the treaty".[^cite_note-127] Four signatory states (Israel, the Russian Federation, Sudan, and the United States) have informed the UN Secretary General that they no longer intend to become parties to the Rome Statute, and as such have no legal obligations arising from their signature.[^cite_note-untreaty-1]

### Bahrain

The government of [Bahrain](18933277.md) originally announced in May 2006 that it would ratify the Rome Statute in the session ending in July 2006.[^cite_note-128] By December 2006, the ratification had not yet been completed, but the [Coalition for the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_the_International_Criminal_Court) said they expected ratification in 2007.[^cite_note-129]

### Israel

[Israel](9282173.md) voted against the adoption of the Rome Statute but later signed it for a short period. In 2002, Israel notified the UN Secretary General that it no longer intended to become a party to the Rome Statute, and as such, it has no legal obligations arising from their signature of the statute.[^cite_note-130]

Israel states that it has "deep sympathy" with the goals of the Court. However, it has concerns that political pressure on the Court would lead it to reinterpret international law or to "invent new crimes". It cites the inclusion of ["the transfer of parts of the civilian population of an occupying power into occupied territory"](15123.md#Legal_status) as a war crime as an example of this, whilst at the same time disagrees with the exclusion of terrorism and drug trafficking. Israel sees the powers given to the prosecutor as excessive and the geographical appointment of judges as disadvantaging Israel which was prevented from joining any of the [UN Regional Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Regional_Groups).[^cite_note-131]

### Kuwait

At a conference in 2007, the [Kuwaiti](7515890.md) Bar Association and the Secretary of the [National Assembly of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Kuwait), Hussein Al-Hereti, called for Kuwait to join the Court.[^cite_note-132]

### Russia

[Russia](25391.md) signed the Rome Statute in 2000. On 14 November 2016 the ICC published a report on its preliminary investigation of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) which found that "the situation within the territory of Crimea and Sevastopol factually amounts to an on-going state of occupation" and that "information, such as reported shelling by both States of military positions of the other, and the detention of Russian military personnel by Ukraine, and vice versa, points to direct military engagement between Russian armed forces and Ukrainian government forces that would suggest the existence of an international armed conflict in the context of armed hostilities in eastern Ukraine".[^cite_note-133] In response, a presidential decree by Russian President Vladimir Putin approved "sending the Secretary General of the United Nations notice of the intention of the Russian Federation to no longer be a party to the Rome Statute".[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] Formal notice was given on 30 November 2016.[^cite_note-136]

### Sudan

[Sudan](27421.md) signed the Rome Statute in 2000. In 2005 the ICC opened an [investigation](33596946.md) into the [war in Darfur](5043637.md), a region of Sudan. [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md), the President of Sudan, was indicted in 2009. On 26 August 2008, Sudan notified the UN Secretary General that it no longer intended to ratify the treaty and therefore no longer bears any legal obligations arising from its signature.[^cite_note-untreaty-1] Following the [2019 Sudanese coup d'état](60469131.md), [Sadiq al-Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_al-Mahdi) a former prime minister of Sudan who backs the opposition, called for Sudan to join the ICC.[^cite_note-137]

On 4 August 2021, the Sudanese government approved unanimously a draft bill to join the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court.[^cite_note-138]

### Thailand

Former Senator [Kraisak Choonhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraisak_Choonhavan) called in November 2006 for [Thailand](30128.md) to ratify the Rome Statute and to accept retrospective jurisdiction, so that former premier [Thaksin Shinawatra](360815.md) could be investigated for crimes against humanity connected to 2,500 alleged extrajudicial killings carried out in 2003 against suspected drug dealers.[^cite_note-139]

### United States

Main article: [United States and the International Criminal Court](4567589.md)

The United States signed the Rome Statute in December 2000 (under President [Bill Clinton](3356.md)), but Clinton decided not to submit the treaty to the [United States Senate](24909346.md) for ratification, stating: "I will not, and do not recommend that my successor \[[George W. Bush](3414021.md)] submit the treaty to the Senate for advice and consent until our fundamental concerns are satisfied."[^cite_note-140] Opponents of the ICC in the U.S. Senate are "skeptical of new international institutions and still jealously protective of American sovereignty"; before the Rome Statute, opposition to the ICC was largely headed by Republican Senator [Jesse Helms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Helms).[^cite_note-Omestad-141] On May 6, 2002, the Bush administration stated that the U.S. did not intend to become a state party to the ICC; in a letter to [Secretary-General of the United Nations](162415.md) [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan), [Under Secretary of State for Arms Control and International Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_State_for_Arms_Control_and_International_Security) [John Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Bolton) stated that "the United States does not intend to become a party to the treaty," and that "the United States has no legal obligations arising from its signature on December 31, 2000."[^cite_note-142] This letter is sometimes called the "unsigning" of the treaty, but legal opinions on its actual legal effects differ,[^cite_note-143] with some scholars arguing that the president does not have the power to unilaterally "unsign" treaties.[^cite_note-144]

The United States "adopted a hostile stance towards the Court throughout most of the Bush presidency."[^cite_note-Lambert-145] In 2002, Congress enacted the [American Servicemembers' Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Servicemembers'_Protection_Act) (ASPA), which was signed into law on August 2, 2002; the "overriding purpose of the ASPA was to inhibit the U.S. government from supporting the ICC."[^cite_note-Lambert-145] Major provisions of the ASPA blocked U.S. funding of the ICC and required the U.S. "to enter into agreements with all ICC signatory states to shield American citizens abroad from ICC jurisdiction, under the auspices of Article 98 of the Rome Statute," which bars the ICC "from prosecuting individuals located on the territory of an ICC member state, where such action by the Court would cause the member state to violate the terms of any other bilateral or multilateral treaty to which it is a party."[^cite_note-Lambert-145] Traditionally, Article 98 was used in relation to traditional [status of forces agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_forces_agreement) (SOFAs) and status of mission agreements (SOMAs), in which nations hosting U.S. military personnel by invitation agreed to immunize them from prosecution in foreign courts.[^cite_note-Lambert-145] The Bush administration, supported by opponents of the ICC in Congress, adopted a new strategy of aggressively pursuing Bilateral Immunity Agreements (BIAs), "which guarantee immunity from ICC prosecution for all American citizens in the country with which the agreement is concluded" rather than just U.S. military forces.[^cite_note-Lambert-145] "Under the original ASPA, nations who refused to conclude BIAs with the United States were subject to sanctions, including the loss of military aid (though these provisions have since been repealed)."[^cite_note-Lambert-145] As of December 2006, the U.S. State Department reported that it had signed 102 BIAs.[^cite_note-BIAs-146] In 2002, the United States threatened to veto the renewal of all United Nations [peacekeeping](84617.md) missions unless its troops were granted immunity from prosecution by the Court.[^cite_note-res1422-147] In a compromise move, the Security Council passed [Resolution 1422](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_1422) on 12 July 2002, granting immunity to personnel from ICC non-states parties involved in United Nations established or authorized missions for a renewable twelve-month period.[^cite_note-res1422-147] This was renewed for twelve months in 2003 but the Security Council refused to renew the exemption again in 2004, after pictures emerged of [US troops abusing Iraqi prisoners in Abu Ghraib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ghraib_torture_and_prisoner_abuse), and the US withdrew its demand.[^cite_note-148]

Under the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_administration), the U.S. did not take moves to ratify the Rome Statute, but did adopt a "cautious, case-by-case approach to supporting the ICC" by supporting cases before the ICC. Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) stated that the U.S. encouraged "effective ICC action in ways that promote U.S. interests by bringing war criminals to justice."[^cite_note-Lambert-145] U.S. steps in support of the ICC undertaken under the Obama administration included participating in the annual Assembly of States Parties as an observer; using the U.S.'s [permanent seat on the UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) to support the referral of cases to the ICC (including [Libya in 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigations#Libya)); "sharing intelligence on fugitives and providing other substantial in-kind support" to the ICC; and expanding the War Crimes Rewards Program."[^cite_note-Lambert-145]

The [first Trump administration](52231341.md) strained relations with the ICC, stating it would revoke visas for any ICC staff seeking to investigate Americans for war crimes. Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) stated that such revocations could be applied to any staff involved with investigating war crimes committed by Israel or other allied nations as well.[^cite_note-149]

### Yemen

On 24 March 2007, the Yemeni parliament voted to ratify the Rome Statute.[^cite_note-yemen-150][^cite_note-ai_yemen-151] However, some MPs claim that this vote breached parliamentary rules, and demanded another vote. In that further vote, the ratification was retracted.[^cite_note-152]

### China

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) has opposed the Court, on the basis that it goes against the [sovereignty](28791.md) of nation states, that the principle of [complementarity](14880.md#Complementarity) gives the Court the ability to judge a nation's court system, that war crimes jurisdiction covers internal as well as international conflicts, that the Court's jurisdiction covers peacetime crimes against humanity, that the inclusion of the crime of aggression weakens the role of the UN Security Council, and that the Prosecutor's right to initiate prosecutions may open the Court to political influence.[^cite_note-158]

### India

The government of [India](14533.md) has consistently opposed the Court. It abstained in the vote adopting the statute in 1998, saying it objected to the broad definition adopted of crimes against humanity; the rights given to the UN Security Council to refer and delay investigations and bind non-states parties; and the use of nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction not being explicitly criminalized.[^cite_note-159] Other anxieties about the Court concern how the principle of complementarity would be applied to the [Indian criminal justice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_criminal_justice_system), the inclusion of war crimes for non-international conflicts, and the power of the Prosecutor to initiate prosecutions.[^cite_note-160]

### Indonesia

In October 1999, the [Indonesian](14579.md) representative at the [United Nations General Assembly Sixth Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Sixth_Committee) stated that Indonesia supports the adoption of the Rome Statute, and that "universal participation should be the cornerstone of the International Criminal Court".[^cite_note-AI_ID_ICC-161] The Indonesian 2004 National Plan of Action on Human Rights (Rencana Aksi Nasional Hak Asasi Manusia, RANHAM)[^cite_note-162] stated Indonesia's intention to ratify the statute in 2008.[^cite_note-163] This was repeated in 2007 by Foreign Minister [Hassan Wirajuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Wirajuda) and the head of the Indonesian [People's Representative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Representative_Council)'s Committee on Security and International Affairs, Theo L. Sambuaga.[^cite_note-164] In May 2013, Defense Minister [Purnomo Yusgiantoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purnomo_Yusgiantoro) stated that the government needed "more time to carefully and thoroughly review the pros and cons of the ratification".[^cite_note-165]

### Iraq

In February 2005, the [Iraqi Transitional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Transitional_Government) decided to ratify the Rome Statute. However, two weeks later it reversed this decision,[^cite_note-166] a move that the [Coalition for the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_the_International_Criminal_Court) claimed was due to pressure from the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-167]

### Lebanon

In March 2009, Lebanese Justice Minister said the government had decided not to join for now. The [Coalition for the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_the_International_Criminal_Court) claimed this was due in part to "*intense pressure*" from the United States, who feared it could result in the prosecution of [Israelis](9282173.md) in a future conflict.[^cite_note-168] Following the outbreak of the [Gaza war](74998519.md) which triggered clashes between Israel and the Lebanese armed group Hezbollah, in April 2024 Lebanon's cabinet decided to submit a declaration to the ICC accepting the court's jurisdiction over crimes committed on Lebanese territory since 7 October 2023. However, the following month this decision was reversed, and the declaration was not submitted.[^cite_note-169]

### Libya

On 12 May 2025, the UN recognized Libyan government ([Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\))) submitted a declaration acceptance of the court's jurisdiction with respect to alleged crimes committed in its territory from 2011 to the end of 2027.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

### Malaysia

Malaysia submitted an instrument of accession to the Rome Statute on 4 March 2019, which was to enter into force on 1 June.[^cite_note-MYAC-154] However, on 29 April 2019, Malaysia submitted a notice withdrawing its instrument of accession effective immediately to the Secretary General of the United Nations, preventing it from acceding.[^cite_note-MYWD-155] Prime Minister [Mahathir Mohamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahathir_Mohamad) explained that the withdrawal was due to concerns over its constitutionality as well as possible infringement of the sovereignty of the [Malay Rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchies_of_Malaysia).[^cite_note-172]

### Nepal

On 25 July 2006, the [Nepalese](171166.md) [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_House_of_Representatives) directed the government to ratify the Rome Statute. Under Nepalese law, this motion is compulsory for the Executive.[^cite_note-173]

Following a resolution by Parliament requesting that the government ratify the Statute, [Narahari Acharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narahari_Acharya), [Ministry of Law, Justice, Constituent Assembly and Parliamentary Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Law,_Justice,_Constituent_Assembly_and_Parliamentary_Affairs_\(Nepal\)) of Nepal, said in March 2015 that it had "formed a taskforce to conduct a study about the process". However, he said that it was "possible only after promulgating the new constitution", which was being debated by the [2nd Nepalese Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Nepalese_Constituent_Assembly).[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

### Pakistan

[Pakistan](23235.md) has supported the aims of the International Court and voted for the Rome Statute in 1998. However, Pakistan has not signed the agreement on the basis of several objections, including the fact that the Statute does not provide for reservations upon ratification or accession, the inclusion of provisional arrest, and the lack of immunity for heads of state. In addition, Pakistan (one of the [largest suppliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping_missions_involving_Pakistan) of UN peacekeepers) has, like the United States, expressed reservations about the potential use of politically motivated charges against peacekeepers.[^cite_note-176]

### South Sudan

[South Sudan](32350676.md)'s President [Salva Kiir Mayardit](2363203.md) said in 2013 that the country would not join the ICC.[^cite_note-177]

### Turkey

[Turkey](11125639.md) is currently a [candidate country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Turkey_to_the_European_Union) to join the [European Union](9317.md), which has required progress on human rights issues in order to continue with accession talks. Part of this has included pressure, but not a requirement, on Turkey to join the Court which is supported under the EU's [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy).[^cite_note-178] Turkish prime minister [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) stated in October 2004 that Turkey would "soon" ratify the Rome Statute,[^cite_note-179] and the Turkish constitution was amended in 2004 to explicitly allow nationals to be surrendered to the Court.[^cite_note-180] However, in January 2008, the Erdoğan government reversed its position, deciding to shelve accession because of concerns it could undermine efforts against the [Kurdistan Workers Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Workers_Party) (PKK).[^cite_note-181]
