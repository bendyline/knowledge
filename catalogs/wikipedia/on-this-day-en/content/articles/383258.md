The **Russian Navy**[^cite_note-4] is the [naval arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) of the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md). It has existed in various forms since 1696. Its present iteration was formed in January 1992 when it succeeded the Navy of the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) (which had itself succeeded the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy) following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in late December 1991).

The [Imperial Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Navy) was established by [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) (Peter I) in October 1696. The symbols of the Russian Navy, the [St. Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrew)'s ensign, and most of its traditions were established personally by Peter I.[^cite_note-5]

The Russian Navy possesses the vast majority of the former Soviet naval forces, and currently comprises the [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet), the [Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_\(Russia\)), the [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet), the [Baltic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet), the [Caspian Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Flotilla), the [permanent task force in the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_task_force_of_the_Russian_Navy_in_the_Mediterranean_Sea), [Naval Aviation](5698051.md), and the Coastal Troops (consisting of the [Naval Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Naval_Infantry) and the Coastal Missile and Artillery Troops).

The Russian Navy suffered severely with the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Soviet_Union) due to insufficient maintenance, lack of funding, and subsequent effects on the training of personnel and timely replacement of equipment. Another setback was attributed to Russia's domestic shipbuilding industry, which was in decline due to the absence of modern hardware and technology.

In 2013, a rise in gas and oil prices enabled a sort of renaissance of the Russian Navy due to increased available funds, which may have allowed Russia to begin "developing the capacity to modernize".[^cite_note-6] In August 2014, Defence Minister [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu) said that Russian naval capabilities would be bolstered with new weapons and equipment within the next six years in response to the [enlargement of NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO) and the beginning of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-reuters.com-7] Following the full-scale [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the Russian Navy has lost several ships, including the *[cruiser Moskva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Moskva)*, flagship of the Black Sea Fleet.&#x20;

As of September 2024 the Russia's only aircraft carrier, *[Admiral Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)*, is non-operational, with reports indicating that the carrier's crew of approximately 1,500 was reassigned to the [Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Army) for [combat duty in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). This suggests that, for the moment, there is no plan to make the Russian Navy a carrier navy once again.[^cite_note-forbes-2024-09-22-8]

The Russian Admiralty in [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg) is famed for its gilded steeple topped by a golden weather-vane in the shape of a sailing ship.

&#x20;The 1991 [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) led to a severe decline in the Russian Navy. [Defence expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_Russia) were severely reduced. Many ships were scrapped or laid up as accommodation ships at naval bases, and the building program was essentially stopped. [Sergey Gorshkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Gorshkov)'s buildup during the Soviet period had emphasised ships over support facilities, but Gorshkov had also retained ships in service beyond their effective lifetimes, so a reduction had been inevitable in any event.[^cite_note-9] The situation was exacerbated by the impractical range of vessel types which the Soviet [military-industrial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%E2%80%93industrial_complex), with the support of the leadership, had forced on the navy—taking modifications into account, the Soviet Navy in the mid-1980s had nearly 250 different classes of ship.[^cite_note-10]

The [*Kiev-*&#x63;lass aircraft carrying cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev-class_aircraft_carrier) and many other ships were prematurely retired, and the incomplete second [*Admiral Kuznetsov*-class aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuznetsov-class_aircraft_carrier) *[Varyag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_aircraft_carrier_Liaoning)* was eventually sold to the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) by Ukraine. Funds were only allocated for the completion of ships ordered prior to the collapse of the USSR, as well as for refits and repairs on fleet ships taken out of service since. However, the construction times for these ships tended to stretch out extensively: in 2003 it was reported that the [*Akula*-class submarine *Nerpa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Nerpa_\(K-152\)) had been under construction for fifteen years.[^cite_note-11]

Storage of decommissioned nuclear submarines in ports near [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk) became a significant issue, with the [Bellona Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellona_Foundation) reporting details of lowered readiness. Naval support bases outside Russia, such as [Cam Ranh Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Ranh_Bay) in Vietnam, were gradually closed, with the exception of the modest technical support base in [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus), [Syria](7515849.md) to support ships deployed to the Mediterranean. Naval Aviation declined as well from its height as [Soviet Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Naval_Aviation), dropping from an estimated 60,000 personnel with some 1,100 combat aircraft in 1992 to 35,000 personnel with around 270 combat aircraft in 2006.[^cite_note-12] In 2002, out of 584 naval aviation crews only 156 were combat ready, and 77 ready for night flying. Average annual flying time was 21.7 hours, compared to 24 hours in 1999.[^cite_note-13]

Training and readiness also suffered severely. In 1995, only two missile submarines at a time were being maintained on station, from the Northern and Pacific Fleets.[^cite_note-14] The decline culminated in the loss of the Oscar II-class submarine [*Kursk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk) during the Northern Fleet summer exercise that was intended to back up the publication of a new naval doctrine.[^cite_note-15]

As of February 2008, the Russian Navy had 44 nuclear submarines with 24 operational; 19 diesel-electric submarines, 16 operational; and 56 first and second rank surface combatants, 37 operational.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-16] Despite this improvement, the November [2008 accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-152_Nerpa_accident) on board the [*Akula*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akula-class_submarine) [submarine](28825.md) attack boat *Nerpa* during sea trials before lease to India represented a concern for the future.[^cite_note-17]

The strength and quality of the Russian Navy started to improve during the 2010s. From 2010-2014 Russian officials negotiated the purchase of four [*Mistral*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral-class_landing_helicopter_dock) [landing helicopter docks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_helicopter_dock). On 3 September 2014, French President announced that due to Russia's "[recent actions in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Russian_military_intervention_in_Ukraine)", the two ships would not be delivered.[^cite_note-BBC1-18] In November 2014, François Hollande placed a hold on the delivery of the first *Mistral* to Russia in view of the conflict in east Ukraine. Hollande set two conditions for delivery: the observation of a ceasefire in Ukraine and a political agreement between Moscow and Kiev.[^cite_note-19] On 5 August 2015 it was announced that France was to pay back Russia's partial payments and keep the two ships initially produced for Russia.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The ships eventually were sold to Egypt.

In 2012, as part of an ambitious rebuilding effort, [President](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) announced a plan to build 51 modern ships and 24 submarines by 2020.[^cite_note-22] Of the 24 submarines, 16 were to be nuclear-powered.[^cite_note-23] On 10 January 2013, the Russian Navy finally accepted its first new Borei-class SSBN ([*Yury Dolgorukiy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Yury_Dolgorukiy)) for service.[^cite_note-24] A second Borei (*[Aleksandr Nevskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Alexander_Nevsky_\(K-550\))*) was undergoing sea trials and entered service on 21 December 2013.[^cite_note-25]

A third *Borei*-class boat ([*Vladimir Monomakh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Vladimir_Monomakh)) was launched and began trials in early 2013, and was commissioned in late 2014.[^cite_note-26] As of early 2025, eight Borei-class boats are in service, along with five [Yasen-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasen-class_submarine) nuclear attack/cruise missile submarines. More vessels of these classes are building along with additional [Lada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada-class_submarine) and [Improved-Kilo-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilo-class_submarine#Project_636.3_units) conventional attack submarines. The surface fleet is also being modernized, principally by introducing at least [six new classes of corvette/offshore patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_Russian_Navy#Corvettes), a new class of frigate ([the *Admiral Gorshkov* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Gorshkov-class_frigate)), as well as new classes of amphibious ships and support vessels. In 2019, total tonnage of the Russian Navy stood at 1,216,547 tonnes.[^cite_note-27]

On 31 July 2022 during Russian Navy Day, Putin approved a new maritime doctrine for Russia. The new doctrine suggests an increased state focus on the Arctic and the [Northern Sea Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sea_Route), as well as an increased naval presence in the Mediterranean and Red Seas, both using already-established bases and establishing new bases on other territories in the area. Plans for the development of shipbuilding industries in Crimea were also mentioned, as well as the development of new [LNG terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas_terminal) and shipbuilding industries in the Far East. With the US and Allies identified as the major maritime threat, cooperation with India and Middle Eastern allies was also emphasised, as well as the necessity for increased foreign port visits by Russian Navy vessels.[^cite_note-newdoctrine2022-28] In 2023, Russian state media claimed that the Russian Navy received 3 submarines, 7 surface ships (including a modernized one), 33 multipurpose, raid and support vessels, 11 aircraft and helicopters, and also over 7,700 cruise, anti-ship and anti-submarine missiles.[^cite_note-29]

### Invasion of Ukraine and losses

See also: [List of ship losses during the Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ship_losses_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war)



[Slava-class cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slava-class_cruiser) [Moskva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Moskva), [sunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Moskva) in the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)

In 2022, the Russian Navy took part in the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), starting with the [attack on Snake Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Snake_Island) at the beginning of the war, at which the Ukrainian defenders told the [Russian cruiser *Moskva*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Moskva), flagship of the Black Sea; "[Russian warship, go fuck yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_warship,_go_fuck_yourself)",[^cite_note-m792-30][^cite_note-n641-31] before subsequently being captured by Russian forces. *Moskva* sank on 14 April 2022 after a fire broke out and forced the crew to evacuate. The Ukrainian military reported that they hit the ship with [Neptune anti-ship missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-360_Neptune), however the Russian military did not confirm this. The ship subsequently capsized and sank while the Russian Navy was attempting to tow her into port. The [sinking of *Moskva*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Moskva) is the most significant Russian naval loss in action since World War II.[^cite_note-32] In December 2023, the [Russian landing ship Novocherkassk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Novocherkassk) was also destroyed after being hit by Ukrainian cruise missiles.[^cite_note-33]

On 31 January 2024, Ukrainian [sea drones](7808557.md) struck the Russian [Tarantul-class corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarantul-class_corvette) [Ivanovets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_corvette_Ivanovets) in the Black Sea, causing the ship to sink.[^cite_note-The_Telegraph_2024_e989-34][^cite_note-Greenall_2024_j308-35] Two weeks later on 14 February, the same type of Ukrainian sea drones struck and sank the [Russian Landing ship Tsezar Kunikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ship_Tsezar_Kunikov).[^cite_note-Goryashko_2024_x892-36][^cite_note-Tom_Balmforth_2024_k016-37] On 5 March, the patrol boat Sergei Kotov was also lost to sea drones.[^cite_note-Murphy_2024_l650-38] While precise information on Russian ship losses during the war are not available, the fleet has lost at least four major warships (with others damaged), three major amphibious ships (with others damaged) and one submarine ([*Rostov-na-Donu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Rostov-na-Donu)), which was either seriously damaged or possibly destroyed while in dry dock.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] The fleet has continued to operate and attempted to adjust to Ukrainian tactics by integrating new defensive systems in ships and by redeploying vessels to the eastern Black Sea, or out of the region entirely, until sufficiently upgraded.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-Espreso-43][^cite_note-44] By early 2026, it was reported that, despite no longer having a navy, Ukraine's surface, sub-surface and aerial drone capabilities had effectively driven the Russian fleet from open waters, forcing it to seek shelter in ports on the eastern Black Sea that themselves were no longer fully secure.[^cite_note-45]

Since 2012 the headquarters of the Russian Navy (Russian Navy Main Staff) is once again located in The Admiralty in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md). Russian naval manpower is a mixture of conscripts serving one-year terms and volunteers (Officers and Ratings). In 2006 the [IISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IISS) assessed there were 142,000 personnel in the Russian Navy. This personnel number includes the Naval Infantry (Marines) and the Coastal Missile and Artillery Troops. As of 2008 the conscription term was reduced to one year and a major downsizing and reorganization were underway. In 2008, plans were announced to move the headquarters to the [Admiralty building in St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_building,_Saint_Petersburg), the historic location of the headquarters of the Imperial Russian Navy. The Navy Staff finally relocated there in November 2012.

The Russian Navy is organised into four combat services - the Surface Forces, the Submarine Forces, the Naval Aviation and the Coastal Troops.[^cite_note-46] Additionally the navy also includes support units afloat and ashore. It does not include special forces. The [Naval Spetsnaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_commando_frogmen) brigades are part of the [Main Intelligence Directorate](261749.md) attached to the respective fleets and the Counter-Diversionary Forces and Assets (*ПДСС*) (which are units, protecting the Navy from incursions of enemy special forces) fall within the Coastal Forces.

During the Cold War the Soviet Armed Forces made the distinction between the various naval commands. The main fleets were the [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet) and the [Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_\(Russia\)). They were tasked with independent operations on the high seas and for that reason included strategic surface, submarine and air forces, including the country's naval nuclear deterrent. Due to the limited geography of the Baltic and the Black Seas the respective [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet) and [Black Sea Fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) were given a more circumscribed role in support of adjacent ground formation (the Main Command of the Troops of the Western Direction in [Legnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legnica) ([Poland](22936.md)) and the Main Command of the Troops of the South-Western Direction in [Chișinău](56635.md)).

These two fleets were armed with shorter-range weapon systems than the main fleets (diesel-electric submarines, [Sukhoi Su-24](382335.md) fighter-bombers and a larger quantity of [frigates](11464.md) and [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette)). Due to the closed nature of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) (still connected to the Baltic and Black Seas through the [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) and the system of rivers and canals and navigable for ships of corvette size) its [Caspian Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Flotilla) had an even more limited role than the Fleets and played a defensive role supporting the Main Command of the Troops of the Southern Direction in [Baku](4566.md).

With the end of the [Cold War](325329.md) a significant reduction in forces followed. Before the [2008 Russian military reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Russian_military_reform), the four fleets were ranked as equal in status to the six Military Districts. With the reform measures going into force the number of Military Districts was reduced and became new Joint Strategic Commands and the four fleets and one flotilla were subordinated to them with status equal to the Ground Forces and the Air Forces armies. Due to Russia's increased interests in the Arctic region and the importance of Russia's western/northwestern maritime defence the Northern Fleet, originally part of the Joint Strategic Command West (Western Military District), on 12 December 2014 became the basis for the newly formed fifth [Northern Fleet Joint Strategic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet_Joint_Strategic_Command_\(Russia\)).

In 2024, amid increasing tensions with NATO, and owing to a significantly weakened position for Russia in northern Europe, resulting from the [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden%E2%80%93NATO_relations) and [Finnish entry into NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland-NATO_relations), both the [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet) and [Baltic Fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet) were subordinated to the command of the [Leningrad Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Military_District). The Military District was now responsible for the command of all Russian ground, sea and air forces stretching from [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad) to the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean). The Russian Black Sea Fleet and the Caspian Flotilla remained subordinate to the [Southern Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Military_District) while the Pacific Fleet was subordinate to the [Eastern Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Military_District).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Submarine and surface forces



[Oscar II class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar-class_submarine) cruise missile submarine *Tomsk*

The submarine and surface forces form the backbone of the Navy. Submarines can form part of dedicated submarine squadrons and flotillas or, alternatively, they can operate as part of squadrons and flotillas of mixed composition together with major and/or minor surface combatants.

Over the past two decades there has been an attempt to modernize and update the fleet by emphasizing the production and introduction of modern light units to begin to replace large numbers of obsolescent corvettes, missile boats and mine counter-measures ships from the Soviet-era. In addition, there has been significant emphasis on submarine production with the introduction of nuclear-powered ballistic missile, nuclear-powered cruise missile as well as new classes of conventionally-powered attack submarines. This trend is continuing through the 2020s, particularly in relation to the submarine component of [Russia's nuclear deterrent forces](588941.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Coastal troops

Main articles: [Coastal Troops of the Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Troops_of_the_Russian_Navy) and [Russian Naval Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Naval_Infantry)



Russian Naval Infantry during the Vostok Strategic Exercise in the [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) area, 2010



Russian Naval Infantrymen

Coastal Troops include the Naval Infantry and the Coastal Missile and Artillery Troops.

The Russian Naval Infantry are the [amphibious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) force of the Russian Navy and can trace their origins back to 1705, when [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) issued a decree for an infantry [regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment) "of naval equipage". Since its formation it has seen action in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War), the First and Second World Wars, and the Chechen and Georgian conflicts. Under the leadership of [Admiral Gorshkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Gorshkov) during the Cold War, the Soviet Navy expanded the reach of the Naval Infantry and deployed it worldwide on numerous occasions, but since the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) its role has been greatly reduced.

The Soviet Naval Infantry and their Russian successors have a reputation as elite shock troops. For their black uniforms and ferocious performance in combat in the Black Sea and Baltic Sea regions during [World War II](32927.md) they received the nickname "The Black Death" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *der schwarze Tod*). The Russian Naval Infantry is a mechanised force, organised in brigades, independent regiments and independent battalions. The 55th Naval Infantry Division of the Pacific Fleet has been disbanded in 2009 and replaced by two separate brigades. Each brigade has a tank battalion, a self-propelled artillery battalion, a self-propelled air defence battalion, mechanised marine infantry battalions, other support units and one Airborne Assault Naval Infantry Battalion (*десантно-штурмовой батальон морской пехоты*), parachute and [air assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_assault) qualified, with the mission to spearhead amphibious landings.

The coastal defence troops of the Russian Navy are conventional mechanised brigades with the main task to prevent enemy amphibious landings. An example of coastal defence troops are those of the Baltic Fleet. With Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania declaring independence at the end of the Soviet Union the [Baltic Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Military_District) practically disintegrated. The massive ground forces formations left landlocked in the [Kaliningrad Oblast](17311.md) were transferred from the ground forces to naval command and control. The integration of naval infantry and coastal defence troops is a relatively new tendency from the 2010s in order to simplify the naval command structure and the new Arctic infantry brigades in formation under the [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet) fall within that process.&#x20;

As of 2022, there appeared to be a longer-term intent to expand all Russian naval infantry brigades into division-sized formations, coupled with the planned acquisition of both the new [Ivan Rogov-class helicopter assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_23900_amphibious_assault_ship) as well as upgraded [Ivan Gren-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Gren-class_landing_ship) landing ships. However, full implementation of such a plan would likely have to await the conclusion of the Russo-Ukraine War.[^cite_note-54]

The coastal artillery troops also play a very important role for the Navy. The geography of the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea), the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), the [Black Sea](3386.md) and the [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk) as well as the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) makes the deployment of shore-based, anti-ship systems in an area-denying role very effective. They deploy [K-300P Bastion-P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-300P_Bastion-P) supersonic [ASCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ship_cruise_missile), [3M-54 Kalibr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M-54_Kalibr) cruise subsonic ASCM and [A-222E Bereg-E 130mm coastal mobile artillery system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-222E_Bereg-E_130mm_coastal_mobile_artillery_system) as well as self-propelled [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) systems.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The Naval Infantry and Coastal Troops are led by the Deputy Commander for Naval Infantry/Commandant of the Naval Infantry of the Russian Navy, [Lieutenant General](201916.md) (NI) Aleksandr Kolpatsenko. Their motto is: "Where We Are, There is Victory!"

### Naval aviation

Main article: [Russian Naval Aviation](5698051.md)



A [Sukhoi Su-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-33) from the 279th Shipborne Fighter Aviation Regiment on *[Admiral Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)'*&#x73; flight deck

Since the fall of the Soviet Union Russia's naval aviation has been significantly reduced in size. The Tu-22Ms have been transferred to the Aerospace Forces and since then the combat arm of the Naval Aviation is built around [Sukhoi Su-33s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-33), [Mikoyan MiG-29Ks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_MiG-29K), with [Sukhoi Su-30s](552808.md) and [Sukhoi Su-34s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-34) replacing the obsolete [Sukhoi Su-24s](382335.md). Russia's sole aircraft carrier, [*Admiral Flota Sovetskogo Soyuza Kuznetsov* (063)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov), has also been inactive since 2017 and, as of 2025, has been reported as unlikely to return to operational service.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] The carrier's fixed-wing aircraft were then re-deployed to a land-based role.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

As of 2025, the Russian Naval Aviation consists of the following components:[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-AFM65-64]

- Naval missile-carrying aviation

- Shore-based [ASW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) aviation

- [Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft) (*Shturmovik*) aviation

- Shore-based [fighter](10929.md) aviation

- [Reconnaissance](146911.md) aviation

- Shipborne aviation (principally ASW helicopters)

- Auxiliary air units

As of 2025, aircraft losses during the Russo-Ukraine War, for both the [Russian Aerospace Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Aerospace_Force) and shore-based Russian naval aviation, have included at least seven [Sukhoi Su-30SMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-30) and between 10 and 20 of the older [Su-24MRs](382335.md). Russia's total inventory of approximately 110 Su-30s, and reserve stocks of at least 100 Su-24s, may allow for some of these losses to be absorbed but they have not been insignificant.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

### Bastion concept, sensor and radar systems

Historically, the Russian navy has emphasized the creation of [submarine bastions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_\(naval\)), both to protect the security of its submarine-based nuclear forces and to secure the Arctic and Pacific approaches to Russian territory. In order to strengthen its submarine bastion in the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea), in 2025 it was reported that Russia has likely deployed a network of sonar systems in that region in order to detect submarines. The network, known as "Harmony", is reported to be composed of detection devices arrayed in an arc stretching from Murmansk, via [Novaya Zemlja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Zemlja) to [Franz Josef Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Josef_Land).[^cite_note-70]

The Russian Navy uses Podsolnukh over-the-horizon surface wave radar for detection of ships. As of 2019, four radars have been delivered to the coasts of Caspian Sea, Okhotsk Sea, Sea of Japan[^cite_note-71] and Baltic Sea.[^cite_note-72]

## Military districts and fleets

The Russian Navy consists of four fleets and one flotilla with all of them subordinated to different Military Districts.

### Leningrad Military District

#### Northern Fleet

Main article: [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet)



Major bases and headquarters of the Northern Fleet



Russian Navy sailors onboard the cruiser [*Marshal Ustinov*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Marshal_Ustinov) in a joint exercise of the [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet) and [Black Sea fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet)

The Russian Northern Fleet, dating to 1733 but established as a modern formation in 1933, is headquartered at [Severomorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severomorsk) and spread around various bases in the greater [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk) area. It is the main fleet of the Russian Navy.[^cite_note-warfare.ru-73] Since 2024, the fleet has been subordinate to the [Leningrad Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Military_District).[^cite_note-74]

The Black Sea Fleet, established on 2 May 1783, is based at the Sevastopol, Karantinnaya, and Streletskaya Bays in [Sevastopol](51587.md) which is also the location of its headquarters, and at [Novorossiysk](1190336.md) in Krasnodar Kray. Since the start of the [Russo-Ukraine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukraine_War), the Black Sea Fleet has experienced serious losses in ships and materiel. In the initial stages of the war, the fleet's flagship, the cruiser [*Moskva*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Moskva), was [sunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Moskva) on 14 April 2022 during the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-76] Other losses, including the [Tapir-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapir-class_landing_ship) landing ship *[Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Saratov)* followed.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-warfare.ru-73] For a broader list of Russian and Ukrainian ship losses in the war see:&#x20;

While the fleet has attempted to continue operations, information on its precise order of battle is necessarily imprecise and Ukrainian surface, sub-surface and aerial drone capabilities have increasingly driven the fleet from open waters.[^cite_note-78]

#### Operational Command South - Tartus

Main article: [Russian naval facility in Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_naval_facility_in_Tartus)

Main article: [Permanent task force of the Russian Navy in the Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_task_force_of_the_Russian_Navy_in_the_Mediterranean_Sea)

The Russian Navy has maintained a base under the command of the Southern Military District in [Syria](7515849.md) at [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus). The Mediterranean squadron was disestablished soon after the collapse of the Soviet Union, but a small naval logistics support facility remained there. In January 2017 Russia and Syria signed an agreement, to be valid for 49 years, to expand the Tartus facility with a view to improving the support at Tartus.[^cite_note-rabs-79]

On 4 February 2022, naval detachments from the Northern Fleet and Baltic Fleet arrived at Tartus. Large amphibious assault ships [*Pyotr Morgunov*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Pyotr_Morgunov), [*Georgy Pobedonosets*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Georgy_Pobedonosets), *[Olenegorsky Gornyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olenegorsky_Gornyak)*, [*Korolyov*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Korolyov), [*Minsk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Minsk) and *Kaliningrad* were under the direction of Russian Navy Commander-in-Chief Admiral [Nikolay Yevmenov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Yevmenov).[^cite_note-tass22-80] In April 2022 there were around 20 Russian naval ships in the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md).[^cite_note-tarf24-81] On 11 May it was reported that the flotilla had remained without change since early February. There were 13 ships and 5 support vessels of the four Russian fleets, including 9 attack missile ships. The formation of a "Mediterranean Squadron" of the Russian Navy in February 2022, using the Northern, Baltic, and Pacific Fleets' ships allowed the Russian Black Sea Fleet to return a large number of ships to the Black Sea some time prior to Russia’s attack on Ukraine. On 27 February three days after the commencement of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) Turkey (who acts as guarantor of the [Montreux Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Convention)) decided to ban the passage through the straits of any warships whose homeport is not in the Black Sea.[^cite_note-bsn11.5-82]

As of mid-2025, the loss of regular use of the [Russian base at the port of Tartus in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus_naval_base), as a result of the [Fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) in late 2024, has significantly impacted the ability of the Russian navy to maintain an effective presence in the Mediterranean region. As of mid-2025, Russia and Syria were reportedly engaged in negotiations about the future of Russian naval and air facilities in the country though it remained unclear how extensive Russia's military presence in Syria would be going forward.[^cite_note-83] As of March 2026, the Russian navy appeared to be using the naval facility intermittently though more regularized use going forward seemed probable.[^cite_note-84]

In mid-2026 it was reported that there were no major Russian warships currently in the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-85] Nevertheless, in August 2026 it was announced that an agreement had been signed between Russia and Syria governing future Russian use of the bases[^cite_note-86] although Russia also seemed increasingly to be looking at the ports of [Tobruk](405871.md) in [Libya](17633.md) as well as [Algiers](1644.md) as more consistent ports of call.[^cite_note-87]

## Future and modernization



[Lada-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada-class_submarine) diesel-electric submarine [*Sankt Petersburg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Sankt_Peterburg_\(B-585\))

Russia's military budget expanded from 1998 until 2015, but economic problems including a sharp decline in oil prices led to budget cuts in 2016.[^cite_note-90] Higher expenditure led to an increase in numbers of ships under construction, initially focusing on submarines, such as the conventional [*Petersburg* (*Lada*)-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada-class_submarine) and nuclear [*Severodvinsk* (*Yasen*)-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasen-class_submarine). Some older vessels have been refitted as well.&#x20;

According to the Russian Defence Ministry, share of modern armament in the Navy has reached more than 50% in 2014.[^cite_note-91] A report from December 2019 estimated the figure at 68%.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] However, in September 2020 it was reported that the defence budget was to be cut by 5% as part of a shift to social spending and in response the financial impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-overtdefense.com-96] The resulting impact of such a cut on Russian Navy modernization plans was not immediately apparent.&#x20;

Likely more significant is the impact of sanctions imposed on the Russian Federation after 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine. In 2021, Russian Security Council Secretary [Nikolai Patrushev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Patrushev) reportedly acknowledged that the Russian defence industry “is still dependent on foreign technologies.” The impact of [international sanctions](70323386.md) on naval procurement projects, given both reduced access to foreign technologies and significant pressure on the defence budget, had yet to be determined.[^cite_note-97]



[Admiral Gorshkov-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Gorshkov-class_frigate) guided missile frigate [*Admiral Gorshkov*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_frigate_Admiral_Gorshkov), commissioned in 2018



A [Karakurt-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakurt-class_corvette) guided-missile corvette, capable of launching [Kalibr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibr_\(missile_family\)) or [Oniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-800_Oniks) supersonic cruise missiles



[Borei-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borei-class_submarine) nuclear ballistic missile submarine [*Alexander Nevsky*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Alexander_Nevsky_\(K-550\))

Since the 2010s, the primary focus of production for the Russian navy has been on new classes of lighter units such as [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) and [frigates](11464.md) as well as on the procurement of several classes of new nuclear and conventionally-powered submarines. In addition, the new [*Admiral Gorshkov*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Gorshkov-class_frigate) have begun to enter service and the large [Priboy-class helicopter assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_23900_amphibious_assault_ship) have also started construction. These programs are expected to continue through the 2020s and, depending on available funding, projects to acquire larger frigates (the *Super-Gorshkov* - Project 22350M) and destroyers/cruisers ([Lider-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lider-class_destroyer)) may also be initiated. However, until the arrival of such heavier units, older classes of Soviet-era cruisers and destroyers are being modernized.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] As of 2026, the Russian Navy has not been able to start construction on large surface combatants such as destroyers. The Russian shipbuilding industry also currently does not have the ability to build an aircraft carrier.[^cite_note-USM25_Carrier-100]

### Submarine projects as of 2026

#### Borei SSBN



[*Yury Dolgorukiy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Yury_Dolgorukiy) is a [Borei-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borei-class_submarine), one of the two nuclear submarine classes (the other being the [Yasen-class attack submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasen-class_submarine)) the Russian Navy has ordered.

Before 2012 there were only about ten nuclear submarine patrols a year, each lasting three months at most and usually a lot less.[^cite_note-101]

However, since that time considerable emphasis has been placed on strengthening the submarine component of [Russia's nuclear forces](588941.md).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] In 2013, the first unit of the [Borei-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borei-class_submarine) [ballistic missile submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) entered service. Three *Borei*-class boats were initially built. The lead boat, [*Yuriy Dolgorukiy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Yury_Dolgorukiy), was launched in April 2007, began sea trials in June 2009 and was commissioned as a part of the Northern Fleet in 2012.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

As of the end of 2025, there are eight units of the *Borei*-class in active service and an additional two boats are under construction. Two further vessels are planned for construction and they are to be in service by the early 2030s.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] In order to strengthen its [submarine bastion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_\(naval\)) in the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea), in 2025 it was reported that Russia has likely deployed a network of sonar systems in the region in order to detect submarines. The network, known as "Harmony", is reported to be composed of detection devices arrayed in an arc stretching from Murmansk, via [Novaya Zemlja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Zemlja) to [Franz Josef Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Josef_Land).[^cite_note-109]

#### Yasen SSGN



K-560 *Severodvinsk*

12 [Yasen-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasen-class_submarine) nuclear attack cruise missile-armed submarines are planned for delivery to the Russian Navy. [*Severodvinsk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Severodvinsk) (keel [laid down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keel_laying) on 21 December 1993), was the first boat of the class slated for launch in 1998 delayed due to problems in financing. In 1996 work on the submarine appeared to have stopped completely. In 2003, the project received additional funding and the work of finishing the submarine continued. In 2004 it was reported that the work on the submarine was moving forward, but due to the priority given to the *Borei*-class submarines, *Severodvinsk*, the lead unit of the *Yasen*-class, would not be ready before 2010. In July 2006 the deputy chairman of the Military-Industrial Commission, Vladislav Putilin, stated that two *Yasen*-class submarines were to join the Russian Navy before 2015.[^cite_note-110] On 24 July 2009 the work on a second Yasen submarine, named [*Kazan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kazan_\(K-561\)), was started. Initial sea trials for the first boat of the class (*Severodvinsk*) took place in the fall of 2011.[^cite_note-111]

As of the end of 2025, *Severodvinsk,* *Kazan*, [*Novosibirsk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Novosibirsk), [*Krasnoyarsk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Krasnoyarsk_\(K-571\)) and [*Arkhangelsk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Arkhangelsk_\(K-564\)) are operational while [*Perm*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Perm) began sea trials in mid-2025.[^cite_note-112] There were four more submarines of the class under construction as of mid-2026, with more planned.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] In 2026, the Commander-in-Chief of the Russian Navy, Admiral Alexander Moiseyev, stated that the Russian navy aims to replace all of its Project 971, 945, and 949 submarines with between 12 and 14 *Yasen*-class vessels by 2035.[^cite_note-115] Since this would result in a less than one-for-one replacement of older vessels, it remained unclear whether a further class of submarine, notably the [Laika-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laika-class_submarine), would be built on a sufficiently timely basis as well.

#### Improved Kilo SSK

Following on from the success of the Kilo-class submarines, improved design Project 636.3 units are being built for the Russian Navy. As of 2025, six [Improved Kilo-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improved_Kilo-class_submarine) have been built for the Black Sea Fleet, six for the Pacific Fleet, and further units are on order for either the Northern or Baltic Fleets.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

In an interview on 15 August 2022, the head of [United Shipbuilding Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Shipbuilding_Corporation) Alexei Rakhmanov stated that the company is currently producing one Project 636.3 submarine per year, however this can be increased to two per year with more funding. He also stated that a few modernisation proposals are being considered, including the ability to carry more [Kalibr cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M-54_Kalibr).[^cite_note-uscinterview_aug2022-118]

#### Lada and Amur SSK

The [Lada-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada-class_submarine) (Project 677) began construction in the latter 1990s. The class experienced significant problems and delays, resulting in a halt to series production and a redesign of the vessels. Series production resumed in the mid-2010s and as of 2025 two vessels are in service and three more are under construction or ordered; up to nine may be planned. In 2023, the lead (prototype) unit ([Sankt Peterburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Sankt_Peterburg)) was decommissioned owing to several years of developmental problems which appear never to have been successfully rectified.[^cite_note-119]

On 18 May 2013 Russian Navy Commander-in-Chief Admiral [Viktor Chirkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Chirkov) announced that the *Lada*-class would receive an air-independent propulsion system by 2016–2017. However, in 2019, Alexander Buzakov, the head of the Admiralty Shipyard, indicated that there were no plans to equip the Lada class with an air-independent propulsion system.[^cite_note-120] The [Amur class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur-class_submarine) is advertised as an export version of the Lada class.

#### Haski (Husky) SSN

The **Laika class**, Russian designation [**Project 545 *Laika***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laika-class_submarine) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Лайка),[^cite_note-Laika-121] also referred to as **Husky class** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Хаски, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) '[husky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husky)'),[^cite_note-Husky-122] is a series of [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_propulsion) fifth-generation multi-purpose submarines currently under development by [Malakhit Marine Engineering Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakhit_Marine_Engineering_Bureau) for the [Russian Navy](383258.md).[^cite_note-tass_rakhmanov-123][^cite_note-aif_husky-124]

In an interview on 15 August 2022, the head of [United Shipbuilding Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Shipbuilding_Corporation) Alexei Rakhmanov confirmed that the development of fifth-generation submarines is continuing according to schedule. It is unknown if he was referring to the [Laika-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laika-class_submarine), or an as-of-yet (August 2022) unannounced project.[^cite_note-uscinterview_aug2022-118]

***Khabarovsk*** (Project 09851) & ***Ulyanovsk*** (Project 09853) **Special Operations Submarines**

Following on the delivery of the [*Belgorod*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Belgorod) special operations submarine to the Russian Navy in July 2022, the [*Khabarovsk* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk-class_submarine) is a new submarine class based on the Borei class but with missile tubes removed. She was launched in November 2025 and is to be armed with [Status-6 Oceanic Multipurpose System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status-6_Oceanic_Multipurpose_System).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

Another submarine, *Ulyanovsk*, which had previously been identified as a separate class but is a variant of the *Yasen*-class, was launched in July 2026.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

### Surface Warship Projects

#### Amphibious operation vessels



&#x20;Official model of the [Ivan Rogov-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Rogov-class_amphibious_assault_ship) [amphibious assault ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship)

From 2010 to 2014 Russian officials negotiated the purchase of four [*Mistral*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral-class_amphibious_assault_ship) [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship). On 3 September 2014, French President announced that due to Russia's "[recent actions in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Russian_military_intervention_in_Ukraine)", the two ships would not be delivered.[^cite_note-BBC1-18] In November 2014, François Hollande placed a hold on the delivery of the first *Mistral* to Russia in view of the conflict in east Ukraine. Hollande set two conditions for delivery: the observation of a ceasefire in Ukraine and a political agreement between Moscow and Kiev.[^cite_note-129] On 5 August 2015 it was announced that France was to pay back Russia's partial payments and keep the two ships initially produced for Russia.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] The ships eventually were sold to Egypt.

In June 2017, the construction of two future amphibious assault ships for the Russian Navy was included in Russia's new state armament programme for 2018–2025.[^cite_note-132] The cost for one ship is to be about 40 billion RUB (\$675 million).[^cite_note-133] The new class of vessel is the [Project 23900 *Ivan Rogov*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_23900_amphibious_assault_ship) and as of 2025 is under construction at the [Zalyv Shipbuilding yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalyv_Shipbuilding_yard) in Crimea.[^cite_note-134] The design calls for a ship of more than 30,000 tons with the delivery of both units anticipated in the latter 2020s/early 2030s.[^cite_note-135]

In January 2018, it was reported the construction of the ships would take place at the [Severnaya Verf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severnaya_Verf) in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md).[^cite_note-136] However, in July 2020 two units of the class were laid down at the Zalyv Shipbuilding yard, which made them potentially vulnerable to attack following the outbreak of the Russo-Ukraine War.

The Russian navy has suffered serious losses of amphibious vessels in the Russo-Ukraine War. At least three Black Sea Fleet vessels, the [Alligator-class landing ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alligator-class_landing_ship) *[Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Saratov)* and the [Ropucha-class landing ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ropucha-class_landing_ship) [*Tsezar Kunikov*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ship_Tsezar_Kunikov) and *[Novocherkassk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_landing_ship_Novocherkassk)*, have been confirmed as sunk: *Sartov* in March 2022 attack at [Berdyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdyansk), *Novocherkassk* in a cruise missile strike in December 2023,[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] and *Tsezar Kunikov* in February 2024 attack off Crimea.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] In addition at least one Project 02510 *BK-16* assault boat is claimed to have been destroyed by a [Bayraktar TB2 drone](62445596.md) at Snake Island in 2022.[^cite_note-141] Another *BK-16* was reported to have been attacked and destroyed or damaged in August 2025.[^cite_note-142] Russia may be attempting to replace these losses by increasing the production and deployment of additional *BK-16s* as well as Project 02450 *BK-10s*.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

As of 2025, additional and upgraded amphibious landing ships of the [Project 11711 *Ivan Gren*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Gren-class_landing_ship) (in addition to two already in service) were also reported to be under construction.[^cite_note-145]

#### Frigates



[Admiral Gorshkov-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Gorshkov-class_frigates)

The Russian Navy has been developing the [Project 22350 (Gorshkov-class) frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_22350_frigate) for a considerable period of time, together with a larger variant identified as the *Project 22350M* (*Super Gorshkov*) frigate. Three *Gorshkov*-class frigates are in commission and active as of 2025, all with the [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet). Additionally, three frigates of the [Project 11356R Admiral Grigorovich-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_11356R_frigate) were also built for the [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) with the other vessels of the class being sold abroad. Two other vessel classes (the [Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette) and the [Project 20385 Gremyashchiy-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_20385) corvettes) are identified as "frigates" by NATO due to their size, armament and role though the Russian Navy classifies them as [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette).[^cite_note-146] As of 2025, three classes of frigates/corvettes (the *Project 22350*, *Project 20380* & *Project 20385* classes) are in production for the Russian Navy, though the larger *Project 22350M* variant of the *Gorshkov*-class, remains in development. Production of the *Project 11356* frigates has been completed.

Since the start of the [Russo-Ukraine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukraine_War), the production of both the *Gorshkov* and *Gremyashchiy*-classes has been impeded by western sanctions since both classes have been dependent on the supply of German and Ukrainian engines.[^cite_note-147] Projects 20385 (as well as the [Project 21631 (the Buyan-class corvette)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyan-M-class_corvette)) used German diesel engines, while the 22350 and 11356 used Ukrainian-assembled turbines.[^cite_note-148] Given the sanctions and standoff over the Russo-Ukrainian War, these components were not available after 2015. As a result, two more [*Steregushchiy*-class corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette), the seventh and eighth of the class, were laid down in February 2015 since this class of vessel uses Russian-built Kolomna engines. Similarly, the power plant in units of the *Admiral Gorshkov* class (following on the first two) were adapted to use a CODAG diesel and gas turbine engine of Russian manufacture (UEC-Saturn).[^cite_note-149] In November 2020 it was announced that United Engine Corporation had initiated delivery of the DGTA M55R diesel-gas power plant which would be installed on frigates of the 22350-class beginning with *Admiral Isakov*.[^cite_note-150] *Admiral Isakov* is the fourth vessel of the Gorshkov-class and is reported "fitting out" for the [Russian Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_\(Russia\)) as of 2025.

## Main Naval Parade in St. Petersburg

Main articles: [Navy Day (Russia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Day_\(Russia\)) and [Culture of the Russian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Russian_Armed_Forces)



Passage of warships to [Kronstadt](763125.md)

Since 2017, by the decree of the President of Russia dated 27 July 2017,[^cite_note-157] the tradition of holding the "Main Naval Parade" in St. Petersburg on the Navy Day has been restored. Prior to 2017, in Soviet and Russian Federation times the previously held annual St. Petersburg Navy Day parade was not so specifically named. The parade is composed of ships and sailors representing the several fleets and the Caspian Flotilla with small ships and submarines in the Neva River and the larger ones arrayed off Kronshtadt in the Gulf of St. Petersburg. It is celebrated annually on the last Sunday of July during the [Navy Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Day_\(Russia\)) holiday.

## Deployments from 2018



The nuclear-powered missile cruiser *Peter the Great* during a naval exercise

### Ocean Shield

In the years 2018–2021, the Russian Navy has been organizing central annual naval exercise called Ocean Shield.

Between 1–8 September 2018, Ocean Shield exercise was held for the first time. Unlike 2019 and 2020 exercises, conducted in the Baltic Sea, the first exercise took place in the Mediterranean Sea. 26 ships, 2 submarines and 34 aircraft were included.[^cite_note-158] Among participants were cruiser *Marshal Ustinov*, destroyers *Smetlivy* and *Severomorsk*, frigates *Admiral Grigorovich*, *Admiral Essen*, *Admiral Makarov*, *Pytlivy* and *Yaroslav Mudry*, corvettes *Vishny Volochyok*, *Grad Sviyazhsk* and *Veliky Ustyug* and conventional submarines *Kolpino* and *Velikiy Novgorod*.[^cite_note-rg.ru-159]

The aircraft present included Tu-160 bombers, Tu-142 and Il-38 anti-submarine aircraft and Su-33 and MiG-29K maritime fighters. This was the largest Russian naval exercise in the Mediterranean Sea of the post-Cold War era and the largest Russian post-Cold War naval exercise in the far sea zone. In terms of distant location and number of capital ships participating it's comparable only to June 2021 exercises of the Pacific Fleet off the Hawaii islands.[^cite_note-rg.ru-159]

Organized between 1–9 August 2019, the second Ocean Shield exercise was the exercise with the largest number of participating ships (69 ships, including 49 warships and 20 support ships) in the independent Russia and took place in the Baltic Sea. The 22 known ships of the exercise "Ocean Shield 2019" include cruiser *Marshal Ustinov*, destroyer *Severomorsk* and frigate *Admiral Gorshkov* of the Northern Fleet, as well as Baltic Fleet's frigate *Yaroslav Mudry*, corvettes *Steregushchy*, *Soobrazitelny*,[^cite_note-160] *Stoykiy*, *Boikiy*, *Passat*, *Geyzer*, *Serpukhov*, *Mitishchi*, *Chuvashiya*, *Morshansk*, *Liven*, *Urengoy*, R-257 and LSTs *Aleksandr Shabalin*, *Kaliningrad*, *Minsk* and *Korolyov*,[^cite_note-161] as well as nuclear submarine *Smolensk*.[^cite_note-162] Other possible participants include ships, participating in the July Naval Parade in St. Petersburg, i.e. frigate *Admiral Kasatonov*, corvettes *Gremyashchy* and *Sovetsk*, submarine *Kronshtadt* and minesweepers *Ivan Antonov*, *Aleksandr Obukhov* and *Pavel Khenov*.[^cite_note-163]

On 3 August 2020, third Ocean Shield exercise started in the Baltic Sea and included Northern Fleet's destroyer *Vice-Admiral Kulakov* and LST *Pyotr Morgunov* and Baltic Fleet's corvettes *Steregushchy*, *Boikiy* and *Stoikiy*.[^cite_note-164]

### Combined-fleet exercises

#### June 2021

In 2021, no usual Ocean Shield exercise was conducted in August or September in the Baltic Sea. However, in June, the Pacific, Northern and Black Sea fleets conducted large-scale exercises. In mid June, four Russian cruisers and four destroyers were simultaneously at sea, or all operational large surface combatants except destroyer *Severomorsk*, which was probably the first time in the post-Cold war era. A complex large-scale exercise took place in the central Pacific Ocean, where the Russian Navy conducted possibly the strongest exercise of the post-Soviet era.

It took place prior to the 2021 Putin-Biden summit, similarly to Aport and Atrina exercises that were held in 1985 and 1987 prior to the Geneva and Washington summits of Gorbachev and Reagan to improve Soviet negotiation position. Officially, however, it was stated that the exercise is an answer to the exercise Agile Dagger 2021 of the US Pacific Fleet, employing one third of the operational submarines of the US Pacific Fleet.

In the Barents Sea, cruisers *Marshal Ustinov* and *Pyotr Veliky*, destroyer *Vice-admiral Kulakov* and submarines *Kaluga*, *Gepard* and *Dmitry Donskoy* were active.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169]



The location of the exercise in the Pacific Ocean in the vicinity of 23°, -170°

Between 7 and 24 June, a large-scale exercise was conducted by the Pacific Fleet in the central Pacific Ocean, being the first post-Cold war Russian naval exercise in that area (minor exceptions being destroyer *Admiral Panteleev* taking part in RIMPAC-2012 exercise and frigate *Admiral Gorshkov* sailing near Hawaii in 2019[^cite_note-TASS-170]). It included cruiser *Varyag*, destroyers *Marshal Shaposhnikov* and *Admiral Panteleyev*, corvettes *Sovershenny*, *Gromky* and *Aldar Tsydenzhapov*,[^cite_note-171] (a) nuclear submarine(s) (likely *Omsk*[^cite_note-172] and *Kuzbass*[^cite_note-173]) and intelligence ship *Kareliya*.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

The exercise started in the central Pacific Ocean on 10 June, and on 21 June the ships 2500 nautical miles southeast of the Kuril islands simulated an attack on the enemy carrier strike group. Prior to that, the ships operated in two groups, sailing at 300 nautical miles from each other, one of them playing the role of enemy. The largest auxiliary ship of the Russian Navy *Marshal Krylov* also took part in the exercise and acted as a command ship for the commander of the exercise, rear admiral Konstantin Kabantsev, commander of Primorskaya Flotilla, as well as hospital ship *Irtysh*[^cite_note-176] and MiG-31 interceptors and Il-38 and Tu-142 anti-submarine aircraft.

On 24 June, the final day of the exercise, three Tu-95 bombers, several Tu-22M bombers, escorted by interceptors MiG-31BM and two Il-78 tankers flew to the central Pacific Ocean as well. The Tu-95s delivered conditional strikes against enemy's critical infrastructure and Tu-22M delivered strikes against enemy's conditional carrier strike group together with *Varyag* and *Marshal Shaposhnikov*.[^cite_note-177]

An additional destroyer *Admiral Tributs* was deployed to the South China Sea, accompanying nuclear submarine *Nerpa*.

On 18 June 2021, the Black Sea Fleet deployed cruiser *Moskva* to the Mediterranean Sea,[^cite_note-178] which, amid deployment of [*Queen Elizabeth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Queen_Elizabeth_\(R08\)) to the Eastern Mediterranean, took part in an unprecedented anti-ship exercise with bombers Tu-22M and interceptors MiG-31K both deployed to Syria for the first time in May and June. A MiG-31K reportedly fired a [Kinzhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-47M2_Kinzhal) missile against a ground target in Syria,[^cite_note-179] while a newest air defence system [S-500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-500_missile_system) was reportedly tested at Khmeymim airbase and obtained a lock on F-35 fighter from *Queen Elizabeth*.[^cite_note-180] The exercise included rocket fire 30 km away from *Queen Elizabeth*.[^cite_note-181]

In January–February 2022 large-scale exercise of all Russian fleets took place with some 140 warships and support ships.

In the Northern Fleet it included two task groups. In the Pacific Fleet, cruiser *Varyag*, destroyer *Admiral Tributs* and tanker *Boris Butoma* were deployed to the Indian Ocean, participating in the third Russo-Sino-Iranian naval exercise, first Russo-Chinese naval exercise away from Russian/Chinese coast that took place in the western Indian Ocean and will finally strengthen Mediterranean squadron. In the Baltic Fleet, corvettes *Soobrazitelny* and *Stoykiy* were deployed to the Northern Sea,[^cite_note-188] while *Zeleny Dol*, *Mytishchi*, *Odintsovo*, *Aleksin*, *Kabardino-Balkariya*[^cite_note-189] were active in the Baltic Sea.[^cite_note-190] Additionally, three LSTs were deploying to the Black Sea: *Korolyov*, *Minsk* and *Kaliningrad* and intelligence ship *Vasily Tatishchev* to the Mediterranean Sea, where it will monitor three-carrier exercise with CVN *Harry Truman*, *Cavour* and *Charles de Gaulle* in February 2022.[^cite_note-191]

Since 2012, Russia and China have conducted an annual naval exercise.[^cite_note-241] In even years, they take place off Chinese coast (usually in the Yellow Sea), and in odd years off Russian coast (usually in the Sea of Japan). In 2015 and 2017, in addition to the exercises in the Sea of Japan, additional exercises in the Mediterranean and Baltic Sea, respectively, were held. In 2021, for the first time the exercise surpassed the defensive character as Russian and Chinese warships passed through the Tsugaru Strait between Japanese islands Hokkaido and Honshu.

- On 23 October 2021, Russian and Chinese Navies conducted first ever joint patrol.

  [^cite_note-242]

  &#x20;Five warships of each navy participated, including two destroyers, two corvettes and a command ship. Russian Navy was represented by destroyers *Admiral Panteleyev* and *Admiral Tributs*, corvettes *Gromky* and *Geroy Rossiyskoy federatsii Aldar Tsydenzhapov* and tracking ship *Marshal Krylov*. The patrol group passed through the Tsugaru Strait. Joint Russo-Chinese operations imply readiness of both superpowers to cooperate to limit the power of the American-led order in the Asia-Pacific region.

- From 1–3 December 2021, the first naval exercise between Russia and ASEAN occurred in the Indonesian territorial waters.

  [^cite_note-243]

  &#x20;Russia was represented by destroyer *Admiral Panteleyev*, Vietnam by frigate *Ly Thai To*,

  [^cite_note-244]

  &#x20;Indonesia by frigate *Raden Eddy Martadinata*, Malaysia by frigate *Lekiu*, Singapore by corvette *Vigour*, Brunei by off-shore patrol vessel *Daruttaqwa*, Thailand by frigate *Kraburi* and Myanmar by frigate *Kyansittha*,

  [^cite_note-245]

  &#x20;while Philippines joined as an observer.
