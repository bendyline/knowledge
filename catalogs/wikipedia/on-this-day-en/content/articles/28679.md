**Sumatra**[^cite_note-3] ([/sʊˈmɑːtrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is one of the [Sunda Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Islands) of western [Indonesia](14579.md). It is the largest island that is fully within Indonesian territory, as well as the [sixth-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) in the world at 482,286.55 km<sup>2</sup> (182,812 mi.<sup>2</sup>), including adjacent islands such as [Simeulue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeulue), [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias), the [Mentawai Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentawai_Islands_Regency), [Enggano](2489623.md), the various archipelagoes comprising the [Riau Islands](453745.md) Province, [Bangka and Belitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Belitung_Islands) and the [Krakatoa archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakatoa_archipelago).

Sumatra is an elongated landmass spanning a diagonal northwest–southeast axis. The [Indian Ocean](14580.md) borders the northwest, west, and southwest coasts of Sumatra, with the island chain of [Simeulue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeulue), [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias), [Mentawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentawai_Islands_Regency), and [Enggano](2489623.md) off the western coast. In the northeast, the narrow [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca) separates the island from the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula), which is an extension of the [Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) continent.&#x20;

In the southeast, the narrow [Sunda Strait](177179.md), containing the Krakatoa archipelago, separates Sumatra from [Java](69336.md). The northern tip of Sumatra is near the [Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_Islands), while off the southeastern coast lie the islands of Bangka and Belitung, [Karimata Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimata_Strait) and the [Java Sea](467143.md). The [Bukit Barisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Mountains) mountains, which contain several active volcanoes, form the backbone of the island for effectively its entire length, while the northeastern area contains large plains and lowlands with swamps, [mangrove forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest) and complex river systems.&#x20;

The [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) crosses the island at its centre in [West Sumatra](453590.md) and [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) provinces. The climate of the island is [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics), hot, and humid. Lush [tropical rain forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) once dominated the landscape.

Sumatra has a wide range of plant and animal species but has lost almost 50% of its tropical rainforest in the last 35 years. Many species are now [critically endangered](7451605.md), including the [Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger), the [Sumatran elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_elephant), the [Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros), and the [Sumatran orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_orangutan). [Deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and industrial agriculture on the island has also resulted in serious seasonal [smoke haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze), causing severe air pollution in Indonesia and neighbouring countries.&#x20;

The widespread deforestation and other environmental destruction in Sumatra and other parts of Indonesia has often been described by academics as an [ecocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide).[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:2-5][^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:4-7][^cite_note-8]

## Etymology



Before it was known as Sumatra, the region was referred to as *Melayu* (also known as Malay in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)), named after the ancient [Melayu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_Kingdom) based in [Jambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambi). The name Melayu appeared in early historical records, including inscriptions and Chinese chronicles, before being replaced by Sumatra in the 14th century.

[^cite_note-9]

Sumatra was known in ancient times by the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) names of *Svarṇadvīpa* ('Island of Gold') and *[Svarṇabhūmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvarnabhumi)* ('Land of Gold'), because of the gold deposits in the island's [highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland).[^cite_note-10] The earliest known mention of the current form "Sumatra" was in 1017, when the [local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya) king [Haji Sumatrabhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haji_Sumatrabhumi) ("king of the land of Sumatra")[^cite_note-11] sent an envoy to [China](5405.md). In the Maldives, Sumatra was known as Sarunadipa (meaning Golden island).

[Arab](2185.md) traders first referenced the island as *Ramni* in the ninth century, and later on as *[Lamuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambri)*, in reference to a kingdom near modern-day [Banda Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_Aceh) which was the first landfall for traders.[^cite_note-12] The island has also been known by other names, including *[Andalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalas)*[^cite_note-13] or *Percha Island*.[^cite_note-14] Scholars suggest that mention of Suwarnadwipa in the Hindu Epic the *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana)* may be a reference to Sumatra. According to experts on *[The Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana),* the epic is one of the first sources to document [the relationship between India and the Indonesian archipelago](19319107.md#History).[^cite_note-15]

In the late 13th century, [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo) referred to the kingdom as *Samara*, while his contemporary fellow Italian traveller [Odoric of Pordenone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoric_of_Pordenone) used the form *Sumoltra*. Later in the 14th century the local form "Sumatra" became popular abroad due to the rising power of the kingdom of [Samudera Pasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudera_Pasai_Sultanate) and the subsequent [Sultanate of Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_Sultanate).[^cite_note-sneddon-16][^cite_note-17]

From then on, subsequent European writers mostly used *Sumatra* or similar forms of the name for the entire island.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## History





[Muaro Jambi Temple Compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muaro_Jambi_Temple_Compounds), built by the [Melayu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_Kingdom), is one of the largest and best-preserved ancient temple complexes in South East Asia.

By [692 CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijayan_conquest_of_Kedah), the [Melayu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_Kingdom) was absorbed by [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya).[^cite_note-Coedes-20]<sup>: 79–80 </sup> Srivijaya's influence decreased in the 11th century, [specifically in the year 1025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_invasion_of_Srivijaya), after suffering defeat at the hands of the [Chola Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_Empire) in southern India[^cite_note-place-21] By the end of the 12th century, Srivijaya had been reduced to a kingdom, and its dominant role in South Sumatra ended with the last king, Ratu Sekekhummong, who founded the milestone of Kepaksian Sekala Brak in the 13th century AD with the Dalom building.[^cite_note-place-21][^cite_note-22]

At the same time, the [spread of Islam in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam_in_Indonesia) occurred gradually and indirectly, starting from the western regions such as the Sumatra area which became the first place for the spread of Islam in the archipelago, then Java, then to the eastern regions of Indonesia, Sulawesi and Maluku.[^cite_note-literasi-23] The island of Sumatra became the first area to receive the spread of Islam because of the position of the island of Sumatra which is close to the Malacca strait.[^cite_note-literasi-23]

The initial process of Islamization related to trade and also the formation of the kingdom.[^cite_note-literasi-23] Islam entered Sumatra through pious [Arabs](2185.md) and [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils) traders in the 6th and 7th centuries AD.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] At the beginning and end of the 13th century the formation of the kingdom, the king of the [Samudra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudera_Pasai_Sultanate) kingdom had converted to Islam. [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo) visited the island in 1292, and his fellow Italian [Odoric of Pordenone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoric_of_Pordenone) in 1321.

Aceh in the north of Sumatra became known in the 16th century as a trading centre for the [pepper trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade) by shipping high-quality [peppercorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piperaceae). Aceh became the main commercial centre of the [Aceh Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_Sultanate) and trading routes were established to the [Mediterranean](19006.md) via the [Red Sea](26191.md) to rival the Portuguese [shipping lanes](2018420.md). The reign of [Iskandar Muda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskandar_Muda) is known as the *golden age* of Sumatra because he extended the cultural influence of the Aceh Sultanate to [Padang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang) and [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor).[^cite_note-27]

The Aceh Sultanate sustained the rivalry with the [Johor sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Sultanate), the Dutch, and the Portuguese throughout the 16th and 17th century. When the Dutch were weakened in the 18th century the British empire began to actively intervene in Aceh, establishing close relations between Banda Aceh and [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang). In the 17th and 18th century the Aceh Sultanate battled the [Siak sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Siak_Sri_Indrapura) in the south of Sumatra. The port city of [Banda Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_Aceh) was recorded in European historical writings since the 13th century.&#x20;

In terms of [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development) the port of Banda Aceh only started to face competition in the 18th century when more ports were constructed in Sumatra for [maritime transport](28769.md). Nevertheless, major pepper suppliers used the port of Banda Aceh at the beginning of the 19th century. The port in [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan) grew swiftly in the late 19th and early 20th century. Meanwhile the medium sized port of [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang) faced a stiff economic decline as the heritage of the Srivijaya empire was superseded by the [economic policy](210522.md) of the [Singhasari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhasari) and [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit). The [Palembang sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang_Sultanate) experienced a terminal decline in the early 19th century.[^cite_note-28]

With the coming of the [Dutch Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire), the many Sumatran princely states gradually fell under their control. Aceh posed major obstacles, as the Dutch were involved in the long and costly [Aceh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_War) (1873–1903).

During the [Second World War](32927.md), [Japan invaded Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Sumatra) in 1942.

The [Free Aceh Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Aceh_Movement) fought against Indonesian government forces in the [Aceh Insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Aceh) from 1976 to 2005.[^cite_note-29] Security crackdowns in 2001 and 2002 resulted in several thousand civilian deaths.[^cite_note-30]

The island was heavily impacted by both the [1883 Krakatoa eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1883_eruption_of_Krakatoa) and the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami).

### Languages

Speakers of Acehnese.



[Kedukan Bukit Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedukan_Bukit_Inscription), using [Pallava alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallava_alphabet), is the oldest surviving specimen of the [Old Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Malay) language in [South Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sumatra), Indonesia.

There are over 52 [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Indonesia) spoken, all of which (except Chinese and Tamil) belong to the [Malayo-Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian_languages) branch of the [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages) language family. Within Malayo-Polynesian, they are divided into several sub-branches: [Chamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamic_languages) (which are represented by [Acehnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_language) in which its closest relatives are languages spoken by [Ethnic Chams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams) in Cambodia and Vietnam), [Malayic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayic_languages) ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_language) and other closely related languages), [Northwest Sumatra–Barrier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Sumatra%E2%80%93Barrier_Islands_languages) ([Batak languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_languages), [Gayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayo_language) and others), [Lampungic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampung_language) (includes Proper Lampung and [Komering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komering_language)) and [Bornean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_North_Borneo_languages) (represented by [Rejang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejang_language) in which its closest linguistic relatives are [Bukar Sadong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukar%E2%80%93Sadong_language) and [Land Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Dayak_languages) spoken in [West Kalimantan](453636.md) and [Sarawak](28258.md) ([Malaysia](3607937.md))).&#x20;

Northwest Sumatra–Barrier Islands and Lampungic branches are endemic to the island. Like all parts of Indonesia, [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) (which was based on Riau Malay) is the official language and the main lingua franca. Although Sumatra has its own local lingua franca, [variants of Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayic_languages) like Medan Malay and [Palembang Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musi_language)[^cite_note-34] are popular in North and South Sumatra, especially in urban areas. Minangkabau (Padang dialect)[^cite_note-35] is popular in West Sumatra, some parts of North Sumatra, Bengkulu, Jambi and Riau (especially in [Pekanbaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekanbaru) and areas bordered with [West Sumatra](453590.md)) while Acehnese is also used as an inter-ethnic means of communication in some parts of Aceh province.

## Administration

Sumatra, together with its adjacent smaller islands and groups as mentioned above, forms one of seven geographical [regions of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Indonesia). Sumatra was one of the eight original [provinces of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) between 1945 and 1948. Including adjacent archipelagoes normally included with Sumatra (such as the Riau Islands Province, Nias Island, the Bangka-Belitung group and other offshore islands), it now comprises ten of Indonesia's [38 provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia), which are set out below with their areas and populations.[^cite_note-38]

## Geography



Map of geological formation of Sumatra island



[Mount Sinabung](8494859.md), North Sumatra

The longest axis of the island runs approximately 1,790 km (1,110 mi) northwest–southeast, crossing the equator near the centre. At its widest point, the island spans 435 km (270 mi). The interior of the island is dominated by the [Barisan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Mountains) in the west and swampy plains in the east. Sumatra is the closest Indonesian island to mainland Asia.

To the southeast is [Java](69336.md), separated by the [Sunda Strait](177179.md). To the north is the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) (located on the Asian mainland), separated by the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca). To the east is [Borneo](4518.md), across the [Karimata Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimata_Strait). West of the island is the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).

Sumatra is very prone to earthquakes. The [Great Sumatran fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Sumatran_fault) (a [strike-slip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)#Strike-slip_faults) fault), and the [Sunda megathrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_megathrust) (a [subduction zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction)), run the entire length of the island along its west coast. Huge earthquakes have been recorded throughout history. In 1797, an 8.9 earthquake shook Western Sumatra, and in 1833, a 9.2 earthquake shook Bengkulu and Western Sumatra. Both events caused large [tsunamis](31161.md). Earthquakes are very common throughout the coastal area of the west and center of the island, and tsunamis are common due to the high seismicity in the area.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

On 26 December 2004, the western coast and islands of Sumatra, particularly [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh) province, were struck by a [tsunami](31161.md) following the [Indian Ocean earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami). This was the longest earthquake recorded, lasting between 500 and 600 seconds (8.33–10 minutes).[^cite_note-41] More than 170,000 Indonesians were killed, primarily in Aceh. Other recent earthquakes to strike Sumatra include the [2005 Nias–Simeulue earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Nias%E2%80%93Simeulue_earthquake) and the [2010 Mentawai earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Mentawai_earthquake_and_tsunami).

[Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba) is the site of a [supervolcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervolcano) [eruption](8921015.md) that occurred around 74,000 years ago, temporarily changing the earth's climate.[^cite_note-42] The most important rivers in Sumatra belong to the catchment area of the [South China Sea](74209.md). Heading north to south, the [Asahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahan_River), [Rokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokan_River), [Siak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siak_River), [Kampar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampar_River), [Indragiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indragiri_River), [Batanghari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batang_Hari_River) flow into the [Malacca Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), while the island's largest river, the [Musi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musi_River_\(Indonesia\)), flows into the sea at [Bangka Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_Strait) in the south. To the east, big rivers carry silt from the mountains, forming the vast lowland interspersed by swamps. Even if mostly unsuitable for farming, the area is currently of great economic importance for Indonesia. It produces oil from both above and below the soil – [palm oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaeis) and [petroleum](23195.md).

Sumatra is the largest producer of [Indonesian coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Indonesia). Small-holders grow Arabica coffee (*[Coffea arabica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffea_arabica)*) in the highlands, while Robusta (*[Coffea canephora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffea_canephora)*) is found in the lowlands. Arabica coffee from the regions of Gayo, Lintong and Sidikilang is typically processed using the [Giling Basah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giling_Basah) (wet hulling) technique, which gives it a heavy body and low acidity.[^cite_note-43]

## Flora and fauna

See also: [List of national parks of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Indonesia) and [List of amphibians of Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_amphibians_of_Sumatra)



[Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger)



*[Rafflesia arnoldii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafflesia_arnoldii)*

Sumatra supports a wide range of vegetation types that are home to a rich variety of species, including 17 endemic genera of plants.[^cite_note-Whitten1999-45] Unique species include the [Sumatran pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_merkusii) which dominates the [Sumatran tropical pine forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tropical_pine_forests) of the higher mountainsides in the north of the island and rainforest plants such as *[Rafflesia arnoldii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafflesia_arnoldii)* (the world's largest individual flower), and the [titan arum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_arum) (the world's largest unbranched [inflorescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflorescence)).

The island is home to 201 mammal species and 580 bird species. There are nine endemic mammal species on mainland Sumatra and 14 more endemic to the nearby [Mentawai Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentawai_Islands_Regency).[^cite_note-Whitten1999-45] There are about 300 freshwater fish species in Sumatra.[^cite_note-46] There are 93 [amphibian species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_amphibians_of_Sumatra) in Sumatra, 21 of which are endemic to Sumatra.[^cite_note-47]

The [Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger), [Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros), [Sumatran elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_elephant), [Sumatran orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_orangutan) and [Tapanuli orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapanuli_orangutan) are all critically endangered, indicating the highest level of threat to their survival. In October 2008, the Indonesian government announced a plan to protect Sumatra's remaining forests.[^cite_note-48]

The island includes more than 10 national parks, including three which are listed as the [Tropical Rainforest Heritage of Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Rainforest_Heritage_of_Sumatra) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) – [Gunung Leuser National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunung_Leuser_National_Park), [Kerinci Seblat National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerinci_Seblat_National_Park) and [Bukit Barisan Selatan National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Barisan_Selatan_National_Park). The [Berbak National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbak_National_Park) is one of three national parks in Indonesia listed as a [wetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) of international importance under the [Ramsar Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention).

Sumatra has lost almost 50% of its tropical rainforest since 1980. Many species are now [critically endangered](7451605.md), such as the [Sumatran ground cuckoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_ground_cuckoo), the [Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger), the [Sumatran elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_elephant), the [Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros), and the [Sumatran orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_orangutan). [Deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) on the island has also resulted in serious seasonal smoke haze over neighbouring countries, such as the [2013 Southeast Asian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Southeast_Asian_haze) which caused considerable tensions between Indonesia and affected countries [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md).[^cite_note-\:0-49] The widespread deforestation and other environmental destruction in Sumatra and other parts of Indonesia has often been described by academics as an [ecocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide).[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:2-5][^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:4-7]

## Rail transport

Several unconnected railway networks built during [Netherlands East Indies](23476997.md) exist in Sumatra, such as the ones connecting [Banda Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_Aceh)-[Lhokseumawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhokseumawe)-Besitang-[Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan)-[Tebingtinggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tebing_Tinggi)-[Pematangsiantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pematangsiantar)-[Rantau Prapat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rantau_Prapat) in Northern Sumatra (the Banda Aceh-Besitang section was closed in 1971, but is currently being rebuilt).[^cite_note-50] [Padang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang)-[Solok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solok)-[Bukittinggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukittinggi) in [West Sumatra](453590.md), and [Bandar Lampung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar_Lampung)-[Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang)-[Lahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahat_Regency)-[Lubuklinggau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubuklinggau) in Southern Sumatra.
