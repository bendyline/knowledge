**Ketamine** is a [cyclohexanone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclohexanone)-derived dissociative [general anesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_anaesthetic) and [NMDA receptor antagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMDA_receptor_antagonist) with [analgesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analgesic) and [hallucinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogen) properties, used medically for [anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesia), [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)), and [pain management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_management).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Sachdeva_2023-21] Ketamine exists as its two [enantiomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomers), [*S*- (esketamine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esketamine) and [*R*- (arketamine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arketamine), and has antidepressant action likely involving NMDA antagonism as well as other mechanisms.

At anesthetic doses, ketamine induces a state of dissociative anesthesia, a [trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance)-like state providing pain relief, [sedation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedation), and [amnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesia).[^cite_note-GreenRoback2011-22] Its distinguishing features as an anesthetic are preserved breathing and airway reflexes, stimulated heart function with increased [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure), and moderate [bronchodilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchodilation).[^cite_note-GreenRoback2011-22] As an anesthetic, it is used especially in trauma, [emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services), and [pediatric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatrics) cases. At lower, sub-anesthetic doses, it is used as a treatment for [pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain) and [treatment-resistant depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatment-resistant_depression).

Ketamine is legally used in medicine but is also tightly controlled, as it is used as a [recreational drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_drug) for its [hallucinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogen) and [dissociative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociative) effects.[^cite_note-morgan11-23] When used recreationally, it is found both in crystalline, powder and liquid form, and is often referred to by users as "Ket", "Special K" or simply "K". The long-term effects of repeated use are largely unknown and are an area of active investigation.[^cite_note-pmid33065824-24][^cite_note-pmid33174760-25][^cite_note-pmid33162856-26] Liver and urinary toxicity have been reported among regular users of high doses of ketamine for recreational purposes.[^cite_note-Or-27] Ketamine can cause [dissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_\(psychology\)) and nausea, and other adverse effects, and is [contraindicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraindication) in severe [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease) or [liver disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_disease), and uncontrolled [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis). Ketamine's clinical and antidepressant effects can be influenced by co-administration of other drugs, though these interactions are variable and not yet fully understood.

Ketamine was first synthesized in 1962; it was derived from [phencyclidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phencyclidine) in pursuit of a safer anesthetic with fewer hallucinogenic effects.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-pmid29870458-29] It was approved for use in the United States in 1970.[^cite_note-Sachdeva_2023-21] It has been regularly used in [veterinary medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary_medicine) and was extensively used for [surgical anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_anaesthesia) in the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-pmid20693870-30] It later gained prominence for its rapid antidepressant effects discovered in 2000, marking a major breakthrough in depression treatment. Racemic ketamine, especially at higher doses, may be more effective and longer-lasting than esketamine in reducing depression severity.[^cite_note-Nikolin_2023-31] It is on the [World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines).[^cite_note-WHO23rd-32] It is available as a [generic medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_medication).[^cite_note-KetPres2013-33]

## Medical uses

### Anesthesia

The use of ketamine in anesthesia reflects its characteristics. It is a drug of choice for short-term procedures when [muscle relaxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_contraction) is not required.[^cite_note-Ketamine-34] The effect of ketamine on the [respiratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system) and [circulatory systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_system) is different from that of other anesthetics. It suppresses breathing much less than most other available anesthetics.[^cite_note-heshmati-35] When used at anesthetic doses, ketamine usually stimulates rather than depresses the circulatory system.[^cite_note-36] Protective airway reflexes are preserved,[^cite_note-WongLee2014-37] and it is sometimes possible to administer ketamine anesthesia without protective measures to the airways.[^cite_note-Ketamine-34] [Psychotomimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychotomimetic) effects limit the acceptance of ketamine; however, [lamotrigine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamotrigine)[^cite_note-pmid10711913-38] and [nimodipine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimodipine)[^cite_note-pmid11750186-39] decrease psychotomimetic effects and can also be counteracted by [benzodiazepines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzodiazepine) or [propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol) administration.[^cite_note-pmid32826629-40] [Ketofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketofol) is a combination of ketamine and propofol.

Ketamine is frequently used in severely injured people and appears to be safe in this group.[^cite_note-The_effect_of_ketamine_on_intracran-41] It has been widely used for emergency surgery in field conditions in war zones,[^cite_note-pmid25886322-42] for example, during the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-pmid28731926-43] A 2011 [clinical practice guideline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_guideline) supports the use of ketamine as a [sedative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedative) in [emergency medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medicine), including during physically painful procedures.[^cite_note-GreenRoback2011-22] It is the drug of choice for people in [traumatic shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traumatic_shock) who are at risk of [hypotension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension).[^cite_note-44] Ketamine often raises blood pressure upon administration and is unlikely to lower blood pressure in most patients, making it useful in treating severe head injuries for which low blood pressure can be dangerous.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

Ketamine is an option in children as the sole anesthetic for minor procedures or as an induction agent followed by [neuromuscular blocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_blocker) and [tracheal intubation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracheal_intubation).[^cite_note-pmid25886322-42] In particular, children with [cyanotic heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanotic_heart_disease) and [neuromuscular disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_disorders) are good candidates for ketamine anesthesia.[^cite_note-pmid32826629-40][^cite_note-48]

Due to the bronchodilating properties of ketamine, it can be used for anesthesia in people with [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma), [chronic obstructive pulmonary disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_obstructive_pulmonary_disease), and with severe reactive airway disease, including active [bronchospasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchospasm).[^cite_note-pmid25886322-42][^cite_note-pmid32826629-40][^cite_note-Goyal-49]

### Pain

Ketamine infusions are used for acute pain treatment in emergency departments and in the perioperative period for individuals with refractory or [intractable pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intractable_pain). The doses are lower than those used for anesthesia, usually referred to as sub-anesthetic doses. Adjunctive to [morphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphine) or on its own, ketamine reduces morphine use, pain level, nausea, and vomiting after surgery. Ketamine is likely to be most beneficial for surgical patients when severe post-operative pain is expected, and for opioid-tolerant patients.[^cite_note-pmid29870457-50][^cite_note-51]

Ketamine is especially useful in the pre-hospital setting due to its effectiveness and low risk of respiratory depression.[^cite_note-SvensonBiedermann2011-52] Ketamine has similar efficacy to opioids in a hospital emergency department setting for the management of acute pain and the control of procedural pain.[^cite_note-pmid30019434-53] It may also prevent [opioid-induced hyperalgesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid-induced_hyperalgesia)[^cite_note-pmid26495312-54][^cite_note-pmid21412369-55] and [postanesthetic shivering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postanesthetic_shivering).[^cite_note-56]

For chronic pain, ketamine is used as an intravenous analgesic, mainly if the pain is [neuropathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuropathic_pain).[^cite_note-pmid29870458-29] It has the added benefit of counteracting [spinal sensitization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_sensitization) or [wind-up phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_wind-up) experienced with [chronic pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_pain).[^cite_note-elia-57] In multiple clinical trials, ketamine infusions delivered short-term pain relief in neuropathic pain diagnoses, pain after a traumatic spine injury, [fibromyalgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibromyalgia), and [complex regional pain syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_regional_pain_syndrome) (CRPS).[^cite_note-pmid29870458-29] However, the 2018 consensus guidelines on chronic pain concluded that, overall, there is only weak evidence in favor of ketamine use in spinal injury pain, moderate evidence in favor of ketamine for CRPS, and weak or no evidence for ketamine in mixed neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, and [cancer pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_pain). In particular, only for CRPS, there is evidence of medium to longer-term pain relief.[^cite_note-pmid29870458-29]

### Depression

See also: [Esketamine § Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esketamine#Depression), and [Ketamine-assisted psychotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketamine-assisted_psychotherapy)

An [enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer) of ketamine – [esketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esketamine) – was approved as an antidepressant by the [European Medicines Agency](1427054.md) in 2019.[^cite_note-58] Esketamine was approved as a nasal spray for treatment-resistant depression in the United States[^cite_note-fda-spravato-59] and elsewhere in 2019. The Canadian Network for Mood and Anxiety Treatments (CANMAT) recommends esketamine as a third-line treatment for depression.[^cite_note-pmid33174760-25]

Ketamine maintenance therapy appears effective and generally safe for treatment-resistant depression, with regular dosing, early intervention, and concurrent psychotherapy improving outcomes; standardized protocols and patient selection are crucial.[^cite_note-60]

The French drug regulatory agency’s 2026 approval of intravenous racemic ketamine for severe suicidal crisis marks the first official rapid-acting pharmacological option for imminent suicide risk.[^cite_note-61] Most systematic reviews suggest that ketamine shows short-term efficacy in reducing suicidality with generally mild side effects, but the evidence is largely low quality and its long-term efficacy remains unclear.[^cite_note-62]

Existing evidence, though limited and low to very low certainty, suggests that a single intravenous dose of ketamine as an add-on to [mood stabilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_stabilizer) may produce a rapid but transient antidepressant effect in [bipolar depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder).[^cite_note-dean-63]

In February 2022, the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) issued an alert to healthcare professionals concerning [compounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compounding) nasal spray products containing ketamine intended to treat depression.[^cite_note-fda-2/22-64]

**Comparative efficacy of ketamine and esketamine**

The antidepressant effect of ketamine appears to be stronger and more sustained for racemic ketamine, while the effects of esketamine are smaller and may diminish after treatment ends.[^cite_note-Nikolin_2023-31]

### Other uses

#### Seizures

Ketamine is used to treat [status epilepticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_epilepticus)[^cite_note-pmid33923061-65] that has not responded to standard treatments, but only case studies and no randomized controlled trials support its use.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-pmid30232735-67]

#### Agitation

Ketamine is used to treat acute [agitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_agitation), [aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggression), and [excited delirium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excited_delirium) in [pre-hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-hospital_emergency_medicine) and [emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_department) settings, including symptoms refractory to other drugs such as [antipsychotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipsychotic) and [benzodiazepines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzodiazepine).[^cite_note-LinderRossWeant2018-68][^cite_note-PeterSuhasGowda2025-69][^cite_note-BrownEvansTucker2023-70][^cite_note-BarbicAndolfattoGrunau2021-71][^cite_note-LinFigueradoMontgomery2021-72]

#### Asthma

Ketamine has been suggested as a possible therapy for children with severe acute asthma who do not respond to standard treatment.[^cite_note-Jat_2012-73] This is due to its [bronchodilator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchodilator) effects in the respiratory system.[^cite_note-Jat_2012-73] A 2012 Cochrane review found there were minimal adverse effects reported, but the limited studies showed no significant benefit.[^cite_note-Jat_2012-73]

## Contraindications

Some major [contraindications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraindication) for ketamine are:[^cite_note-pmid29870458-29][^cite_note-pmid29870457-50]

- Severe [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease) such as [unstable angina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_angina) or poorly controlled [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension)

- Increased [intracranial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracranial_pressure#Increased_ICP) or [intraocular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intraocular_pressure) pressure (however these remain controversial, with recent studies suggesting otherwise)

  [^cite_note-pmid29870457-50]

- Poorly controlled [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis)

- Severe liver disease such as [cirrhosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirrhosis)

- [Pregnancy](1771587.md)

- Active [substance use disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_use_disorder) (for serial ketamine injections)

- Age less than 3 months

  [^cite_note-pmid23521979-11]

At anesthetic doses, 10–20% of adults and 1–2% of children[^cite_note-pmid23521979-11] experience adverse psychiatric reactions that occur during emergence from anesthesia, ranging from dreams and [dysphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysphoria) to hallucinations and [emergence delirium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergence_delirium).[^cite_note-StrayerNelson2008-75] Psychotomimetic effects decrease when adding [lamotrigine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamotrigine)[^cite_note-pmid10711913-38] and [nimodipine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimodipine)[^cite_note-pmid11750186-39] and can be counteracted by pretreatment with a [benzodiazepine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzodiazepine) or [propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol).[^cite_note-StrayerNelson2008-75][^cite_note-pmid32826629-40] Ketamine anesthesia commonly causes [tonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonic_\(physiology\))-[clonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonic) movements (greater than 10% of people) and rarely [hypertonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertonia).[^cite_note-Quibell2011-17][^cite_note-StrayerNelson2008-75] Vomiting can be expected in 5–15% of the patients; pretreatment with propofol mitigates it as well.[^cite_note-pmid23521979-11][^cite_note-StrayerNelson2008-75] [Laryngospasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laryngospasm) occurs only rarely with ketamine. Ketamine, generally, stimulates breathing; however, in the first 2–3 minutes of a high-dose rapid intravenous injection, it may cause a transient respiratory depression.[^cite_note-StrayerNelson2008-75]

At lower sub-anesthetic doses, psychiatric side effects are prominent. The most common psychiatric side effects are [dissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_\(psychology\)), visual distortions, and [numbness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbness). Also common (20–50%) are difficulty speaking, confusion, euphoria, drowsiness, and difficulty concentrating. [Hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination) are described by 6–10% of people. Dizziness, blurred vision, dry mouth, hypertension, nausea, increased or decreased body temperature, or flushing are the common (>10%) non-psychiatric side effects. All these adverse effects are most pronounced by the end of the injection, dramatically reduced 40 minutes afterward, and completely disappear within 4 hours after the injection.[^cite_note-pmid31791675-76]

### Liver toxicity and urologic disease

[Urologic diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urologic_disease) occur primarily in people who use large amounts of ketamine routinely, with 20–30% of frequent users having bladder complaints.[^cite_note-pmid29870458-29][^cite_note-pmid21102971-77] It includes a range of disorders from [cystitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cystitis) to [hydronephrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydronephrosis) to [kidney failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_failure).[^cite_note-pmid32212278-78] The typical symptoms of ketamine-induced cystitis are [frequent urination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequent_urination), [dysuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysuria), and [urinary urgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_urgency) sometimes accompanied by pain during urination and [blood in urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematuria).[^cite_note-Middela2011-79] The damage to the bladder wall has similarities to both [interstitial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitial_cystitis) and [eosinophilic cystitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eosinophilic_cystitis). The wall is thickened and the functional bladder capacity is as low as 10–150 mL.[^cite_note-pmid32212278-78] Studies indicate that ketamine-induced cystitis is caused by ketamine and its metabolites directly interacting with [urothelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urothelium), resulting in damage of the [epithelial cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelium) of the bladder lining and increased permeability of the urothelial barrier which results in clinical symptoms.[^cite_note-80]

Management of ketamine-induced cystitis involves ketamine cessation as the first step. This is followed by [NSAIDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSAID) and [anticholinergics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticholinergic) and, if the response is insufficient, by [tramadol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramadol). The second-line treatments are epithelium-protective agents such as oral [pentosan polysulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentosan_polysulfate) or [intravesical instillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravesical_drug_delivery) of [hyaluronic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyaluronic_acid). Intravesical [botulinum toxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botulinum_toxin) is also useful.[^cite_note-pmid32212278-78] Some research also indicates that [epigallocatechin-3-gallate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigallocatechin-3-gallate) (EGCG) may mitigate bladder dysfunction in ketamine-induced cystitis by normalizing the collagen-to-muscle ratio and restoring storage capacity.[^cite_note-pmid34105799-81]

[Hepatotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatotoxicity) (toxicity to the liver) of ketamine involves higher doses and repeated administration. In a group of chronic high-dose ketamine users, the frequency of liver injury was reported to be about 10%.[^cite_note-82] There are case reports of increased liver enzymes involving ketamine treatment of chronic pain.[^cite_note-pmid32212278-78] Chronic ketamine abuse has also been associated with [biliary colic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biliary_colic),[^cite_note-83] [cachexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachexia), [gastrointestinal diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_diseases), [hepatobiliary disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatobiliary_disorder), and [acute kidney injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_kidney_injury).[^cite_note-84]

### Near-death experience

Most people who were able to remember their dreams during ketamine anesthesia report [near-death experiences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-death_experience) (NDEs) when the broadest possible definition of an NDE is used.[^cite_note-Jansen2001b-85] Ketamine can reproduce features that commonly have been associated with NDEs.[^cite_note-Peinkhofer-86] A 2019 large-scale study found that written reports of ketamine experiences had a high degree of similarity to written reports of NDEs in comparison to other written reports of drug experiences.[^cite_note-87]

### Addiction and tolerance

Though there is no evidence that ketamine can cause [physical dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_dependence), some people who regularly use it can develop an addiction to it. Animal experiments also confirm the risk of misuse.[^cite_note-morgan11-23] Additionally, the rapid onset of effects following [insufflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insufflation_\(medicine\)) may increase potential use as a recreational drug. The short duration of effects promotes [bingeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_binge). Ketamine [tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_tolerance) rapidly develops, even with repeated medical use, prompting the use of higher doses. Some users have reported [withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_withdrawal) symptoms, primarily anxiety, tremor, sweating, and palpitations, following the attempts to stop; however, these effects have never been objectively measured in the way that physical withdrawal symptoms from drugs like opioids or benzodiazepines have.[^cite_note-morgan11-23]

### Brain damage

Despite the balance of palliative benefits which planned courses of therapy can confer when patients face serious medical conditions, long-term ketamine abuse is known to cause brain damage, including reduction in both white and grey matter seen on MRI imaging and atrophy seen on CT scans.[^cite_note-88] Cognitive deficits as well as increased dissociation and [delusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delusion) were observed in frequent recreational users of ketamine.[^cite_note-Morgan2009-89]

## Interactions

Ketamine [potentiates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentiator) the sedative effects of [propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol)[^cite_note-propofol-90] and [midazolam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midazolam).[^cite_note-midazolam-91] [Naltrexone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naltrexone) potentiates [psychotomimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychotomimetism) effects of a low dose of ketamine,[^cite_note-pmid16395307-92] while [lamotrigine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamotrigine)[^cite_note-pmid10711913-38] and [nimodipine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimodipine)[^cite_note-pmid11750186-39] decrease them. [Clonidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonidine) reduces the increase of salivation, heart rate, and blood pressure during ketamine anesthesia and decreases the incidence of nightmares.[^cite_note-pmid10773503-93]

Clinical observations suggest that benzodiazepines may diminish the antidepressant effects of ketamine.[^cite_note-pmid28858450-94] It appears most conventional antidepressants can be safely combined with ketamine.[^cite_note-pmid28858450-94]

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacodynamics

#### Mechanism of action

Ketamine is a mixture of equal amounts of two [enantiomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer): [esketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esketamine) and [arketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arketamine). Esketamine is a far more [potent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potency_\(pharmacology\)) NMDA receptor pore blocker than arketamine.[^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12] Pore blocking of the [NMDA receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMDA_receptor) is responsible for the anesthetic, analgesic, and psychotomimetic effects of ketamine.[^cite_note-pmid29945898-95][^cite_note-pmid27028535-96] Blocking of the NMDA receptor results in analgesia by preventing [central sensitization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_sensitization) in [dorsal horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posterior_horn_of_spinal_cord) neurons; in other words, ketamine's actions interfere with pain transmission in the [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord).[^cite_note-Quibell2011-17]

The mechanism of action of ketamine in alleviating depression is not well understood, but it is an area of active investigation. Due to the hypothesis that NMDA receptor antagonism underlies the antidepressant effects of ketamine, esketamine was developed as an antidepressant.[^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12] However, multiple other [NMDA receptor antagonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMDA_receptor_antagonist), including [memantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memantine), [lanicemine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanicemine), [rislenemdaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rislenemdaz), [rapastinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapastinel), and [4-chlorokynurenine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-chlorokynurenine), have thus far failed to demonstrate significant effectiveness for depression.[^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12][^cite_note-GarayZarate2018-97] Furthermore, animal research indicates that arketamine, the enantiomer with a weaker NMDA receptor antagonism, as well as [(2*R*,6*R*)-hydroxynorketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/\(2R,6R\)-hydroxynorketamine), the [metabolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite) with negligible affinity for the NMDA receptor but potent [alpha-7 nicotinic receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-7_nicotinic_receptor) antagonist activity, may have antidepressant action.[^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12][^cite_note-pmid23183107-98] This furthers the argument that NMDA receptor antagonism may not be primarily responsible for the antidepressant effects of ketamine.[^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12][^cite_note-AdisInsight-HR-071603-99][^cite_note-GarayZarate2018-97] Acute inhibition of the [lateral habenula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_habenula), a part of the brain responsible for inhibiting the [mesolimbic reward pathway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolimbic_reward_pathway) and referred to as the "anti-reward center", is another possible mechanism for ketamine's antidepressant effects.[^cite_note-pmid29532791-100][^cite_note-pmid29879390-101][^cite_note-pmid29446381-102]

Possible biochemical mechanisms of ketamine's antidepressant action include direct action on the [NMDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-Methyl-D-aspartic_acid) receptor and downstream effects on regulators such as [brain-derived neurotrophic factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain-derived_neurotrophic_factor) (BDNF) and [mTOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTOR).[^cite_note-pmid29532791-100] It is not clear whether ketamine alone is sufficient for antidepressant action or its metabolites are also important; the active metabolite of ketamine, [hydroxynorketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxynorketamine), which does not significantly interact with the NMDA receptor but nonetheless indirectly activates [AMPA receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMPA_receptor), may also or alternatively be involved in the rapid-onset antidepressant effects of ketamine.[^cite_note-pmid29945898-95][^cite_note-pmid29532791-100][^cite_note-pmid29516301-103] As an NMDA [receptor antagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_antagonist), ketamine triggers a paradoxical acute burst of [glutamate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamate_\(neurotransmitter\)) by selectively blocking inhibitory GABAergic neurons. This surge activates AMPA receptors, which modulate downstream [signaling pathways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signaling_pathway) in the [limbic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbic_system) to produce rapid antidepressant effects.[^cite_note-pmid29736744-104][^cite_note-pmid29532791-100][^cite_note-pmid29668918-105] Such downstream actions of the activation of AMPA receptors include [upregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upregulation) of BDNF and activation of its signaling receptor [tropomyosin receptor kinase B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropomyosin_receptor_kinase_B) (TrkB), activation of the [mammalian target of rapamycin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammalian_target_of_rapamycin) (mTOR) pathway, deactivation of [glycogen synthase kinase 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycogen_synthase_kinase_3) (GSK-3), and inhibition of the [phosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorylation) of the [eukaryotic elongation factor 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotic_elongation_factor_2) (eEF2) [kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinase).[^cite_note-pmid29736744-104][^cite_note-pmid29532791-100][^cite_note-pmid26519901-106][^cite_note-pmid27425886-107]

Ketamine principally acts as a pore blocker of the [NMDA receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMDA_receptor), an [ionotropic glutamate receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionotropic_glutamate_receptor).[^cite_note-pmid28418641-124] The *S*-(+) and *R*-(–) [stereoisomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoisomer) of ketamine bind to the dizocilpine site of the NMDA receptor with different [affinities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_affinity), the former showing approximately 3- to 4-fold greater affinity for the receptor than the latter. As a result, the *S* isomer is a more potent anesthetic and analgesic than its *R* counterpart.[^cite_note-pmid8942324-125]

Ketamine may interact with and inhibit the NMDAR via another [allosteric site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allosteric_site) on the receptor.[^cite_note-Orser-126]

With a couple of exceptions, ketamine's actions at other receptors are far weaker than its antagonism of the NMDA receptor (see the activity table to the right).[^cite_note-MathewZarate2016-7][^cite_note-pmid26075331-127]

Although ketamine is a very weak ligand of the [monoamine transporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_transporter) (K<sub>i</sub> > 60 μM), it has been suggested that it may interact with [allosteric sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allosteric_site) on the monoamine transporters to produce [monoamine reuptake inhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_reuptake_inhibition).[^cite_note-pmid23527166-109] However, no functional inhibition ([IC<sub>50</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IC50)) of the human monoamine transporters has been observed with ketamine or its [metabolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite) at concentrations of up to 10,000 nM.[^cite_note-pmid27469513-115][^cite_note-pmid28418641-124] Moreover, [animal studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preclinical_research) and at least three human [case reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_report) have found no interaction between ketamine and the [monoamine oxidase inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_inhibitor) (MAOI) [tranylcypromine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranylcypromine), which is of importance as the combination of a monoamine reuptake inhibitor with an MAOI can produce severe toxicity such as [serotonin syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin_syndrome) or [hypertensive crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_crisis).[^cite_note-pmid28097909-128][^cite_note-pmid26302763-129] Collectively, these findings shed doubt on the involvement of monoamine reuptake inhibition in the effects of ketamine in humans.[^cite_note-pmid28097909-128][^cite_note-pmid28418641-124][^cite_note-pmid27469513-115][^cite_note-pmid26302763-129] Ketamine has been found to increase [dopaminergic neurotransmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic_pathways) in the brain, but instead of being due to dopamine reuptake inhibition, this may be via [indirect/downstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstream_and_downstream_\(transduction\)) mechanisms, namely through antagonism of the NMDA receptor.[^cite_note-pmid28418641-124][^cite_note-pmid27469513-115]

Whether ketamine is an agonist of D<sub>2</sub> receptors is controversial. Early research by the [Philip Seeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Seeman) group found ketamine to be a D<sub>2</sub> partial agonist with a potency similar to that of its NMDA receptor antagonism.[^cite_note-pmid12232776-114][^cite_note-pmid18720422-130][^cite_note-pmid19391150-131] However, later studies by different researchers found the affinity of ketamine of >10 μM for the regular human and rat D<sub>2</sub> receptors,[^cite_note-pmid23527166-109][^cite_note-pmid27469513-115][^cite_note-pmid16730695-116] Moreover, whereas D<sub>2</sub> receptor agonists such as [bromocriptine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromocriptine) can rapidly and powerfully suppress [prolactin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactin) [secretion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretion),[^cite_note-Springer2012-132] subanesthetic doses of ketamine have not been found to do this in humans and in fact, have been found to dose-dependently *increase* prolactin levels.[^cite_note-pmid8122957-133][^cite_note-pmid11282259-134] [Imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging) studies have shown mixed results on inhibition of [striatal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striatum) \[<sup>11</sup>C] [raclopride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raclopride) binding by ketamine in humans, with some studies finding a significant decrease and others finding no such effect.[^cite_note-pmid17591653-135] However, changes in \[<sup>11</sup>C] raclopride binding may be due to changes in dopamine concentrations induced by ketamine rather than binding of ketamine to the D<sub>2</sub> receptor.[^cite_note-pmid17591653-135]

#### Relationships between levels and effects

[Dissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociative) and [psychotomimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychotomimetic) effects are reported in people treated with ketamine at plasma concentrations of approximately 100 to 250 ng/mL (0.42–1.1 μM).[^cite_note-pmid29945898-95] The typical intravenous antidepressant dosage of ketamine used to treat depression is low and results in maximal plasma concentrations of 70 to 200 ng/mL (0.29–0.84 μM).[^cite_note-pmid28249076-136] At similar plasma concentrations (70 to 160 ng/mL; 0.29–0.67 μM) it also shows analgesic effects.[^cite_note-pmid28249076-136] In 1–5 minutes after inducing anesthesia by rapid intravenous injection of ketamine, its plasma concentration reaches as high as 60–110 μM.[^cite_note-pmid526385-137][^cite_note-pmid7198883-138] When the anesthesia was maintained using [nitrous oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide) together with continuous injection of ketamine, the ketamine concentration stabilized at approximately 9.3 μM.[^cite_note-pmid526385-137] In an experiment with purely ketamine anesthesia, people began to awaken once the plasma level of ketamine decreased to about 2,600 ng/mL (11 μM) and became oriented in place and time when the level was down to 1,000 ng/mL (4 μM).[^cite_note-pmid3970799-139] In a single-case study, the concentration of ketamine in [cerebrospinal fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebrospinal_fluid), a proxy for the brain concentration, during anesthesia varied between 2.8 and 6.5 μM and was approximately 40% lower than in plasma.[^cite_note-pmid7248132-140]

### Pharmacokinetics

Ketamine can be absorbed by many different routes due to its water and lipid solubility. [Intravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous) ketamine [bioavailability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioavailability_\(medicine\)) is 100% by definition, intramuscular injection bioavailability is slightly lower at 93%,[^cite_note-MathewZarate2016-7] and [epidural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidural) bioavailability is 77%.[^cite_note-Kintz2014-10] Subcutaneous bioavailability has never been measured but is presumed to be high.[^cite_note-Mao2016-141] Among the less invasive routes, the intranasal route has the highest bioavailability (45–50%)[^cite_note-MathewZarate2016-7][^cite_note-pmid23521979-11] and oral – the lowest (16–20%).[^cite_note-MathewZarate2016-7][^cite_note-pmid23521979-11] Sublingual and rectal bioavailabilities are intermediate at approximately 25–50%.[^cite_note-MathewZarate2016-7][^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12][^cite_note-pmid23521979-11]

After absorption ketamine is rapidly [distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(pharmacology\)) into the brain and other tissues.[^cite_note-pmid27028535-96] The [plasma protein binding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_protein_binding) of ketamine is variable at 23–47%.[^cite_note-pmid6884418-13]



Major routes of ketamine metabolism

[^cite_note-pmid29945898-95]

In the body, ketamine undergoes extensive [metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism). It is [biotransformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotransformation) by [CYP3A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYP3A4) and [CYP2B6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYP2B6) [isoenzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoenzyme) into [norketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norketamine), which, in turn, is converted by [CYP2A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYP2A6) and CYP2B6 into [hydroxynorketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxynorketamine) and [dehydronorketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehydronorketamine).[^cite_note-pmid29945898-95] Low oral bioavailability of ketamine is due to the [first-pass effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-pass_effect) and, possibly, ketamine intestinal metabolism by CYP3A4.[^cite_note-pmid27763887-15] As a result, norketamine plasma levels are several-fold higher than ketamine following oral administration, and norketamine may play a role in anesthetic and analgesic action of oral ketamine.[^cite_note-MathewZarate2016-7][^cite_note-pmid27763887-15] This also explains why oral ketamine levels are independent of CYP2B6 activity, unlike subcutaneous ketamine levels.[^cite_note-pmid27763887-15][^cite_note-pmid25702819-142]

After an intravenous injection of [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium)-labelled ketamine, 91% of the radioactivity is recovered from urine and 3% from feces.[^cite_note-pmid4603048-18] The medication is excreted mostly in the form of [metabolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite), with only 2% remaining unchanged. Conjugated hydroxylated derivatives of ketamine (80%) followed by dehydronorketamine (16%) are the most prevalent metabolites detected in urine.[^cite_note-pmid20693870-30]

## Chemistry

### Structure



*(S)*-ketamine



*(R)*-ketamine

In chemical structure, ketamine is an [arylcyclohexylamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arylcyclohexylamine) derivative. Ketamine is a [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality_\(chemistry\)) compound. The more active enantiomer, [esketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esketamine) (*S*-ketamine), is also available for medical use under the brand name Ketanest S,[^cite_note-Kruger1998-143] while the less active enantiomer, [arketamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arketamine) (*R*-ketamine), has never been marketed as an [enantiopure drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiopure_drug) for clinical use. While S-ketamine is more effective as an analgesic and anesthetic through NMDA receptor antagonism, R-ketamine produces longer-lasting effects as an antidepressant.[^cite_note-Sachdeva_2023-21]

The [optical rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_rotation) of a given enantiomer of ketamine can vary between its [salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_\(chemistry\)) and [free base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_base) form. The free base form of (*S*)‑ketamine exhibits [dextrorotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextrorotation_and_levorotation) and is therefore labelled (*S*)‑(+)‑ketamine. However, its [hydrochloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrochloride) salt shows [levorotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextrorotation_and_levorotation) and is thus labelled (*S*)‑(−)‑ketamine hydrochloride.[^cite_note-144]

### Detection

Ketamine may be quantified in blood or plasma to confirm a diagnosis of poisoning in hospitalized people, provide evidence in an impaired driving arrest, or assist in a medicolegal death investigation. Blood or plasma ketamine concentrations are usually in a range of 0.5–5.0 mg/L in persons receiving the drug therapeutically (during general anesthesia), 1–2 mg/L in those arrested for impaired driving, and 3–20 mg/L in victims of acute fatal overdosage. Urine is often the preferred specimen for routine drug use monitoring purposes. The presence of norketamine, a pharmacologically active metabolite, is useful for confirmation of ketamine ingestion.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

## History

Ketamine was first synthesized in 1962 by [Calvin L. Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_L._Stevens),[^cite_note-Sachdeva_2023-21] a professor of chemistry at [Wayne State University](302260.md) and a [Parke-Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parke-Davis) consultant. It was known by the developmental code name *CI-581*.[^cite_note-Sachdeva_2023-21] After promising preclinical research in animals, ketamine was tested in [human prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimentation_on_prisoners) in 1964.[^cite_note-pmid20693870-30] These investigations demonstrated ketamine's short duration of action and reduced behavioral toxicity made it a favorable choice over [phencyclidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phencyclidine) (PCP) as an anesthetic.[^cite_note-148] The researchers wanted to call the state of ketamine anesthesia "dreaming", but Parke-Davis did not approve of the name. Hearing about this problem and the "disconnected" appearance of treated people, Mrs. Edward F. Domino,[^cite_note-pmid27965560-149] the wife of one of the pharmacologists working on ketamine, suggested "dissociative anesthesia".[^cite_note-pmid20693870-30] Ketamine was first described in the literature in 1963[^cite_note-Kaump1963-150][^cite_note-DillChangPeterson1963-151] and its effects in humans were first described by Edward F. Domino and colleagues in 1965.[^cite_note-DominoChodoffCorssen1965-152][^cite_note-McCarthyChenKaump1965-153]

Following FDA approval in 1970, ketamine anesthesia was first given to American soldiers during the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-CESAR-154]

The discovery of antidepressive action of ketamine in 2000[^cite_note-pmid10686270-155] has been described as the single most important advance in the treatment of depression in more than 50 years.[^cite_note-pmid28395988-156][^cite_note-Hashimoto2019-12] It has sparked interest in NMDA receptor antagonists for depression,[^cite_note-157] and has shifted the direction of antidepressant research and development.[^cite_note-pmid27960559-158]

### Recreational use

Main article: [Ketamine in society and culture § Recreational use](66087885.md#Recreational_use)



Ketamine hydrochloride on a glass plate

At sub-anesthetic doses, ketamine produces a [dissociative state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_\(psychology\)), characterised by a sense of detachment from one's physical body and the external world that is known as [depersonalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depersonalization) and [derealization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derealization).[^cite_note-Giannini2000-169] At sufficiently high doses, users may experience what is called the "[K-hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-hole)", a state of dissociation with visual and auditory hallucination.[^cite_note-170] [John C. Lilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Lilly), [Marcia Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcia_Moore), and [D. M. Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._M._Turner) (among others) have written extensively about their own [entheogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entheogen) and [psychonautic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychonautics) experiences with ketamine.[^cite_note-RecreationalLiterature-171] Turner died prematurely due to drowning during presumed unsupervised ketamine use.[^cite_note-Jansen2001-172]

Recreational ketamine use has been implicated in deaths globally, with more than 90 deaths in England and Wales in 2005–2013.[^cite_note-DalyVice14-173] They include accidental poisonings, drownings, traffic accidents, and [suicides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicides).[^cite_note-DalyVice14-173] The majority of deaths were among young people.[^cite_note-TheCrownONS13-174] Several months after being found dead in his hot tub, actor [Matthew Perry](233338.md)'s October 2023 apparent drowning death was revealed to have been caused by a ketamine overdose, and, while other factors were present, the acute effects of ketamine were ruled to be the primary cause of death.[^cite_note-175] Due to its ability to cause confusion and [amnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterograde_amnesia), ketamine has been used for [date rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_rape).[^cite_note-CAMHDYK-176][^cite_note-CESAR-154]

## Research

See also: [List of investigational hallucinogens and entactogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_investigational_hallucinogens_and_entactogens)

A 2023 meta-analysis found that racemic ketamine, particularly at higher doses, is more effective than esketamine in reducing depression severity, with more sustained benefits over time.[^cite_note-Nikolin_2023-31] No biomarker has been validated to predict ketamine’s antidepressant response; it may modulate depression-associated [neurocircuitry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_circuit) and [inflammatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation) pathways.[^cite_note-177]

Ketamine appears to provide rapid (<12 h) and sustained (up to 1–2 weeks) reduction in anxiety across various clinical settings, with improvements correlating with depression improvements but not with peak dissociation.[^cite_note-178] Ketamine has shown potential for rapid and tolerable symptom relief in [obsessive-compulsive disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsessive%E2%80%93compulsive_disorder), but evidence is limited and inconsistent.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

Several systematic reviews and meta-analyses suggest ketamine may produce short-term (≈ 24 hr) reductions in [post-traumatic stress disorder](82974.md) symptoms, but overall evidence quality is low, long-term effects are unclear, and side effects occur.[^cite_note-181]

Ketamine shows promising short-term effects in reducing withdrawal, cravings, and improving abstinence across substance use disorders, especially when combined with therapy; however, evidence remains limited and heterogeneous, with unresolved concerns about optimal dosing, long-term safety, and misuse risk.

Ketamine, especially when combined with psychotherapy, shows potential to reduce [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism) and [cocaine use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_dependence), but evidence is limited, mixed, and low-quality, with inconclusive results for [opioids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid_use_disorder).[^cite_note-182]

The [British](19097669.md) [critical psychiatrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_psychiatrist) [Joanna Moncrieff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Moncrieff) has critiqued the use and study of ketamine and related drugs like [psychedelics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_drug) for treatment of psychiatric disorders, highlighting concerns including excessive hype around these drugs, questionable biologically-based theories of benefit, blurred lines between medical and recreational use, flawed clinical trial findings, financial conflicts of interest, strong expectancy effects and large placebo responses, small and short-term benefits over placebo, and their potential for difficult experiences and adverse effects, among others.[^cite_note-Moncrieff2025-183]

## Veterinary uses



An empty vial of Ketamine used by veterinarians for injection

In [veterinary anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary_anesthesia), ketamine is often used for its anesthetic and analgesic effects on cats,[^cite_note-184] dogs,[^cite_note-185] [rabbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit), [rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat), and other small animals.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] It is frequently used in induction and anesthetic maintenance in horses. It is an important part of the "[rodent cocktail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodent_cocktail)", a mixture of drugs used for anesthetising [rodents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodents).[^cite_note-188] Veterinarians often use ketamine with sedative drugs to produce balanced anesthesia and analgesia, and as a constant-rate infusion to help prevent [pain wind-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_wind-up). Ketamine is also used to manage pain among large animals. It is the primary intravenous anesthetic agent used in equine surgery, often in conjunction with [detomidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detomidine) and [thiopental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiopental), or sometimes [guaifenesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaifenesin).[^cite_note-pmid7212404-189]

Ketamine appears not to produce sedation or anesthesia in snails. Instead, it appears to have an excitatory effect.[^cite_note-190]
