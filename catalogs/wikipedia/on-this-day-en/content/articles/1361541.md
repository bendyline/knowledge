**Deportivo Toluca Fútbol Club S.A. de C.V.** **(Toluca FC)** is a professional [football](10568.md) club based in [Toluca](17551726.md), Mexico. The club competes in [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX), the top division of [Mexican football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_football_league_system), and plays its home matches at [Nemesio Diez Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesio_Diez_Stadium). Toluca is [one of the most successful clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_in_Mexico_by_major_honours_won) in Mexican football, having won twelve Liga MX titles, the second highest total in the competition's history, along with several cup and super cup titles and three [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) titles, for a total of [23 official titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Toluca_FC_records_and_statistics).

Founded in 1917, Toluca was a founding member of the [Segunda División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Premier_de_M%C3%A9xico), Mexico's second-tier division, in 1950, before earning promotion to the top flight in 1953, from which it has never been [relegated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relegated). The club established itself in Mexican football in the 1960s, winning back-to-back league titles and their first international title. A second successful period followed in the late 1990s and early 2000s, when the club claimed four league titles and a second international title, and a third successful period in the mid-2020s, when Toluca won multiple titles, including two consecutive league championships and two more international tournaments. The club is set to compete in the [2026 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_Intercontinental_Cup) and the [2029 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2029_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), both for the first time in the club's history.

The club is commonly known as ***Diablos Rojos*** (Red Devils), with their traditional home [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) in [scarlet red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_\(color\)) also earning them the nickname ***Los Escarlatas*** (The Scarlets). They are also known as ***Los Choriceros*** (The Sausage Makers), a reference to [chorizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorizo), one of the city's most traditional foods. Toluca is one of the few major Mexican clubs without a formal derby. The club's all-time leading scorer is [José Saturnino Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saturnino_Cardozo), who played from 1995 to 2005 and scored 258 goals. Toluca also fields a [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca_FC_\(women\)) and maintains a [reserve and youth academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca_FC_Reserves_and_Academy) whose sides have won several league titles.

## History

### Foundation and early years (1917–1950)

See also: [List of Toluca FC seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Toluca_FC_seasons)

Deportivo Toluca Fútbol Club S.A. de C.V., known simply as Toluca FC,[^cite_note-1] was founded on 12 February 1917,[^cite_note-2] in [Toluca, State of Mexico](17551726.md), by a group of businessmen and local sports enthusiasts.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The club traces its origins to two amateur football teams, La Huerta and Xinantécatl, both linked to the Henkel Bross family, a [German-descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Mexicans) family based in Toluca.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960491-5] La Huerta drew players from a family-owned *[hacienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda)*, while Xinantécatl drew from workers employed by the [Toluca–Tenango and San Juan Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca%E2%80%93Tenango_and_San_Juan_Railways) and a utilities company owned by the family.[^cite_note-DigitalMex-seg-6] Between 1915 and 1917, both sides competed in friendly matches that attracted local attention, under the guidance of physical education coach Filiberto Navas Valdés.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960491-5] In 1917, brothers Gerardo and Román Ferrat Alday, owners of the clothing business La Valenciana, formed their own side to compete with Henkel's teams.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960492-7][^cite_note-8] Following discussions among the Ferrat brothers, the brothers Francisco and Manuel Henkel Bross, Navas Valdés, and other organizers, the parties formally established the club, naming it Club Deportivo Toluca (CDT).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960492-7][^cite_note-NuestraHistoria-9]

Following the club's founding, several other company-sponsored teams emerged in Toluca. In 1918, the club organized a local competition featuring La Huerta, Xinantécatl, Unión, and Anahuác, which Toluca won. The following year, the club hosted [Real España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Club_Espa%C3%B1a), a [Mexico City](18987.md) football club, in their first match against outside opposition.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960493-10] Toluca remained the dominant club in the region for several years, though rivals emerged to challenge them. Fernando Barreto, a prominent local player, organized a succession of sides with the aim of defeating Toluca, beginning with Águila. When Águila failed to defeat Toluca and was dissolved, Barreto founded Cuauhtémoc, then later Reforma, which finally defeated Toluca in 1923. Having achieved his goal, Barreto dissolved Reforma, and many of the amateur players from these sides joined Toluca by 1925.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960493–494-11]



Toluca FC's team picture in 1917.

[^cite_note-NuestraHistoria-9]

In the 1920s, other clubs continued to emerge across Mexico, including Industrial, Triángulo Verde, Constancia, and Necaxa. Facing limited local competition, Toluca sought matches against outside clubs, including [Irapuato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Irapuato), [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Guadalajara), [América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica), and [ADO Orizaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADO_Orizaba?action=edit\&redlink=1). Among the prominent players of the era were the trio of Barreto, Ricardo Barraza, and Vicente Téllez, alongside the emerging [Benito Contreras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Contreras), who had come up through the club's ranks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960495–496-12] By 1930, Jesús Piña had become the club's leading player, while Barreto had retired from playing and moved into a management role.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960496–497-13]

Toluca's first recorded international match was played in 1935 against Costa Rican club [La Libertad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Sport_La_Libertad?action=edit\&redlink=1), a 2–0 home defeat. In 1937, Toluca defeated the [Basque Country national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_national_football_team_results) during the latter's tour of Mexico, in which the touring side also faced several local clubs and the Mexico national team. In the national amateur tournaments of 1937 and 1938, the State of Mexico representative team was composed largely of Toluca players.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960497-14] By 1943, Toluca had shifted focus to local and inter-state competitions, appointing Samuel Martínez García to their management. Under his direction, the club sought more involvement in the Reserve Tournament, organized in Mexico City, to expose the team to more competitive opposition. Toluca won the championship in 1945, with Héctor Barraza as their leading player.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960497–498-15] In 1947, the club attempted to establish the [Segunda División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Premier_de_M%C3%A9xico), Mexico's second-tier football division, but was unsuccessful, with the league not being formally constituted until 1950.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECid_y_Mulet1960499-16]

### Entry into professional football and promotion (1950–1960)

On 18 January 1950, thirty-three years after its founding,[^cite_note-17] Toluca joined the newly established Segunda División in the [1950–51 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950%E2%80%9351_Mexican_Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_season) as part of the expansion of professional football in Mexico,[^cite_note-NuestraHistoria-9][^cite_note-18] alongside founding clubs Irapuato, [Zamora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Zamora), [Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Pachuca), [Querétaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quer%C3%A9taro_F.C.), [Morelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Morelia), and [Zacatepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacatepec_F.C.).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Toluca won the Segunda División title in the [1952–53 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952%E2%80%9353_Mexican_Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_season) under Spanish coach Tomás Fábregas, earning promotion to the [Primera División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX), Mexico's top-tier football division. The title was secured on 18 January 1953, following a 3–3 draw against Irapuato at the [Estadio Revolución](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Revoluci%C3%B3n_\(Irapuato\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), in which Toluca came from three goals down, with [Rubén Pichardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Pichardo?action=edit\&redlink=1) scoring twice and [Mateo de la Tijera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mateo_de_la_Tijera?action=edit\&redlink=1) adding the equalizer.[^cite_note-21] Toluca finished the season with 35 points, three ahead of second-placed [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Veracruz). De la Tijera ended the season as the tournament's top scorer with 22 goals.[^cite_note-22]

Toluca made its Primera División debut on 9 August 1953, winning 2–1 against [Atlante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlante_F.C.).[^cite_note-Marca65-23][^cite_note-24] Since their promotion, the club has never been relegated,[^cite_note-25] establishing one of the longest uninterrupted tenures in the top tier flight of [Mexican football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Mexico).[^cite_note-Marca65-23] On 8 August 1954, Toluca inaugurated their home stadium, then known as Campo Patria and now as [Nemesio Diez Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesio_Diez_Stadium), in an exhibition match against Yugoslavia's [Dinamo Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNK_Dinamo_Zagreb), losing 1–4.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Toluca won their first official title with the [1955–56 Copa México](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955%E2%80%9356_Copa_M%C3%A9xico),[^cite_note-28] defeating Irapuato 2–1 in the final on 27 May 1956 at the [Estadio Olímpico Ciudad de los Deportes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Ciudad_de_los_Deportes). In the [1956 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones?action=edit\&redlink=1) super cup, they faced league champions [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Le%C3%B3n), losing 2–1.[^cite_note-29]

In 1959,[^cite_note-30] following the death of club president [Luis Gutiérrez Dosal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Guti%C3%A9rrez_Dosal?action=edit\&redlink=1), Toluca faced a period of institutional instability.[^cite_note-31] [President of Mexico](24356889.md) [Adolfo López Mateos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_L%C3%B3pez_Mateos), a native of the State of Mexico, personally requested Spanish-born businessman [Nemesio Diez Riega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesio_Diez_Riega?action=edit\&redlink=1) to assume control of the club.[^cite_note-32] Diez, who had previously held management roles at Toluca and had served as a sponsor, agreed with the ambition of making the club league champions.[^cite_note-33]

### First success era and transitional period (1960s–1980s)

Toluca's first major period of success came under coach [Ignacio Trelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Trelles),[^cite_note-34] who joined the club in October 1966.[^cite_note-ESPN-47-35] The club won its first league title in the [1966–67 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966%E2%80%9367_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season), clinching it on 19 February 1967, with a 2–0 victory over Necaxa, both goals scored by [Juan Dosal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Dosal?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-36] That season, Toluca striker [Amaury Epaminondas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaury_Epaminondas) finished as the league's top scorer with 21 goals.[^cite_note-ESPN-47-35] In the [1967 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones?action=edit\&redlink=1) super cup, Toluca defeated León 1–0 to claim the title.[^cite_note-37] Toluca retained the title in the [1967–68 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season),[^cite_note-38] with the championship secured on 4 January 1968, through a 3–0 victory over [Pumas UNAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumas_UNAM), who ended second in the league table. The goals came from an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) by [José Luis González Dávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Gonz%C3%A1lez_D%C3%A1vila) and strikes from Dosal and [Vicente Pereda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Pereda).[^cite_note-39] In the [1968 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones?action=edit\&redlink=1) super cup, Toluca faced [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_F.C.), drawing 3–3 on aggregate before winning on penalties. [Albino Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albino_Morales) converted all three penalties to secure the title for Toluca.[^cite_note-40]



[Vicente Pereda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Pereda), Toluca's all-time leading Mexican scorer and a key player of the 1960s.

[^cite_note-41]

In international competition, Toluca entered the [1968 CONCACAF Champions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_CONCACAF_Champions'_Cup), advancing past the first round with a 7–3 aggregate victory over [New York Greek American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Greek_American_SC). Toluca were subsequently declared champions on 19 December 1968 without a final being played after the other finalists, [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_FC_\(Guatemala\)) of Guatemala and [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.V._Transvaal) of Suriname, were disqualified from the competition.[^cite_note-42] Having won the CONCACAF tournament,[^cite_note-Estudiantes-1969-43] Toluca qualified for the inaugural [Copa Interamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Interamericana) against Argentina's [Estudiantes de La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estudiantes_de_La_Plata).[^cite_note-44] After losing 2–1 at home and winning 2–1 away in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), a third play-off match was held in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo), where Estudiantes won 3–0 to claim [the title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Copa_Interamericana).[^cite_note-Palmares_Interamericana-45]

Toluca won its third league championship in the [1974–75 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season) under Uruguayan manager [Ricardo de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_de_Le%C3%B3n_\(footballer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). The club finished top of the league table and advanced to the playoffs, where they faced [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul), León, and [Unión de Curtidores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_de_Curtidores) in home and away matches.[^cite_note-46] Against Cruz Azul, Toluca lost the first leg 1–0 before winning the second 1–0. Against Curtidores, they won 3–0 away and 2–1 at home.[^cite_note-47] The title was sealed on 26 June 1975 in the decisive match against León, with [Ítalo Estupiñán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Dtalo_Estupi%C3%B1%C3%A1n) scoring the only goal in a 1–0 victory.[^cite_note-48] In the [1975 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones) super cup, Toluca lost 1–0 against Pumas UNAM in the [Estadio Olímpico Universitario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Ol%C3%ADmpico_Universitario).[^cite_note-Palmares_CdC-49] Throughout his tenure, De León implemented a defensive system inspired by the Italian *[catenaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenaccio)*, earning Toluca the nickname ***Cangrejo Rojo*** (Red Crab) for the club's defensive style of play.[^cite_note-50]

Following its previous successes, Toluca endured a prolonged period without league titles through the 1980s and into the early 1990s,[^cite_note-SI-Sequias-51] frequently finishing in the bottom half of the table and qualifying for the playoffs only occasionally, though the club never dropped out of the top flight.[^cite_note-52] An exception came in 1989, when Toluca won the [1988–89 Copa México](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Copa_M%C3%A9xico), defeating [Leones Negros UdeG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leones_Negros_UdeG) 3–2 on aggregate in the final, ending a 14-year wait for a title.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-milenioTolincomprendido-54] However, the league title remained out of reach during this period.[^cite_note-SI-Sequias-51]

### Second dominant era (1998–2010)

In August 1997, Toluca appointed Mexican coach [Enrique Meza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Meza).[^cite_note-55] Ahead of the [Verano 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Verano_1998) season, Meza took the squad on a pre-season tour of Croatia and Slovenia in December 1997, with the aim of building international experience and team cohesion. The tour yielded two wins and a draw against local sides, with Meza noting a stronger sense of unity among the players upon their return.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Toluca won the Verano 1998 tournament, defeating Necaxa 6–4 on aggregate in the final to secure their fourth league title on 10 May 1998,[^cite_note-58] their first in 23 years.[^cite_note-59] Toluca's striker [José Saturnino Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saturnino_Cardozo), who had joined the club in 1995,[^cite_note-cardozoclausura2005-60] finished as the tournament's top scorer with 18 goals, 13 in the regular season and five in the playoffs.[^cite_note-61] The following year, Toluca won its fifth league title with the [Verano 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Verano_1999),[^cite_note-62] topping the table before defeating Atlas on penalties after a 5–5 aggregate in the final.[^cite_note-63] The match is widely regarded as one of the greatest finals in Mexican football.[^cite_note-64] Cardozo again finished as the league's top scorer with 20 goals, 15 in the regular season and five in the playoffs.[^cite_note-65]

Toluca continued its run of success under Meza into the new century, winning the [Verano 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Verano_2000) title with a 7–1 aggregate victory over [Santos Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Laguna) to claim their sixth league championship.[^cite_note-66] Three days after the victory, in June 2000, club owner Nemesio Diez Riega died, with his son [Valentín Diez Morodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valent%C3%ADn_Diez_Morodo?action=edit\&redlink=1) assuming ownership of the club.[^cite_note-67] In the [Invierno 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Invierno_2000), Meza departed mid-season in September to take charge of the Mexico national team.[^cite_note-68] Toluca nonetheless reached the final against [Monarcas Morelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Morelia), drawing 3–3 on aggregate before losing on penalties.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] In the [Invierno 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Invierno_2001) season, Argentine manager [Ricardo La Volpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_La_Volpe) joined Toluca.[^cite_note-71] Under La Volpe, Toluca had a strong [Apertura 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2002) campaign, although he departed during the season to take charge of the Mexico national team.[^cite_note-72] Toluca went on to win the championship in December under newly-appointed Argentine coach [Alberto Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Jorge), defeating Monarcas Morelia 4–2 on aggregate in the final to claim the club's seventh league title.[^cite_note-73] Cardozo finished as the season's top scorer with 36 goals: 29 in the regular season and seven in the playoffs. The total remains the highest goal tally recorded in a single tournament in Mexican football history.[^cite_note-74]



Paraguayan forward [José Saturnino Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saturnino_Cardozo) is the club's all-time goalscorer.

[^cite_note-75]

In October 2003, under Brazilian coach [Ricardo Ferretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Ferretti), Toluca won their second international title with the [2003 CONCACAF Champions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_CONCACAF_Champions'_Cup), defeating Monarcas Morelia 5–4 on aggregate.[^cite_note-76] On 1 November, in the [Apertura 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2003) season, Toluca [defeated América 6–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca_FC_6%E2%80%930_Club_Am%C3%A9rica_\(2003\)) at Nemesio Diez Stadium, a match that featured a collective team goal by Cardozo widely regarded as one of the finest ever scored in Liga MX history.[^cite_note-77] About two weeks later, Toluca competed in the [2003 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones?action=edit\&redlink=1) super cup, drawing 1–1 in regular time before defeating Monterrey on penalties.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

Cardozo departed at the end of the [Clausura 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Clausura_2005) as the club's all-time leading scorer with 258 goals.[^cite_note-cardozoclausura2005-60][^cite_note-80] In the [Apertura 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2005), under Argentine coach [Américo Gallego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9rico_Gallego), Toluca reached the final against [Monterrey](547412.md), drawing 3–3 at home before winning 3–0 away to claim the title.[^cite_note-81] Gallego was often criticized for his disciplined, defensively oriented style of play throughout the tournament.[^cite_note-82] In the [2006 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones) super cup, Toluca defeated [Pachuca](465062.md) 2–0 on aggregate to win the title.[^cite_note-83] In April 2006, Toluca reached the [2006 CONCACAF Champions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_CONCACAF_Champions'_Cup) final against América. After a goalless first leg, Toluca lost 2–1 away at the [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md) in the second leg, with all the goals scored in extra time.[^cite_note-84] Later that year, Toluca reached the [Apertura 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2006) final against Guadalajara but lost 3–2 on aggregate to finish as runners-up.[^cite_note-85]

Under Mexican coach [José Manuel de la Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_de_la_Torre), Toluca claimed the [Apertura 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2008) championship, defeating Cruz Azul on penalties after a 2–2 aggregate draw in the final to secure their ninth league title.[^cite_note-86] De la Torre credited the team's tactical discipline and mental resilience as key factors in their success throughout the tournament.[^cite_note-87] Toluca striker [Héctor Mancilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Mancilla) finished as the league's top scorer with 13 goals, 11 in the regular season and two in the playoffs.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] De la Torre led Toluca to their tenth league title in the [Bicentenario 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season) tournament, defeating Santos Laguna on penalties after a 2–2 aggregate draw in the final.[^cite_note-90]

### Title absence (2010–2020)

After the Bicentenario 2010 title,[^cite_note-91] Toluca went without a league title for the remainder of the 2010s.[^cite_note-92] In April 2014, with Cardozo serving as head coach, Toluca reached the [2013–14 CONCACAF Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_CONCACAF_Champions_League_final) against Cruz Azul.[^cite_note-93] After a goalless first leg away, the return leg at Nemesio Diez Stadium ended 1–1, with Cruz Azul claiming [the title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_CONCACAF_Champions_League) on away goals.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] In the [Apertura 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Liga_MX_season) season, Toluca reached the final against [Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Tijuana), a club that had recently been promoted from the second division of Mexican football, losing 4–1 on aggregate.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

During the [Apertura 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016-17_Liga_MX_season), Toluca played their home matches at [Estadio Universitario Alberto "Chivo" Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_Alberto_%22Chivo%22_C%C3%B3rdoba) while Nemesio Diez Stadium underwent renovation.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] Their final match at the temporary venue was played on 8 January 2017, a 4–1 victory over Atlas on the opening matchday of the [Clausura 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Liga_MX_season), before the club returned to the renovated Nemesio Diez Stadium.[^cite_note-100]

On 12 February 2017, the date of the club's centenary, Toluca played their anniversary fixture against [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Veracruz) at Nemesio Diez Stadium in the sixth round of the Clausura 2017 season,[^cite_note-101] winning 1–0 through a header by Colombian forward [Fernando Uribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Uribe).[^cite_note-102] Ahead of the match, the club held a commemorative ceremony on the pitch featuring an orchestra and a display of the various crests used throughout the club's history. Tributes were paid to several historical players, and the event was attended by prominent figures in Mexican football. At half time, the club's board presented awards to former players and to the families of deceased players who had made significant contributions to the club.[^cite_note-103] On 25 July 2017, Toluca played a centenary friendly against [Atlético Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid) of Spain at Nemesio Diez Stadium, drawing 0–0.[^cite_note-104]

In 2018, under head coach [Hernán Cristante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cristante), Toluca reached the Copa MX's [Clausura 2018 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausura_2018_Copa_MX_final), losing to an own goal in the closing minutes of a single-leg match against Necaxa at [Estadio Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Victoria).[^cite_note-105] Weeks later, Toluca finished as the top-seeded team of the Liga MX's [Clausura 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Liga_MX_season) season and reached [the finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausura_2018_Liga_MX_final_phase),[^cite_note-106] where they were defeated 3–2 on aggregate by [Santos Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Laguna).[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

### 2021–present

At the end of the [Clausura 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Liga_MX_season) season, Toluca finished 16th in the relegation table under manager [Ignacio Ambriz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Ambr%C3%ADz) following a 4–4 draw with León. The relegation table, calculated from points accumulated over three seasons, placed Toluca in a position that would have made them eligible for [relegation](648049.md). However, as Liga MX had abolished the relegation system, the club was instead required to pay a MXN\$33 million fine.[^cite_note-110] In response, the club's board implemented a significant transfer strategy, investing approximately €92 million over three years to strengthen the team after more than a decade without a league title.[^cite_note-111] In the subsequent [Apertura 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Liga_MX_season), Toluca reached [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apertura_2022_Liga_MX_final_phase) but were defeated by Pachuca 8–2 on aggregate.[^cite_note-112]



Toluca celebrates its eleventh title against [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) in the [Clausura 2025 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausura_2025_Liga_MX_final_phase#Finals)

In December 2024,[^cite_note-113] Argentine coach [Antonio Mohamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Mohamed) was appointed ahead of the [Clausura 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Liga_MX_season#Torneo_Clausura) season.[^cite_note-114] Toluca finished top of the table during the season and reached the final against América, who were seeking a fourth consecutive league title.[^cite_note-115] Toluca defeated them 2–0 on aggregate to claim their eleventh league title, their first in 15 years.[^cite_note-116] Toluca and América faced themselves again for the [2025 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones) super cup, where Toluca claimed the title with a 1–3 victory.[^cite_note-117] The club continued its success in the [Apertura 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Liga_MX_season) season, again finishing top of the table.[^cite_note-118] In the final, they faced [Tigres UANL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_UANL), drawing 2–2 on aggregate before defeating them 9–8 on penalties to secure their twelfth league title and a second consecutive league championship.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

The following year, Toluca competed in the [2026 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup), reaching [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup_final) against Tigres UANL after defeating several North American clubs in the knockout stage.[^cite_note-123] The final was played as a single-leg match at Nemesio Diez Stadium, with Toluca and Tigres drawing 1–1 after extra time before Toluca won 6–5 on penalties.[^cite_note-124] Toluca claimed its third CONCACAF Champions Cup title and its first international title in 23 years.[^cite_note-125] The victory also earned Toluca qualification to the [2026 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_Intercontinental_Cup) and the [2029 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2029_FIFA_Club_World_Cup),[^cite_note-126] both for the first time in the club's history.[^cite_note-127]

On 25 July 2026, Toluca contested the [2026 Campeón de Campeones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones) super cup against [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) at [Dignity Health Sports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignity_Health_Sports_Park) in [Carson, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson,_California), losing the match 3–1.[^cite_note-128] On September 6, 2026, Toluca faced Monterrey in the [2026 Leagues Cup final](82980174.md) at [Shell Energy Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Energy_Stadium) in [Houston, Texas](13774.md), winning 2–0 to claim their first [Leagues Cup](60906407.md) title and becoming the first Liga MX club to win the competition since it received official CONCACAF sanctioning.[^cite_note-129]

## Club identity

Toluca is the second most successful club in Liga MX history with twelve league titles, level with Guadalajara and behind only América. The team has been among the division's most consistent competitors since the introduction of the *torneos cortos* (short tournaments) format in 1996,[^cite_note-130] in which the annual Liga MX season was divided into two tournaments each concluded by a *liguilla* (playoff).[^cite_note-131] Despite this record, Toluca does not hold the same level of popular recognition as the traditional [Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_\(Mexico\)), and there is ongoing debate in Mexican football media over whether Toluca should be included in that group or recognized as part of an expanded Big Five.[^cite_note-milenioTolincomprendido-54][^cite_note-132] Several players, football figures, and sports analysts have cited the club's league titles and sustained presence in the top division as grounds for considering Toluca among the most important clubs in Mexican football.[^cite_note-133]

Toluca's most recognized nickname is ***Diablos Rojos*** (Red Devils), which is meant to denote the team's competitive resolve.[^cite_note-Infobae-mascotas-134] The name is also believed to derive from the [devil's hand tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiranthodendron), a plant native to the State of Mexico whose red coloring matches the club's primary color and whose unusual flower resembles a hand.[^cite_note-135] The club's mascot, *Diablito* (Little Devil), has taken several forms over the years, including designs aimed at younger supporters.[^cite_note-Infobae-mascotas-134] Toluca is also known as ***Los Escarlatas*** (The Scarlets), a reference to the [scarlet red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_\(color\)) of their home kit,[^cite_note-Futbolero-hinchas-136] and as ***Los Choriceros*** (The Sausage Makers),[^cite_note-137] a reference to [chorizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorizo), one of the city's most traditional foods.[^cite_note-Futbolero-hinchas-136]

Toluca is the only major Mexican club without an active, formally defined derby.[^cite_note-FutbolTotal-clasico-138][^cite_note-139] While matches against other clubs carry a competitive weight due to historical playoff encounters, none are classified as traditional derbies.[^cite_note-140] Toluca previously contested a regional derby known as the *[Clásico Mexiquense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1sico_Mexiquense?action=edit\&redlink=1)* against [Toros Neza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toros_Neza), a club based in [Nezahualcóyotl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Nezahualc%C3%B3yotl).[^cite_note-141] The derby took place in the [1993–94 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season) but came to an end when Toros Neza were relegated at the close of the spring 2000 season.[^cite_note-FutbolTotal-clasico-138][^cite_note-142]

## Crest and colors

Toluca's crest has undergone several modifications throughout its history while retaining core design elements.[^cite_note-Futbolero-historia-143] The original featured a white background with a red C-shaped circle enclosing the initials "DT", standing for Deportivo Toluca, with the "C" representing Club and the full abbreviation CDT denoting Club Deportivo Toluca, a constant phrase across all crest versions.[^cite_note-OfficialCrest2006-144][^cite_note-Futbolero-historia-143] Subsequent versions incorporated olive branches and a crown with a cross on top framing the CDT monogram on a white circular background, with the full name Club Deportivo Toluca inscribed.[^cite_note-OfficialCrest2006-144] Stars representing each league championship won by the club were also added over time above the crown.[^cite_note-Futbolero-historia-143][^cite_note-145]

In November 2016,[^cite_note-146] a commemorative version was introduced as part of the club's centennial celebrations, retaining the CDT monogram within a circular frame bearing the inscription Deportivo Toluca Fútbol Club. The circular border is blue on the upper half, transitioning to the green, white, and red of the Mexican flag on the lower half, referencing the club's 100-year history.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] The club currently maintains three versions of its crest: a retro logo based on the original CDT monogram, an institutional crest featuring the crown and olive branches, and a commercial crest incorporating the green, white, red, and blue introduced in the centenary design.[^cite_note-149]

Toluca's primary home kit is red,[^cite_note-150] with an away kit typically in white and a third kit that varies by season.[^cite_note-151] The club's colors have evolved since its founding in 1917, when the team wore white shirts with blue shorts and socks, a combination chosen by co-founder Román Ferrat Alday. Around 1925 a horizontal blue stripe was added to the shirt, and in 1929 the club adopted red and white as its primary colors.[^cite_note-152]

As of 2025, Toluca's kit is supplied by American sportswear company [New Balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Balance); the club having signed a four-year contract with the company in 2023, running through 2027.[^cite_note-Bolavip2026-153][^cite_note-154] Mexican motor oil company Roshfrans is the club's current main sponsor.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] Other sponsors featured on Toluca's kit in 2025 included Red Cola, Caliente,[^cite_note-157] and [Corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Modelo).[^cite_note-158] In January 2025, Mexican airline [Volaris](3717400.md) became the club's official airline partner.[^cite_note-159] Electrolit has also been a club sponsor since 2024, following previous sponsorships of Toluca's women's and youth teams.[^cite_note-160] In May 2026, [GAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAC_Group) became an official partner of Toluca and the club's official automotive sponsor.[^cite_note-161] Previous kit suppliers have included [Under Armour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Armour),[^cite_note-162] [Atletica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atletica),[^cite_note-163] [Diadora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadora), and [Corona Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Modelo).[^cite_note-164]

## Support

The club's primary supporter group, Barra Perra Brava ([lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) fierce female dog group), was formally founded in December 1985 by a group of local fans led by Rolando González Medina,[^cite_note-Universal-PerraBrava-165] many of whom were truck drivers who had gathered regularly at matches before the group grew large enough for Toluca to designate them a section of the stadium. The group takes its name from González Medina's nickname, La Perra,[^cite_note-OEM-PerraBrava-166] and was originally known as Porra Brava before adopting its current name, with the term *[barra brava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_brava)* drawn from Argentine supporter group nomenclature.[^cite_note-Universal-PerraBrava-165] The group is also commonly referred to simply as Perra Brava.[^cite_note-167]

Traditionally located in the non-shaded stands behind the goal at Nemesio Diez Stadium, the section was originally designated for visiting supporters before Perra Brava claimed it as their own and were later formally assigned the area.[^cite_note-OEM-PerraBrava-166] The group is known for a distinctive celebration in which members remove their shirts whenever Toluca scores their first goal, regardless of weather.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] The tradition originated during a match against Monterrey played in cold, sleeting conditions,[^cite_note-Universal-PerraBrava-165] when González Medina removed his shirt to celebrate a goal, a gesture that was adopted by other members and became a permanent custom.[^cite_note-Universal-PerraBrava-165][^cite_note-OEM-PerraBrava-166] On matchdays, the group organizes supporter caravans, banners, and pre-match displays.[^cite_note-170]



Toluca supporters chanting prior to a match outside of Nemesio Diez Stadium

The group is legally registered as a non-profit organization, maintains a formal office, and advocates for a family-oriented atmosphere at matches.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172] It publicly opposes violence in football stadiums and has sought legislation in favor of prosecuting those who engage in violent activities during matches.[^cite_note-173] The group also organizes charitable initiatives in the State of Mexico including toy, clothing, and adaptive equipment donations, athletic events such as road races, tree planting drives, and community outreach programs.[^cite_note-Universal-PerraBrava-165][^cite_note-174]

Other supporter groups have emerged within the Toluca fan base since the Perra Brava.[^cite_note-OEM-BandaRojo-175] Among them is La Banda del Rojo (lit. The Red Ones' Band), founded in February 2004 by Ángel Contreras Valinor with an initial group of 26 members, originally a youth faction of Perra Brava that broke away following internal disagreements.[^cite_note-Milenio-BandaRojo-176] The group adopted a South American style of support characterized by continuous chanting, coordinated banners, and musical instruments, and is also located in the non-shaded stands of the stadium.[^cite_note-Milenio-BandaRojo-176] Initially, the group faced resistance from other supporter groups and the club due to its unorthodox style, but was eventually accepted.[^cite_note-OEM-BandaRojo-175][^cite_note-177] In the 2020s, the group adopted the song La Cumbia de los Trapos by Argentine group [Yerba Brava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerba_Brava?action=edit\&redlink=1) as an unofficial anthem, sung regularly throughout matches.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

Fan attendance for Toluca was the highest in Liga MX across the five league tournaments played between 2023 and 2025, averaging 94 percent occupancy per game.[^cite_note-180] Toluca's national support has fluctuated across surveys conducted by Mexican polling firm Consulta Mitofsky. The club ranked ninth nationally in both 2016 and 2017, with 2.6% and 2.3% of respondents respectively naming it as their preferred club,[^cite_note-181] rising to 2.5% in 2020 and 3.0% in 2022, when it ranked seventh.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] An independent 2024 study by Nielsen Sports Mexico ranked Toluca eighth among Liga MX clubs, with 3.1% of respondents identifying it as their favorite.[^cite_note-184] In Mitofsky's 2025 survey, Toluca ranked fifth among the most-supported clubs in Mexico with 4.4% of preferences, and fifth in the firm's measure of interest at 5.3%.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-TolucaMitofsky2025-186] By 2026, Toluca's share had risen to 6.1%, maintaining fifth place, with Mitofsky attributing part of the growth to the club's championship titles.[^cite_note-187]

## Stadium

Main article: [Nemesio Diez Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesio_Diez_Stadium)



Nemesio Diez Stadium, known for its compact shape and stands' close proximity to the pitch.

[^cite_note-Milenio-NemesioPL-188]

Nemesio Diez Stadium, one of the oldest football stadiums in Mexico, is the home ground of Toluca, located in the city's downtown area.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] Originally inaugurated on 8 August 1954, the stadium has carried several names throughout its history before being renamed in honor of Nemesio Diez Riega.[^cite_note-191] It has a capacity of 30,000 and sits at an elevation of about 2,670 m (8,760 ft) above sea level, making it one of the highest-altitude football stadiums in Mexico.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] The stadium is known by two nicknames: *La Bombonera* (The Chocolate Box), a reference to its compact shape, and *El Infierno* (The Hell), a nod to the club's nickname, *Diablos Rojos* (Red Devils).[^cite_note-194]

The stadium has hosted matches in the [1970](157227.md) and [1986 FIFA World Cups](157231.md),[^cite_note-195] the [1975 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Pan_American_Games),[^cite_note-196] the [1983 FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship),[^cite_note-197] and the [1990 Central American and Caribbean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Central_American_and_Caribbean_Games).[^cite_note-198] The stadium underwent a significant renovation in 2017, expanding its capacity and modernizing its facilities with updated audiovisual infrastructure,[^cite_note-199] while preserving its distinctive English-style architecture, including the stands' close proximity to the pitch.[^cite_note-Milenio-NemesioPL-188]

Prior to playing at Nemesio Diez Stadium, Toluca's earliest facilities were located on what is now Paseo Colón in downtown Toluca, consisting of a football pitch with wooden stands.[^cite_note-DigitalMex-seg-6][^cite_note-TolucaFC-Nemesio-200] In 1919, the club relocated to the grounds of the Presa de Gachupines, which served as their headquarters until 1921, when they moved to Campo del Tívoli, a venue that hosted international matches. Following the club's promotion to professional football, Campo del Tívoli served as their home ground until 1952,[^cite_note-TolucaFC-Nemesio-200] when the club moved to Campo Patria, the site on which Nemesio Diez Stadium now stands.[^cite_note-201]

## Honours

Main article: [List of Toluca FC records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Toluca_FC_records_and_statistics)

Toluca is one of the most successful clubs in Mexican football, having won twelve Liga MX titles, the second highest total in the competition's history, along with two Copa MX titles, five Campeón de Campeones, three CONCACAF Champions Cup titles and one Leagues Cup for a total of 23 official titles.[^cite_note-224]

## International competitions

Toluca has competed in several international tournaments.[^cite_note-DiarioAS-inter-232] As a [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) club, representing the North American, Central American, and Caribbean football confederation, their primary regional competition is the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup),[^cite_note-233] in which they have participated on multiple occasions since 1968,[^cite_note-concacaf-profile-234] winning the title three times in 1968, 2003, and 2026.[^cite_note-235] The 1968 title earned them qualification to the [Copa Interamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Interamericana), where they finished as runners-up.[^cite_note-Estudiantes-1969-43] Toluca has also participated as an invitee in [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) tournaments, the South American football confederation, including the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md), [Copa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Sudamericana), and the now-defunct [Copa Merconorte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Merconorte), with their involvement spanning from 2000 to 2016.[^cite_note-Milenio-Libertadores-236][^cite_note-DiarioAS-inter-232] The club has additionally competed in the [Leagues Cup](60906407.md), a competition between Liga MX and [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS) clubs, since 2023.[^cite_note-RSSSFLeaguesCup-237]

## Affiliate teams

### Women's club

Main article: [Toluca FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca_FC_\(women\))

Toluca's women's team debuted in the [Liga MX Femenil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX_Femenil), Mexico's top women's football league, in 2017,[^cite_note-OEM-TolucaFemenil-243] having hosted the [inaugural Copa MX Femenil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Copa_MX_Femenil) ahead of the league's first season.[^cite_note-244] In their first league match, Toluca defeated [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul_\(women\)) 2–1.[^cite_note-OEM-TolucaFemenil-243] The team plays home matches at Nemesio Diez Stadium and the Metepec Facilities in [Metepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metepec), State of Mexico, the latter also serving as their training ground.[^cite_note-OEM-TolucaFemenil-243][^cite_note-245]

In 2026, the club strengthened its squad with the signing of several high-profile international players, including French players [Eugénie Le Sommer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A9nie_Le_Sommer), [Amandine Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amandine_Henry), and [Faustine Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faustine_Robert), and [Swedish](5058739.md) player [Sofia Jakobsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Jakobsson).[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

### Reserves and academy

Main article: [Toluca FC Reserves and Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca_FC_Reserves_and_Academy)

Toluca maintains several reserve and youth academy teams at the under-23 level,[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249] a number of which have won their respective youth league titles.[^cite_note-250] One reserve team, Atlético Mexiquense, was an affiliate club that competed in [Ascenso MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascenso_MX), Mexico's second-tier division, from 1997 until the end of the Clausura 2009 season.[^cite_note-251] Another reserve team, Toluca Premier, competed in the [Liga Premier de Ascenso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A_de_M%C3%A9xico), Mexico's third-tier division, from 2015 until 2019.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] In addition to its youth academy, Toluca operates approximately 40 sponsored football schools across several states in Mexico,[^cite_note-254] with a pathway for progression into the club's reserve and academy system.[^cite_note-255]
