**Logan Township** is a [township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township_\(New_Jersey\)) in [Gloucester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester_County,_New_Jersey) in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [New Jersey](21648.md). As of the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the township's population was 6,000,[^cite_note-Census2020-8][^cite_note-LWD2020-9] a decrease of 42 (−0.7%) from the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census) count of 6,042,[^cite_note-Census2010-17][^cite_note-LWD2010-18] which in turn reflected an increase of 10 (+0.2%) from the 6,032 counted in the [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census).[^cite_note-19]

Logan Township was formed as **West Woolwich Township** by the [New Jersey Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Legislature) on March 7, 1877, from portions of [Woolwich Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich_Township,_New_Jersey). That name lasted just less than a year, as the name was changed to Logan Township as of March 6, 1878.[^cite_note-Story-20] The community is named for [John Alexander "Black Jack" Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Logan), a [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) general who was behind the establishment of [Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day) as a national holiday.[^cite_note-About-21][^cite_note-22]

## Geography

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the township had a total area of 26.60 square miles (68.89 km<sup>2</sup>), including 21.93 square miles (56.79 km<sup>2</sup>) of land and 4.67 square miles (12.09 km<sup>2</sup>) of water (17.55%).[^cite_note-CensusArea-1][^cite_note-GR1-2]

[Beckett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckett,_New_Jersey) (with a 2020 census population of 4,834) and [Bridgeport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport,_New_Jersey) (population 389) are [unincorporated communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_New_Jersey#Unincorporated_communities) and [census-designated places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census-designated_place) (CDP) located in the township.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Other unincorporated communities, localities, and places located partially or completely within the township include Cadwalader, Center Square, Cooper Wharf, Coopers, Flood Gates, New Bridge, Nortonville, Prospect, Raccoon Island, and [Repaupo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repaupo,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-About-21][^cite_note-25] The township is home to [Pureland Industrial Complex](2840381.md), a 3,000-acre (12 km<sup>2</sup>) [industrial park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_park) that is one of the nation's largest.[^cite_note-26] The township also hosts the Delaware River Equestrian Agriculture Marina (DREAM) Park, a 1,600-acre County Park.[^cite_note-27]

The township borders the Gloucester County municipalities of [Greenwich Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Township,_Gloucester_County,_New_Jersey) and [Woolwich Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich_Township,_New_Jersey). Logan Township also borders the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River). [Oldmans Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldmans_Creek) serves as its border with [Oldmans Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldmans_Township,_New_Jersey) in [Salem County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_County,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] [Raccoon Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_Creek_\(New_Jersey\)) branches off from the Delaware River in Logan Township.

### 2010 census

The [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census) counted 6,042 people, 2,087 households, and 1,634 families in the township. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 275.6 per square mile (106.4/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 2,172 housing units at an average density of 99.1 per square mile (38.3/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup was 81.53% (4,926) [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._census\)), 12.50% (755) [Black or African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_\(U.S._census\)), 0.15% (9) [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._census\)), 2.55% (154) [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._census\)), 0.02% (1) [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._census\)), 1.13% (68) from [other races](273285.md), and 2.14% (129) from two or more races. [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._census\)) of any race were 3.97% (240) of the population.[^cite_note-Census2010-17]

Of the 2,087 households, 40.2% had children under the age of 18; 62.6% were married couples living together; 11.5% had a female householder with no husband present and 21.7% were non-families. Of all households, 17.4% were made up of individuals and 4.7% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.89 and the average family size was 3.29.[^cite_note-Census2010-17]

27.1% of the population were under the age of 18, 8.3% from 18 to 24, 27.6% from 25 to 44, 30.3% from 45 to 64, and 6.8% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 36.8 years. For every 100 females, the population had 96.7 males. For every 100 females ages 18 and older there were 92.6 males.[^cite_note-Census2010-17]

The Census Bureau's 2006–2010 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey) showed that (in 2010 [inflation-adjusted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_adjustment) dollars) [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) was \$87,209 (with a margin of error of +/− \$6,583) and the median family income was \$100,688 (+/− \$14,321). Males had a median income of \$67,192 (+/− \$7,690) versus \$49,914 (+/− \$4,283) for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the borough was \$35,587 (+/− \$2,882). About 1.6% of families and 3.7% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 2.4% of those under age 18 and none of those age 65 or over.[^cite_note-38]

There were 2,001 households, of which 48.1% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 66.3% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) living together, 10.1% had a female householder with no husband present, and 19.5% were non-families. 15.7% of all households were made up of individuals, and 4.3% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. ## Economy

[Pureland Industrial Complex](2840381.md) was established in the early 1970s when 45 farms covering 3,200 acres (1,300 ha) were assembled in Logan Township. Under the ownership of State Mutual Life Assurance Company of America, environmental studies were undertaken by Jack McCormick, the first scientist with a doctorate in environmental science. In 1971, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* described the project as "the nation's first ecologically planned industrial complex".[^cite_note-39]

In 1973, the Pureland Association was formed and Declaration of Environmental Standards were established and recorded in [Gloucester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester_County,_New_Jersey). Approximately 1,200 acres (490 ha) were placed in an environmental reserve in perpetuity and rigid environmental controls were established, prior to the establishment of the [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) or the [New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Department_of_Environmental_Protection). These restrictive covenants protect property owners' value and are enforced by the Pureland Association which consist of property owners.

In 1976, the initial infrastructure was established with public water, sewer, and rail servicing Pureland. In 1977, Center Square Real Estate Development Company took over management of Pureland for State Mutual Life Assurance Company of America. In 2000, Center Square Real Estate Development Company purchased the undeveloped land from State Mutual and continues to manage and develop Pureland today.

Pureland consists of 12,000,000 square feet (1,100,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of space and houses more than 180 companies employing over 8,500 people, mostly in warehousing and distribution. It is the largest industrial complex in New Jersey.

Logan Township was the proposed location for the [Crown Landing LNG Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Landing_LNG_Terminal), a [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) off-loading and processing facility to be sited along the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River). The facility would have included an off-loading pier that would technically enter the waters of the state of [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware). Delaware opposed the project and filed a lawsuit in federal court to stop the project from going forward on the basis that they control the waters in which part of the pier would be situated; a lawsuit the State of Delaware lost. The [Federal Energy Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Energy_Regulatory_Commission) approved the Crown Landing LNG Terminal on June 15, 2006.[^cite_note-40] In 2009, long after the project should have been completed, Hess Energy acquired the project after BP failed to get plans approved and construction started. On January 6, 2012, Hess Energy surrendered its authorization to construct and operate the terminal. In a letter to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), Hess said it determined that the terminal would not be profitable, saying that "significant increases in natural gas production from North American shale resources" would affect prices and demand for natural gas.

## Government

### Local government

Logan Township is governed within the [Faulkner Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faulkner_Act), formally known as the Optional Municipal Charter Law, under the [Small Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faulkner_Act_\(small_municipality\)) (Plan 3) form of New Jersey municipal government, enacted by direct petition as of January 1, 1984.[^cite_note-41] The township is one of 18 municipalities (of the 564) statewide that use this form of government, which is only available to municipalities with less than 12,000 at the time of adoption.[^cite_note-42] The governing body is comprised of a Mayor and a Township Council, with all positions elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large) on a partisan basis as part of the November general elections. The Mayor is elected directly by the voters to a four-year term of office. The four members of the Township Council are elected to serve three-year terms on a staggered basis, with either one or two seats up for election each year.[^cite_note-DataBook-3][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

The governing body oversees the operation of the township, passing resolutions and ordinances, as well as assessing and collecting taxes. Meetings of the Mayor and Township Council are held on a monthly basis and are open to the public. All Legislative powers of the Township are exercised by the Mayor and Council.

As of 2025, the [Mayor](101146.md) of Logan Township is [Democrat](5043544.md) Frank W. Minor, whose term of office ends December 31, 2027. Members of the Logan Township Council are [Deputy Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Mayor) Bernadine E. Jackson (D, 2027), Shleema Lawry (D, 2025), Christopher Morris (D, 2026) and Arthur Smith (D, 2025).[^cite_note-Officials-4][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-GloucesterDirectory-46][^cite_note-Gloucester2024-47][^cite_note-Gloucester2023-48][^cite_note-Gloucester2022-49]

## Education

The [Logan Township School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Township_School_District) serves public school students in [pre-kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-kindergarten) through [eighth grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_grade).[^cite_note-87] As of the 2021–22 school year, the district, comprised of three schools, had an enrollment of 876 students and 82.5 classroom teachers (on an [FTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-time_equivalent) basis), for a [student–teacher ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student%E2%80%93teacher_ratio) of 10.6\:1.[^cite_note-NCES-88] Schools in the district (with 2021–22 enrollment data from the [National Center for Education Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Education_Statistics)[^cite_note-89]) are Francis E. Donnelly Early Childhood Learning Center[^cite_note-90] with 197 students in grades PreK-K, Logan Elementary School[^cite_note-91] with 302 students in grades 1-4 and Logan Middle School[^cite_note-92] with 372 students in grades 5-8.[^cite_note-Narrative-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

Public school students in [ninth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_grade) through [twelfth grades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_grade) are educated at [Kingsway Regional High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsway_Regional_High_School) under a [sending/receiving relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sending/receiving_relationship) in which tuition is paid on a per-pupil basis to the [Kingsway Regional School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsway_Regional_School_District), which serves students in [seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_grade) through [twelfth grades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_grade) from [East Greenwich Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Greenwich_Township,_New_Jersey), [South Harrison Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Harrison_Township,_New_Jersey), [Swedesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedesboro,_New_Jersey) and [Woolwich Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich_Township,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] As of the 2021–22 school year, the district, comprised of two schools, had an enrollment of 2,863 students and 231.8 classroom teachers (on an [FTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-time_equivalent) basis), for a [student–teacher ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student%E2%80%93teacher_ratio) of 12.4\:1.[^cite_note-99] Schools in the district (with 2021–22 enrollment data from the [National Center for Education Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Education_Statistics)[^cite_note-100]) are Kingsway Regional Middle School[^cite_note-101] with 925 students in grades 7-8 and [Kingsway Regional High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsway_Regional_High_School)[^cite_note-102] with 1,893 students in grades 9-12.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Under a 2011 proposal, Kingsway would merge with its constituent member's K–6 districts to become a full K–12 district, with various options for including Logan Township as part of the consolidated district.[^cite_note-106]

Students from across the county are eligible to apply to attend [Gloucester County Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester_County_Institute_of_Technology), a four-year high school in [Deptford Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deptford_Township,_New_Jersey) that provides technical and vocational education. As a public school, students do not pay tuition to attend the school.[^cite_note-107] There are 60 students from the township who attend GCIT.[^cite_note-Narrative-93]

Guardian Angels Regional School (PreK–Grade 3 campus in [Gibbstown CDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbstown,_New_Jersey) and 4–8 campus in [Paulsboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulsboro,_New_Jersey)) takes students from Logan Township.[^cite_note-108] The school operates under the supervision of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Camden).[^cite_note-109]

## Transportation

### Roads and highways

As of May 2010, the township had a total of 72.03 miles (115.92 km) of roadways, of which 32.76 miles (52.72 km) were maintained by the municipality, 19.01 miles (30.59 km) by Gloucester County, 18.04 miles (29.03 km) by the [New Jersey Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Department_of_Transportation) and 2.22 miles (3.57 km) by the [Delaware River Port Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River_Port_Authority).[^cite_note-110]

Several state highways pass through the township.[^cite_note-111] These include [U.S. Route 130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_130),[^cite_note-112] [U.S. Route 322](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_322_in_New_Jersey)/[County Route 536](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Route_536_\(New_Jersey\)),[^cite_note-113] [Route 324](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Route_324), which runs for 1.5 miles (2.4 km) entirely in Logan Township,[^cite_note-114] and [Interstate 295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_295_\(New_Jersey\)).[^cite_note-115]

The [Commodore Barry Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_Barry_Bridge) is a [cantilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilever) bridge that spans the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) from [Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester,_Pennsylvania), [Pennsylvania](23332.md) to the Bridgeport section of Logan Township as part of US 322. Owned and operated by the [Delaware River Port Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River_Port_Authority), construction of the bridge began in 1969 and the bridge opened to traffic in February 1974.[^cite_note-116] The bridge is named for the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) hero and [Philadelphia](50585.md) resident, [John Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Barry_\(1745-1803\)).

### Public transportation

[NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) bus service is available between in the township between [Pennsville Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsville_Township,_New_Jersey) and [Philadelphia](50585.md) on the [402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/402_\(New_Jersey_bus\)) route.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]
