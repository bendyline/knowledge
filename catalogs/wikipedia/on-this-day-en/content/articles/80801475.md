On 20 August 2025, during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), [Israel](9282173.md) announced it had formally begun the "first stages" of a [military offensive](15077993.md) aiming to seize control of [Gaza City](55387.md) from [Hamas](13913.md),[^cite_note-france24-20250820-4] referred to in plans as **Operation Gideon's Chariots II**[^cite_note-jpost20250820-30][^cite_note-newarab20250820-31] or **Operation Gideon's Chariots B**[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-34] ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): מבצע מרכבות גדעון ב', [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *Mivtza Merkavat Gid'on B'*). These early stages were superseded by an expanded main offensive[^cite_note-timesofisrael20250916-8][^cite_note-35] that began on 15 September.[^cite_note-axios20250915-36][^cite_note-jpost20250915-6] Israel framed the offensive as a continuation or a second part of [Operation Gideon's Chariots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2025_Gaza_offensive), which lasted from 16 May to 4 August 2025.[^cite_note-jpost20250820-30][^cite_note-\:ictg-37]&#x20;

Hamas announced a [counter-offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterattack) titled **Operation Moses' Staff** in response to the Israeli operations.[^cite_note-\:ictg-37][^cite_note-shafaq20250903-38] It also allegedly transferred [Israeli hostages](75025451.md) to combat zones in Gaza City,[^cite_note-strikes30august-39][^cite_note-middleeastmonitor20250830-15] where they were intended to be used as human shields.[^cite_note-jpost20250817-40][^cite_note-ynetnews20250817-41]&#x20;

Experts said that the offensive would exacerbate the ongoing [humanitarian crisis in the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_humanitarian_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), with the [Integrated Food Security Phase Classification](16431152.md) (IPC) having confirmed a [famine](51329.md) in Gaza City on 22 August.[^cite_note-apnews20250822-42] Israel also warned that unless Hamas yielded to its terms, the city could be destroyed.[^cite_note-pbs20250822-43] By 30 September, around 1,250 buildings were reported to have been destroyed in the city,[^cite_note-JPost20250930-24] with several residential areas having been destroyed by Israeli bombings.[^cite_note-FR-44]&#x20;

On 4 October 2025, following a positive response by Hamas to [a peace plan](81215079.md) and a call by [American](3434750.md) [president](24113.md) [Donald Trump](4848272.md) for Israel to cease its military advances in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) has halted on orders of Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md).[^cite_note-ynetnews20251004-45][^cite_note-timesofisrael20251004-46] Following the implementation of the peace plan's ceasefire on 10 October, Israeli forces withdrew from parts of Gaza City.[^cite_note-\:0-47][^cite_note-\:1-48]&#x20;

## Prelude

### Background

The last large scale Israeli operation in Gaza City was [a siege](75208470.md) that lasted from November 2023 to January 2025. In April 2025, Israel began [an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Shuja%27iyya_offensive) into [Shuja'iyya](20956585.md), a neighborhood in the city's east. By August 2025, Gaza City was one of only three major population centers still under [Palestinian](23267.md) control.[^cite_note-49]&#x20;

### Preparations

On 4 August 2025, reports emerged that Netanyahu was to lay out a plan to his [war cabinet](75030844.md) the next day to expand Israeli military occupation across the entire [Gaza Strip](12047.md), including areas where hostages were being held. According to official close to the President, the goal of the operation was to secure a complete surrender from Hamas and the release of all hostages.[^cite_note-50] A three-hour preliminary meeting with [Defense Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defence_Minister) [Israel Katz](19041378.md), Strategic Affairs Minister [Ron Dermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Dermer), and IDF operations director and chief of staff [Itzik Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itzik_Cohen_\(officer\)) and [Eyal Zamir](61877756.md) respectively, took place on 5 August, during which Zamir laid out several options regarding the war in Gaza.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Zamir was opposed to Netanyahu's insistence on a full-scale occupation, warning that it would leave the IDF as well as the hostages vulnerable. The full cabinet meeting to decide the course of action was postponed until 7 August.[^cite_note-53]&#x20;

Netanyahu met opposition leader [Yair Lapid](2448072.md) on 6 August to discuss the occupation plan. Lapid reportedly told him that "occupying Gaza is a very bad idea" and would likely lead to the deaths of the hostages.[^cite_note-54] Netanyahu further elaborated on his plan during a [Fox News](11121.md) interview on 7 August, claiming that Israel was intent on capturing the Strip but that it would not annex or govern it, instead handing it over to an administration from other [Arab states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world).[^cite_note-55] Later in the day, the cabinet convened at 6\:00 p.m. local time,[^cite_note-56] in a meeting which lasted 10 hours and stretched into 8 August in Israel.[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

On 8 August 2025, Israel's security cabinet approved a plan to take over Gaza City.[^cite_note-58]&#x20;

Ahead of the offensive, Israel announced plans to relocate Palestinian civilians in Gaza City to the southern Gaza Strip,[^cite_note-aljazeera20250817-59] with the deadline for evacuation set for 7 October 2025,[^cite_note-cnn20250807-60] and intensified bombardments on the Gaza City neighborhoods of [Zeitoun](42179889.md), [Sabra](36311846.md), [Rimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimal), and [Tuffah](22080314.md).[^cite_note-aljazeera20250817-59]&#x20;

On 18 August, the IDF advanced into Sabra, and laid siege to a school and a [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) clinic.[^cite_note-61]&#x20;

On 20 August, Israeli defense minister Israel Katz approved the plans for the takeover of Gaza City.[^cite_note-jpost20250820-30] The IDF announced it would be calling up 60,000 [reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservist) for the offensive.[^cite_note-62]&#x20;

## Humanitarian crisis

### Forced displacement

Conflicting reports emerged about the scale of civilian evacuations from Gaza City. Mustafa Qazzaat, head of the emergency committee in the Gaza municipality, described the situation as "catastrophic", with "large numbers" fleeing eastern neighborhoods.[^cite_note-alarabiya20250821-93] *[Associated Press](18935732.md)* (*AP*) journalists witnessed "small groups" heading south from the city in the week leading up to the offensive, but no large-scale evacuation.[^cite_note-94] *[The New Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Arab)* described a "unified" reaction from residents of Gaza City, with families choosing to remain in their homes due to feeling there was no safe place in all of the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-newarab20250820-31] *Al-Monitor* reported that fleeing residents were mostly heading towards the coast.[^cite_note-95] As of 19 September, the [Palestinian Civil Defense](49984885.md) (PCD) in Gaza says 450,000 Gazans have fled the city,[^cite_note-\:israelwarns-26] while as of October, the IDF says the number is more than 870,000.[^cite_note-timesofisrael20251003-28]&#x20;

According to Ahed Ferwana, a Gaza-based political analyst, Operation Gideon's Chariots II was not only a military maneuever but also a continuation of an Israeli [demographic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_engineering) strategy that aimed to create the conditions for permanent displacement of Gazans.[^cite_note-newarab20250820-31]&#x20;

According to a report in *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)*, the IDF's [Military Advocate General](5216026.md) warned the [IDF Chief of Staff](3011266.md), [Eyal Zamir](61877756.md), that the evacuation orders for Gaza City contradicted [Israeli](8932627.md) and [international law](8195726.md) because the necessary conditions for receiving the population do not exist. This position was also supported by the [Research Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Department_\(Aman\)) in the [Military Intelligence Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Directorate_\(Israel\)). The areas marked as zones for taking in residents were already filled to capacity, and the area allotted per person under the Chief of Staff's plan was significantly smaller than international-law standards. Despite the warnings, the Chief of Staff ignored the position of the Military Advocate General.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]&#x20;

On 1 October, Katz stated that it was the "last opportunity" for Palestinians to evacuate Gaza City and that anyone who remains will be considered "terrorists and terror supporters". Hundreds of thousands of residents nonetheless remained, mostly because they could not afford to leave or were too weak to make the journey to tent camps in the south.[^cite_note-apnews20251001-98]&#x20;

### Famine

On 22 August, the IPC confirmed that a famine was occurring in Gaza City and said it could spread south to [Deir al-Balah](1470463.md) and [Khan Yunis](101330.md) by September. Israel disputed the report.[^cite_note-apnews20250822-42]&#x20;

The IPC said that three "thresholds" that indicate famine in the city were met, namely starvation, malnutrition, and mortality. At least 1 in 5 households faced an extreme shortage in their consumption of food; roughly 1 in 3 children or more were acutely malnourished; and at least 2 in every 10,000 residents were dying daily because of outright starvation or the combination of malnutrition and disease.[^cite_note-99]&#x20;

On 22 August, Katz warned that unless Hamas yields to Israel's terms the city could be destroyed.[^cite_note-pbs20250822-43] This threat had begun to be carried out, with residents of Gaza City reporting that the Israeli military was systematically razing parts of the city to the ground. One resident reported: "There is hardly any fighting going on, but heavy artillery and bulldozers are moving from one street to the other, destroying all of these residential clusters".[^cite_note-FR-44] *[Mondoweiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondoweiss)* reported that Israel had hired private contractors who used bulldozers to destroy entire neighbourhoods.[^cite_note-100]&#x20;

On 17 September, Israeli finance minister [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) said Gaza City was a "[real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_property) bonanza", and that its demolition would make way for [a rebuilding and renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Israeli_resettlement_of_the_Gaza_Strip).[^cite_note-101] Despite statements by Israeli government leaders, IDF officials told reporters that there was no policy to completely raze Gaza City neighborhoods.[^cite_note-102]&#x20;

### Israeli hostages

On 17 August, it was reported that Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad (PIJ) were considering a plan to transfer Israeli hostages to Gaza City in order to deter the offensive.[^cite_note-jpost20250817-40][^cite_note-ynetnews20250817-41] On 29 August, Abu Obeida confirmed that hostages had been transferred to combat zones.[^cite_note-strikes30august-39]&#x20;

On 5 September, Hamas published a new video of two Israeli hostages held in Gaza City, [Guy Gilboa-Dalal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Gilboa-Dalal) and [Alon Ohel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_of_Alon_Ohel). The video, purportedly filmed on 28 August, was rare in that it was filmed above ground, showing Gilboa-Dalal and Ohel being driven around the city in a car. According to statements made by Gilboa-Dalal, at least eight other hostages were being held in Gaza City, and the Israeli offensive endangered all of their lives.[^cite_note-103] Reportedly, Palestinian sources within Gaza informed [Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Public_Broadcasting_Corporation) (*Kan*) that the hostages are being held above ground in tents and residences with the aim of restricting Israeli forces from operating in certain areas.[^cite_note-104]&#x20;

On 17 September, it was reported that Israeli security officials privately informed the families of hostages that they lack specific information on their current locations.[^cite_note-jpost20250919-105] On 18 September, Hamas clarified that the Gaza City offensive means that Israel will not receive any hostages, dead or alive, comparing their fate to that of [Ron Arad](8015452.md).[^cite_note-jpost20250919-105] On 22 September, Hamas released a new video showing Alon Ohel, who appeared to be losing vision in his right eye.[^cite_note-106]&#x20;

On 28 September, the al-Qassam Brigades announced "the loss of contact" with two hostages, [Matan Angrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matan_Angrest) and [Omri Miran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omri_Miran), during Israeli attacks on Sabra and Tel al-Hawa. The Brigades demanded that Israel halt air sorties in those areas for 24 hours so that it could locate the missing hostages.[^cite_note-107]&#x20;

## Ceasefire debate

Further information: [Gaza war peace plan](81215079.md)

### August

A 60-day ceasefire plan presented by [Egyptian](8087628.md) and [Qatari](8238258.md) mediators on 17 August[^cite_note-cnn20250818-108] was accepted by Hamas, and would have halted the offensive if accepted by the Israeli side. Netanyahu did not respond publicly to the ceasefire proposal, and his far-right political allies heavily pressured him to reject it.[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

On 31 August, an Israeli security cabinet meeting was convened during which all defense officials argued in favor of "a limited hostage release deal" and stated that a military takeover of Gaza City would not bring victory over Hamas.[^cite_note-110]&#x20;

### September

&#x20;On 7 September, Trump stated that Israel had accepted his new proposal for a ceasefire and that Hamas must accept it as well. It was unclear what the terms of the proposed ceasefire were.[^cite_note-111] In response to Trump's statement, Hamas said it:

> " \[...] is ready to immediately sit at the negotiating table to discuss the release of all prisoners in exchange for a clear declaration to end the war, a total withdrawal from the Gaza Strip, and creation of a committee of independent Palestinians to run the Gaza Strip."[^cite_note-112]

However, the 9 September [Israeli airstrike in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Israeli_attack_on_Doha), which targeted Hamas leaders that had gathered to discuss the American proposal,[^cite_note-ajstaff9_9-113][^cite_note-Berman-2025a-114] appeared to derail any prospect of an agreement.[^cite_note-115]&#x20;

On 26 September, Trump again stated he was close to forging a deal to end the war and return the Israeli hostages, without revealing any details or timetable.[^cite_note-116] A day later, Hamas said it had not received any plan from Trump.[^cite_note-117] The detailed peace plan was revealed on 29 September: it would include an immediate ceasefire along existing battle lines; the disarmament of Hamas; the release of all Israeli hostages; and "eventual" Palestinian independence. Netanyahu agreed to the deal,[^cite_note-118] but a senior Hamas official stated that some of its points are unacceptable and must be amended, and that an official response would only come after consultations with other Palestinian factions.[^cite_note-apnews20251001-98]&#x20;

### October

On 3 October, Hamas announced it agreed to some of the terms, including the release of all the hostages, but did not clearly state whether it agreed to disarm and stated it was seeking further negotiations. Hamas also stated it was ready to hand over power in Gaza: "to a Palestinian body of independents ([technocrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy)) based on Palestinian national consensus and supported by Arab and Islamic backing".[^cite_note-reuters20251003-119]&#x20;

Following Hamas' response, Trump stated that he believed the group was "ready for a lasting peace", and called on Israel to stop bombing Gaza.[^cite_note-reuters20251003-119] On 4 October, in response to Trump's statement, Netanyahu ordered the IDF to stop the Gaza City offensive.[^cite_note-ynetnews20251004-45]&#x20;

## Aftermath

### Post-offensive fighting

Despite the end of the offensive on 4 October, some fighting between the IDF and Hamas forces continued taking place in Gaza City. The IDF itself said that all of northern Gaza, including the city, was still considered "a dangerous combat zone".[^cite_note-120]&#x20;

Hours after the offensive was paused, the IDF launched an airstrike on Tuffah that reportedly targeted a Hamas operative and killed 18 Palestinians.[^cite_note-121] On 6 October, the al-Qassam Brigades said they shelled IDF positions in Tel al-Hawa.[^cite_note-122] On 8 October, the IDF said that al-Qassam Brigades gunmen had attempted to raid an army encampment in the southern outskirts of the city, near the [Netzarim Corridor](76319177.md), with Israeli forces repelling the attack.[^cite_note-123] On 9 October, an al-Qassam Brigades sniper killed an IDF soldier in Gaza City.[^cite_note-124]&#x20;

### Ceasefire implementation

Following the implementation of [a ceasefire in the Gaza Strip](81215079.md) on 10 October, Israeli forces withdrew from parts of Gaza City.[^cite_note-\:0-47] IDF troops remained deployed in Shuja'iyya.[^cite_note-\:1-48] Thousands of Palestinians also returned to the city and surrounding areas.[^cite_note-125]&#x20;
