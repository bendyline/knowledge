[Ukraine](31750.md) has a [developing](78449.md) [social market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_market_economy). It possesses many of the components of a major European economy, such as rich farmlands,[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] a well-developed industrial base, highly-trained labour, and a well-funded education system.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] It has large [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) deposits across its landmass.[^cite_note-31] Ukraine remains one of [the poorest countries in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_in_Europe_by_GDP_\(PPP\)_per_capita),[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] which some have attributed to high [corruption](33085862.md) levels[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] and the slow pace of [economic liberalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization) and [institutional reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The nation was in an economic depression during the 1990s with [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation) and an output half of its predecessor state, the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). Economic growth returned in 2000, lasting until the [2008–2009 Ukrainian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932009_Ukrainian_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-NBU-38] The country fell into another recession in 2013, with stricter border and customs control by Russia straining their exports.[^cite_note-Reuters171013-39][^cite_note-rsbcdue-40] Its [relationship with Russia](17446303.md) has significantly influenced its economic development. The [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md) and [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas) severely damaged two of Ukraine's most industrial regions.[^cite_note-AJstnste-41][^cite_note-stnstaue-42] The [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) in 2022 suppressed economic growth by 18.9% that year, with the economy rebounding from 2023 to 2026.[^cite_note-\:2-43]

## History

### Before 1917

Main articles: [Economy of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Kievan_Rus'), [Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia#Economy), [Cossack Hetmanate § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate#Economy), and [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria#Economy)

See also: [Kiev in the Golden Horde period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_in_the_Golden_Horde_period) and [History of Kyiv (1362–1657) § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kyiv_\(1362%E2%80%931657\)#Economy)

Geography has long influenced the economy of the Ukrainian lands. Rich fertile soils (such as [chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem) areas) made the area a "[breadbasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadbasket)": for [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece)[^cite_note-44] as well as for early [modern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Europe).[^cite_note-45] The maintenance of trade corridors – the [route from the Varangians to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks) and [access through the Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Straits#Straits_Question) to the Mediterranean world – became important. Mineral resources encouraged industrialisation – notably in the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) – from the 19th century onwards.[^cite_note-46]

The lack of secure borders meant repeated interruptions in economic development. [Steppe nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_nomads) and other conquerors – [Cumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumans), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus'), [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) for example, sometimes saw plundering as more important than fostering economic development. In the 16th to 18th centuries, the wastelands of the [Wild Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Fields) left much of Ukraine as an area of tentatively militarised outposts, prior to tsarist Russia's extension of its power into the region in the 17th and 18th centuries.

### Soviet period: 1917 to 1991

For the history of Ukraine's economy in Soviet times, see [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic#Economy).

### 1991 to 2000

On 24 August 1991, Ukraine [established its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine) from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-47] The new state's economy suffered huge output declines and soaring inflation in the following years.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48] Ukraine saw [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation) in the early 1990s because of a lack of access to financial markets and massive monetary expansion to finance government spending, while output declined sharply this was catastrophic for the economy because it undid decades of hard-fought economic progress and people became poorer.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48] Huge output declines and soaring inflation was at the time common to most [former Soviet republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states), but Ukraine was among the hardest hit by these problems.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48]

In response to hyperinflation the [National Bank of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Ukraine) replaced the national currency, the [karbovanets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_karbovanets), with the [hryvnia](722696.md) in September 1996 and pledged to keep it stable in relation to the U.S. dollar.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48][^cite_note-49] The currency remained unstable through the late 1990s, particularly during the [1998 Russian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Russian_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48]

Deep recession during the 1990s led to a relatively high [poverty rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty), but beginning in 2001, seven straight years of economic growth, raised the living standard for most citizens. A [World Bank](36752.md) report in 2007 noted that: "Ukraine recorded one of the sharpest declines in poverty of any transition economy in recent years. The poverty rate, measured against an absolute poverty line, fell from a high of 32% in 2001 to 8% in 2005."[^cite_note-50] The [UN](31769.md) noted that Ukraine had overcome absolute poverty, and that there was only relative poverty in 2009.[^cite_note-UN-51]

### 2000 to 2014

Main article: [2008–09 Ukrainian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Ukrainian_financial_crisis)

Ukraine stabilised by the early 2000s.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48] The year 2000 saw the first year of economic growth since Ukraine's independence.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] The economy continued to grow thanks to a 50% growth in exports between 2000 and 2008[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] – mainly exports from the traditional industries of metals, metallurgy, engineering, chemicals, and food.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] Between 2001 and 2008, metals and chemicals prices boomed because of fast international economic growth, while the price of natural gas imported from Russia remained low.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30]

[Monetisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetization) also helped to drive the [economic boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_expansion) Ukraine experienced between 2000 and 2008.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] Attracted in part by relatively high interest-rates, foreign cash was injected into Ukraine's economy and money supply grew rapidly: from 2001 to 2010 broad, money increased at an annual rate of 35%.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48] In 2006 and 2007, credit growth averaged 73%.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48]

An effect of this was that Ukrainian assets began to look like a large [economic bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_bubble) and high inflation started to damage Ukraine's export competitiveness.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48] The ratio of credit to GDP grew extremely fast – from 7% to almost 80% over just several years.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] From 2000 to 2007, Ukraine's real growth averaged 7.4%.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] This growth was driven by domestic demand: orientation toward consumption, other structural change, and financial development.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30]

Domestic demand grew in constant prices by almost 15% annually.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] It was supported by expansionary—[procyclical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procyclical_and_countercyclical_variables)—fiscal policy.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] Ukraine benefited from very low labour costs, slightly lower tariffs, and high prices of its main export goods, but at the same time faced notably higher non-tariff barriers.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30] Russia has not charged Ukraine below-world-market prices for natural gas since the end of 2008. This led to various [Russia–Ukraine gas disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Ukraine_gas_disputes).[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever-30][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever_B-53][^cite_note-54]

[Ukraine suffered severely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932009_Ukrainian_financial_crisis) in the [economic crisis of 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession). Because of it Ukraine experienced a drought in capital flows.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48] The hryvnia, which had been pegged at a rate of 5\:1 to the U.S. dollar, was devalued to 8\:1, and was stabilised at that ratio until the beginning of 2014.[^cite_note-VhaUSdGF-55] In 2008, Ukraine's economy ranked [45th in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) according to GDP (nominal), with a total nominal GDP of US\$188 billion, and nominal per capita GDP of US\$3,900. There was 3% unemployment at the end of 2008. Over the first 9 months of 2009, unemployment averaged 9.4%.[^cite_note-56] The final official unemployment rates for 2009 and 2010 were 8.8% and 8.4%,[^cite_note-CIAFactbook-57] although the [CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook) notes a "large number of unregistered or underemployed workers".[^cite_note-CIAFactbook-57] Ukraine's GDP fell by 15% in 2009.[^cite_note-TE_Mar_5th_2014-48]

The Ukrainian economy recovered in the first quarter of 2010[^cite_note-UkrEcoQ12010-58] due to the recovery of the world economy and increasing prices for metals.[^cite_note-UE_The_Underachiever_B-53] Ukraine's real GDP growth in 2010 was 4.3%, leading to a per capita [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) GDP of US\$6,700.[^cite_note-CIAFactbook-57] In 2011, Ukrainian politicians estimated that 40% of Ukraine's economy was a [shadow economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market).[^cite_note-shadow1-59][^cite_note-shadow2-60]

In the summer of 2013, Ukrainian exports to Russia fell substantially due to Russia's stricter customs controls.[^cite_note-rsbcdue-40]

By October 2013, the Ukrainian economy had become stuck in recession.[^cite_note-Reuters171013-39] [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's_Investors_Service) downgraded Ukraine's credit rating to [Caa1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's#Global_Credit_Research) (poor quality and very high credit risk) in September 2013.[^cite_note-Reuters171013-39][^cite_note-Moody's_Definitions-61][^cite_note-IOSCO_03-62] At the time, [swap markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swap_\(finance\)#Swap_market) rated Ukraine's [default probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_of_default) over the next five years at 50%.[^cite_note-Reuters171013-39] In 2013, Ukraine saw no growth in GDP.[^cite_note-AJstnste-41]

### Post-Euromaidan: 2014 to 2022

Due to the loss of Ukraine's largest trading partner, Russia, over the annexation of Crimea in March 2014, and exacerbated by the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas) which started in April 2014[^cite_note-64] Ukraine's economy shrank by 6.8% in 2014.[^cite_note-stnstaue-42] It had been expected to decline by 8%.[^cite_note-Yahoo-65] A Ukrainian government report stated early in February 2016 that Ukraine's economy had shrunk by 10.4% in 2015.[^cite_note-66] For 2015, the National Bank of Ukraine had expected a further decline of 11.6%, and the World Bank anticipated a 12% shrinkage.[^cite_note-radiosvoboda.org-67] The World Bank forecast growth of 1% in 2016.[^cite_note-uatoday.tv-68]

Early in February 2014, the National Bank of Ukraine changed the hryvnia into a fluctuating/floating currency in an attempt to meet [IMF](15251.md) requirements and to try to enforce a stable price for the currency in the [Forex market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market).[^cite_note-69] In 2014 and 2015, the hryvnia lost about 70% of its value against the U.S. dollar.[^cite_note-VhaUSdGF-55][^cite_note-70]

The IMF agreed to a [four-year loan programme](43158902.md) worth about \$17.5 billion in eight tranches over 2015 and 2016, subject to conditions which involved economic reforms.[^cite_note-ut-20160513-71] However, due to lack of progress on reforms, only two tranches worth \$6.7 billion were paid in 2015. A third tranche of \$1.7 billion was provisionally scheduled in June 2016 subject to the bringing into law of 19 further reform measures.[^cite_note-kp-20160514-72][^cite_note-reuters-20160518-73] Some western analysts believed that large foreign loans were not encouraging reform, but enabling the corrupt extraction of funds out of the country.[^cite_note-wp-20160505-74]

Since December 2015, Ukraine has refused to pay and hence *[de facto](43594.md)* defaults on a \$3 billion debt payment to Russia that formed part of a [December 2013 Ukrainian–Russian action plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17_December_2013_Russian%E2%80%93Ukrainian_action_plan).[^cite_note-PaymentMoratorium-75]

The turnover of retail trade in Ukraine in 2014 shrank by 8.6% (from 2013) and shrank by 20.7% in 2015 (from 2014).[^cite_note-76] Ukraine saw a 30.9% decline in exports in 2015,[^cite_note-RUTtwpsp-77] mainly because of a sharp decline in production output in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) and in [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md) (the two regions of the Donbas).[^cite_note-RUTtwpsp-77] These two regions were responsible for 40.6% of the total export-decline rate.[^cite_note-RUTtwpsp-77] Before the war they had been two of the more industrial [oblasts of Ukraine](994124.md).[^cite_note-AJstnste-41]

According to the [Ministry of Economic Development and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_\(Ukraine\)), Ukraine had a surplus in its balance of payments in January–November 2015 of \$566 million and has had a trade deficit of \$11.046 billion during the same period in 2014.[^cite_note-RUTtwpsp-77] On 31 December 2015, Ukraine's public debt stood at 79% of its GDP.[^cite_note-pdu2015-78] It had shrunk \$4.324 billion in 2015 to end up at \$65.488 billion.[^cite_note-pdu2015-78] But calculated in hryvnia, the debt had grown by 42.78%.[^cite_note-pdu2015-78] In 2015, the [Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Social_Policy_of_Ukraine) rated 20–25% of Ukrainian households as poor.[^cite_note-79]

\$2.526 billion entered the Ukrainian economy via remittances in 2015, 34.9% less than in 2014.[^cite_note-tfU4316-80] \$431 million was sent from Ukraine to elsewhere using remittances.[^cite_note-tfU4316-80] In January 2016, [Bloomberg](432006.md) rated Ukraine's economy as the 41st most innovative in the world,[^cite_note-81] down from 33rd in January 2015.[^cite_note-82]

In May 2016, the IMF mission chief for Ukraine, Ron van Rood, stated that [reduction of corruption](33085862.md) was a key test for continued international support.[^cite_note-reuters-20160518-73] In February 2016, historian [Andrew Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wilson_\(historian\)) assessed progress in reducing corruption as poor as of 2016.[^cite_note-nw-20160214-83] [Aivaras Abromavičius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aivaras_Abromavi%C4%8Dius), Ukraine's then-Minister of Economy and Trade, resigned in February 2016, citing ingrained corruption.[^cite_note-guardian-20160204-84] In October of the same year, a survey of potential foreign investors conducted by Dragon Capital identified corruption and lack of trust in the judiciary as the largest obstacles to investment.[^cite_note-euronews-20161011-85]

Late in July 2016, the [State Statistics Service of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Statistics_Service_of_Ukraine) reported that, compared with June 2015, [real wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_wages) had increased by 17.3%.[^cite_note-86] Simultaneously the National Bank of Ukraine reported a \$406 million surplus in Ukraine's January–June 2016 [balance of payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_payments) against a deficit of \$1.3 billion in the same period in 2015.[^cite_note-87] According to Ukraine's State Statistics Service, inflation in 2016 came down to 13.9%; while it had stood at 43.3% in 2015 and at 24.9% in 2014.[^cite_note-iSSS6117-88]

*[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* has compared the severity of Ukraine's recession to that of the Greek recession in 2011–2012 – pointing to Ukraine experiencing an 8–9% decline in GDP from 2014 to 2015 and Greece experiencing an 8.1% decline of GDP in 2011–2012, and noted that not all areas of Ukraine were equally effected by the economic downturn. Donetsk and Luhansk (the conflict zone) saw industrial production falling by 32% and 42% respectively. On the other hand, [Lviv](49968.md), located over 1000 km from the conflict, posted the largest jump in employment in the nation.[^cite_note-89]

The economy of Ukraine has overcome the severe crisis caused by armed conflict in the eastern part of country. A 200% devaluation of the hryvnia in 2014–2015 made Ukrainian goods and services cheaper and more competitive.[^cite_note-90] In 2016, for the first time since 2010, the economy grew by more than 2%.[^cite_note-ukraine-economic-update-spring-2017-91] A 2017 World Bank statement projected growth of 2% in 2017, of 3.5% in 2018, and of 4% in 2019 and 2020.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Inflation in Ukraine in 2017 was 13.7% (12.4% in 2016).[^cite_note-UNIAN2337517-94]

Since about 2015, there has been a growing number of Ukrainians working in the [European Union](9317.md), particularly [Poland](22936.md). [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat) reported that 662,000 Ukrainians received EU residence permits in 2017, with 585,439 issued by Poland. The head of the [National Security and Defence Council of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_and_Defense_Council_of_Ukraine) has estimated that up to 9 million Ukrainians work abroad for some part of the year, and 3.2 million have regular full-time work abroad with most not planning to return. World Bank statistics show that money remittances back to Ukraine have roughly doubled from 2015 to 2018, worth about 4% of GDP.[^cite_note-bl-20190220-95][^cite_note-wilsoncenter-20190514-96]

In Q1 2019, China became Ukraine's largest trading partner, replacing Russia.[^cite_note-eu191001-97][^cite_note-kyivpost200216-98] In Q3 of 2019, real GDP grew by 4.2%. The main driving factors include: increased purchasing power of the population in conditions of increase of the level of wages (during nine months of 2019 real wages increased by 9.5%); high level of business activity and preservation of investment activity, which stimulated mainly the development of construction, in particular, of industrial and transport infrastructure facilities; active consumer lending; maintaining the high dynamics of agricultural development; favourable price situation on selected world commodity markets for domestic exports and others.[^cite_note-99] Ukraine made its largest payment on debt in 2019 at \$1.1 billion.[^cite_note-100]

In 2019, [Fitch Ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitch_Ratings), a global leader in credit ratings and research, upgraded Ukraine's Long-Term foreign and National Currency Issuer Default Ratings (IDR) from "B−" to "B" and improved the outlook on the credit rating from stable to positive. Ukraine has demonstrated timely access to fiscal and external financing, improving macroeconomic stability, and declining public indebtedness.[^cite_note-101]

Ukraine moved up seven positions in the annual World Bank Doing Business 2020 report.[^cite_note-102] Prudent macroeconomic management helped reduce inflation and interest rates in 2019. Inflation eased to 4.1% at the end of 2019 and 2.4% in February 2020.

In 2020, Ukraine's GDP fell by 4.4%, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-103] On 27 October 2020, the [Constitutional Court of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Ukraine) ruled that anti-corruption legislation, including the mandatory electronic declaration of income, was unconstitutional.[^cite_note-unian-20201113-104] President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) warned that if the [Ukrainian parliament](844993.md) did not restore these anti-corruption laws, foreign aid, loans and visa-free travel to the European Union were at risk. The governor of the National Bank of Ukraine reported that Ukraine will not receive the scheduled \$700 million IMF loan before the end of 2020 because of the issue. IMF assessment teams had not visited Kyiv for eight months, which is necessary for further IMF loan tranches to be released.[^cite_note-reuters-20201102-105][^cite_note-intellinews-20201114-106] In February 2021, economist [Anders Åslund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_%C3%85slund) wrote "for months, senior Ukrainian officials have been claiming that the Ukrainian government has done everything the \[IMF] could possibly demand" but "this happy talk was always detached from reality", and the relationship with the IMF remains critical.[^cite_note-107]

On 21 July 2022, Ukraine devalued the Ukrainian hryvnia by 25% against the U.S. dollar due to the economic impact of [ongoing Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), to eliminate currency speculation and to improve the international competitiveness of business. The previous day it requested a two-year payment freeze on international bonds; in 2020 it had \$130 billion of external debt.[^cite_note-reuters-20220721-108] When the 2022 Russian invasion began, Ukraine's economy was predicted by the IMF to shrink by up to 35%.[^cite_note-109] Despite improvements, as in Moldova [corruption in Ukraine](33085862.md) remains an obstacle to [joining the EU](36830143.md); the country was rated 104th out of 180 in the [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index) for 2023.[^cite_note-110]

### Russian invasion of Ukraine: 2022 to present

Following the onset of the [Russian-Ukrainian War](70149799.md) in 2022, Ukraine's GDP declined by nearly 30%. Despite economic growth in 2023 and 2024, the economy remains smaller than it was before the war. In 2023, GDP increased by 5.3%, and in 2024 by 3.6%.[^cite_note-111] The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) forecasts that Ukraine's GDP growth will slow to between 2% and 3% in 2025, citing challenges such as labor shortages, [damage to energy infrastructure](71963495.md), and the ongoing war.[^cite_note-112]

In April 2026, the National Bank of Ukraine forecast that real GDP growth in 2026 would be 1.3% and inflation 9.4%; it kept its bank rate at 15%.[^cite_note-kp-20260430-113] Due to heavy economic dependance on external donors, such as the [United States](3434750.md), the [IMF](15251.md), and the [European Union](9317.md), Ukrainian economist and banker [Kyrylo Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrylo_Shevchenko) coined the term [donornomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donornomics) to describe the [economic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_system) of the country during the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md). The *[Kyiv Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Post)* stated that the [Western economies](21208200.md) were financing Ukraine through the war by about \$100 billion a year.[^cite_note-kp-20260819-114] In July 2026, Ukraine and Russia started [attacking each other's ports and shipping using those ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)#2026_campaigns) in the [Sea of Azov](78019.md) and [Black Sea](3386.md), which had been largely protected by the [Black Sea Grain Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Grain_Initiative) agreement, leading to the closure of the Ukrainian deep-water ports near [Odesa](55545.md) on 23 July majorly impacting exports.[^cite_note-liga-20260723-115][^cite_note-tt-20260806-116] In August 2026, President Zelenskyy asked allies for an additional €23 billion of funding for a military spending shortfall.[^cite_note-euronews-20260901-117]

A [Zenit-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenit-2), produced by the former [Yuzhmash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PA_Pivdenmash), Ukrainian rocket manufacturer

Ukraine is home to companies operating in around 20 major industries, namely power generation, fuel, ferrous and non-ferrous metallurgy, chemical and petrochemical and gas, machine building and metal-working, forest, wood-working and wood pulp and paper, construction materials, light, food, and others.[^cite_note-123] Industry accounted for 26% of GDP in 2012. #### Mining

See also: [List of mines in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mines_in_Ukraine) and [Coal in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_Ukraine)

Ukraine is one of the world's most important mineral producing countries,[^cite_note-125] in terms of both the range and size of its reserves. There are nearly 8,000 separate deposits, harboring some 90 different minerals, of which about 20 are economically significant.[^cite_note-126] About half of all the known deposits are under exploitation. Coal reserves in Ukraine amount to 47.1 billion tons. The annual domestic demand for coal as fuel is about 100 million tons, of which 85 percent can be satisfied by domestic production.

Deposits of iron ore (estimated at 28 billion tons),[^cite_note-127] manganese ore (3 billion tons), chalk and limestone (1.5 billion tons) are also large in Ukraine.

In 2019, Ukraine was the 7th largest world producer of iron ore,[^cite_note-128] the world's 8th largest producer of [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese),[^cite_note-129] 6th largest producer of [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium),[^cite_note-130] and 7th largest producer worldwide of [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite).[^cite_note-131] Ukraine was the world's 9th largest producer of [uranium](31743.md) in 2018.[^cite_note-132] Before the [2022 escalation of the war with Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) Ukraine supplied about 50% of the world's [neon gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon) and 40% of its [krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krypton), both of which are needed for the production of semiconductors.[^cite_note-133] It is estimated that over 90% of U.S. semiconductor-grade neon supplies were imported from Ukraine.[^cite_note-134]

#### Iron and steel

Further information: [Metal production in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_production_in_Ukraine)

Ukraine is rich in mineral deposits, including iron ore (of which it once produced 50 percent of the entire Soviet output), manganese ore (of which it produced 40 percent of world output during the Soviet era), mercury, titanium,[^cite_note-135] and nickel.

Prior to the [Russian-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-Ukrainian_war), Ukraine had a major ferrous metal industry, producing [cast iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast_iron), steel, and pipes. Among its economy leading companies in that field are [Metinvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metinvest), [Kryvorizhstal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal_Kryvyi_Rih), AzovStal, [Ilyich Steel & Iron Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illich_Steel_and_Iron_Works), and others. In 2012, Ukraine was the world's [tenth largest steel producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_steel_production) (according to the [World Steel Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Steel_Association)). In 2026, following ballistic missiles attacks, steel production had fully and indefinitely stopped across the country.[^cite_note-FT202609-136] [^cite_note-137]

#### Chemical

An important branch of the economy of Ukraine is the country's [chemical industry](58721.md), which includes the production of [coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coke_\(fuel\)), [mineral fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_fertilizer),[^cite_note-138] and [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid). Production of coke dropped tremendously due to the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-139]

#### Energy

Main article: [Energy in Ukraine](32021039.md)

See also: [Renewable energy in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Ukraine)



Electricity generation in Ukraine by source

Due to Ukraine's large population and its significant energy consumption, it is one of the largest energy markets on the [European continent](9239.md). Ukraine's location at the intersection of Europe, Russia, the Black Sea, and the Caspian Sea provides its ample natural resources such as coal, oil, natural gas, as well as significant potential for hydro and biomass energy.[^cite_note-\:0-142]

Ukraine relies heavily on imports for natural gas and oil products. It is a key transit site for European imports of Russian gas.[^cite_note-\:0-142][^cite_note-\:1-143] Ukraine transits more natural gas than any other country.[^cite_note-\:0-142] Prior to the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022, economic crises, Russia's closure of its market, and the Russo-Ukrainian War in eastern Ukraine since 2014 had stymied some of the country's growth. Ukraine's energy policy has created incentives for investment in renewable energy sources, but these remain uncertain. Since 2015, Ukrainian energy policy has favoured reducing natural gas subsidies alongside diversifying energy supply. Ukraine participates in the European Union's EU4Energy Programme, an effort to promote evidence-based policy making in national energy sectors.[^cite_note-\:1-143]

##### Oil and gas

See also: [Fracking in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fracking_in_Ukraine), [Druzhba pipeline](1950396.md), and [Natural gas in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_in_Ukraine)

Ukraine has oil and gas fields that meet 10 percent of its oil and 20 percent of its gas consumption, respectively. Ukraine contains natural gas reserves of 39.6 billion cubic feet (1.12 billion cubic metres), but only about 20 percent of Ukraine's demand is met by domestic production.Ukraine increased oil and gas production in 2023 up to 18.7 bcm.[^cite_note-144]

Ukraine remains heavily reliant on oil product and gas imports.[^cite_note-\:0-142] Russia ranks as Ukraine's principal supplier of oil, and Russian firms own and/or operate the majority of Ukraine's refining capacity. Natural gas imports come from Russia – which delivers its own gas, as well as the gas from Turkmenistan.[^cite_note-145]

Ukraine has been a key transit point for [EU imports of Russian gas](24648652.md),[^cite_note-\:1-143] and its pipeline system has been a crucial player in European energy supply, despite recent diversification of transit routes by Russian oil companies such as [Gazprom](320489.md).[^cite_note-\:0-142] Since the [2014 Russia–Ukraine gas disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Ukraine_gas_disputes), the latter's dependence on Russian gas supplies has dramatically affected its economics and foreign policy.

Ukraine was independent in its electricity supply, and exported electricity to Russia and other countries of Eastern Europe. This was achieved through a wide use of nuclear power and hydroelectricity. Its energy strategy intended a gradual decreasing of gas- and oil-based generation in favour of nuclear power, as well as [energy saving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) measures including lower industrial gas consumption. Reform of the still inefficient and opaque energy sector was a major objective of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank programs with Ukraine.

Ukraine was a partner country of the European Union's [INOGATE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INOGATE) energy programme, which had four key topics: enhancing energy security, convergence of member state energy markets on the basis of [EU internal energy market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_the_European_Union) principles, supporting sustainable energy development, and attracting investment for energy projects of common and regional interest. INOGATE was discontinued in 2016.[^cite_note-146]

Russia's full-scale invasion severely affected Ukraine's fuel retail sector. Repeated attacks on oil depots, refineries and retail filling stations disrupted fuel distribution, causing nationwide shortages during 2022 and continued damage to fuel infrastructure in frontline regions. Despite these attacks, Ukrainian fuel retailers gradually restored supply chains through increased imports from the European Union and the expansion of domestic logistics networks.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

As a result of the Russian shelling of the UPG gas station network since the beginning of the full-scale invasion, approximately eight gas stations have been damaged, said [Volodymyr Moroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Moroz), executive director of the network operator, PE Ukrpaletsystem, during a meeting of the Temporary Investigative Commission of the Verkhovna Rada chaired by People's Deputy of Ukraine [Oleksiy Honcharenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksiy_Goncharenko). As a result of this damage, this and other networks are suffering large losses. All this to some extent affects the price of fuel. At the end of 2025, [UPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPG) became one of the three largest fuel retail chains in Ukraine after expanding its network to over 500 gas stations thanks to the lease of former ANP and Avias assets, approved by the [Antimonopoly Committee of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimonopoly_Committee_of_Ukraine).

At the end of June, [WOG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wog) CEO and former Minister of Infrastructure Andriy Pyvovarsky noted that over the past two months, more than 150 [gas stations](61260.md) have burned down in Ukraine. He added that oil depots and other fuel infrastructure facilities are attacked almost weekly[^cite_note-149]

#### Automotive

Main article: [Automotive industry in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_Ukraine)



A LAZ-5208DL built by Lviv Bus Factory



A modern Electron T5L64 tram

Ukraine automobile manufacturers produce diesel locomotives, tractors, trucks, buses, trolleybuses, own-designed cars and trams. There are 12 automobile manufacturers in Ukraine, including [ZAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZAZ), [LuAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LuAZ), [Bogdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Corporation), [KrAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KrAZ), [Eurocar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocar), Electron, and [LAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Bus_Factory).

ZAZ (Zaporizhzhia Automobile Building Plant) is the main automobile-manufacturer of Ukraine, based in the south-eastern city of [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md). Passenger car manufacturing in Ukraine started in 1959. From 1960 to 1994, a total of 3,422,444 [Zaporozhets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZAZ_Zaporozhets) vehicles were manufactured in Zaporizhzhia and engines with air-cooling in Melitopol. In 2011–2012, the Zaporizhzhia Automobile Building Plant started serial full-scale production of two new models of vehicle, the [ZAZ Forza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chery_A13) (a re-badged Chinese Chery A13) and the [ZAZ Vida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Aveo) (a re-badged Daewoo Aveo).

The Bogdan Corporation is a leading Ukrainian automobile-manufacturing group, including several car- and bus-makers of Ukraine. [Bogdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_\(bus_model\)) buses (re-badged Isuzu models) are used as the primary small buses in most Ukrainian cities.[^cite_note-150]

LAZ used to be one of the major bus manufacturers in Ukraine. It manufactured city buses, coach buses, trolley buses, and special purpose buses. The company has been defunct since 2014.

The Lviv-based company [Electrontrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrontrans) is an enterprise of a full-scale production, specialising in design and production of modern urban electric transport – trams, trolleybuses, electric buses, units, and spare parts. In 2013 Electrontrans started producing low-floor trams, the first Ukrainian 100% low-floor tramways.[^cite_note-151]

#### Aircraft and aerospace

- [Aviation industry of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_industry_of_Ukraine?action=edit\&redlink=1)



The Antonov An-225 *Mriya* was the largest aircraft in the world prior to its destruction in 2022.

Ukraine is one of nine countries with a full cycle of [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) hardware engineering and production.[^cite_note-152] Besides the design and production of passenger and transportation aircraft, Ukraine has a network of aircraft repair enterprises, including companies involved in the recovery of military planes and [helicopters](8286923.md).[^cite_note-153] In March 2007, the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine created the state aircraft building concern Aviation of Ukraine (SACAU), which is governed by the [Ministry of Industrial Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Industrial_Policy_\(Ukraine\)). Production of [An-148](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-148) aircraft is now one of the most prospective projects for the Ukrainian plane manufacturing industry with 35 units manufactured since 2009 (together with Russian production).

The aircraft were engineered by the Antonov Scientific and Production Complex Design Office ([Antonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov) ANTK). The largest single airplane in the world, [Antonov An-225 Mriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-225_Mriya) was designed by Antonov ANTK and made in 1988. The gross production of light and ultra light planes in Ukraine does not exceed 200 units per annum. Production of [hang-gliders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang-glider) and [paragliders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraglider) of all designs makes nearly 1,000 units each year. Most of produced devices are exported. The main buyers of Ukrainian-made ultra-light aircraft are the United States, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, France, etc..

Since 2014, aerospace industry revenues have fallen by 80%.[^cite_note-guardian-20160309-154] In June 2016, the Antonov Corporation merged with the state-owned military conglomerate Ukroboronprom, forming Ukrainian Aircraft Corporation within its structure. This merger was done to boost Antonov profits and the production rate.[^cite_note-155] In 2018, Antonov was working on two cargo planes: the [An-178](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-178),[^cite_note-156] a cargo version of the [An-158](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-158), and the [An-132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov/Taqnia_An-132)D, a redesigned version of the [An-32](908204.md).[^cite_note-157] The An-132 was developed jointly with Saudi's Taqnia Aeronautics Company, featuring western avionics and engines. The roll out and first flight was due at the beginning of January 2017.

The space rocket industry in Ukraine has been managed by the [State Space Agency of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Space_Agency_of_Ukraine) since 1992. The agency included 30 enterprises, scientific research institutes, and design offices. [Pivdenne Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KB_Pivdenne) was in general responsible for creating the [Zenit-3SL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenit-3SL) carrier rocket. In 2013, The National Space Agency of Ukraine was involved in cooperation with American [Rockwell Int.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_International), as well as the [Sea Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Launch) project.[^cite_note-158] Yuzhnoye SDO was the designer and manufacturer of the initial [first stage core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multistage_rocket) of the U.S. [Orbital ATK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_Innovation_Systems) [Antares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antares_\(rocket\)) rocket.[^cite_note-159]

#### Shipbuilding

Main article: [Shipbuilding in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding_in_Ukraine)



[MV Minerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Minerva) − a passenger ship built in Ukraine

Ukraine is one of the 10 largest [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) countries in Europe. There are 49 shipbuilding companies registered in Ukraine. They are able to build a wide range of vessel types: [powerboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorboat), [barges](4177.md), [bulk carriers](1324895.md) (dry cargo ship), [tankers](2506366.md), liquefied gas carriers, etc.[^cite_note-shipbuilding-160][^cite_note-auto1-161]

The USSR's collapse put Ukraine's shipbuilding industry into a long-term decline. This lasted until 1999 and was mostly due to a minimum volume of state shipbuilding orders. Between 1992 and 2003, Ukraine's 11 [shipyards](213654.md) produced 237 navigation units for a total value of US\$1.5 billion. Production facilities are not working near full capacity, and customers are not always timely in paying for services. The shipbuilding industry grew between 2000 and 2006, in line with the wider Ukrainian economic expansion at the time.[^cite_note-shipbuilding-160][^cite_note-auto1-161]

State support and the opening of [free economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_economic_zone), foremost at enterprises based in [Mykolaiv](901495.md), were a recent development in Ukraine's shipbuilding industry. Within the Mykolaiv Special Economic Zone, enterprises like [Damen Shipyards Okean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okean_Shipyard), [Chornomorskyi (Black Sea) Shipbuilding Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Shipyard) (defunct since 2021), [61 Communards Shipbuilding Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolayiv_Shipyard), as well as the Veselka (Rainbow) paint and insulation enterprise are implementing investment projects targeted to raise efficiency and quality in primarily export-oriented vessels through production upgrades. The new engineering developments and potential of Ukrainian designers provide the ability to build high quality vessels with competitive prices.[^cite_note-shipbuilding-160][^cite_note-auto1-161]

About 100,000 Ukrainians regularly work on foreign merchant ships, one of the largest group of Ukrainian [labour migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_worker) and the sixth largest number of sailors from any country.[^cite_note-Seas-162] They are attracted by the high salaries of more than [US\$](18717338.md)1,000 per month.[^cite_note-Seas-162]

Every major Ukrainian coastal city has a [maritime university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_maritime_colleges#Ukraine).[^cite_note-Seas-162]

Although typically known as the industrial base of the Soviet Union, agriculture is a large part of Ukraine's economy. Ukraine is one of the world's largest agricultural producers and exporters and is known as the "[breadbasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadbasket)" of Europe.[^cite_note-rferl.org-163]

In 2008, agriculture accounted for 8.29% of Ukraine's GDP and by 2012 had grown to 10.43% of the GDP. Agriculture accounted for \$13.98 billion of value added to the economy of Ukraine in 2012. Despite being a top 10 world producer of several crops such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn), Ukraine still only ranks 24 out of 112 nations measured in terms of overall agricultural production.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

In 2011, Ukraine was the world's largest producer of [sunflower oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower_oil),[^cite_note-UkrInform-166] a major global producer of grain and sugar, and a potential global player on meat and dairy markets. It was one of the largest producers of nuts. Ukraine produced more natural [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey)[^cite_note-167] than any other European country and was one of the world's largest honey producers. An estimated 1.5% of its population was involved in honey production, therefore Ukraine had the highest honey per capita production rate in the world.[^cite_note-168] Because Ukraine possesses 30% of the world's richest [black soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem), makes its agricultural industry have huge potential.

At the beginning of the 21st century, Ukraine's agricultural industry was highly profitable, generating profit margins of 40–60%.[^cite_note-UkrAgroConsult-169] Analysts indicated that the sector had the potential to increase its output by up to four times.[^cite_note-Kra-170] Ukraine was the world's 6th largest, 5th if not including the EU as a separate state, producer of corn in the world and the 3rd largest corn exporter in the world. In 2012, Ukraine signed a contract with China, the world's largest importer of corn, to supply China with 3 million tonnes of corn annually at market price. The deal included a \$3 billion line of credit extension from China to Ukraine.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

In 2014, Ukraine's total grain crop was estimated to be a record 64 million metric tons. In 2014, Ukraine lost control over portions of several regions to Russia, followed by the start of [the war](42085878.md) in Donbas and the [annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation),[^cite_note-174] hence the actual available crop yield was closer to 60.5 million metric tons. Due to the decline of the metallurgy industry, which was previously Ukraine's top export category, agricultural products have become Ukraine's largest export category as a result of the war in Donbas.[^cite_note-175]

Since at least 2011, farmland was remaining as the only major asset in Ukraine that was not [privatised](24661.md).[^cite_note-UkrAgroConsult-169] In March 2020, the Ukrainian parliament lifted a ban on the sale of farmland.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] The land market was fully opened for the first time since independence on 1 July 2021.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-rferl.org-163]

### Information technology

See also: [List of Ukrainian software companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ukrainian_software_companies) and [Outsourcing in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outsourcing_in_Ukraine)



Ukrainian National IT Factory in Kyiv

Ukraine has a long-standing reputation as a major technology region, with a well-developed scientific and educational base. In March 2013, Ukraine ranked 4th in the world in number of certified IT professionals after the United States, India, and Russia.[^cite_note-ITUkrM2013-181] On top of that, experts recognised both the quantitative and qualitative potential of Ukrainian specialists. In 2011, the number of IT specialists working in the industry reached 25,000 people with 20% growth.[^cite_note-182] The volume of the Ukrainian IT market in 2013 was estimated to be up to 3.6 billion U.S. dollars.[^cite_note-ITUkrM2013-181][^cite_note-183]

In 2017, Ukraine emerged as the top outsourcing destination of the year, according to the [Global Sourcing Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Sourcing_Association). By 2017, there were 13 research and development centres of global companies located in Ukraine, including [Ericsson](9424.md) Ukraine, in [Lviv](49968.md).[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-auto-185]

As for 2019, the number of IT specialists involved in the IT industry of Ukraine reached 172,000 people.[^cite_note-186] The share of IT industry in Ukraine's GDP is 4%.[^cite_note-auto-185]

According to the IT sector report of 2019, Ukraine is the largest exporter of [IT services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outsourcing) in Europe, and ranks among the 25 most attractive countries for [software development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development) worldwide.[^cite_note-187]

#### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Ukraine)

In 2012, Ukraine was the 8th most popular tourism destination in Europe with 23 million visitors.[^cite_note-ukrstat-195] The country's tourism industry is generally considered to be underdeveloped, but it does provide crucial support for Ukraine's economy. In 2012, the contribution of tourism to the GDP amounted to ₴28.8 billion, or 2.2% of GDP and directly supported 351,500 jobs (1.7% of total employment).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197]

Ukraine's neighbours ([Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians), [Hungarians](17106358.md), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Russians](59510.md), and [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks)) are known to come to Ukraine to purchase products (such as food or gasoline) and presents that are cheaper in Ukraine than in their home countries.[^cite_note-198]

Ukraine has impressive landscapes, ruins of ancient castles, historical parks, vineyards where they produce native wines, unique structures such as [Saint Sophia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral,_Kyiv) or [Chersonesos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesus). Officially, there are seven [World Heritage Sites in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Ukraine). The Carpathian Mountains suitable for skiing, hiking, fishing and hunting. [Bukovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovel) — is the largest ski resort in Ukraine situated in the [Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk_Oblast) (province) of western Ukraine. The 2010–2011 winter season recorded 1,200,000-day visits with foreigners amounting to 8–10% of all visitors. In 2012, the Bukovel was named the fastest growing ski resort worldwide. The coastline on the Black Sea is a popular summer destination for vacationers, especially [Odesa](55545.md).

[Transport in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Ukraine) includes ground transportation (road and rail), water (sea and river), air transportation, and pipelines. The transportation sector accounts for roughly 11% of the country's gross domestic product and 7% of total employment.[^cite_note-Transport_in_Ukraine_WorldBank2024-210]

HRCS2 unit

[HRCS2 multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRCS2_multiple_unit). Rail transport [is heavily utilised in Ukraine](3149341.md).

In total, Ukrainian paved roads stretch for 164,732 kilometres (102,360 mi).[^cite_note-Transport_in_Ukraine_cia-211] Major routes, marked with the letter 'M' for 'International' ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): *Міжнародний*), extend nationwide and connect all major cities of Ukraine, and provide cross-border routes to the country's neighbours.

International maritime travel is mainly provided through the [Port of Odesa](20974479.md), from where ferries sail regularly to [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria) and [Haifa](55606.md). The largest ferry company presently operating these routes is [Ukrferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UkrFerry).[^cite_note-212]



The Kharkiv–Dnipro motorway (M18)

[Rail transport in Ukraine](6011024.md) connects all major urban areas, port facilities and [industrial centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) with neighbouring countries. The heaviest concentration of [railway track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_track) is the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) region of Ukraine. Although [rail freight transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_freight_transport) fell in the 1990s, Ukraine is still one of the [world's highest rail users](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_usage_statistics_by_country).[^cite_note-213]

The total amount of railroad track in Ukraine extends for 22,473 kilometres (13,964 mi), of which 9,250 kilometres (5,750 mi) was electrified in the 2000s.[^cite_note-Transport_in_Ukraine_cia-211] The state has a monopoly on the provision of passenger rail transport, and all trains, other than those with cooperation of other foreign companies on international routes, are operated by its company [Ukrzaliznytsia](3149341.md).

[Kyiv Boryspil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boryspil_International_Airport) is Ukraine's largest international airport. It has three main passenger terminals and is the base for the country's flag carrier, [Ukraine International Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_International_Airlines). Other large airports in the country include those in [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_International_Airport), [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_International_Airport) and [Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk_International_Airport) (now destroyed). In addition to its flag carrier, Ukraine has a number of airlines including [Windrose Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windrose_Airlines), [Dniproavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniproavia), [Azur Air Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azur_Air_Ukraine), and [AtlasGlobal Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AtlasGlobal_Ukraine). [Antonov Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_Airlines), a subsidiary of the Antonov Aerospace Design Bureau, was the only operator of the world's largest fixed wing aircraft, the [An-225](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-225).

Ukraine ranks eighth among the world's nations in terms of the Internet speed with the average download speed of 1,190 kbit/s.[^cite_note-214] Five national providers of fixed (DSL, ADSL, XDSL) internet access — [Ukrtelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrtelecom), Vega Telecom, Datagroup, Ukrnet, Volia, and 3 national operators of mobile internet – [Vodafone Ukraine](2428085.md), [Kyivstar](2254340.md), [lifecell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifecell) are currently operating in Ukraine. ### Science and technology

Main article: [Science and technology in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Ukraine)

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Science and technology in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Ukraine).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Science_and_technology_in_Ukraine\&action=edit)]

[Science and technology in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Ukraine) has its modern development and historical origins in the 18th and 19th centuries and is associated, first of all, with the [Kyiv Mohyla Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Mohyla_Academy), [University of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv) and [University of Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Kharkiv). The founding of Ukraine's main research institution, the [National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](2281470.md), in 1918 by [Volodymyr Vernadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Vernadsky) marked an important milestone in the country's subsequent scientific and technological development.

Ukraine's space science advanced rapidly in the aftermath of [World War II](32927.md), with [Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Korolyov) and [Chelomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Chelomey) leading the [rocket](1306157.md) and [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) development in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) during the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race).

The [Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology) was the site of the world's first [torsatron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsatron)-type stellarator, [Saturn-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn-1_\(stellarator\)).[^cite_note-219] Today it is the site of the larger [Uragan-2M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uragan-2M) torsatron experiment, which was the largest stellarator in Europe until the commissioning of [Wendelstein 7-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendelstein_7-X) in 2015.

Ukraine was ranked 66th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025, down from 57th in 2023, and 49th in 2021.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221][^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Ukraine_WIP21-222][^cite_note-223]

### Foreign direct investment

See also: [List of special economic zones § Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_special_economic_zones#Ukraine)

To encourage foreign trade and investment, the Ukrainian parliament approved a [foreign investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) law that would allow foreigners to purchase businesses and property, to repatriate revenue and profits, and to receive compensation if the property is nationalised by a future government. Complex laws and regulations, poor [corporate governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_governance), weak enforcement of contract law by courts, and corruption, continued to prevent direct large-scale foreign investment in Ukraine. There is a functioning [stock market](52328.md), but portfolio investment activities have been restricted by a historic lack of protection of shareholders' rights.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

As of April 2011, total foreign direct investment stock in Ukraine stood at \$44.7 billion.[^cite_note-236] Statistics from [FDi Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDi_Intelligence) show that Ukraine suffered a year on year decline in foreign direct investment between 2010 and 2013.[^cite_note-237]

State enterprise [InvestUkraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InvestUkraine) was created[^cite_note-238] under the [State Agency for Investment and National Projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Agency_for_Investment_and_National_Projects?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*National Projects*)[^cite_note-239] to serve as a [one-stop stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-stop_shop) for investors and to deliver investment consulting services.

Ukraine signed a [shale gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_gas) exploration deal with [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc) on 25 January 2013.[^cite_note-SGBigdealUS-240] The \$10 billion deal was the largest foreign direct investment ever for Ukraine.[^cite_note-SGBigdealUS-240][^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242]

Many companies, owned by foreigners, have been successfully operating in Ukraine since its independence. These include companies in agriculture, such as [Kyiv-Atlantic Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv-Atlantic_Group?action=edit\&redlink=1), founded in 1994 by David Sweere. He sold its business in Minnesota and invested in Ukraine, believing in its huge potential. The company has been operating at a profit since 2002. As a result, he became the fifth richest among the Westerners who made their fortune in Ukraine.[^cite_note-KyiMost-243]

In 2016, foreign direct investment in Ukraine's economy amounted to \$3.8 billion in 2016, which was almost twice more than in 2015.[^cite_note-USNon-244]

## Legal environment and economic support

### Background

Since the late 1990s, the Ukrainian government has pledged to reduce the number of government agencies, streamline the regulatory process, create a legal environment to encourage entrepreneurs, and enact a comprehensive tax overhaul.[^cite_note-ElbLew-245] In 2003, the IMF encouraged Ukraine to quicken the pace and scope of reforms, threatening to withdraw financial support.[^cite_note-246]

On 24 June 2010, Ukraine's Foreign Minister [Kostyantyn Hryshchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostyantyn_Gryshchenko) signed a free trade agreement with the [European Free Trade Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association) (EFTA).[^cite_note-247] According to the [Global Competitiveness Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Competitiveness_Report) of 2012–2013, "the country's most important challenge is the needed overhaul of its institutional framework, which cannot be relied on because it suffers from red tape, lack of transparency, and favouritism."[^cite_note-248] In reports by the *[Kyiv Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Post)* in 2010 and 2011 respectively, a "double taxation avoidance" treaty with [Cyprus](5593.md) signed in 1982 by the Soviet Union has cost Ukraine billions of U.S. dollars of [tax revenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_revenue).[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

The 2014 campaign of [lustration in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lustration_in_Ukraine) was criticised by Mark Varga, writing in *[The National Interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_Interest)* as it might have adverse economic effects.[^cite_note-251] On 29 May 2014, Ukraine entered into the [European Union–Ukraine Association Agreement](39410190.md),[^cite_note-Eur-252] that reached its full application on 1 September 2017.[^cite_note-253]

## Environmental issues

See also: [Environmental impact of the Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war)

|  | This section needs to be **updated**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(January 2016)* |
| --- | --- |

Ukraine is interested in cooperating on regional environmental issues. Conservation of natural resources is a stated high priority, although implementation suffers from a lack of financial resources. Ukraine established its first nature preserve, [Askania-Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askania-Nova), in 1921 and has programs to breed [endangered species](20110668.md).[^cite_note-264]

Ukraine has a [Ministry of Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Environmental_Protection_and_Natural_Resources_\(Ukraine\))[^cite_note-265] and has introduced a pollution fee system that levies taxes on air and water emissions and solid [waste disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management). The resulting revenues are channelled to environmental protection activities, but enforcement of this pollution fee system is lax. Industrial pollution remains a considerable problem in Ukraine.

Significant environmental problems result from the [Chernobyl nuclear power plant disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) in 1986. In accordance with its previously announced plans, Ukraine permanently closed the [Chernobyl Atomic Energy Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Nuclear_Power_Plant) in December 2000.[^cite_note-266] In November 2001, Ukraine withdrew an application it had made to the [EBRD](37963.md) for funding to complete two new reactor units to compensate for the energy once produced by Chernobyl. Ukrainian concern over reform conditions attached to the loan – particularly tariff increases needed to ensure loan repayment—led the Ukrainian government to withdraw the application on the day the EBRD Board was to have considered final approval.

The Russo-Ukrainian War significantly impacts the environmental situation. Thirty-six thousand tons of waste, including toxic chemicals such as [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), have entered water bodies, posing dangers to human organs and the nervous system. Three million hectares of forests need to be demined, and forest fires caused by shelling occur in 30% of the country's forest area. The war has released approximately 67,532,788 tons of hazardous emissions, causing respiratory and [cardiovascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_system) issues.[^cite_note-267]
