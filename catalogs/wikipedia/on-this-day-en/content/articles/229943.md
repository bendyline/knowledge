The **Ilyushin Il-76** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Илью́шин Ил-76; [NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **Candid**) is a multi-purpose, fixed-wing, four-engine [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) [strategic airlifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_airlift) designed by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)'s [Ilyushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin) design bureau as a commercial freighter in 1967, to replace the [Antonov An-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-12). It was developed to deliver heavy machinery to remote and poorly served areas. Military versions of the Il-76 have been widely used in Europe, Asia and Africa, including use as an [aerial refueling](237949.md) tanker and command center.

The Il-76 has seen extensive service as a commercial freighter for ramp-delivered cargo, especially for outsized or heavy items that cannot be carried by other means. It has also been used as an emergency response transport for civilian evacuations as well as for humanitarian aid and disaster relief around the world. Due to its ability to operate from unpaved runways, it has been useful in undeveloped areas. Specialized models have also been produced for [aerial firefighting](1143974.md) and [reduced-gravity training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced-gravity_aircraft).

## Design and development



The Il-76 is a [high-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-wing) freighter with [four turbofans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-engined_jet_aircraft) and a [T-tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-tail)

### Origins

The aircraft was conceived by [Ilyushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin) in 1967 to meet a requirement for a freighter able to carry a payload of 40 tonnes (88,000 lb) over a range of 5,000 kilometres (2,700 nmi; 3,100 mi) in less than six hours, able to operate from short (500 metres or less for a commercial airliner) and unprepared airstrips, and capable of coping with the harsh weather conditions likely to be experienced in Siberia and the Soviet Union's Arctic regions. It was intended to replace the [Antonov An-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-12). Another project design for a double-decked 250-passenger airliner was canceled. The Il-76 first flew in March 1971 (1971-03).[^cite_note-2]

Production of Il-76s was allocated to the [Tashkent Aviation Production Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent_Aviation_Production_Association) in [Tashkent](57533.md), [Uzbekistan](31853.md), then a republic of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Some 860 of the basic transport variants were manufactured.[^cite_note-butowski-3] In the 1990s, modernized variants also equipped with [Soloviev D-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soloviev_D-30) turbofan engines[^cite_note-4] were developed (MF, TF), with a cargo compartment 20 m (66 ft) long by 3.4 m (11 ft) wide by 3.4 m (11 ft) tall; these larger variants were not produced in significant quantity due to the financial difficulties being experienced by the Russian Air Force, which was the primary operator of the type.

### Further development

From 2004 onwards, a number of aircraft in commercial service were modernized to the Il-76TD-90VD version; this involved the adoption of the newly developed [PS-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviadvigatel_PS-90) engine to comply with European noise limitations.[^cite_note-but2004-1] In 2005, the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) placed an order for 34 new Il-76MDs and four Il-78 tankers. In June 2013, Russian military export agency [Rosoboronexport](3094695.md) announced an order by China for 12 Il-76MD aircraft.[^cite_note-5]



Landing gear of an Ilyushin Il-76

The Il-76 has also been modified into an [airborne refueling tanker](237949.md), designated the [Il-78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-78), around 50 aircraft having been produced.[^cite_note-butowski-3] A variant of the Il-76 also serves as a firefighting [waterbomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterbomber). Its airframe was used as a base for the [Beriev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beriev) [A-50 'Mainstay'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beriev_A-50) [AEW\&C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_Early_Warning_and_Control) (airborne early warning and control) aircraft; around 25 aircraft were made.[^cite_note-butowski-3] Another application for the type was found in [Antarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic) support flights and for conducting [simulated weightlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced-gravity_aircraft) training for [cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmonauts) (akin to the "Vomit Comet" used by NASA).[^cite_note-6] Beriev and [NPO Almaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPO_Almaz) also developed an [airborne laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_laser) flying laboratory designated [A-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beriev_A-60), of which two were built, much of this project's details remaining classified.[^cite_note-7]

### Il-76MD-90A

It was announced in 2010 that the production of a modernized Il-76, the Il-76MD-90A (also known as *project Il-476* during the design stage), would begin; a proposed new production line would be located in [Aviastar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviastar-SP)'s facility in [Ulyanovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulyanovsk), Russia, and be operated in cooperation with the Tashkent works.[^cite_note-butowski-3] At that point, the construction of two Il-76MD-90A prototypes had begun at the Ulyanovsk facility.[^cite_note-8] The first Il-76MD-90A was rolled out at Aviastar's Ulyanovsk plant on 16 June 2014.[^cite_note-DID_Il-476-9] On 29 April 2015, it was reported that the [Russian Aerospace Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Aerospace_Forces) received the first Il-76MD-90A built at the Ulyanovsk plant "Aviastar-SP" from the 2012 contract for 39 aircraft.[^cite_note-10] The [Russian Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ministry_of_Defence) (MoD) received its first serial production Ilyushin Il-76MD-90A airlifter on 2 April 2019.[^cite_note-11] As of late 2025, 27 aircraft are ordered to be delivered in the period up to 2028 and 27+ had been built, six in 2023, six in 2024, and seven in 2025.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] In 2025, the production rate of the Il-76 reached 7 aircraft per year.[^cite_note-24] There are plans to expand production at the plant in Ulyanovsk and in 2027 it is expected to assemble 12 aircraft, and later - up to 18 per year.[^cite_note-25]

## Operational history

The first aircraft was delivered to the Soviet Air Force in June 1974[^cite_note-but2004-1] and subsequently became the main Soviet strategic transport aircraft. From 1976, it was operated by [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot).

Video of Il-76MD landing on an unpaved runway

Between 1979 and 1991, Soviet Air Force Il-76s made 14,700 flights into Afghanistan, transporting 786,200 servicemen and 315,800 tons of freight. The Il-76 carried 89% of Soviet troops and 74% of the freight that was airlifted. As Afghan rebels were unable to shoot down high-flying Il-76s, their tactics were to try and damage it on takeoff or landing. Il-76s were often hit by shoulder-launched [Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger) and [Strela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K34_Strela-3) heat-seeking missiles and large-calibre machine gun fire, but because the strong airframes were able to take substantial damage and remain operational, the aircraft had a remarkably low [attrition rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_rate) during this period of conflict. Building on that earlier experience, during 2006 the bulk of the Canadian Forces equipment airlifted into Afghanistan was flown in using rented civilian Il-76s.[^cite_note-26] In 2006, the Russian Air Force had about 200 Il-76s. Civilian users in Russia have 108.[^cite_note-butowski-3]



[USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) and [IAF](403355.md) airmen work inside the cockpit of an Indian Il-76

In 2004, a Chinese [People's Liberation Army Air Force](381445.md) (PLAAF) Il-76 carried out a flight mission in Afghanistan, and later in 2011, PLAAF Il-76s were sent to Libya to evacuate Chinese citizens. The two missions were the reported first steps of PLAAF developing long-range transportation capability.[^cite_note-27]

[Syrian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Air_Force) Il-76s, operating as civil [Syrianair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrianair) aircraft, have been reportedly used to ship weapons, money, and other cargo from Russia and Iran to Syria, according to a defected Syrian military pilot. Since the start of the war, in April 2011 (and up to July 2012), around 20 military flights have been conducted to and from [Tehran](57654.md), via [Iraqi](7515928.md) airspace. Further information exposes that since around 2012, Syrian Il-76s have regularly flown to Moscow's [Vnukovo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vnukovo_Airport) to fetch shipments of [Syrian banknotes](456708.md) that have been useful to [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md)'s government to survive [international sanctions](67328508.md).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

On 30 January 2017, an Il-76 firebomber of the Russian EMERCOM agency was deployed to [Chile](5489.md) to assist firefighters. The assignment took 39 days.[^cite_note-31]

All Il-76 transport aircraft in service with the RF Aerospace Forces were to receive anti-missile systems, and aircraft reconfiguration started in spring 2019.[^cite_note-32]

## Accidents and incidents

Main page: [Category\:Aviation accidents and incidents involving the Ilyushin Il-76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Aviation_accidents_and_incidents_involving_the_Ilyushin_Il-76)



An Il-76MD that was damaged during the [Sknyliv air show disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sknyliv_air_show_disaster) on 27 July 2002, during which the [Sukhoi Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27) involved struck a glancing blow against the aircraft's nose before crashing into spectators

As of July 2024, the [Aviation Safety Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Safety_Network) has tracked 137 incidents involving Il-76 series aircraft resulting in the 1,158 fatalities. 99 have been written off in crashes and other accidents.[^cite_note-75] Some of the most notable incidents can be found here.

- On 23 November 1979, a [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) Il-76, [registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) CCCP-86714, banked left during an approach to [Vitebsk Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk_Vostochny_Airport). Control of the aircraft was lost and the aircraft crashed, killing the crew of seven; this was the first loss of an Il-76.

  [^cite_note-76]

- [*Christmas day crash*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_in_Afghanistan#25_December_1979). On 25 December 1979, a [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) Il-76 crashed 36 km from Kabul, killing 48 onboard.

  [^cite_note-77]

- *[1988 Soviet Air Force Il-76 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Soviet_Air_Force_Il-76_crash)*. On 11 December 1988, an [Aeroflot Il-76 crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Soviet_Air_Force_Il-76_crash) on approach to [Leninakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninakan), Armenia killing 77 of the 78 on board. The aircraft was on an air relief operation following the [1988 Armenian earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake).

  [^cite_note-78]

- [*Soviet Air Force Ilyushin Il-76 crash*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_Air_Force_Ilyushin_Il-76_crash). On 18 October 1989, a Soviet Air Force Il-76 (CCCP-76569) [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_Air_Force_Ilyushin_Il-76_crash) in the Caspian Sea off Sumqayit, Azerbaijan following wing separation caused by an engine fire, killing all 57 in Azerbaijan's deadliest air accident. The cause of the engine fire was traced back to a design flaw.

  [^cite_note-79]

### 1990s

- On 1 February 1990, a [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) Il-76 [registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) СССР-86021 crashed 14 minutes after takeoff from [Panevėžys Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panev%C4%97%C5%BEys_Air_Base), killing all 8 members of the crew.

  [^cite_note-80]

- On 24 May 1991, a Metro Cargo Il-76TD (LZ-INK, named *Lugano*), crashed near Kermanshah Airport while attempting a forced landing following fuel exhaustion, killing four of ten crew.

  [^cite_note-81]

- On 8 July 1993, a Russian Air Force Il-76M ([RA-86039](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RA-86039?action=edit\&redlink=1)) crashed near [Pskov Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Airport) due to loss of control following an unexplained in-flight fire, killing the 11 crew.

  [^cite_note-82]

- *[1995 Airstan Ilyushin Il-76 hijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Airstan_Ilyushin_Il-76_hijacking)*. On 3 August 1995 Taliban-controlled fighter aircraft intercepted an Airstan Ilyushin Il-76TD transport aircraft, and held its seven crew members for over a year before escaping.

  [^cite_note-83]

  [^cite_note-84]

- On 19 August 1996, [Spair Airlines Flight 3601](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spair_Airlines_Flight_3601), an Il-76T, crashed while trying to land at [Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Nikola_Tesla_Airport) following total electrical failure due to pilot error, killing all 11 occupants on board.

  [^cite_note-85]

  &#x20;The crew had forgotten to turn on the AC/DC converter following engine startup.

- *[Charkhi Dadri mid-air collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Charkhi_Dadri_mid-air_collision)*. - *[1996 Abakan Ilyushin Il-76 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Abakan_Ilyushin_Il-76_crash)*. All 21 occupants on board died in the accident.

## Specifications (Il-76MD-90AE)



*Data from* [UAC](https://uacrussia.ru/en/aircraft/lineup/transport/IL-76MD-90A/#aircraft-specific),[Rosoboronexport](https://roe.ru/en/production/aerospace-forces/aircraft/military-transport-and-special-purpose-aircraft/il-76/), *Ilyushin*,[^cite_note-135] *Aviadvigatel*,[^cite_note-136] *Volga-Dnepr Airlines*.[^cite_note-137]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 5

- **Capacity:** **Il-76M** 42,000 kg (93,000 lb); **Il-76MD** 48,000 kg (106,000 lb); **Il-76MD-90A** 60,000 kg (130,000 lb)

- **Length:** 46.6 m (152 ft 11 in)

- **Wingspan:** 50.5 m (165 ft 8 in)

- **Height:** 14.42 m (47 ft 4 in)

- **Wing area:** 300 m

  2

  &#x20;(3,200 sq ft)

- **Empty weight:** 92,500 kg (203,928 lb) Il-76TD-90

- **Max takeoff weight:** 210,000 kg (462,971 lb) Il-76MD-90A

- **Powerplant:** 4 × [Aviadvigatel PS-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviadvigatel_PS-90)A-76 [turbofans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan), 156.9 kN (35,300 lbf) thrust each

**Performance**

- **Cruise speed:** 750–800 km/h (470–500 mph, 400–430 kn)

- **Range:** 5,000 km (3,100 mi, 2,700 nmi) Il-76MD-90A with 52 t (51 long tons; 57 short tons) payload.

- **Ferry range:** 9,700 km (6,000 mi, 5,200 nmi) Il-76MD-90A with no payload.

- **Service ceiling:** 12,200 m (40,000 ft)

- **[Thrust/weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-to-weight_ratio):** 0.32 at 200 t (200 long tons; 220 short tons) take-off weight and max. take-off thrust

- **Minimum landing run**: 450 m (1,480 ft) with thrust reversers

**Armament**\


- **Guns:** 2 × 23 mm (0.906 in) [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocannon) in radar-directed manned turret at base of tail

- **Bombs:** Some military models have four [hardpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint) under each outer wing capable of supporting 500 kg (1,100 lb) bombs.
