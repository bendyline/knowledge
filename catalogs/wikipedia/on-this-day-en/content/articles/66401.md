**Baden-Württemberg** ([/ˌbɑːdən ˈvɜːrtəmbɜːrɡ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BAH-dən VURT-əm-burg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-6] German: [\[ˌbaːdn̩ ˈvʏʁtəmbɛʁk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2d/De-Baden_W%C3%BCrttemberg.ogg/De-Baden_W%C3%BCrttemberg.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2d/De-Baden_W%C3%BCrttemberg.ogg/De-Baden_W%C3%BCrttemberg.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), commonly shortened to **BW** or **BaWü**, is a landlocked [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) (*Land*) in [Southwest Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Germany), east of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), which forms the southern part of the country's western border with [France](5843419.md). With more than 11.07 million inhabitants as of 2019 across a total area of nearly 35,752 km<sup>2</sup> (13,804 sq mi), it is the third-largest German state by both [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_area) (behind [Bavaria](3764.md) and [Lower Saxony](18435.md)) and [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_population) (behind [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) and Bavaria).[^cite_note-Our_State-7] The [largest city in Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg_by_population) is the state capital of [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), followed by [Mannheim](99627.md) and [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe). Other major cities are [Freiburg im Breisgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau), [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg), [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn), [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanz), [Pforzheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pforzheim), [Reutlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reutlingen), [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCbingen), and [Ulm](189455.md).

Modern Baden-Württemberg includes the historical territories of [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden), Prussian [Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Hohenzollern), and [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg). Baden-Württemberg became a state of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in April 1952 through the merger of [South Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Baden), [Württemberg-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Baden), and [Württemberg-Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Hohenzollern). These states had been created by the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) as they separated traditional states into occupation zones after [World War II](32927.md).

Baden-Württemberg is especially known for its strong economy with various industries like car manufacturing, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, the service sector, and more.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Ranking third among the [German states by gross regional product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_GRP) (GRP) and first [by research and development spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_research_and_development_spending), Baden-Württemberg is considered one of the most prosperous[^cite_note-11] and innovative[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] regions in Europe. Part of the [Four Motors for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Motors_for_Europe) and located in the [Blue Banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Banana), some of the [largest German companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_German_companies) are headquartered in Baden-Württemberg, including [Mercedes-Benz Group](42977.md), [Schwarz Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarz_Group), [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche), [Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bosch_GmbH) and [SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP).

The [sobriquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobriquet) *Ländle*, a [diminutive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminutive) of the word *Land* in the local [Swabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_German), [Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) and [Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconian_\(linguistics\)) dialects, is sometimes used as a synonym for Baden-Württemberg.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## History

Main article: [History of Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg)

Baden-Württemberg is formed from the historical territories of [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg), [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden) and Prussian [Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Hohenzollern).[^cite_note-Planet-17] Baden spans along the flat right bank of the river [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) from north-west to the south ([Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance)) of the present state, whereas Württemberg and Hohenzollern lie more inland and hillier, including areas such as the [Swabian Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Jura) mountain range. The [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest) formed part of the border between Baden and Württemberg.

In 100 AD, the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) invaded and occupied Württemberg, constructing a [limes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limes_\(Roman_Empire\))[^cite_note-18] along its northern borders. Over the course of the third century AD, the [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) forced the Romans to retreat west beyond the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) and [Danube](21209639.md) rivers. In 496 AD the [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) were defeated by a [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) invasion led by [Clovis I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_I).

The Holy Roman Empire was later established. The majority of people in this region continued to be Roman Catholics, even after the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation) influenced populations in northern Germany.

In the late 18th and early 19th century, [Künzelsau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCnzelsau), the capital of the [Hohenlohe district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenlohe_\(district\)), became the centre of emigration to the UK of pork butchers and bacon factors. The pioneers noticed a niche for speciality pork products in the rapidly growing English cities, especially those in the industrial centre and North. Many married local women and sent word home that a good living could be made in England; others followed.[^cite_note-19]

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, numerous people emigrated from this primarily rural area to the [United States](3434750.md) for economic reasons.

### 20th century to present

At the beginning of the 20th century, the territory of modern-day Baden-Württemberg consisted of the [Grand Duchy of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden),[^cite_note-20] the [Kingdom of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg)[^cite_note-21] and the [province of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Hohenzollern)[^cite_note-22] of the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia). Since 1871, these had been part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). In the aftermath of World War I and as part of the [German revolution of 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Revolution_of_1918%E2%80%931919), the monarchs of Baden,[^cite_note-23] Württemberg[^cite_note-24] and Prussia[^cite_note-25] were deposed, and these states became democratic republics: the [Republic of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Baden), the [Free People's State of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_People's_State_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) and the [Free State of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Prussia).

Following [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) becoming [chancellor of Germany](20890626.md) in 1933, the democratic institutions of Baden, Württemberg and Prussia were abolished as part of the *[Gleichschaltung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleichschaltung)*.

After World War II, the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) established three states in the territory of modern-day Baden-Württemberg: (South) [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Baden),[^cite_note-26] [Württemberg-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Baden)[^cite_note-27] and [Württemberg-Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Hohenzollern).[^cite_note-28] [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Baden) and [Württemberg-Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Hohenzollern) were occupied by [France](5843419.md), while [Württemberg-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg-Baden) was occupied by the [United States](3434750.md). The new artificial borders were a consequence of France requesting its own occupation zone in Germany after World War II, and the Americans' wish to keep the [A8 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_8), which spans east-west across northern Baden and northern Württemberg, wholly within their occupation zone.

In 1949, each state became a founding member of the [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) (West Germany), with Article 118 of the [German constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_constitution) providing an accession procedure. On 9 December 1951, [a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg_referendum) was held in Württemberg-Baden, Württemberg-Hohenzollern and (South) Baden over a possible merger, or the restoration of the former pre-war states.[^cite_note-Our_State-7] There was strong support for the merger in Württemberg and Hohenzollern, but opposition in Baden. While a majority in the historic area of Baden (52%) voted to restore the former pre-war states, the majority of voters overall (69%) voted in favor of a merger.[^cite_note-Our_State-7] Baden-Württemberg officially became a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) on 25 April 1952.[^cite_note-Our_State-7]

There were still opponents to the merger of Baden and Württemberg, however. In 1956 the [Federal Constitutional Court](34781059.md) decided that the population of Baden should have their say in a separate referendum. The second referendum was delayed, however, and the Federal Constitutional Court decided in 1969 that another referendum should be held by 30 June 1970. The referendum in the historic area of Baden was finally held on 7 June 1970, with 81.9% of the voters voting in favour of the merger of Baden and Württemberg.[^cite_note-29]

## Geography

Baden-Württemberg shares borders with the German states of [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md), [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse), and [Bavaria](3764.md), and also shares borders with [France](5843419.md) ([Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace), within the region of [Grand Est](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Est)), and [Switzerland](26748.md) (cantons of [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft), [Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Stadt), [Aargau](2467.md), [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Z%C3%BCrich), [Schaffhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schaffhausen) and [Thurgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgau)).[^cite_note-Our_State-7]

Most of the major cities of Baden-Württemberg straddle the banks of the [Neckar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neckar_River), which has its source in [Villingen-Schwenningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villingen-Schwenningen) and runs downstream (from southwest to the centre, then northwest) through the state past [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCbingen), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn), [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg), and [Mannheim](99627.md).

The [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) (German: *Rhein*) forms the western border as well as large portions of the southern border. The [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest) (Schwarzwald), the central mountain range of the state, rises east of the [Upper Rhine valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Rhine_valley). The high plateau of the [Swabian Alb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Alb), between the Neckar, the Black Forest, and the Danube, is an essential European [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_divide). Baden-Württemberg shares [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance) (Bodensee, also known regionally as the [Swabian](616237.md) Sea) with Switzerland, Austria and Bavaria, the international borders within its waters not being clearly defined. It shares the foothills of the [Alps](981.md) (known as the [Allgäu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allg%C3%A4u)) with Bavaria and the Austrian [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg), but Baden-Württemberg itself has no mainland border with Austria.

The [Danube](21209639.md) is conventionally taken to be formed by the confluence of the two streams [Brigach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigach) and [Breg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breg_\(river\)) just east of [Donaueschingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaueschingen). The source of the [Donaubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaubach), which flows into the Danube, in Donaueschingen is often referred to as the "source of the Danube" (*Donauquelle*). Hydrologically, the source of the Danube is the source of the Breg as the larger of the two formative streams, which rises near [Furtwangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furtwangen_im_Schwarzwald).

The forests in this region are home to common pests such as *[Melolontha Hippocastrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melolontha_hippocastani)*, that cause damage to the foliage and soil.

## Climate

Baden-Württemberg is–along with Bavaria–the southernmost part of Germany.

The climate across the states varies. This is mostly due to a high amount of mountains and highlands inside of the state. Most parts in the western parts (Baden) lower than 500–800 metres (1,600–2,600 ft) enjoy an almost year round mild [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (cfb in Köppen classification). The climate in the eastern parts of the state is more continental.[^cite_note-30] For instance, winters in the city of Ulm are colder than in Berlin.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] While winters in the warmest areas often lack snow, the [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest), [Swabian Alb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Jura) and [the Alps](981.md) tend to get snow frequently, especially in areas of high elevation. Summers here have more rain than in the valleys, but winters tend to have more sun.

Due to the differences of the landscapes, average annual temperatures reach from only 5 °C (41 °F) in the microclimates of Black Forest and [Allgäu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allg%C3%A4u) up to 13 °C (55 °F) in the Upper Rhine Valley.

### Politics

Main article: [Politics of Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg)



Baden-Württemberg state parliament in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart)

The state [parliament of Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg) is the *Landtag*, located in Stuttgart. The state government is currently formed by a [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens)-[CDU](60584.md) coalition as the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96zdemir_cabinet) of [Minister-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-President_of_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg) [Cem Özdemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_%C3%96zdemir) (Greens).

The politics of Baden-Württemberg have traditionally been dominated by the conservative [Christian Democratic Union of Germany](60584.md) (CDU), which had led all but one government since 1952 until 2011. In the *Landtag* [elections held on 27 March 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg_state_election), voters replaced the Christian Democrats and centre-right [Free Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_\(Germany\)) (FDP) coalition with an alliance of the [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_'90/The_Greens) and [Social Democrats](60585.md) (SPD), which secured a four-seat majority in the state parliament. The alliance elected the Greens-led [first Kretschmann cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Kretschmann_cabinet) under [Winfried Kretschmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfried_Kretschmann) because the Greens had surprisingly won 36 seats, one more than the Social Democrats' 35 seats. In the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg_state_election), the popular Kretschmann and his Greens were reelected and, with their nationwide best result, turned out first place for the first time in any election in German history. However, because of the Social Democrats' heavy losses, the Greens formed a coalition government with the Christian Democrats, the [second Kretschmann cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Kretschmann_cabinet). After the elections in 2021 and 2026, the Greens-CDU coalition was upheld.

## Economy



[SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP) headquarters in [Walldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walldorf)

Although Baden-Württemberg has relatively few natural resources compared to other regions of Germany,[^cite_note-Our_State-7] the state is among the most prosperous[^cite_note-Planet-17] and wealthiest regions in Europe, with a generally low unemployment rate historically. The state's economy benefits from and relies on its well-developed infrastructure.[^cite_note-36] Apart from the city-states of [Berlin](3354.md), [Bremen](9420388.md) and [Hamburg](13467.md), Baden-Württemberg offers the fourth-shortest routes to trains and buses on average among all German states.[^cite_note-37] Despite it not being predominantly reliant upon an industrial capacity, Baden-Württemberg is one of the strongest economic states in Germany.

Baden-Württemberg has the [highest exports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_exports) (2019)[^cite_note-38] and [third-highest imports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_imports) (2020),[^cite_note-39] the [second-lowest unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_unemployment_rate) with 4.3% (March 2021),[^cite_note-40] the most patents pending per capita (2020),[^cite_note-41] the second-highest absolute and highest relative number of companies considered "[hidden champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_champions)",[^cite_note-42] and the [highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_research_and_development_spending) absolute and relative research and development expenditure (2017)[^cite_note-43] among all states in Germany, as well as the highest measured Innovationsindex (2012),[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] making it the German state with the [third-highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_GRP) [gross regional product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_product) (GRP) as of 2019 (behind [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) and [Bavaria](3764.md)) with €524,325 billion (around US\$636.268 billion).[^cite_note-46] Baden-Württemberg also has the most employees (233,296) in the automotive industry of all German states as of 2018,[^cite_note-47] as well as the third-highest number of motor vehicles of all German states (2020).[^cite_note-48] If Baden-Württemberg were a sovereign country (2020), it would have an economy comparable to [that of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Sweden) in terms of nominal [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP).[^cite_note-49]

A number of well-known enterprises are headquartered in the state, for example [Mercedes-Benz Group](42977.md), [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche), [Robert Bosch GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bosch_GmbH) (automobile industry), [Carl Zeiss AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Zeiss_AG) (optics), [SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP) (Europe's most valuable brand[^cite_note-50] as well as [the largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_software_companies) non-American software enterprise) and [Heidelberger Druckmaschinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberger_Druckmaschinen) (precision mechanical engineering). Despite this, Baden-Württemberg's economy is dominated by [small and medium-sized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium-sized_enterprises) enterprises, like [most companies in German-speaking countries are](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittelstand). Although poor in workable natural resources (formerly lead, zinc, iron, silver, copper, and salts) and still very rural in some areas, the region is heavily industrialised overall. In 2003, there were almost 8,800 manufacturing enterprises with more than 20 employees, but only 384 with more than 500. There are 3,779 companies in Baden-Württemberg corporate family which come to 1000–5000 employees in total.

The latter category accounts for 43% of the 1.2 million persons employed in the industry. The *Mittelstand* or mid-sized company model is the backbone of the Baden-Württemberg economy.[^cite_note-51] Medium-sized businesses and a tradition of branching into different industrial sectors have ensured specialisation over a wide range. A fifth of the "old" Federal Republic's industrial gross value added is generated by Baden-Württemberg. Turnover for manufacturing in 2003 exceeded 240,000 million, 43% of which came from exports. The region depends to some extent on global economic developments, though the great adaptability of the region's economy has generally helped it through crises. Half of the employees in the manufacturing industry are in mechanical and electrical engineering and automobile construction. This is also where the largest enterprises are to be found. The importance of the precision mechanics industry also extends beyond the region's borders, as does that of the optical, clock making, toy, metallurgy and electronics industries. The textile industry, which formerly dominated much of the region, has disappeared from Baden-Württemberg. [Research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) (R\&D) is funded jointly by the state and industry. In 2001, more than a fifth of the 100,000 or so persons working in R\&D in Germany were located in Baden-Württemberg, most of them in the [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) area.[^cite_note-52] Baden-Württemberg is also the region with the highest GDP of the [Four Motors for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Motors_for_Europe).

A study performed in 2007 by the neo-liberal thinktank [Initiative for New Social Market Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiative_Neue_Soziale_Marktwirtschaft) and the trade newspaper *[Wirtschaftswoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftswoche)* awarded Baden-Württemberg for being the "economically most successful and most dynamic state" among the 16 states.

The unemployment rate stood at 3% in October 2018 and was the second lowest in Germany behind only Bavaria and one of the lowest in the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-53]

The [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest) as seen from the [Belchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belchen)

Baden-Württemberg is a popular holiday destination, ranking second among the German states by the total number of visitors' overnight stays behind Bavaria.[^cite_note-55] Main sights include the capital and biggest city, [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), modern and historical at the same time, with its urban architecture and atmosphere (and famously, its inner-city parks and historic [Wilhelma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelma) zoo), its castles (such as [Castle Solitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Solitude)), its museums as well as a rich cultural programme (theatre, opera) and [mineral spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_spring) baths in Bad Cannstatt (also the site of a Roman [Castra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra)); it is the only major city in Germany with vineyards in an urban territory.

The residential (court) towns of [Ludwigsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigsburg) and [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe), the spas and casino of [Baden-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Baden), the medieval architecture of [Ulm](189455.md) ([Ulm Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_M%C3%BCnster) is the tallest church in the world), the traditional university towns of [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) and [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCbingen) with the castles looking out above the river Neckar, are popular smaller towns. Baden-Württemberg also contains old [Free Imperial Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Imperial_Cities) such as [Biberach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biberach_an_der_Riss), [Esslingen am Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esslingen_am_Neckar), [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn), [Ravensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravensburg), [Reutlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reutlingen), [Künzelsau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCnzelsau), [Schwäbisch Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schw%C3%A4bisch_Hall) and [Aalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalen) as well as the southernmost and sunniest city of Germany, [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg), close to Alsace and Switzerland, being a base for exploring the [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest) (e.g., for [skiing](28478.md) in winter or for hiking in summer) with its villages and the surrounding wine country of the Rhine Valley of South Baden.[^cite_note-Planet-17]

The countryside of the Upper Neckar valley (where [Rottweil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rottweil) is famous for its [*Fastnacht*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian-Alemannic_Fastnacht) carnival) and the pristine [Danube](21209639.md) valley [Swabian Alb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Alb) (with [Hohenzollern Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenzollern_Castle) and [Sigmaringen Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmaringen_Castle)), as well as the largely pristine [Swabian Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Forest?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Upper Rhine Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Rhine_Valley), and [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance), where all kinds of water sports are popular, with the former Imperial, today border town of [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanz) (where the [Council of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Constance) took place), the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) village at [Unteruhldingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unteruhldingen), the flower island of [Mainau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau), and the hometown of the [Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin), [Friedrichshafen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrichshafen) a.o., are especially popular for outdoor activities in the summer months.[^cite_note-Planet-17]

In spring and autumn (April/May and September/October), beer festivals (fun fairs) take place at the [Cannstatter Wasen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannstatter_Wasen) in Stuttgart. The [Cannstatter Volksfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannstatter_Volksfest), in the autumn, is the second-largest such festival in the world after the Munich [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest). In late November and early December [Christmas markets](1340652.md) are a tourist magnet in all major towns, the largest being in Stuttgart during the three weeks before Christmas.

The [Bertha Benz Memorial Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertha_Benz_Memorial_Route) is a 194 km signposted scenic route from [Mannheim](99627.md) via [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) and [Wiesloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesloch) to [Pforzheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pforzheim) and back, which follows the route of the world's first long-distance [journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_trip) by automobile which [Bertha Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertha_Benz) undertook in August 1888.

Baden-Württemberg also is home to [Europa-Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa-Park) in [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust,_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg). The largest [theme park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park) in Germany, and the second most popular theme park in Europe, after [Disneyland Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland_Paris).[^cite_note-56]

Baden-Württemberg is home to some of the oldest, most renowned, and prestigious universities in Germany, such as the universities of [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) (founded in 1386, the oldest university within the territory of modern Germany), [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) (founded in 1457), and [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_T%C3%BCbingen) (founded in 1477). It also contains three of the eleven German *excellence universities* (Heidelberg, Tübingen, [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Konstanz) and [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology) and formerly, Freiburg ).[^cite_note-61]

Other university towns are [Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mannheim) and [Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm). Furthermore, two universities are located in the state capital [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), the [University of Hohenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hohenheim), and the [University of Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart). Ludwigsburg is home to the national film school [Filmakademie Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmakademie_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg) (Film Academy Baden-Wuerttemberg). The private [International University in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_University_in_Germany?action=edit\&redlink=1) was situated in [Bruchsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruchsal), but closed in 2009. Another private university is located in Friedrichshafen, [Zeppelin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin_University).

Furthermore, there are more than a dozen *[Fachhochschulen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule)*, i.e., universities of applied sciences, as well as *Pädagogische Hochschulen*, i.e., teacher training colleges, and other institutions of tertiary education in Baden-Württemberg.[^cite_note-62] [Pforzheim University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pforzheim_University_of_Applied_Sciences) is one of the oldest Fachhochschulen in Germany which is renowned and highly ranked for its engineering and MBA programs.

The state has the highest density of universities of any state in Germany.[^cite_note-63]

## Transport

### Railway



Map of rail transport infrastructures in Baden-Württemberg

[DB Regio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Regio) regional train on the [Black Forest Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest_Railway_\(Baden\))



[SWEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWEG_S%C3%BCdwestdeutsche_Landesverkehrs-AG) local train on the [Münstertal branch line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Krozingen%E2%80%93M%C3%BCnstertal_railway)&#x20;

[Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) form a major part of the transport infrastructure in Baden-Württemberg. As of 2017, the main [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) railway network managed by [DB InfraGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_InfraGO) consists of about 3,350 kilometres (2,080 mi) of railway lines connecting all major settlements of the state,[^cite_note-64] with about 6,500 trains operating every day. As part of [high-speed rail in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Germany), the [Mannheim–Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim%E2%80%93Stuttgart_high-speed_railway) and [Stuttgart–Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendlingen%E2%80%93Ulm_high-speed_railway) high-speed lines were built, and the [Karlsruhe–Basel high-speed line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe%E2%80%93Basel_high-speed_railway), paralleling the traditional [Mannheim–Karlsruhe–Basel railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim%E2%80%93Karlsruhe%E2%80%93Basel_railway), is currently under construction. In and around Stuttgart, the old [terminal station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_Hauptbahnhof) is currently being replaced with an underground through station as part of the controversial [Stuttgart 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_21) project.

Local branch lines of around 860 kilometres (530 mi), managed by the state-owned [SWEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWEG_S%C3%BCdwestdeutsche_Landesverkehrs-AG) and [Hohenzollerische Landesbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenzollerische_Landesbahn) (HzL), Karlsruhe-owned [Albtal-Verkehrs-Gesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albtal-Verkehrs-Gesellschaft) (AVG), private [Württembergische Eisenbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttembergische_Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft) (WEG) and other smaller rail infrastructure operators, complete the state's railway infrastructure.[^cite_note-65]

[Passenger train](15461460.md) services in Baden-Württemberg are operated partly by various subsidiaries of [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn), the national railway operator, such as [DB Fernverkehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Fernverkehr) with its high-speed [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_Express), as well as [IC trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_\(Deutsche_Bahn\)); and [DB Regio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Regio), operating some regional train services in the state. Cross-border train services are also provided by [French Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNCF)' [TGV](57212.md) trains, as well as by [Swiss Federal Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Railways) and [Austrian Federal Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96BB) (including its [Nightjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightjet) night trains).

Since the 1990s, the [around 120 individual regional train services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regional_railway_routes_in_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg) in Baden-Württemberg have been managed by the state-owned [Nahverkehrsgesellschaft Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahverkehrsgesellschaft_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg?action=edit\&redlink=1) (NVBW),[^cite_note-66] which has started to commercially tender out these train operations.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] DB Regio, SWEG, HzL, AVG and WEG have been joined in operating regional trains by [Agilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agilis), [Bodensee-Oberschwaben-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodensee-Oberschwaben-Bahn?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Arverio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arverio), [SBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Railways), [Schwäbische Alb-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schw%C3%A4bische_Alb-Bahn?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [VIAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vias_\(rail_company\)), which entered the market.[^cite_note-69]

Starting in the 1970s, regional rail around major cities has been transformed into high-frequent [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn) networks, currently the following systems exist (partly) in Baden-Württemberg: [Stuttgart S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_S-Bahn), [Rhine-Neckar S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Neckar_S-Bahn), [Breisgau S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breisgau_S-Bahn) and [Basel S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_S-Bahn). In and around the cities of Karlsruhe and Heilbronn, the [Karlsruhe Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Stadtbahn) system combines elements of traditional S-Bahns with tram operations within the urban cores ([tram-trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram-train)).

Historically, the railway system in Baden-Württemberg was developed at first by the state's predecessors' state railways: the [Grand Duchy of Baden State Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden_State_Railway) opened its first railway line between [Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim_Hauptbahnhof) and [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Hauptbahnhof) on 12 September 1840; uniquely in Germany, it used a [broad gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_ft_3_in_gauge_railways) of 1,600 mm (5 ft 3 in) in its early years. In Württemberg, the [Royal State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_W%C3%BCrttemberg_State_Railways) opened their first line on 22 October 1845 between two present-day suburbs of Stuttgart.[^cite_note-70] In 1900, the [Hohenzollerische Landesbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenzollerische_Landesbahn) (HzL) was founded to expand the rail system in the Prussian province, which had previously only been served by short sections of Württemberg lines passing through "foreign" territory. After the construction of the main lines, various private or local government initiatives constructed branch or local railway lines, some of which survive today as AVG, SWEG, [RNV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhein-Neckar-Verkehr) or WEG lines. In 1920, the Baden and Württemberg state railways merged with other state railways to form [Deutsche Reichsbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Reichsbahn), which was replaced by [Deutsche Bundesbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bundesbahn) after World War II. A lot of smaller railway lines, both DB and remaining local or private lines, closed in the decades after World War II, or were at least run-down with minimal service for passengers. Since the 1990s, some of these lines have been revived and revitalised marking good examples for increased ridership through attractive trains and timetables, with some examples being the [Kraichgau Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraichgau_Railway) (taken over by AVG) and the [Schönbuch Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%B6nbuch_Railway) (reopened by WEG).[^cite_note-71]

A popular TV programme portraying railways around Germany and the world, *[Eisenbahn-Romantik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenbahn-Romantik)*, originates in Baden-Württemberg and is produced by its public broadcaster [SWR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCdwestrundfunk).

Baden-Württemberg's area is covered by 19 [*Verkehrsverbünde*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund) (transport associations), organising and managing local public transport, as well as ensuring harmonised fares between different bus and train operators.[^cite_note-72] For inter-*Verbünde* journeys, the *bwtarif*, created in 2018, offers seamless tickets across the state.[^cite_note-73]

As of 2023, tram and light rail systems exist in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Freiburg_im_Breisgau), [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Heidelberg),[^cite_note-74] [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn_Stadtbahn),[^cite_note-75] [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Karlsruhe),[^cite_note-76] [Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Mannheim/Ludwigshafen),[^cite_note-77] [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_Stadtbahn) and [Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Ulm). International tram lines also reach Baden-Württemberg: [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Basel)'s tram 8 serves [Weil am Rhein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_am_Rhein), while [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg_tramway)'s tram D extends to [Kehl](1171482.md). There is also a [trolleybus system in Esslingen am Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Esslingen_am_Neckar).

### Airports

Baden-Württemberg is served by several airports. The largest is [Stuttgart Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart_Airport), which functions as the state’s main international hub. Other commercial airports include [Karlsruhe/Baden-Baden Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe/Baden-Baden_Airport) and [Friedrichshafen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrichshafen_Airport). The [EuroAirport Basel Mulhouse Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAirport_Basel_Mulhouse_Freiburg), located on the French–German–Swiss border, [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) and [Zurich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Airport) are also frequently used by passengers from the region.

The population of Baden-Württemberg was 10,486,660 in 2014, of which 5,354,105 were female and 5,132,555 male. In 2006, the birth rate of 8.61 per 1000 was almost equal to the death rate of 8.60 per 1000. 14.87 percent of the population was under the age of 15, whereas the proportion of people aged 65 and older was at 18.99 per cent (2008). The [dependency ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependency_ratio)–the ratio of people aged under 15 and over 64 in comparison to the working-age population (aged 15–64)–was 512 per 1000 (2008). In 2018, Baden-Württemberg [ranked 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_Human_Development_Index) on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI) among all states in Germany, after [Hamburg](13467.md). With an average [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) of 79.8 years for men and 84.2 years for women (2017–2019 [life table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_table)), Baden-Württemberg [ranks first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_life_expectancy) in this category among all states in Germany for both sexes.[^cite_note-80]

Baden-Württemberg has long been a preferred destination of immigrants. As of 2013, almost 28% of its population had a [migration background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_background) as defined by the Federal Statistical Office of Germany; this number clearly surpassed the German average of 21% and was higher than in any other German state with the exception of the city states of Hamburg and Bremen.[^cite_note-81] As of 2014, 9,355,239 of the population held German citizenship, whereas 1,131,421 were [foreign nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\)).[^cite_note-82]

Northern and most of central Württemberg has been traditionally [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) (particularly [Lutheran](23371382.md)) since the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) in 1534 (with its centre at the famous [Tübinger Stift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCbinger_Stift)). The former [Electorate of the Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_the_Palatinate) (Northwestern Baden) with its capital Heidelberg was shaped by [Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism) before being integrated into Baden. [Upper Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Swabia), and the Upper Neckar Valley up to the bishop seat of [Rottenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rottenburg_am_Neckar), and Southern Baden (the Catholic archbishop has its seat in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg)) have traditionally been bastions of [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism). Catholics have a very narrow plurality in the state, with 6% of the population adhering to Islam and 24% of the population disclaiming any religion or adhering to other faiths.

### Sports



[MHPArena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MHPArena) in Stuttgart, one of the largest stadiums in Germany

#### Football

Football is the biggest sport in Baden-Württemberg. Clubs currently competing in the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) include [SC Freiburg](1438835.md), [TSG 1899 Hoffenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSG_1899_Hoffenheim) and the most successful club in the state, [VfB Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfB_Stuttgart), meanwhile [Karlsruher SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruher_SC), [1. FC Heidenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_Heidenheim), [SV Sandhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SV_Sandhausen) and [Waldhof Mannheim](3438052.md) also compete in the top three German soccer divisions.

#### Handball

[Handball-Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball-Bundesliga) multiple champions [Frisch Auf Göppingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisch_Auf_G%C3%B6ppingen) and [Rhein-Neckar Löwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhein-Neckar_L%C3%B6wen), as well as [TVB 1898 Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Bittenfeld) are based in Baden-Württemberg. Frisch Auf Göppingen won [EHF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_EHF_Champions_League) (Europe's premier club tournament) twice, in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959%E2%80%9360_European_Cup_\(handball\)) and [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961%E2%80%9362_European_Cup_\(handball\)). Several major women's handball clubs are also based here, including 3-time [Frauen Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball-Bundesliga_Frauen) champions [SG BBM Bietigheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SG_BBM_Bietigheim).

#### Basketball

Compared to other German states, Baden-Württemberg has a particularly high density of professional basketball teams such as [Riesen Ludwigsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riesen_Ludwigsburg), [ratiopharm Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratiopharm_Ulm), [USC Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Heidelberg), [PS Karlsruhe Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_Karlsruhe_Lions) and others.

#### Ice hockey

One of the most decorated German ice hockey clubs, [Adler Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adler_Mannheim), is based in the city of [Mannheim](99627.md). Other [DEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Eishockey_Liga) clubs, such as [Bietigheim Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Bietigheim_Steelers) and [Schwenninger Wild Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwenninger_Wild_Wings) are also based in the state.

#### Volleyball

Baden-Württemberg is home to the most successful club in German volleyball history, the [Volleyball-Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Volleyball-Bundesliga) club [VfB Friedrichshafen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfB_Friedrichshafen), which won [CEV Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEV_Champions_League) in [2006–07](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_CEV_Champions_League) season.

#### Motorsport

There are also multiple motorsport facilities, the most famous one being long-time [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1) [circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_circuits) [Hockenheimring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockenheimring).

### Dialects

[Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) and [Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconian_\(linguistics\)) dialects of German are spoken in Baden-Württemberg. Slightly different variants of the Alemannic dialect [Swabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Jura) are spoken in central and southern Württemberg, including in [Upper Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Swabia), the [Swabian Alb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Jura), and the central [Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neckar) Valley of the Stuttgart region. In [South Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Baden), the local dialects are [Low Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Alemannic_German) and [High Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Alemannic_German) (i.e., variants of what is also [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German)). In the northern part of Baden, i.e., the area around [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe), [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn) and [Mosbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosbach), [South Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Franconian_German) dialects are predominant. In the *[Kurpfalz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Palatinate)*, however, with the cities of [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) and [Mannheim](99627.md), the idiom is [Rhine Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenish_Franconian_languages) (i.e., [Palatinate German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_German_language)), while in the Northeast of Baden-Württemberg [East Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Franconian_German) is spoken.

The same or similar Alemannic dialects are also spoken in the neighboring regions, especially in [Bavarian Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabia_\(Bavaria\)), Alsace ([Alsatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsatian_dialect)), German-speaking Switzerland (Swiss German), and the Austrian [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg). In contrast, the other Franconian dialects range from the Netherlands over the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland), [Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine), and [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) up to [Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia) in northern [Bavaria](3764.md).

[Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) and [Pleißne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotwelsch) were spoken while [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_language) is still being used by some.[^cite_note-Grab_von_Josef_Reinhard-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

A variant of the Alemannic German of Baden developed into the [Colonia Tovar dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonia_Tovar_dialect), spoken by descendants of immigrants from Baden who went to [Venezuela](32374.md) in 1843.
