**Jabalpur** (IPA: [\[d͡ʒəbəlpʊɾ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)), formerly anglicised as **Jubbulpore**, is the fourth largest city by area of the [Indian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_and_union_territory_capitals_in_India) of [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md). It is situated on the banks of the [Narmada River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) in the state of [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), India. Jabalpur is the administrative headquarters of the [Jabalpur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_district) and the [Jabalpur division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_division). The [Madhya Pradesh High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh_High_Court) is located in the city.

It is generally accepted that the game of [snooker](27378.md) originated in Jabalpur.[^cite_note-auto-9] Jabalpur is also the railway headquarters of the [West Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Central_Railway). [Jabalpur Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Cantonment) is one of the largest cantonments in India and houses the army headquarters of five states[^cite_note-10] ([Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), [Chhattisgarh](47734.md), [Orissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa), [Bihar](442482.md) and [Jharkhand](197225.md)). The city is renowned for the [Marble Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Rocks) at [Bhedaghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhedaghat), an 8 km gorge sculpted entirely from pure marble by the ferocious Narmada River, as it descends from the [Dhuandhar Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhuandhar_Falls) boasting a rare status of being one of only three such gorges in the world, alongside [Taroko Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taroko_Gorge) in [Taiwan](25734.md) and [Trigrad Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigrad_Gorge) in [Bulgaria](3415.md).

Jabalpur holds the distinction of being the first dinosaur fossil discovery site in [Asia](689.md) in 1828 near Bara Simla Hill by British officer [William Henry Sleeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Sleeman), with later finds in the region including dinosaur eggshells and fossils from species like *[Titanosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosaurus)* and *[Rajasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasaurus)*.

## Etymology

According to a prevalent theory, Jabalpur was named after a sage named Jabali, who meditated on the banks of the Narmada river.[^cite_note-11] Another theory suggests an [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) origin of the word since *jabal* (جبل) in Arabic means granite boulders or huge boulders, which were common in the region.[^cite_note-12] According to a fringe theory, the name refers to *Jauli Pattala*, a sub-divisional unit, mentioned in [Kalachuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalachuris_of_Tripuri) inscriptions. Jauli also refers to the [Huna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huna_people) queen of the Kalachuri king, Karna. It was spelled as *Jubbulpore* during British rule[^cite_note-13]

In 2006, the Jabalpur Municipal Corporation renamed the city to Jabalpur.[^cite_note-14]

## History

Mythology describes three [Asuras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asura) (evil spirits) in the Jabalpur region, who were defeated by the Hindu god [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva). [Tripurasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripurasura) being the main asura, gave the city its *puranic* name Tripur Tirth.[^cite_note-city-15] [Tripuri region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalachuris_of_Tripuri) corresponds to the ancient [Chedi Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chedi_Kingdom) of Mahabharata times, to which king [Shishupala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shishupala) belongs.

[Ashokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) relics dating to 300 BCE have been found in Rupnath, 84 kilometres (52 mi) north of the city, indicating the presence of the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) (322 to 185 BCE) in the region.[^cite_note-city-15] When the empire fell, Jabalpur became a [city-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state) before coming under the rule of the [Satavahana dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) (230 BCE to 220 CE). After their reign, the region was ruled locally by the Bodhis and the Senas, following which it became a [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state) of the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) (320 to 550).[^cite_note-city-15]

The region was conquered by the [Kalachuri Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalachuris_of_Tripuri) in 875 CE. The best known Kalachuri ruler was Yuvaraja-Deva I (r. 915–945), who married Nohla Devi (a princess of the [Chalukya dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty)).[^cite_note-16] One of the Kalachuri ministers, Golok Simha Kayastha, was instrumental in founding the [Chausath Yogini Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chausath_Yogini_Temple,_Jabalpur) near [Bhedaghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhedaghat). His descendants include Bhoj Simha, who was the [Dewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan) to the Gond king [Sangram Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangram_Shah) (1491–1543); Dewan Aadhar Singh Kayastha, who was the prime minister to [Rani Durgavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_Durgavati) (r. 1550–1564),[^cite_note-17] and Beohar Raghuvir Sinha, the last [Jagirdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagir) of Jabalpur who reigned until 1947.

### Gond rule



Rani Durgavati preparing for the battle of Narrai; fresco by Beohar Rammanohar Sinha in Jabalpur's Shaheed-Smarak

Jabalpur was an important centre of power during the rule of the [Gond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondi_people) kings of [Garha-Mandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garha-Mandla).[^cite_note-18] The ruler of [Garha-Mandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garha-Mandla), Madan Shah, (1138–1157) built a watchtower and a small hilltop fort at [Madan Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Mahal,_Jabalpur), an area in Jabalpur. In the 1500s, the Gond king, [Sangram Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangram_Shah) held [Singorgarh fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singorgarh_fort). [Rani Durgawati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_Durgawati) was a princess of the [Chandela Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandela_dynasty#Descendants) who was married to [Dalpat Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalpat_Shah) of the [Gond dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garha-Mandla). She was well aware of the importance of water conservation, and hence she built more than 85 ponds in Jabalpur, mainly in Ranital, Haathital, Madhatal and Hanumantal.[^cite_note-19]

The Gond king, [Hriday Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hridayshah) (1634–1668), moved his court to the [Mandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramnagar,_Madhya_Pradesh) fort. He secured water sources and built irrigation structures. The kingdom was invaded in 1742 by the [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy) [peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) (prime minister), [Balaji Baji Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Baji_Rao) along with Visaji Chandorkar, the [Maratha governor of Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathas_of_Saugor), and made a tributary state of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-seoni-21]

### Maratha rule

The [Maratha rulers of Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathas_of_Saugor) finally annexed the weakened [Garha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garha_Kingdom) in 1781.[^cite_note-22] Around 1798, the Maratha [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) gave the [Nerbuddah valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) to the [Bhonsle kings of Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur_Kingdom), who ruled the area until 1818, when it was seized by the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) after the [Battle of Sitabuldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sitabuldi).[^cite_note-imperial-23]

### British rule

The British made Jabalpur the commission headquarters of the Narmada territories and established a cantonment here. Under the British Raj, Jabalpur became the capital of the [Saugor and Nerbudda Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugor_and_Nerbudda_Territories), which was part of the British North-Western Province. The Saugor and Nerbudda Territories became part of the new [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces) in 1861 which in 1903 became the Central Provinces and [Berar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berar_Division). By the early 20th Century Jabalpur was the headquarters of a brigade in the 5th division of the Southern Army.

A significant event was the Tripuri Congress session in 1939, led by [Subhash Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhash_Chandra_Bose).[^cite_note-24] Under the guidance of [Lokmanya Tilak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokmanya_Tilak), the [Flag Satyagraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Satyagraha) was successfully launched. [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) visited Jabalpur four times.&#x20;

The Congress session in 1939, held at Tripuri near Jabalpur in present day Madhya Pradesh, became one of the most consequential moments in the history of the Indian national movement. The session took place at Tilwaraghat on the banks of the Narmada River and is remembered primarily for the re election of Subhas Chandra Bose as president of the [Indian National Congress](149333.md), despite opposition from Mahatma Gandhi and the Congress high command.

Bose defeated [Pattabhi Sitaramayya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattabhi_Sitaramayya), the candidate backed by Gandhi, highlighting deep ideological divisions within the Congress. While Gandhi and his supporters favored a more cautious and non confrontational approach, Bose argued for a more assertive strategy to achieve independence at the earliest. These differences dominated debates during the Tripuri session and strained relations between Bose and the Congress leadership.

The conflict did not end with the election. Continued resistance from senior leaders and the Working Committee made it difficult for Bose to function effectively as president. The tensions that surfaced at Tripuri ultimately led to Bose’s resignation in April 1939, marking a turning point that reshaped both his political path and the broader direction of the freedom movement.[^cite_note-jabalpur.cantt.gov.in-25]

A memorial gate, Kamania Gate, was built in the city to commemorate these events.

After India's independence in 1947, the [Central Provinces and Berar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces_and_Berar) became the state of Madhya Pradesh.[^cite_note-jabalpur.cantt.gov.in-25]

Under British rule, and among others in the works of [Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling), the city name was spelled *Jubbulpore*.

The city's institutional buildings still showcase the [British colonial architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonial_architecture).

Jabalpur has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) typical of north-central India (Madhya Pradesh and southern [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md)). Summer begins in late March, lasting until June. May is the hottest month, with an average temperature exceeding 40 °C (104 °F). Summer is followed by the [southwest monsoon](57630.md), which lasts until early October and produces 889 mm (35 in) of rain from July to September. The average annual precipitation is nearly 1,386 mm (54.6 in). The Winter begins in late November and lasts until early March. January is the coldest month, with an average daily temperature near 15 °C (59 °F).

### Flora and fauna

#### Extinct species

A species of [noasaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noasaurid) [theropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theropod) dinosaur named *[Laevisuchus indicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laevisuchus_indicus)* was discovered near Jabalpur in 1917 by [Charles Alfred Matley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Alfred_Matley) and described by [Friedrich von Huene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_von_Huene) and Matley in 1932.[^cite_note-Carrano-30] Another small dinosaur discovered at that time by Friedrich von Huene and described by the team in 1932, named *[Jubbulpuria tenuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubbulpuria_tenuis)*, was categorised as [junior synonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_synonym) of *Laevisuchus indicus* in 2024.[^cite_note-Carrano-30][^cite_note-31]

## Government and public services

Main article: [Jabalpur Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Municipal_Corporation)

### Civic administration

Jabalpur covers an area of 263 square kilometres (102 sq mi).[^cite_note-Jabalpur_City-3] The [Jabalpur Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Municipal_Corporation) (JMC), is charged with governance of the city's civic and infrastructural assets. The corporation has two wings: deliberative and executive. The head of the executive wing is a municipal commissioner who is responsible for the corporation's day-to-day operation and assists the deliberative wing in the decision-making process. The JMC council has one elected representative (corporate) from each ward. Council elections, by popular vote, are held every five years. A corporate from the majority party is selected as mayor.

Jabalpur contributes one member to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). [Ashish Dubey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashish_Dubey) of [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) had been elected as the [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_parliament,_Lok_Sabha) in the [2024 Lok Sabha election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_general_election).[^cite_note-35] The city sends eight members to the [State Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly): four from the city (Jabalpur Purba, Jabalpur Uttar, [Jabalpur Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Cantonment) and Jabalpur Paschim) and four from rural areas of the district. Jabalpur is divided into eight zones, each consisting of several wards.

### Municipal finance

According to financial data published on the CityFinance Portal of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs,[^cite_note-36] the Jabalpur Municipal Corporation reported total revenue receipts of ₹618 crore (US\$74 million) and total expenditure of ₹569 crore (US\$68 million) in 2022–23. Tax revenue accounted for about 21.5% of the total revenue, while the corporation received ₹151 crore in grants during the financial year.

### Division headquarters

Jabalpur is the [divisional headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_India) for eight districts: Jabalpur, [Seoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoni_district), [Mandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandla_district), [Chhindwara](2815567.md), [Narsinghpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narsinghpur_district), [Katni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katni_district), [Dindori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dindori_district) and [Balaghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaghat_district). The district, which was reconstituted on 25 May 1998, has seven *[tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil)*: Jabalpur, [Sihora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihora), [Patan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patan,_Madhya_Pradesh), [Majholi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majholi), [Shahpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahpura,_Jabalpur), [Panagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagar) and [Kundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundam). The city is the headquarters of the Madhya Pradesh State Electricity Board, the Home-guards and other state and central-government offices.

### Military establishments

The Jabalpur Cantonment is one of the largest cantonments in India.[^cite_note-37] In addition to the ordnance factories, other organisations present in the city include HQ Madhya Bharat Area, the [Jammu & Kashmir Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_&_Kashmir_Rifles) Regimental Centre, [the Grenadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grenadiers) Regimental Centre, 1 Signal Training Centre, College of Material Management, Central Ordnance Depot, 506 Army Base Workshop, Military Hospital, HQ Chief Engineer Jabalpur Zone, Military Dairy Farm, and HQ Recruiting Zone. Civilian organisations which are part of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) are the Cantonment Board, Controller of Defence Accounts, Defence Standardisation Cell and the [Canteen Stores Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canteen_Stores_Department_\(India\)).

## Economy



[Vehicle Factory Jabalpur (VFJ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VFJ)'s Matang truck

### Agriculture

The [Narmada River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) bringing in freshwater from the [Vindhya Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya_Range) has developed Jabalpur district into an agrarian economy. The land of the Narmada basin with its fertile alluvial soil gives good yields of sorghum, wheat, rice, and millet in the villages around Jabalpur. Important among commercial crops are pulses, oilseeds, cotton, sugar cane, and medicinal crops. [Kharif crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharif_crop) occupy 60% of total farmland and [rabi crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_crops) occupy 40% with 71.4% area under food grain production. Nearly 59% of landholders are marginal whereas small farmers share 18% of farmland.

### Industries

Jabalpur has a variety of industries largely based in mineral substances of economic value found in the district, although the ready-made garments industry is a substantial portion of production in Jabalpur.

Defence establishments started in the early 20th century. Jabalpur has [Vehicle Factory Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_Factory_Jabalpur), Grey Iron Foundry, [Gun Carriage Factory Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Carriage_Factory_Jabalpur) and [Ordnance Factory Khamaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Factory_Khamaria) which belong to the [Ordnance Factories Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Factories_Board) manufacturing various products for the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md). The Gun Carriage Factory was started in the year 1904 is well equipped and manufacture gun parts, mounting, shells, and a variety of the other product for war purposes. Vehicle Factory Jabalpur (VFJ) was started as a manufacturer of trucks and other defence vehicles. The other two are Grey Iron Foundry (GIF) and Ordnance Factory Khamaria (OFK).

The city has three regimental centres: Grenadiers, Jammu and Kashmir rifles and the Signals regiment. Jabalpur is also the army headquarters of Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, and Orissa. Jabalpur is an important divisional headquarters, having nine districts: Jabalpur, Seoni, Mandla, Chhindwara, Narsimhapur, Katni, Dindori, Balaghat, and Pandhurna. The Jabalpur District has been reconstituted on 25 May 1998. It now has four tehsils Jabalpur, Sihora, Patan, and Kundam. Jabalpur also has the headquarters of the Madhya Pradesh State Electricity Board, Homeguards, and many other state and central government offices. There are seven blocks in the district with 1449 inhabited villages, 60 uninhabited, 1209 revenue villages, and 4 forest villages. The presence of several industries in Jabalpur bolstered the industrial scenario of the city. However, the industrial growth of the area owes much to the defence establishments and the four ordnance factories.

The presence of the military base and the ordnance factories have improved the infrastructure of the city. This has boosted the industrial development of Jabalpur. The important industries in Jabalpur are:

- Tobacco business

- Vendors for Coca-Cola India & Parle

Jabalpur is home to production facilities of companies such as [Jupiter Wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Wagons), [KEC International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEC_International), [Sheela Foam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheela_Foam_Limited), and Udaipur Beverages Ltd.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

The nominal GDP of Jabalpur District was estimated at Rs. 42,518 crores for the year 2020–21.[^cite_note-41]

## Notable sites

Main article: [List of tourist attractions in Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tourist_attractions_in_Jabalpur)



[Marble-art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_marble)

Notable sites include [Marble Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Rocks) in [Bhedaghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhedaghat), [Hanumantal Bada Jain Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanumantal_Bada_Jain_Mandir), [Madan Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Mahal,_Jabalpur), [Dhuandhar Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhuandhar_Falls), Chausath-Yogini, Gwarighat, the balancing rock near Madan Mahal Fort and the Shiv Statue at Kachnar City.

The world-renowned tiger reserves like [Kanha National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanha_National_Park), [Bandhavgarh National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandhavgarh_National_Park), [Panna National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panna_National_Park), and [Pench National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pench_National_Park) can be easily visited via Jabalpur. The largest wildlife sanctuary by area, [Nauradehi Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauradehi_Wildlife_Sanctuary) is close to Jabalpur and can be easily visited. The recently notified [Veerangana Durgavati Tiger Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veerangana_Durgavati_Tiger_Reserve), 7th tiger reserve in Madhya Pradesh also lies in close vicinity to Jabalpur.

Hanumantal Bada Jain Mandir is a 17th-century Jain temple that appears like a fortress with numerous [shikharas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikhara). The temple has 22 shrines (vedis), making it the largest independent Jain temple in India.

Madan Mahal is a fort built by the Gond king Madan Shahi in 1116 which is situated atop a hill in Jabalpur.

Kachnar city is known for a 23-metre-high (76 ft) [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) statue housing a cavern with replicas of Shiva [lingas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linga) from 12 shrines nationwide.[^cite_note-50] The city also houses the [Rangawati Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_Durgawati_Museum) which was built in 1964 to commemorate [Rani Durgavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_Durgavati). The museum also hosts ancient relics, sculptures and a collection of items related to Mahatma Gandhi.

[Dumna Nature Reserve Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumna_Nature_Reserve_Park) is an ecotourism site located near [Jabalpur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Airport). It houses Khandari Dam, a source of drinking water to the city.

The Tilwadeshwar temple is located near the Tilwara Ghat and it is also the place where Gandhi's ashes were immersed.

Other tourist destinations near the city include [Paat Baba Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paat_Baba_Mandir), Gupteshwar Mandir, Bhadbhada falls,[^cite_note-51] Gughra Fall,[^cite_note-52] Osho Amritdham,[^cite_note-53] [Pisanhari Ki Madiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisanhari_Ki_Madiya) which is a historic [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) pilgrimage near [Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhash_Chandra_Bose_Medical_College),[^cite_note-54] and Nandishwardeep Jain temple.

[Maharishi Mahesh Yogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharishi_Mahesh_Yogi)'s ashram in Karondi village is located about 90 kms from the city. Lying on the Tropic of Cancer, the location is claimed to be the geographical central point of India by Yogi.[^cite_note-55]

Jabalpur Airport Terminal Building

Railway Station

[Jabalpur Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Junction_railway_station), is one of the five major railway stations located in the city. [Madan Mahal railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Mahal_railway_station) is located in the heart of Jabalpur City.



Office of the General Manager, West Central Railway, Jabalpur

Jabalpur city homes the Zonal Headquarters of [West Central Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Central_Railway_zone) (WCR). Jabalpur is also one of the three [divisional headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_railway_division) in WCR. The boundaries of divisional headquarters extend up to [Itarsi Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itarsi_Junction_railway_station) in the south-west, [Bina Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bina_Junction) station in the north, [Manikpur Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manikpur_Junction_railway_station) and [Rewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewa,_Madhya_Pradesh) station in the North East and [Singrauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singrauli) station in the east. Now this line provides direct connectivity to [Nagpur Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur_Junction_railway_station) and [Raipur Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raipur_Junction_railway_station), via Gondia Junction.

Jabalpur Junction is well connected with Rail and has dedicated trains to [New Delhi](51585.md), [Mumbai](19189.md), [Kolkata](47905.md), [Lucknow](392095.md), [SVDK Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katra,_Jammu_and_Kashmir), [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer), [Somnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_temple), [Pune](164634.md), [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore), [Coimbatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbatore) etc.

### Road&#x20;

Jabalpur has excellent road connectivity. [NH-45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_45_\(India\)) connects it to Bhopal, [NH-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_30_\(India\)) connects it to Varanasi and Mandla, and [NH-44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_44_\(India\)) connects it to Nagpur. Apart from this, Jabalpur also has a good network of state highways.

The Jabalpur Outer Ring Road is an under-construction 114 km (71 mi) corridor being developed to divert long-distance and heavy traffic away from Jabalpur. With a cost of ₹3,540 crore (US\$370 million), it will be the second-longest ring road in India after [Hyderabad's ORR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Ring_Road,_Hyderabad).[^cite_note-56] The longest elevated corridor of Madhya Pradesh is also present in Jabalpur City which is nearing completion. This will be one of the longest elevated roads in India.

Main article: [List of educational institutions in Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_educational_institutions_in_Jabalpur)

Jabalpur became a centre of higher education by the end of the 19th century, with institutions such as the [Hitkarini Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitkarini_Sabha), established by local citizens in 1868,[^cite_note-57] and [Robertson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robertson_College_Jabalpur) (now bifurcated into the [Government Science College, Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Science_College,_Jabalpur), and Mahakoshal Arts & Commerce College) was established in Sagar in 1836 and moved to Jabalpur in 1873.[^cite_note-58] [Government Engineering College, Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Engineering_College) was the first technical institution in [Central India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India) to be established by the British. [IIITDM Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Information_Technology,_Design_and_Manufacturing,_Jabalpur) was founded in 2005. [Gyan Ganga Institute of Technology & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyan_Ganga_Institute_of_Technology_&_Sciences), established in 2003, is an autonomous private engineering institute in Jabalpur.

Jabalpur is known for many universities such as [Rani Durgavati University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_Durgavati_University) (also called the University of Jabalpur), [Madhya Pradesh Medical Science University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh_Medical_Science_University), [Nanaji Deshmukh Veterinary Science University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaji_Deshmukh_Veterinary_Science_University) and [Dharmashastra National Law University, Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmashastra_National_Law_University,_Jabalpur), [Indian Council of Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Council_of_Medical_Research)-NIRTH. Other institutions like [Tropical Forest Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Forest_Research_Institute) is based in Jabalpur.

Jabalpur also hosts a government medical college named [Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhash_Chandra_Bose_Medical_College).
