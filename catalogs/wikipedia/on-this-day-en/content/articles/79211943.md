The **Kamil Idris government** is the internationally recognised[^cite_note-2] [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Sudan) of the [Republic of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Sudan) formed when [Kamil Idris](5065735.md) was appointed as [prime minister of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Sudan) by the president of the [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md), [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md) on 31 May 2025.[^cite_note-3]

## Background

Sudanese president [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) was overthrown in a [coup d'état](60469131.md) in April 2019 following a series of [large-scale protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_revolution). A 39-month [transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)) was planned with the role of head of state being performed by a [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md) and a [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok_government) led by [Abdalla Hamdok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok) was formed to govern the country until [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Sudanese_general_election) planned for July 2023. The transition period ended abruptly when the transitional government was dissolved following a further [coup d'état](69110231.md), led by [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md), in October 2021. A [civil war](73556699.md) between the al-Burhan led [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) (SAF) and the paramilitary [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) led by [Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Hamdan_Dagalo) broke out in April 2023. Initially the RSF gained control of the capital, [Khartoum](17320.md) and parts of Southern and Western Sudan. However, by early 2025, the SAF had [regained ground](73563331.md) around Khartoum and by mid-February 2025, had control over about two thirds of the Khartoum metropolitan area.[^cite_note-\:0-4] In January 2026 the government returned to Khartoum after operating from [Port Sudan](213572.md).[^cite_note-5]

## Formation

Plans for the formation of a [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_government) were announced by on 10 February 2025, following sweeping gains made by the Sudanese Armed Forces in recent weeks.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:0-4] Al-Burhan stated that a technocratic [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Sudan) would be led by a civilian prime minister and include independent experts.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] [Sudan's foreign ministry](18387433.md) published a roadmap including the formation of a transitional government and a national dialogue with civil society groups, leading to free and fair elections at the end of the transition period.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

The foreign ministry requested support from the [African Union](10634933.md), the [United Nations](31769.md), and the [Arab League](52625.md) to support the roadmap.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] On 18 February 2025, the Rapid Support Forces announced plans to form their own [rival government](80144294.md), the [Government of Peace and Unity](79260861.md), to administer the parts of Sudan under RSF control.[^cite_note-13] On 19 February 2025, the Transitional Sovereignty Council updated the transitional constitution, paving the way for the formation of a new transitional government.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

[Kamil Idris](5065735.md) was named prime minister by the Transitional Sovereignty Council on 19 May 2025.[^cite_note-SudanTribune-16] The appointment of a new prime minister and formation of a civilian-led transitional government was welcomed by the [United Nations](31769.md), [African Union](10634933.md) and the [Intergovernmental Authority on Development](1921406.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] On 29 July 2025, the African Union's [Peace and Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Security_Council) reiterated its recognition of the Transitional Sovereignty Council and the transitional civilian government as the two legitimate authorities in Sudan.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Ministers

Kamil Idriss was named prime minister by the [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md) on 19 May 2025 and sworn in on 31 May 2025. Plans for a 22 ministerial team, dubbed the *"Government of Hope"*, were outlined by prime minister Kamil Idris on 19 June 2025.[^cite_note-22]
