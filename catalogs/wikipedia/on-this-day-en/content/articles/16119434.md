[Greece](12108.md) and [Israel](9282173.md) have extensive diplomatic, military, intelligence and economic relations and are considered allies.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Economist-gas-3] Both countries are part of the proposed [Energy Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Triangle) to deliver oil and gas extracted from Israel and [Cyprus](5593.md) to mainland [Europe](9239.md) with a pipeline through Greece.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The deterioration of [Israel's relations with Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Turkey_relations) following the [2010 Gaza flotilla raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid)[^cite_note-6] and during the [Gaza genocide](75668427.md) and [war](74998519.md) has contributed to the strengthening of Greek-Israeli relations. Both countries are partners in the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) (OSCE), and members of the [Union for the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Mediterranean), the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO) and other international organizations. Israel is the second largest importer of Greek products in the Middle East.[^cite_note-7]

While a mid-2024 poll found that 61% of Greeks considered Israel an ally of Greece,[^cite_note-8] two 2026 polls reported that 65% or more of Greeks viewed Israel negatively, amid global backlash during the Gaza genocide and war.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## History

### 1940s

Along with [Cuba](5042481.md), Greece was one of only two Christian-majority nations to vote against the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine). This was mainly in order not to damage Greek-Arab economic ties, and to avert a threat to expel the [Egyptian Greek community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Egypt) (which would [eventually happen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956-57_Expulsions_from_Egypt) after Nasser's coming to power).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

Following the signing of armistice agreements confirming Israel's survival in the aftermath of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), Greece *de facto* recognized the State of Israel on 15 March 1949,[^cite_note-14] and it was diplomatically represented in Tel Aviv on a lower-than-embassy level.

### 1960–70s

Relations between Israel and Greece slightly improved during the [Greek junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_junta) which ruled from 1967 to 1974. The junta praised the "martial aspects of the Israeli state" and assisted the United States in supporting Israel during the [Six-Day War](29328.md). Greece also sent a [consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consul_\(representative\)) to Jerusalem during this period, but refrained from granting full recognition to balance its relations with the Arab states.[^cite_note-15]

### 1980–90s

Particularly under Prime Minister [Andreas Papandreou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Papandreou)'s terms in 1981–89 and 1993–96, tensions existed between Greece and Israel due to Greece's traditional support of the [Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people).[^cite_note-16] In 1982, the Greek government coordinated with [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat), leader of the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization), to allow his evacuation from [Tripoli, Lebanon](268796.md) to Athens by Greek ships when [Israel invaded Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:0-18] Israeli military cooperation with Turkey[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] and controversies over the [Greek Orthodox Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Jerusalem) in Jerusalem also dampened relations.[^cite_note-21]

In the 1990s, efforts were made to improve ties between the two countries. Greece formally recognized Israel in 1990 under Prime Minister [Konstantinos Mitsotakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantinos_Mitsotakis)[^cite_note-22] and their first defense cooperation agreement was signed in 1994, although it was never implemented.[^cite_note-23] Bilateral trade doubled between 1989 and 1995. That year Israel, exported \$200 million worth of chemicals and oil products to Greece and imported \$150 million worth of cement, food, and building materials.[^cite_note-24]

### 2000–2010s



Prime Minister of Greece [George Papandreou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Papandreou) and Prime Minister of Israel [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) in July 2010.



President of Greece [Prokopis Pavlopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokopis_Pavlopoulos) and President of Israel [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin) in March 2016.

Relations between Greece and Israel further grew due to the decline of Israeli-Turkish relations under the [AK Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK_Party) of [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdo%C4%9Fan), in power in Turkey since 2002, especially in the aftermath of the [Gaza flotilla raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid) in May 2010. Greece's desire to increase its deterrent power against Turkey and the improvement in [U.S.-Greece relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece%E2%80%93United_States_relations) were also factors.[^cite_note-25]

In 2006, President [Moshe Katsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Katsav) visited Greece, in what was the first official visit by an Israeli head of state.[^cite_note-TDS_travel_document_systems-26][^cite_note-27] In July 2010, [Greek Prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Prime_minister) [George Papandreou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Papandreou_\(junior\)) (son of Andreas Papandreou) made an official visit to Israel.[^cite_note-28]

In August 2010, Prime Minister of Israel [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) became the first Israeli Prime Minister to visit Greece. On his two-day tour, Netanyahu and Greek Prime Minister [George Papandreou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Papandreou) discussed greater cooperation between the nations' militaries, defense industries and economies.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

In January 2011 Foreign Minister of Israel [Avigdor Liberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Liberman) made a state visit to [Athens](1216.md). During that visit, the two countries reportedly set up a joint committee to study ways of improving cooperation on strategic and anti-terror issues.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In June 2017 Prime Minister Netanyahu paid an official visit to [Thessaloniki](40471.md), meeting with members of the [city's ancient Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Thessaloniki) and visiting the historic [Monastir synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastir_Synagogue_\(Thessaloniki\)).[^cite_note-34]

### 2020s

On January 2, 2020, the Prime Ministers of Greece, Cyprus and Israel signed the [EastMed pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EastMed_pipeline) deal in Athens.[^cite_note-35]

In May 2021, an agreement was reached after a [government-to-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-to-government) negotiation for the establishment of the [International Flight Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Flight_Training_Center,_Kalamata) within the [Kalamata Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamata_International_Airport) premises in [Kalamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamata), Greece. The €1.375 billion deal includes a 22-year [public-private partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership) with Israeli military company [Elbit Systems](1341669.md) to modernize Greece’s pilot training capabilities.[^cite_note-TimesDeal-36][^cite_note-37]

The [Greek state surveillance scandal](71635140.md), first revealed in 2022, in which the Mitsotakis government deployed [Predator spyware](74888377.md) against political targets, led to scrutiny of the spyware's link to former Israeli intelligence figures[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] and the role of Israel in exporting private spyware to foreign governments.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]



The [2026 Global Sumud Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Global_Sumud_Flotilla) in [Ierapetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ierapetra), Crete, on 3 May 2026, after its interception offshore by the [IDF](39237.md).

Relations between Greece and Israel continued to strengthen, with Greece abstaining to vote on the [October 2023 UN General Assembly resolution for a humanitarian truce and aid access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-10/21) during the [Gaza war](74998519.md) and a visit to Tel Aviv by Prime Minister [Kyriakos Mitsotakis](2780816.md) later that month.[^cite_note-\:0-18]

In April-May 2026, Greece allowed the release in [Crete](6591.md) of around 175 [Global Sumud Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Global_Sumud_Flotilla) members who had been detained by Israeli forces in international waters in the Greek search and rescue zone, while en route to deliver humanitarian aid to Gaza.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

## Military collaboration



The Israeli and Greek Navy joined forces near [Piraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piraeus)



Two [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) [Apache Longbows](37746.md) alongside a Greek Apache during a joint exercise, June 2011

In October 2010, the Israeli and Greek air forces trained jointly in Greece. According to the BBC, this signified a boost in ties that was due in large part to Israel's rift with Turkey.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

[Israeli President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_Israel) [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres) thanked Greece for its role in thwarting the planned [second Gaza flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_II) in 2011.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

In November 2011, the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) hosted Greece's [Hellenic Air Force](363254.md) in a joint exercise at the [Uvda base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovda_Airport).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-reuters.com-53] Greece sent five [F-16](11642.md) block 52 fighter jets for a five-day exercise, which included practice air fights as well as ground attacks. Israeli [F-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15) and F-16 fighter jets also participated in the exercise, along with Boeing fuel supplier airplanes.[^cite_note-jointexercise-54] Similar training was held in 2012 by the IAF in cooperation with the Hellenic Air Force in the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) and parts of southern Greece in response to the need of the IAF to train pilots in unfamiliar areas.[^cite_note-jointexercise-54][^cite_note-55]

On March 14, 2013, the navies of Israel, Greece and the US held a two-week joint military exercise for the third year in a row. The annual operation is nicknamed Noble Dina and was established in 2011. Similar to Noble Dina in 2012, the exercise in 2013 included defending offshore natural gas platforms and simulated air-to-air combat and anti-submarine warfare.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

Also, on March 27, 2017, Israel participated in the large-scale Iniohos military exercise, which is organized annually by Greece, along with the USA, Italy and the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] It also joined the exercise in 2019.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

### Blue Flag exercise



Israeli and Greek naval officers in May 2012, when the two navies conducted a joint-drill near [Piraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piraeus)

In November 2013, Israel hosted a "massive" aerial-maneuver [drill](1225714.md), code-named "Blue Flag," at the [Ovda air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovda_Israeli_Air_Force_Base), near the southern city of [Eilat](66162.md), in the [Negev Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Desert), modeled after the [US Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Air_Force)'s annual [Red Flag exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Red_Flag).[^cite_note-preps-64] The exercise included seven combat [squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(aviation\)) from the Israeli Air Force and one squadron each from the air forces of Greece, the United States, and Italy. Half of Israel's air space had been closed to traffic for the exercise, from approximately the center of the country southwards. Pilots practised attacks on enemy bases and tactics for defeating anti-aircraft measures, such as shoulder-held missiles, advanced surface-to-air missiles, and radar systems.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-preps-64] Observers to the drill included [military attachés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_attach%C3%A9) and political representatives from [Cyprus](5593.md) and [Bulgaria](3415.md).[^cite_note-66]

### Opening of military attaché office in Athens

In April 2014 the IDF and the Ministry of Defense of Israel announced the closure of their military attaché office in Switzerland during the summer of 2014. It was decided to open a new military attaché office in Greece due to the growing military cooperation between the two countries and as a counterweight to the decline of defense relations with Turkey. Until April 2014 the military attaché in Italy was also responsible for transactions and security relations in Greece, but after the announcement of the decision Athens will host a permanent military attaché who will address security relations between Greece and Israel directly.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### Status of forces agreement

Greece is one of only three countries Israel has signed a [status of forces agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_forces_agreement) (SOFA), with the other being the United States and Cyprus. The agreement enables Israel in hosting Greek military forces in its territory or stationing Israeli military forces in the territory of Greece, as part of the military agreements and comprehensive security arrangements between the two countries.[^cite_note-71]

### Cooperation on anti-drone systems and cybersecurity

In January 2026, Greece and Israel agreed to deepen their defence cooperation with a focus on counter-unmanned systems and [cybersecurity](7398.md). Greek Defence Minister [Nikos Dendias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikos_Dendias) and Israeli Defence Minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) announced that the two countries would exchange technical expertise and collaborate on systems to counter swarms of unmanned aerial and underwater drones, and strengthen readiness against cyber threats following talks in [Athens](1216.md). The initiative builds on existing military ties, including joint exercises and the operation of an air training centre in Greece, and is part of broader efforts to enhance bilateral defence collaboration amid evolving security challenges in the Eastern Mediterranean.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

In July of the same year, Greece approved a €3.5 billion agreement to acquire a new multi-layered air defence system based on Israeli defence technology. The "Achilles Shield" system is expected to include Israeli-made radar and missile systems developed by [Rafael Advanced Defense Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems) and [Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries), and further capabilities like counter-drone technology.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

On 31 August 2026, Israel and Greece signed a €3 billion defense agreement, worth more than [NIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel) 10 billion, under which Israel would develop and deliver a "multi-layered" national air-defense system for Greece as part of the Achilles Shield project. The system would integrate [David's Sling](18975145.md), [BARAK MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BARAK_MX?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [SPYDER](9830103.md) missile-defense systems, alongside [IAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries) radars and a command-and-control system developed by [Rafael Advanced Defense Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems).[^cite_note-76] The deal signed between the two nations is part of a plan by Greece to spend €28 billion overall by 2036 for a comprehensive system to protect the country from attack by aircraft, ballistic missiles and drones.[^cite_note-77]

## Energy cooperation



Greek Foreign Minister Lambrinidis (l.) and Israeli Foreign Minister Avigdor Lieberman during a break in the [66th U.N. General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixty-sixth_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly), 2011.

The joint Cyprus-Israel oil and gas explorations centered on the [Leviathan gas field](30257584.md) are also an important factor for Greece, given its strong links with [Cyprus](5593.md).[^cite_note-Economist-gas-3][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] ΔΕΗ-Quantum Energy, a Cyprus-based group including Greece's state-controlled power utility [Public Power Corporation of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Power_Corporation_of_Greece) (PPC, also known as ΔΕΗ) is planning to lay the world's longest subsea power cable, linking Israel, Cyprus and Greece. The link, called the [EuroAsia Interconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAsia_Interconnector) project, would be the longest in the world.[^cite_note-reuters.com-53][^cite_note-80] The tripartite energy memorandum of understanding came after nearly a year of negotiations and was signed in Nicosia, Cyprus, by Energy and Water Resources Minister Silvan Shalom; Nicos Kouyialis, the Cypriot minister of agriculture, natural resources and environment; and George Lakkotrypis, the Greek minister for the environment, energy and climate change.

On 8 August, Greece, Israel and Cyprus signed the tripartite energy memorandum of understanding after the completion of one year of negotiation in [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia). Negotiations were held between the Energy and Water Resources Minister of Israel [Silvan Shalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvan_Shalom), the Cypriot minister of agriculture, natural resources and environment Nicos Kouyialis and the Greek minister of environment, energy and climate change George Lakkotrypis.[^cite_note-81] The 2,000-mega-watt EuroAsia Interconnector is planned to lift Cyprus and Israel out of energy isolation through cheaper electricity as supported by George Lakkotrypis. Silvan Shalom announced that the agreement is "historic" and said that it demonstrated the powerful relations between the three countries, adding that the electric conduit will become a cable and is going to export electricity to the European energy market.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] The Greek Prime Minister [Antonis Samaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonis_Samaras) proclaimed in 2013 that Israel has a special role to play in supplying Europe with energy resources and supported that it can become a key energy hub.[^cite_note-85]

### Electricity connection between Cyprus, Israel and Greece

Main article: [EuroAsia Interconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAsia_Interconnector)

Electricity connection between Hadera of Israel and Vasilikos in Cyprus is one of the projects that will be funded by the European Union in the framework of the programme [Connecting Europe Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecting_Europe_Facility).[^cite_note-86] According to a press release issued by the European Commission the amount earmarked for the Hadera Israel-Vasilikos Cyprus connection is approximately €1.325m. [The European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_European_Commission) on February 17, 2017. approved €14.5 million as financial support for final detailed studies prior to Project Implementation.[^cite_note-mirror35197-87][^cite_note-P310-88][^cite_note-INE-89] The project is based on an undersea cable for the connection of the electricity systems of Israel, Cyprus and Greece. Its capacity will be 2000 MW and its length approximately 1518 km. It will include three connections: 329 km between Israel and Cyprus, 879 km between Cyprus and Crete and 310 km between Crete and mainland Greece. It will allow electricity transmission in both directions.[^cite_note-90]

## Economic relations



A hotel rooftop in Athens. Israeli investors have bought properties and hotels in the city.

Trade between Greece and Israel was \$1.3 billion in 2024, growing from \$920 million in 2023.[^cite_note-\:1-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Greek exports to Israel were \$900 million, led by metal products and electrical equipment; Israeli exports to Israel were \$350 million, led by refined petroleum and other chemicals and metal products.[^cite_note-\:1-91][^cite_note-94]

In the 2020s, there has been extensive Israeli investment in the Greek property sector and it is estimated at over €500 million annually.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] This has included purchases of hotels in the [tourism industry](159622.md) and investments as part of the Greek [golden visa](29574973.md) scheme.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] During the Gaza war, 10,000 Israelis have relocated to Greece.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

Greece is a major travel destination for Israelis. In 2025, Greece was the top international destination for air passengers from Israel, with 2.2 million,[^cite_note-102] while in 2024, 621,000 Israelis visited Greece and spent €419 million.[^cite_note-103] Cruise ships with Israeli tourists entering Greek ports faced disruption by anti-[genocide](75668427.md) and pro-Palestine protesters in 2025 and 2026.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

## Cooperation between Israeli and Greek lobbies in the United States



Foreign Minister [Dimitris Avramopoulos](1068047.md) meeting with [David Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Harris_\(advocate\)), the President of the Organisation of American Jewish Committee in Athens in July 2012

A new joint action committee for the Greek-Israeli alliance was created in the U.S. Congress in early 2013. The creation and goals of the Greek-Israeli Caucus under the name Congressional Hellenic-Israel Alliance were announced at a special event held in the Congress.[^cite_note-106] It is co-chaired by Congress members [Gus Bilirakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Bilirakis) the Republican representative from Florida and [Ted Deutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Deutch) the Democrat from Florida, and the Greek-Israeli Caucus consists of powerful members of both Republican and Democratic party. It was estimated that it may become the most important pressure group in Congress by 2014.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

On 13 March 2013 in Washington the Israeli ambassador [Michael Oren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Oren) hosted the launching of a new congressional grouping dedicated to improving Israeli-Greek-Cypriot ties.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Attending the launch were the co-chairmen of the newly established Hellenic-Israel Caucus, [Ted Deutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Deutch) and [Gus Bilirakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Bilirakis) as well as lawmakers including [John Sarbanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sarbanes) and [Eliot Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_Engel), the senior Democrat on the US House of Representatives Foreign Affairs Committee. Israeli Ambassador Michael Oren in his remarks at the dinner at his residence touted shared economic and strategic interests among Greece, Cyprus and Israel. The Greek ambassador Christos Panagopoulos in Washington announced that cooperation among the three countries would bring "peace, stability and prosperity" to the region. Also addressing the event was Olympia Neocleous, the chargé d'affaires at the Cypriot embassy in Washington.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

With the passing of Greek-American community leader Andrew Athens, President of the [World Council of Hellenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_%E2%80%93_World_Council_of_Hellenes_Abroad), the [American Jewish Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_Committee) has commemorated his work to advance Greek-Jewish and Hellenic-Israeli ties. The most recent occasion occurred in recognition of Athens' 90th birthday before AJC's National Board of Governors and invited guests from the political and diplomatic communities, in his hometown of Chicago in 2011. Partnering early on with his friend [Maynard Wishner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynard_Wishner), a fellow Chicagoan and AJC national leader, Athens spearheaded a number of joint AJC and Greek-American delegations to Greece, Cyprus and Israel.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

## Agricultural cooperation



Firefighting delegation with airplanes and helicopters from [Greece](12108.md) rallied to help Israel stop the spread of the fires in 2010.

The arid topography of Israel has spurred Israeli scientists to develop innovative farming methods and desalination technologies. The start of an efficient desalination by Israeli scientists as planned will be a boost to many of Greece's islands such as [Santorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santorini) that suffer inadequate freshwater reserves and must often rely on shipped water.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] Agricultural Development and Foods Minister Athanassios Tsaftaris in a visit to Israel with Israeli Agriculture and Rural Development Minister [Orit Noked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orit_Noked) in 2011 emphasised that both governments are very interested in further boosting agricultural development.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-ekathimerini.com-122]

## Cultural relations



The Greek Altar of Calvary, [Church of the Holy Sepulchre](7810.md), [Jerusalem](16043.md)

Many of the most prominent Orthodox Christian and Jewish interfaith officials, scholars and clerics held a three-day conference in [Thessaloniki](40471.md) in June 2013 to discuss the crucial importance of protecting the environment and religious values and condemned events of anti-Semitism and religious prejudice around the world. The meeting had an aim to help improve even further relations between these two ancient faith communities.[^cite_note-123] Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew has declared 2013 the Year of Global Solidarity, thus Metropolitan Emmanuel declared: "It is well documented that Greeks living in Thessaloniki at the time of the Shoah stood with their Jewish neighbors and friends. Today, more than ever, we must stand together to battle the evils of anti-Semitism, religious prejudice and all forms of discrimination.".[^cite_note-ekathimerini.com-122][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

The presence of the [Romaniote Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romaniote_Jews) in Greece and in modern Israel is historically important. The Romaniotes have used the [Judaeo-Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevanic) for centuries in their [Jewish prayer liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusach_\(Jewish_custom\)).[^cite_note-127]

With the financial help of the [Onassis Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onassis_Foundation) a faculty for [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_studies) and Modern [Hellenic studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_studies) in the [University of Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Haifa) has been established, and many faculties for the study of the [Ancient Greek Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics#Philology) are spread around Israel. In 2015 a faculty for [Judaic studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaic_studies) was opened in the [Aristotle University of Thessaloniki](715160.md) with the financial help of the [Jewish community of Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Thessaloniki).[^cite_note-128]

### Greek singers and Israel



[Glykeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glykeria) during a concert held in [Rishon LeZion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishon_LeZion), September 2013

Main article: [Greek music in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_music_in_Israel)

Greek music is considered the most popular foreign genre in Israel after American and British music. Israel is the top destination for Greek music concerts alongside [Germany](11867.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Cyprus](5593.md).[^cite_note-129] Popular Greek singers who have performed in Israel include [Manolis Angelopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolis_Angelopoulos),[^cite_note-130] [Eleftheria Arvanitaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleftheria_Arvanitaki), [George Dalaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dalaras),[^cite_note-131] [Haris Alexiou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haris_Alexiou),[^cite_note-132] [Glykeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glykeria)[^cite_note-133] and [Natassa Theodoridou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natassa_Theodoridou).[^cite_note-134] In December 2012 Natassa Theodoriou performed some of her songs in [Tel Aviv Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Performing_Arts_Center) in Hebrew. In 2007 during the interview of [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres) by [Hellenic Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Broadcasting_Corporation) on George Dalaras concert in Israel, President Peres stated: "In Israel we love the Greek music. For us Greece is a country but also a melody".

A special broadcasting day of Greek classical and modern music was organised on 12 June 2013 by the [Israel Broadcasting Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Broadcasting_Authority), in cooperation with the Embassy of Greece in [Tel Aviv](31453.md). The program started with an hourly show, presented by Ambassador Lampridis and the Director of the Radio Station Arie Yass with an emphasis on the roots and the historical evolution of modern Greek music. During the whole day, Kol Ha Musica broadcast works of modern Greek composers including Hatzidakis, Theodorakis, Spanoudakis, Remboutsika, Karaidrou, Mikroutsikos and Markopoulos. The programme also included Sephardi music from [Thessaloniki](40471.md) and [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes).

The Israeli radio show *Kol Shishi*, hosted by Yaron Enosh, is almost completely dedicated to Greece and its culture, music, philosophy and history, and has an audience of approximately 800,000 Israeli listeners. In Israel there are 12 internet radio stations that broadcast exclusively Greek music.[^cite_note-135]
