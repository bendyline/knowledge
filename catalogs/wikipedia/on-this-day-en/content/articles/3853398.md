The **Spanish Air and Space Force** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Ejército del Aire y del Espacio*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Army of the Air and Space') is the [aerial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare) and [space warfare](956982.md) branch of the [Spanish Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armed_Forces). It is part of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Spain\)) and is one of the three constitutionally recognized military branches.

The Spanish Air Force traces its origins to 28 February 1913, when King [Alfonso XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_XIII) issued a royal decree creating the "Military Aeronautical Service" within the [Spanish Army](2142325.md).[^cite_note-2] This service functioned as the air branch of the Armed Forces until the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), when it split into two factions. After the war, the current Air Force was created. In June 2022, it was renamed as "Air and Space Force" to reflect the new space capabilities this branch has acquired in recent years.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## History

### Early stages



The [*Plus Ultra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_Ultra_\(aircraft\)) at [Palos de la Frontera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_de_la_Frontera)&#x20;

[Hot air balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon) have been used with military purposes in Spain as far back as 1896. In 1905, with the help of [Alfredo Kindelán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Kindel%C3%A1n), [Leonardo Torres Quevedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Torres_Quevedo) directed the construction of the first Spanish [dirigible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirigible) in the Army Military Aerostatics Service, created in 1896 and located in [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara,_Spain). The new airship was completed successfully in 1908 and, named 'Torres Quevedo', made numerous test and exhibition flights.[^cite_note-Redondo-2008-5]

The [Spanish Army](2142325.md)'s air arm, however, took off formally in 1909 when Colonel [Pedro Vives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Vives) and Captain [Alfredo Kindelán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Kindel%C3%A1n) made an official trip to different European cities to check the potential of introducing airships and [airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane) in the Spanish Armed Forces. One year later, a Royal decree established the National Aviation School (*Escuela Nacional de Aviación (civil)*) in [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe), near [Madrid](41188263.md), under the [Ministry of Development](19633684.md) (*Ministerio de Fomento*).

The established institution became militarized under the name *Aeronáutica Española* when Colonel Pedro Vives was chosen to lead it as director of the *Aeronáutica Militar*, Military Aeronautics, the name of the air arm of the [Spanish Army](2142325.md). Captain Alfredo Kindelán was named Chief of Aviation, *Jefe de Aviación*.[^cite_note-trece-6]

On 17 December 1913, during the war with [Morocco](19291.md), a Spanish expeditionary squadron of the *Aeronáutica Española* became the first organized military air unit to see combat during the first systematic bombing in history by dropping [aerial bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb) from a [Lohner *Flecha*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohner_B.I) (Arrow) airplane on the plain of Ben Karrix in Morocco.[^cite_note-trece-6][^cite_note-7] During the years that followed, most of the military activity of the Spanish Air Force would take place in Northern Morocco.

In 1915 Spain's first seaplane base was opened at [Los Alcázares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alc%C3%A1zares) on the Mar Menor in the Murcia region and Alfredo Kindelán was named Military Aeronautics Director, displacing Pedro Vives. The Catalan Flying School was established in [Can Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_Tunis?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Barcelona](4443.md) the following year and Getafe Aerodrome became a full-fledged military air base. In 1919 General [Francisco Echagüe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Echag%C3%BCe?action=edit\&redlink=1) replaced Kindelán as leader of the *Aeronáutica Española*.

In 1920 two [Nieuport 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuport_80) and one [Caudron G.3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudron_G.3) were first painted with squadron identification numbers and the Spanish Air Force [roundel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundel). Shortly thereafter the *Aeronáutica Naval*, the air branch of the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy), already established through a Royal decree four years earlier, became functional in [El Prat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Prat_de_Llobregat), in the same location as present-day [Barcelona Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Airport).[^cite_note-8]



Headquarters of the Spanish Air and Space Force in Madrid

In 1921, following the [Spanish defeat at Annual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_defeat_at_Annual), known as *Desastre de Annual* in Spain, the Zeluán Aerodrome was taken over by the [Rif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif) army and another aerodrome was built at [Nador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nador). Lieutenant Colonel Kindelán was named *Jefe Superior de Aeronáutica*, becoming chief-commander of the air force in 1926, at the time when [Spanish Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Morocco) was retaken and the [Rif War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif_War) ended.

In 1926 a crew of Spanish aviators, that included [Ramón Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Franco), [Julio Ruiz de Alda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Ruiz_de_Alda), [Juan Manuel Duran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Duran_\(aviator\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Pablo Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Rada?action=edit\&redlink=1), completed the first Trans-Atlantic flight between Spain and South America in January 1926 on the [*Plus Ultra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_Ultra_\(aircraft\)). That same year, pilots González Gallarza, Joaquín Loriga Taboada and Rafael Martínez Esteve completed the first flight between Spain and the Philippines, in just one month. The expedition was flown with two [Breguet 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breguet_19) and known as the *Escuadrilla Elcano* or "Elcano Squadron".

In 1930 the Aeronaval Base in [San Javier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia%E2%80%93San_Javier_Airport) was established and in the same year a pro-Republican revolt in the [Cuatro Vientos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuatro_Vientos) military aerodrome near Madrid was quashed. After the proclamation of the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) in 1931, General [Luis Lombarte Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Lombarte_Serrano?action=edit\&redlink=1) replaced Kindelán as chief-commander of the air force, but he would be quickly succeeded by Commander [Ramón Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Franco), younger brother of later dictator [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco). Captain [Cipriano Rodríguez Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipriano_Rodr%C3%ADguez_D%C3%ADaz?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Lieutenant [Carlos de Haya González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_de_Haya_Gonz%C3%A1lez?action=edit\&redlink=1) flew non-stop to [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md), then a Spanish colonial outpost. Under Capitan Warlela [cadastral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadastral) surveys of Spain were carried out using modern methods of [aerial photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_photography) in 1933. The following year Spanish engineer [Juan de la Cierva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_la_Cierva) took off and landed on [seaplane carrier Dédalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_seaplane_carrier_D%C3%A9dalo) with his [autogyro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autogyro) C-30P. In 1934 Commander [Eduardo Sáenz de Buruaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_S%C3%A1enz_de_Buruaga) became new chief-commander of the air force.

Following a Government decree dated 2d October 1935, the Dirección General de Aeronáutica was placed under the authority of the War Ministry, *Ministerio de la Guerra*, instead of under the *[Presidencia del Gobierno](203556.md)*, following which in 1936 the Air Force regional units became restructured. Accordingly, the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy)-based *Escuadra* model was replaced by *Región Militar*divisions which are still operative today.[^cite_note-Ejército_del_Aire-9]

### Air warfare in the Spanish Civil War

See also: [Spanish Republican Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_Air_Force)

After the military rebellion that triggered the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), Spanish military aviation was divided into the Air Force of the [Spanish republican government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) and the **National Aviation** (*[Aviación Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviaci%C3%B3n_Nacional)*), established by the rebel army.



Spanish [Cierva C.30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cierva_C.30) autogyro

In July 1936, right after the coup, the first German [Junkers Ju 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_52) and Italian [Savoia-Marchetti SM.81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoia-Marchetti_SM.81) arrived to help the rebels and the [Fiat CR.32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_CR.32) fighters began operating in the [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain) front. In August [Heinkel He 51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinkel_He_51) fighters were also deployed. These planes helped the rebel army to gain full control of the air, as did the German and Italian expeditionary forces, the [Condor Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_Legion) and the [Aviazione Legionaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviazione_Legionaria). At first, the Spanish Republican Air Force had the control of great swathes of Spanish territory using a motley selection of planes, but the unwavering help received by [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) from [Nazi Germany](21212.md) and [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy) reversed the situation. In September 1936 the Navy and Air Ministry, *Ministerio de Marina y Aire*, and the Air Undersecretariat, *Subsecretaria del Aire* were established under the command of [Indalecio Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indalecio_Prieto) as minister. The first serious air combat took place over Madrid when Italian bombers attacked the city in a massive bombing operation.[^cite_note-Ejército_del_Aire-9] In the reorganization of the military in the areas of Spain that had remained loyal to the government, the new military structure of the republic merged the *Aeronáutica Militar* and the *Aeronáutica Naval*, the former being the air arm of the [Spanish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_Army) and the latter the [naval aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aviation) of the [Spanish Republican Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_Navy), and formed the [Spanish Republican Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_Air_Force).[^cite_note-10] The Republican tricolor roundel was replaced by red bands for identification purposes, an insignia that had previously been used on *Aeronáutica Naval* aircraft during the monarchy in the 1920s, before the time of the Republic.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Many innovative, and often lethal, aeronautical bombing techniques were tested by Germany's Condor Legion forces on Spanish soil against the areas that remained loyal to the Republican Government with the permission of Generalísimo Franco. Nazi help to the Nationalist Air Force was part of Hitler's [German rearmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_rearmament) strategy and the techniques that German Nazi pilots learned in Spain would later be used in [World War II](32927.md). Despite the devastation and the human casualties caused by the [bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Guernica) of the [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)) city of [Guernica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernica) in 1937, known by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) as [Operation Rügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Guernica), Hitler insisted that his longterm designs in Spain were peaceful. He called his strategy "Blumenkrieg" (Flower War), as evidenced in a January 1937 speech.[^cite_note-13] The international outcry over Guernica, however, would not bring about any increase in the military help provided to the beleaguered Spanish Republic. The pilots of the Spanish Republican Air Force, often young and poorly trained were unable to check the Nazi German and Fascist Italian modern-warfare attacks. Despite Franco's claim that both air forces were equal, and despite the help of foreign pilots, Spanish Republican planes were mostly obsolete and often in a bad state of disrepair. Even after acquiring more planes from the Soviet Union in the mid-stages of the war, the Spanish Republican Air Force was no longer able to control the Spanish skies nor match the power of the German and Italian expeditionary forces in specific combat situations.[^cite_note-14]

The Spanish Republican Air Force became practically irrelevant after the [Battle of the Ebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Ebro) in 1938 when the root of the Spanish Republican Armed Forces was broken.[^cite_note-15] Finally it was completely disbanded after the victory on April 1, 1939.

### Post-Civil War era



A Spanish [Hispano HA-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispano_HA-200) *"Saeta"* (Bolt)

The present Spanish Air Force (*Ejército del Aire*, or EdA) was officially established on 7 October 1939, after the end of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War). The EdA was a successor to the Nationalist and Republican Air Forces. [Spanish Republican colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Second_Spanish_Republic) disappeared and the black roundel of the planes was replaced by a yellow and red roundel. However, the black and white [Saint Andrew's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Burgundy_flag) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Aspa de San Andrés*) [fin flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin_flash), the tail insignia of Franco's air force, as well as of the [Aviazione Legionaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviazione_Legionaria) of [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic) and the [Condor Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_Legion) of [Nazi Germany](21212.md), is still in use in the present-day Spanish Air Force.[^cite_note-16]

After the changes introduced at the beginning of Franco's regime the Air Regions and their Command centres were the following:

- 1st Air Region. Central.

- 2nd Air Region. Straits.

- 3rd Air Region. East.

- 4th Air Region. Pyrenees.

- 5th Air Region. Atlantic.

- Balearic Islands Air Zone

- Morocco Air Zone

- Canary Islands and West Africa Air Zone



Former [F-104 Starfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_F-104_Starfighter) of the Spanish Air Force

The [Blue Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Squadron) (*Escuadrillas Azules*) was an air unit that fought alongside the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) at the time of the Blue Division, *[Division Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_Azul)* Spanish volunteer [formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(military\)) in [World War II](32927.md). The *Escuadrilla azul* operated with the Luftwaffe on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) and took part in the [battle of Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk). This squadron was the "*15 Spanische Staffel*"/[JG 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JG_27) *Afrika* of the [VIII Fliegerkorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Air_Corps_\(Germany\)), [Luftflotte 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftflotte_2).[^cite_note-17]

During the first years after World War II the Spanish Francoist Air Force consisted largely of German and Italian planes and copies of them. An interesting example was the [HA-1112-M1L *Buchón*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispano_Aviaci%C3%B3n_HA-1112) ([Pouter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pouter)), this was essentially a licensed production of the [Messerschmitt Bf 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Bf_109) re-engined with a [Rolls-Royce Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Merlin) 500-45 for use in Spain.[^cite_note-18]

In March 1946 the first Spanish military paratroop unit, the *Primera Bandera de la Primera Legión de Tropas de Aviación*, was established in [Alcalá de Henares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares). It first saw action in the [Ifni War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifni_War) during 1957 and 1958.[^cite_note-19] Because of US Government objection to use airplanes manufactured in the US in her colonial struggles after World War II, Spain used at first old German aircraft, such as the T-2 (Junkers 52, nicknamed "Pava"), the B-2I (Heinkel 111, nicknamed "Pedro"), the [C-4K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-4K) (Spanish version of the Bf 109, nicknamed "Buchón"), and some others. Still, [Grumman Albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_Albatross) seaplanes and [Sikorsky H-19B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_H-19B) helicopters were used in rescue operations.[^cite_note-20] This is why still now in present times, EdA maintains a policy of having jet fighters from two different origins, one first line fighter of North American origin, and one from French-European origin ([F-4C Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-4C_Phantom) / [Mirage F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_F1), [Mirage III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_III); [EF-18A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EF-18A) / [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md)).

Although in sheer numbers the EdA was impressive, at the end of World War II technically it had become more or less obsolete due to the progress in aviation technology during the war. For budget reasons Spain actually kept many of the old German aircraft operative well into the 1950s and 1960s. As an example the last [Junkers Ju 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_52) used to operate in Escuadrón 721 training [parachutists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachutist) from [Alcantarilla Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcantarilla_Air_Base) near [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia), until well into the 1970s. The CASA 352 and the CASA 352L were developments built by [CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA) in the 1950s.[^cite_note-21]

Links were established in the 1950s with the United States. Spain received its first jets, like the [F-86 Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-86_Sabre) and [Lockheed T-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_T-33) together with training and transport planes like the [T-6 Texan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-6_Texan), [C-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-47) and [C-54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-54),[^cite_note-22] and the [Beechcraft T-34 Mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_T-34_Mentor).[^cite_note-23] The first series of American jets was replaced in the 1960s by newer fighters like the [F-104 Starfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-104_Starfighter), [F-4C Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-4C_Phantom) and [F-5 Freedom Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-5_Freedom_Fighter)

### Present times



Spanish [Dassault Mirage F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_F1)

After the death of dictator Franco in 1975 and the ensuing [Spanish transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy) years, the organization and equipment of the Spanish Air Force was again modernised to prepare for [Spain](26667.md)'s membership of [NATO](21133.md) in 1982. Planes like the [Mirage III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_III) and [Mirage F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_F1) were bought from [France](5843419.md) and became the backbone of the Air Force during the 1970s and part of the 1980s. French fighters formed the air force's mainstay until the arrival of the American [F/A-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18). Spanish F/A-18s participated in the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) and the [Kosovo War](16760.md) under [NATO](21133.md) command, based in [Aviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviano), [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-24] Assisted by [USAF](32090.md) [F-16s](11642.md), Spanish Air Force [EF-18As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EF-18A) dropped [laser-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb) on [Bosnian Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) ammunition depots at [Pale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), on [25 and 26 May 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Pale_air_strikes).[^cite_note-25]



[EF-18A](45998.md#Spain) taking off and banking to the left in 2015

The Spanish Air and Space Force is replacing older aircraft in the inventory with newer ones including [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) and the recently introduced [Airbus A400M Atlas](371265.md) airlifter. Both are manufactured with Spanish participation; [EADS CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA) makes the Eurofighter's right wing and leading edge slats,[^cite_note-26] and participates in the testing and assembly of the airlifter.[^cite_note-27]

Unlike the air forces of most major [NATO](21133.md) member states, the Spanish Air and Space Force currently [do not operate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AEW\&C_aircraft_operators) any [AEW\&C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEW\&C) aircraft.

Its aerobatic display team is the [Patrulla Aguila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrulla_Aguila), which flies the [CASA C-101 Aviojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_C-101_Aviojet).[^cite_note-28] Its helicopter display team, [Patrulla Aspa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrulla_Aspa?action=edit\&redlink=1), flies the [Eurocopter EC-120 Colibrí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Colibri).

In July 2014 the Spanish Air Force joined the [European Air Transport Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Air_Transport_Command), headquartered at [Eindhoven Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_Airport) in the [Netherlands](21148.md).[^cite_note-29]

The [Spanish Government](1306113.md) announced in June 2022 that the Spanish Air Force would be renamed as the Spanish Air and Space Force.[^cite_note-30]

On January 9, 2024, the Space Command was formally established.[^cite_note-31]

### Force structure

The main operational formation of the SAF is the *ala* ([wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_\(military_unit\))), roughly equivalent to an army brigade. An *ala* is normally composed of three *grupos* (groups, army regiment equivalents) - an operations group called *Grupo de Fuerzas Aéreas* (Air Force Group, shortened to *Grupo* and followed by a numerical) including the aviation squadrons and a flight operations support squadron. An operations group is normally composed of two or three *escuadrones* ([squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(aviation\))), each one normally consisting of 18 to 24 aircraft. Thus, Ala 15, with its base in [Zaragoza Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza_Air_Base), is formed by two squadrons with 18 F/A-18s each. Another group within the wing is the *Grupo de Material*, providing maintenance and repairs to the aircraft, their weapons and systems. The *Grupo de Apoyo* completes the typical wing structure and it is the air base group, providing the functioning of the air base as a military installation. A variation of the wing structure is the *Ala 11* in Morón de la Frontera air base, which has not one, but two operational groups. The *Grupo 11* operates Eurofighter aircraft in the multi-mission fighter role, while the *Grupo 22* operates P-3 Orion aircraft in the maritime patrol and ASW role and correspondingly there are two separate maintenance squadrons for the two aircraft types.[^cite_note-32]

Smaller operational units are the separate groups. They are also army regiment equivalents, but unlike the wings they are composite units, in which the operational aircraft, the maintenance and the air base squadrons report directly to the group. Such example is the *47/o. Grupo Mixto de Fuerzas Aéreas* (47th Air Force Composite Group) a mixed intelligence, electronic warfare and aerial navigation systems calibration unit at Torrejón de Ardoz air base.[^cite_note-33]

When an air base houses more than one *ala* or multiple separate *grupos*, the function of a lodger unit is provided by an air force installation unit (an army regiment equivalent) called Groupment of the ... Air Force Base (*Agrupación de la Base Aérea de ...*). Three such examples are the *Agrupación de la Base Aérea de Torrejon*,[^cite_note-34] the *Agrupación de la Base Aérea de Cuatro Vientos*[^cite_note-35] and the *Agrupación de la Base Aérea de Zaragoza*.[^cite_note-36] An *agrupación* could be responsible for the support of air force operations at more than one airfield (military or civilian). As an example the Groupment of the Zaragoza Air Force Base is responsible for the mixed use military / civilian airfields of Zaragoza, Logroño-Agoncillo and Huesca-Pirineos. An air force base, which does not house flying units is classified as an Acuartelamiento Aéreo (roughly translated as Air Force Installation in English, one such example is the *Acuartelamiento Aéreo Bardenas*, supporting the Bardenas Reales training range) and an airfield, which does not house permanently flying units is classified as an *Aerodromo Militar* (military airfield), such as the *Aerodromo Militar de Pollensa*.

## Aircraft identification



Northrop F-5 at Talavera la Real, identified with the serial prefix A (attack) E (training).9 in the Spanish system

The Spanish Air and Space Force has its own alphanumeric system for identifying aircraft. This forms a prefix to the airframe serial number, usually marked on the tail. The letter or letters, correspond to the use given. Thus, C means *cazabombardero* (fighter bomber); A, *ataque* (attack); P, *patrulla* (patrol); T, *transporte* (transport); E, *enseñanza* (training); D, search and rescue; H, helicopter; K, tanker; V, Vertical Take Off and Landing ([VTOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTOL)); and U, utility. An example would be that the F/A-18 with "C.15-08" on the tail is the fifteenth type of fighter that arrived in the Spanish Air and Space Force (the Eurofighter is the C.16) and is the eighth example of this type to enter the SAF. On the nose or fuselage the aircraft has a numeral specific to the unit in which it is based.

Variants of planes in service, for example two-seater versions or tanker versions of transports planes, add another letter to differentiate their function, and have their own sequence of serial numbers separate from the primary versions. Example: "CE.15-02" will be the second F/A-18 two-seater (Fighter Trainer) delivered to the SAF. In addition, the aircraft used by the Spanish Air and Space Force usually carry a code consisting of one or two digits followed by a dash and two numbers, painted on the nose or fuselage. The first number corresponds to the unit to which they belong, and the second the order in which they entered service. Example: the fourth F/A-18 arriving at Ala 12 will have on the nose the code "12-04". Those codes do change when the aircraft is re-allocated to a different unit.
