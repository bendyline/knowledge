**Kurdish–Iranian conflict**[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] or the **Kurdish separatism in Iran**[^cite_note-habeeb-20] is an ongoing[^cite_note-cornell10-1][^cite_note-uarkansas-4][^cite_note-habeeb-20][^cite_note-elling-21] separatist dispute between the [Kurdish](3821779.md) opposition in Western [Iran](14653.md) and the governments of Iran[^cite_note-habeeb-20] since the emergence of [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah) in 1918.[^cite_note-cornell10-1]

The earliest Kurdish separatist activities in modern times refer to tribal revolts in today's [West Azerbaijan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Azerbaijan_Province) of the [Imperial State of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_State_of_Iran), which began during the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period). The largest of these revolts were led by [Simko Shikak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak), Jafar Sultan and [Hama Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Rashid_revolt). Many however, put the starting point of the organized Kurdish political-nationalist separatism at 1943,[^cite_note-uarkansas-4] when Komala (shortly after the [Kurdish Democratic Party of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_Democratic_Party_of_Iran)) began their political activities in Iran with the aim to gain partial or complete autonomy in the Kurdish regions. Transformation from tribal to Kurdish political struggle in Iran took place in the aftermath of World War II, with the KDPI establishing the [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad) during the [1946 Iran crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946).[^cite_note-uarkansas-4] The [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR)-supported attempt to establish a Kurdish state in Western Iran failed.[^cite_note-uarkansas-4][^cite_note-22] More than a decade later, peripheral tribal [uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_revolt_of_1967)[^cite_note-uarkansas-4] launched with KDPI support throughout 1966–7. In the most violent episode of the conflict, more than 30,000 Kurds died in the [1979 rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Kurdish_rebellion_in_Iran) and the consequent [KDPI insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDPI_insurgency_\(1989%E2%80%931996\)).[^cite_note-hicks2000-16] Though the KDPI's armed struggle ended in late 1996, another Kurdish armed organization emerged in Iran by the early 2000s. The ongoing [Iran-PJAK conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-PJAK_conflict) started in 2004.[^cite_note-23]

Though Iran has not been as brutal against its own [Kurdish](17068.md) population as its neighbouring countries, it has always staunchly opposed Kurdish separatism.[^cite_note-kreyenbroek1719-24]

## History

### Tribalism and early nationalism

#### Simko's first revolt (1918–1922)

Main article: [Simko Shikak revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak_revolt)

The [Simko Shikak revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak_revolt) was an armed [Ottoman](22278.md)-backed[^cite_note-bruinessen-25][^cite_note-26] tribal Kurdish uprising against the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) of [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) ([Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Iran)) from 1918 to 1922, led by [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) chieftain [Simko Shikak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak). This tribal rebellion is sometimes regarded as the first major bid for establishing an [independent Kurdish state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_separatism) in [Persia](14653.md),[^cite_note-entessar-27] but scholars view the revolt as an attempt by a powerful tribal chief to establish his personal authority vis-à-vis the central government throughout the [region](61733686.md).[^cite_note-kreyenbroek138141-28] While elements of [Kurdish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_nationalism) were present in this movement, historians agree these were hardly articulate enough to justify a claim that recognition of Kurdish identity was a major issue in Simko's movement, and he had to rely heavily on conventional tribal motives.[^cite_note-kreyenbroek138141-28] It lacked any sort of administrative organization and Simko was primarily interested in [plunder](209811.md).[^cite_note-entessar-27] Historian [Ervand Abrahamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ervand_Abrahamian) describes Simko as "notorious" for [massacring thousands of Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_genocide) and "harassing" democrats.[^cite_note-29] Still, some Kurds today revere Simko as a hero of the independence movement.[^cite_note-elling-21]

#### 1926 Simko rebellion in Persia

Main article: [1926 Shikak revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Shikak_revolt)

By 1926, Simko had regained control of his tribe and begun another outright rebellion against the state.[^cite_note-cornell-30] When the army engaged him, half of his troops defected to the tribe's previous leader and Simqu fled to Iraq.[^cite_note-cornell-30]

#### Jafar Sultan revolt

Main article: [Jafar Sultan revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafar_Sultan_revolt)

Jafar Sultan of [Hewraman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewraman) region took control of the region between [Marivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marivan) and north of [Halabja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halabja) and remained independent until 1925. After four years under Persian rule, the tribal leader revolted in 1929, but was effectively crushed.

#### Hama Rashid revolt

[Hama Rashid revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Rashid_revolt) refers to a tribal uprising in [Pahlavi Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) following the [Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran).[^cite_note-Jwaideh-31] The tribal revolt erupted in the general atmosphere of anarchy throughout Iran, with its main faction led by Muhammed Rashid. The first stage of the revolt revolt lasted from late 1941 till April 1942 and the second stage began in 1944, but ultimately resulted in Rashid's defeat. It has been considered as one of the leading factors of the establishment of the Kurdish political [independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad).

### Political separatism

#### Mahabad crisis

Main articles: [Iran crisis of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946) and [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad)



Qazi Muhammad and Mustafa Barzani during the 1946 events

The possibility of secession in modern Iran became evident shortly after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) when the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) refused to relinquish [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran) northwestern Iranian territory.[^cite_note-kreyenbroek1719-24] [Iran crisis of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946) included a separatist attempt by the [KDP-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDP-I) and [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) groups[^cite_note-32] to establish a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) [puppet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_government),[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] and declare the [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad) in [Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md) (today's southern part of [West Azerbaijan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Azerbaijan_Province)). It arose along with another Soviet puppet state known as the [Azerbaijan People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_People's_Government).[^cite_note-kreyenbroek1719-24][^cite_note-36] The state itself encompassed a very small territory, only including [Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad) and its adjacent cities. This state couldn't incorporate southern Iranian Kurdistan as it fell inside the Anglo-American zone and also failed to attract the tribes outside Mahabad to the nationalist cause.[^cite_note-kreyenbroek1719-24] As a result, when the Soviets withdrew from Iran in December 1946, government forces were able to enter Mahabad unopposed.[^cite_note-kreyenbroek1719-24] At least 1,000 died during the crisis.[^cite_note-uarkansas-4]

[Iran crisis of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946) included an attempt of the KDPI to establish an independent Kurdish-dominated [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad) in Iranian Kurdistan.[^cite_note-uarkansas-4] Afterwards, [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) insurgencies led by the [KDP-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDP-I) and [Komala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komala_Party_of_Iranian_Kurdistan) continued for decades, but these two organization never advocated for a separate Kurdish state or greater Kurdistan as did the [PKK](69680.md) in [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-romano240-37][^cite_note-kreyenbroek138141-28][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

#### 1967 Kurdish revolt

Main article: [1967 Kurdish revolt in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Kurdish_revolt_in_Iran)

During the mid-1960s, a series of [Kurdish tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_tribes) disturbances erupted in [Western Iran](55024578.md) as they were fed up by the revival of the Kurdish Democratic Party of Iran (KDP-I).[^cite_note-cornell10-1] In 1967-8, Iranian government troops suppressed a Kurdish revolt in Western Iran,[^cite_note-uarkansas-4] consolidating the previous Kurdish uprisings in the Mahabad-Urumiya region.

#### 1979 rebellion

The [1979 Kurdish rebellion in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Kurdish_rebellion_in_Iran) was an insurrection led by the KDPI and [Komala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komala_Party_of_Iranian_Kurdistan) in Iranian Kurdistan, which eventually became the most serious rebellion against the [ new Iranian regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran). The rebellion ended in December 1982, with 10,000 killed and 200,000 displaced.[^cite_note-uarkansas-4]

#### 1989–1996 KDPI insurgency

Main article: [KDPI insurgency (1989–1996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDPI_insurgency_\(1989%E2%80%931996\))

Further information: [List of Iranian assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Iranian_assassinations)

[Insurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDPI_insurgency_\(1989%E2%80%931996\)) by the KDPI took place in [Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md) throughout the early and mid-90s, fueled by the assassination of its [leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahman_Ghassemlou) in exile in July 1989. The KDPI insurrection ended in 1996, following a successful Iranian campaign of targeted assassinations of KDPI leaders and crackdown on its support bases in Western Iran. In 1996, the KDPI announced a unilateral ceasefire, and has since acted at a low profile before renewing clashes in 2015.[^cite_note-40]

### Twenty-first century

#### 2004

PJAK insurrection

Main article: [Iran–PJAK conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93PJAK_conflict)

The [Iran–PJAK conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93PJAK_conflict) is an ongoing rebellion of [PJAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJAK) in which hundreds of Kurdish militants and Iranian forces as well as civilians have died, officially beginning from April 2004.[^cite_note-uarkansas-4] PJAK is based in the border area with [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md) and is affiliated with the Marxist [PKK](69680.md) from [Turkey](11125639.md),[^cite_note-katzman-41] though PJAK themselves tend to neglect this alleged relation. Although sometimes described as an organization demanding human rights for Kurds in Iran, it is regarded as a separatist organization by [Iranian media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_media) and various Western analysts.[^cite_note-habeeb-20][^cite_note-katzman-41][^cite_note-lovelace-42] The PJAK goal is an establishment of Kurdish autonomy and according to Habeeb, they do not intend to pose any serious threat to the regime of the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-habeeb-20]

In one of the first actions of the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_administration), PJAK was declared a "[terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization)".[^cite_note-katzman-41][^cite_note-lovelace-42] PJAK and Iranian government agreed on a ceasefire deal following the 2011 Iranian [offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Iran-Iraq_cross-border_raids) on PJAK bases. After the ceasefire agreement, a number of clashes between the [PJAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJAK) and the [IRGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC) took place in 2012,[^cite_note-elling-21] and by mid-2013, the fighting resumed in sporadic incidents, escalating in 2016.

PDKI call for federalism

In 2004, the [Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan](3197182.md) (PDKI) called for [federalisation of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Iran), "endors\[ing] a federal system for a multi-national, multi-ethnic, and multi-religious Iran".[^cite_note-JPost_only_federalism_can_save-43]

#### Renewed insurgency



[PDKI](3197182.md) fighters.

Further information: [Mahabad riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad_riots)

In January 2014, Iranian forces killed a KDPI party member while he was disseminating leaflets.[^cite_note-44]

In September 2014, the KDPI engaged Iranian security for the first time in many years, killing at least 6 Iranian soldiers.[^cite_note-45] It was unclear whether this was a result of change of policy by the [Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan](3197182.md) (which avoided violence since 1996) or an isolated sequence of incidents.

In May 2015, a suspected Iranian attack (allegedly disguised as PKK fighters) on PJAK forces on the Iran–Iraqi Kurdistan border resulted in 6 killed—2 KDPI and 4 PKK[^cite_note-46] (or allegedly Iranian agents).

On 7 May 2015, ethnic Kurds rioted in [Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad), [Iran](14653.md), following the unexplained death of Farinaz Khosravani on 4 May 2015, a 25-year-old Kurdish hotel chambermaid. Unrest and violence spread to other Kurdish cities in Iran, such as [Sardasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardasht,_West_Azerbaijan), where police clashed with hundreds of protesters on 9 May 2015, where one protestor was reportedly killed.[^cite_note-47] Soran Khedri, a former PJAK official, stated that PJAK had attacked an Iranian checkpoint, killing two Iranian personnel.[^cite_note-48] According to ARA sources, as of 11 May, the death toll rose to 6 protesters killed.[^cite_note-49] The incidents prompted harsh responses from other Kurdish opposition parties, including the [Kurdistan Freedom Party](49010325.md) and the [PDKI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDKI).

In June 2015, a KDPI attack on the Revolutionary Guard forces reportedly left 6 people dead.[^cite_note-50]

#### Low-level insurgency (2016–2023)

Main article: [Western Iran clashes (2016–2023)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\))

[Military clashes in West Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\))[^cite_note-51] refers to the ongoing military clashes between Kurdish insurgent party [Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan](3197182.md) (PDKI) and the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC), which began in April 2016. The [Kurdistan Freedom Party](49010325.md) (PAK) and [Komalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komala_Party_of_Iranian_Kurdistan) expressed their support to the [Kurdish cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_nationalism) of PDKI as well, with both clashing with Iranian security forces in 2016 and 2017 respectively. In parallel, the leftist Iranian Kurdish rebel group PJAK resumed military activities against the IRGC in 2016, following a long period of stalemate.

The 2016 clashes came following a background of what the [PDKI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDKI) described as "a growing sense of discontent and alienation in Rojhelat".[^cite_note-mee-52] The commander of the PAK military wing described their engagement and declaration of hostilities against the Iranian government were due to the fact that "the situation in eastern Kurdistan ([Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md)) has become unbearable, especially with the daily arbitrary executions of Kurds \[in Iran]".[^cite_note-ara05-53]

Iran has periodically launched strikes against the KDPI and other Iranian Kurdish dissident groups based in the frontier of the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) of Iraq. In March 2023, Iraqi Prime Minister [Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Shia'_Al_Sudani), who came to power via a coalition of Iranian-backed parties, signed a border security agreement with Iran to tighten up the frontier between the two countries. On 28 August, Iraq agreed to disarm and relocate these groups to camps near [Mosul](200475.md) by 19 September.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] According to the [New Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Arab), these groups were not disarmed as of October 2023, nor did Iraq seem to have the capability to do so.[^cite_note-56]

#### 2026 Kurdish–Iranian crisis

Main article: [2026 Kurdish–Iranian crisis](82575260.md)

In 2026, following the [2025–2026 Iran protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_Iran_protests) and subsequent [United States military buildup in the Middle East](82251337.md), major Iranian Kurdish parties created the [Coalition of Political Forces of Iranian Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_Political_Forces_of_Iranian_Kurdistan) (CPFIK), to "bring down the Islamic Republic of Iran and realize the [Kurdish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people)'s right to [self-determination](29269.md)."[^cite_note-\:3-57]

#### Western Iran clashes (2026–present)

Main article: [Western Iran clashes (2026–present)](83601452.md)

Starting in early June 2026, [new clashes](83601452.md) broke out inside Iran between Kurdish opposition groups and the IRGC.
