**Gyeonggi Province** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 경기도; Korean pronunciation: [\[kjʌŋ.ɡi.do\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)) is the most populous [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_South_Korea) in [South Korea](27019.md).

[Seoul](19159283.md), the nation's largest city and capital, is in the heart of the area but has been separately administered as a [provincial-level *special city*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial-level_cities_of_South_Korea) since 1946. [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon), the nation's third-largest city, is on the coast of the province and has been similarly administered as a [provincial-level *metropolitan city*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial-level_cities_of_South_Korea) since 1981. The three jurisdictions are collectively referred to as *[Sudogwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Capital_Area)* and cover 11,730 km<sup>2</sup> (4,530 sq mi), with a combined population of over 26 million – amounting to over half (50.25%) of the entire population of South Korea, and a third of the population of the [Korean peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea) at the 2020 census.

## Etymology

Its name, *Gyeonggi*, means "[京](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E4%BA%AC) (the capital) and [畿](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%95%BF) (the surrounding area)". Thus, *Gyeonggi Province* can be translated as "Seoul and the surrounding areas of Seoul".[^cite_note-3]

Gyeonggi Province has been a politically important area since 18 BCE, when Korea was divided into three nations during the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_of_Korea) period. Ever since [King Onjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onjo_of_Baekje), the founder of [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje) (one of the three kingdoms), founded the government in [Wiryeseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiryeseong) of Hanam, the [Han River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Korea\)) was absorbed into [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo) in the mid-fifth century, and became [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla)'s territory in the year 553 (the 14th year of [King Jinheung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinheung_of_Silla)).[^cite_note-4] Afterward, the current location of Gyeonggi Province, one of the nine states of [Later Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Silla), was called Hansanju.

The Gyeonggi region started to rise as the central region of [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo) as [King Taejo of Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Goryeo) (the kingdom following Silla) set up the capital in Gaesong. Since 1018 (the 9th year of Goryeo's [King Hyeonjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeonjong_of_Goryeo)), this area has been officially called "Gyeonggi".



Headquarters of the provincial governor, Joseon dynasty

During the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon), which was founded after the Goryeo, [King Taejo of Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Joseon) set the capital in Hanyang, while restructuring Gyeonggi's area to include Gwangju, Suwon, Yeoju, and Anseong, along with the southeast region. Since the period of King Taejong and [Sejong the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejong_the_Great), the Gyeonggi region has been very similar to the current administrative area of Gyeonggi Province.

In 1895 the 23-Bu system, which reorganized administrative areas, was effected. The Gyeonggi region was divided into Hanseong (modern [Seoul](19159283.md)), [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon), [Chungju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chungju), [Gongju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongju), and [Kaesong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaesong).

During the [Japanese colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), Hanseong-bu was incorporated into Gyeonggi Province. On October 1, 1910, it was renamed Keijo and a provincial government was placed in Keijo according to the reorganization of administrative districts.

After liberation and the foundation of two separate Korean states, Gyeonggi Province and its capital, Seoul, were separated with partial regions of Gyeonggi Province being incorporated into Seoul thereafter in 1946. Additionally, Kaesong became [North Korean](21255.md) territory, the only city to change control after the countries were divided at the [38th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_parallel_north), which is now part of North Korea's [North Hwanghae Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hwanghae_Province).

In 1967 the seat of the Gyeonggi provincial government was transferred from Seoul to [Suwon](502693.md). After Incheon separated from Gyeonggi Province in 1981, Gyeonggi regions such as [Ongjin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongjin_County,_Incheon) and [Ganghwa County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganghwa_County) were incorporated into [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon) in 1995.

## Geography

Gyeonggi Province is in the western central region of the Korean Peninsula, which is vertically situated in Northeast Asia and is between east longitude of 126 and 127, and north latitude of 36 and 38. Its dimension is 10% of Korea's territory, 10,171 square kilometres (3,927 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-5] It is in contact with 86 kilometres (53 mi) of cease-fire line to the north, 413 kilometres (257 mi) of coastline to the west, [Gangwon Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangwon_Province,_South_Korea) to the east, [North Chungcheong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Chungcheong_Province) and [South Chungcheong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Chungcheong_Province) to the south, and has Seoul, the capital of the Republic of Korea, in its center. Its provincial government is in Suwon, but some of its government buildings are in Uijeongbu for the administrative conveniences of the [northern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Gyeonggi_Province).

### Climate

The climate of Gyeonggi Province is the continental climate, which has a severe differentiation of temperature between summer and winter, and has distinctions of four seasons. Spring is warm, summer is hot and humid, autumn is cool, and winter is cold and snowy. The annual average temperature is between 11–13 °C (52–55 °F), where the temperature in the mountainous areas to the northeast is lower and the coastal areas to the southwest is higher. For January's average temperature, the Gyeonggi Bay is −4 °C (25 °F), the Namhangang (River) Basin is −4 to −6 °C (25 to 21 °F), and the Bukhangang (River) and Imjingang Basins are −6 to −8 °C (21 to 18 °F). It becomes colder and higher in temperature differentiation from coastal to inland areas. Summer has a lower local differentiation compared to winter. The inland areas are hotter than the Gyeonggi Bay area, the hottest area is [Pyeongtaek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyeongtaek), making the average temperature of August 26.5 °C (79.7 °F).

The annual average precipitation is around 1,100 millimetres (43 in), with a lot of rainfall. It is rainy in summer and dry during winter. The northeastern inland areas of Bukhangang and the upper stream of Imjingang has a precipitation of 1,300–1,400 millimetres (51–55 in), whereas the coastal area has only 900 millimetres (35 in) of precipitation.

### Nature and national parks

The topography of Gyeonggi Province is divided into [southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Gyeonggi_Province) and [northern areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Gyeonggi_Province) by the [Han River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Korea\)), which flows from east to west. The area north to the Han River is mainly mountainous, while the southern area is mainly plain.

The configuration of Gyeonggi Province is represented by *Dong-go-seo-jeo* (high in the east and low in the west), where the [Gwangju](323128.md) Mountain Range and the Charyeong Mountain Range spreads from the east and drops in elevation in the west. The fields of Gimpo, Gyeonggi, and Pyeongtaek extend to the west.

Gyeonggi Province natural environment includes its rivers, lakes, mountains, and seas. Its representative rivers are the Hangang, Imjingang, and Anseongcheon Fg(Stream), which flow into the Yellow Sea, with Gyeonggi Plain, Yeonbaek Plain and Anseong Plain forming a fertile field area around the rivers. The Gwangju Mountain Range and the Charyeong Mountain Range stretch toward China in Gyeonggi Province. Most of the mountains that rise above 1,000 metres (3,300 ft), such as [Myeongjisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myeongjisan) (1,267 metres (4,157 ft)), Gukmangbong (1,168 metres (3,832 ft)) and Yongmunsan (1,157 metres (3,796 ft)) in the [Gwangju](323128.md) Mountain Range. It iriidc Ktihas a developed granite area which, due to the granite's exfoliation effect, makes it full of strangely shaped cliffs and deep valleys. The Charyeong Mountain Range forms the boundary between Gyeonggi Province and [North Chungcheong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Chungcheong_Province), but is a relatively low-altitude hilly area.

In Gyeonggi Province, there is [Bukhansan National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhansan_National_Park) in Uijeongbu. For provincial parks, there are the Chukryeongsan Natural Recreation Area, Namhan-sanseong Provincial Park, Gapyeong [Yeoninsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeoninsan) Provincial Park, and Mulhyanggi Arboretum. Besides the listed, the scenery of well-known mountains including Soyosan of Dongducheon City, Yongmunsan of [Yangpyeong County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangpyeong_County), and [Gwanaksan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanaksan) of [Anyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyang,_Gyeonggi) and [Gwacheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwacheon), along with Hangang and Imjingang are tourist sites of Gyeonggi Province.

## Economy

As the backbone of Seoul in the means of manufacturing complex, Gyeonggi Province is evenly developed in [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) (electronics, machine, heavy and chemical industry, steel), [light industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_industry) (textile), and farm, livestock and fisheries industry. Due to the influence of recent high wages, the weight of manufacturing industries has decreased in Korea's economy. Gyeonggi Province is making efforts in many ways to improve and modernize the conventional industry structure, resulting in quick growth of innovative [small and medium-sized enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium-sized_enterprises) such as [U-JIN Tech Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-JIN_Tech_Corp.) Gyeonggi Province is unsparingly investing in the promotion of service industries related to soft competitive power such as state-of-the-art IT industry, designing, conventions and tourism, along with its great leap as a commercial hub in Northeast Asia using the Pyeongtaek Harbor.[^cite_note-9]

Besides this, it is known for its special local products such as Icheon rice and Gwangju ceramics. Leading companies representing Korea, including [Samsung Electronics](46445426.md)' headquarters, [SK Hynix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Hynix)'s headquarters, [NAVER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naver_Corporation)'s headquarters, [Samsung SDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_SDI)'s headquarters, and [Paju LG Corporation's LCD complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Corporation), are gathered in southern Gyeonggi Province, including Suwon City.[^cite_note-10]

## Transportation

Gyeonggi Province's proximity to Seoul, South Korea's capital, and Incheon, its second-busiest port, has contributed to its extremely well-developed transportation infrastructure. It is close to both [Incheon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport), South Korea's main international gateway and busiest airport, and [Gimpo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport), its second-busiest airport. Use of water transportation from the harbor at [Pyeongtaek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyeongtaek) is also high.

### Road

### Rail

Gyeonggi Province is served by [Korail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korail) commuter, standard and high-speed ([KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Train_Express)) services. It is home to Korea's first railroad, the [Gyeongin Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongin_Line), and includes portions of the [GTX-A Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Train_eXpress), [Gyeongbu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_Line), [Gyeongui Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongui_Line), [Jungang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungang_Line), and [Honam Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honam_Line). Gyeonggi has stations on the [Suin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suin_Line), [Bundang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundang_Line), [Gyeongchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongchun_Line), and [Shinbundang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinbundang_Line) [commuter rail](6286.md) services and the [Gyeongbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_High_Speed_Railway) and [Honam High Speed Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honam_High_Speed_Railway).

The area has numerous connections to the [Seoul Metropolitan Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Subway) system. [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_1) (formerly Korea National Railroad of Seoul) extends to Cheonan past Gyeonggi Province to the southwest, and to Dongducheon to the north. [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_3) connects to Goyang to the north, while [Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_4) is connected to Gwacheon and Ansan to the southwest. [Line 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_7) is connected to Uijeongbu to the north and Gwangmyeong to the south, [Line 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_8) is connected to Seongnam to the south, while the [GTX-A line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Train_eXpress) connects [Paju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paju) and [Goyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goyang) to [Yeonsinnae Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeonsinnae_Station) in [Eunpyeong-gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunpyeong-gu) and [Seoul Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Station) in [Jung-gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jung-gu).

[Uijeongbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uijeongbu) has its own light rail system, the [U Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Line), which connects to Line 1.

A short section of the [AREX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AREX) line between Gimpo and Incheon airports passes through Gyeonggi, but there are no stops within the province.

## Culture

### Historical landmarks

Gyeonggi Province has long been a capital area, leaving many historic relics and ruins. For royal tombs (called reung), there are Donggureung of [Guri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guri), and Gwangreung, Hongreung and Yureung of [Namyangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namyangju). For castles (called seong), there are [Suwon Hwaseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_Fortress), which is designated as the World Cultural Heritage, [Namwonsanseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namwonsanseong?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Haengjusanseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haengjusanseong), [Ganghwasanseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganghwasanseong), and [Doksan Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doksan_Fortress). For Buddhist temples, there are many aged temples within Gyeonggi Province where one can experience 'temple stay'. You can view folk culture in the [Korean Folk Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Folk_Village) in [Yongin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongin), and the scene of Korea's division at [Panmunjom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panmunjom) in [Paju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paju).

### Performing arts

Gyeonggi Province is investing a lot of money at a provincial level so that people do not have to go to Seoul to enjoy a high-class cultural life. There are performances at Gyeonggi Arts Center in Suwon as well as at Gyeonggi Korean Traditional Music Center in [Yongin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongin). Gyeonggi Provincial Museum in [Yongin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongin), [Nam June Paik Art Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_June_Paik_Art_Center) in [Yongin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongin), Gyeonggi Museum of Art in [Ansan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansan), and the Ceramics Museum in [Gwangju](323128.md) are some of the facilities that are currently run by the province. There are also sightseeing opportunities at Jangheung Art Park, Publication Art Complex at Heyri, [Paju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paju), and the Icheon Ceramics Exposition.

### Heyri Art Valley

[Heyri Art Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heyri_Art_Valley) is Korea's largest art town. Various Korean artists constructed the cultural town of Heyri and it features several art galleries and museums; there are about 40 museums, exhibitions, concert halls and bookstores.

## Fauna

While Korean fauna is relatively uniform, there are some differences across the country. Animals living in Gyeonggi Province include the following.

### Mammals

The [raccoon dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_dog) is widespread in the province,[^cite_note-raccoondog-19] as are the [Japanese mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_mole), [Siberian weasel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_weasel), [water deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_deer), [Korean hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_hare), [red squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_squirrel), [house mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_mouse), [striped field mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_field_mouse), and the [Eurasian harvest mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_harvest_mouse).[^cite_note-GwanggyosanMammals-20]

### Birds

#### Waterfowl

The [common merganser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_merganser), [common pochard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_pochard), [green-winged teal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-winged_teal), [Eastern spot-billed duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_spot-billed_duck), [mallard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallard), [mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_duck), [Taiga bean goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga_bean_goose), [tundra bean goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_bean_goose), [whooper swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whooper_swan), [great crested grebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_crested_grebe), [little grebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_grebe) [common moorhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_moorhen), [Eurasian coot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_coot), and [white-breasted waterhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-breasted_waterhen)[^cite_note-Waterhen-21] all inhabit the province.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22][^cite_note-eBird-23]

#### Herons and cormorants

Herons frequent the provinces waterways. These include the [black-crowned night heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-crowned_night_heron), [great white egret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_white_egret), [grey heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_heron), [little egret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_egret), [medium egret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_egret), [striated heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striated_heron), [great cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_cormorant), and [Japanese cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cormorant).[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22][^cite_note-eBird-23]

#### Raptors

Raptors in Gyeonggi Province include the [Eurasian sparrowhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_sparrowhawk), [Goshawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goshawk),[^cite_note-24] [common kestrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_kestrel), and [Eurasian hobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_hobby).[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

#### Crows and jays

A range of crows and jays are found in Gyeonggi Province, including the [azure-winged magpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azure-winged_magpie) [carrion crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrion_crow), [Eurasian jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_jay), [large-billed crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-billed_crow), [Oriental magpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_magpie), and [rook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rook_\(bird\)).[^cite_note-RookArticle-25][^cite_note-iNaturalist-22][^cite_note-eBird-23]

#### Tits

There are also many tits in the province, including the [coal tit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_tit), [Japanese tit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_tit), [marsh tit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh_tit), and [varied tit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varied_tit).[^cite_note-BirdsMSc-26][^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

#### Others

Other birds in Gyeonggi Province include the [common sandpiper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_sandpiper), [long-toed stint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-toed_stint), [wood sandpiper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_sandpiper), [ring-necked pheasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring-necked_pheasant), [white-winged tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-winged_tern), [Oriental turtle dove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_turtle_dove), [rock dove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_dove), [Oriental dollarbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_dollarbird), [common kingfisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_kingfisher), [common hoopoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_hoopoe) [great spotted woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_spotted_woodpecker), [grey-headed woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-headed_woodpecker) [Japanese pygmy woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_pygmy_woodpecker), [black-naped oriole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-naped_oriole), [Eurasian skylark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_skylark), [brown-eared bulbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-eared_bulbul), [long-tailed tit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-tailed_tit), [Oriental reed warbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_reed_warbler), [vinous-throated parrotbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinous-throated_parrotbill), [white-cheeked starling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-cheeked_starling), [dusky thrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_thrush), [Asian brown flycatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_brown_flycatcher), [Daurian redstart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daurian_redstart), [Eurasian tree sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_tree_sparrow), [brambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brambling), and [Oriental greenfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_greenfinch).[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22][^cite_note-eBird-23]

### Reptiles

The [river cooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_cooter), [peninsula cooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula_cooter), [Amur softshell turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_softshell_turtle), and [pond slider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pond_slider) live in the province's lakes and streams, while the [steppe rat snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_rat_snake) and [mountain grass lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takydromus_wolteri) are among its land-dwelling reptiles.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

### Amphibia

Gyeonggi Province has a variety of amphibia: the [Boreal digging frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_digging_frog), [Imienpo Station frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imienpo_Station_frog), [Korean brown frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_brown_frog), [Suwon tree frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_tree_frog),[^cite_note-TreeFrogNumbersBorzee-27][^cite_note-TreeFrogRangeDonga-28] and Sakhalin toad.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

### Arachnids

The province has a variety of spiders, including the [Joro spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trichonephila_clavata), *[Sernokorba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sernokorba) allidipatellis*, and *[Uroctea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uroctea) lesserti*.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

### Insects

#### Moths

A wide range of moths live in Gyeonggi Province, e.g., the [box tree moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cydalima_perspectalis), [euonymus defoliator moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pryeria_sinica), [gypsy moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymantria_dispar), [larch hawk moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphinx_morio), [meal moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyralis_farinalis), [rush veneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomophila_noctuella), *[Monema flavescens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monema_flavescens)*, *[Nordstromia duplicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordstromia_duplicata)*, *[Orthogonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonia) sera*, *[Ostrinia palustralis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrinia_palustralis)*, *Sinna extrema*, and *[Striglina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striglina) cancellata*.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

#### Butterflies

Butterflies in the province include the [Asian comma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonia_c-aureum), [common straight swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parnara_guttata), [lesser purple emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apatura_ilia), [painted lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_cardui), [pale grass blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudozizeeria_maha), [Pallas' sailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptis_sappho), [red ring skirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hestina_assimilis), [small white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieris_rapae), and *[Colias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colias) poliographus*.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

#### Bees, wasps, and hornets

Gyeonggi Province's bees, wasps, and hornets include the [western honey bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_honey_bee), dark-waisted paper wasp, [Asian hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_hornet), [European hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_hornet), and [yellow-vented hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespa_analis).[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

#### Ants

There is a wide variety of ant species in Gyeonggi Province. These include the [Asian needle ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachyponera_chinensis), [Japanese carpenter ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camponotus_japonicus), [Japanese pavement ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetramorium_tsushimae), Japanese queenless ant, yellow-footed ant, *[Aphaenogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphaenogaster) japonica*, *[Camponotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camponotus) concavus*, *[Camponotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camponotus) itoi*, *[Camponotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camponotus) nipponensis*, *[Camponotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camponotus) quadrinotatus*, *[Crematogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crematogaster) matsumurai*, *[Crematogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crematogaster) teranishii*, *[Dolichoderus sibiricus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolichoderus_sibiricus)*, *[Lasius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasius) spathepus*, *[Temnothorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temnothorax) wui*, and *[Vollenhovia emeryi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vollenhovia_emeryi)*.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]

#### Damselflies and dragonflies

The province's damselflies and dragonflies include the [common bluetail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischnura_senegalensis), *[Ischnura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischnura) asiatica*, *[Platycnemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platycnemis) phyllopoda*, blue-spotted emperor foot-tipped darter, regal pond cruiser, [white-tailed skimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthetrum_albistylum), *Deielia phaon*, and *[Sympetrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympetrum) infuscatum*.[^cite_note-iNaturalist-22]
