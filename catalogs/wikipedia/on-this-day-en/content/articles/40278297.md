**Elizabeth Amy Eaton**[^cite_note-1] (born April 2, 1955) is an American bishop who served as the fourth [Presiding Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presiding_Bishop), and the first female Presiding Bishop,[^cite_note-2] of the [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](248255.md) (ELCA) from 2013 to 2025. She was first elected to this post in 2013 and was re-elected for a second term in 2019, serving until 2025. She was succeeded by Bishop [Yehiel Curry](80581978.md) of the Metropolitan Chicago Synod to be presiding bishop of the [ELCA](248255.md). Prior to becoming presiding bishop, she served as bishop of the Northeastern Ohio Synod.[^cite_note-3]&#x20;

## Early life and education

Eaton was raised in [West Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Park,_Cleveland), in [Cleveland](5951.md), [Ohio](22199.md).[^cite_note-\:0-4] She attended the [College of Wooster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Wooster) where she earned a Bachelor of Arts in music education in 1977. Drawn to the ministry, she attended [Harvard Divinity School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Divinity_School) where she earned a [Master of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Divinity) degree in 1980.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

## Ministry

Eaton was ordained in 1981, and served as the associate pastor at All Saints Lutheran Church in [Worthington, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worthington,_Ohio), from 1981 to 1990.[^cite_note-\:0-4] In 1984, she served as a delegate to the Lutheran World Federation assembly in Budapest, Hungary. In 1990, Eaton was appointed to a one-year term as interim pastor at Good Hope Lutheran Church in [Youngstown, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngstown,_Ohio). She then moved to [Ashtabula, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashtabula,_Ohio) in 1991, where she became the pastor at Messiah Lutheran Church. She served there for fifteen years.&#x20;

Eaton was elected to the national ELCA Church Council in 2005, and in 2006, she was elected bishop of the Northeastern Ohio Synod.[^cite_note-6] Eaton became the first woman to serve as bishop in the synod when she was installed on February 7, 2007, at St. Paul's Episcopal Church in [Akron, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron,_Ohio).[^cite_note-7] She succeeded Marcus J. Miller, who resigned in 2006 to become the president of the [Lutheran Theological Southern Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Theological_Southern_Seminary) in South Carolina.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

### Election as Presiding Bishop

Eaton was elected Presiding Bishop of the ELCA on Wednesday, August 14, 2013, on the fifth ballot. She received 600 votes by the Churchwide Assembly, and the incumbent Presiding Bishop [Mark Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hanson) received 287 votes.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:0-4] She was installed as presiding bishop of the ELCA on October 5, 2013, at [Rockefeller Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Chapel) in [Hyde Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Chicago) ([Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois)).[^cite_note-11] Chicago is also the location of the ELCA headquarters. Her first six-year term as presiding bishop of the ELCA began November 1, 2013.[^cite_note-12] In 2019, she was reelected to a second term as Presiding Bishop at the ELCA Churchwide Assembly.[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

In 2016, Eaton was awarded an honorary [Doctor of Humane Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Humane_Letters) degree by [Luther College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_College_\(Iowa\)).[^cite_note-14]&#x20;

## Personal life

Eaton is married to T. Conrad Selnick, a priest of the [Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)), who is vice president of the Bexley Seabury Seminary Federation in Chicago, and together they have two adult daughters, Rebeckah and Susannah.&#x20;
