**Stuart Highway** is a major [Australian](4689264.md) highway. It runs from [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), in the [Northern Territory](21638.md), via [Tennant Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek) and [Alice Springs](183935.md), to [Port Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta) in [South Australia](26716.md); it has a distance of 2,720 km (1,690 mi). Its northern and southern extremities are segments of Australia's [Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_1_\(Australia\)). The principal north–south route through the central interior of mainland Australia, the highway is often referred to simply as "The Track".

The highway is named after Scottish explorer [John McDouall Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McDouall_Stuart), who was the first European to cross Australia from south to north.[^cite_note-3] The highway approximates the route Stuart took.

## Route description

### Overview



The highway passes through the [Woomera Prohibited Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woomera_Prohibited_Area)

Stuart Highway runs from [Darwin, Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), in the north, via [Tennant Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek) and [Alice Springs](183935.md), to [Port Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta), [South Australia](26716.md), in the south – a distance of 2,720 km (1,690 mi).

The [Royal Flying Doctor Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Flying_Doctor_Service) uses the highway as an emergency landing strip and sections of the highway are signed to that effect. These sections of highway have been specially selected and prepared for the landing of aircraft which only takes place after the piece of road has been closed by the police.

There are petrol and other facilities (meals, toilets, etc.) available at reasonable intervals (usually around 200 km (120 mi)) and more frequent rest stops. Some of the rest stops are located at scenic points with information boards, but others are little more than a picnic table and a rubbish bin in an otherwise deserted area.

### Northern Territory



Stuart Highway intersection in [Berrimah, Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berrimah,_Northern_Territory)

The Northern Territory section of Stuart Highway starts from the edge of the [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory) central business district at Daly Street and continues as a dual-carriageway to [Arnhem Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Highway) in [Howard Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Springs,_Northern_Territory). From there the highway runs 317 km (197 mi) south, passing [Kakadu Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakadu_Highway) and reaching [Victoria Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Highway) at [Katherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine,_Northern_Territory).

At [Daly Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daly_Waters,_Northern_Territory), the route number changes from A1 to A87. The highway then continues 673 km (418 mi) south, passing [Roper Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roper_Highway), [Carpentaria Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentaria_Highway) and [Buchanan Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchanan_Highway) before reaching [Barkly Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barkly_Highway) at [Tennant Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek). From there it runs a further 508 km (316 mi) south into [Alice Springs](183935.md), passing [Plenty Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenty_Highway). It passes through the [MacDonnell Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacDonnell_Ranges) and finally crosses the [Northern Territory](21638.md)/[South Australia](26716.md) border south of [Kulgera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulgera,_Northern_Territory).[^cite_note-4]

The highway was only fully sealed in February 1987 as part of the [Australian Bicentenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bicentenary) roadworks programme.[^cite_note-5] There are no police patrolling the majority of this remote highway and until the end of 2006 there was no speed limit outside towns and other built-up areas on the Northern Territory part.[^cite_note-6] The unrestricted limit has now been generally set at 130 kilometres per hour (80 mph). The bulk of the Northern Territory's population not living in Darwin lies along its track.

### South Australia



Sign near the southern end of the highway at [Port Augusta West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta_West)

Stuart Highway passes through the [Far North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_\(South_Australia\)) region to [Port Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta). The highway passes through the [Woomera Prohibited Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAAF_Woomera_Range_Complex) where travellers may not leave the road. The highway continues south-east towards Adelaide.

## History

### Background

[John McDouall Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McDouall_Stuart) led the first successful expedition to traverse the Australian mainland from south to north and return, through the centre of the continent, in 1861–1862. In 1871–1872 the [Australian Overland Telegraph Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Overland_Telegraph_Line) was constructed along Stuart's route. The principal road from [Port Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta) to [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory) was also established on a similar route.

A track developed along the route of the telegraph,[^cite_note-7] and by 1888 the road between Adelaide and Alice Springs was well known.[^cite_note-8] Several wells along the route provided water, although these could run dry or be contaminated by dead animals, resulting in sections as long as 144 miles (232 km) without water.[^cite_note-9]

The route was traversed by motor vehicles in the 1920s. While passable, sections of the road could be sandy, boggy, washed away in the winter, or rugged with boulders. Several creek crossings were required, though few were difficult.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] North of Alice Springs the road was in comparatively good condition, with sections allowing speeds of up to 50 miles per hour (80 km/h).[^cite_note-12]

### Highway planning and construction

With the onset of [World War II](32927.md), supply roads leading to the north of the country were considered vital by the [federal government](1222540.md).[^cite_note-history_of_road_and_rail-13][^cite_note-year_book_1974-14] A central north–south highway was planned to connect the [railheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railhead) at Alice Springs and [Birdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdum,_Northern_Territory),[^cite_note-rush_job-15] with surveying completed in August 1940. The task of constructing the highway was split between the Main Roads Departments[^cite_note-Engineers_Show_The_World-16] of three states, to ensure completion before the next wet season. New South Wales would construct the northern section of 91 miles (146 km), Queensland the central section of 90 miles (145 km), and South Australia the southern section of 131 miles (211 km).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The Alice Springs–Birdum road was completed by December 1940,[^cite_note-19] upgraded from an often impassable track to an all-weather sealed highway that could cope with heavy military traffic.[^cite_note-history_of_road_and_rail-13][^cite_note-year_book_1974-14] The 306-mile (492 km) highway was built in under 90 days. In one week, 11 miles (18 km) was constructed, which was claimed to be a world record.[^cite_note-Engineers_Show_The_World-16] The new highway, in conjunction with the railways at either end, reduced the impact of Darwin's isolation. Quick and efficient movement of military equipment and troop was possible, with the road remaining open throughout the wet season.[^cite_note-20]

By March 1941, military authorities advocated extending the Alice Springs–Birdum road to Darwin. During the wet season, the road north of Birdum was impassable, which meant that a single railway line was the only connection through to Darwin.[^cite_note-21] Construction was underway by October 1941, once again at a fast pace in an attempt to finish before the next wet season.[^cite_note-22] The road was nearing completion in July 1942,[^cite_note-23] although some sections were yet to be bitumenised.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

However, the above section understates the fact that, in 1941, US Military "authorities" were horrified that there was not a "decent" all weather link between Darwin and Adelaide.

The U.S. military then worked alongside Australian forces and civilian groups to build and use the vital overland supply route between Alice Springs and Darwin (now known as the Stuart Highway). American and Australian engineering and transport units helped turn the rough track into a functional military supply line to move troops and equipment north after the Japanese attacks on Darwin, which were greater than those inflicted on Hawaii.

### Speed limits

Before 1 January 2007 there was no absolute speed limit in the Northern Territory, but maximum speed limits are now posted along the length of Stuart Highway. Previously drivers were required only to travel at a safe speed for the conditions, meaning the Northern Territory section of Stuart Highway had no speed limits at all.[^cite_note-26] The Northern Territory traffic laws were updated from 1 January 2007 to be similar to the rest of Australia. This included placing a speed limit on all roads (130 km/h or 81 mph on major highways such as Stuart Highway) and significantly increasing penalties for speeding.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

The South Australian section is signposted as 110 km/h (68 mph) outside built-up regions, between Port Augusta and the Northern Territory border.

In October 2013 the Northern Territory Government announced a trial period of reverting to an open speed limit on the 200 km (120 mi) stretch between Alice Springs and Barrow Creek, beginning 1 February 2014.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] In September 2015, following the conclusion of the trial, a 276 km (171 mi) stretch of the highway had its speed limits derestricted.[^cite_note-31] However, speed limits were restored to this stretch in November 2016; the highest is 130 km/h (81 mph).[^cite_note-32]

In March 2021, the road was closed due to a serious accident 500 km north of Adelaide at Wirraminna where the road collapsed due to burning diesel fuel that melted the road's plastic culverts. Plastic culverts had been used in place of concrete as they were seen as the best material for this particular location as the flood plain of the river is acidic and can destroy concrete culverts.

The road was closed while repair works took place. The asphalt also had to be given time to cure and the section of road underwent testing with a [road train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_train#Australia). With this now complete, both lanes are reopened with speed restrictions in place. Traffic signage is now in place and road users are asked to take extra care while travelling through this section of road.[^cite_note-33]

## Motor racing



Solar car "Tokai Challenger" during the 2009 World Solar Challenge

Motor races have been proposed or undertaken on the highway since the 1950s.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In 1994 the first and only Cannonball Run in Australia ran from Darwin to [Yulara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulara,_Northern_Territory) and back again. Based on [similar events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_Run_Challenge) in the United States, this event ended in tragedy when an out-of-control [Ferrari F40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_F40)[^cite_note-39] crashed into a checkpoint south of Alice Springs, resulting in the death of the two event officials manning the checkpoint as well as the two competitors.[^cite_note-40] The remainder of the race had a 180 km/h (112 mph) speed limit imposed to prevent further accidents.

Stuart Highway is the highway taken in the [World Solar Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Solar_Challenge). The 3,000 kilometres (1,900 mi) race starts in Darwin, follows Stuart Highway to Port Augusta, and then [Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_1_\(South_Australia\)) through to Adelaide.[^cite_note-41]

## Engineering heritage award

Stuart Highway North received a Historic Engineering Marker from [Engineers Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineers_Australia) as part of its [Engineering Heritage Recognition Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineers_Australia#Engineering_Heritage_Recognition_Program).[^cite_note-42]
