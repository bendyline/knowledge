Hong Kong is located on the southern coast of China. Its territories can be divided into [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island), the [Kowloon Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Peninsula), and the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories), as well as [263 outlying islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_and_peninsulas_of_Hong_Kong).[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-2] Geographically, the Outlying Islands, including [Lantau Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantau_Island), are sometimes regarded as a separate geographical region. Much of Hong Kong is mountainous, with limited natural lowlands confined mainly to coastal plains and reclaimed land. [Victoria Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Harbour), the deep natural harbour separating Hong Kong Island from Kowloon Peninsula, has been central to the territory's development as an international port and trading centre.

Since the 1960s, extensive land reclamation, high-rise construction, and the development of new towns have accommodated rapid urban growth while preserving large areas of countryside as country parks. Today, more than seven million people live within just over 1,100 km² of land, making Hong Kong one of the world's most densely populated territories.

## Physical geography

Hong Kong borders [Guangdong Province](65109.md) to the north, lies west of the [Pearl River estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_estuary) and [Macau](19068.md), and faces the [South China Sea](74209.md) to the south. The territory extends between approximately 22°08′ and 22°35′ north latitude and 113°49′ and 114°31′ east longitude. Hong Kong observes [Hong Kong Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Time) ([UTC+8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+08\:00)), eight hours ahead of [Coordinated Universal Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time). To the north, Hong Kong is separated from Shenzhen, Guangdong Province, by the [Shenzhen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_River) and [Sha Tau Kok River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha_Tau_Kok_River), while its southern waters adjoin the [Wanshan Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanshan_Archipelago) of Zhuhai. As of October 2025, Hong Kong has a land area of 1,114.57 km² and a total area, including inland and territorial waters, of 2,754.97 km².[^cite_note-\:0-1] Of the land area, approximately 78.42 km² has been created through [land reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Geology



Geological map of Hong Kong showing the distribution of faults and different rock types in Hong Kong. Modified from Civil Engineering Development Department, HKSAR.

[^cite_note-\:4-3]

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Geology of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Hong_Kong).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Geology_of_Hong_Kong\&action=edit)]

The [geology of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Hong_Kong) is dominated by [igneous rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock) (including [granitic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and [volcanic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_rock)) formed during a major volcanic eruption period in the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) era. It makes up 85% of Hong Kong's land surface, and the remaining 15% are mostly [sedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock) located in the northeastern [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories). There is also a very small percentage (<1%) of [metamorphic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock) within the New Territories, formed by deformation of pre-existing sedimentary rocks ([metamorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphism)).[^cite_note-Geology_of_Hong_Kong_\:02-4]

The geological history of Hong Kong started as early as the [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian) period (\~420 [Mya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_years_ago)) which is marked by the discovery of fragmentary [placoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placodermi) (a Devonian fish) fossils in northeast Hong Kong.[^cite_note-Geology_of_Hong_Kong_\:1-5][^cite_note-6] While the youngest rocks in Hong Kong were formed during the [Paleogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleogene) period(\~50 Mya). They are today exposed in [Tung Ping Chau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tung_Ping_Chau)[^cite_note-Geology_of_Hong_Kong_\:2-7][^cite_note-Geology_of_Hong_Kong_\:3-8] in northeastern Hong Kong.

Each of the three types of rocks— igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic— form spectacular geological features in Hong Kong. Igneous rock forms the hexagonal columns in [Sai Kung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Kung_Peninsula), sedimentary rock forms various erosional landforms such as [wave-cut platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave-cut_platform) and [sea stacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_\(geology\)) in Tung Ping Chau,[^cite_note-Geology_of_Hong_Kong_\:2-7] and metamorphic rock forms the iron ore deposits in [Ma On Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_On_Shan_\(town\)).

In terms of [structural geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_geology), [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) in Hong Kong mainly run from the northeast to the southwest. Deformation features such as sheared rocks, [folds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fold_\(geology\)), and faulted rocks can be found near major faults, such as the banks of the Tolo Channel. Past fault activity can be observed in some structures such as the Lantau [dyke swarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dike_swarm), deformed [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera), etc.[^cite_note-9]

## Climate

Hong Kong's climate is [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical) and [monsoonal](57630.md) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): Cwa), with cool dry winters and hot and wet summers. As of 2006, its annual average rainfall is 2,214 mm (87.2 in), though about 80% of the rain falls between May and September. It is occasionally affected by [tropical cyclones](8282374.md) between May and November, most often from July to September. The mean temperature of Hong Kong ranges from 17 °C (62.6 °F) in January and February to 29 °C (84.2 °F) in July and August.[^cite_note-10]

January and February are cloudier, with occasional [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) followed by dry northerly winds. It is not uncommon for temperatures to drop below 10 °C (50 °F) in urban areas. Sub-zero temperatures and [frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost) occur at times on high ground and in the New Territories. March and April can be pleasant although there are occasional spells of high humidity. [Fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fog) and [drizzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drizzle) are common on high ground which is exposed to the southeast. May to August are hot and humid with occasional [showers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shower_\(precipitation\)) and [thunderstorms](70807.md). Afternoon temperatures often exceed 31 °C (87.8 °F) whereas at night, temperatures generally remain around 26 °C (78.8 °F) with high humidity. In November and December there are pleasant breezes, plenty of sunshine and comfortable temperatures.[^cite_note-11]

The [Hong Kong Special Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Special_Administrative_Region) (HKSAR), can be divided into three geographical regions: [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island), [Kowloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon) (including the [New Kowloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kowloon)), and the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories) (including the [Outlying Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlying_Islands,_Hong_Kong)). The present-day boundaries were defined by the Memorandum of Understanding Concerning the Boundary of Administration between Hong Kong and Guangdong (粵港邊界管理範圍線諒解備忘錄), signed by the Guangdong and Hong Kong governments on 19 June 1997, and promulgated by [State Council](44498.md) Order No. 221.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In 1982, the government introduced the District Administration Scheme, dividing Hong Kong into 18 districts to facilitate the coordination and management of public services and facilities at the local level, while encouraging public participation in district affairs. ### Location

Hong Kong is on China's southern coast, 60 km (37 mi) east of [Macau](19068.md), on the east side of the mouth of the Pearl River estuary. It is surrounded by the [South China Sea](74209.md) on all sides except the north, which neighbours the Guangdong city of [Shenzhen](83368.md) along the [Sham Chun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_Chun_River). The territory's 2,755 km<sup>2</sup> (1,064 sq mi) area consists of Hong Kong Island, the [Kowloon Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Peninsula), the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories), Lantau Island, and over 200 other islands. Of the total area, 1,073 km<sup>2</sup> (414 sq mi) is land and 35 km<sup>2</sup> (14 sq mi) is water.[^cite_note-cia-19] The territory's highest point is [Tai Mo Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Mo_Shan), 957 metres (3,140 feet) above sea level.[^cite_note-20] Urban development is concentrated on the Kowloon Peninsula, Hong Kong Island, and in [new towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_towns_of_Hong_Kong) throughout the New Territories.[^cite_note-21] Much of this is built on [reclaimed land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation_in_Hong_Kong), due to the lack of developable flat land; 70 km<sup>2</sup> (27 sq mi) (six per cent of the total land or about 25 per cent of developed space in the territory) is reclaimed from the sea.[^cite_note-reclamation-22]

Undeveloped terrain is hilly to mountainous, with very little flat land, and consists mostly of grassland, woodland, shrubland, or farmland.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] About 40 per cent of the remaining land area is [country parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_in_Hong_Kong) and nature reserves.[^cite_note-25] The territory has a diverse ecosystem; over 3,000 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant) occur in the region (300 of which are native to Hong Kong), and thousands of insect, avian, and marine species.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### Islands

[Hong Kong](13404.md) has [263 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_of_Hong_Kong) over 500 m<sup>2</sup> (5,400 sq ft),[^cite_note-mapping-29] including [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island), [Lantau Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantau_Island), [Cheung Chau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheung_Chau), [Lamma Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamma_Island), [Peng Chau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peng_Chau) and [Tsing Yi Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsing_Yi).

### Terrain

Hong Kong's terrain is hilly and mountainous with steep slopes. There are lowlands in the northern part of Hong Kong. A significant amount of land in Hong Kong, especially on the Hong Kong Island and the Kowloon peninsula, is [reclaimed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation_in_Hong_Kong).

### Extreme points

The lowest elevation in Hong Kong is in the South China Sea (0 m) while the highest elevation is at [Tai Mo Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Mo_Shan) (957 m (3,140 ft)) in Tsuen Wan, the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories).

### Natural resources

The natural resources of Hong Kong can be divided into three main categories:

- Metalliferous minerals and non-metalliferous industrial minerals in the onshore area;

- Quarried rock and building stone;

- Offshore sand deposits.

Despite its small size, Hong Kong has a relatively large number of mineral occurrences. Some mineral deposits [have been exploited commercially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_Hong_Kong). Metalliferous mineral occurrences are grouped into four broad categories: [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin)-[tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten)-[molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum) mineralisation, [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper)-[lead](17747.md)-[zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) mineralisation, [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) mineralisation and placer deposits of [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) and [gold](12240.md). [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) igneous activity is largely responsible for this diversity of mineral deposits and the mineral concentrations have been variably enhanced by hydrothermal activity associated with faulting. Concentrations of non-metalliferous minerals that have been commercially exploited include [kaolin clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaolin_clay), [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar), [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [beryl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryl) and [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite).[^cite_note-rypjxf-30]

For many years, [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and [volcanic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_rocks) have been quarried locally for road base metal, [riprap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riprap), armour stone and asphalt, although the main purpose now is for concrete [aggregates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_aggregate). At present, there are three quarries operating in Hong Kong. These are principally in granite and are located at [Lam Tei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lam_Tei), [Shek O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shek_O) and [Anderson Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Road,_Hong_Kong). All the quarries are in the process of rehabilitation and have a life expectancy of between two and eight years.[^cite_note-rypjxf-30]

Offshore sand bodies have been dredged for aggregate sand and reclamation fill in Hong Kong as the rate of urban development has increased.[^cite_note-31]

Additional natural resources include forest and wildlife.
