The **Ministry of Finance** ([Abrv:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation) MOF; [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กระทรวงการคลัง, [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *_Krasuang Kan Khlang_*) is a [cabinet ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cabinet_Ministries_of_Thailand) in the [Government of Thailand](19068660.md).

Considered one of the country's most important ministries, the Ministry of Finance has numerous responsibilities over [public finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_finance), [taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation), the treasury, government properties, the operations of government monopolies, and revenue-generating enterprises. The ministry is also vested with the power to provide loan guarantees for governmental agencies, financial institutions, and state enterprises.[^cite_note-1]

## Management and budget

The head of the ministry is the Minister of Finance ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): รัฐมนตรีกระทรวงการคลัง). He is a member of the [Cabinet of Thailand](7366930.md) and is therefore appointed by the [King of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Thailand) on the advice of the [Prime Minister](273644.md). As of 2020, the Minister of Finance is Mr Apisak Tantivorawong.[^cite_note-2] The MOF permanent secretary is Prasong Poontaneat.[^cite_note-MoF-20180622-3]

The MOF was allocated 242,948 million [baht](89761.md) in the [FY2019](5752099.md) budget.[^cite_note-4]

## History

The ministry has existed in its current form since the 15th century during the [Ayutthaya Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom). Then, the ministry was called the "Kromma Khlang" ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กรมคลัง, Literally: Finance Department), finally upgraded to “Krom Phra Khlang” ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กรมพระคลัง, sometimes written as "Berguelang" or "Barcelon" by foreign authors). The "Phra Khlang" or minister responsible had wide-ranging powers, including those related to taxation, trade, monopolies, tributes, and even foreign affairs.

Most of these features were retained during the [Rattanakosin era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattanakosin_era). In 1855, King [Mongkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongkut) signed the [Bowring Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowring_Treaty) with the [United Kingdom](31717.md). The treaty exposed Siam to modern trade and international commerce; the king was compelled to set customs duty rates at no more than three percent; although the country was at a disadvantage, international trade grew, nonetheless. Soon, the king was forced to establish a [Customs House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Customs_Department) ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): ศุลกสถาน) and the [Royal Thai Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_Mint) to address new challenges.

During the reign of King [Chulalongkorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chulalongkorn) (Rama V), the ministry took its present form. The king issued a royal decree in 1875 consolidating all powers and agencies under one ministry with a more focused portfolio. He appointed one of his uncles, [Prince Maha Mala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Mala?action=edit\&redlink=1), as the first Minister of Finance. The ministry formally came into its own in 1933 with the [Civil Service Reform Act of 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Service_Reform_Act_of_1933?action=edit\&redlink=1). The Royal Treasury Ministry was subsequently renamed to the Ministry of Finance, which now comprises 10 departments and 14 state-owned enterprises.[^cite_note-5]

The MOF played a key role in the [COVID-19 pandemic in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Thailand) as it was responsible for disbursing aid to needy citizens.[^cite_note-6]

- The Revenue Department ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กรมสรรพากร): During the reign of King Rama V, the Revenue Department was organised as two separate departments: the External Revenue Department and the Internal Revenue Department. The External Revenue Department was responsible for collecting taxes and duties outside Bangkok and was under the Royal Treasury Ministry, which is now the Ministry of Finance. Due to personnel shortages, district and sub-district chief officers were assigned to collect taxes and duties, and the department was subsequently brought under the [Ministry of Interior](20545894.md). The Internal Revenue Department was responsible for the collection of taxes and duties within greater Bangkok (including areas in [Pathum Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathum_Thani), [Nonthaburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonthaburi_Province), and [Samut Prakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samut_Prakan) and was under the Ministry of Metropolis. This department was established following the advice of Mr. W. A. Graham, who was the comptroller of the Ministry of Metropolis at the time. The two departments were finally combined and named the Revenue Department. It was established on 2 September 1915. The Revenue Department collects, administers, and develops six types of taxes: personal income tax, corporate income tax, [value-added tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_added_tax), specific business tax, stamp duties, and petroleum income tax. These taxes combined account for more than 80 percent of total government revenue. The department operates 12 regional revenue offices, 119 area revenue offices, and 850 area revenue branch offices throughout the country, as well as 14 bureaus at its headquarters. The department collaborates with international organizations, including the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), the [World Bank](36752.md), and the [International Bureau of Fiscal Documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Fiscal_Documentation?action=edit\&redlink=1), to ensure international best practices for tax administration and policies.
