**Daraa Governorate** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مُحافظة درعا, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Muḥāfaẓat Darʿā*) is one of the fourteen [governorates](690220.md) (provinces) of [Syria](7515849.md). It is situated in the south-west of the country and covers an area of 2594 km<sup>2</sup>. It is bordered by [Jordan](7515964.md) to the south, [Quneitra Governorate](7445647.md) and [Golan Heights](7515865.md) to the west, [Rif Dimashq Governorate](4521946.md) to the north and [Suwayda Governorate](3096933.md) to the east. The capital is the city of [Daraa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa).&#x20;

The governorate had a population of 843,478 in the [2004 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Syrian_census).[^cite_note-1] [Several clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_Governorate_campaign) have occurred within the governorate throughout the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).

## History

### Civil War (2011–2024)

Main article: [Syrian civil war](30741795.md)

#### Protests and subsequent clashes (2011–2012)

Main article: [Siege of Daraa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Daraa)

Civilians in Daraa began protesting against the Assad government's authoritarian practices, which later prompted [similar protests across the country.](39750126.md)[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera_Mapping_Southern_Syria's_Armed_Opposition-3] The Syrian government responded by [besieging the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Daraa), which prompted some protesters to arm themselves.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera_Mapping_Southern_Syria's_Armed_Opposition-3][^cite_note-ISW_The_Free_Syrian_Army-4] The escalating tensions eventually lead to the creation of the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) on 29 July 2011 by defectors from the Syrian army.[^cite_note-5] Several smaller rebel groups were also created by protesters, which operated under the umbrella of the larger Free Syrian Army.[^cite_note-ISW_The_Free_Syrian_Army-4]

#### Daraa contested by Syrian government and rebels (2012–2018)

The Syrian government would begin an intense shelling of Daraa on 16 February 2012 in response to the Free Syrian Army aiding protesters.[^cite_note-6] The Syrian army would later attack parts of the city with anti-aircraft guns in response to the Free Syrian Army reportedly controlling one district of Daraa, bordering Jordan.[^cite_note-7] By February 2014, the strongest opposition faction fighting in the Daraa governorate would be the [Southern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Front_\(Syrian_rebel_group\)), which is a loose series of military alliances.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera_Mapping_Southern_Syria's_Armed_Opposition-3]

[Various offensives between 2013–2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_Governorate_campaign) would see the Syrian rebels gain territory until 25 June 2015, where the Syrian army would wage their first [successful offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_offensive_\(June%E2%80%93July_2015\)) against the rebels. This was shortly thereafter followed by a second [successful offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Al-Shaykh_Maskin_\(2015%E2%80%932016\)), capturing the city of [Ash-Shaykh Maskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash-Shaykh_Miskin). This would later be followed by a successful offensive by rebel forces, [recapturing parts of Daraa.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_offensive_\(February%E2%80%93June_2017\)) After several ceasefires and minor offensives, the Syrian army launched a [successful offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Southern_Syria_offensive) to retake most of the rebel-controlled territory in Daraa. This was followed by a [major insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_insurgency) between 23 November 2018 and 5 September 2021, with a minor insurgency following it. Due to the weakened state of rebel forces, several groups accepted reconciliation talks with the Syrian government.[^cite_note-Daraa_Reconciliation_Talks-8]

#### ISIL Presence in Southern Daraa (2016–present)

Main article: [Islamic State](9087364.md)

[An offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_offensive_\(March%E2%80%93April_2016\)) by two groups allegedly associated with the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (ISIS) would see a cell established around the settlement of Nahj, roughly 9 km away from the capital of Daraa.[^cite_note-ISIL_reaches_outskirts_of_Daraa-9] The Free Syrian Army would later recapture most of the lost territory, with ISIS-affiliated forces remaining in the Yarmouk Valley on the border with Jordan.[^cite_note-10] On 20 February 2017, ISIS launched a [successful offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Daraa_offensive_\(February_2017\)) to capture [Tasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasil). ISIS would remain there until the Syrian army launched [an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Southern_Syria_offensive) to retake territory from both ISIS and other rebels. As of today, ISIS presence in Daraa is weak, with it only executing sporadic attacks.[^cite_note-11]

#### Lesser fighting and sporadic reconciliation efforts (2018–2024)

After the offensive which put most of southern Syria back into the Syrian army's control, several rebel forces would accept reconciliation talks.[^cite_note-Daraa_Reconciliation_Talks-8] However, these reconciliation talks would restart several times due to clashes between rebel forces and the Syrian army. The [March 2020 Daraa clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2020_Daraa_clashes) resulted in a return to the status quo, with the [2021 Daraa offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Daraa_offensive) resulting in the Syrian government recapturing Daraa city.[^cite_note-12]

#### Rebel resurgence (2024)

Main article: [Southern Syria offensive (2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Syria_offensive_\(2024\))

The Southern Front launched an [offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Syria_offensive_\(2024\)) on 29 November 2024 to capture the entire Daraa governorate. On the 6 December 2024, the Southern Front announced a military alliance with various [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) rebel groups, creating the [Southern Operations Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Operations_Room).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] As of the 8 December 2024, the Southern Operations Room fully controlled Daraa Governorate.[^cite_note-15]

### Continued hostilities (2024-present)

#### Israeli invasion (2024-present)

Main article: [Israeli invasion of Syria (2024–present)](78549398.md)

On 8 December 2024, Israeli soldiers crossed into the [UN-buffer zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Disengagement_Observer_Force) in violation of the [1974 Agreement on Disengagement between Israel and Syria](40148472.md), invading the [Quneitra](7445647.md) and Daraa governorates. Israeli Army Radio cited a need to strengthen its border with Syria as justification for the invasion,[^cite_note-16] and claiming that the agreement reached under the Assad regime was no longer in affect.[^cite_note-17] On 23 February 2025, Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) demand the complete demilitarization of southern Syria in the provinces of Daraa, Quneitra and [Suweyda](3096933.md),[^cite_note-18] and the withdrawal of Syrian forces from Syrian territory south of Damascus.[^cite_note-19]

#### Clashes with Assad Loyalists (2025)

[Clashes broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2025_Daraa_clashes) on 4 March 2025 between the then-newly-appointed [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government) and [Assad loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_loyalist_insurgency) in the city of [As-Sanamayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Sanamayn).[^cite_note-20] Clashes had died down by 6 March with the victory of the caretaker government.[^cite_note-21]
