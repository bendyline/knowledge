A **baton charge** is a coordinated tactic for dispersing crowds of people, usually used by [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police), [paramilitary](146730.md) or [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) in [response to public disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_control). In the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), a long bamboo stick, called a *[lathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathi_\(stick\))* in [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) and [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), is used for crowd control, and the expression **lathi charge** is commonly employed to describe the action.

The tactic involves [police officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_officers) charging at a crowd of people with [batons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_\(law_enforcement\)) and in some cases [riot shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_shield). They run at the crowd hitting people with their batons, and in some situations use riot shields to push them away. Baton charging is designed to cause [pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_compliance) or fear of pain, in the hope that crowds would be compelled to move away from the scene, dispersing them.

## Indian subcontinent

In the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), notably [India](14533.md), [Nepal](171166.md), [Bangladesh](3454.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), and [Sri Lanka](26750.md), a long bamboo stick, or staff, called [lathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathi_\(stick\)), is used for crowd control. The term *lathi charge* is used by the [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_India) and [Pakistani](14937814.md) media.[^cite_note-1]
