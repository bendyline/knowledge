A **Marine Expeditionary Unit** (**MEU**, pronounced as one syllable "[*M'you*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)" IPA: [/mjuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is the smallest form of [Marine Air–Ground Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Air%E2%80%93Ground_Task_Force) (MAGTF) in the United States [Fleet Marine Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Marine_Force).[^cite_note-WiaMEU-1] Each MEU is an [expeditionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare) [rapid reaction force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_reaction_force) ready to answer any crisis, whether it be disaster aid or a combat mission.[^cite_note-WiaMEU-1] **Marine Amphibious Unit** (**MAU**) was the name used until the late 1980s.

A MEU normally is composed of

- a reinforced USMC [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) (designated as a Battalion Landing Team) as the [Ground Combat Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Combat_Element)

- a composite medium [tiltrotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiltrotor) [squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(aviation\)) forming the [Aviation Combat Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Combat_Element)

- a combat logistics battalion providing the [Logistics Combat Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics_Combat_Element)

- a company-size [Command Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_Element_\(United_States_Marine_Corps\)) serving as the MEU headquarters group.

Troop strength of a MEU is about 2,200 (normal and peacetime) to 4,400 (mobilization and wartime). A MEU is usually commanded by a [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_States\)), and is deployed from [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship). Currently, a MEU embarks personnel and equipment on the [amphibious warfare ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_ready_group) of an [expeditionary strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_strike_group) (ESG), which also includes escort warships and submarines to protect them from air, surface, and submarine threats. For further protection and strong air support, an ESG is often deployed along with one or more [carrier strike groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_strike_group).

## Attributes



Marines loading on a [Landing Craft Utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Craft_Utility) in 1999

The MEU is unique in that its air and ground combat elements are combined with a logistics combat element under one commander; other services do not unite the command of air and ground forces until much higher command levels.

The MEU's ground combat element also combines [artillery](2508.md), [light armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAV_25), and tanks at a much lower level than was common in the Army until the development of the [Brigade Combat Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_Combat_Team) early in the [war on terror](13425800.md), with a similar concept, the [combat command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_command), being utilized in [World War II](32927.md).

The [Marine Air–Ground Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Air%E2%80%93Ground_Task_Force) concept is designed to thoroughly exploit the combat power inherent in air and ground assets by closely integrating them into a single force. The MEU brings all the supplies and logistical support it needs to sustain itself for quick mission accomplishment or to prepare the way for follow-up forces. This self-sustainment allows more flexibility in disposition and operations of forces, and allows the MEU to initiate operations sooner and let support catch up later, without having to wait for external logistical support to begin a mission. Deployments on [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_amphibious_warfare_ships) allows MEUs to [seabase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabasing) around the globe, ready for deployment at short notice.

## Elements

### Ground combat element

Marines from the [22nd Marine Expeditionary Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Marine_Expeditionary_Unit) load a forklift onto a [Medium Tactical Vehicle Replacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_Tactical_Vehicle_Replacement)

The **[Ground Combat Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_combat_element)** (**GCE**) is based on the *Battalion Landing Team (BLT)*, an infantry battalion reinforced with an [artillery battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_battery), [amphibious assault vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_vehicle) platoon, [combat engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_engineering) platoon, [light armored reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Light_Armored_Reconnaissance) company, [reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Reconnaissance_Battalions) platoon, and other units as the mission and circumstances require. The total strength is approximately 1,100 members, including Navy sailors.

The [Maritime Special Purpose Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Special_Purpose_Force) is a subgroup of the MEU, formed for low-profile missions. The MSPF force consists of four elements: an [assault platoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Special_Purpose_Force#Assault_Element_\(AE\)) (a [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action_\(military\)) platoon augmented from [Force Recon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps_Force_Reconnaissance)), a [security platoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Special_Purpose_Force#Security_Element) (a selected infantry platoon from the battalion landing team), [reconnaissance and surveillance assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Special_Purpose_Force#Support_Element), and a [headquarters section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Special_Purpose_Force#Command_Element). The total strength is approximately 350 members, including Navy sailors.

### Aviation combat element

The **[Aviation Combat Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_combat_element)** (**ACE**) is a USMC composite squadron (reinforced) composed of a [medium tiltrotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_United_States_Marine_Corps_aircraft_squadrons#Marine_Medium_Tiltrotor_Squadrons) squadron augmented with detachments of heavy, light, and attack helicopters, one detachment of amphibious flight-deck-[capable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTOL) jets, and a [Marine air control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Marine_Corps_aviation_support_units) group detachment with tactical air command, [air traffic control](48563.md), [direct air support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Air_Support_Center), and [anti-aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft) assets, as well as wing headquarters, wing communications, and wing support squadron personnel. Total strength is approximately 600 troops.

### Logistics combat element

The **[Logistics Combat Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics_Combat_Element)** (**LCE**) (formerly *Combat Service Support Element* or *CSSE*) is based on the MEU combat logistics battalion (CLB) (formerly *MEU service support group* or *MSSG*). It contains all the [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) specialists and equipment necessary for the MEU to support and sustain itself for up to 15 days in an austere expeditionary environment. It includes service support (postal and disbursing), [medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_Corpsman), [dental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentistry), intermediate maintenance, intermediate supply (consumables and secondary reparable), transportation (distribution and landing support), [explosive ordnance disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_ordnance_disposal), utilities production and distribution, bulk fuels, internal communications, and various other technical experts. It consists of approximately 300 members, including Navy sailors.

### Command element

The **[Command Element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_element_\(United_States_Marine_Corps\))** (**CE**), which includes the MEU commander and his [supporting staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_\(military\)), provides [command and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_Control_\(Military\)) over the other three elements. It includes specialized detachments for [air naval gunfire liaison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Naval_Gunfire_Liaison_Company), [reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Recon), [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance), specialized communications, [radio reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Marine_Corps_battalions#Radio_battalions) ([SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence)), [electronic warfare](82272.md), [Marine Corps Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Intelligence) and [counterintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterintelligence), [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police), and [public affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_affairs_\(military\)) missions. The overall strength is about 200 members, including Navy sailors.

## Expeditionary strike group

Recently, MEUs have been deployed within an [expeditionary strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_strike_group) (ESG) in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean), the Western [Pacific](23070.md), and periodically, the [Atlantic](698.md) and [Indian Oceans](14580.md). An ESG is typically composed of three [amphibious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) ships that embark the necessary troops and equipment and are escorted by a [guided missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile) [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) (CG) and guided missile [destroyers](8779.md) (DDG) and [submarine](28825.md) ([SSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN_\(U.S._Navy\))) support.

Before the ESG, MEUs were typically deployed as part of an [amphibious ready group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_ready_group) (ARG).

## The MEU Cycle



Marines with the [31st Marine Expeditionary Unit](5885654.md) train from the amphibious transport dock ship [USS *Green Bay* (LPD-20)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Green_Bay_\(LPD-20\)) in the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea) in 2022

For detailed information on special operations certification, see [Marine expeditionary unit (special operations capable)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_expeditionary_unit_\(special_operations_capable\)).

MEUs maintain their subordinate elements in fifteen month cycles: nine months stateside (with six set aside for training), and a six-month deployment aboard ship. These cycles ensure that at least two of the seven MEUs are deployed forward at any given time.

**Interim or buildup period**: Upon completion of a deployment, the MEU remains "special operations capable" for approximately one month, prepared to respond to events around the world. They are not, however, considered a special operations unit by the Department of Defense. The MEU then releases its major subordinate elements (MSEs), retaining only its command element. This period provides the command element a chance to rotate select personnel and begin planning for the addition of newly assigned MSEs and “work-up” training. When the MSEs are received, the MEU begins six months of intense pre-deployment training.

**Work-up period**: Training during the six-month work-up period is often referred to as "crawl, walk, run". Marines and sailors progress through curriculum and exercises that teach individual, small unit, and unit tactics while integrating the separate MEU elements into a cohesive, flexible, and powerful force. The work-up period includes training in many combat and [noncombat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_operations_other_than_war) skills, to include:

- urban sniper

- mechanized and motorized [raids](6873191.md)

- [non-combatant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-combatant) evacuation operations

- [humanitarian assistance](434679.md)

- [mass casualty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triage)

- [scout swimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogman)

- [jungle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_warfare) and/or [mountain warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_warfare)

- [riot control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_control)

Exercises conducted during the work-up period can include:

- Amphibious squadron—MEU integration training (PMINT)

- Realistic urban training exercise (RUT), formerly training in an urban environment exercise (TRUEX)

- Expeditionary strike group exercise (ESGEX)

- Special operations capable certification exercise (CERTEX or SOCCEX); prior to deployment, the MEU receives certification as special operations capable and then referred to as a "MEU(SOC)".



Marines with the 31st MEU embark [USS *Tripoli*](36035298.md) in Okinawa, Japan, November 30, 2025

**Deployment**: Following the work-up period, the MEU deploys for six months in support of geographic [combatant commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command). During this time, the MEU is a forward-deployed, self-sustaining force that combatant commanders can direct to accomplish a variety of special operations and conventional missions.

The missions may include:

- [Conventional operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_warfare) ([amphibious assaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) and raids)

- Tactical recovery of aircraft and personnel (TRAP)

- Humanitarian assistance operations (HAO)

- Noncombatant evacuation operations (NEO)

- Security operations.
