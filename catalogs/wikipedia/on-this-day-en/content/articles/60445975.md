# West London

This article is about the western part of London, England. For the West End district, see [West End of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_of_London). For other uses, see [West London (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_London_\(disambiguation\)).

&#x20;**West London** is the western part of London, England, north of the [River Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames), west of the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), and extending to the [Greater London](45367389.md) boundary.

The term is used to differentiate the area from the other parts of London: [Central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London), [North London](380308.md), [East London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London) and [South London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_London).[^cite_note-1] West London was part of the [historic county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) of [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex).

## Emergence

Early West London had two main focuses of growth, the area around [Thorney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorney_Island_\(Westminster\)), site of [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey) and the [Palace of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster), and [ribbon development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_development) heading west - towards Westminster - from gates in the [walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Wall) of the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London). In the 17th century these areas of growth would be linked by high status new developments, which formed a focal point in their own right, later becoming known as the [West End of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_of_London).

### Initial growth



The consecration of Westminster Abbey stimulated population growth west of the City of London.

The development of the area began with the establishment of the [Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey) on a site then called [Thorney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorney_Island_\(Westminster\)), the choice of site may in part relate to the [natural ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watling_Street#Westminster_ford) which is thought to have carried [Watling Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watling_Street) over the [River Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames) in the vicinity.[^cite_note-2] Tradition dates the foundation to the 7th century AD with written records dating back to the 960s or early 970s.[^cite_note-wpage-3] The Island and surrounding area became known as Westminster in reference to the church.

The legendary origin[^cite_note-4] is that in the early 7th century, a local fisherman named Edric ferried a stranger in tattered foreign clothing over the River Thames to [Thorney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorney_Island_\(London\)). It was a miraculous appearance of [St Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter), a fisherman himself, coming to the island to [consecrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecration) the newly built church, which would subsequently develop into Westminster Abbey. He rewarded Edric with a bountiful catch when he next dropped his nets. Edric was instructed to present the [King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A6berht_of_Essex) and [St. Mellitus, Bishop of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellitus) with a salmon and various proofs that the consecration had already occurred. Every year on 29 June, St Peters day, the [Worshipful Company of Fishmongers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Fishmongers) presents the Abbey with a salmon in memory of this event.[^cite_note-5]

The Palace of Westminster subsequently developed, with Parliament being based there from its establishment in 1265. The presence of the centre of government as a distinct focus for growth, accompanied by the proximity of the city, ensured that western London was the fastest growing part of early London.

### First West End

The growth of the City of London beyond its city walls was much faster outside the western gates of [Ludgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludgate) and [Newgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newgate) than it was outside the gates to the north or to the east; this rapid growth was due to the roads from these western gates leading to the political centre of Westminster. The large and prosperous extramural ward of [Farringdon Without](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farringdon_Without), extensively urbanised during the 12th century, has been described as London's *First West End*.[^cite_note-6]

From the 15th to 17th centuries, growth along the roads from Ludgate ([Fleet Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Street) and [The Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strand,_London)) and Newgate (*Holborn* and *[High Holborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Holborn)*) accelerated, and came to extend far beyond Farringdon Without, into [Holborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holborn), [Bloomsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomsbury) and [Westminster](37432.md).

### New West End

Urban growth extending from the Westminster urban area, linked up with that extending from the City in the time of [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII). It was at around that time that Westminster first acquired City status.[^cite_note-7]

In the mid 17th century [Henry Jermyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Jermyn,_1st_Earl_of_Saint_Albans), was instrumental in developing the [St James's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James's) and [Mayfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfair) districts of Westminster. These districts provided a fashionable new focus for western London, that came to be known as the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_of_London). Jermyn would become known as the *Father of the West End*.[^cite_note-8]

In 1720, [John Strype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Strype)'s "Survey of London" described Westminster as one of the then four distinct areas of London;[^cite_note-9] in it he describes the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), [Westminster (West London)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_Liberty_of_Westminster), [Southwark (South London)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwark), and ['That Part beyond the Tower' (East London)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London). The area now usually referred to as [North London](380308.md) developed later.

A detailed copy of [John Rocque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rocque)'s Map of London, 1741-5

As well as the proximity of the centre of government, the West End was long favoured by the rich elite as a place of residence because it was usually upwind of the smoke drifting from the crowded City.[^cite_note-10] A further factor facilitating rapid growth in West London was the very large number of bridges linking the area to South London and the area beyond; by contrast, even today, there are no bridges east of [Tower Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_Bridge), partly as the river becomes wider as it heads east.

The term "West End of London" gained widespread currency as a proper noun, rather than just a geographical description in the 19th century.

### Rapid growth

Like other areas of the capital, West London grew rapidly in the Victorian era as a result of railway-based commuting; with the building of the termini at [Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Paddington_station) and [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone_station), and the lines radiating from them, having a particularly profound effect. This trend continued in the twentieth century and was subsequently reinforced by motorcar-based commuting.

The size of London stabilised after the establishment of the [Metropolitan Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Green_Belt) shortly after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

## Geography

### Topography



The [Grand Union Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Union_Canal) in [Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayes,_Hillingdon), Hillingdon

West London is an informally and inexactly defined area lying north of the [River Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames) and extending west from the edge of the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), to West London's historic and commercial core of [Westminster](37432.md) and the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_of_London) (areas also regarded as part of [Central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London)) and on to the [Greater London](45367389.md) boundary, much of which is formed by the [River Colne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Colne,_Hertfordshire). Some interpretations of the area include the boroughs of [Brent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Brent) and [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Harrow), taking [ancient Watling Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watling_Street) as the boundary in those [outer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_London) areas.[^cite_note-Places-11] The [Grand Union Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Union_Canal) is West London's major internal waterway.

West London is bordered by the administrative counties of [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey) to the south west and south; [Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire) to the west and north west; [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire) to the north west; and [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) to the north.

A publication by the [Mayor of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_London) in 2011 referred to the London boroughs of [Brent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Brent), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Harrow), [Ealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Ealing), [Hammersmith and Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hammersmith_and_Fulham), [Hounslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hounslow), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Richmond_upon_Thames), and [Hillingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hillingdon) as West London. The London Borough of Richmond upon Thames spans the River Thames so its status can be ambiguous.

The term West London is used to differentiate the area from other informal radial divisions of London, the *Metropolitan Compass*;[^cite_note-12] [North London](380308.md), [East London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London) and [South London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_London).[^cite_note-13]

### Official designations



Sub-regions used in the London Plan. The West sub-region covers parts of West London.

The term "West London" has been used for a variety of formal purposes with the boundaries defined according to the purposes of the designation.

#### Planning Policy sub-region

The 2011 iteration of the [London Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Plan)[^cite_note-Places-11] included an altered "West" sub-region, to be used for planning, engagement, resource allocation and progress reporting purposes. It consists of the [London Boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough) of [Brent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Brent), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Harrow), [Ealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Ealing), [Hammersmith and Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hammersmith_and_Fulham), [Hillingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hillingdon), [Hounslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hounslow) and [Richmond upon Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Richmond_upon_Thames). As well as including outer areas of West London, the sub-region also includes areas south of the river, not usually counted as part of West London; areas of the cross-river [London Borough of Richmond upon Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Richmond_upon_Thames).

The [2004-2008 and 2008-2011 versions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sub-regions_used_in_the_London_Plan) of the sub-region varied in their composition.

#### W postcode area



Map of postcodes in Greater London. The "Western" district is a sub-set of West London.

The W (Western) postcode area was introduced in 1857[^cite_note-14] to facilitate the distribution of mail. The postcode area is a sub-set of West London.

## Economy

### London Plan

The [London Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Plan)[^cite_note-15] defines two areas of London as International Centres, the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_of_London) and [Knightsbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knightsbridge), both in west London. Five of the thirteen Metropolitan Centres in the plan are also in West London: [Ealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ealing), [Hounslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hounslow), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow,_London), [Uxbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uxbridge) and [Shepherd's Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd's_Bush).

Eleven of the London Plan's thirty-eight Opportunity Areas are part of West London; [Kensal Canalside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensal_Green), [Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington), [Earl's Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl's_Court) and [West Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Kensington), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow,_London) and [Wealdstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealdstone), [Park Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Royal), [Old Oak Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Oak_Common), [Southall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southall), [Tottenham Court Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Court_Road), Victoria, [Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley) and [White City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City,_London).

### Major employers

[London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport) is a major employer in West London, and the [University of West London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_West_London) has more than 47,000 students.[^cite_note-UWL-16]
