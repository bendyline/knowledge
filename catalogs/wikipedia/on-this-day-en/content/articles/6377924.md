**Domokos** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Δομοκός), the ancient **Thaumacus** or **Thaumace** (Θαυμακός, Θαυμάκη),[^cite_note-2] is a town and a municipality in [Phthiotis](461105.md), [Greece](12108.md). The town Domokos is the seat of the municipality of Domokos[^cite_note-Kallikratis-3] and of the former [Domokos Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domokos_Province). The town is built on a mountain slope overlooking the plain of [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly), 38 km from the city of [Lamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamia_\(city\)).

## History

### Ancient

Main article: [Thaumaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaumaci)

### Ottoman Era

In 1521 ([Hijri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijri_year) 927) the town, known in Ottoman Turkish as *Dömeke*, had six Muslim and 311 Christian households in nine neighborhoods.[^cite_note-4] The castle of the town is mentioned as *Dimoko* in the [Seyahatnâme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyahatn%C3%A2me) of [Evliya Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_%C3%87elebi), which he visited in 1668. He mentions that there were around a hundred tiled houses, with Muslims constituting only one neighborhood and having a mosque, and that the inner castle was inhabited by the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum_Millet) since the beginning of the Ottoman conquest. He points out that the [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Muslims) are mixed with Christians and are unaware of their denomination, and that they are relieved of paying the [haraç](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hara%C3%A7) tax. He also mentioned the [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_Greece) of the town, saying that "they do not allow Jews in this city, and the Jews do not come out of fear".[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

### Modern

The area of Domokos became part of Greece in 1881 when the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) ceded [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly) and a few adjacent areas to Greece. Until 1899, it was part of the [Larissa Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larissa_Prefecture).

### Battle of Domokos

In 1897, during the [Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_\(1897\)), about 2,000 [Italian](14532.md) volunteers under the command of [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi)'s son, [Ricciotti Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricciotti_Garibaldi), helped the Greeks in the [Battle of Domokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Domokos). Among them there was also a member of the Italian Parliament, [Antonio Fratti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Fratti?action=edit\&redlink=1), who died in the fighting. The Turkish Army was victorious over the Greek Army.

## Transport

The town is served by [Domokos railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domokos_railway_station) on the [Piraeus–Platy Mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piraeus%E2%80%93Platy_railway), which is located 5 km from the city and serves the surrounding area.

You can also visit the town via frequent buses that pass by the town.

## Municipality

The municipality Domokos was formed during the 2011 local government reforms by the merger of the following 3 former municipalities, that became municipal units:[^cite_note-Kallikratis-3]

- Domokos

- [Thessaliotida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaliotida)

- [Xyniada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xyniada)

The municipality has an area of 707.953 km<sup>2</sup>, the municipal unit 346.129 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-stat01-7]

## Province

The province of Domokos ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Επαρχία Δομοκού) was one of the [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Greece) of Phthiotis. It had the same territory as the present municipality.[^cite_note-census91-8] It was abolished in 2006.
