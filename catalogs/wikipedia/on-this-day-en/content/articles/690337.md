[Ethiopia](187749.md) is a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) subdivided into **ethno-linguistically based regional states** ([Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic): plural: ክልሎች *kililoch*; singular: ክልል *kilil*; [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_language): singular: *Naannoo*; plural: *Naannolee*) and **chartered cities** (Amharic: plural: አስተዳደር አካባቢዎች *astedader akababiwoch*; singular: አስተዳደር አካባቢ *astedader akabibi*). This system of administrative regions replaced the [provinces of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Ethiopia) in 1992.[^cite_note-mula-1]

There are twelve regional states and two chartered cities ([Addis Ababa](52593.md) and [Dire Dawa](1242250.md)). Being based on ethnicity and language, rather than physical geography or history, the regions vary enormously in area and population; the most notable example is the [Harari Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harari_Region), which has a smaller area and population than either of the chartered cities.

## Governance

The regions are each governed by a regional council whose members are directly elected to represent [woredas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Ethiopia) (districts). Each council has a president, who is elected by the council. Each region also has an executive committee, whose members are selected by the president from among the councilors and approved by the council. Each region has a sector bureau, which implements the council mandate and reports to the executive committee.[^cite_note-2]

## History

Ethiopia was historically divided into [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Ethiopia). The current system of administrative regions was introduced in 1992 by the [Transitional Government of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Government_of_Ethiopia), and was formalised in 1995 when the current [Constitution of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Constitution_of_Ethiopia) came into force.[^cite_note-mula-1]

There were 13 regions initially, but five regions were merged to form the multi-ethnic [Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nations,_Nationalities,_and_Peoples'_Region) (SNNPR) later in 1992, following the first elections of regional councils on 21 June 1992.[^cite_note-3] [Addis Ababa](52593.md), the country's capital, and [Dire Dawa](1242250.md) became chartered cities in 2004.

### New regions



The current regions and chartered cities of Ethiopia, their respective flags, their capitals, and their largest cities

During the premiership of [Abiy Ahmed](56624029.md), 3 new regions have been created.

In November 2019, a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sidama_Region_referendum) was held in the [Sidama Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidama_Zone) of the SNNPR, in which voters supported a proposal for Sidama Zone to become a region in its own right.[^cite_note-4] The Sidama Region was created in June 2020.[^cite_note-5]

The [South West Ethiopia Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Ethiopia_Region) was created on 23 November 2021 following a [successful referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_West_Region_referendum) earlier that year. The new region was split off from the SNNPR and consisted of [Keffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keffa_Zone), [Sheka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheka_Zone), [Bench Sheko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_Maji_Zone), [Dawro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawro_Zone), and [West Omo](69384638.md) [Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_zones_of_Ethiopia), along with [Konta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konta_special_woreda) special district.[^cite_note-6]

On 19 August 2023 the [South Ethiopia Regional State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ethiopia_Regional_State) was created following the [2023 South Ethiopia Region referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_South_Ethiopia_Region_referendum), with the remainder becoming the [Central Ethiopia Regional State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ethiopia_Regional_State), thus dissolving the SNNPR.[^cite_note-7]
