The **Family Code of the Philippines** codifies [family law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_law) in the [Philippines](23440.md).

## History

In 1987, [President](271126.md) [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) enacted into law The Family Code of 1987, which was intended to supplant Book I of the [Civil Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Code_of_the_Philippines) concerning persons and family relations. Work on the Family Code had begun as early as 1979, and it had been drafted by two successive committees, the first chaired by future Supreme Court Justice [Ruth Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flerida_Ruth_Pineda-Romero), and the second chaired by former [Supreme Court](1335534.md) Justice [J.B.L. Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._L._Reyes). The Civil Code needed amendment via the Family Code in order to alter certain provisions derived from foreign sources which had proven unsuitable to [Filipino culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Philippines) and to attune it to contemporary developments and trends.[^cite_note-1]

## Content

The Family Code covers fields of significant public interest, especially the laws on [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage). The definition and requisites for marriage, along with the grounds for [annulment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annulment), are found in the Family Code, as is the law on conjugal property relations, rules on establishing [filiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paternity_\(law\)), and the governing provisions on [support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_support), [parental authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_authority), and [adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption).
