The **2030 Winter Paralympics** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Jeux paralympiques d'hiver de 2030*), also known as the **Alpes 2030 Paralympic Winter Games**, will be the 15th edition of the [Winter Paralympic Games](18670284.md), an international [winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_sports) [multi-sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) [parasports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasports) event governed by the [International Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Paralympic_Committee). The Games are scheduled to take place from 1 to 10 March 2030 in the [French Alps](961317.md).[^cite_note-1] These will be the second Winter Paralympics, and the third Paralympics overall, to be hosted by France. Events will be held in the department of [Alpes-Maritimes](80989.md) on its [Hautes-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hautes-Alpes), in [Lyon](8638634.md) and in the departments of [Haute-Savoie](90605.md) and [Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoie) in [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](45093325.md).

## Bidding process

Further information: [Bids for the 2030 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2030_Winter_Olympics)

Under the new bidding process established under Olympic Agenda 2020, the Future Host Commission of the IOC engages in ongoing, non-committal "continuous dialogue" with parties that are interested in hosting a future Olympic and Paralympic Games. The Commission then recommends preferred candidates to be invited to "targeted dialogue" with the IOC Executive Board. In addition, a "host" can now consist of multiple regions or countries rather than just cities.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

### Host selection

On 29 November 2023, per the recommendation of the Future Host Commission, the IOC Executive Board invited the [French Paralympic and Sports Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Paralympic_and_Sports_Committee) to targeted dialogue proposing that the 2030 Winter Paralympics be held in the French Alps.

The French Alps were conditionally ratified as hosts during the [142nd IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/142nd_IOC_Session) on 24 July 2024 in [Paris](22989.md), France; as per the new bid process, the vote was in the form of a [referendum](51582.md) to the 95 IOC delegates. The decision to award the Games was made under the condition that financial guarantees from federal and state governments be secured by October 2024; usually, these are secured as part of the bid process, but this was hindered by the [French political crisis](77741666.md) and [2024 French legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_legislative_election).

In October 2024, then-prime minister [Michel Barnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Barnier) sent a letter to IOC president [Thomas Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bach) to confirm the French government's financial support.

## Marketing

### Emblem

The emblem of the Games was unveiled on 18 June 2026 during an event at Briançon.[^cite_note-5] The emblem is a stylized mountain peak with a blue, purple, and red gradient, formed by rays of light; it is stated to represent the "promise of clarity, warmth and renewal" and the "convergence" of nations at its summit. The Paralympic emblem uses a similar design, but with the mountain formed in [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space) by lights shining behind it. Alongside the emblem, the branding of the Games was also shortened from "French Alps 2030"/"Alpes Françaises 2030" to simply "Alpes 2030".[^cite_note-6] The emblems were designed by Paris based design agency Saint-Lazare.[^cite_note-7]
