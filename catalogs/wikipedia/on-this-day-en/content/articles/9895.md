[Afghanistan](737.md) has a [developing economy](78449.md) that is considered [low income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty) and among the [world's least developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries). With [a population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan) of around 43 million people, the nation generates \$19.66 billion in nominal [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) and \$101 billion by [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) (PPP) GDP, as of 2026. The official [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency) of Afghanistan is the [afghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_afghani) (AFN).[^cite_note-Afghani_reverses_to_7-year_old_value_against_US_dollar-16] Its [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) – known as [Da Afghanistan Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Afghanistan_Bank) – oversees monetary policy. A burgeoning banking system operates within the country, including the [Afghanistan International Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_International_Bank), [Azizi Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azizi_Bank), [New Kabul Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kabul_Bank), and [Pashtany Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtany_Bank). The nation exports around \$2 billion, with [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural), [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) and [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry) products accounting for 94% of total exports.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-TOLO-182736-11] It's [external debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_debt) has been around \$1.4 billion since the early 2020s.[^cite_note-TOLOnews-179626-13][^cite_note-gross_external_debt-14]

The Afghan economy continues to improve with strengthening [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route), infrastructure development and the expansion of the strategically important sectors of agriculture, [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan), [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Afghanistan) and [mining](17967521.md).[^cite_note-World_Bank:_Afghanistan's_economy_is_growing_for_the_second_consecutive_year-18][^cite_note-19] Economic development and political stability is supported by a large and [remittance-rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance)[^cite_note-20] [Afghan diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_diaspora).[^cite_note-Chinese_firm_offers_IEA_\$10_billion_investment_in_lithium_reserves-21][^cite_note-22] The country holds mineral deposits worth over \$2.5 trillion, with vast amounts of copper, rare earths and minerals. Around 48% of its population lives below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line).[^cite_note-OCHA-6][^cite_note-23] The population of Afghanistan increased by more than 50% between 2001 and 2014, while its GDP grew eightfold.[^cite_note-24] After the [U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Afghanistan) in 2021, the U.S. government confiscated \$9.5 billion worth of Afghanistan's assets to stop the [Taliban](30635.md) from accessing it.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Economic history

Further information: [Democratic Republic of Afghanistan § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan#Economy), and [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan (1996–2001) § Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)#Economy)

When [Afghanistan](737.md) was ruled by [Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) [Abdur Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Khan) (1880–1901) and his son [Habibullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habibullah_Khan) (1901–1919), a great deal of commerce was controlled by the government. These monarchs were eager to develop the stature of government and the country's military capability, and so attempted to raise money by the imposition of state monopolies on the sale of commodities and high taxes. This slowed the long-term development of Afghanistan during that period. Western technologies and manufacturing methods were introduced at the command of the Afghan ruler, but in general only according to the logistical requirements of the growing army. An emphasis was placed on the manufacture of weapons and other military material. This process was in the hands of a small number of foreign experts invited to Kabul by the Afghans.[^cite_note-Helmand's_Golden_Age-28] Otherwise, it was not possible for non-Afghans, particularly westerners, to set up large-scale enterprises in Afghanistan during that period.[^cite_note-29]

In the post-independence period, Da Afghanistan Bank strongly financed the cultivation of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton); at one point, the Spinzar Cotton Company in [Kunduz Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz_Province) was one of the largest providers of cotton in the world, most of which were exported to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Fruits were mainly exported to British-controlled India (now Pakistan and India).[^cite_note-30]

&#x20;The first prominent plan to develop Afghanistan's economy in modern times was the [Helmand Valley Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand_Valley_Authority) project of 1952, modeled on the [Tennessee Valley Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Valley_Authority) in the [United States](3434750.md), which was expected to be of primary economic importance.[^cite_note-tudor-31] Glenn Foster, an American contractor working in Afghanistan in the 1950s, stated this about the Afghan people:

> Even though there are masses of people, the country seems able to feed them all. Although their diet may not be abundant, you don't see the hunger that you do in some countries....[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Helmand's_Golden_Age-28]

The same can be said today.[^cite_note-33] Afghanistan began facing severe economic hardships after the 1979 [Soviet invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), which caused the permanent closure of all its [border checkpoints](288329.md) with neighboring Iran and Pakistan. The [civil war of the 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)) destroyed much of the country's limited infrastructure, and disrupted normal patterns of economic activity. Eventually, Afghanistan went from a [traditional economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_economy) to a [centrally planned economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrally_planned_economy) up until 2002 when it was replaced by a [free market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market_economy) following the [2001 war in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_war_in_Afghanistan).[^cite_note-34] Gross domestic product has fallen substantially since the 1980s due to disruption of trade and transport as well as loss of labor and capital. Continuing internal strife severely hampered domestic efforts to rebuild the nation or provide ways for the international community to help.

According to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), the Afghan economy grew 20% in the [fiscal year](5752099.md) ending in March 2004, after expanding 30% in the previous 12 months. The growth was mainly attributed to United Nations assistance. Billions of dollars in international aid and remittances from expats had entered Afghanistan from 2002 to 2021. A GDP of \$4 billion in fiscal year 2003 was recalculated by the IMF to \$6.1 billion, after adding proceeds from [opium production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_production_in_Afghanistan). Mean graduate pay was \$0.56 per [man-hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-hour) in 2010. The country is still striving to be self-sufficient in [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [poultry](23197.md) and [dairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy) production.[^cite_note-35]

The recent reestablishment of the Islamic Emirate led to temporary suspension of international development aid to Afghanistan.[^cite_note-36] The [World Bank](36752.md) and [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) also halted payments during that period.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] In this regard, [Hibatullah Akhundzada](50633776.md) stated that "the economy of a country is built when its people work together and do not rely on foreign aid"[^cite_note-39] The Biden administration froze about \$9 billion in assets belonging to the Da Afghanistan Bank, which was intended to block the Islamic Emirate from accessing the money.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] The recent [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought_in_Afghanistan), [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Afghanistan) and [floods](34961117.md) in the country have created further adverse economic situation for many residents.[^cite_note-42] The [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Afghanistan\)) has collected over \$2 billion in 2022.[^cite_note-Over_193.9_billion_afs_revenue_collected_last_year:_MoF-43]

The GDP of Afghanistan is estimated to have dropped by 20% following the Islamic Emirate's return to power. Following this, after months of free-fall, the Afghan economy began stabilizing, as a result of restrictions on smuggled imports, limits on banking transactions, and UN aid. In 2023, the Afghan economy began seeing signs of revival. This has also been followed by stable exchange rates, low inflation, stable revenue collection, and the rise of trade in exports.[^cite_note-44] In the third quarter of 2023, the [Afghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_afghani) rose to be the best performing currency in the world, climbing over 9% against the [US dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_dollar).[^cite_note-45]

## Economic development, recovery and downturn

Further information: [Reconstruction in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_in_Afghanistan) and [Foreign aid to Afghanistan](78868098.md)



From left to right: Indian Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md), Iranian President [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani), and Afghan President [Ashraf Ghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf_Ghani), during the signing of the [Chabahar Port](15313649.md) transit agreement in May 2016

Afghanistan embarked on a modest economic development program in the 1930s. The government founded banks; introduced paper money; established a university; expanded primary, secondary, and technical schools; and sent students abroad for education. In 1952 it created the [Helmand Valley Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand_Valley_Authority) to manage the economic development of the Helmand and Arghandab valleys through irrigation and land development,[^cite_note-tudor-31] a scheme which remains one of the country's most important capital resources.[^cite_note-46]

In 1956, the government promulgated the first in a long series of ambitious development plans.[^cite_note-Helmand's_Golden_Age-28] By the late 1970s, these had achieved only mixed results due to flaws in the planning process as well as inadequate funding and a shortage of the skilled managers and technicians needed for implementation.[^cite_note-47] Afghanistan became a member of [ECO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Cooperation_Organization), [OIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_OIC), [SAARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAARC), and [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTO). It has an observer status in the [SCO](152422.md).



A shopping area in the [Khair Khana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khair_Kh%C4%81na) neighborhood of Kabul

Some of the ongoing national mega projects include the [Qosh Tepa Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qosh_Tepa_Canal), the [TAPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan%E2%80%93Afghanistan%E2%80%93Pakistan%E2%80%93India_Pipeline) gas pipeline, the [CASA-1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA-1000) electricity line, the [Afghanistan Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Ring_Road) modernization, and the New Kabul housing project.[^cite_note-48] Other smaller housing projects include the Qatar Township in Kabul, Aino Mena in Kandahar and the [Ghazi Amanullah Khan Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Amanullah_Khan_Town) east of [Jalalabad](16274.md). Hundreds of similar projects are also found in [Herat](14128.md) in the west, [Mazar-e-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-e-Sharif) in the north, [Khost](400068.md) in the east, and in other cities.[^cite_note-49]

After the Islamic Emirate returned to power, the country suffered from a major liquidity crisis and lack of banknotes.[^cite_note-50] Because outside donors have severely cut funding to support Afghanistan's [health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan), [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Afghanistan), and other essential sectors, many Afghans lost their incomes. Under the assessment system of the [World Food Programme](33582.md) (WFP), almost 20 million people suffered either level-3 “crisis” or level-4 “emergency” levels of food insecurity. The impact of the crisis on women and girls was especially severe.[^cite_note-51] Officials under the Islamic Emirate continue to provide [communication services](6684.md) to areas that lacked them.[^cite_note-52] They collected 61 billion afghanis in [tariffs](55551.md) in 2022,[^cite_note-53] which increased to 76 billion in 2023.[^cite_note-54] And they continue to attract foreign investors.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

## Agriculture, animal husbandry and forestry

Main articles: [Almond production in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond_production_in_Afghanistan), [Opium production in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_production_in_Afghanistan), [Pomegranate production in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate_production_in_Afghanistan), [Kangina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangina), [Date palm farming in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm_farming_in_Afghanistan), [Tobacco in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_in_Afghanistan), and [Women in agriculture in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_agriculture_in_Afghanistan)

Agriculture remains Afghanistan's most important source of employment: 60-80 percent of Afghanistan's population works in this sector, although it accounts for less than a third of GDP due to insufficient irrigation, drought, lack of market access, and other structural impediments. Most Afghan farmers are primarily subsistence farmers.[^cite_note-61]

Afghanistan produces around 1.5 million tons of fresh [fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit) annually,[^cite_note-63] which could be increased significantly.[^cite_note-64] It produces some of the finest fruits, especially [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate_production_in_Afghanistan), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_grape_production), [melons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon), [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apples), [cherries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherries) and [mulberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberries).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Other fruits grown in the country include [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot), [figs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figs), [kiwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiwifruit), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranges), [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach), [pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear), [persimmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persimmon), [plums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum), and [strawberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberries).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] [Organic farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_farming) has always been widely practiced in Afghanistan than modern farming.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Many of its citizens have recently turned to using [greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse).[^cite_note-75] There were about 5,000 greenhouses reported in 2017.[^cite_note-76]

Afghanistan's dried fruit exports rose to \$667 million in 2025.[^cite_note-77] The country's northern and western provinces are known for [pistachio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistachio) cultivation.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] In recent years, farmers in the southern provinces began growing [American pistachio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_pistachio) trees.[^cite_note-80] The provinces of [Paktika](457294.md), [Paktia](457274.md), [Khost](457249.md), [Nangarhar](457263.md), [Nuristan](457267.md) and [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz_Province) are famous for [pine nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_nut).[^cite_note-81] The dried fruits basically grow in all provinces of Afghanistan, and they are not only sold locally but also exported to other countries.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

The northern and central provinces are more famous for [almond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond_production_in_Afghanistan) and [walnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut) production.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] The [Bamyan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamyan_Province) is the leader of superior potatoes, which produced 370,000 tons in 2020. Nangarhar, [Laghman](457257.md) and [Kunar](2159600.md) are the only provinces in the country where major farming of grapefruits, lemons, limes, and oranges can be found.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] Nangarhar also has farms of [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm_farming_in_Afghanistan), [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), and [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-sugarcane-90] Cultivation of these products have gradually spread to more provinces.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] Other agricultural products such as [avocados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avocado), [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana), [guavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guava), [mangos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango) and [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple) have recently been planted in the provinces of [Balkh](457218.md), [Helmand](457235.md), Laghman, Nangarhar, and Paktia.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

[Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) near [Jalalabad](16274.md) in [Nangarhar Province](457263.md)

Afghanistan is listed as the [54th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_vegetable_production) largest vegetables producing country. Most of its vegetables are for domestic consumption and include beans, broccoli, cabbages, carrots, cauliflowers, chickpeas, coriander, corns, cucumbers, eggplants, leeks, lettuces, okras, onions, peppers, potatoes, pumpkins, radishes, rhubarb, spinach, tomatoes, turnips, and zucchinis. [Wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [cereal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal) production is Afghanistan's traditional agricultural mainstay. The nation is nearing self-sufficiency in grain production. It requires an additional 2.33 million tons of wheat to become self-sufficient,[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] which is predicted to be accomplished in the near future.[^cite_note-98]

Arable land in Afghanistan was reported to be over 7.5 million hectares.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Wheat production had stood at 4.83 million metric tons in 2024,[^cite_note-101] nurseries held 119,000 hectares of land, and grape production is at 615,000 tons. Cotton production has jumped to 500,000 tons.[^cite_note-102] Around 12,000 ha (30,000 acres) of farm land in Afghanistan is used to cultivate [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron),[^cite_note-103] mostly in the west, north and south of the country.[^cite_note-104] Sugarcane is currently grown on 1,750 ha (4,300 acres) of land,[^cite_note-sugarcane-90] and [asafoetida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asafoetida) on nearly 980 ha (2,400 acres) of land.[^cite_note-105]

### Animal husbandry

Further information: [Animal husbandry in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry_in_Afghanistan)

[Animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry_in_Afghanistan) continues to play an important role in the nation's economy.[^cite_note-106] Afghanistan's [livestock](25160767.md) population mainly includes [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goats), [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camels), [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horses) and [donkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkeys).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] The country has around 15,000 [poultry farms](12116844.md).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] They are mostly in the warmer parts of the country.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

### Forestry

Further information: [Environmental issues in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Afghanistan)

These days up to 2.8% of Afghanistan is forested, which amounts to nearly 2,000,000 ha (4,900,000 acres) of the land.[^cite_note-TOLO-196900-114] In 2010, it was about 2.1% (or 1,350,000 ha (3,300,000 acres)).[^cite_note-115] It can be increased significanly by planting more trees, including in the non-rocky mountains that trap underground water.[^cite_note-116] Some small steps have been taken in recent years in planting trees all across Afghanistan.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-TOLO-196900-114] [Felling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felling) has been made illegal nationally.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

### Fishing

Further information: [List of rivers of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Afghanistan) and [List of dams and reservoirs in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dams_and_reservoirs_in_Afghanistan)

Afghanistan is landlocked with its citizens having no direct access to an ocean. The country has many [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake), [ponds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pond), [reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dams_and_reservoirs_in_Afghanistan), [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Afghanistan), [springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(hydrology\)), [streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream), etc.,[^cite_note-122] which make it a suitable climate for [fish farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farming).[^cite_note-123] Historically, [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) constituted a smaller part of the [Afghan diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_cuisine) because of the unavailability of modern fish farms. Fishing only took place in the lakes and rivers, particularly in the [Amu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amu_Darya), [Helmand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand_River) and [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) rivers.[^cite_note-124] Consumption of fish has increased sharply due to the establishment of thousands of fish farms.[^cite_note-125] Over 7,000 new ones are being established in the coming years.[^cite_note-126] The largest ones are at the [national reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dams_and_reservoirs_in_Afghanistan), which supply fish eggs to smaller fish farms.[^cite_note-127]

The country also has direct trade with [China](5405.md) and [India](14533.md) via [air corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_corridor).[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] In addition, imported goods also enter by rail from neighboring Central Asia, Iran and China.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

The [Afghanistan–Pakistan Transit Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan%E2%80%93Pakistan_Transit_Trade_Agreement) (APTTA) allows Afghan and Pakistani cargo [trucks](31456.md) to transit goods within both nations. This revised US-sponsored APTTA agreement also allows Afghan trucks to transport exports to India via Pakistan up to the [Wagah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagah) crossing point.&#x20;

There are multiple [dry ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_port) and over a dozen official [border crossing point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_crossing_point) all around Afghanistan. They include [Abu Nasir Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nasir_Port?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Farah Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farah_Province), [Ai-Khanoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai-Khanoum) in [Takhar Province](457289.md), [Angur Ada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angur_Ada) in [Paktika Province](457294.md), [Aqina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqina) in [Faryab Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faryab_Province), [Dand-aw-Patan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dand_Aw_Patan_District) in [Paktia Province](457274.md), [Ghulam Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghulam_Khan) in [Khost Province](457249.md), [Hairatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hairatan) in [Balkh Province](457218.md), [Ishkashim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishkashim,_Afghanistan) in [Badakhshan Province](1282198.md), [Islam Qala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Qala) in [Herat Province](312559.md), [Sher Khan Bandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Khan_Bandar) in [Kunduz Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz_Province), [Torghundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torghundi) in Herat Province, [Torkham](15844053.md) in [Nangarhar Province](457263.md), [Spin Boldak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesh%E2%80%93Chaman_border_crossing) in [Kandahar Province](457241.md), and [Zaranj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaranj) in [Nimruz Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimruz_Province).[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

The country also has legal access to two major seaports in Pakistan, the [Gwadar Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_Port) in Balochistan and the [Port Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Qasim) in Sindh. Afghanistan also has legal access to major seaports in Iran, which include the one in [Bandar Abbas](464348.md) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and the [Chabahar Port](15313649.md) in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md).[^cite_note-140]

&#x20;\
&#x20; Afghanistan is endowed with a wealth of natural resources, which include extensive deposits of [barites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barites), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [gold](12240.md), [gemstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone), [iron ore](145352.md), [lead](17747.md), [lithium](17561.md), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [natural gas](22131.md), [petroleum](23195.md), [salt](1605200.md), [sulfur](27127.md), [talc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talc), [uranium](31743.md), and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc).[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-Chinese_firm_offers_IEA_\$10_billion_investment_in_lithium_reserves-21][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] [Rare-earth elements](145440.md) can be found all over the country.[^cite_note-147] It is estimated that Afghanistan has as much as 1,000×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> (36×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>9</sup> cu ft) of natural gas, 570×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> (3.6 Gbbl) of oil and condensate reserves.[^cite_note-148] Geologists also found indications of abundant deposits of colored stones and gemstones, including [emerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald), [garnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet), [kunzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunzite), [lapis lazuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapis_lazuli), [peridot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peridot), [ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby), [sapphire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire), [spinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinel), and [tourmaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourmaline).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

Afghanistan has at least \$1 trillion in untapped mineral deposits.[^cite_note-151] A memo from the Pentagon stated that Afghanistan could become the "Saudi Arabia of [lithium](17561.md)".[^cite_note-152] Some believe that the untapped minerals may be worth up to \$3 trillion.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] The [Khanashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanashin) [carbonatites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonatite) in the [Helmand Province](457235.md) of the country have an estimated 1 million metric tonnes of [rare earth elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_element).[^cite_note-156]

Afghanistan currently has a copper mining deal with [China Metallurgical Group Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Metallurgical_Group_Corporation),[^cite_note-157] which involves the investment of \$2.8 billion by China and an annual income of about \$400 million to the Afghan government. The country's Ainak copper mine, located in [Logar Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province), is one of the biggest in the world. It is estimated to hold at least 11 million tonnes or US\$33 billion worth of copper.[^cite_note-158]

The previous Islamic Republic has signed a 30-year contract with investment group Centar and its operating company, Afghan Gold and Minerals Co., to explore and develop a copper mining operation in [Balkhab District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkhab_District) in [Sar-e Pol Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sar-e_Pol_Province), including a gold mining operation in [Badakhshan Province](1282198.md). The copper contract involved a \$56 million investment and the gold contract a \$22 million investment.[^cite_note-159]

The country's other treasure is the [Hajigak iron mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajigak_mining_concession), located 210 km (130 mi) west of Kabul and is believed to hold an estimated 1.8 billion to 2 billion metric tons of the mineral used to make steel.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] The country also has a number of coal mines.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

Afghanistan's important resource in the past has been natural gas, which was first tapped in 1967. During the 1980s, gas sales accounted for \$300 million a year in export revenues (56% of the total). About 90% of these exports went to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) to pay for imports and debts. However, during the withdrawal of Soviet troops in 1989, the natural gas fields were capped to prevent sabotage by criminals. Gas production has dropped from a high of 8.2×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> (290×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> cu ft) per day in the 1980s to a low of about 600,000 m<sup>3</sup> (21×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> cu ft) in 2001. Production of natural gas was restored during the [Karzai administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karzai_administration) in 2010.[^cite_note-165]

It is predicted that by pumping-out its own oil reserves,[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] Afghanistan will no longer need to import oil products.[^cite_note-169] Originally, the Karzai administration and [China National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Petroleum_Corporation) (CNPC) signed a contract for the development of three oil fields in the northern provinces of [Sar-e Pol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sar-e_Pol_Province), [Jowzjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jowzjan) and [Faryab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faryab).[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] It was later reported that CNPC began extracting 240,000 m<sup>3</sup> (1.5×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> bbl) of oil annually.[^cite_note-172] In early 2023, the [Xinjiang Central Asia Petroleum and Gas Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Central_Asia_Petroleum_and_Gas_Company) signed a similar contract with the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan.[^cite_note-173]

There are more than 5,000 factories operating in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] Most are locally owned,[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] while others involve foreign investors.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180] They produce construction materials, furniture, household items, apparel, food, beverages, pharmaceutical products, etc.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] The country imports roughly \$500 million of textile goods from other countries.[^cite_note-184] It exported about \$168 million worth of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) in 2022.[^cite_note-185] [Afghan handwoven rugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_rug) are some of the most popular products for exportation. Other products include hand crafted antique replicas as well as leather and furs. Afghanistan is the third largest exporter of [cashmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashmere_wool).[^cite_note-186]



Afghan United Bank

[Da Afghanistan Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Afghanistan_Bank) serves as the [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) of the nation. The "[afghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_afghani)" (AFN) is the national currency, which has an exchange rate of around 65 afghanis to [1 US dollar](18717338.md).[^cite_note-Afghani_reverses_to_7-year_old_value_against_US_dollar-16][^cite_note-Business-187][^cite_note-Today's_Currency_Rate-188] There are about a dozen different [banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banks_in_Afghanistan) operating in the country, including [Afghanistan International Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_International_Bank), [Kabul Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Bank), [Azizi Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azizi_Bank), [Pashtany Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtany_Bank), [Standard Chartered Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chartered_Bank), and [First Micro Finance Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_MicroFinance_Bank-Afghanistan). Cash is still widely used for most transactions. A new law on private investment provides three to seven-year [tax holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_holiday) to eligible companies and a four-year exemption from exports [tariffs](55551.md) and duties. Improvements to the business-enabling environment have resulted in more than \$1.5 billion in [telecom](33094374.md) investment and created more than 100,000 jobs since 2003.[^cite_note-189]

## Energy and water supply

Main articles: [Energy in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Afghanistan), [Renewable energy in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Afghanistan), and [Water supply in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_in_Afghanistan)



[Aerial photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_photography) of [Kandahar](17260.md) at night in 2011. Its electricity is provided mainly by two sources, the [Kajaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajaki_Dam) [power station](212141.md) in neighboring [Helmand Province](457235.md) and [solar farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_farms) on the outskirts of the city.

[^cite_note-190]

Energy in Afghanistan is provided by [hydropower](14073.md) followed by [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) and [solar power](13690575.md).[^cite_note-auto-5] The nation currently generates over 600 [megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts) (MW) of electricity from its several [hydroelectric plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Afghanistan) as well as using fossil fuel and [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel).[^cite_note-auto-5][^cite_note-191] Over 720 MW is imported from neighboring Iran, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [petrolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarakhil_Power_Plant) and [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayat_Power) are also used to produce electricity, and the [CASA-1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA-1000) is expected to add another 300 MW of electricity to the national grid. Due to the large influx of [Afghan expats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_diaspora) from [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghans_in_Iran), [Pakistan](22663356.md) and other countries,[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] Afghanistan may require as much as 10,000 MW of electricity in the future.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198]

Da Afghanistan Breshna Sherkat (DABS) is the national electricity provider.[^cite_note-199] It charges customers 2.5 afghanis per kw in Kabul Province, 4 afghanis in Herat Province, and around 6 afghanis in Balkh Province.[^cite_note-200] In certain remote areas private companies may provide electricity to villagers by charging them different prices.

[Renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Afghanistan) alternatives, such as [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) and [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy), has been identified as a high value power source for the future. Afghanistan has the potential to produce over 222,000 MW of electricity by using [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel).[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] The country also has the potential to produce over 66,000 MW of electricity by using [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine). A number of major solar and [wind farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm) exist in the country, with more under development.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan)



Due to lack of major [rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Afghanistan) service, [transport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan) is mostly done by road and air.

Afghanistan has five international airports, which include: [Kabul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport) in the capital city; the [Mawlana Jalaluddin Mohammad Balkhi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawlana_Jalaluddin_Mohammad_Balkhi_International_Airport) in [Mazar-i-Sharif](20517.md); the [Khwaja Abdullah Ansari International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwaja_Abdullah_Ansari_International_Airport) in [Herat](14128.md); the [Ahmad Shah Baba International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Baba_International_Airport) in [Kandahar](17260.md); and [Khost International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost_International_Airport) in [Khost](400068.md). It also has many [domestic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_airport) [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Afghanistan).[^cite_note-205] The major [airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_of_Afghanistan) of the country include [Ariana Afghan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariana_Afghan_Airlines) and [Kam Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kam_Air). Its national [rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Afghanistan) is slowly expanding to connect Central Asia with Pakistan and Iran.[^cite_note-206]

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Afghanistan)

[Tourism in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Afghanistan) was at its peak in 1977. Many foreign tourists visited Afghanistan, including from as far away as [Europe](9239.md) and [North America](21139.md). After a long halt since 1978, it has restarted.[^cite_note-207] Over 20,000 foreign tourists visit Afghanistan every year.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] As many as 371,000 Afghans have visited different parts of the country in 2022.[^cite_note-211] Although the country is considered safe now, [deadly attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_terrorist_incidents_linked_to_Islamic_State_%E2%80%93_Khorasan_Province) on tourists could not be ruled out. Certain foreign nationals have been strongly advised by their governments to avoid visiting Afghanistan.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213]

[Ariana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariana_Afghan_Airlines), [Flydubai](18432521.md) and [Kam Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kam_Air) all provide flight services between [Dubai International Airport](154742.md) and [Kabul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport).[^cite_note-214] The city of Kabul has many [guest houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guest_house) and big hotels, such as the [Kabul Grand Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Grand_Hotel), the [Hotel Inter-Continental Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Inter-Continental_Kabul), the Kabul Taj Hotel, the [Safi Landmark Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safi_Landmark_Hotel), and the Kabul Star Hotel. Guest houses and hotels are also available in other cities such Kandahar, Herat, Mazar-i-Sharif, Jalalabad, Bamyan and Fayezabad. For those wanting to travel by road, there are bus terminals with [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_Afghanistan), Afghan style restaurants and small shops in the major cities.[^cite_note-215] These bus terminals are now being expanded and shifted to the outskirts of the cities.[^cite_note-216]
