**Asadabad** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto);[^cite_note-4] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari):[^cite_note-5] اسعد‌آباد), historically called **Chighansarai** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto);[^cite_note-6] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari):[^cite_note-7] چغسرای),[^cite_note-Iranica-8][^cite_note-Beveridge-9] is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Afghanistan) in eastern [Afghanistan](737.md), serving as the [capital](181337.md) of [Kunar Province](2159600.md). It is within the jurisdiction of [Asadabad District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asadabad_District) and has an estimated population of 42,396 people.[^cite_note-population-3] [Mullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah) Nazar Mohammad Attiq is the current [mayor](101146.md) of the city.[^cite_note-Overcrowding,_chaos_haunt_Asadabad-1][^cite_note-10]

Asadabad is home to the [Sayed Jamaluddin Afghani University](34374263.md), which is located in the southwestern part of the city. Asadabad also has a number of [bazaars](556904.md), business centers, public parks, banks, hotels, restaurants, [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_Afghanistan), [hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Afghanistan), [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan), and places to play [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Afghanistan) or just relax. In 2015 there were 6,350 [dwelling units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwelling_unit) in the city.[^cite_note-Volume-I-2][^cite_note-Volume-II-11]

Asadabad is located within a valley at the confluence of the [Pech River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_River) and the [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River), between two mountain ridgelines running along both sides of the valley from northeast to southwest. It is in Afghanistan's [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountains about 13 km (8.1 mi) northwest of the [Durand Line](203570.md) (Afghanistan-Pakistan border) and 80 km (50 mi) northeast of [Jalalabad](16274.md) in [Nangarhar Province](457263.md).

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

The surrounding [Kunar Province](2159600.md) lies near the historic routes connecting Central Asia with [Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan), such as the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) and the [Grand Trunk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road) through the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass), as well as routes through the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountains. Asadabad lies at the confluence of the two major rivers of the area, the [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River) and the [Pech River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_River). The region would have come under the borders of various empires of the ages, including the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and the [Greco-Bactrian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Bactrian_Kingdom). It was very close to the paths [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) passed by on his way from conquering [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) to invading Hindustan. Asadabad is also on the eastern boundary of what was [Kafiristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafiristan) - a small part of [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Afghanistan) not converted to Islam until the 1890s. One testament to this is the [Waigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalasha-ala) name of nearby [Dam Kelay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dam_Kelay?action=edit\&redlink=1), *Dam Kil*.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Girardet-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In the past, the area near Asadabad was referred to as "Chighan Sarai", with various spellings (Chaga Serai, Chagasaray, Chigur Serai, Chughansuraee).[^cite_note-Iranica-8] [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) claimed to have taken over the town as part of his military campaigns in the area. He described it very briefly in [Baburnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baburnama). He also mentioned relations with the locals, whom he claimed helped defend the town against him.[^cite_note-Beveridge-9]

In the late 19th century/early 20th century the capital of the region was moved from Pushoot/Pasat/Pasad to the present Asadabad location. Over the 20th century, various technical improvements were made such as roads, a shopping area, schools, a bridge, a gas station, etc.[^cite_note-Iranica-8]

### Soviet Afghan war

In the opening times of the [Soviet Afghan war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Afghan_war) (1979–1988), Kunar province saw some of the first rebellions against the [People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Party_of_Afghanistan) (PDPA). As punishment, PDPA troops with Soviet advisors massacred the males of the nearby village of Kerala, burying their bodies in a field near [Pech River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_River).[^cite_note-Girardet-13] Asadabad was later linked to a Soviet military base during the war, which included [Spetsnaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spetsnaz).[^cite_note-Adamec-18] Several Russian websites, like [http://asadabad.ru](http://asadabad.ru/) [Archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20170609092935/http://www.asadabad.ru/) 2017-06-09 at the [Wayback Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayback_Machine), contain discussions and photos regarding this era.

In January 1979, an estimated 5,000 members of the [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen) attempted to capture the town, which was the headquarters of the [Afghan Army](212681.md)'s 9th Division.[^cite_note-19] In 1985, in the [Battle of Maravar Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maravar_Pass) the mujahideen killed 31 Soviet troops in an ambush.

The mujahideen later took control of the town from the Soviet forces. [Gulbuddin Hekmatyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbuddin_Hekmatyar)'s [Hezbi Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbi_Islami) had heavy influence in the area. [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) also spent time in Asadabad.[^cite_note-20]

On 20 April 1991, the marketplace of Asadabad was hit by two [Scud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud) missiles, which killed 300 and wounded 500 inhabitants. Though the exact toll is unknown, these attacks resulted in heavy civilian casualties.[^cite_note-21] The explosions destroyed the headquarters of [Jamil al-Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamil_al-Rahman) and killed a number of his followers.[^cite_note-Adamec-18]

### 21st century

In 2001 the [war on terror](13425800.md) began with the invasion of Afghanistan, including Kunar Province. [Forward Operating Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Operating_Base) [Camp Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NATO_installations_in_Afghanistan#Kunar_Province) was set up a few kilometres south of Asadabad. According to an article, the camp was originally built by the Soviets.[^cite_note-22] Asadabad was captured by the Taliban on 14 August 2021, as part of the wider [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive).[^cite_note-23] On April 28, 2026, Pakistan bombed a university in Asadabad killing 7 civilians and injuring 75 civilians.[^cite_note-24]

## Geography

Further information: [Geography of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Afghanistan)

Asadabad is a city in eastern Afghanistan. It is located within a valley at the confluence of the [Pech River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_River) and [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River), between two mountain ridgelines running along both sides of the valley from northeast to southwest. It is in Afghanistan's [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountains about 13 km (8.1 mi) northwest of the [Durand Line](203570.md) (Afghanistan-Pakistan border) and 80 km (50 mi) northeast of [Jalalabad](16274.md) in Nangarhar Province.

Asadabad is administratively divided into 7 city districts (*[nahias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiyah)*), covering a land area of 92 km<sup>2</sup> (36 sq mi) or (9,245 ha (22,840 acres)).[^cite_note-Volume-I-2][^cite_note-Volume-II-11]

### Climate

Asadabad has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*) under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system. The average annual temperature in Asadabad is 18.1 °C (64.6 °F). About 960 mm (37.80 in) of precipitation falls annually.

## Economy

Further information: [Economy of Afghanistan](9895.md)

The economy of Asadabad is mainly based on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade), and [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan). About 10 to 15 percent of the valley surrounding the city is used for agriculture by using an ancient land development technique called [terracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_\(agriculture\)). However, flooding and erosion have been an issue in the past. The primary crops are [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) and vegetables. Multiple international agencies have assisted with these issues and other issues throughout the province.

As of January 2009, growth around Asadabad has been substantial. Examples of development are:

- Kunar province's main marketplace now has more than 600 stores, up from 100 just three years ago.

- [Provincial Reconstruction Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Reconstruction_Team) (PRTs) have constructed 16 schools, 20 medical clinics, and 8 district centers.

- PRTs completed construction on 13 roads and 11 bridges.

- Agribusiness Developmental Teams (ADTs) have several evolving projects and over 10 demonstration farms.

- Jalalabad-Asmar and [Pech River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_River) roads have cut travel times in half and connected Asadabad centers of commerce with Jalalabad.

## Sport

Further information: [Sport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Afghanistan)

[Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_Afghanistan), [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Afghanistan), [futsal](188197.md) and [volleyball](32558.md) are the most popular sports in Afghanistan. The [Kunar Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_Cricket_Ground) opened in 2018 and has played host to [first-class cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) matches in the [Ahmad Shah Abdali 4-day Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Abdali_4-day_Tournament) and the [Mirwais Nika Provincial 3-Day Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Mirwais_Nika_Provincial_3-Day). The ground is most notable for a match between [Kabul Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Eagles) and [Boost Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost_Defenders) in the [2018 Ahmad Shah Abdali 4-day Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Ahmad_Shah_Abdali_4-day_Tournament), when Kabul batsman [Shafiqullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafiqullah_\(cricketer\)) scored the fastest double century in first-class cricket. He scored 200 [not out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_out) from 89 balls,[^cite_note-30] and also scored the most sixes in a first-class match, with 24.[^cite_note-31]

## Education

Further information: [Education in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Afghanistan)

There are a number of [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school) and [private](612857.md) [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Afghanistan) in Asadabad.[^cite_note-32] There are also a number of [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan). The [Sayed Jamaluddin Afghani University](34374263.md) is one of the largest ones.

## Notable residents and incidents

- U.S. Navy Lieutenant [Michael P. Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_P._Murphy) was posthumously awarded the [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor) for his actions during [Operation Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Red_Wings), which took place in the mountainous terrain near Asadabad from June to July 2005.

- US Operations: "Big East Wind" and [Operation Mountain Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mountain_Lion)

- A man named [Abdul Wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Wali_\(torture_victim\)) was tortured to death by CIA contractor [David Passaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Passaro) at the nearby US military base in 2003. Wali had brought himself in for questioning regarding the rocket attacks on the base, at the suggestion of the provincial governor, who told him he wouldn't be harmed. Passaro was later convicted of assault after [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) troops testified against him. He received 8+ years in prison.
