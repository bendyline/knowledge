**Baotou**[^cite_note-4] is the second-largest city by [urban population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_population) in [Inner Mongolia](55064.md), China. Governed as a [prefecture-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city), its built-up (or *metro*) area within its five urban districts is home to 2,261,089 people while the total population is 2,709,378 as of the 2020 census.[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-2]

Baotou's southern border is delineated by the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River). The city's namesake, literally translating to "place with deer", is [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language), bringing about the nickname of "Lucheng" ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 鹿城; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Lùchéng*), meaning "City of Deer".[^cite_note-5] Alternatively, Baotou is known as the "City of Steel in the Grasslands" (草原钢城; *Cǎoyuán Gāngchéng*), since steel was a major industry in the city which is built on the [Mongolian grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_grassland) and contains a large [grassland park](#Prominent_locations). Today, Baotou mines and refines over half of the [rare-earth minerals](4697772.md) produced in the world. This has led to environmental contamination near the industrial sites.

## History



The Deer monument in urban Baotou

### Ancient times

The area now known as Baotou was inhabited by nomads, some of whose descendants would later be categorized as Mongols. Near the end of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (206 BC–220 AD), [Lü Bu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BC_Bu), a particularly noteworthy warrior, was born in today's [Jiuyuan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuyuan_District) of Baotou.

### Foundation of the town

Compared to the capital of Inner Mongolia, [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot), Baotou's construction as a city came relatively late, being incorporated as a town in 1809. The city's site was chosen because it was in an arable region of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River)'s Great Bend.

### Early 20th century

The [Gelaohui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelaohui) secret society and the [Hui Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) General [Ma Fuxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Fuxiang) came to an agreement in 1922, in which Ma Fuxiang agreed to allow the Gelaohui to extort protection money from wool merchants in Baotou.[^cite_note-6]

A railway from Beijing was constructed in 1923, and the city began spurring some industrial sites. A German-Chinese joint-venture in 1934 constructed the [Baotou Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baotou_Airport) and opened a weekly route connecting Baotou with [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia) and [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou).

When young [Owen Lattimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Lattimore) visited Baotou in 1925, it was still "a little husk of a town in a great hollow shell of mud ramparts, where two busy streets made a traders' quarter", but already an important railhead. [Qinghai](173818.md) and [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) wool and hides were brought down the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) by raft and boat from [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou) to Baotou, and shipped from Baotou by rail to the east (in particular, to [Tianjin](50821.md) for export). The river traffic was one-way only, however, as the fast current made sailing up the Yellow River impractical. To travel from Baotou back to Lanzhou or [Yinchuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yinchuan), one would use a cart and camel road. There were also caravan roads from Baotou to [Ordos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordos_\(city\)) and the [Alxa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alxa_League).[^cite_note-7]

### Mid-20th century

#### Second Sino-Japanese War

Baotou was under Japanese control from 1937 until 1945.

#### Chinese civil war

On September 19, 1949, after the September 19 Rebellion, Baotou fell under Communist control. The People's Government was formed in February 1950.

#### Socialist Industrial City (1950s–1960s)

The early decades of the People's Republic of China marked Baotou's transformation into a flagship socialist industrial center. In 1954, it was designated one of the nation's eight key cities for heavy industry development, alongside Chengdu, Datong, Lanzhou, Luoyang, Taiyuan, Wuhan and Xi'an. This status, awarded based on the presence of major industrial enterprises, guaranteed Baotou prioritized state funding and support for construction.[^cite_note-\:5-8] The Iron and Steel Base in Baotou is one of the "156 projects", which were constructed with the help of the Soviet Union to develop China's national economy in the 1950s and 1960s, and it continues this reputation until this day. The city's urban layout itself reflected this new era, as Soviet architects contributed their experience in designing "socialist cities" – planned communities centered around major industrial plants.[^cite_note-\:5-8] This period saw Baotou rapidly expand and develop according to this integrated model of industry and urban living. Until the middle of the 1960s, the steel complex at Baotou was one of the rare examples of industrialization in the periphery of China.[^cite_note-\:12-9]<sup>: 316 </sup>

### Late 20th century

#### Modernization and diversification

Following the economic reforms, Baotou entered a new phase of growth and transformation. While the Baotou Iron and Steel Company remained the cornerstone of the local economy, the city began to diversify its industrial base. It evolved from a purely steel-producing center into a comprehensive hub for rare earth metallurgy, machinery manufacturing, and energy production, leveraging its rich mineral resources.

#### 1996 earthquake

Main article: [1996 Baotou earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Baotou_earthquake)

On 3 May 1996, at 03\:32AM [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC) (11\:32AM [local time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+8)), an [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Baotou_earthquake) of M<sub>S</sub> 6.4 occurred. Since the epicenter of the earthquake was located close to the city,[^cite_note-10] Baotou was very damaged by the earthquake: 26 people were killed, 453 injured and 196,633 lost their homes. The electrical infrastructure of the city was also damaged, and [soil liquefaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefaction) occurred around the swamps of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River).

The earthquake, which destroyed many old houses, led to the reconstruction of Baotou. In 2002, the Baotou Municipal Government was awarded by UN-HABITAT for the improvements in shelter and the urban environments.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### 21st century

In the early 21st century, Baotou's economy grew rapidly, growing about tenfold from 2000 to 2010.[^cite_note-\:1-13] The city's [gross domestic product](12594.md) peaked at about 386.763 billion [renminbi](66958.md) (RMB) in 2016, and has shrunk significantly since then.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

## Economy

Baotou is the largest economy of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region,[^cite_note-Information-14] and accounted for approximately 21.3% of [Inner Mongolia](55064.md)'s total [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) as of 2012.[^cite_note-GDP-15] As of 2018, the city's GDP is 295.180 billion [renminbi](66958.md) (RMB), a 7.22% increase from the previous year, but much lower than the city's peak of 386.763 billion RMB in 2016.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

Baotou's [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) has proven crucial to the city's economy. As of 2016, it contributed 182.215 RMB to the city's economy, 47.11% of the city's total GDP.[^cite_note-\:2-16] However, like the city's total economy, it has shrunk since then.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-\:2-16] As of 2020, the city's secondary sector contributed 115.300 billion RMB to Baotou's economy.[^cite_note-\:2-16]

Baotou Xingsheng Economic & Technological Development Zone is an industrial zone in Baotou.

As noted, in the early Communist years, Baotou served as an industrial centre, with a significant portion of its economy coming from its industry around metals, mostly steel. The Iron and Steel Base of Bautou was constructed with the help of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) to help China in developing its economy; it was one of the *156 Projects* that the Soviets helped build for that purpose in the 1950s and 1960s.

Baotou is a major center for rare-earth metals.[^cite_note-\:04-17] [Rare-earth minerals](4697772.md) from the [Bayan Obo Mining District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayan_Obo_Mining_District), about 120 kilometres from Baotou, are processed in the city. They are used in many electrical and electronic devices, such as smartphones, TVs, wind turbines, and electric vehicle motors.[^cite_note-\:0-18] The toxic byproducts from the refineries are contained by the [Baotou Tailings Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baogang_Tailings_Dam).[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-\:4-19]

## Demographics

&#x20;According to the [2020 Chinese Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census), Baotou has a permanent population of about 2,709,400 people, up about 59,000 from the [2010 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Chinese_census).[^cite_note-\:3-20] The average [household](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household) in Baotou comprises 2.27 people, down from 2.65 as of 2010.[^cite_note-\:3-20]

### Age and sex distribution

Baotou, like many places in [China](5405.md), has an [aging population](60912277.md). As per the 2020 Census, 20.19% of Baotou's population is aged 60 and older, 1.49 percentage points above the Chinese national average of 18.70%.[^cite_note-\:3-20] 13.70% of Baotou's population is aged 65 and older, 0.20 percentage points above the Chinese national average of 13.50%.[^cite_note-\:3-20] The number of people aged 60 and older has grown by 7.42% since 2010, and the number of people aged 65 and older has grown by 4.88% during that same span.[^cite_note-\:3-20]

Per the census, 50.7% of the city's population is [male](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male), and 49.3% is [female](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female).[^cite_note-\:3-20]

### Floating population

As of 2020, there are approximately 1,026,400 [floating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_population) residents of Baotou,[^cite_note-\:3-20] that is, residents of the city with a [hukou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukou) registration elsewhere. This population has increased by 144,100, or 22.37%, since 2010.[^cite_note-\:3-20]

### Urbanization

As of the 2020 Census, 2,334,400 people, or 86.16% of the city's population, lives in urban areas.[^cite_note-\:3-20] The remaining 375,000 people, 13.84% of the city's population, lives in rural areas.[^cite_note-\:3-20]

- Baotou is a terminus for both the [Baolan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baolan_Railway) and the [Jingbao Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingbao_Railway), heading for [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou) in the west and [Beijing](18603746.md) in the east, respectively. ## Geography

Baotou is located in the west of Inner Mongolia, located at the junction of two economic zones: the [Bohai Economic Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Economic_Rim) and the [Upper Yellow River Natural Resources Enrichment Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Yellow_River_Natural_Resources_Enrichment_Zone?action=edit\&redlink=1) (黄河上游资源富集区). Its administrative area borders Mongolia's [Dornogovi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornogovi_Province) to the north, while the Yellow River, which flows for 214 kilometres (133 mi) in the prefecture,[^cite_note-Geo-24] is south of the urban area itself. The [Tumochuan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumochuan_Plateau?action=edit\&redlink=1) (土默川平原), [Hetao Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetao_Plateau), and [Yin Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_Mountains) cross the urban area and central part of the prefecture. Baotou City ranges in latitude from 41° 20' to 42° 40' N and in longitude from 109° 50' to 111° 25' E.

### Climate

Baotou features a cold [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSk*), marked by long, cold and very dry winters, hot, somewhat humid summers, and strong winds, especially in spring. Temperatures often fall below −15 °C (5 °F) in winter and rise above 30 °C (86 °F) in summer. The annual precipitation is approximately 300 millimetres (11.8 in), with more than half of it falling in July and August alone. Due to the aridity and elevation, temperature differences between day and night can be large, especially in spring. In 2002, there were 12 instances of [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm).[^cite_note-Geo-24] Temperature extremes recorded since 1951 range from −31.4 °C (−25 °F) on 27 January 1971 (though an unofficial record low of −32.8 °C (−27 °F) on 23 December 1935)[^cite_note-25] to 40.4 °C (105 °F) on 22 June 2005.[^cite_note-26]
