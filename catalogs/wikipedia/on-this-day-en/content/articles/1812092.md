The **Christian Democratic Party** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Partido Demócrata Cristiano*, **PDC**) is a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) in [Bolivia](3462.md). Historically a [Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Way)[^cite_note-Greenwood-p131-5] party, it has now been described as a [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics)[^cite_note-yahoo_212505913-6][^cite_note-pagina12_850427-7] [conservative](6675.md)[^cite_note-dw2025-3] party that is supportive of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism)[^cite_note-17] and [decentralisation](49139.md).[^cite_note-newindianexpress_2025-10-20-4]

## History

Founded on 6 February 1954 as the Social Christian Party ([Spanish](26825.md): *Partido Social Cristiano*, PSC), it assumed its present name at a party congress in November 1964. Its intellectual foundations were study centres of the Church's social doctrine, the Bolivian Catholic Action and "Integral Humanism" (a centre for the study of the philosophy of [Jacques Maritain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Maritain)). It remains a conventionally "tercerista" party, calling for a [third way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_way) between capitalism and socialism – a way that would be more humane and truly democratic than either competing sociopolitical system. It was founded by Remo Di Natale, [Benjamín Miguel Harb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjam%C3%ADn_Miguel_Harb), Javier Caballero, and Emanuel Andrade.[^cite_note-Greenwood-p131-5]

The Christian Democratic Party took part in the 1958 and 1962 congressional elections, and in 1962 [Benjamín Miguel Harb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjam%C3%ADn_Miguel_Harb) became its first deputy. It boycotted the 1964 and 1966 presidential votes.[^cite_note-18]

In 1967, the party took part in the Government of the president of [René Barrientos Ortuño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Barrientos), being given responsibility for the Ministry of Labour and Social Security. This was a major misjudgment by the PDC leadership. When military forces carried out bloody raids against mining camps, the Christian Democratic Party was forced to withdraw in anger and embarrassment, with severe internal divisions resulting. The party's youth organization had been discontented with the third-road philosophy for some time, and the mine camp invasions helped to crystallize their rebellion; they favored revolutionary socialism as a solution to Bolivia's dilemmas. In the late 1960s, the youth wing seceded to form the Revolutionary PDC which later became the [Revolutionary Left Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Left_Movement_\(Bolivia\)) (MIR). Several discontented members of the party, including [Jose Luis Roca Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Luis_Roca_Garcia?action=edit\&redlink=1), also left to join General [Alfredo Ovando Candía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Ovando_Cand%C3%ADa)'s short-lived nationalist revolutionary government in 1969–1970.[^cite_note-Greenwood-p131-5]

Under the dictatorship of President [Hugo Banzer Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Banzer) the Christian Democrats fought for human rights, fundamental freedoms and the holding of elections, but its president [Benjamín Miguel Harb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjam%C3%ADn_Miguel_Harb) was exiled in 1974 and its organizing secretary [Felix Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Vargas?action=edit\&redlink=1) forced to leave the country shortly afterwards.[^cite_note-19]

The PDC took part in 1978 general elections, running former Defense Minister General [René Bernal Escalante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Bernal_Escalante?action=edit\&redlink=1), a leader of the right-wing faction which supported the [Hugo Banzer Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Banzer) regime.[^cite_note-handbook-p70-20] After the 1978 election, [René Bernal Escalante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Bernal_Escalante?action=edit\&redlink=1) split from the PDC and founded the [Christian Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_\(Bolivia\)).[^cite_note-handbook-p70-20]

For the elections held on 1 July 1979, the party joined the [Revolutionary Nationalist Movement-Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Nationalist_Movement-Alliance) with four other parties — the [Revolutionary Nationalist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Nationalist_Movement) (MNR), the [Authentic Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentic_Revolutionary_Party) (PRA), the [Marxist Leninist Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Bolivia_\(Marxist%E2%80%93Leninist\)) (PCML) and the [Tupaj Katari Revolutionary Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupaj_Katari_Revolutionary_Movement) (MRTK). The Alliance ran a MNR's leader [Víctor Paz Estenssoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Paz_Estenssoro) as its presidential candidate and a PDC's leader [Luis Ossio Sanjines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Ossio) as its vice-presidential candidate.[^cite_note-Nohlen-p151-21] In 1979 the Christian Democratic Party won nine seats in the Chamber of Deputies and three in the Senate.

In 1980 the PDC took part in an electoral coalition [Democratic Revolutionary Front-New Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Revolutionary_Front-New_Alternative) backing ex-President [Luis Adolfo Siles Salinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Adolfo_Siles_Salinas), which polled few votes; the leader of the PDC [Benjamín Miguel Harb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjam%C3%ADn_Miguel_Harb) ran as vice-presidential candidate.[^cite_note-Nohlen-p151-21]

Soon after the restoration of democratic government, in November 1982, Christian Democrats took a seat in the [Hernán Siles Zuazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Siles_Zuazo) Government, but withdrew from the coalition in October 1984.[^cite_note-handbook-p126-22]

The PDC took part in 1985 general elections, running [Luis Ossio Sanjines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Ossio) as its presidential candidate and [Jaime Ponce García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Ponce_Garc%C3%ADa?action=edit\&redlink=1) as vice-presidential candidate, and won three seats in the Chamber of Deputies.[^cite_note-Nohlen-p151-21] Although winning no legislative seats as an ally of the [Nationalist Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Democratic_Action) in May 1989, [Luis Ossio Sanjines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Ossio), was elected vice-president of the Republic as a result of its adherence to the [Nationalist Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Democratic_Action) – [Revolutionary Left Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Left_Movement_\(Bolivia\)) pact ([Patriotic Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Agreement?action=edit\&redlink=1)) in August. The PDC campaigned as a member of the [Patriotic Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Agreement?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1993 elections.[^cite_note-handbook-p126-22] The Christian Democratic Party was one of the founding components of [Social and Democratic Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_Democratic_Power) (PODEMOS), for which it provided its electoral registration. Following the 2005 election, this alliance led the parliamentary opposition to President Evo Morales.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The party joined [Creemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creemos) for the [2020 Bolivian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Bolivian_general_election).

For the [2025 Bolivian general election](77237927.md), the PDC nominated senator from [Tarija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarija) [Rodrigo Paz](69731047.md) as its presidential candidate.[^cite_note-25] In a major upset, Paz advanced to the second round of voting, where he defeated former President [Jorge Quiroga](344089.md), becoming Bolivia's first President to belong to the PDC.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The party also won 16 seats in the Senate and 49 seats in the Chamber of Deputies.[^cite_note-28] The electoral results gave the PDC the largest parliamentary bloc, allowing it to form a government.[^cite_note-29]
