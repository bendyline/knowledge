**Marseille**[^cite_note-7] ([Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language): *Marselha*) is a city in [southern France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_France), the [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) of the [Bouches-du-Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-du-Rh%C3%B4ne) [department](38523.md) and of the [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) [region](58846.md). Situated in [Provence](48503.md), it marks the eastern end of the [Côte Bleue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_Bleue) on the [Mediterranean](19006.md) coast. Marseille is the [second-most populous city proper](76050805.md) in France, after [Paris](22989.md), with 886,040 inhabitants in 2023[^cite_note-pophist-8] over a municipal territory of 241 km<sup>2</sup> (93 sq mi). Together with its [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) and [exurbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurb), the Marseille [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area), which extends over 3,972 km<sup>2</sup> (1,534 sq mi), had a population of 1,917,728 as of 2023.[^cite_note-compterr-2] The cities of Marseille, [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence), and 90 suburban municipalities have formed since 2016 the [Aix-Marseille-Provence Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille-Provence_Metropolis), an [indirectly elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirectly_elected) [metropolitan authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tropole) now in charge of wider metropolitan issues, with a population of 1,939,077 as of 2023.[^cite_note-compterr-2] The southeast of Marseille is in [Calanques National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calanques_National_Park), making Marseille the only large city in France to have a [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_France) within city limits.

Founded c. 600 BC by Greek settlers, Marseille is the oldest city in France, as well as one of Europe's [oldest continuously inhabited settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuchêneContrucci1998page_needed_A-9] It was known to the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks) as *[Massalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massalia)* and to [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans) as *Massilia*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuchêneContrucci1998page_needed_A-9][^cite_note-10] Marseille has been a trading port since ancient times. It experienced a commercial boom during the colonial period and especially in the 19th century, becoming a prosperous industrial and trading city. The [Old Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Port_of_Marseille) still lies at the heart of the city, where the manufacture of [Marseille soap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_soap) began some six centuries ago. Overlooking the port is the [Notre-Dame de la Garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_la_Garde) – known as "*la Bonne Mère*" by the people of Marseille – a [Romano-Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_architecture) church and a symbol of the city. Today, the Grand Port Maritime de Marseille (GPMM) and the maritime economy play a major role in regional and national economic activity. Marseille remains the first French port, the second Mediterranean port and the fifth European port.[^cite_note-11] Since its origins, Marseille's openness to the Mediterranean Sea has made it a [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism) city marked by cultural and economic exchanges with Southern Europe, the Middle East, North Africa and Asia. In Europe, the city has the third largest Jewish community after [London](17867.md) and Paris.[^cite_note-12]

In the 1990s, the [Euroméditerranée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurom%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e) project for economic development and urban renewal was launched. New infrastructure projects and renovations were carried out in the 2000s and 2010s: the [tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_tramway), the renovation of the [Hôtel-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterContinental_Marseille_Hotel_Dieu) into a luxury hotel, the expansion of the [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome), the [CMA CGM Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMA_CGM_Tower), as well as other quayside museums such as the [Museum of European and Mediterranean Civilisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_European_and_Mediterranean_Civilisations) (MuCEM). As a result, Marseille now has the most museums in France after Paris. The city was named [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2013 and European Capital of Sport in 2017. Home of [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), one of the most successful and widely supported football clubs in France, Marseille has hosted matches at the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) and [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016). It is home to several higher education institutions in the region, including the [University of Aix-Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aix-Marseille). A resident of Marseille is a *Marseillais*.

## Name

The name Marseille most likely comes from the name Massalia given to the ancient city initially founded by Greeks. Variations of the name include:

- In English *Marseille*, or formerly *Marseilles*, both pronounced [/mɑːrˈseɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*mar-SAY*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);

- In French *Marseille*, which is pronounced [\[maʁsɛj\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/22/Fr-Normandie-Marseille.ogg/Fr-Normandie-Marseille.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/22/Fr-Normandie-Marseille.ogg/Fr-Normandie-Marseille.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> in [Standard French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_French) and [\[maχˈsɛjə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f8/Fr-Marseille.ogg/Fr-Marseille.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f8/Fr-Marseille.ogg/Fr-Marseille.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> in [local French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridional_French);

- In [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) ([Provençal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven%C3%A7al_dialect)) *Marselha* (pronounced [\[maʀˈsejɔ, maʀˈsijɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Occitan)) according to the Classical orthographic norm, which may be written *Marsiho* according to the [Mistralian norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistralian_norm), from the [Medieval Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Occitan) *Marselha* or *Masselha*;

- In [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *Massilia*, from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) Μασσαλία (*Massalía*), which is the oldest attestation of the name, since [the city was founded by Greek settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massalia) around 600 BC, and remained for a long time a Greek-speaking place even after it fell under Roman rule.

## Geography



A [Sentinel-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-2) satellite view of Marseille

Marseille is the [second-largest metropolitan area in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area_\(France\)#List_of_France's_aires_urbaines_\(metropolitan_areas\)) after Paris. To the east, starting in the small fishing village of Callelongue on the outskirts of Marseille and stretching as far as [Cassis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassis), are the [Calanques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calanque), a rugged coastal area interspersed with small [fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)-like inlets. Farther east still are the [Sainte-Baume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Baume) (a 1,147 m (3,763 ft) mountain ridge rising from a forest of [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) trees), the city of [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon) and the [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera). To the north of Marseille, beyond the low [Garlaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlaban) and Etoile mountain ranges, is the 1,011 m (3,317 ft) [Montagne Sainte-Victoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montagne_Sainte-Victoire). To the west of Marseille is the former artists' colony of [l'Estaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Estaque); farther west are the [Côte Bleue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_Bleue), the [Gulf of Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Lion) and the [Camargue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camargue) region in the [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne) [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta). The [Marseille Provence Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Provence_Airport) lies to the north west of the city at [Marignane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marignane) on the [Étang de Berre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tang_de_Berre).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-13]

The city's main thoroughfare (the wide boulevard called the [Canebière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canebi%C3%A8re)) stretches eastward from the [Old Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Port_of_Marseille) to the Réformés quarter. Two large forts flank the entrance to the Old Port—Fort Saint-Nicolas[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] on the south side and [Fort Saint-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint-Jean_\(Marseille\)) on the north. Farther out in the Bay of Marseille is the [Frioul archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frioul_archipelago) which comprises four islands, one of which, If, is the location of [Château d'If](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_d'If), made famous by the [Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Dumas) novel *[The Count of Monte Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Count_of_Monte_Cristo)*. The main commercial centre of the city intersects with the Canebière at Rue St Ferréol and the Centre Bourse (one of the city's main shopping malls). The centre of Marseille has several pedestrianised zones, most notably Rue St Ferréol, Cours Julien near the Music Conservatory, the Cours Honoré-d'Estienne-d'Orves off the Old Port and the area around the Hôtel de Ville. In 2024, the Cours Julien was named one of the 38 coolest neighborhoods in the world by [Time Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_\(magazine\)).[^cite_note-TimeOutNeighbourhoods2024-16] To the south east of central Marseille in the [6th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_arrondissement_of_Marseille) are the Prefecture and the monumental fountain of Place Castellane, an important bus and metro interchange. To the south west are the hills of the [7th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_arrondissement_of_Marseille) and [8th arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_arrondissement_of_Marseille), dominated by the [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica) of [Notre-Dame de la Garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_la_Garde). Marseille's main railway station—[Marseille-Saint-Charles station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Saint-Charles_station)—is north of the Centre Bourse in the 1st arrondissement; it is linked by the Boulevard d'Athènes to the Canebière.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-13]

### Climate

The city has a hot-summer [mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) with cool-mild winters with moderate rainfall, because of the wet westerly winds, and hot, mostly dry summers.[^cite_note-Marseille,_France-17] December, January, and February are the coldest months, averaging temperatures of around 12 °C (54 °F) during the day and 4 °C (39 °F) at night. July and August are the hottest months, averaging temperatures of around 28–30 °C (82–86 °F) during the day and 19 °C (66 °F) at night in the Marignane airport \[35 km (22 mi) from Marseille] but in the city near the sea the average high temperature is 27 °C (81 °F) in July.[^cite_note-18]

Marseille receives the most sunlight of any French city, 2,897.6 hours per year on average,[^cite_note-19] while the average sunshine in the country is around 1,950 hours. It is also the driest major city with only 532.3 mm (21 in) of precipitation annually, mainly due to the [mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(wind\)), a cold, dry wind originating in the [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne) Valley that occurs mostly in winter and spring and which generally brings clear skies and sunny weather to the region. Less frequent is the [sirocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirocco), a hot, sand-bearing wind, coming from the Sahara. Snowfalls are infrequent; over 50% of years do not experience a single snowfall.

The hottest temperature was 40.6 °C (105.1 °F) on 26 July 1983 during a great heat wave, the lowest temperature was −16.8 °C (1.8 °F) on 13 February 1929 during a strong cold wave.[^cite_note-marseilleobsnormals-20]

## History

Main article: [History of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Marseille)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Marseille).



A silver [drachma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_drachma) inscribed with MASSA\[LIA] (ΜΑΣΣΑ\[ΛΙΑ]), dated 375–200 BC, during the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) of Marseille, bearing the head of the [Greek goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Greek_deities) [Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis) on the [obverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse) and a lion on the reverse

Marseille was founded as the [Greek colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies_in_antiquity) of [Massalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massalia) c. 600 BC, and was populated by Greek settlers from [Phocaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phocaea) (modern [Foça](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fo%C3%A7a), Turkey). It became the preeminent Greek *[polis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polis)* in the [Hellenized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenized) region of [southern Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_pre-Roman_Gaul).[^cite_note-32] The city-state sided with the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) against [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) during the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War) (218–201 BC), retaining its independence and commercial empire throughout the [western Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Mediterranean) even as Rome expanded its empire into [Western Europe](33800.md) and [North Africa](21714.md). However, the city lost its independence following the Roman [Siege of Massilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Massilia) in 49 BC, during [Caesar's Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar's_Civil_War), in which Massalia sided with the [exiled faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimates) at war with [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar). Afterward, the [Gallo-Roman culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Roman_culture) was initiated.

The city maintained its position as a premier maritime trading hub even after its capture by the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) in the fifth century AD, although the city went into decline following the sack of AD 739 by the forces of [Charles Martel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Martel) against the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate). It became part of the [County of Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Provence) during the tenth century, although its renewed prosperity was curtailed by the Black Death of the 14th century and a sack of the city by the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) in 1423.[^cite_note-33] The city's fortunes rebounded with the ambitious building projects of [René of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Anjou), Count of Provence, who strengthened the city's fortifications during the mid-15th century. During the 16th century, the city hosted a naval fleet with the combined forces of the [Franco-Ottoman alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Ottoman_alliance), which threatened the ports and navies of the [Genoese Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_Republic).[^cite_note-34]

Marseille lost a significant portion of its population during the [Great Plague of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plague_of_Marseille) in 1720, but the population had recovered by mid-century. In 1792, the city became a focal point of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), and though France's [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem) was born in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), it was first sung in Paris by volunteers from Marseille, hence the name the crowd gave it: *[La Marseillaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Marseillaise)*. The [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) and establishment of the [second French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_colonial_empire) during the 19th century allowed for the further expansion of the city, although it was occupied by the German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) in November 1942 and subsequently heavily damaged during [World War II](32927.md). The city has since become a major center for immigrant communities from former French colonies in Africa, such as [French Algeria](292341.md).

## Economy

Marseille is a major hub of trade and industry in France, supported by a well-developed transportation network that includes roads, a seaport, and an airport. Marseille Provence Airport is the fourth largest in France. In May 2005, the French financial magazine *L'Expansion* named Marseille the most dynamic of France's large cities, citing figures showing that 7,200 companies had been created in the city since 2000.[^cite_note-35] As of 2019, the Marseille metropolitan area had a [GDP amounting to US\$81.4 billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP),[^cite_note-36] or US\$43,430 per capita (purchasing power parity).[^cite_note-37]

### Port

Main articles: [Marseille-Fos Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Fos_Port), [Old Port of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Port_of_Marseille), and [Docks (Marseille)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docks_\(Marseille\))



The entrance to the Old Port, flanked by Fort Saint-Jean and Fort Saint-Nicolas

Historically, the economy of Marseille was dominated by its role as a port of the French Empire, linking the North African colonies of Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia with [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France). The [Old Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Port_of_Marseille) was replaced as the main port for trade by the Port de la Joliette (now part of [Marseille-Fos Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Fos_Port)) during the [Second Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire) and now contains restaurants, offices, bars and hotels and functions mostly as a private marina. The majority of the port and [docks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docks_\(Marseille\)), which experienced decline in the 1970s after the [oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), have been recently redeveloped with funds from the [European Union](9317.md). Fishing remains important in Marseille and the food economy of Marseille is fed by the local catch; a daily fish market is still held on the Quai des Belges of the Old Port.

The economy of Marseille and its region is still linked to its commercial port, the first French port and the fifth [European port by cargo tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_ports_in_Europe), which lies north of the Old Port and eastern in [Fos-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fos-sur-Mer). Some 45,000 jobs are linked to the port activities and it represents €4 billion of added value to the regional economy.[^cite_note-fos-38] 100 million tons of [freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo) pass annually through the port, 60% of which is petroleum, making it number one in France and the Mediterranean and number three in Europe. However, in the early 2000s, the growth in container traffic was being stifled by the constant strikes and social upheaval.[^cite_note-39] The port is among the 20th firsts in Europe for container traffic with 1,062,408 [TEU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) and new infrastructure has already raised the capacity to 2 million TEU.[^cite_note-40] Marseille is connected with the Rhône via a [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) and thus has access to the extensive waterway network of France. Petroleum is shipped northward to the Paris basin by pipeline. The city also serves as France's leading centre of oil refining.

### Companies, services and high technologies



From left to right: [La Joliette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Joliette) neighbourhood ([old docks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docks_\(Marseille\))), ferry ship docks, new port, [Euroméditerranée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurom%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e) business district ([CMA CGM Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMA_CGM_Tower)) and surrounding areas

In recent years, the city has also experienced a large growth in [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) employment and a switch from [light manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) to a cultural, [high-tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech) economy. The Marseille region is home to thousands of companies, 90% of which are [small and medium enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium_enterprises) with less than 500 employees.[^cite_note-41] Among the most famous are [CMA CGM](1903569.md), container-shipping giant; [Compagnie maritime d'expertises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_maritime_d'expertises) (Comex), a leader in sub-sea engineering and hydraulic systems; [Airbus Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Helicopters), an [Airbus](26220236.md) division; Azur Promotel, an active [real estate development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_development) company; *La Provence*, the local [daily newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper); RTM, Marseille's public transport company; and Société Nationale Maritime Corse Méditerranée (SNCM), a major provider of passenger, vehicle and freight transportation in the Western Mediterranean. The [Euroméditerranée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurom%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e) urban development project has significantly expanded the city's office stock, making Marseille home to one of the largest business districts in France.

Marseille is the home of three main [technopoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technopole): Château-Gombert (technological innovations), Luminy (biotechnology) and La Belle de Mai (17,000 sq.m. of offices dedicated to multimedia activities).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### Tourism and attractions



Pointe Rouge Beach



[Palais du Pharo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_du_Pharo)

The port is also an important arrival base for millions of people each year, with 2.4 million including 890,100 from cruise ships.[^cite_note-fos-38] With its beaches, history, architecture and culture (24 museums and 42 theatres), Marseille is one of the most visited cities in France, with 4.1 million visitors in 2012.[^cite_note-44]

They take place in three main sites, the [Palais du Pharo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_du_Pharo), Palais des Congrès et des Expositions (Parc Chanot) and World Trade Center.[^cite_note-45] In 2012 Marseille hosted the [World Water Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Water_Forum). Several urban projects have been developed to make Marseille attractive. Thus new parks, museums, public spaces and real estate projects aim to improve the city's quality of life ([Parc du 26e Centenaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_du_26e_Centenaire), [Old Port of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Port_of_Marseille),[^cite_note-46] numerous places in [Euroméditerranée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurom%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e)) to attract firms and people. Marseille municipality acts to develop Marseille as a regional nexus for entertainment in the south of France with high concentration of museums, cinemas, theatres, clubs, bars, restaurants, fashion shops, hotels, and [art galleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_museum).

### Employment

The unemployment rate in Marseille fell from 20% in 1995 to 14% in 2004.[^cite_note-47] In 2022, the unemployment rate in Marseille was 13.1%, according to data from the French National Institute of Statistics and Economic Studies ([INSEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSEE)).[^cite_note-48] However, Marseille's unemployment rate remains higher than the national average. In some parts of Marseille, youth unemployment is reported to be as high as 40%.[^cite_note-49]

## Administration

Main articles: [Arrondissements of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Marseille) and [Cantons of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Marseille)



The sectors and arrondissements of Marseille



Political majority in each sector since 2014

The city of Marseille is divided into 16 [municipal arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_arrondissements_of_France), which are themselves informally divided into 111 neighbourhoods (French: *quartiers*). The arrondissements are regrouped in pairs, into 8 sectors, each with a mayor and council (like the arrondissements in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Paris) and [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Lyon)).[^cite_note-50] Municipal elections are held every six years and are carried out by sector. There are 303 councilmembers in total, two-thirds sitting in the sector councils and one third in the city council.

The [9th arrondissement of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_arrondissement_of_Marseille) is the largest in terms of area because it comprises parts of [Calanques National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calanques_National_Park). With a population of 89,316 (2007), the [13th arrondissement of Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_arrondissement_of_Marseille) is the most populous one.

From 1950 to the mid-1990s, Marseille was a [Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) (PS) and [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party) (PCF) stronghold. [Gaston Defferre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Defferre) (PS) was consecutively reelected six times as Mayor of Marseille from 1953 until his death in 1986. He was succeeded by [Robert Vigouroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Vigouroux) of the [European Democratic and Social Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Democratic_and_Social_Rally) (RDSE). [Jean-Claude Gaudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Gaudin) of the conservative [UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement) was elected Mayor of Marseille in 1995. Gaudin was reelected in 2001, 2008 and 2014.

In recent years, the Communist Party has lost most of its strength in the northern boroughs of the city, whereas the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_\(France\)) has received significant support. At the [last municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_French_municipal_elections) in 2014, Marseille was divided between the northern arrondissements dominated by the left (PS) and far-right (FN) and the southern part of town dominated by the conservative (UMP). Marseille is also divided in [twelve cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Marseille), each of them sending two members to the [Departmental Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_councils_\(France\)) of the [Bouches-du-Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-du-Rh%C3%B4ne) [department](38523.md).

Because of its pre-eminence as a Mediterranean port, Marseille has always been one of the main gateways into France. This has attracted many immigrants and made Marseille a cosmopolitan [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot). By the end of the 18th century about half the population originated from elsewhere in Provence mostly and also from southern France.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuchêneContrucci1998page_needed_E-60]

Economic conditions and political unrest in Europe and the rest of the world brought several other waves of immigrants during the 20th century: Greeks and Italians started arriving at the end of the 19th century and in the first half of the 20th century, up to 40% of the city's population was of Italian origin;[^cite_note-eu-61] Russians in 1917; Armenians in 1915 and 1923; Vietnamese in the 1920s, 1954 and after 1975;[^cite_note-62] [Corsicans](5714828.md) during the 1920s and 1930s; Spanish after 1936; [Maghrebis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebis) (both [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_people) and [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people)) in the [inter-war period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period); Sub-Saharan Africans after 1945; [Maghrebi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebi_Jews) in the 1950s and 1960s; the [Pieds-Noirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied-Noir) from the former [French Algeria](292341.md) in 1962; and then from [Comoros](5403.md).

At the 2019 census, 81.4% of the inhabitants of the Marseille [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area_\(France\)) were natives of [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France), 0.6% were born in [Overseas France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France), and 18.0% were born in foreign countries (two-fifth of whom French citizens from birth, in particular [Pieds-Noirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieds-Noirs) from Algeria arrived in Metropolitan France after the independence of Algeria in 1962).[^cite_note-immig_1-56] A quarter of the immigrants living in the Marseille metropolitan area were born in Europe (half of them in Italy, Portugal, and Spain), 46% were born in the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) (almost two-third of them in Algeria), 14% in the rest of Africa (almost half of them in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) islands of [Comoros](5403.md), [Madagascar](18964.md), and [Mauritius](19201.md), not counting those born in [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) and [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) who are not legally immigrants), and 15.0% in the rest of the world (not counting those born in the [French overseas departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_regions_of_France) of the Americas and in the French territories of the South Pacific, who are not legally immigrants).[^cite_note-immig_2-57]

In 2002, about one third of the population of Marseille can trace their roots back to Italy.[^cite_note-\:3-63] Marseille also has the second-largest [Corsican](5714828.md) and [Armenian](387816.md) populations of France. Other significant communities include [Maghrebis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebis), [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), [Comorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorians_in_France), Chinese, and [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people).[^cite_note-64]

The largest immigrant communities (including descendants) in 2002 were Italians (290,000 Italians, or 33%), then Muslims - mainly Maghrebis (200,000 Muslims, or 23%), then Corsicans (100,000 Corsicans, or 11.5%), then Armenians (80,000 Armenians, or 9%).[^cite_note-\:3-63]

In 1999, in several arrondissements, about 40% of the young people under 18 were of Maghrebi origin (at least one immigrant parent).[^cite_note-65]

Since 2013 a significant number of Central- and Eastern European immigrants have settled in Marseille, attracted by better job opportunities and the good climate of this Mediterranean city.[^cite_note-66]

### Crime

Since the early 2020s, the city has struggled with a notable rise in [narcotrafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotrafficking) and other issues related to organized criminal networks, with 2023 being described as the city's "deadliest year."[^cite_note-68] In November 2025, France's Justice Minister, [ Gérald Darmanin](54070416.md), described the threat posed by these groups as "at least equivalent to terrorism."[^cite_note-69] According to [The Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Global_Initiative_Against_Transnational_Organized_Crime), Marseille "has become a hotspot of violence," witnessing "more than 300 drug-related killings in the last decade, with a 50 per cent increase between 2022 and 2023 alone."[^cite_note-70]

## Culture



[Paul Cézanne](24472.md)'s *The Bay of Marseille, Seen from L'Estaque*



[Bastille Day military parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastille_Day_military_parade) in Marseille, 2012

Marseille is a city that has its own unique culture. Today it is a regional centre for culture and entertainment, with an [opera house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_de_Marseille), historical and maritime museums, five art galleries and numerous cinemas, clubs, bars and restaurants.

Marseille has a large number of theatres, including La Criée, Le Gymnase and the Théâtre Toursky. There is also an extensive [arts centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_centre) in [La Friche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Friche), a former match factory behind the Saint-Charles station. The [Alcazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazar_\(Marseille\)), until the 1960s a well known [music hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_hall) and [variety theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_show), has recently been completely remodelled behind its original façade and now houses the central municipal library.[^cite_note-71] Other music venues in Marseille include Le Silo (also a theatre) and [GRIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRIM).

Marseille has also been important in the arts. It has been the birthplace and home of many French writers and poets, including [Victor Gélu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_G%C3%A9lu?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Valère Bernard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val%C3%A8re_Bernard), [Pierre Bertas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Bertas?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-72] [Edmond Rostand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond_Rostand) and [André Roussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Roussin). The small port of [l'Estaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Estaque) on the far end of the Bay of Marseille became a favourite haunt for artists, including [Auguste Renoir](24546.md), [Paul Cézanne](24472.md) (who frequently visited from his home in [Aix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence)), [Georges Braque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Braque) and [Raoul Dufy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Dufy).

### Multi-cultural influences

Rich and poor neighborhoods exist side by side. Although the city has crime, Marseille has a large degree of multicultural tolerance. Urban geographers[^cite_note-73] say the city's geography, being surrounded by mountains, helps explain why Marseille does not have the same problems as Paris. In Paris, ethnic areas are segregated and concentrated in the periphery of the city. Residents of Marseille are of diverse origins, yet appear to share a similar particular identity.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-\:0-75] An example is how Marseille responded in 2005, when ethnic populations living in other French cities' suburbs [rioted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_riots), but Marseille remained relatively calm.[^cite_note-76]



Poster of [Marseille-Provence 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Provence_2013) designed by [Guillaume Bottazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Bottazzi)

Marseille served as the [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) for 2013 along with [Košice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1ice).[^cite_note-77] It was chosen to give a 'human face' to the European Union to celebrate cultural diversity and to increase understanding between Europeans.[^cite_note-78] One of the intentions of highlighting culture is to help reposition Marseille internationally, stimulate the economy, and help to build better interconnection between groups.[^cite_note-79] [Marseille-Provence 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Provence_2013) (MP2013) featured more than 900 cultural events held throughout Marseille and the surrounding communities. These cultural events generated more than 11 million visits.[^cite_note-80] The European Capital of Culture was also the occasion to unveil more than 600 million euros in new cultural infrastructure in Marseille and its environs, including the [MuCEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MuCEM) designed by [Rudy Ricciotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Ricciotti).

Early on, immigrants came to Marseille locally from the surrounding Provence region. By the 1890s immigrants came from other regions of France as well as Italy.[^cite_note-\:1-81] Marseille became one of Europe's busiest ports by 1900.[^cite_note-\:0-75] Marseille has served as a major port where immigrants from around the Mediterranean arrive.[^cite_note-\:1-81] Marseille continued to be multicultural. Armenians from the Ottoman Empire began arriving in 1913. In the 1930s, Italians settled in Marseille. After World War II, a wave of Jewish immigrants from North Africa arrived. In 1962, a number of French colonies gained their independence, and the French citizens from Algeria, Morocco, and Tunisia arrived in Marseille.[^cite_note-\:2-82] The city had an economic downturn and lost many jobs. Those who could afford to move left and the poorest remained. For a while, the mafia appeared to run the city, and for a period of time the communist party was prominent.[^cite_note-\:2-82]

Multi-cultural Marseille can be observed by a visitor at the market at Noailles, also called Marché des Capucins, in old town near the Old Port. There, Lebanese bakeries, an African spice market, Chinese and Vietnamese groceries, fresh vegetables and fruit, shops selling couscous, shops selling Caribbean food are side by side with stalls selling shoes and clothing from around the Mediterranean. Nearby, people sell fresh fish and men from Tunisia drink tea.[^cite_note-\:2-82]

Although most Armenians arrived after the [Armenian Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Genocide), Armenians had a long presence even before the 20th and late 19th centuries. Armenians, having an extensive trade network worldwide, massively traded with Marseille and its port. Most notably, during the 16th century, and after the Armenians gained a monopoly over Iranian silk, which was granted to them by Shah Abbas of Iran, the trade flow of Armenians of Marseille increased tremendously. Merchants of Armenian origin received trade privileges in France by Armand Jean du Plessis de Richelieu (1585–1642) and later on Jean-Baptiste Colbert (1619–1683) Marseille a free port in 1669. One notable Armenian-Iranian merchant gained a patent from Louis XIV (1638–1715) over Iranian silk.[^cite_note-83] Armenians also became successful money-lenders and bankers in the city. Due to these policies and the multiculturalism of the city of Marseille, Armenians became very wealthy, and the legacy of the Armenians in the city still lives on.

Multiculturalism in Marseille is not without its challenges; the city still does not have an official mosque, despite some estimates for the Muslim population in Marseille being as high as 40%. In addition, the city is still heavily segregated, between the poor, majority-Muslim north, and the richer, less Muslim south. And, "according to the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development thinktank, Marseille is the most economically divided city in Europe."[^cite_note-84]

Languages spoken in Marseille include French, Arabic, Occitan,[^cite_note-85] and Marseillais.[^cite_note-86]

### Tarot de Marseille



[Marseille tarot card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot_of_Marseilles)

The most commonly used [tarot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot) deck takes its name from the city; it has been called the *[Tarot de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot_of_Marseille)* since the 1930s—a name coined for commercial use by the French cardmaker and cartomancer Paul Marteau, owner of B–P Grimaud. Previously this deck was called *Tarot italien* (Italian Tarot) and even earlier it was simply called Tarot. Before being *de Marseille*, it was used to play the local variant of tarocchi before it became used in [cartomancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartomancy) at the end of the 18th century, following the trend set by [Antoine Court de Gébelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Court_de_G%C3%A9belin). The name *Tarot de Marseille* (Marteau used the name *ancien Tarot de Marseille*) was used by contrast to other types of Tarots such as *Tarot de [Besançon](86918.md)*; those names were simply associated with cities where there were many cardmakers in the 18th century (previously several cities in France were involved in cardmaking).[^cite_note-87]

Another local tradition is the making of [santons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santon_\(figurine\)), small hand-crafted figurines for the traditional Provençal Christmas [creche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_scene). Since 1803, starting on the last Sunday of November, there has been a Santon Fair in Marseille; it is currently held in the Cours d'Estienne d'Orves, a large square off the Vieux-Port.

### Opera



The [Opéra de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_de_Marseille)

Marseille's main cultural attraction was, since its creation at the end of the 18th century and until the late 1970s, the [Opéra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra_de_Marseille). Located near the Old Port and the Canebière, at the very heart of the city, its [architectural style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_style) was comparable to the classical trend found in other opera houses built at the same time in [Lyon](8638634.md) and [Bordeaux](4097.md). In 1919, a fire almost completely destroyed the house, leaving only the stone [colonnade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnade) and [peristyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peristyle) from the original façade.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The classical façade was restored and the opera house reconstructed in a predominantly [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) style, as the result of a major competition. Currently the Opéra de Marseille stages six or seven operas each year.[^cite_note-90]

Since 1972, the [Ballet national de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_national_de_Marseille) has performed at the opera house; its director from its foundation to 1998 was [Roland Petit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Petit).

### Popular events and festivals

There are several popular festivals in different neighborhoods, with concerts, animations, and outdoor bars, like the Fête du Panier in June. On 21 June, there are dozens of free concerts in the city as part of France's [Fête de la Musique](745073.md), featuring music from all over the world. Being free events, many Marseille residents attend.

Marseille hosts a Gay Pride event in early July. In 2013, Marseille hosted [Europride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europride), an international [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) event, 10 July–20.[^cite_note-91] At the beginning of July, there is the International Documentary Festival.[^cite_note-92] At the end of September, the electronic music festival [Marsatac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsatac) takes place. In October, the *Fiesta des Suds* offers many concerts of world music.[^cite_note-93]

### Hip hop music

Marseille is also well known in France for its [hip hop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_hip_hop).[^cite_note-94] Bands like [IAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAM_\(band\)) originated from Marseille. - [Bouillabaisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouillabaisse) is the most famous seafood dish of Marseille. It is a fish stew containing at least three varieties of very fresh local fish: typically red rascasse ([Scorpaena scrofa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpaena_scrofa)); [sea robin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_robin) (fr: *grondin*); and [European conger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_conger) (fr: *congre*).

  [^cite_note-95]

  &#x20;It can include gilt-head [bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bream) (fr: *dorade*); [turbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbot); [monkfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkfish) (fr: *lotte* or *baudroie*); [mullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullet_\(fish\)); or silver [hake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hake) (fr: *merlan*), and it usually includes shellfish and other seafood such as [sea urchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_urchin) (fr: *oursins*), [mussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussels) (fr: *moules*); [velvet crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_crab) (fr: *étrilles*); [spider crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider_crab) (fr: *araignées de mer*), plus potatoes and vegetables. In the traditional version, the fish is served on a platter separate from the broth.

  [^cite_note-96]

  &#x20;The broth is served with [rouille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouille), a mayonnaise made with egg yolk, olive oil, red bell pepper, saffron, and garlic, spread on pieces of toasted bread, or *croûtons*.

  [^cite_note-david-97]

  [^cite_note-98]

  &#x20;In Marseille, bouillabaisse is rarely made for fewer than ten people.

  [^cite_note-99]

- [Aïoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aioli) is a sauce made from raw garlic, lemon juice, eggs and olive oil, served with boiled fish, hard boiled eggs and cooked vegetables.

  [^cite_note-david-97]

- [Anchoïade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancho%C3%AFade) is a paste made from anchovies, garlic, and olive oil, spread on bread or served with raw vegetables.

  [^cite_note-david-97]

- [Bourride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourride) is a soup made with white fish (monkfish, European sea bass, whiting, etc.) and aïoli.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrott2007104-100]

- [Fougasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fougasse_\(bread\)) is a flat Provençal bread, similar to the Italian [focaccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focaccia). It is traditionally baked in a wood oven and sometimes filled with olives, cheese or anchovies.

- [Navette de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navette_de_Marseille?action=edit\&redlink=1) are, in the words of food writer [M. F. K. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._F._K._Fisher), "little boat-shaped cookies, tough dough tasting vaguely of orange peel, smelling better than they are."

  [^cite_note-101]

  &#x20;They are sold in one specific shop, dedicated to its fabrication since 1781 called "[le four des navettes](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four%20des%20navettes)".

- [Cade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farinata#French_variations) is chickpea flour boiled into a thick mush, allowed to firm up, then cut into blocks and fried.

  [^cite_note-102]

- [Pastis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastis) is an alcoholic beverage made with [aniseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anise) and spice. It is extremely popular in the region.

  [^cite_note-103]

- [Pieds paquets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieds_paquets) is a dish prepared from sheep's feet and offal.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrott2007104-100]

- [Pistou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistou) is a combination of crushed fresh basil and garlic with olive oil, similar to the Italian [pesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesto). The "soupe au pistou" combines pistou in a broth with pasta and vegetables.

  [^cite_note-david-97]

- [Tapenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapenade) is a paste made from chopped olives, capers, and olive oil (sometimes anchovies may be added).

### Marseille Dialect

The Marseille [dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridional_French), a variant of the French language influenced by [Provençal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven%C3%A7al_dialect), is renowned for its colloquial and melodious nature. It is characterized by an exaggerative style and also incorporates influences from Arabic, Italian, and other local dialects. Despite its colorful expressions, the dialect remains predominantly localized to the city of Marseille, with limited use beyond the region.[^cite_note-105]

Common expressions in the Marseille dialect include:

- **Fada**: Refers to a person who is considered eccentric, cheeky, or almost crazy.

- **Oh fan!**: An exclamation used to express surprise, annoyance, or frustration.

- **Minot**: A term for a child.

- **Peuchère**: An expression of sympathy or even pity, often used to convey empathy for someone.

- **Emboucaner**: To drive someone crazy or to irritate them.

Most of the attractions of Marseille (including shopping areas) are located in the 1st, 2nd, 6th and 7th arrondissements. These include:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrott2007251–253-106][^cite_note-highlights-107]

- The Old Port or Vieux-Port, the main harbour and marina of the city. It is guarded by two massive forts (Fort Saint-Nicolas and [Fort Saint-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint-Jean_\(Marseille\))) and is one of the main places to eat in the city. Dozens of cafés line the waterfront. The Quai des Belges at the end of the harbour is the site of the daily fish market. Much of the northern quayside area was rebuilt by the architect Fernand Pouillon after its destruction by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) in 1943.

- The [Hôtel de Ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Ville,_Marseille) (City Hall), a [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) building dating from the 17th century.

- The Centre Bourse and the adjacent Rue St Ferreol district (including Rue de Rome and Rue Paradis), the main shopping area in central Marseille.

- The [Porte d'Aix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porte_d'Aix), a [triumphal arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumphal_arch) commemorating French victories in the [Spanish Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Thousand_Sons_of_Saint_Louis).

- The Hôtel-Dieu, a former hospital in *Le Panier*, transformed into an [InterContinental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterContinental) hotel in 2013.

- [La Vieille Charité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Vieille_Charit%C3%A9) in *Le Panier*, an architecturally significant building designed by the Puget brothers. The central baroque chapel is situated in a courtyard lined with arcaded galleries. Originally built as an [alms house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almshouse), it is now home to an archeological museum and a gallery of African and Asian art, as well as bookshops and a café. It also houses the Marseille International Poetry Centre.

  [^cite_note-108]

- The [Cathedral of Sainte-Marie-Majeure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Cathedral) or La Major, founded in the fourth century, enlarged in the 11th century and completely rebuilt in the second half of the 19th century by the architects [Léon Vaudoyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Vaudoyer) and [Henri-Jacques Espérandieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri-Jacques_Esp%C3%A9randieu). The present day cathedral is a gigantic edifice in Romano-Byzantine style. A [romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) [transept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transept), [choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir_\(architecture\)) and [altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar) survive from the older medieval cathedral, spared from complete destruction only as a result of public protests at the time.

- The 12th-century [parish church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_church) of [Saint-Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_of_Rome) and adjoining 17th-century chapel of Sainte-Catherine, on the quayside near the cathedral.

- The [Abbey of Saint-Victor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Victor,_Marseille), one of the oldest places of [Christian worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_worship) in Europe. Its fifth-century [crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypt) and [catacombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs) occupy the site of a [Hellenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) burial ground, later used for [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity) martyrs and venerated ever since. Continuing a [medieval tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Magdalene#Speculations),

  [^cite_note-109]

  &#x20;every year at [Candlemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_Jesus_at_the_Temple) a [Black Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Madonna) from the crypt is carried in procession along Rue Sainte for a blessing from the archbishop, followed by a mass and the distribution of "navettes" and green [votive candles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_candle).

### Museums

In addition to the two in the Centre de la Vieille Charité, described above, the main museums are:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrott2007264–267-110]

The MuCEM is devoted to the history and culture of European and Mediterranean civilisations. The adjacent Villa Méditerranée, by [Stefano Boeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Boeri), an international centre for cultural and artistic interchange, is partially constructed underwater. - The Musée Regards de Provence, opened in 2013, is located between the Cathedral of Notre Dame de la Majeur and the Fort Saint-Jean. It now houses a permanent collection of historical artworks from Provence as well as temporary exhibitions.

## Transport

### International and regional transport



[Marseille Provence Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Provence_Airport), the fifth busiest in France



Motorways around Marseille

The city is served by an international airport, [Marseille Provence Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Provence_Airport), located in Marignane. The airport is the fifth busiest French airport, was known as the fourth most important European traffic growth in 2012.[^cite_note-125] An extensive network of motorways connects Marseille to the north and west ([A7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A7_autoroute)), Aix-en-Provence in the north ([A51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A51_autoroute)), Toulon ([A50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A50_autoroute)) and the [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) to the east ([A8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A8_autoroute)). It is the most populous city in the French Riviera.



Front view of [Marseille-Saint-Charles station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Saint-Charles_station) showing its stone exterior, arched windows and engraved architectural details.

[Gare de Marseille Saint-Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Saint-Charles_station) is Marseille's main railway station. It operates direct regional services to Aix-en-Provence, [Briançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian%C3%A7on), Toulon, [Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon), [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), [Montpellier](73369.md), [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), [Bordeaux](4097.md), [Nantes](67453.md), etc. Gare Saint-Charles is also one of the main terminal stations for the [TGV](57212.md) in the south of France making Marseille reachable in three hours from Paris (a distance of over 750 km) and just over one and a half hours from Lyon. There are also direct TGV lines to [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), Brussels, Nantes, [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) and [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) as well as [Eurostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar) services to [London](17867.md) (just in the summer) and Thello services to Milan (just one a day), via Nice and Genoa.

There is a new long-distance bus station adjacent to new modern extension to the Gare Saint-Charles with destinations mostly to other [Bouches-du-Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-du-Rh%C3%B4ne) towns, including buses to [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence), Cassis, [La Ciotat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ciotat) and [Aubagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubagne). The city is also served with 11 other regional trains stations in the east and the north of the city, including [Marseille-Blancarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Blancarde_station).

Marseille has a large [ferry terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry_slip), the *Gare Maritime*, with services to [Corsica](5714828.md), [Sardinia](29376.md), [Algeria](358.md) and [Tunisia](30188.md).

### Public transport



Metro and tramway network

Marseille is connected by the [Marseille Métro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Metro) train system operated by the *[Régie des transports de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9gie_des_transports_de_Marseille)* (RTM). It consists of two lines: Line 1 (blue) between Castellane and La Rose opened in 1977 and Line 2 (red) between Sainte-Marguerite-Dromel and Bougainville opened between 1984 and 1987. An extension of the Line 1 from Castellane to La Timone was completed in 1992, another extension from La Timone to La Fourragère (2.5 km (1.6 mi) and 4 new stations) was opened in May 2010. The Métro system operates on a turnstile system, with tickets purchased at the nearby adjacent automated booths. Both lines of the Métro intersect at Gare Saint-Charles and Castellane. Three [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) lines are under construction to better connect the Métro to farther places (Castellane -> Luminy; Capitaine Gèze – La Cabucelle -> Vallon des Tuves; La Rose -> Château Gombert – Saint Jérôme).



The new tramway



[SNCF BB 67400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNCF_Class_BB_67400) (567573) on the Viaduc de Corbières, near [L'Estaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Estaque) and towards the [Côte Bleue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_Bleue), 2006

An extensive [bus network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_bus_service) serves the city and suburbs of Marseille, with 104 lines and 633 buses. The three lines of the [tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramway_de_Marseille),[^cite_note-126] opened in 2007, go from the [CMA CGM Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMA_CGM_Tower) towards Les Caillols.

As in many other French cities, a bike-sharing service nicknamed "Le vélo", free for trips of less than half an hour, was introduced by the city council in 2007.[^cite_note-127]

A free ferry service operates between the two opposite quays of the Old Port. From 2011 ferry shuttle services operate between the Old Port and Pointe Rouge; in spring 2013 it will also run to l'Estaque.[^cite_note-128] There are also ferry services and boat trips available from the Old Port to [Frioul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frioul_archipelago), the [Calanques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calanques) and Cassis.

## Sport



The Stade Vélodrome, home of [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille)

The city boasts a wide variety of sports facilities and teams. The most popular team is the city's [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_team), [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), which was the finalist of the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) in 1991, before becoming the first French winner of the competition in 1993. The club also became finalists of the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) in 1999, 2004 and 2018. The club had a history of success under then-owner [Bernard Tapie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Tapie). The club's home, the [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome), which can seat around 67,000 people, also functions for other local sports, as well as the [national rugby team](955656.md). Stade Velodrome hosted a number of games during the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), [2007 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rugby_World_Cup), [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) and [2023 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rugby_World_Cup). The local rugby teams are [Marseille XIII Avenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_XIII_Avenir) and [Marseille Vitrolles Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Vitrolles_Rugby).

Marseille is famous for its important [pétanque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9tanque) activity, it is even renowned as the *pétanque capitale*.[^cite_note-129] In 2012 Marseille hosted the Pétanque World Championship and the city hosts every year the [Mondial la Marseillaise de pétanque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondial_la_Marseillaise_de_p%C3%A9tanque), the main pétanque competition.



Match Race France 2008

Sailing is a major sport in Marseille. The wind conditions allow regattas in the warm waters of the Mediterranean. Throughout most seasons of the year it can be windy while the sea remains smooth enough to allow sailing. Marseille has been the host of 8 (2010) Match Race France events which are part of the [World Match Racing Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Match_Racing_Tour). The event draws the world's best sailing teams to Marseille. The identical supplied boats (J Boats J-80 racing yachts) are raced two at a time in an on the water dogfight which tests the sailors and skippers to the limits of their physical abilities. Points accrued count towards the World Match Racing Tour and a place in the final event, with the overall winner taking the title ISAF World Match Racing Tour Champion. Match racing is an ideal sport for spectators in Marseille, as racing in close proximity to the shore provides excellent views. The city was also considered as a possible venue for 2007 [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup).[^cite_note-130]

[CN Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CN_Marseille) has traditionally been one of France's dominant [Water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo) teams as it won the [Championnat de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championnat_de_France_\(water_polo\)) a total of 36 times.

Marseille is also a place for other [water sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_water_sports) such as [windsurfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsurfing) and [powerboating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerboating). Marseille has three [golf courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course). The city has dozens of gyms and several public swimming pools. Running is also popular in many of Marseille's parks such as Le Pharo and Le Jardin Pierre Puget. An annual footrace is held between the city and neighbouring Cassis: the [Marseille-Cassis Classique Internationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Cassis_Classique_Internationale).

The [2025 Tour de la Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Tour_de_la_Provence) began in Marseille with stage 1 on 14 February 2025.
