**Alexandra Maniego Eala**[^cite_note-1] (born May 23, 2005) is a Filipino professional [tennis](29773.md) player. She reached a career-high ranking of world No. 18 by the [Women's Tennis Association](57768.md) (WTA) in August 2026, making her the highest ranking Filipino and [Southeast Asian](28741.md) in [WTA Tour](1398173.md) history.[^cite_note-2] She is the first Filipino to reach top 20, defeat top 10 opponents, reach the fourth round of a [major](197638.md#Tournaments), reach the semifinals of a WTA 1000 event, and win a WTA Tour title.

Eala reached a [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_junior_circuit) combined ranking of world No. 2 on October 6, 2020, and became the first Filipino to win a junior major title, winning the [girls' singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles) title at the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_\(tennis\)). She rose to prominence when she defeated [Iga Świątek](51838558.md) as part of her semifinal run at the [2025 Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as a wildcard, followed by her first WTA Tour final appearance at the [Eastbourne Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Eastbourne_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). Following two [WTA 125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_125) titles (at the [2025 Guadalajara Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Guadalajara_125_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) and the [2026 Birmingham Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Birmingham_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)), she reached the fourth round of the [2026 Wimbledon Championships](83354904.md). Eala had her breakthrough by winning her first WTA Tour title at the [Washington Open](83780909.md), which propelled her to breaking into the top 20 in the [WTA rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_rankings).

## Early life and background

Alexandra Maniego Eala was born on May 23, 2005, in [Quezon City](284223.md), Philippines, to Michael Eala, a business executive,[^cite_note-3] and [Rosemarie Maniego-Eala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizza_Maniego-Eala), [Globe Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Telecom) chief financial officer until 2024, and a former national swimmer and bronze medalist in the [100-meter backstroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatics_at_the_1985_Southeast_Asian_Games) at the [1985 SEA Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_SEA_Games). Her paternal uncles are [Noli Eala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noli_Eala), former [Philippine Sports Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sports_Commission) chairperson and [Philippine Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Basketball_Association) commissioner,[^cite_note-Philstar-4][^cite_note-5] and fashion designer Rhett Eala.[^cite_note-6] Her older brother, Michael Francis "Miko" Eala, played tennis for the [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) [Nittany Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions) from 2020 to 2024.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-PEP2-8]

Eala began playing tennis at age four,[^cite_note-9] introduced to the sport by her maternal grandfather, Roberto Maniego, who was also her first coach.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

She attended the Immaculate Conception Academy in [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Metro_Manila) and [Colegio San Agustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colegio_San_Agustin_%E2%80%93_Makati) in [Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati) before joining the [Rafa Nadal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafa_Nadal_Academy) in [Manacor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manacor), Spain. She graduated from the academy in 2023.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## Junior years

In 2013, Eala won the U8[^cite_note-14] division of the Little Mo International Tennis Grand Slam Championship in Palm Beach Gardens, Florida, marking her first international junior success.[^cite_note-15] In 2015, she captured the U11[^cite_note-16] title at the Dubrovnik Dub Bowl in Croatia.[^cite_note-17] By 2017, Eala had risen to the top of the Asian Tennis Federation rankings and, alongside [Priska Nugroho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priska_Madelyn_Nugroho), was named Doubles Player of the Year by Tennis Europe.[^cite_note-EalaTimeline-18] In 2018, she made history at [Les Petits As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petits_As) in France, becoming the first wild card champion in the tournament's history, which earned a wildcard entry to the French Open juniors.[^cite_note-19]

In 2019, she helped the Philippines qualify for the ITF World Junior Tennis Finals for the first time in 26 years. Later that year, she debuted at the US Open juniors, becoming the first Filipino to qualify for a junior major main draw since 1991, and capped the season with a doubles crown at the Orange Bowl International Tennis Championships in Florida.[^cite_note-20] In 2020 she cracked the ITF World Juniors top 10, reaching No. 9 in the rankings. At the Australian Open, she reached the third round in jr. singles and won her first jr. major tournament doubles title with Nugroho.[^cite_note-21] Later that year, she advanced to the semifinals of the French Open jr. singles, climbing to world juniors rank No. 2—the highest by a Filipino since [Felix Barrientos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Barrientos) in 1985.[^cite_note-22]

In 2021, Eala continued her rise by winning her second junior major doubles title at the French Open with [Oksana Selekhmeteva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oksana_Selekhmeteva).[^cite_note-23] She followed with a singles‑doubles sweep at the [Trofeo Bonfiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofeo_Bonfiglio) in Milan, reached the second round at Wimbledon, and advanced to the quarterfinals of the US Open juniors.[^cite_note-WTAEALAProf-24] Then in 2022, she became the first Filipino to win a junior major singles title, defeating [Lucie Havlíčková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucie_Havl%C3%AD%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1) in the US Open final.[^cite_note-25]

## Professional

### 2020–2024: Five ITF Circuit championships



Eala training ahead of the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_\(tennis\))

Eala turned professional in March 2020, making her debut on the [ITF Women's World Tennis Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Women's_World_Tennis_Tour) at the W15 Monastir series in Tunisia, where she won her first match as a professional.[^cite_note-Rappler2020-26]

In January 2021, she captured her maiden ITF title at the W15 Manacor event in Spain, becoming the youngest and lowest-seeded junior reserve to do so.[^cite_note-27] The victory earned her entry into the [WTA rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_rankings), where she initially broke into the top 1000.[^cite_note-28] Later that year, she reached her first ITF doubles final at the W25 Platja d'Aro in Spain with partner Selekhmeteva,[^cite_note-29] and made her [WTA Tour](1398173.md) debut at the [Romanian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Winners_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where she became the first Filipino to win a tour-level match.[^cite_note-30]

Eala continued to gain exposure in 2022, receiving a wildcard into the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), though she exited in the opening round.[^cite_note-31] That year, she also secured her second ITF singles crown at the W25 Chiang Rai in Thailand.[^cite_note-it-32]

In 2023, she entered major competition for the first time, appearing in the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles_qualifying) qualifiers.[^cite_note-33] She had early exits at the [Thailand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Thailand_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), and [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-34] but claimed ITF titles at the W25 Yecla in Spain and the W25 Roehampton in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-it-32]

The 2024 season marked further progress, highlighted by her victory at the W100 [Open Araba en Femenino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Open_Araba_en_Femenino_%E2%80%93_Singles) in Spain—her biggest ITF singles title to date.[^cite_note-it-32] She also collected three ITF doubles trophies, partnering with [Darja Semeņistaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darja_Seme%C5%86istaja) to win the W50 Pune in India, and with [Estelle Cascino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_Cascino) to claim the W75 [Open de Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Open_de_Seine-et-Marne_%E2%80%93_Doubles) in [Croissy-Beaubourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croissy-Beaubourg), France and the W100 [Open Araba en Femenino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Open_Araba_en_Femenino_%E2%80%93_Doubles) in Spain.[^cite_note-dt-35] On the WTA Tour, Eala reached the quarterfinals of the [Veneto Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Veneto_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), her best result of the year,[^cite_note-36] and advanced to the semifinals of the [WTA 125 Canberra International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Canberra_Tennis_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles) doubles event with [Laura Pigossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Pigossi).[^cite_note-37]

In 2024, she was unable to progress beyond the qualifying rounds at all four Grand Slam tournaments.[^cite_note-WTAEALAProf-24] Her 2024 campaign included first round exits at [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), [Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Abu_Dhabi_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Guadalajara 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Guadalajara_Open_Akron_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Guangzhou_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Jiangxi_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), and second round of [Guadalajara 125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Guadalajara_125_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-WTAEALAProf-24]

### 2026: WTA 500 title, top 20

Eala opened her 2026 season at the [Auckland Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_ASB_Classic_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), reaching the semifinals in both singles and doubles; she was defeated by [Wang Xinyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xinyu) in singles, while in doubles she partnered with [Iva Jovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iva_Jovic) and lost to [Xu Yifan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Yifan) and [Yang Zhaoxuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Zhaoxuan).[^cite_note-AucklandSingles-57][^cite_note-AucklandDoubles-58] She later reached the quarterfinals of the inaugural WTA 125 [Philippine Women's Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Philippine_Women's_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as a wildcard, falling to eventual champion [Camila Osorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Osorio).[^cite_note-59] Her [Australian Open](81715708.md) debut ended in the first round of both singles and doubles, partnered with [Ingrid Martins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrid_Martins).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

Eala began her Middle East campaign with a strong showing at the [Abu Dhabi Open doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Abu_Dhabi_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles), reaching the semifinals alongside [Janice Tjen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janice_Tjen) before falling to [Tereza Mihalíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tereza_Mihal%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) and [Olivia Nicholls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Nicholls). In [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Abu_Dhabi_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) at the same event, she advanced to the quarterfinals before being stopped by [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] She later reached the quarterfinals of the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she was defeated by Gauff.[^cite_note-64] Her run at the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) ended in the first round.[^cite_note-65]

At the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Eala advanced to the fourth round, before falling to [Linda Nosková](67929086.md). In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), she and Jovic were eliminated in the opening round.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] She followed this with another fourth-round appearance at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she was defeated by [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md).[^cite_note-68]

Eala's clay performance peaked at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she reached the third round, before losing to [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md), while in doubles she partnered with [Hailey Baptiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailey_Baptiste) but was eliminated in the opening round.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] She also reached the second round in both singles and doubles at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), partnering with [Zeynep Sönmez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeynep_S%C3%B6nmez) in doubles,[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] while her other clay appearances ended in early-round defeats, including the second round of the [Linz Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Singles) and first-round exits at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Strasbourg International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Internationaux_de_Strasbourg_%E2%80%93_Singles), and the [French Open](83033049.md).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-WTAEALAProf-24]

Shifting to grass courts, Eala won her second WTA 125 singles title at the [Birmingham Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Birmingham_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), defeating [Nikola Bartůňková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Bart%C5%AF%C5%88kov%C3%A1) in the final. In the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Birmingham_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles) event, she partnered with Bartůňková but was eliminated in the opening round.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] She reached the second round of the [Queen's Club Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Queen's_Club_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in London before losing to Jovic.[^cite_note-76] At the [Berlin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Berlin_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), Eala entered as a wild card and defeated Rybakina and [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md) to advance to the semifinals, where she lost to eventual champion Nosková.[^cite_note-77] At the [Bad Homburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bad_Homburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), she was defeated in the first round of singles but partnered with [Venus Williams](167378.md) in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bad_Homburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles) to reach the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] At [Wimbledon](83354904.md), she defeated defending champion Świątek in the third round,[^cite_note-WIM26SwiUpset2-80] before losing in the fourth round to Paolini.[^cite_note-81] In doing so, she became the first Filipino player to reach the fourth round of a major singles tournament in the Open Era. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), she partnered with Bartůňková for a first-round exit result.[^cite_note-82]

At the [Washington Open](83780909.md), she captured her first WTA 500 title by defeating top seed Pegula in the final. En route to the championship, she beat [Zheng Qinwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen), defending champion Fernandez, Svitolina, and [Naomi Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Osaka).[^cite_note-83] Following a rain delay, Eala rallied from a set down before dominating the decider. With the victory, she became the first Filipino to win a WTA title and rose to a career-high No. 20 in the rankings.[^cite_note-84] At the Canadian Open, she reached the [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) fourth round, where she lost to [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic), ending a seven-match winning streak that spanned her Washington Open title run and continued into Toronto. She was also eliminated in the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles) first round alongside Williams.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she was defeated by [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova) in the third round.[^cite_note-88] At the [US Open](82729.md), Eala defeated [Mary Stoiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Stoiana) and [Oleksandra Oliynykova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandra_Oliynykova) before losing a tight three-set match to Jovic in the third round.[^cite_note-89]

Following her run in the US Open, she entered [Singapore Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Singapore_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as the third seed. She received a first-round bye before losing in the second round to [Tatiana Prozorova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatiana_Prozorova) in three sets.[^cite_note-90]

## National representation



Eala with her bronze medals from the [2021 SEA Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_SEA_Games)

Eala has represented the Philippines in several events. At the [2021 SEA Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2021_SEA_Games),[^cite_note-91] she won bronze in women's singles, team, and mixed doubles.[^cite_note-92] She repeated with bronzes in [women's singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [mixed doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Mixed_doubles) at the [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2022_Asian_Games).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

In 2024, she led [Team Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_Billie_Jean_King_Cup_team) to a 5–0 sweep in the [Billie Jean King Cup Asia/Oceania Group III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Billie_Jean_King_Cup), finishing undefeated and securing promotion to Group II.[^cite_note-95] She was not part of the national squad in 2025 or 2026. The 2024 roster included Eala together with [Marian Capadocia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Capadocia), [Khim Iglupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khim_Iglupas), and [Shaira Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaira_Rivera), while the following two years featured Stefi Aludo, [Tennielle Madis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennielle_Madis), Alexa Milliam, and Rivera.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

At the [2025 SEA Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2025_SEA_Games) in Thailand, she captured gold in women's singles, and bronzes in mixed doubles and women's team events, making her only the third Filipino ever—and the first in the 21st century—to win the gold medal in women's singles since [Maricris Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricris_Fernandez) in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_1999_SEA_Games).[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

Eala skipped the mandatory [2026 China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_China_Open), risking penalties from the WTA, to participate in the [2026 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2026_Asian_Games) in Aichi and Nagoya, Japan.[^cite_note-100] She lost to China's [Wang Xiyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xiyu) in the semifinals, ending her bid for the gold medal and an early qualification for the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md) in Los Angeles. Eala finished the tournament with a bronze medal.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

## Playing style

Eala is an aggressive baseliner, using her forehand to dictate rallies and her consistent two‑handed backhand to control exchanges.[^cite_note-103] Her left‑handed topspin pushes opponents deep, while her return game is a strength due to anticipation. Her serve remains her main weakness, often lacking pace and variation, though she excels on hard courts.[^cite_note-104] Analysts emphasize improving her serve, adding tactical variety, and building physical strength as key to further success.

Former pros [Greg Rusedski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Rusedski), [Andrea Petkovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Petkovic), and [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova) all highlight the need for more power, reliability, and variation in Eala's serve, alongside greater conditioning.[^cite_note-105]

### Coaches

Eala has worked with several coaches during her career, many of them associated with the Rafa Nadal Academy. Daniel Gomez coached Eala during her formative years. His work emphasized consistency, mental focus, and physical conditioning, which provided the foundation for her transition to the professional circuit.[^cite_note-Coach1-106] Among her early mentors at the academy, Joan Bosch accompanied Eala during her run to the semifinals of the 2025 Miami Open and later at Wimbledon 2026, where he highlighted the importance of discipline and continuous training in Grand Slam competitions.[^cite_note-Coach2-107][^cite_note-Coach3-108]

Alexandro "Sandro" Viaene became her main coach ahead of the 2025 season. He oversaw her pre‑season training and guided her attempt to qualify for the Australian Open, with an approach that emphasized structured preparation and conditioning for the demands of the WTA Tour.[^cite_note-Coach2-107] [Toni Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Nadal) joined her team during the 2025 Miami Open. Eala described his involvement as a "blessing," citing his experience from coaching Rafael Nadal as valuable to her development, particularly in discipline and adaptability.[^cite_note-Coach1-106][^cite_note-109] She has also worked with Lluc Bauzá, a medical fitness coach from the Rafa Nadal Academy, who supervised conditioning before her Wimbledon 2026 match against Iga Świątek.[^cite_note-Coach3-108]

## Sponsorships and endorsements

Eala signed her first endorsement deal at eight years old, as an ambassador for Filipino telecommunications company [Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Telecom).[^cite_note-110] Starting from her junior career, she has been sponsored by French tennis brand [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat).[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] In 2019, she signed a sponsorship deal with [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.).[^cite_note-113] In 2022, Eala was announced as an endorser for Filipino bank [BPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_the_Philippine_Islands).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] She has appeared on fashion spreads and magazine covers, including those of the November 2022 issue of *[Vogue Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_\(magazine\))* and the January 2025 issue of *[Tatler Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatler_Asia)*.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

In July 2025, for her Wimbledon debut, Nike gifted Eala with a hair tie designed in the form of a [sampaguita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasminum_sambac) blossom, the [national flower of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_the_Philippines#List_of_official_national_symbols).[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] During the same month, Eala was announced as a brand ambassador for Filipino juice brand [Locally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NutriAsia).[^cite_note-120] In August, Nike released an Eala-inspired limited edition shirt designed by Filipino artist Georgina Camus, featuring the "national flower of the Philippines overlaid on the [All England Lawn Tennis Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_England_Lawn_Tennis_and_Croquet_Club)'s grass courts."[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

In February 2026, she became a brand ambassador for [Milo Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_\(drink\)).[^cite_note-123] In August, she was announced as the face of [Marriott Bonvoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriott_International#Loyalty_program_%E2%80%93_Marriott_Bonvoy)'s "Tennis The World" campaign in the Asia Pacific (APEC) region (excluding China), which features early Australian Open 2027 ticket access and other custom member experiences.[^cite_note-124] In the same month, she was also announced as [Panasonic Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic) ambassador.[^cite_note-125]

## Personal life

Eala's favorite comfort food is [caldereta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaldereta), while she prefers [matcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matcha) over coffee.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] She speaks English and Spanish, in addition to her native [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language). [Wimbledon](91195.md) is her favorite Grand Slam tournament, and says its [Centre Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Court) is the most beautiful tennis court.[^cite_note-128] She cites [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) and [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) as her biggest influences.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

Her breakthrough at the 2025 Miami Open gave rise to her status as one of the Philippines’ leading sports figures, with many drawing parallels to [Manny Pacquiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Pacquiao),[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] although Eala has downplayed such comparisons.[^cite_note-133] By addressing fans and supporters in [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) during some of her post-match interviews, she helped spark greater interest in tennis among Filipinos both at home and across the [diaspora](2257094.md).[^cite_note-mo-134] Eala has been noted for drawing large crowds to her matches.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

## Awards and accolades



President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) honors Eala with a presidential citation in July 2026

Eala has been recognized by the Philippine Sportswriters Association as an eight-time honoree (2019–2026) for her "outstanding achievements in tennis" at the [PSA Annual Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSA_Annual_Awards).[^cite_note-137] In 2021, *[Tatler Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatler_Asia)* included Eala in its annual list of Asia's most influential people.[^cite_note-138] In April 2025, she was awarded the *Premios Tanglaw* trophy by the Philippine embassy in Madrid for her contributions in strengthening [Philippines–Spain relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines%E2%80%93Spain_relations).[^cite_note-139] The following year, she was recognized by the 2026 editions of the [*Time 100 Next*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100)[^cite_note-140] and *[Forbes Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Asia)*'s [30 Under 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_30_Under_30) icons in entertainment and sports for elevating the Philippines in global tennis.[^cite_note-141] She received a presidential citation at the [Malacañang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaca%C3%B1ang_Palace) in Manila on July 13, 2026, in recognition of her achievements at the 2026 Wimbledon tournament.[^cite_note-142]
