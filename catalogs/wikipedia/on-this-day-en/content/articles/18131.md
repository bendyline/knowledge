**Los Angeles International Airport**[^cite_note-7] ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **LAX**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **KLAX**, [FAA](11186.md) [LID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_identifier): **LAX**) is the primary [international airport](1519705.md) serving [Los Angeles](18110.md) and its [surrounding metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles), in the U.S. state of [California](5407.md). LAX is located in the [Westchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood of the [City of Los Angeles](18110.md), 18 miles (29 km; 16 nmi) southwest of [downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), with the commercial and residential areas of Westchester to the north, the city of [El Segundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Segundo,_California) to the south, and the city of [Inglewood](107640.md) to the east. LAX is the closest airport to [the Westside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westside_\(Los_Angeles_County\)) and the [South Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bay_\(Los_Angeles_County\)).

The airport is operated by [Los Angeles World Airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_World_Airports) (LAWA), a branch of the [Los Angeles city government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Los_Angeles), that also operates the [Van Nuys Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Nuys_Airport) for general aviation. The airport covers 3,500 acres (1,400 ha) of land and has four parallel runways.[^cite_note-FAA-4][^cite_note-8]

In 2023, LAX handled 75,050,875 passengers, making it the [world's eleventh-busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic), according to the [Airports Council International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Council_International) rankings.[^cite_note-9] In 2024, LAX served 76,587,980 passengers, a 2.04% increase from 2023. As the largest and busiest international airport on the [West Coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States), LAX is a major international gateway for the country, serving as a connection point for passengers traveling internationally (such as East and Southeast Asia, [Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia), Mexico, and Central America).

The airport holds the record for the world's busiest origin and destination airport,[^cite_note-10] because relative to other airports, many more travelers begin or end their trips in Los Angeles than use it as a connection. In 2019, LAWA reported approximately 88% of travelers at LAX were origination and destination passengers, and 12% were connecting.[^cite_note-11] It is also the only airport to rank among the top five U.S. airports for both passenger and cargo traffic.[^cite_note-12] LAX serves as a [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub), [focus city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_city), or operating base for more passenger airlines than any other airport in the United States.

Although LAX is the busiest airport in the Greater Los Angeles area, [several other airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Los_Angeles_area) serve the region including [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Burbank_Airport), [John Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport) (Orange County), [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Airport), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_International_Airport), and [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_International_Airport).

## History

Main article: [History of Los Angeles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Los_Angeles_International_Airport)

In 1926, the [Los Angeles City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_City_Council) and the [Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_commerce) recognized the need for the city to have its own airport to tap into the fledgling, but quickly growing, aviation industry. Several locations were considered, but the final choice was a 640-acre (1 sq mi; 259 ha) field in the southern part of [Westchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester,_California). The location had been promoted by real estate agent William W. Mines, and **Mines Field** as it was known had already been selected to host the 1928 National Air Races. On August 13, 1928 the city leased the land and the newly formed Department of Airports began converting the fields, once used to grow [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), and [lima beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phaseolus_lunatus), into dirt landing strips.[^cite_note-2001mp-13]

The airport opened on October 1, 1928[^cite_note-Daily_Breeze-14] and the first structure, [Hangar No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_One_\(Los_Angeles\)), was erected in 1929. The building still stands at the airport, remaining in active use and listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).[^cite_note-15] Over the next year, the airport started to come together: the dirt runway was replaced with an all-weather surface and more hangars, a restaurant, and a control tower were built. On June 7, 1930, the facility was dedicated and renamed Los Angeles Municipal Airport.[^cite_note-2001mp-13]

The airport was used by private pilots and flying schools, but the city’s vision was that Los Angeles would become the main passenger hub for the area. However, the airport failed to entice any carriers away from the established [Burbank Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank_Airport) or the [Grand Central Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Airport_\(California\)) in Glendale.[^cite_note-2001mp-13]

[World War II](32927.md) put a pause on any further development of the airport for passenger use. Before the United States entered the war, the aviation manufacturers located around the airport were busy providing aircraft for the [Allied powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II), while the flying schools found themselves in high demand. In January 1942, the military assumed control of the airport, stationing fighter planes there, and building naval gun batteries in the ocean dunes to the west.[^cite_note-2001mp-13]

Meanwhile, airport managers published a master plan for the land and, in early 1943, convinced voters to back a \$12.5 million bond for airport improvements. With a plan and funding in place, the airlines were finally convinced to make the move.

After the end of the war, four temporary terminals were quickly erected on the north side of the airport and, on December 9, 1946, [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines), [Trans World Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_World_Airlines) (TWA), [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines), [Southwest Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airways), and [Western Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Airlines) began passenger operations at the airport, with [Pan American Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am) (Pan Am) joining the next month.[^cite_note-Daily_Breeze-14][^cite_note-2001mp-13] The airport was renamed Los Angeles International Airport in 1949.[^cite_note-SearchHistory-17]

The temporary terminals remained in place for 15 years but quickly became inadequate, especially as air travel entered the "[jet age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_age)" and other cities invested in modern facilities. Airport leaders once again convinced voters to back a \$59 million bond on June 5, 1956.

The current layout of the passenger facilities was established in 1958 with a plan to build a series of terminals and parking facilities, arranged in the shape of the letter U, in the central portion of the property. The original plan called for the terminal buildings to be connected at the center of the property by a huge steel-and-glass dome. The dome was never built, but a smaller [Theme Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_Building), constructed in the central area, became a focal point for people coming to the airport. The structure was dedicated on June 25, 1961, by [Vice President](32759.md) [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson).[^cite_note-18]



[Continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Airlines) passengers arriving at LAX, 1962

The first of the new passenger buildings, Terminals 7 and 8, were opened for [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) on June 25, 1961, following opening festivities that lasted several days.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Terminals 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 opened later that same year.

There was a major expansion of the airport in the early 1980s, ahead of the [1984 Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympic_Games). In November 1983, a second-level roadway was added,[^cite_note-21] Terminal 1 opened in January 1984[^cite_note-22] and the Tom Bradley International Terminal opened in June 1984.[^cite_note-23] The original terminals also received expansions and updates in the 1980s.

Since 2008, the airport has been undergoing another major expansion. All of the terminals are being refurbished, and the Tom Bradley International Terminal was substantially rebuilt, with a West Gates satellite concourse added.[^cite_note-24] Outside of the terminal area, the [LAX West Intermodal Transportation Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_West_Intermodal_Transportation_Facility) with 4,300 parking spaces opened in 2021, replacing the former Lot C.[^cite_note-25] A new [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center) and an [LAX Rental Car Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_Rental_Car_Center) (RCC) were built. All will be connected to the terminal area by [SkyLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyLink_\(Los_Angeles_International_Airport\)).[^cite_note-Russell_2014-26] Altogether, those projects are expected to cost \$30 billion and bring LAX's total gates from 146 to 182.[^cite_note-Wells_2019-27][^cite_note-Wait_2024-28] [Ultra low-cost carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_low-cost_carrier) say project costs have made operations at LAX unsustainable under their low-fare model, with fees hitting around \$50 per departing passenger. As a result, [Allegiant Air](1372018.md) is shutting its LAX crew base and cutting several routes.[^cite_note-Snyder_2025-29]

### The "X" in LAX

Before the 1930s, US airports used a two-letter abbreviation and "LA" served as the designation for Los Angeles Airport.[^cite_note-30] In 1947, with rapid growth in the aviation industry, the identifiers were expanded to three letters, and "LA" received an extra letter to become "LAX". The "X" does not have any specific meaning.[^cite_note-31] "LAX" is also used for the [Port of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Los_Angeles) in [San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro,_Los_Angeles) and by [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) for [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Los_Angeles\)) in [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles).

LAX is located with the Pacific Ocean to the west and residential communities on all other sides. Since 1972, Los Angeles World Airports has adopted a "Preferential Runway Use Policy" to minimize noise levels in the communities closest to LAX.[^cite_note-PrefRunwayUse-32]

Typically, the loudest operations at an airport are from departing aircraft, with engines operating at high power, so during daytime hours (6\:30am to midnight), LAX prefers to operate under the "Westerly Operations" air traffic pattern, named for the prevailing west winds. Under "Westerly Operations", departing aircraft take off to the west, over the ocean, and arriving aircraft approach from the east. To reduce noise to areas north and south of the airport, LAX prefers to use the "inboard" runways (06R/24L and 07L/25R) for departures, closest to the central terminal area and further from residential areas, and the "outboard" runways for arrivals. Historically, over 90% of flights have used the "inboard" departures and "outboard" arrivals scheme.[^cite_note-PrefRunwayUse-32]

During night-time hours, when there are fewer aircraft operations and residential areas tend to be more noise sensitive, additional changes are made to reduce noise. Between 10pm and 7am, air traffic controllers try to use the "outboard" runways as little as possible and, between midnight and 6\:30am, the air traffic pattern shifts to "Over-Ocean Operations", under which departing aircraft continue to take off to the west, but arriving aircraft also approach from the west, over the ocean.[^cite_note-PrefRunwayUse-32]

There are times when the Over-Ocean and Westerly operations are not possible, particularly when the winds originate from the east, typically during inclement weather and when [Santa Ana winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_winds) occur. In those cases, the airport shifts to the non-preferred "Easterly Operations" air traffic pattern, under which departing aircraft take off to the east, and arriving aircraft approach from the west.[^cite_note-PrefRunwayUse-32]



An [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380) departing from runway 25R in the South Airfield Complex, with widebody aircraft at the gates of the [Tom Bradley International Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Bradley_International_Terminal) behind

The South Airfield Complex tends to see more operations than the North, because there are a larger number of passenger gates and air cargo operations areas on the south side of the airport grounds.[^cite_note-PrefRunwayUse-32] In 2007, the southernmost runway (07R/25L) was moved 55 feet (17 m) to the south to accommodate a new central taxiway.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Runways in the North Airfield Complex are separated by 700 feet (210 m).[^cite_note-35] There were plans to increase the separation by 260 feet (79 m), which would have allowed a central taxiway between runways to have been built, but faced opposition from residents living north of LAX.[^cite_note-36] These plans were scrapped in 2016, in favor of lifting a gate cap at the airport and building a new park on the airport's north side.[^cite_note-37]

### Terminals

This section is an excerpt from [Terminals of Los Angeles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminals_of_Los_Angeles_International_Airport).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Terminals_of_Los_Angeles_International_Airport\&action=edit)]



Map of LAX showing Terminals 1 through 8, plus the Tom Bradley International Terminal (B) and the Regional Terminal (R)

Los Angeles International Airport has 161 gates in nine passenger terminals arranged in the shape of the letter U or a horseshoe. On the [landside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landside) of the airport, [LAX Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_Shuttle) route A buses allow passengers to move between all terminals. On the [airside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airside), various pedestrian corridors allow passengers to move between all terminals on foot without having to [exit and reenter airport security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport#Landside_and_airside_areas). Additionally,[^cite_note-Terminals_of_Los_Angeles_International_Airport_planopen-38] the airport will be served by [SkyLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyLink_\(Los_Angeles_International_Airport\)), which will connect terminals to one another on the [landside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landside), along with providing connections to the [LAX Rental Car Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_Rental_Car_Center), parking facilities, and the [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center), which is served by the [Los Angeles Metro Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Rail) system and public bus routes.[^cite_note-39] In addition to these terminals, there are 2 million square feet (190,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of cargo facilities.

### Theme Building

Main article: [Theme Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_Building)



The [Theme Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_Building) at LAX, built in 1961, is a [Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Historic-Cultural_Monument).

The distinctive [Theme Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_Building) in the [Googie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Googie_architecture) style was built in 1961 and resembles a flying saucer that has landed on its four legs. A restaurant with a sweeping view of the airport is suspended beneath two arches that form the legs. The [Los Angeles City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_City_Council) designated the building a [Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Historic-Cultural_Monument) in 1992. A \$4 million renovation, with [retro-futuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro-futurism) interior and electric lighting designed by [Walt Disney Imagineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Imagineering), was completed before the Encounter Restaurant opened there in 1997. The restaurant is no longer in business.[^cite_note-40] Visitors are able to take the elevator up to the observation deck of the "Theme Building", which had previously been closed after the [September 11, 2001 attacks](5058690.md) for security reasons.[^cite_note-41] A memorial to the victims of the 9/11 attacks is located on the grounds, as three of the four hijacked planes were originally destined for LAX.[^cite_note-42] The Bob Hope USO expanded and relocated to the first floor of the Theme Building in 2018.[^cite_note-43]

### Modernization projects

In 2017, [LAWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_World_Airports) adopted a plan to modernize LAX at a cost of \$30 billion.[^cite_note-Wait_2024-28] The improvements are included in either the Airfield & Terminal Modernization Project (ATMP) or the Landside Access Modernization Program (LAMP). These include terminal and runway improvements, which will "enhance the passenger experience, reduce overcrowding, and provide airport access to the latest class of very large passenger aircraft"; this will bring the number of LAX's total gates from 146 to 182.[^cite_note-Wells_2019-27]

## Statistics



An [Air Tahiti Nui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Tahiti_Nui) [Airbus A340-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340-300) and four [Airbus A380-800s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380-800) from [Lufthansa](97733.md), [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways), [Air France](14998012.md) and [Singapore Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Airlines) parked at Tom Bradley International Terminal in 2015.



An [Avianca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avianca) [Airbus A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321) with two [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) and one [JetBlue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue) aircraft in the background in 2017.

It is the [world's eighth-busiest airport by passenger traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) and [eleventh-busiest by cargo traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_cargo_traffic),[^cite_note-300] serving over 87 million passengers and 2 million tons of freight and mail in 2018. It is the busiest airport in the state of California, and the [fifth-busiest (2022) airport by passenger boardings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_the_United_States) in the United States. In terms of international passengers, the second busiest airport for international traffic in the United States, behind only JFK in New York City. The number of aircraft movements (landings and takeoffs) was 700,362 in 2017, the third most of any airport in the world.

## Ground transportation and access



LAX sign as seen near the entrance of the airport

### Transiting between terminals

In the secure area of the airport, tunnels or above-ground connectors link all the terminals except for the [regional terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminals_of_Los_Angeles_International_Airport#Regional_Terminal).

LAX Shuttle route A operates in a counter-clockwise loop around the Central Terminal Area, providing frequent service for connecting passengers. However, connecting passengers who use these shuttles must leave and then later re-enter security.

### LAX Shuttle routes



Shuttle Route M bus to [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center) at Terminal 1

LAX operates several shuttle routes to connect passengers and employees around the airport area:[^cite_note-308]

**Route A – Terminal Connector** operates in a counter-clockwise loop around the Central Terminal Area, providing frequent service for connecting passengers. However, connecting passengers who use these shuttles must leave and then later re-enter security.

**Route E – Economy Parking** connects the Central Terminal Area and the [West Intermodal Transportation Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_West_Intermodal_Transportation_Facility), the airport's economy parking garage.

**Route M – Metro Connector** connects the Central Terminal Area with the [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center) on the Metro [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) and [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) lines.

**Route X – LAX Employee Lots** connects the Central Terminal Area and the Employee Parking Lots. The route has three service patterns: the East Lot route only stops at Terminals 1, 2, 3, and B; the West Lot route only stops at Terminals 4, 5, 6, and 7; and the South Lot route stops at all terminals.

Several of these routes are expected to be discontinued following the opening of [SkyLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyLink_\(Los_Angeles_International_Airport\)), which will provide access to economy parking, the Metro station, and a relocated LAX employee parking lot on the [LAX Rental Car Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_Rental_Car_Center) rooftop.[^cite_note-309]

### FlyAway Bus

Main article: [FlyAway (bus)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlyAway_\(bus\))



[Flyaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlyAway_\(bus\)) bus at [Los Angeles Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Los_Angeles\))

The FlyAway bus is a nonstop motorcoach/shuttle service run by [LAWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_World_Airports), which provides scheduled service between LAX and [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Los_Angeles\)) in Downtown LA or the FlyAway terminal at the [Van Nuys Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Nuys_Airport) in the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley).[^cite_note-310]

FlyAway buses stop at every LAX terminal in a counter-clockwise direction, starting at terminal 1. The service hours vary based on the line, with most leaving on or near the top of the hour. Buses use the regional system of [high-occupancy vehicle lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-occupancy_vehicle_lane) and [high-occupancy toll lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-occupancy_toll_lane) ([Metro ExpressLanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_ExpressLanes)) to expedite their trips.

LAX Shuttle route M offers free connections between the Central Terminal Area and the [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center) on the [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) and [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)), about 1+1⁄2 miles (2.4 km) away.

[SkyLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyLink_\(Los_Angeles_International_Airport\)), currently under construction by LAWA, is a 2+1⁄4-mile (3.6 km) rail line that will connect the terminal area with long- and short-term parking facilities, a connection to the [Los Angeles Metro Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Rail) and other transit at the [LAX/Metro Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX/Metro_Transit_Center), and a [consolidated facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAX_Rental_Car_Center) for all airport rental car agencies.[^cite_note-311][^cite_note-312]

The APM project is estimated to cost \$5.5 billion and is scheduled to begin operation in 2026.[^cite_note-313][^cite_note-314][^cite_note-315][^cite_note-urbanize.la-316]

## Other facilities



Hotels next to LAX

The airport has the administrative offices of [Los Angeles World Airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_World_Airports).[^cite_note-323]

[Continental Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Airlines) once had its corporate headquarters on the airport property. At a 1962 press conference in the office of [Mayor of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Los_Angeles) [Sam Yorty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Yorty), Continental Airlines announced that it planned to move its headquarters to Los Angeles in July 1963.[^cite_note-324] In 1963 Continental Airlines headquarters moved to a two-story, \$2.3 million building on the grounds of the airport.[^cite_note-325][^cite_note-326] The July 2009 *[Continental Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Magazine)* issue stated that the move "underlined Continental Airlines western and Pacific orientation".[^cite_note-TheCompany-327] On July 1, 1983 the airline's headquarters were relocated to the [America Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_General_Center) in the [Neartown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neartown,_Houston) area of [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas).[^cite_note-328]

In addition to [Continental Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Airlines), [Western Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Airlines) and [Flying Tiger Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Tiger_Line) also had their headquarters at LAX.[^cite_note-329][^cite_note-330]

### Flight Path Museum LAX

The Flight Path Museum LAX, formerly known as the Flight Path Learning Center,[^cite_note-331] is a museum located at 6661 Imperial Highway and was formerly known as the "West Imperial Terminal". This building used to house some charter flights. It sat empty for 10 years until it was re-opened as a learning center for LAX.

The center contains information on the history of aviation, several pictures of the airport, as well as aircraft scale models, flight attendant uniforms, and general airline memorabilia such as playing cards, china, magazines, signs, and a [TWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_World_Airlines) gate information sign.

The museum's library contains an extensive collection of rare items such as aircraft manufacturer company newsletters/magazines, technical manuals for both military and civilian aircraft, industry magazines dating back to World War II and before, historic photographs and other invaluable references on aircraft operation and manufacturing.[^cite_note-332]

The museum has on display "The Spirit of Seventy-Six," a [DC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-3) that flew in commercial airline service, before serving as a corporate aircraft for [Union 76 Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Oil_Company) for 32 years. The plane was built in the [Douglas Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Aircraft_Company) plant in [Santa Monica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California) in January 1941, which was a major producer of both commercial and military aircraft.[^cite_note-333]

## Accidents and incidents



LAX Airport Response Coordination Center, used to coordinate emergency response

During its history there have been numerous incidents, but only the most notable are summarized below:[^cite_note-334]

### 1930s

- On January 23, 1939, the sole prototype [Douglas 7B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-20_Havoc) twin-engine attack bomber, designed and built as a company project, suffered a loss of the vertical fin and rudder during a demonstration flight over Mines Field, flat spun into the parking lot of [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation), and burned. Another source states that the test pilot, in an attempt to impress the Gallic passenger, attempted a snap roll at low altitude with one engine feathered, resulting in a fatal spin.

  [^cite_note-335]

  &#x20;Douglas test pilot Johnny Cable bailed out at 300 feet, his chute unfurled but did not have time to deploy, he was killed on impact, the flight engineer John Parks rode in the airframe and died, but 33-year-old French Air Force Capt. Paul Chemidlin, riding in the aft fuselage near the top turret, survived with a broken leg, severe back injuries, and a slight concussion. The presence of Chemidlin, a representative of a foreign purchasing mission, caused a furor in Congress by isolationists over neutrality and export laws. The type was developed as the [Douglas DB-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DB-7).

  [^cite_note-336]

### 1940s

- On June 1, 1940, the first [Douglas R3D-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-5) for the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md), BuNo *1901*, crashed at Mines Field, before delivery. The Navy later acquired the privately owned [DC-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-5) prototype, from [William E. Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Boeing) as a replacement.

  [^cite_note-337]

- On November 20, 1940, the prototype [NA-73X Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-51_Mustang), *NX19998*,

  [^cite_note-338]

  &#x20;first flown October 26, 1940, by test pilot [Vance Breese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vance_Breese), crashed.

  [^cite_note-339]

  &#x20;According to P-51 designer [Edgar Schmued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Schmued), the NA-73 was lost because test pilot Paul Balfour refused, before a high-speed test run, to go through the takeoff and flight test procedure with Schmued while the aircraft was on the ground, claiming "one airplane was like another". After making two high speed passes over Mines Field, he forgot to put the fuel valve on "reserve" and during the third pass ran out of fuel. An emergency landing in a freshly plowed field caused the wheels to dig in, the aircraft flipped over, the airframe was not rebuilt, the second aircraft being used for subsequent testing.

  [^cite_note-340]

- On October 26, 1944, [WASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_Airforce_Service_Pilots) pilot [Gertrude Tompkins Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Tompkins_Silver) of the 601st Ferrying Squadron, fifth Ferrying Group, [Love Field, Dallas, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Love_Field), departed Los Angeles Airport, in a [North American P-51D Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_P-51_Mustang), *44-15669*,

  [^cite_note-341]

  &#x20;at 1600 hrs PWT, headed for the East Coast. She took off into the wind, into an offshore fog bank, and was expected that night at Palm Springs. She never arrived. Owing to a paperwork foul-up, a search did not get under way for several days, and while the eventual search of land and sea was massive, it failed to find a trace of Silver or her plane. She is the only missing WASP pilot. She had married Sgt. Henry Silver one month before her disappearance.

  [^cite_note-342]

### 1960s

- On January 13, 1969, [Scandinavian Airlines System Flight 933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Airlines_System_Flight_933), a [Douglas DC-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-8)-62, crashed into [Santa Monica Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Bay), approximately 6 nautical miles (11 km) west of LAX at 7\:21 pm, local time. The aircraft was operating as flight SK933, nearing the completion of a flight from [Seattle](11388236.md). Of nine crewmembers, three drowned, while 12 of the 36 passengers also drowned.

- On January 18, 1969, [United Airlines Flight 266](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines_Flight_266), a [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727)-100 bearing the registration number N7434U, crashed into [Santa Monica Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Bay) approximately 11.3 miles (18.2 km) west of LAX at 6\:21 pm local time. The aircraft was destroyed, resulting in the death of all 32 passengers and six crew members aboard.

### 1970s

- On the evening of June 6, 1971, [Hughes Airwest Flight 706](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Airwest_Flight_706), a [Douglas DC-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-9) jetliner that had departed LAX on a flight to Salt Lake City, Utah, was struck nine minutes after takeoff by a U.S. Marine Corps [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas) [F-4 Phantom II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-4_Phantom_II) fighter jet over the [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains). The midair collision killed all 44 passengers and five crew members aboard the DC-9 airliner and one of two crewmen aboard the military jet.

- On August 4, 1971, [Continental Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Airlines) Flight 712, a [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707), collided in midair with a [Cessna 150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_150) over [Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California). Although the Cessna was destroyed upon landing, there were no fatalities.

- On August 6, 1974, a [bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Los_Angeles_International_Airport_Bombing) exploded near the [Pan Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_World_Airways) ticketing area at Terminal 2; three people were killed and 35 were injured.

  [^cite_note-344]

- On March 1, 1978, two tires burst in succession on a [McDonnell Douglas DC-10-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10-10) on [Continental Airlines Flight 603](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Airlines_Flight_603) during its takeoff roll at LAX and the plane, bound for [Honolulu](13887.md), veered off the runway. A third tire burst and the DC-10's left landing gear collapsed, causing a fuel tank to rupture. Following the aborted takeoff, spilled fuel ignited and enveloped the center portion of the aircraft in flames. During the ensuing emergency evacuation, a husband and wife died when they exited the passenger cabin onto the wing and dropped down directly into the flames. Two additional passengers died of their injuries approximately three months after the accident; 74 others aboard the plane were injured, as were 11 firemen battling the fire.

- On the evening of March 10, 1979, [Swift Aire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_Aire_Lines) Flight 235, a twin-engine [Aerospatiale Nord 262A-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_N_262) turboprop en route to [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_California), was forced to [ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditching) in [Santa Monica Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Bay) after experiencing engine problems upon takeoff from LAX. The pilot, co-pilot, and a female passenger drowned when they were unable to exit the aircraft after the ditching. The female flight attendant and the three remaining passengers—two men and a pregnant woman—survived and were rescued by several pleasure boats and other watercraft in the vicinity.

### 1980s

- In January 1985, a woman was found dead in a suitcase that was lying on the [baggage carousel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baggage_carousel) for a while. She had been denied a US visa in West Germany and therefore decided to enter the US in this way.

- On August 31, 1986, [Aeroméxico Flight 498](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerom%C3%A9xico_Flight_498), a [DC-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-9) en route from [Mexico City](18987.md), Mexico, to Los Angeles, began its descent into LAX when a [Piper Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_Cherokee) collided with the DC-9's left [horizontal stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_\(aircraft\)) over [Cerritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerritos,_California), causing the DC-9 to crash into a residential neighborhood. All 67 people on the two aircraft were killed, in addition to 15 people on the ground. 5 homes were destroyed and an additional 7 were damaged by the crash and resulting fire. The Piper went down in a nearby schoolyard and caused no further injuries on the ground. As a result of this incident, the FAA required all commercial aircraft to be equipped with [Traffic Collision Avoidance System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_Collision_Avoidance_System) (TCAS).

### 1990s

The collision killed all 12 occupants of the SkyWest plane and 23 of the 89 people aboard the USAir 737.

## Aircraft spotting

The "Imperial Hill" area of [El Segundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Segundo,_California) is a prime location for [aircraft spotting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_spotting), especially for takeoffs. Part of the Imperial Hill area has been set aside as a city park, Clutter's Park.

Another popular spotting location sits under the final approach for runways 24 L\&R on a lawn next to the [Westchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester,_Los_Angeles) [In-N-Out Burger](452982.md) on Sepulveda Boulevard. This is one of the few remaining locations in Southern California from which spotters may watch such a wide variety of low-flying commercial airliners from directly underneath a flight path.

Another aircraft spotting location is at a small park in the take-off pattern that normally goes out over the Pacific. The park is on the east side of the street Vista Del Mar, from which it takes its name, Vista Del Mar Park.

### Space Shuttle *Endeavour*

At 12\:51 p.m. on Friday, September 21, 2012, a [Shuttle Carrier Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttle_Carrier_Aircraft) carrying the [Space Shuttle *Endeavour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Endeavour) landed at LAX on runway 25L.[^cite_note-379] An estimated 10,000 people saw the shuttle land. [Interstate 105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_105_\(California\)) was backed up for miles at a standstill. [Imperial Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Highway) was shut down for spectators. It was quickly taken off the Shuttle Carrier Aircraft, a modified Boeing 747, and was moved to a [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) hangar. The shuttle spent about a month in the hangar while it was prepared to be transported to the [California Science Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Science_Center).

Main article: [Los Angeles International Airport in popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_International_Airport_in_popular_culture)
