**Audrey Werro** (born 27 March 2004) is a Swiss [track and field](194904.md) [athlete](43719.md). She is the reigning [European champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_European_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) for the [800 metres](585165.md), having won the gold medal at the [2026 European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_European_Athletics_Championships) in Birmingham. That year, Werro won the [World Ultimate Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Ultimate_Championship_%E2%80%93_Women's_1500_metres) during an unbeaten outdoor season and ran the third fastest 800 m time in history with 1\:53.70 in Zurich, and also ran the fifth fastest time for the distance on a short track when winning the silver medal in the 800 m at the [2026 World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in Toruń.

At junior levels, she won the gold medal at the [2025 European U23 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_European_Athletics_U23_Championships), the silver medal at the [2022 World Under-20 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_U20_Championships), and gold medals at the [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_European_Athletics_U20_Championships) and [2023 European U20 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_European_Athletics_U20_Championships).

She is a nine-time gold medalist over 800 m at the Swiss Championships ([four outdoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Athletics_Championships) and [five indoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Indoor_Athletics_Championships)). She holds national records at senior level as well as the U18 and U20 levels.

## Early life

Audrey Werro was born on 27 March 2004[^cite_note-WAprofile-1] to a Swiss father, Claude, and an [Ivorian](57744.md) mother, Philomène. She has a sister and two brothers.[^cite_note-2] She is from [Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fribourg), in west Switzerland, and studied at nearby Gambach College.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Werro began athletics at the age of nine. She joined Club Athlétique Belfaux, and was coached by Christiane Berset Nuoffer, who continued as her coach into her professional career.[^cite_note-8]

## Career

### 2021

In June 2021, at age 17, Werro was runner-up in the [800 metres](585165.md) at the Swiss Championships, running 2\:02.88 to finish behind champion [Lore Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lore_Hoffmann).[^cite_note-9] She set new national U18 records over the [400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/400_metres) and 800 metres that year.[^cite_note-10] In July, she won the gold medal in the 800 m at the [European Under-20 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_European_Athletics_U20_Championships) held in [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), Estonia, competing against athletes up to two years her senior.[^cite_note-11]

### 2022

In February 2022, Werro broke the Swiss U23 indoor 400 m record by 0.84 s with a time of 53.03 seconds in [Magglingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magglingen).[^cite_note-12] She next became the national indoor champion over the 800 m, running 2\:04.95 also in Magglingen.[^cite_note-WAprofile-1] In June, she broke the Swiss U20 and U23 records in the outdoor event, previously held by [Delia Sclabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delia_Sclabas) (2\:01.29), with a time of 2\:00\:28 in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva).[^cite_note-13] The same month, Werro also won the Swiss 800 m outdoor title, clocking 2\:02.72 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich).[^cite_note-14]

In August, she won the silver medal in the event at the [World U20 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_U20_Championships) held in [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali), Colombia with an U23 national record of 1\:59.53, behind [Roisin Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roisin_Willis) for whom it was also the first time she ran sub-two minutes in an 800 m race.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The same month, Werro made her debut in the senior championships at the [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_Athletics_Championships) in [Munich](19058.md), where she was eliminated in the heats in 2\:06.34.[^cite_note-WAprofile-1] In December, she was nominated for [Radio SRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schweizer_Radio_und_Fernsehen) Best Talent Sport award.[^cite_note-17]

### 2023



Werro (second runner from the left) at [2022 Athletissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Athletissima)

In early February, aged 18 years, Werro ran a Swiss U23 indoor 800 m record of 2\:00.57 in [Val-de-Reuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Reuil), just 0.19 s off [Selina Rutz-Büchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selina_Rutz-B%C3%BCchel)'s national senior record.[^cite_note-18] This came six days after Werro had lowered Rutz-Büchel's Swiss indoor 600 m record to 1\:26.14.[^cite_note-19] In March, at the [European Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_European_Athletics_Indoor_Championships), held in [Istanbul](3391396.md), she finished fifth in the 800 m final in a time of 2\:00.91, 0.06 s away from bronze.[^cite_note-20]

In July, Werro won her second national title in the outdoor 800 m with a time of 2\:01.70 at the Swiss Championships in [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona). She was also the indoor champion over the 800 m for the second year in a row.[^cite_note-21] The following month, she attended the [European U20 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_European_Athletics_U20_Championships), held in [Jerusalem](16043.md), and won the gold medal in the 800 m for the second time since 2021.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

In August, Werro set a new 800 metres personal best at the [Diamond League](21778161.md) meeting in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), running 1\:59.50.[^cite_note-25] She competed at the [2023 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships) in [Budapest](36787.md) in August 2023 finishing 6th in her heat.[^cite_note-26] Shortly after the World Athletics Championships, Werro broke her own national U20 record by running 1\:58.13 at the [Galà dei Castelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A0_dei_Castelli) in [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona).[^cite_note-27]

### 2024

She competed in the 800 metres at the [World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres). She won her qualifying heat in a time of 2\:01.83. In the semi-final she ran a new indoor personal best time of 2\:00.16.[^cite_note-28]

She competed in the 800 metres at the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) in [Paris](22989.md) in August, being eliminated in the repechage round.[^cite_note-29] She set a new Swiss national record time of 1\:57.76 for the 800 metres in [Bellinzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellinzona) in September.[^cite_note-30]

### 2025

She was the fastest qualifier for the 800 metres final at the [European Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_European_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in [Apeldoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apeldoorn), Netherlands.[^cite_note-31] However, in the final she clashed with eventual medalist [Anita Horvat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anita_Horvat) and fell to the floor with the race carrying on ahead of her.[^cite_note-32] She reached the final of the 800 metres at the [World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing).[^cite_note-33] In the final, she ran a personal best and Swiss national indoor record of 1\:59.81 to finish in fourth place, 0.01 seconds behind bronze medal winner [Patricia Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Silva_\(runner\)) of Portugal.[^cite_note-34]



Werro competing at the [Zurich Diamond League Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Weltklasse_Z%C3%BCrich) in 2025

In May 2025, she ran a new 800 metres national record of 1\:57.25 at the World Athletics Continental Tour Gold meeting in [Bydgoszcz](74593.md).[^cite_note-35] She placed fourth in the 800 metres in June in Stockholm at the [BAUHAUS-galan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BAUHAUS-galan) event, part of the [Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Diamond_League).[^cite_note-36] She ran a 2\:34.88 personal best over 1000 metres in Monaco at the [Herculis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Herculis).[^cite_note-37] She ran a championship record of 1\:57.42 to win the gold medal in the 800 metres at the [European U23 Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_European_Athletics_U23_Championships) in Bergen, Norway.[^cite_note-38] The following month, she finished runner-up to [Keely Hodgkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keely_Hodgkinson) over 800 m in 1\:57.34 at the [Athletissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Athletissima) event in Lausanne and lowered her personal best to 1\:56.29 in winning the Swiss Championships.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] She ran an 800 m personal best 1\:55.91 to win the [Diamond League Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Weltklasse_Z%C3%BCrich) in Zurich ahead of [Georgia Hunter Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Hunter_Bell) on 28 August.[^cite_note-41]

In September, she was a finalist in the [women's 800 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) at the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships) in [Tokyo](30057.md), Japan, placing sixth in a time of 1\:56.17.[^cite_note-42]

### 2026

Werro opened her 2026 indoor season at the Meeting de l’Eure in [Val-de-Reuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Reuil), a World Athletics Indoor Tour Silver meeting on 1 February, winning over 800 m in 1\:57.49, taking more than two seconds off her own national indoor record. A few days later, she moved to eighth on the world all-time list with 1\:57.27 in Belgrade.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] On 19 February, she was runner up in [Liévin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A9vin), as Keely Hodgkinson set a new indoor 800 m world record.[^cite_note-45] Werro won the Swiss Indoor Championships over 800 metres on 1 March 2026, running 1\:57.98 in the final.[^cite_note-46] Competing in the 800 metres at the [World Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in [Toruń](31258.md), Poland, in March, Werro won her semi-final in 1\:59.27, before winning the silver medal behind Hodgkinson in the final in 1\:56.64, moving to fifth on the world all-time indoor list.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]



Werro winning the 800 m at the [2026 Weltklasse Zürich](84064248.md).

On 31 May, she ran a meeting record of 1\:56.56 to win the 800 metres at the [Diamond League](81417246.md) in [Rabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Meeting_International_Mohammed_VI_d'Athl%C3%A9tisme_de_Rabat).[^cite_note-49] On 7 June, at the [Stockholm Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bauhausgalan), Werro defeated Keely Hodgkinson in the 800 metres in a new Swiss record of 1\:53.98. This was the third fastest time in history, and the fastest for 43 years.[^cite_note-50] On 16 June, Werro won ahead of [Femke Broeders-Bol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femke_Broeders-Bol) in 1\:54.45 at the Golden Spike meeting in Ostrava.[^cite_note-51] On 28 June, at the [Paris Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Meeting_de_Paris), Werro improved her national record in the 800 metres to 1\:53.80.[^cite_note-52]

Competing at the [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_European_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) in Birmingham, England, on 13 August, Werro fell while leading in the final 120 metres of her semi-final. Having returned to her feet and slowing finishing the race, she was reinstated on appeal into the final having been adjudged to have fallen following inadvertent contact on her foot by the hand of French woman [Anaïs Bourgoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana%C3%AFs_Bourgoin) running behind her. She then won the final the following day in a championship record of 1\:54.81, finishing ahead of Hodgkinson and Broeders-Bol who had qualified from the other semi-final, with Werro controlling the pace for much of the final before pulling away in the final 100 metres.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] On 21 August, she set a new meeting record at the [Athletissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Athletissima) in Lausanne, winning in 1\:55.33 to finish ahead of Broeders-Bol and [Saida Tsehaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saida_Tsehaye).[^cite_note-55] On 27 August, she ran a new Diamond League record and personal best of 1\:53.70 to win the [2026 Weltklasse Zürich](84064248.md).[^cite_note-56] At the [2026 Diamond League Final](84136543.md) in Brussels on 5 September, Werro broke [Pamela Jelimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Jelimo)'s 18-year-old meeting record to claim the 800 metres title in a narrow win over Broeders-Bol, winning by 0.06 seconds in 1\:54.75.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] On 12 September, she won [the 800 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Ultimate_Championship_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres) in the inaugural [World Athletics Ultimate Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Ultimate_Championships) in Budapest in 1\:55.88 to complete an unbeaten outdoor season.[^cite_note-59]
