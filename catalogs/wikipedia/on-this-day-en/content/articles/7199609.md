Since the 1990s, the Republic of India and the State of Israel have had a comprehensive economic, military, and political relationship.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] In 1947, India voted against the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine), but nonetheless [recognized Israeli sovereignty](32359767.md) in 1950. Israel opened a consulate in [Bombay](19189.md) in 1953. Collaboration gradually increased as Israel became a key Indian ally amidst the [India–Pakistan conflict](142331.md); Israel supplied India with armaments, ammunition, and intelligence during the [Indo-Pakistani war of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_war_of_1971) and the [Indo-Pakistani war of 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) (in [Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil)). Full diplomatic relations were established in 1992, when India opened an embassy in [Tel Aviv](31453.md) and Israel opened an embassy in [New Delhi](51585.md). Both countries are members of the [I2U2 Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I2U2_Group), formed in October 2021,[^cite_note-3] and have stated that they have a strong bilateral relationship, sharing similarities in spirit and facing common challenges, increasingly cooperating in the industrial and technological sectors.[^cite_note-4]

By 2019, India was Israel's third-largest Asian trade partner and tenth-largest overall trade partner – bilateral trade, excluding military sales, stood at around US\$6.3 billion.[^cite_note-5] As of 2015, both countries are negotiating an extensive bilateral free-trade agreement, focusing on areas such as information technology, biotechnology, and agriculture.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-FTAECO-7] As of 2022, India is Israel's largest client for military equipment sales, and Israel is India's second-largest supplier of military equipment after [Russia](25391.md);[^cite_note-8] approximately 42.1% of all Israeli arms exports are received by India.[^cite_note-9] Between the [1999 Kargil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) and 2010, India has spent \$9 billion on defense purchases from Israel.[^cite_note-10] By 2022, Israel has spent \$3.2 billion on [crude oil](23195.md) and [diamonds](8082.md) from India.[^cite_note-11] Their strategic ties extend to joint military training as well as intelligence-sharing on the activity of various terrorist groups.[^cite_note-Boudreaux2008-12][^cite_note-ATON-13] Israel provided [humanitarian aid](434679.md) to India after the [2001 Gujarat earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Gujarat_earthquake).[^cite_note-14] Since the [2023 Hamas-led attack on Israel](74998317.md), India has reportedly been providing significant military assistance to Israel. One key area of support has been the provision of [Hermes 900 drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit_Hermes_900).[^cite_note-15] With increased [tension between Iran and Israel in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iran%E2%80%93Israel_conflict), there has been a geopolitical shift by India away from Iran and towards Israel.[^cite_note-thediplomat.com-16][^cite_note-indiatoday.in-17]

Israel is represented in India through an embassy in [New Delhi](51585.md) as well as consulates in [Mumbai](19189.md) and [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore). India is represented in Israel through an embassy in [Tel Aviv](31453.md); the Indian government does not currently recognize [Jerusalem](16043.md) as Israel's capital city (see [status of Jerusalem](4024423.md)). In 2009, an international study on the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md) revealed that around 58 percent of Indian respondents sympathized with Israel—the most positive opinion of any country surveyed.[^cite_note-Eichner2009-18] In 2023, a survey revealed that Israelis hold the most favorable views of India among all countries surveyed. According to the data, 71% of Israeli respondents expressed a favorable opinion of [India](14533.md), while 20% held unfavorable views and 9% did not express an opinion.[^cite_note-19]

## History

### Ancient relations

See also: [Achaemenid conquest of the Indus Valley § Indian tributes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_conquest_of_the_Indus_Valley#Indian_tributes)

Excavation at [Tel Megiddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Megiddo) shows evidence of [Indo-Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean) trade relations from mid-second millennium BCE between the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent)- and [southern Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Levant)-based on the presence of turmeric, banana, sesame, all originating from the Indian subcontinent.[^cite_note-20] Geographical analysis suggests that the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) ([Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament)) may be talking about India, where the trade of animals such as monkeys and peacocks existed.[^cite_note-Goodman1994-21]<sup>: 28 </sup> According to [Chaim Menachem Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Menachem_Rabin), the connection between ancient Israel and the Indian subcontinent was recorded during the reign of King [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) (10th century BCE) in [Book of Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books_of_Kings) 10.22.[^cite_note-22] Ancient trade and cultural communication between India and the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) is documented in the [Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea) and the accounts surrounding [Queen of Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Sheba) in the Hebrew Bible. Jews who have settled in [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi), [Kerala](4349459.md), trace their origin back to the time of King Solomon and are called [Cochin Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochin_Jews). Later on, [Paradesi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradesi_Jews) migrated to Kochi, Kerala, during the 15th and 16th centuries following the [expulsion of Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Jews_from_Spain) from the [Iberian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) (comprising both [Spain](26667.md) and [Portugal](23033.md)).

Trade relations can be traced back to 1,000 BCE and earlier to the time of the [Indus Valley Civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilization) of the Indian subcontinent and the Babylonian culture of the [Middle East](19323.md). A [Buddhist](3267529.md) story describes [Indian](7564733.md) merchants visiting *Baveru* ([Babylonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia))[^cite_note-23] and selling peacocks for public display. Similar, earlier accounts describe monkeys exhibited to the public.[^cite_note-Goodman1994-21]<sup>: 25 </sup> Trade connections between India and Palestine and Mediterranean Jewish communities continued, and later, the languages of these cultures started to share linguistic similarities.[^cite_note-Goodman1994-21]<sup>: 25–30 </sup>

Judea played a minor role in trade between the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and India during the period of [Roman rule in Judaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaea_\(Roman_province\)). It is known that there were expensive garments in the [Temple in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem) imported from India via [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) (in present-day [Egypt](8087628.md)).[^cite_note-24]

### Non-recognition period (1948–1950)



[Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) with [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](21648.md), [United States](3434750.md), in 1949

[Hermann Kallenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Kallenbach) (1 March 1871 – 25 March 1945) was a Lithuanian-born Jewish South African [architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architect) who was one of the foremost friends and associates of [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi). Kallenbach was introduced to the young Mohandas Gandhi while they were both working in [South Africa](17416221.md) and, after a series of discussions, they developed a long-lasting association. As a Zionist, Kallenbach planned to accompany Gandhi to India in 1914, but with the outbreak of World War I, he was interned as an enemy alien at detention camps and shifted to the Isle of Man as a prisoner of war from 1915 until 1917. After the war he returned to South Africa, where he resumed his work as an architect and continued to correspond with Gandhi. The rise of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) and [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md)'s [anti-Semitic](1078.md) [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany) shocked Kallenbach into a rediscovery of his Jewish roots. He became a [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) and served on the executive board of the South African Zionist Federation and planned to settle in Palestine ("[Eretz Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel)" in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language)). He wanted society there to involve no state, military, or industry, in order to avoid colonialism through Zionist settlements. At the request of [Moshe Shertok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Sharett) (Sharett), Kallenbach visited Gandhi in May 1937 to enlist his sympathy and support for Zionism. The architect once again became a simple man, participating in all the activities of Gandhi's ashram life. Kallenbach wrote, "I join the whole programme... It is 'almost' as the old joint life, as if the 23 years, with all the events that affected millions of people, had disappeared." Kallenbach disagreed with Gandhi over Zionism and the need to violently resist Hitler. Nevertheless, Kallenbach continued his deep friendship with Gandhi, visiting him again in 1939

India's position on the establishment of the State of Israel was affected by many factors, including India's own [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) on religious lines, and India's relationship with other nations.[^cite_note-INIS-25] Indian independence leader [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) believed the Jews had a good case and a prior claim for Israel,[^cite_note-GH-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] but opposed the creation of Israel on religious[^cite_note-GH-26][^cite_note-H1-29] or [mandated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Israel) terms.[^cite_note-GH-26][^cite_note-H1-29][^cite_note-30] Gandhi believed that the Arabs were the "rightful occupants" of Palestine, and was of the view that the Jews should return to their countries of origin.[^cite_note-Nissim-31] [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) wrote a four-page letter to [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru) on 13 June 1947, to persuade India to support the setting up of a Jewish state. Nehru, however, couldn't accept Einstein's request, and explained his dilemma stating that national leaders "have to unfortunately pursue policies that are essentially selfish".[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] India voted against the [partitioning of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) plan of 1947[^cite_note-36] and voted against [Israel's admission to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_273) in 1949.[^cite_note-37]

Various proponents of [Hindutva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva) supported or sympathised with the creation of Israel. [Hindu Mahasabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Mahasabha) leader [Veer Savarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinayak_Damodar_Savarkar) supported the creation of Israel on both moral and political grounds, and condemned and deplored India's vote at the UN against Israel.[^cite_note-RPaper1-38][^cite_note-Savarkar1-39] [Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtriya_Swayamsevak_Sangh) leader [Madhav Sadashiv Golwalkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhav_Sadashiv_Golwalkar) admired [Jewish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) and believed [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) was the natural territory of the Jewish people, essential to their [aspiration for nationhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

### Informal recognition (1950–1991)

On 17 September 1950, India officially recognised the State of Israel.[^cite_note-INIS-25] Indian [Prime Minister](24452.md) [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru) stated, "We would have \[recognised Israel] long ago, because Israel is a fact. We refrained because of our desire not to offend the sentiments of our friends in the Arab countries."[^cite_note-INIS-25] In 1953, Israel was permitted to open a consulate in Bombay (now Mumbai).[^cite_note-RAW-MOSSAD-42]



[Jayaprakash Narayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayaprakash_Narayan) meeting [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion), 1958

During the [Six-Day War](29328.md) India took a pro-Arab stance.[^cite_note-43] Israel supported India during the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971).[^cite_note-44] Israeli arms manufacturer [Shlomo Zabludowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Zabludowicz) provided India and the [Bengali nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_nationalism) and [Bangladeshi independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) organization [Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini) with [mortars](324499.md), ammunition and instructors.[^cite_note-45] India's opposition to official diplomatic relations stemmed from domestic and foreign considerations. Domestically, politicians in India feared losing the Muslim [votebank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votebank) (motivated in large part by Muslim appeasement and pseudosecularism) if relations were normalised with Israel.[^cite_note-NJA-46][^cite_note-DEF1-47]

Additionally, India did not want to jeopardise its citizens working in [Arab states of the Persian Gulf](3088555.md), who were helping India maintain its [foreign-exchange reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign-exchange_reserves_of_India).[^cite_note-NJA-46][^cite_note-rubin1-48] India's domestic need for energy was another reason, in order to safeguard the flow of oil from Arab nations.[^cite_note-NJA-46][^cite_note-rubin1-48] India's foreign policy goals and alliances also proved problematic to formal relations with Israel, including India's support for the pro-[Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), India's tilt towards the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) during the [Cold War](325329.md), and India's desire to counter Pakistan's influence with the Arab states.[^cite_note-rubin1-48] On an ideological level, the dominant political party in India during this era, namely the [Indian National Congress](149333.md), opposed Israel due to their perception that it was a state based on religion, analogous to [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-rubin1-48]

Although there was no formal relationship for several decades, meetings and cooperation took place between both countries, including figures such as [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan).[^cite_note-49] Israel also provided India with crucial information during its multiple wars.[^cite_note-50]

### Full recognition (1992–2022)

After decades of [non-aligned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) and pro-Arab policy, India formally established relations with Israel when it opened an embassy in [Tel Aviv](31453.md) in January 1992.[^cite_note-51] Ties between the two nations have flourished since, primarily due to common strategic interests and security threats, particularly from [terrorism](30636.md). In 1999, Israel supported India in the [Kargil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_war) by providing arms and ammunition.[^cite_note-IndiaToday2017-52] The formation of [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) (OIC), which allegedly neglected the sentiments of [Indian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India), and the blocking of India by [Pakistan](23235.md) from joining the OIC are considered to be the causes of this diplomatic shift.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] On a diplomatic level, India and Israel have maintained healthy relations despite India's repeated condemnations of Israeli military actions in [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories), which are believed to be motivated by the [United Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance) (UPA) government.

Since his election in 2014, the [Modi government](52507144.md) has maintained a [de-hyphenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-hyphenation) policy, where it keeps its relations with Israel and Palestine independent from each other.[^cite_note-56] At the height of the [2014 Israel–Gaza war](43243199.md), India blamed both sides and asked Israel to "stop disproportionate use of force." External Affairs Minister [Sushma Swaraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushma_Swaraj) insisted that there was no change in India's policy of supporting the Palestinian cause while maintaining good relations with Israel.[^cite_note-57] Nevertheless, India joined other [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS) nations in voting at the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) for a probe into alleged [human rights violations in Gaza](6184856.md), which generated mixed response among media and analysts in India.[^cite_note-58] When the UNHRC report alleging that Israel had committed war crimes was tabled for vote, India abstained from voting, one of five countries to do so.[^cite_note-59] Israeli envoy to India [Daniel Carmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Carmon) thanked India for not supporting what he described as another anti-Israel bashing resolution.[^cite_note-60]



Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) with Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) in New Delhi, India, January 2018

India–Israel relationship has been very close and warm under the premiership of [Narendra Modi](444222.md) since 2014. In 2017, he was the first ever Prime Minister of India to visit Israel.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] India was the largest arms customer of Israel in 2017. Defence relations between the two countries are longstanding.[^cite_note-63]

India voted in favour of Israel's resolution to deny observer status to Palestinian non-governmental organization Shahed at the [United Nations Economic and Social Council](31958.md) (ECOSOC) on 6 June 2019.[^cite_note-64]

In August 2022, President [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) joined the Indian Embassy in Tel Aviv for its 75th [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(India\)) celebrations. In his speech, he welcomed India's rise as a regional and global power and described Israel and India as two modern republics proudly bound together by creativity and democracy, by ingenuity coupled with deep respect for timeless faiths and belief systems.[^cite_note-65] Meeting [Indian External Affairs Minister](812043.md) [Subrahmanyam Jaishankar](40435868.md), Herzog underscored his personal commitment to strengthening the India–Israel relationship.[^cite_note-66]

Despite Indian leaders' opposition to Israel in the past, India in 2025 is a strong ally of Israel. India was one of the first countries to condemn the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) on Israel by [Hamas](13913.md) and other Palestinian [groups designated as terrorists](63578.md). Three weeks after the attack, India abstained in the vote on [UN General Assembly Resolution ES-10/21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-10/21) calling for an immediate and unconditional ceasefire because it did not explicitly condemn Hamas.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] In 2025, the opposition [Indian National Congress](149333.md) criticised Prime Minister Narendra Modi for his stance on the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-70]

Israel condemned the [Pahalgam terrorist attack](79778862.md) on 22 April 2025 and expressed support for India. On 7 May 2025, Israel publicly praised India's airstrikes on terrorists, which were launched in retaliation for the [Pahalgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahalgam) massacre. The Israeli government described the operation as a "measured and decisive response to terrorism" and reaffirmed its support for India's right to self-defence, adding, "Israel supports India's right for self defense". Terrorists should know there's no place to hide from their heinous crimes against the innocent." The airstrikes, part of what was reported as [Operation Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sindoor), targeted terrorist launchpads and infrastructure across the [Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control).[^cite_note-71]

## Diplomatic visits

### 1997

[Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman) became the first Israeli president to visit India in 1997.[^cite_note-RivlinJPos-78]

### 2000

In 2000, [L.K. Advani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.K._Advani) became the first Indian [Home Minister](1974473.md) to visit the state of Israel.[^cite_note-79]

Later that year, [Jaswant Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaswant_Singh) became the first Indian [Foreign Minister](812043.md) to visit Israel.[^cite_note-80] Following the visit, the two countries set up a joint anti-terror commission. The foreign ministers of the two countries said intensified co-operation would range from counter-terrorism to information technology.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

### 2003



Israeli and Indian flags in New Delhi during Ariel Sharon's visit, September 2003

In 2003, [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) was the first [Israeli Prime Minister](24898.md) to visit India. He was welcomed by the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP) led [National Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance_\(India\)) coalition government of India.[^cite_note-83] Several newspapers expressed positive views on his visit, and [Indian Prime Minister](24452.md) [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee) voiced confidence that Sharon's visit would pave the way for further consolidating bilateral ties.[^cite_note-84] Sharon's visit was condemned in [leftist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_India)[^cite_note-85] and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) circles.[^cite_note-bbc2-86] Hundreds of supporters of India's various communist parties rallied in [New Delhi](51585.md) while nearly 100 Muslims were arrested in [Mumbai](19189.md).[^cite_note-bbc2-86] Students of [Aligarh Muslim University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligarh_Muslim_University) demanded that India sever ties with Israel and increase ties with Palestine.[^cite_note-87] The [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi)-language daily *[Navbharat Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navbharat_Times)* called Sharon an important friend of India. The [Hindu nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalist) [Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtriya_Swayamsevak_Sangh) (RSS) condemned the protest against Sharon.[^cite_note-rss-88][^cite_note-rss-88] Sharon expressed satisfaction over his talks with Indian leaders. Indian prime minister [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee) said the visit would increase ties between India and Israel.[^cite_note-89] Sharon invited Vajpayee to visit Israel.[^cite_note-90] Sharon said that Israelis regard India to be one of the most important countries in the world, and Vajpayee was sure that Sharon's visit would bring the two countries closer together.[^cite_note-bbc2-86]

### 2006

In early 2006 Indian government ministers [Sharad Pawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharad_Pawar), [Kapil Sibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapil_Sibal), and [Kamal Nath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamal_Nath) visited Israel.[^cite_note-talk-91] Then-Gujarat Chief Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) visited Israel in October 2006.[^cite_note-92]

### 2012

Despite India's "unwavering support for the Palestinian cause", Foreign Minister [S.M. Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.M._Krishna) made a two-day visit to Israel in 2012. ### Intelligence-sharing cooperation

When the [Research and Analysis Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_Analysis_Wing) (RAW) was founded in September 1968 by [R.N. Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rameshwar_Nath_Kao), he was advised by then Prime Minister [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi) to cultivate links with [Mossad](27619007.md). This was suggested as a countermeasure to military links between that of Pakistan and China, as well as with [North Korea](21255.md). Israel was also concerned that Pakistani army officers were training Libyans and Iranians in handling Chinese and North Korean military equipments.[^cite_note-RAW-MOSSAD-42]

Pakistan believed intelligence relations between India and Israel threatened Pakistani security. When young Israeli tourists began visiting the Kashmir valley in the early 1990s, Pakistan suspected they were disguised Israeli army officers there to help Indian security forces with anti-terrorism operations. Israeli tourists were attacked, with one slain and another kidnapped. Pressure from the [Kashmiri Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_Muslims) diaspora in the [United States](3434750.md) led to the kidnapped tourist's eventual release. Kashmiri Muslims feared that the attacks could isolate the American Jewish community, and result in them lobbying the US government against Kashmiri separatist groups.[^cite_note-RAW-MOSSAD-42]

A [Rediff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rediff) story in 2003 revealed clandestine links between R\&AW and Mossad. In 1996, R.K. Yadav, a former RAW official had filed a disproportionate assets case in the Delhi High Court against Anand Kumar Verma, RAW chief between 1987 and 1990. Yadav listed eight properties that he claimed were purchased illegally by Verma using RAW's unaudited funds for secret operations. Although his petition for a CBI inquiry into Verma's properties was dismissed, Yadav managed to obtain more information through a [right to information request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Information_Act,_2005) in 2005 and filed another case in 2009. In 2013, the CBI carried out an investigation of Verma's properties. Proceedings in the Delhi High Court revealed the names of two companies operated by RAW in 1988 – Piyush Investments and Hector Leasing and Finance Company Ltd. The firms were headed by two senior RAW officials V. Balachandran and B. Raman. Balachandran and Raman retired in 1994 and 1995 respectively. The companies were listed as trading houses that dealt in several kinds of minerals, automobiles, textiles, metals and spare parts, and also claimed to produce feature films. The companies purchased two flats in Gauri Sadan, a residential building on Hailey Road, New Delhi in March 1989 for ₹ 23 lakh.[^cite_note-indiatoday.intoday.in-177]

*[India Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Today)* reported that the two flats were RAW safe houses used as operational fronts for Mossad agents and housed Mossad's station chief between 1989 and 1992. RAW had reportedly decided to have closer ties to Mossad, and the subsequent secret operation was approved by then Prime Minister [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi). *India Today* cites "RAW insiders" as saying that RAW agents hid a Mossad agent holding an [Argentine passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_passport) and exchanged intelligence and expertise in operations, including negotiations for the release of an Israeli tourist by the [Jammu and Kashmir Liberation Front militants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Kashmir_Liberation_Front) in June 1991. When asked about the case Verma refused to speak about the companies, but claimed his relationship with them was purely professional. Raman stated, "Sometimes, spy agencies float companies for operational reasons. All I can say is that everything was done with government approval. Files were cleared by the then prime minister \[Rajiv Gandhi] and his cabinet secretary. Balachandran stated, "It is true that we did a large number of operations but at every stage, we kept the Cabinet Secretariat and the prime minister in the loop."[^cite_note-indiatoday.intoday.in-177]

In November 2015, *The Times of India* reported that agents from Mossad and [MI5](100076.md) were protecting Prime Minister Narendra Modi during his visit to Turkey. Modi was on a state visit to the United Kingdom and was scheduled to attend the 2015 G-20 Summit in Antalya, Turkey. The paper reported that the agents had been called in to provide additional cover to Modi's security detail, composed of India's Special Protection Group and secret agents from RAW and IB, in wake of the [November 2015 Paris attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2015_Paris_attacks).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

[On 14 February 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pulwama_attack), a convoy of vehicles carrying security personnel on the [Jammu-Srinagar National Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu%E2%80%93Srinagar_National_Highway) was attacked by a vehicle-borne suicide bomber at Lethpora in the Pulwama district, Jammu and Kashmir, India. 40 [Central Reserve Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Reserve_Police_Force) personals were killed by the bomber. Israel responded to India, offering unconditional support to the Indian Army and the Government. Israel has informed that they will share intelligence and technology to help India respond.[^cite_note-180]

Following the [Pahalgam attack](79778862.md) on 22 April 2025, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu condemned the incident as "barbaric" and affirmed that Israel stands with India in its fight against terrorism, with similar messages of solidarity echoed by Israel's Foreign Minister. After India's [Operation Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_strikes) on 7 May, Israel's Ambassador to India, Reuven Azar, reiterated support for India's right to self-defense, stating that terrorists must know there is no safe haven for those committing heinous crimes against the innocent.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

## Science and technology collaboration

In 1993, during the visit to India of then [Israeli Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Foreign_Minister) [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres), India and Israel signed an agreement on science and technology, which allowed for direct scientific cooperation between both governments. Specific areas of cooperation included [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [biotechnology](4502.md), [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasers), and [electro-optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-optics). Additionally, a joint committee to monitor collaboration between the two nations was established and set to meet biennially.[^cite_note-BookRef01-192] In 1994, a \$3 million joint science and technology fund was set up to facilitate [R\&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R\&D) collaboration between both countries.[^cite_note-BookRef01-192]

In 1996, Indian scientists attended a seminar on [advanced materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science) in Israel. In 1997, Israeli scientists attended a seminar on biotechnology in [Delhi](37756.md). In 1998, India and Israel had 22 ongoing joint research projects. A joint symposium on the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome) was held in [Jerusalem](16043.md), where six Indian scientists took part.[^cite_note-BookRef01-192][^cite_note-193] In November 1999, India and Israel agreed on four proposals for joint research projects in the field of human genome research.[^cite_note-BookRef01-192] In 2000, even more joint projects related to human genome research were agreed on, and a status seminar on this field was held in India.[^cite_note-194] In early 1999, more than 20 Israeli scientists participated in a physics symposium on [condensed matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter) in Delhi.[^cite_note-BookRef01-192] In 2001, a similar symposium was held in Jerusalem, with 18 Indian scientists attending.[^cite_note-195]

In 2003, both countries discussed doubling their investment in their ongoing science and technology collaboration to \$1 million each, starting in October 2004.[^cite_note-TimesofIndia-196] In 2005, India and Israel signed a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) to set up a fund to encourage bilateral investment into industrial research and development and specific projects. Under the agreement, at least one Indian and one Israeli company must be collaborating on a project for that project to qualify for the fund. From 2006 to 2014, the fund, named i4RD, has been used in seven projects.[^cite_note-IE-183][^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] In 2012, the two countries signed a five-year \$50 million academic research agreement for promoting collaborative research across a wide range of disciplines, including medical and information technology, social and life sciences, humanities, and the arts.[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201]

In 2012, Israel stated its intent to increase technological and economic cooperation with the Indian state of [Bihar](442482.md), in the fields of agriculture, water management, solar energy, and medical insurance.[^cite_note-202] In 2014, Israel made plans to open two agricultural [centers of excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_excellence) in Bihar, focusing on increasing productivity of vegetable and mango crops.[^cite_note-203]

Israel has offered to help the India government with a project to clean the Ganga. An Israeli delegation visited India in August 2015 and met with officials of the Union Ministry of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation. Israeli ambassador to India Daniel Carmon also called on Union Urban Development and Parliamentary Affairs Minister M. Venkaiah Naidu to offer Israel's expertise in water management to battle [water scarcity](15380061.md). Ohad Horsandi, spokesperson of the Israeli Embassy in New Delhi stated that Israel was keen to help in India meet its water needs for agriculture and drinking, and was pushing for more government-to-government agreements.[^cite_note-204]

Following Prime Minister Modi's visit to Israel in 2017, there has been an increased call for collaboration between Israel and India on innovation development. The non-profit Indian based global trade body [NASSCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Software_and_Services_Companies), along with the professional service company [Accenture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accenture), released the report [Collaborative Innovation: The Vehicle Driving Indo-Israel Prosperity](https://www.nasscom.in/knowledge-center/publications/collaborative-innovation-vehicle-driving-indo-israel-prosperity), to highlight areas of scientific and technological collaboration between the two countries.[^cite_note-\:2-205] Additionally, the non-profit organization TAVtech Ventures is launching a program that connect students from Israel and the United States with local Indian students to come up with tech-based startups.[^cite_note-\:2-205][^cite_note-206]

### Space collaboration

In 2002, India and Israel signed a cooperative agreement promoting space collaboration between both nations.[^cite_note-HARS-207]

In 2003, the [Israel Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Space_Agency), or ISA, expressed interest in collaborating with the [Indian Space Research Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Space_Research_Organisation), or ISRO, in using satellites for improved management of land and other resources. Israel also expressed interest in participating in ISRO's proposed [mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrayaan-1) of sending an unmanned craft to the moon.[^cite_note-TimesofIndia-196] Additionally, the two countries signed an agreement outlining the deployment of [TAUVEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAUVEX), an Israeli space telescope array, on India's [GSAT-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSAT-4), a planned navigation and communication satellite.[^cite_note-TimesofIndia-196][^cite_note-HARS-207][^cite_note-208] In 2010, the TAUVEX array was removed from GSAT-4 by the ISRO, and the array was never subsequently launched.[^cite_note-spacefal-209] The GSAT-4 itself failed to launch, due to the failure of its [cryogenic engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenic_engine).[^cite_note-spacefal-209][^cite_note-210]

In 2005, Israel decided to launch [TecSAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TecSAR), its first [synthetic aperture radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_aperture_radar) imaging satellite, on India's [Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_Satellite_Launch_Vehicle), or PSLV.[^cite_note-space1-211] TecSAR was chosen to launch through India's PSLV due to Israeli concerns about the reliability and technical limitations of its own [Shavit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shavit_2) space launch vehicle, economic considerations, and also due to Israel's desire to increase strategic cooperation with India.[^cite_note-space1-211][^cite_note-212] In 2008, TecSAR was successfully inserted into orbit by India's PSLV.[^cite_note-OpallRome2008-213] One of TecSAR's primary functions is to monitor [Iran](14653.md)'s military activities.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-OpallRome2008-213][^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216]

In 2009, India successfully launched [RISAT-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RISAT-2), a synthetic aperture radar imaging satellite.[^cite_note-217] RISAT-2 was manufactured by [Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries), or IAI, in conjunction with ISRO.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219] The launch of the RISAT-2 satellite aimed to provide India with greater earth observation power, which would improve disaster management, and increase surveillance and defense capabilities.[^cite_note-ATON-13][^cite_note-220] The acquisition and subsequent launch of the RISAT-2 satellite was accelerated after the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks), to boost India's future surveillance capabilities.[^cite_note-221]

### Agriculture collaboration

India has chosen Israel as a strategic partner (G2G) in the field of agriculture.This partnership evolved into the Indo-Israel Agricultural Project (IIAP), under the Indo Israel Action Plan, based on a MOU signed by Indian and Israeli ministers of Agriculture in 2006.[^cite_note-222] The partnership aim to introduce crop diversity, increasing productivity & increasing water use efficiency. IIAP has been initiated in 2009 after signing a bilateral agreement between Indian and Israeli ministers of Agriculture (2006). IIAP is implemented via establishment of Centers of Excellence (CoE), in which Israeli Technologies and know-how are disseminated tailored to local Indian conditions. Till date three phases of IIAP has been channeled. Each IIAP phase lasts for three years (2009–2012; 2012–2015, 2015–2018).Within the 16 States that has been invited to take part in the IIAP, 22 CoE's are currently into the fully active stage.[^cite_note-223]

Acknowledging the success of MIDH MASHAV IIAP Program as implemented during the last decade a three-year work program in Agriculture" 2018–2020 was signed between the [Ministry of Agriculture and Farmer's Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_&_Farmers_Welfare) of the Republic of India and [Mashav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashav)[^cite_note-224]- Ministry of Foreign Affairs Israel to increase the value chain demonstrated with the fully operative Indo-Israel Center of Excellence by introducing new components including the [Indo-Israeli Centre of Excellence for Animal Husbandary & Dairying, Hisar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Israeli_Centre_of_Excellence_for_Animal_Husbandary_&_Dairying,_Hisar). Center of Excellence (CoE) is a platform for knowledge transfer and Israeli Agro-Technology. As a goal the CoE aims to serve the farmer with a focus on a key crop. Each CoE is composed of Nursery management, Cultivation techniques, and Irrigation and fertigation.

Also in 2008, Israel and India finalised an agricultural plan introducing crops native to the [Middle East](19323.md) and [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin) to India, with a particular focus on [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_\(fruit\)).[^cite_note-225] Subsequently, around 112,000 olive trees were planted in the desert of [Rajasthan](26291.md).[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227] In 2014, more than 100 tonnes of olives were produced in Rajasthan.[^cite_note-228]

### Workforce collaboration

Israel is conducting a recruitment drive in India to address its growing demand for skilled construction workers. This initiative, facilitated by a government-to-government agreement, is focused on recruiting up to 10,000 Indian workers. The recruitment process is taking place at various locations across India, including the Industrial Training Institute in Aundh, Pune.

The Indian government is supporting this effort by providing infrastructure and logistical assistance. This recruitment drive offers Indian workers the opportunity for international employment and competitive salaries. To date, thousands of Indian workers have already been deployed to Israel.[^cite_note-229]

With the recent discovery of the [Tamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamar_gas_field) and [Levianthan](30257584.md) gas fields off the coast of Israel, India has been one of the first countries to bid for an [exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) license in order to extract and import natural gas from the fields.[^cite_note-230] India's [ONGC Videsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ONGC_Videsh), Bharat PetroResources, Indian Oil and Oil India were awarded an exploration license by the Israeli government, a clear sign of the ongoing diversification in ties between the two countries.

## Cultural ties and cross-country perceptions

In 2011, cultural artists and performers from India arrived in Israel to participate in a three-week festival commemorating 20 years of diplomatic relations between the two countries. According to India's then ambassador to Israel [Navtej Sarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navtej_Sarna), the purpose of the festival was to improve the bilateral relationship between the two countries by facilitating a greater understanding of each other's culture.[^cite_note-ET-231]

According to a 2009 international study commissioned by the [Israeli Foreign Ministry](18133296.md), the greatest level of sympathy towards Israel can be found in India, with 58% of Indian respondents showing sympathy towards Israel.[^cite_note-Eichner2009-18]

As reported in 2015, [opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polls) taken in India showed 70% and above of respondents had favorable views of Israel.[^cite_note-232]

In 2015, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) voted unanimously in favour of adopting 21 June as [International Yoga Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Yoga_Day).[^cite_note-233] In a clear sign of growing affinity between the two countries, the Indian Embassy in Tel Aviv organizes annual yoga day celebrations, where Israelis from all walks of life take part in various yogic exercises. Yoga has proven to be immensely popular in Israel and is a sign of Israel's cultural connection to India.[^cite_note-234]

In 2019, Israel was a country partner at an event scheduled to be held in the central university [Jamia Millia Islamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamia_Millia_Islamia). Israel's involvement was protested against by students for their "occupation of Palestine." The university relented and announced that "it would not allow Israeli delegates to take part in events on the campus in future." Some Members of Parliament (MPs) had also lent support to the protesting students. The teachers association at Jamia had in 2014 protested against the [Israeli offensive in Gaza](43243199.md) and were joined by various activists, academicians, human rights defenders and members of civil society.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

## Tourism



A Hebrew [Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad) sign in [Kasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasol), [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), India

Around 40,000 Israelis, many of whom have just finished [military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_Israel),[^cite_note-238] visit India annually.[^cite_note-ET-231] There are dozens of [Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad)-operated community centers in India, where many Israelis celebrate holidays and observe religious traditions.[^cite_note-239] Popular destinations for Israelis include [Goa](40010153.md), the [Himalayas](37966.md), [Manali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manali,_Himachal_Pradesh), [Vashisht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vashisht_\(Village\)), [Naggar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naggar), [Kasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasol), and the villages surrounding [Dharamsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharamsala,_Himachal_Pradesh).[^cite_note-JTOI-240] In many of these areas, Hebrew signs on businesses and public transportation are widely noticeable.[^cite_note-JTOI-240]

The number of tourists from India visiting Israel touched 15,900 in the year 2000.[^cite_note-HAA-241] By 2010, the number of tourists had increased to 43,439.[^cite_note-PIB-242] In 2014, the number of tourists from India visiting Israel was 34,900.[^cite_note-TC-243] A popular destination for Indian tourists traveling to Israel is [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-244] In part of 2010, Indian tourists were the biggest spenders in Israel, spending an average of \$1,364 per tourist; the average tourist expenditure in Israel during this time was \$1,091.[^cite_note-245]

In 2011, representatives from both countries met in Delhi, and planned to enhance tourism through collaboration in the spheres of destination management and promotion, as well as in manpower development. Plans for tour-operators and travel agents in both countries to coordinate were also discussed.[^cite_note-PIB-242] In 2015, 600 travel agents from India arrived in Israel for the annual Travel Agents Federation of India conference, and ways to decrease barriers to tourism were discussed.[^cite_note-TC-243] Currently, [El Al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Al) flies between Tel Aviv and Mumbai, [Air India](26457880.md) flies between Delhi and Tel Aviv, and [Arkia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkia) flies between Tel Aviv and [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi) as well as Tel Aviv and [Goa](40010153.md).

In March 2018, Air India, operating flight number AI139, became the first airline to fly non-stop from [New Delhi](51585.md) to [Tel Aviv](31453.md), via the airspace of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), overturning an overfly ban on flights to [Israel](9282173.md) that had lasted 70 years. Currently, Air India is the only airline in the world that has been given such permission, and indicates a behind-the-scenes improvement in relations between Israel and the Arab world.[^cite_note-246] The new flight takes approximately 7 hours to traverse the distance between India and Israel, which is 2 hours and 10 minutes shorter than the route taken by El Al from Mumbai to Tel Aviv. In recent days, the success of the route has prompted the airline to increase the frequency of flights to one each day.[^cite_note-247]

In recent months, [Israel](9282173.md) has observed a constant rise in the number of Indian tourists to the country. Towards an additional effort to boost tourism from India, the Israeli government has simplified visa procedures for Indians who have already availed visas from either [Canada](5042916.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [United States](3434750.md), [Schengen countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_countries) or Israel and have completed their travel to these countries.[^cite_note-248] Visa processing fees for Indian applicants has also been reduced from the original 1700 to 1100.[^cite_note-249] In the year 2017, Indian tourist arrivals to Israel rose by 31%, with over 60,000 tourists visiting the country that year.[^cite_note-250] Israel plans to meet a target of over 100,000 Indian tourists for the year 2018.[^cite_note-251]

From 1 January 2025, the [Ministry of Tourism of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism_\(Israel\)) has launched a digital e-visa system for Indian travellers. This initiative aligns with Israel's broader strategy to enhance tourism ties with India, a rapidly growing market for international visitors.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

## Interfaith relations

The history of the Jewish people in India dates back to ancient times.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-Weil-263][^cite_note-264] [Judaism](15624.md) was one of the first foreign religions to arrive in India in [recorded history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_history).[^cite_note-265] [Indian](14533.md) [Jews](25955086.md) are a religious minority of India, but unlike many parts of the world, have historically lived in India without any instances of [antisemitism](1078.md) from the local majority populace, the [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu). The better-established ancient communities have assimilated a large number of local traditions through [cultural diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_diffusion).[^cite_note-266] The Jewish population in India is hard to estimate since each Jewish community is distinct with different origins; while some allegedly arrived during the time of the [Kingdom of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah), others are seen by some as descendants of Israel's [Ten Lost Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Lost_Tribes).[^cite_note-267] In addition to Jewish expatriates[^cite_note-268] and recent immigrants, there are several distinct Jewish groups in India:

- [Cochin Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochin_Jews), also called [Malabar Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_Jews), are of [Mizrahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews) and [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi) heritage. They are the oldest group of Jews in India, with possible roots claimed to date to the time of [King Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Solomon).

  [^cite_note-269]

  [^cite_note-270]

  &#x20;The Cochin Jews settled in the [Kingdom of Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cochin) in [South India](678583.md).

  [^cite_note-271]

  [^cite_note-272]

  [^cite_note-273]

- The so-called [Spanish and Portuguese Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_and_Portuguese_Jews), [Paradesi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradesi_Jews) and [British Jews](5380456.md) arrived at [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras) during the 16th century, mainly as traders and diamond businessmen. They also have a large presence in the former Portuguese colony of [Goa](40010153.md), where the [Goan Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goan_Inquisition) was initiated in 1560.

  [^cite_note-274]

- The [Bene Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Israel) arrived in the state of [Maharashtra](20629.md) 900 years ago.

  [^cite_note-275]

  &#x20;Another branch of the [Bene Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Israel) community, resided in Karachi until the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, when they fled to India (in particular: Mumbai). Many of them also moved to [Israel](9282173.md). The Jews from [Sindh](28972.md), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) or [Pathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathan) area are often incorrectly called Bani Israel Jews. The Jewish community who used to reside in other parts of what became Pakistan (such as [Lahore](125315.md) or [Peshawar](66230.md)) also fled to India in 1947, in a similar manner to the larger Karachi Jewish community.

- The [Baghdadi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdadi_Jews) arrived in the city of Surat from Iraq (and other Arab states), Iran and Afghanistan about 250 years ago.

  [^cite_note-Weil-263]

- The [Bnei Menashe](1674436.md) are [Mizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_people) and [Kuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people) tribesmen in [Manipur](422642.md) and [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) who are recent converts to Judaism.

  [^cite_note-Weil_2-276]

- The [Bene Ephraim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Ephraim) (also called Telugu Jews) are a small group who speak [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language); their observance of Judaism dates to 1981.

The majority of Indian Jews have made [Aliyah](351513.md) (migrated) to [Israel](9282173.md) since the creation of the modern state in 1948. Over 70,000 Indian Jews now live in Israel (over 1% of Israel's total population). Of the remaining 5,000, the largest community is concentrated in [Mumbai](19189.md), where 3,500 have stayed over from the over 30,000 Jews registered there in the 1940s, divided into [Bene Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Israel) and [Baghdadi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdadi_Jews),[^cite_note-277] though the Baghdadi Jews refused to recognize the B'nei Israel as Jews, and withheld dispensing charity to them for that reason.[^cite_note-Katz-278] There are reminders of Jewish localities in Kerala still left such as synagogues.

In the beginning of the 21st century, new Jewish communities have been established in Mumbai, New Delhi, Bangalore, and other cities in India. The new communities have been established by the Chabad-Lubavitch movement which has sent rabbis to create those communities. The communities serve the religious and social needs of Jewish business people who have immigrated to or are visiting India, and Jewish backpackers touring India. The largest centre is the [Nariman House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nariman_House) in Mumbai. There are currently 33 [synagogues in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogues_in_India), although many no longer function as such and today vary in their levels of preservation.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280][^cite_note-281]
