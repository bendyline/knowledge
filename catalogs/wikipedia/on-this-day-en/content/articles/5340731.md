**Ramat David Airbase** ([ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **LLRD**, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): בָּסִיס חֵיל-הַאֲוִיר רָמַת דָּוִד *Basis Kheil HaAvir Ramat David*, English: David Heights) is an [Israeli Air Force (IAF)](177619.md) base located 20 km southeast of [Haifa](55606.md) in the [Northern District](324281.md) of [Israel](9282173.md), close to [kibbutz](106200.md) [Ramat David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_David) in the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley). It is the northernmost IAF base in Israel with fighter jets and UAVs based on it. And it has three runways, each about 2.5 km long, and a heliport. For many years, there have been plans to convert Ramat David into an international airport for the city of [Haifa](55606.md) and northern Israel.[^cite_note-1] This has now been officially decided by the [Israeli government](72545811.md) in February 2026. (see [here](#International_airport)).

## History

### Kibbutz and military camp

Before the airbase was built during [World War II](32927.md), there was already a British military camp here. [Kibbutz](106200.md) [Ramat David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_David) "David Heights", founded in 1926, takes its name from [British Prime Minister](24150.md) [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George), who was in office at the time of the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration) (1917),[^cite_note-2] where the establishment of a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine was announced.

### RAF Ramat David

[Roald Dahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Dahl), in his World War II autobiography '[Going Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_Solo)', mentions landing his RAF [Hawker Hurricane Mk.I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hurricane) at [Ramat David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_David) in June 1941.[^cite_note-3] At that time it was *a ribbon of dry earth that had been rolled out in the middle of a large field of sweet-corn* built by the Brits and residents of the nearby [kibbutz](106200.md), as Roald Dahl also reports near the end of his book.[^cite_note-4] This secret airstrip behind [Mount Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel) was originally installed as an alternative runway in case the [Haifa Aerodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa_Airport) (RAF Haifa) 20 km northwest of it was attacked and damaged by the Germans or Italians.

In 1942, the **RAF Ramat David** military airfield was finally established by the [Royal Air Force (RAF)](25679.md) under the [British Mandate for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine). From this point on, several British aircraft squadrons with fighter aircraft, bombers and transport aircraft were stationed there in turn (see list of [former RAF units](#Former_RAF_units) below).

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) Jewish paratroopers trained at Ramat David to serve in RAF special operation commandos and to drop behind enemy lines in German or German-occupied territory. They were supposed to help bring downed Allied airmen safely back and help Jews hide from the Nazis. Several of them died (see memorial stone in the gallery below).

After the [Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) on 14 May 1948 and the start of the [First Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) the next day, the base was temporarily maintained by the RAF to cover the withdrawal of British forces from Palestine. On 22 May 1948, the [Royal Egyptian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Air_Force) attacked the base, mistakenly believing it was now an Israeli controlled airbase. In a series of three attacks, several aircraft were destroyed or damaged, a hangar was destroyed, and four British airmen were killed. In the further course of the fighting, five Egyptian fighter planes (all British made) were shot down. The British were furious with their allies.[^cite_note-22] A short time later the base was indeed taken over by the [Israel Defense Forces (IDF)](39237.md).

### Ramat David IAF Base

Over time, the Airbase was expanded to the main base of IAF operations north of Israel in [Syria](7515849.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md).

The [69 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Hammers" with three decommissioned [B-17 Flying Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-17) bombers smuggled from the US to Israel in 1948 was initially stationed here. This was done by the help of [Charles Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Winters), a Miami businessman who was imprisoned for this and was posthumously pardoned by President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) in 2008.[^cite_note-23]

The [103 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/103_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Elephants" with three [DC-3 Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) and one [Douglas DC-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-5) transport aircraft was initially stationed here also, but both squadrons (69 and 103) were relocated to other Israeli bases in the following years.

The future [Israeli president](153049.md) [Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman) (1924–2005) was squadron and base commander at Ramat David in the 1950s before he finally took over command of the [IAF](177619.md). In 2011 the base' Wing 1 there was named after him (see photos in gallery below).

A brief historical summary of the aircraft squadrons currently and recently stationed at Ramat David:

#### 101 Squadron "First Fighter" – active



The [101 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101_Squadron_\(Israel\)) is Israel's "First Fighter" squadron, formed on 20 May 1948, six days after Israel declared its independence. Initially flying the [Avia S-199](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avia_S-199), it has since operated the [Supermarine Spitfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire), [North American Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Mustang), [Dassault Mystere IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mystere_IV), [Dassault Mirage III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_III)CJ, [IAI Nesher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Nesher), [IAI Kfir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Kfir) and [F-16C](11642.md#F-16C/D) *Barak*. It has taken part in combat operations in all of Israel's wars and conflicts. In 2021 it relocated from [Hatzor Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatzor_Airbase) to Ramat David.

#### 105 Squadron "Scorpion" – active



The [105 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/105_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Scorpion" was founded in December 1950 as a [Spitfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire) squadron. It has since operated the [North American P-51 Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_P-51_Mustang), [Dassault Super Mystère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Super_Myst%C3%A8re), [IAI Sa'ar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Sa'ar) (upgraded Super Mystère), [McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II](11759.md) *Kurnas* and [F-16C/D](11642.md#F-16C/D) *Barak* on other airbases. The name "Scorpion" is intended to refer to a ground attack aircraft. In 2021 the squadron was relocated to Ramat David where it is based until the present day.[^cite_note-scorpion-24]

#### 109 Squadron "The Valley" – active



The [109 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/109_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "The Valley" was established in 1951 under a different name at [Tel Nof Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Nof_Airbase) and moved to Ramat David in 1956, where it still exists today. It got its name “The Valley” after the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley) where the base is located. At Ramat David it flew the [Dassault Mystère IV A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Myst%C3%A8re_IV), [Douglas A-4H/N Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk) *Ayit*, [IAI *Kfir* C.7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Kfir) and finally from 1991 to the present day the two-seater [General Dynamics F-16D](11642.md#F-16C/D) *Barak* as a strike fighter jet.[^cite_note-valley-25]

#### 110 Squadron "Knights Of The North" – closed



The [110 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Knights Of The North" was established at [Hatzor Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatzor_Airbase) in 1953 and relocated to Ramat David in 1957. It flew the [De Havilland Mosquito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Mosquito) at Chazor, and subsequently the following types at Ramat David: [S.O. 4050 Vautour IIA/B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sud_Aviation_Vautour), [Gloster Meteor F.8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloster_Meteor), [Douglas A-4H/N Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk) *Ayit*, [General Dynamics F-16A/B](11642.md#F-16A/B) *Netz*, [F-16C/D](11642.md#F-16C/D) *Barak* and – together with [117 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/117_Squadron_\(Israel\)) – took part in the destruction of the Iraqi nuclear reactor in 1981. It was finally shut down in 2017.[^cite_note-north-26]

#### 117 Squadron "First Jet" – moved



The [117 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/117_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "First Jet" was inaugurated on 17 June 1953 as the IAF's first fighter jet squadron with British [Gloster Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloster_Meteor) at Ramat David. In 1962 these were replaced by French [Dassault Mirage IIICJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_III) *Shahak*, which then took part in the [Six-Day War](29328.md), the [War of Attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Attrition) and the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War). In 1980, together with the 110 Squadron, they received the first [F-16A/B](11642.md#F-16A/B) *Netz* fighter jets from the USA. In June 1981, four jets from 117 Squadron took part in [Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera), the destruction of the Iraqi nuclear reactor Osirak near [Baghdad](4492.md). From 1986/87 these were then replaced by the IAF's first [F-16C/D](11642.md#F-16C/D) *Barak*, which remained until the squadron was temporarily closed in 2020 .[^cite_note-27] On 1 July 2021, the 117 Squadron was reactivated at [Nevatim Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevatim_Airbase) with [F-35I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35I) jets.

#### Underground hangars

The fighter jets are housed in large underground hangar systems and shelters into which they disappear after each landing and which have several entrances and exits. This protects them from missiles and at the same time hides them from view and precise localization. [Syria](7515849.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md) are only 50 to 60 kilometers away, from where rockets are repeatedly fired at northern Israel. During the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) in 1973, this airbase was the only one where rockets hit and not only destroyed facilities but also caused casualties.[^cite_note-28]

Since the onset of direct attacks between Israel and Iran in April 2024 (see: [April 2024 Iranian strikes on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2024_Iranian_strikes_on_Israel)), Ramat David has had to anticipate coming under rocket fire from that quarter as well – something that has indeed occurred.[^cite_note-Iran1-29][^cite_note-Iran2-30]

## Today

### F-16 fighter jets

At the beginning of October 2020, as part of an IAF efficiency program, the [117 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/117_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "First Jet" with [F-16](11642.md) fighter jets on Ramat David was disbanded and most of the jets and pilots were assigned to other units. It was involved in all of the country's wars since 1953 and, among other things, also involved in [Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera), the destruction of an unfinished [Iraqi](7515928.md) nuclear reactor in 1981.[^cite_note-firstjet-31] In July 2021, the squadron reopened at [Nevatim Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevatim_Airbase) with new [F-35I](11812.md) *Adir* jets.[^cite_note-32]

From March 2021, the two F-16 [Squadrons 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "First Fighter" and [105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/105_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Scorpion" were gradually relocated here from [Hatzor Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatzor_Airbase) to bring all remaining F-16C/D *Barak* jets under one roof.[^cite_note-33] They were united with [109 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/109_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "The Valley" of F-16D machines. Three squadrons with the newer [F-16I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-16_Fighting_Falcon_variants#F-16I_Sufa) *Sufa*, adapted to Israeli needs, are based at [Ramon Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airbase), as well as a fourth squadron of them at [Hatzerim Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatzerim_Airbase).

In December 2024, the first [ultra-Orthodox](53835.md) technician unit of the [IAF](177619.md) was established on Ramat David, after 26 so-called *Haredim* had completed their training. They will be responsible for the [105 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/105_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Scorpion" with F-16D fighter jets and maintain their weapons systems. The aim of this training is to better integrate ultra-Orthodox Jews into the army. The challenge is to enable soldiers to live a religious lifestyle within the army. This includes separate accommodation, strict kosher cuisine and the construction of a synagogue on the base. The IAF called the now completed training a "groundbreaking pilot project".[^cite_note-34]

### Helicopters

From 1996 to 2025, the [Eurocopter AS565 Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_AS565_Panther) *Atalef* of [193 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/193_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Defenders Of The West" at Ramat David served as maritime reconnaissance, surveillance and [SAR](172599.md) helicopters and were used in close cooperation with the [Israeli Navy](1370788.md) as on-board helicopters on ships of the [Sa'ar 5-class corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa'ar_5-class_corvette) and [Sa'ar 6-class corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa'ar_6-class_corvette).[^cite_note-west-35] These have their home port at the [Haifa naval base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa_naval_base), 25 km away.

On 31 August 2025, the 193 Squadron at Ramat David was closed. It is expected to reopen in 2026 at [Palmachim Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmachim_Airbase) with eight [SH-60F Seahawk](202031.md), which had already been purchased by the IAF and Navy from [US Navy](20518076.md) stocks in 2015. The helicopters were extensively converted and equipped with Israeli systems.[^cite_note-crash22-36][^cite_note-seahawk1-37]

Until then, the remaining Atalefs will continue to serve on the ships. The relocation of [193 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/193_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Defenders Of The West" is another indication that Ramat David is being prepared to be abandoned by the IAF and converted into a civilian airport.

### Secret UAVs

There are two squadrons of still-secret [UAVs](58900.md) at Ramat David: the [157 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/157_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "In The Valley" and the [160 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/160_Squadron_\(Israel\)) "Shadow Hunter" (see gallery below). It is assumed that the reason for the secrecy is their [stealthiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_technology). Since other countries have such stealth UAVs for a long time, it can also be assumed that Israel – a leading manufacturer of drones – also has such aircraft, but wants to keep their appearance secret for as long as possible. Large UAVs are also designed to fly for at least 24 hours at a time, which would make the whole of Iran accessible, for example.[^cite_note-38]

### Hezbollah attacks

On 22 August and 22 September 2024, the militant group [Hezbollah](13919.md) launched many rockets from [Lebanon](17771.md) in an attempt to hit the base, but no serious hit has been reported. An [Iron Dome](18342427.md) defense system (see photo above and [32°40′04″N 35°09′38″E  /  32.667847°N 35.160449°E  / 32.667847; 35.160449  (Iron Dome Defense Missiles on Ramat David Airbase)](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Ramat_David_Airbase\&params=32.667847_N_35.160449_E_region\:IL_type\:landmark\&title=Iron+Dome+Defense+Missiles+on+Ramat+David+Airbase)) stationed there had probably intercepted most of the incoming missiles. Hezbollah had previously published a disturbing video showing the base from above by a UAV flying over for several minutes and named various buildings and installations on it. The video also showed that some [Apache attack helicopters](37746.md) from the [Ramon Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airbase) are temporarily stationed there to take part in military operations in Lebanon against Hezbollah.[^cite_note-39]

### 2026 Iran war

During the [Iran war](82533582.md) in early June 2026, [Iran's Revolutionary Guards](1285472.md) say they targeted Israel's Ramat David Airbase.[^cite_note-Iran1-29] The [IDF](39237.md) confirmed that the airbase was hit by a missile strike. A hangar was damaged that is reportedly used for vehicle maintenance.[^cite_note-Iran2-30]

## International airport

Since 2014, there have been considerations to convert Ramat David into a third major international airport alongside [Ben Gurion Airport](113149.md) near [Tel Aviv](31453.md) and [Ramon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airport) near [Eilat](66162.md).[^cite_note-40] The [Nevatim Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevatim_Airbase) in the south of Israel was also being discussed, which could be used for both military and civilian purposes, as was previously the case with the [Ovda Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovda_Airbase) for over 30 years.[^cite_note-41]

In the meantime, the civilian conversion of Ramat David seemed to be off the table, because the local resistance to it is too strong.[^cite_note-42] In 2021, the government's policy was to build two smaller international airports at both [Haifa](55606.md) in the north and [Beersheba](312755.md) in the south.[^cite_note-43] However, after the presentation of an extensive study and audit in 2023, Ramat David is again the top priority for a major airport.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

In July 2025, Israel's National Planning and Construction Council approved a review of plans for two international airports at Ramat David and [Ziklag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziklag) (15 kilometers northwest of [Beersheba](312755.md)), including environmental adjustments, a review of security aspects, and addressing existing construction constraints.[^cite_note-46] In February 2026, the [Israeli government](72545811.md) had finally approved the construction of the two international airports at the locations mentioned above.[^cite_note-47]
