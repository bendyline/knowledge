**Brazil–Lithuania relations** are the [bilateral relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_relations) between [Brazil](3383.md) and [Lithuania](17675.md).

## History



Igreja São José (St Joseph's Church) founded in 1936 by the Lithuanian Community in [Vila Zelina, São Paulo, Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vila_Zelina,_S%C3%A3o_Paulo,_Brazil).

The first [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians) to set foot on Brazilian soil, according to a record dated in 1866, was a certain colonel Andrius Višteliauskas. His mission was to aid the Brazilian armed forces in the [Paraguayan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_War) that was going on at that time. Višteliauskas' experience in Brazil may have influenced people in his homeland, perhaps by his writings or perhaps after he traveled home. A few years later, a group of Lithuanian immigrants and their families arrived in Brazil. In 1890, twenty-five Lithuanian families entered the land of Brazil. Their destination was the newly established colony of [Ijuí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iju%C3%AD), situated on the red and fertile soil of the northwestern part of the state of [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md). Between 1920 and 1930, there was a community of 30,000 Lithuanians residing in Brazil.[^cite_note-Relations-1]

In 1921, Brazil recognized Lithuania's independence, however, Lithuania was [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_the_Baltic_states_\(1944\)) and incorporated into the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1944. Brazil re-recognized Lithuania's independence on 5 November 1991 after the [Dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union).

In March 1996, Lithuanian Prime Minister, [Algirdas Brazauskas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas_Brazauskas), made an official visit to Brazil.[^cite_note-Relations-1] In November 2002, Brazilian Foreign Minister, [Celso Lafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celso_Lafer), visited Lithuania. In July 2008, Lithuanian President, [Valdas Adamkus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdas_Adamkus), made an official visit to Brazil and signed an Agreement of Cultural Cooperation between the two nations.[^cite_note-Relations-1] In June 2012, Lithuanian President, [Dalia Grybauskaitė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalia_Grybauskait%C4%97), paid a visit to Brazil to attend the [United Nations Conference on Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_Sustainable_Development) in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md).[^cite_note-2]

In 2011, Brazil opened an honorary consulate in [Vilnius](32597.md). In 2012, Lithuania closed its embassy in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), [Argentina](18951905.md) (its only embassy in [Latin America](18524.md)), and opened a consulate-general in [São Paulo](390875.md) soon afterwards. São Paulo is the second largest community in the Lithuanian diaspora in Latin America, after Buenos Aires.[^cite_note-Relations-1]

In 2026, Foreign Affairs Committee of [Seimas](571739.md) voted to recommend commencing establishment of an embassy in Brazil to represent bilateral relations with [Mercosur](25869317.md) and [Latin American](18524.md) countries.[^cite_note-3]

## Diaspora

Between 200,000 and 300,000 Brazilians are of [Lithuanian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Brazilians), making Brazil the second country with the largest Lithuanian community abroad (after the [United States](3434750.md)).[^cite_note-Um_atalho_para_a_Europa-4][^cite_note-5]

## Resident diplomatic missions



Honorary consulate of Brazil in Vilnius

- Brazil is accredited to Lithuania from its embassy in [Copenhagen](5166.md), [Denmark](76972.md) and maintains an honorary consulate in [Vilnius](32597.md).
