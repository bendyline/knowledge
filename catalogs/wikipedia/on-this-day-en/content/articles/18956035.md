The **Caribbean**[^cite_note-5] is a geo-political region in the middle of the [Americas](29833.md) centered around the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) in the [North Atlantic Ocean](698.md), mostly overlapping with the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies). Bordered by [North America](21139.md) to the north and also the west through [Central America](6121.md), and [South America](26769.md) to the south, it comprises [numerous islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Caribbean_islands), cays, islets, reefs, and banks.&#x20;

It includes the [Lucayan Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucayan_Archipelago), [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles) of the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies); the [Quintana Roo islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Islands_of_Quintana_Roo) and [Belizean islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Belize) of the [Yucatán Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula); and the [Bay Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Islands_Department#Islands), [Miskito Cays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_Cays), [Archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago_of_San_Andr%C3%A9s,_Providencia_and_Santa_Catalina), [Corn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Islands), and [San Blas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Blas_Islands) of Central America. It also includes the coastal areas on the [continental mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland) of the Americas bordering the region from the Yucatán Peninsula in North America through Central America to the [Guianas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guianas) in South America.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]&#x20;

## Overview



Island groups comprising the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies) in the Caribbean

Situated largely on the [Caribbean plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_plate), the region has thousands of islands, [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet), [reefs](240844.md), and [cays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cay).[^cite_note-brief-8] [Island arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_arc) delineate the northern and eastern edges of the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md):[^cite_note-brief-8] the [Greater Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Antilles) in the north and the [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles), which includes the [Leeward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Islands), [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands), and the [Leeward Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Antilles), to the east and south. The nearby northwestern [Lucayan Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucayan_Archipelago), comprising [The Bahamas](3451.md) and the [Turks and Caicos Islands](30217.md), and the island of [Barbados](3455.md) in the Lesser Antilles, are considered to be a part of the Caribbean despite not bordering the Caribbean Sea. All the islands in the [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles), including the Lucayan Archipelago, form the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies), a term often interchangeable with the *Caribbean*. The [archipelago of Bermuda](3460.md) is not part of the Caribbean, as it lies in the [Sargasso Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargasso_Sea) to the north, but it is an associate member of the [Caribbean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community).[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-\:1-10]&#x20;



[Exclusive economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ) in the Caribbean, with [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone#United_States) zones in blue, [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone#United_Kingdom) in pink, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone#France) in purple, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) in orange, and [Ecuadorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone#Ecuador) in green

On the continental [mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland) of the [Americas](29833.md), the Caribbean coasts of [Mexico](3966054.md), Central America, and South America, including the [Yucatán Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula) of Mexico, [Bay Islands Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Islands_Department) of [Honduras](13394.md), the [Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) region, [Cartagena](63927.md) and [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla) in [Colombia](5222.md), [Maracaibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracaibo) and [Cumaná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuman%C3%A1) in [Venezuela](32374.md), are considered part of the Caribbean.[^cite_note-11] As with the coastal areas of the mainland, [Guyana](17238691.md), [Suriname](26828.md), and [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana), even if they do not border the Caribbean Sea, are often completely included within the Caribbean due to their strong [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography) and [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_geography) ties with the region.&#x20;

Geopolitically, the [islands of the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Caribbean_islands) are often regarded as a subregion of [North America](21139.md), though sometimes they are included in [Middle America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_America_\(Americas\)), or regarded as its own subregion as the *Caribbean*.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The Caribbean is sometimes considered alongside Central America as a region.[^cite_note-14]&#x20;



Political map of Caribbean

Generally, the Caribbean region is organized into 33 [political entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity), including 13 [sovereign states](23604120.md), 12 [dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory), 7 [overseas territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory#Overseas_territory), and various [disputed territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_dispute). From 15 December 1954 to 10 October 2010, there was a territory known as the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles) composed of five islands, all of which were [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) dependencies.[^cite_note-15] From 3 January 1958 to 31 May 1962, there was also a short-lived political union called the British [West Indies Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_Federation) composed of ten English-speaking Caribbean territories, all of which were then British dependencies.&#x20;

The modern Caribbean is one of the most ethnically diverse regions on the planet, as a result of [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) by the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonization_of_the_Americas), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonization_of_the_Americas), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonization_of_the_Americas); the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md) from [Africa](5334607.md); [indentured servitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servitude) from the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) and East Asia; as well as modern immigration from around the world.&#x20;

## Etymology and pronunciation

The region takes its name from the [Caribs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago), an Amerindian ethnic group historically present in the [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles) and parts of adjacent South America that the [Spanish colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) named the region after at the time of the [European conquest of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]&#x20;

The two most prevalent pronunciations of "Caribbean" outside the Caribbean are [/ˌkærɪˈbiːən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*KARR-ə-BEE-ən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), with the primary [stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(linguistics\)) on the third [syllable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syllable), and [/kəˈrɪbiən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*kə-RIB-ee-ən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), with the stress on the second. Most authorities of the last century preferred the stress on the third syllable.[^cite_note-18] This is the older of the two pronunciations, but the stressed-second-syllable variant has been established for over 75 years.[^cite_note-19] It has been suggested that speakers of [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) prefer [/ˌkærɪˈbiːən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*KARR-ə-BEE-ən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) while [North American speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_English) more typically use [/kəˈrɪbiən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*kə-RIB-ee-ən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-20] but major American dictionaries and other sources list the stress on the third syllable as more common in [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) too.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] According to the American version of Oxford Online Dictionaries, the stress on the second syllable is becoming more common in UK English and is increasingly considered "by some" to be more up to date and more "correct".[^cite_note-oed-25]&#x20;

The Oxford Online Dictionaries claim the stress on the second syllable is the most common pronunciation in the Caribbean itself, but according to the *[Dictionary of Caribbean English Usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_Caribbean_English_Usage)*, the most common pronunciation in [Caribbean English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_English) stresses the first syllable instead, [/ˈkærɪbiæn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*KARR-ih-bee-an*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)).[^cite_note-dce-4][^cite_note-oed-25]&#x20;

## Definition



Map of the Caribbean

The word *Caribbean* has multiple uses. Its principal ones are geographical and political. The Caribbean can [also be expanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Caribbean) to include territories with strong cultural and historical connections to Africa, [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery), [European colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) and the [plantation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_system).&#x20;

- The [United Nations geoscheme for the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme_for_the_Americas) presents the Caribbean as a distinct region within the [Americas](29833.md).

- Physiographically, the Caribbean region is mainly a chain of islands surrounding the Caribbean Sea. To the north, the region is bordered by the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), the [Straits of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Florida) and the Northern [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), which lies to the east and northeast. To the south lies the coastline of the [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent) of [South America](26769.md).

## History

Main articles: [History of the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Caribbean) and [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas)



Pre-Columbian languages of the West Indies. [Ciboney Taíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciboney_Ta%C3%ADno), [Classic Taíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Ta%C3%ADno), and [Iñeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C3%B1eri_language) were Arawakan, [Karina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karina_language) and [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_language_\(Trinidad\)) were Cariban. [Macorix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macorix_language), [Ciguayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciguayo_language) and [Guanahatabey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanahatabey_language) are unclassified.

The oldest evidence of humans in the Caribbean is in southern [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad) at [Banwari Trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banwari_Trace), where remains have been found from 7,000 years ago. These pre-ceramic sites, which belong to the Archaic (pre-ceramic) age, have been termed [Ortoiroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortoiroid). The earliest archaeological evidence of human settlement in [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola) dates to about 3600 BC, but the reliability of these finds is questioned. Consistent dates of 3100 BC appear in [Cuba](5042481.md). The earliest dates in the [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles) are from 2000 BC in [Antigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua). A lack of pre-ceramic sites in the [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands) and differences in technology suggest that these Archaic settlers may have Central American origins. Whether an Ortoiroid colonization of the islands took place is uncertain, but there is little evidence of one.&#x20;

[DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) studies changed some of the traditional beliefs about pre-Columbian indigenous history. According to *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic)*, "studies confirm that a wave of pottery-making farmers—known as Ceramic Age people—set out in canoes from the northeastern coast of South America starting some 2,500 years ago and island-hopped across the Caribbean. They were not, however, the first colonizers. On many islands they encountered a foraging people who arrived some 6,000 or 7,000 years ago ... The ceramicists, who are related to today's [Arawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawak_language)-speaking peoples, supplanted the earlier foraging inhabitants—presumably through disease or violence—as they settled new islands."[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

Between 400 BC and 200 BC, the first ceramic-using agriculturalists, the [Saladoid culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladoid_culture), entered Trinidad from South America. They expanded up the Orinoco River to Trinidad, and then spread rapidly up the islands of the Caribbean. Some time after 250 AD another group, the Barancoid, entered Trinidad. The Barancoid society collapsed along the Orinoco around 650 AD and another group, the Arauquinoid, expanded into these areas and up the Caribbean chain. Around 1300 AD a new group, the Mayoid, entered Trinidad and remained the dominant culture until Spanish settlement.&#x20;

At the time of the European discovery of most of the islands of the Caribbean, three major Amerindian indigenous peoples lived on the islands: the [Taíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%ADno) in the [Greater Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Antilles), [The Bahamas](3451.md) and the [Leeward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Islands); the [Island Caribs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago) and [Galibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galibi) in the Windward Islands; and the [Ciboney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciboney) in western Cuba. The Taínos are subdivided into Classic Taínos, who occupied [Puerto Rico](23041.md) and part of Hispaniola; Western Taínos, who occupied the Bahamian archipelago, Cuba, [Jamaica](15660.md), and part of Hispaniola; and the Eastern Taínos, who occupied the northern [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles). The southern Lesser Antilles, including [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) and [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad), were inhabited by both [Carib-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago_language) and Arawak-speaking groups.&#x20;

### European contact

See also: [Slavery in the British and French Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_British_and_French_Caribbean) and [Encomienda § Deaths, disease, and accusations of ethnocide or genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda#Deaths,_disease,_and_accusations_of_ethnocide_or_genocide)

[Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) arrived in the Caribbean on the island of Hispaniola in 1492. Soon afterward, both Portuguese and Spanish explorers began claiming territories in Central and South America. These early colonies brought gold to Europe; most specifically England, the Netherlands, and France. These nations hoped to establish profitable colonies in the Caribbean. Colonial rivalries made the Caribbean a battleground for European wars for centuries.&#x20;

In 1512, after pressure from [Dominican friars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_friars), the [Laws of Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_Burgos) were introduced by the Spanish Crown to better protect the rights of the New World natives. The Spanish used a form of slavery called the *[Encomienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda)*, where slaves would be awarded to the conquistadors, who were charged with protecting and converting their slaves. This had a devastating impact on the population,[^cite_note-27] so starting in 1503, slaves from Africa were imported to the colony. [Jamaica](15660.md) was ceded to [England](9316.md) by [Spain](26667.md) while both [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) and the western third of [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola) were ceded to [France](5843419.md).&#x20;

&#x20;While early slave traders were Portuguese and Spanish, known as the First Atlantic System, by the 17th century the trade became dominated by British, French, and Dutch merchants. This was known as the Second Atlantic System. 5 million African slaves would be taken to the Caribbean, and around half would be traded to the British Caribbean islands. Slavery was abolished first in the Dutch Empire in 1814. Spain abolished slavery in its empire in 1811, with the exceptions of Cuba, Puerto Rico, and Santo Domingo. Slavery was not abolished in Cuba until 1886.[^cite_note-28] Britain [abolished the slave trade in 1807](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Trade_Act_1807), and [slavery proper in 1833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_Abolition_Act_1833).[^cite_note-29] France abolished slavery in its colonies in 1848.



The [Battle of the Saintes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Saintes) between British and French fleets in 1782, by [Nicholas Pocock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Pocock)

The Caribbean was known for [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_Caribbean), especially between 1640 and 1680. The term "[buccaneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccaneer)" is often used to describe a pirate operating in this region. The Caribbean region was war-torn throughout much of its colonial history, but the wars were often based in Europe, with only minor battles fought in the Caribbean. Some wars, however, were born of political turmoil in the Caribbean itself.&#x20;

#### Decolonisation and modern period

In 1791, [a slave rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) in the French colony of Saint-Domingue led to the establishment in 1804 of [Haiti](13373.md), the first republic in the Caribbean.[^cite_note-30] Neighboring Santo Domingo (present-day [Dominican Republic](8060.md)) would attain its independence on three separate occasions in 1821, 1844 and 1865.[^cite_note-BulmerThomas2012-31] Cuba became independent in 1898 following American intervention in the [War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_War_of_Independence) during the [Spanish-American war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War). Following the war, Spain's last colony in the Americas, [Puerto Rico](23041.md), became an unincorporated territory of the United States. The U.S. also took control of [Cuba](5042481.md). The U.S., since the [Monroe Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Doctrine) in the 19th century, has excerted a major influence over the Caribbean. The so-called [Banana Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_Wars) in the early 20th century saw the temporary U.S. occupations of [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_occupation_of_Haiti) and the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Government_of_Santo_Domingo).[^cite_note-\:2-32]&#x20;

&#x20;Between the 1960s and '80s, most of the British holdings in the Caribbean achieved political independence, starting with [Jamaica in 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Independence_Act_1962), then [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_Independence_Act_1962) (1962), [Guyana](17238691.md) (1966), [Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados_Independence_Act_1966) (1966), [The Bahamas](3451.md) (1973), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada) (1974), [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica) (1978), [St. Lucia](27208.md) (1979), [St. Vincent and the Grenadines](27228.md) (1979), [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) (1981) [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md) (1981), and [St. Kitts and Nevis](27198.md) (1983). In case of the Netherlands, [Suriname](26828.md) achieved independence in 1975, while the Netherlands Antilles received autonomy status within the Kingdom in 1954, with Aruba receiving its own in 1986 and Curaçao and St. Maarten in 2010. Presently, the United States, Britain, France and the Netherlands still have some [Caribbean possessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Caribbean#Islands_currently_under_colonial_administration). During the [Cold War](325329.md), the U.S. would again intervene militarily into Caribbean countries including [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Pigs_Invasion), the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Civil_War) and [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Grenada).[^cite_note-\:2-32]



Counter-attack by [Cuban Revolutionary Armed Forces](73251.md) supported by [T-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-34) tanks near Playa Giron during the [Bay of Pigs Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Pigs_Invasion), 19 April 1961.

The decline of the export industries meant a need to diversify the economies of the Caribbean territories. [The tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Caribbean) started developing in the early 20th century, rapidly developing in the 1960s when regular international flights made vacations affordable and is now a \$50 billion industry. Another industry that developed in the early 20th century was [offshore banking and financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_bank), particularly in The Bahamas and the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands), as the proximity of the Caribbean islands to North America made them an attractive location for branches of foreign banks seeking to avail themselves of less complicated regulations and lower tax rates.&#x20;

## Geography



&#x20; The [Caribbean plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_plate) is the reason islands exist here. On the north, [obduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obduction) has scraped rocks onto the [North American plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_plate) (such as [in western Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Cuba)). On the east, the [Lesser Antilles subduction zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles_subduction_zone) produces [volcanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism) and an [island arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_arc). Complex interactions with the [South American plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_plate) has created islands along the southern boundary. [Coral islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_island) are found in [insular Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago_of_San_Andr%C3%A9s,_Providencia_and_Santa_Catalina) near the east coast of Central America.

The geography and climate in the Caribbean region varies: Some islands in the region have relatively flat terrain of non-volcanic origin. These islands include [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) (which has minor volcanic features), [Curaçao](65205.md), [Barbados](3455.md), [Bonaire](108737.md), the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands), [Saint Croix](229431.md), [The Bahamas](3451.md), and [Antigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua). Others possess rugged towering mountain-ranges like the islands of [Saint Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Martin_\(island\)), [Cuba](5042481.md), [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola), [Puerto Rico](23041.md), [Jamaica](15660.md), [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica), [Montserrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montserrat), [Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_\(island\)), [Sint Eustatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint_Eustatius), [Saint Kitts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Kitts), [Saint Lucia](27208.md), [Saint Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas,_U.S._Virgin_Islands), [Saint John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John,_U.S._Virgin_Islands), [Tortola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortola), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), [Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_\(Antilles\)), [Guadeloupe](12343.md), [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md).&#x20;

Definitions of the terms Greater Antilles and Lesser Antilles often vary. As part of the [Puerto Rico Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Bank) and the [Puerto Rico-Virgin Islands microplate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico-Virgin_Islands_microplate), the Virgin Islands are sometimes included with the Greater Antilles. The term Lesser Antilles is often used to define an island arc that includes Grenada but excludes Trinidad and Tobago and the Leeward Antilles.&#x20;

[^cite_note-33]

[^cite_note-34]

The waters of the Caribbean Sea host large, migratory schools of fish, turtles, and [coral reef](87410.md) formations. The [Puerto Rico Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Trench), located on the fringe of the Atlantic Ocean and Caribbean Sea just to the north of the island of Puerto Rico, is the deepest point in all of the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-35]&#x20;

The region sits in the line of several major shipping routes with the [Panama Canal](24849.md) connecting the western Caribbean Sea with the Pacific Ocean.&#x20;

### Climate



Tropical monsoon climate in [San Andrés island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andr%C3%A9s_\(island\)), Caribbean, [Colombia](5222.md).



[Köppen climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) map of the islands of the Caribbean.

The climate of the area is [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical), varying from [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) in some areas to [tropical monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) and [tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) in others. There are also some locations that are [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) climates with considerable drought in some years, and the peaks of mountains tend to have cooler [temperate climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate).&#x20;

Rainfall varies with elevation, size and water currents, such as the cool upwellings that keep the [ABC islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_islands_\(Lesser_Antilles\)) arid. Warm, moist [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds) blow consistently from the east, creating both rain forest and semi arid climates across the region. The tropical rainforest climates include lowland areas near the Caribbean Sea from [Costa Rica](5551.md) north to [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), as well as the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) and [Puerto Rico](23041.md), while the more seasonal dry tropical savanna climates are found in [Cuba](5042481.md), northern [Colombia](5222.md) and [Venezuela](32374.md), and southern [Yucatán, Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula). Arid climates are found along the extreme northern coast of Venezuela out to the islands including [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) and [Curaçao](65205.md), as well as the northwestern tip of Yucatán.&#x20;

While the region generally is sunny much of the year, the wet season from May through November sees more frequent cloud cover (both broken and overcast), while the dry season from December through April is more often clear to mostly sunny. [Seasonal rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) is divided into 'dry' and 'wet' seasons, with the latter six months of the year being wetter than the first half. The air temperature is hot much of the year, varying from 25 to 33 C (77 F to 90 F) between the wet and dry seasons. Seasonally, monthly mean temperatures vary from about 5 C (7 F) in the northern most regions, to less than 3 C in the southernmost areas of the Caribbean.&#x20;

Hurricane season is from June to November, but they occur more frequently in August and September and more common in the northern islands of the Caribbean. [Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricanes) that sometimes batter the region usually strike northward of [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada) and to the west of Barbados. The principal hurricane belt arcs to northwest of the island of Barbados in the Eastern Caribbean. A great example being recent events of [Hurricane Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma) devastating the island of Saint Martin during the 2017 hurricane season.&#x20;

Sea surface temperatures change little annually, normally running from 30 °C (87 °F) in the warmest months to 26 °C (76 °F) in the coolest months. The air temperature is warm year round, in the 70s, 80s and 90s, and varies from winter to summer about 2–5 degrees on the southern islands and about a 10–20 degrees difference on the northern islands of the Caribbean. The northern islands, like The Bahamas, Cuba, Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic, may be influenced by continental masses during winter months, such as cold fronts.&#x20;

### Biodiversity

The Caribbean islands have one of the world's most diverse ecosystems. The animals, fungi, and plants are [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International) [biodiversity hotspots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot) because of their exceptionally diverse terrestrial and marine ecosystems, ranging from montane [cloud forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_forests) to [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) to [cactus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus) [scrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrubland). In the Caribbean, the area of forest increased by 55,500 hectares per year in 1990–2000, 64,200 ha per year in 2000–2015 and 14,200 ha per year in 2015–2025.[^cite_note-42]&#x20;

The region also contains about 8% (by surface area) of the world's coral reefs[^cite_note-43] along with extensive seagrass meadows,[^cite_note-44] often found in the shallow marine waters bordering the region's island and continental coasts.&#x20;

For the fungi, there is a checklist based on nearly 90,000 records derived from specimens in reference collections, published accounts, and field observations.[^cite_note-45] It includes more than 11,250 species of fungi recorded in the region. The checklist is not exhaustive, and it is likely that the true number of fungal species already known in the Caribbean is higher. The number of fungal species in the Caribbean, including species not yet recorded, is likely far higher, given the generally accepted estimate that only about 7% of all fungi worldwide have been discovered.[^cite_note-46] Estimates have been made for endemic fungal species on some Caribbean islands. For Cuba, 2,200 species of fungi have been tentatively identified as possible endemics of the island;[^cite_note-47] for [Puerto Rico](23041.md), the number is 789;[^cite_note-48] for the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), 699;[^cite_note-49] for Trinidad and Tobago, 407.[^cite_note-50]&#x20;

Many ecosystems in the Caribbean islands have been affected by [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), pollution, and human encroachment. The arrival of the first humans is correlated with extinction of [giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyto_pollens) [owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornimegalonyx) and [dwarf ground sloths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalocnus).[^cite_note-51] The hotspot contains dozens of highly threatened animals (birds, mammals, and reptiles), fungi, and plants. The threatened animals include the [Puerto Rican amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_amazon), two species of [solenodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenodon) (giant shrews) in Cuba and Hispaniola, and the [Cuban crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_crocodile).&#x20;



[Saona Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saona_Island), Dominican Republic

The region's coral reefs, which contain about 70 species of hard corals and 500 to 700 species of reef-associated fishes,[^cite_note-52] have undergone rapid decline in ecosystem integrity and are considered vulnerable to global warming and ocean acidification.[^cite_note-53] According to a [UNEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNEP) report, the Caribbean coral reefs might go extinct in next 20 years due to human population explosion along the coastlines, overfishing, pollution of coastal areas, and global warming.[^cite_note-54]&#x20;

Some Caribbean islands have terrain that Europeans found suitable for cultivation for agriculture. [Tobacco](30942.md) was an important early crop during the colonial era, but was eventually overtaken by [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) production as the region's staple crop. Sugar was produced from sugarcane for export to Europe. [Cuba](5042481.md) and [Barbados](3455.md) were historically the largest producers of [sugar](27712.md). The tropical plantation system came to dominate Caribbean settlement. Other islands were found to have terrain unsuited for agriculture, for example [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica), which remains heavily forested. The islands in the southern [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles), [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba), [Bonaire](108737.md) and [Curaçao](65205.md), are extremely arid, making them unsuitable for agriculture, but they have salt pans that were exploited by the Dutch. Sea water was pumped into shallow ponds, producing coarse salt when the water evaporated.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

The natural environmental diversity of the Caribbean islands has led to recent growth in [eco-tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-tourism). This type of tourism is growing on islands lacking sandy beaches and dense human populations.[^cite_note-56]&#x20;

## Politics

### Regionalism



Flag of the [Caribbean Common Market and Community (CARICOM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community)

Caribbean societies are very different from other Western societies in terms of size, culture, and degree of mobility of their citizens.[^cite_note-57] The current economic and political problems the states face individually are common to all Caribbean states. Regional development has contributed to attempts to subdue current problems and avoid projected problems. From a political and economic perspective, [regionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalism_\(international_relations\)) serves to make Caribbean states active participants in current international affairs through collective coalitions. In 1973, the first political regionalism in the [Caribbean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Basin) was created by advances of the English-speaking Caribbean nations through the institution known as the Caribbean Common Market and Community ([CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM)),[^cite_note-58] in Guyana.&#x20;

Certain scholars have argued both for and against generalizing the political structures of the Caribbean. On the one hand the Caribbean states are politically diverse, ranging from socialist systems toward more capitalist Westminster-style parliamentary systems. Other scholars argue that these differences are superficial, and that they tend to undermine commonalities in the various Caribbean states. Contemporary Caribbean systems seem to reflect a "blending of traditional and modern patterns, yielding hybrid systems that exhibit significant structural variations and divergent constitutional traditions yet ultimately appear to function in similar ways".[^cite_note-59] The political systems of the Caribbean states share similar practices.&#x20;

The influence of regionalism in the Caribbean is often marginalized. Some scholars believe that regionalism cannot exist in the Caribbean because each small state is unique. On the other hand, scholars also suggest that there are commonalities amongst the Caribbean nations that suggest regionalism exists. "Proximity as well as historical ties among the Caribbean nations has led to cooperation as well as a desire for collective action".[^cite_note-serbin-60] These attempts at regionalization reflect the nations' desires to compete in the international economic system.[^cite_note-serbin-60]&#x20;

Furthermore, a lack of interest from other major states promoted regionalism in the region. In recent years, the Caribbean has suffered from a lack of U.S. interest. "With the end of the Cold War, U.S. security and economic interests have been focused on other areas. As a result there has been a significant reduction in U.S. aid and investment to the Caribbean."[^cite_note-61] The lack of international support for these small, relatively poor states, helped regionalism prosper.&#x20;

Following the Cold War another issue of importance in the Caribbean has been the reduced economic growth of some Caribbean States due to the United States and [European Union](9317.md)'s allegations of special treatment toward the region by each other.&#x20;

#### United States–European Union trade dispute

See also: [Lomé Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9_Convention)

The [Lomé Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9_Convention), which allowed [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana) exports from the former colonies of the [Group of African, Caribbean and Pacific states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACP_countries) (ACP) to enter Europe cheaply, came into effect in 1976.[^cite_note-62] In 1999, the United States under President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) launched a challenge in the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) against the European Union over its preferential program. The World Trade Organization sided in the United States' favour and the beneficial elements of the convention to African, Caribbean and Pacific states have been partially dismantled and replaced by the [Cotonou Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotonou_Agreement).[^cite_note-63]&#x20;

During the US/EU dispute, the United States threatened to impose large tariffs on European Union goods (up to 100%) to pressure Europe to change the agreement with the Caribbean nations in favour of the Cotonou Agreement.[^cite_note-64]&#x20;

Farmers in the Caribbean have complained of falling profits and rising costs as the Lomé Convention weakens.[^cite_note-65] Some farmers have faced increased pressure to turn toward the cultivation of illegal drugs, which has a higher profit margin and fills the sizable demand for these illegal drugs in North America and Europe.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]&#x20;

#### African Union relations

Many Caribbean nations have sought to deepen ties with the continent of [Africa](5334607.md). The African Union-bloc has referred to the Caribbean as the potential "Sixth Region" of the [African Union](10634933.md).[^cite_note-68] Some Caribbean states have already moved to join Africa institutions including Barbados, Grenada, Guyana, and The Bahamas, which have all become members of the African Export Import Bank.[^cite_note-69] And the Caribbean Development Bank signing a cooperation strategic partnership agreement with the African Development Bank (AfDB)[^cite_note-70] At present Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Jamaica, Guyana, and Suriname are at various stages of establishing direct air flights with Africa to boost person-to-person links and boost trade between both regions.&#x20;

The first inter-regional Africa-Caribbean Community (CARICOM) Summit took place in September 2021. In August 2023 the African Union's [African Export–Import Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Export%E2%80%93Import_Bank) officially opened its first Caribbean Community office in Barbados beginning the process of integrating willing Caribbean states as the 6th region of the African Union.AFREXIMBANK opens Caribbean office in Barbados[^cite_note-71]&#x20;

#### Caribbean Financial Action Task Force and Association of Caribbean States

Caribbean nations have also started to more closely cooperate in the [Caribbean Financial Action Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Financial_Action_Task_Force) and other instruments to add oversight of the offshore industry. One of the most important associations that deal with regionalism amongst the nations of the [Caribbean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Basin) has been the [Association of Caribbean States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Caribbean_States) (ACS). Proposed by CARICOM in 1992, the ACS soon won the support of the other countries of the region. It was founded in July 1994. The ACS maintains regionalism within the Caribbean on issues unique to the Caribbean Basin. Through coalition building, like the ACS and CARICOM, regionalism has become an undeniable part of the politics and economics of the Caribbean. The successes of region-building initiatives are still debated by scholars, yet regionalism remains prevalent throughout the Caribbean.&#x20;

#### Bolivarian Alliance

The President of [Venezuela](32374.md), [Hugo Chavez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Chavez) launched an economic group called the [Bolivarian Alliance for the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_Alliance_for_the_Americas) (ALBA), which several eastern Caribbean islands joined.&#x20;

At the time of [European contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), the dominant ethnic groups in the Caribbean included the [Taíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%ADno) of the [Greater Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Antilles) and northern [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles), the [Island Caribs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Caribs) of the southern Lesser Antilles, and smaller distinct groups such as the [Guanajatabey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanajatabey) of western Cuba and the [Ciguayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciguayo) of eastern Hispaniola. The population of the Caribbean is estimated to have been about 750,000 before European contact. After contact, social disruption and epidemic diseases such as smallpox and measles (to which they had no natural immunity)[^cite_note-85] led to a decline in the Amerindian population,[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] who were replaced by groups such as the [Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people), [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people), [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people), [Fon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fon_people), and [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people), as well as military prisoners from [Ireland](147575.md), who were deported during the Cromwellian reign in [England](9316.md). Immigrants from [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain), [Italy](14532.md), [France](5843419.md), [Spain](26667.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Portugal](23033.md), and [Denmark](76972.md) also arrived, although the mortality rate was high for both groups.[^cite_note-88]&#x20;

The population is estimated to have reached 2.2 million by 1800.[^cite_note-89] Immigrants from [India](14533.md), [China](5405.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), and other countries arrived in the mid-19th century as indentured servants.[^cite_note-90] After the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md) ended, the population increased naturally.[^cite_note-91] The regional population was estimated at 37.5 million by 2000.[^cite_note-p2000-92]&#x20;

In [Haiti](13373.md) and most of the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Caribbean), [Anglophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone_Caribbean) and [Dutch Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_Indies), the population is predominantly of [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Caribbean) origin; on many of the islands/nations there are also significant populations of mixed racial origin (including [Mulatto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulatto)-[Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples), [Dougla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dougla), [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), [Quadroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadroon), [Cholo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholo), [Castizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castizo), [Criollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criollo_people), [Zambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambo), [Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardo), [Chindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chindian), [Cocoa panyols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_panyols), and Eurasian). On the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands), [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) and [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) [mixed-race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_people) people form the majority of the population. There are also populations of European ancestry; [English](18803164.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_people), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) and [Spanish](963152.md) ancestry. [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asians), especially those of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Caribbean), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Caribbean) descent, and [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) [Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_people), form a significant minority in parts of the region. [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Caribbean) form a plurality of the population in [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), [Guyana](17238691.md), and [Suriname](26828.md). Most of their ancestors arrived in the 19th century as indentured laborers.&#x20;

The [Spanish-speaking Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Caribbean) populations are primarily of [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Latin_American), African, or racially mixed origins. [Cuba](1288758.md) has a European majority, along with a significant population of African ancestry. Puerto Rico has a mixed race majority with a mixture of European-African-Native American (tri-racial), and a large White and West African (black) minority. The Dominican Republic has the largest mixed-race population, primarily descended from Europeans, [West Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African), and Amerindians.&#x20;



Carnival in Trinidad and Tobago

The majority of [Jamaica](15660.md) is of West African origin, in addition to a significant population of mixed racial background and minorities of [Chinese](372983.md), Europeans, [Indians](7564733.md), Indigenous, [Jews](25955086.md), and [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab). This is a result of years of importation of slaves and indentured laborers, and migration. Most multi-racial Jamaicans call themselves either mixed-race or brown. Similar populations can be found in the [Caricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caricom) states of [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), [Guyana](17238691.md), [Suriname](26828.md) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md). [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) has a multiracial cosmopolitan society due to the arrivals of [Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Trinidadian), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Trinidadian), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Trinidadian), [Arabs](2185.md) and [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Trinidadian) along with the [native indigenous Amerindians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindians) population. This multiracial mix has created sub-ethnicities that often straddle the boundaries of major ethnicities, such as the [Mulatto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulatto)-[Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples), [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), [Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardo), [Zambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambo), [Dougla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dougla), [Chindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chindian), [Afro-Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asians), Eurasian, and [Cocoa panyols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_panyols).&#x20;

### Language

Main article: [Languages of the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Caribbean)

[Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Spanish) (64%), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) (25%), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_English) (14%), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), [Haitian Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Creole), and [Papiamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papiamento) are the predominant official languages of various countries in the region. Almost every Caribbean country has a distinct [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) or dialect that serves as its [vernacular language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular_language). Most of these do not have official status, with the aforementioned Haitian Creole and Papiamento as notable exceptions. Other languages such as [Caribbean Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Hindustani), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_language), [Amerindian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas), other [African languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_languages), other [European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Europe), and other [Indian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India) like [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language) are also spoken.&#x20;

### Sport

This section is an excerpt from [Culture of the Caribbean § Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Caribbean#Sport).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Culture_of_the_Caribbean\&action=edit)]



[Plaquita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaquita), a Dominican street version of cricket. The Dominican Republic was first introduced to cricket through mid-18th century British contact,

[^cite_note-96]

&#x20;but switched to baseball after the [1916 American occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_occupation_of_the_Dominican_Republic_\(1916%E2%80%931924\)).

[^cite_note-97]

Cricket is a very popular sport in the countries and dependencies that formed the [British West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indies). The [West Indies cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_cricket_team) competes in [Test matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket), [One Day Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) and [Twenty20 Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty20_International). The West Indies' major domestic competitions are the [Regional Four Day Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Four_Day_Competition) ([first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket)) and the [Regional Super50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Super50) ([List A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_A) one-day) and more recently the [Caribbean Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Premier_League) ([Twenty20](770690.md)).&#x20;

The regional football governing body is the [Caribbean Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Football_Union). National teams have competed in the [CFU Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFU_Championship) from 1978 to 1988 and the [Caribbean Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Cup) since 1989. The top 4 teams in the tournament qualify for the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). Cuba qualified to the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in 1938, Haiti in 1974, Jamaica in 1998 and Trinidad and Tobago in 2006.&#x20;

The [CFU Club Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFU_Club_Championship) is the regional tournament for football clubs. The winner qualified for the [CONCACAF Champions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions%27_Cup) from 1997 until 2008. Since 2008–09, the top 3 clubs qualify for a preliminary round of the [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League).&#x20;

However, the most popular sport in [Cuba](5042481.md), [Dominican Republic](8060.md), [Puerto Rico](23041.md), [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) and [Curaçao](65205.md) is [baseball](3850.md). The [Caribbean Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Series), held since 1949, features the national champions from the top Caribbean leagues. The Cuba national team has claimed the [Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup) 25 times, whereas the Dominican Republic won the 2013 [World Baseball Classic](1877416.md). In the [Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Pan_American_Games), Cuba has won 12 editions and Dominican Republic once, whereas Puerto Rico earned the silver medal twice. In the [Central American and Caribbean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Central_American_and_Caribbean_Games), Cuba has won 15 gold medals, whereas Dominican Republic has won three and Puerto Rico two. Several Caribbean players have joined the [Major League Baseball](38776.md): in the 2014 there were 82 Dominicans, 19 Cubans and 11 Puerto Ricans.
