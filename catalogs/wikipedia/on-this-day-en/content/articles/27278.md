**Senegal**,[^cite_note-15] officially the **Republic of Senegal**,[^cite_note-16] is the westernmost country of mainland [West Africa](67393.md), situated along the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) coast. It borders [Mauritania](18778516.md) to [the north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania%E2%80%93Senegal_border), [Mali](19127.md) to [the east](62957126.md), [Guinea](12176.md) to [the southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea%E2%80%93Senegal_border) and [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md) to [the southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau%E2%80%93Senegal_border). Senegal nearly surrounds [the Gambia](65656.md), a country occupying a narrow strip of land along the banks of the [Gambia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia_River), which separates Senegal's southern region of [Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance) from the rest of the country.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] It also shares a maritime border with [Cape Verde](18962637.md). The capital and largest city of Senegal is [Dakar](44251.md).

Senegal is the westernmost country in the mainland of the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World), or [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia).[^cite_note-19] It owes its name to the [Senegal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River), which borders it to the north and east.[^cite_note-\:2-20] The climate is typically [Sahelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel), with a [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season). Senegal covers a land area of almost 197,000 square kilometres (76,000 sq mi) and has a population of around 18 million.[^cite_note-UN_WPP-21][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-22] The state is a [semi-presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system); since the country's foundation in 1960, it has been recognized as one of the most stable countries on the African continent.[^cite_note-23] On the 2024 [V-Dem Democracy Indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_Indices), Senegal is ranked 68th in electoral democracy worldwide and 10th in [electoral democracy in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_democracy_in_Africa).[^cite_note-vdem_dataset-24]

The state was formed as part of the independence of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) from [French colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire). Because of this history, French is the official language, but it is understood by only a small minority of the population.[^cite_note-\:1-25] [Over 30 languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Senegal) are spoken in Senegal. [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language) is the most widely spoken, with 80% of the population speaking it as a first or second language,[^cite_note-What_Languages_Are_Spoken_in_Senegal?-26] acting as Senegal's *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)* alongside French. [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Pulaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulaar) also remain popular among certain communities.[^cite_note-27] Like other African nations, the country includes a wide [mix of ethnic and linguistic communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Senegal), with the largest being the [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_people), [Fula](102383.md), and [Serer people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_people). Senegalese people are predominantly [Muslim](19541.md).[^cite_note-\:0b-28]

As a developing nation, Senegal is classified as a [heavily indebted poor country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavily_indebted_poor_countries), with a relatively low ranking on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (169th out of 193). Most of the population lives on the coast and works [in agriculture or other food industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Senegal); other major industries include mining, tourism, and services.[^cite_note-29] The historic lack of natural resources in Senegal has redirected efforts towards increasing literacy and educational attainment.[^cite_note-30] Senegal is a member state of the [African Union](10634933.md), the United Nations, the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS), [Organisation internationale de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie), the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), and the [Community of Sahel–Saharan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Sahel%E2%80%93Saharan_States).

## Etymology

The country of Senegal is named after the [Senegal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River). The name of the river may derive from a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) transliteration of the name of the [Zenaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenaga_language), also known as the [Sanhaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanhaja). Alternatively, it could be a combination of the supreme deity in [Serer religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_religion) (*Rog Sene*) and *o gal,* meaning ‘*body of water’* in the [Serer language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_language). French author and priest David Boilat proposed that the name comes from the [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language) phrase "sunuu gaal", which means "our canoe".[^cite_note-\:2-20]

## History

Main article: [History of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Senegal)

### Early and pre-colonial eras

Archaeological findings throughout the area indicate that Senegal was inhabited in prehistoric times and has been continuously occupied by various ethnic groups. Some kingdoms were created between the 6th and 14th centuries, such as [Takrur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takrur) in the 6th century, Namandiru, and the [Jolof Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolof_Empire) in the 13th and 14th centuries. Eastern Senegal was once part of the [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire).

Islam was introduced by the [Toucouleur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toucouleur_people) and [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people) through their contact with the [Almoravid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid_dynasty) of the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), who helped spread it with their Toucouleur allies. This movement faced resistance from ethnicities of traditional religions, the Serers in particular.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In the 13th and 14th centuries, the area came under the influence of empires to the east; the Jolof Empire was also founded during this time. In the Senegambia region, between 1300 and 1900, close to one-third of the population was [enslaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Africa), typically as a result of being taken captive in warfare.[^cite_note-33]

In the 14th century, the Jolof Empire grew more powerful, having united [Cayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayor) and the kingdoms of [Baol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baol), [Siné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sine), [Saloum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saloum), [Waalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waalo), [Futa Tooro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futa_Tooro) and [Bambouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambouk), covering much of what is now Senegal and part of present-day West Africa. The empire was a voluntary confederacy of various states rather than a state built on military conquest.[^cite_note-charles-34][^cite_note-35] The empire was founded by Ndiadiane Ndiaye, a part [Serer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_people)[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] and part Toucouleur, who was able to form a coalition with many ethnicities, but collapsed around 1549 following the defeat and death of Lele Fouli Fak at the hands of Amari Ngone Sobel Fall.

### Colonial era

Main articles: [Senegambia (Dutch West India Company)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia_\(Dutch_West_India_Company\)) and [French conquest of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_conquest_of_Senegal)



The [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) was the first European power to colonize Senegal, beginning with the arrival of [Dinis Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinis_Dias) in 1444 at [Gorée Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e) and ending in 1888, when the Portuguese gave [Ziguinchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziguinchor) to the French.

In the mid-15th century, the Portuguese landed on the Senegalese coast, followed by traders representing other countries, including the French.[^cite_note-ross-38] Various European powers—Portugal, the Netherlands, and Great Britain—competed for trade in the area from the 15th century onward.

In 1677, France gained control of what had become a minor hub in the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md): the island of [Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e), near modern Dakar, used as a base to purchase slaves from the warring chiefdoms on the mainland.[^cite_note-h-net.org-39][^cite_note-40]

European missionaries introduced Christianity to Senegal and the [Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance) in the 19th century. It was only in the 1850s that the French began to expand onto the Senegalese mainland, after they abolished slavery and began promoting an [abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_France) doctrine,[^cite_note-41] adding native kingdoms like the Waalo, Cayor, Baol, and Jolof. French colonists under Governor [Louis Faidherbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Faidherbe) progressively invaded and took over all the kingdoms, except the Serer Kingdoms of [Sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sine) and [Saloum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saloum).[^cite_note-charles-34][^cite_note-42]



French and local slave traders in [Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e), 18th century

[Yoro Dyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoro_Dyao) was in command of the canton of Foss-Galodjina and was set over Wâlo (Ouâlo) by Louis Faidherbe,[^cite_note-Royal_II-43] where he served as a chief from 1861 to 1914.[^cite_note-Wolof-44] Senegalese resistance to the French expansion was led in part by [Lat-Dior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lat-Dior), [Damel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damel) of Cayor, and [Maad a Sinig Kumba Ndoffene Famak Joof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maad_a_Sinig_Kumba_Ndoffene_Famak_Joof) (the [Maad a Sinig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maad_a_Sinig), King of Sine), resulting in the famous [Battle of Logandème](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Logand%C3%A8me)―the battle in which the Serer King of [Sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sine) went to war against the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire), where the French decided to take revenge against Sine following their humiliating defeat at [the Battle of Djilor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Djilor?action=edit\&redlink=1). The Battle of Logandème was the first battle on Senegambian soil in which the French employed cannonballs.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

The [Battle of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dakar) (23–25 September 1940) was an unsuccessful [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) attempt to capture the strategic port and overthrow the pro-German [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French) colonial administration.[^cite_note-49]

On 25 November 1958, Senegal became an autonomous republic within the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Community).[^cite_note-50]

### Independence



The short-lived [Mali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation)

In January 1959, Senegal and the [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan) merged to form the [Mali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation), which became fully independent on 20 June 1960, as a result of a transfer of power agreement signed with France on 4 April 1960. Due to internal political difficulties, the Federation broke up on 20 August 1960 when Senegal and French Sudan (renamed the Republic of [Mali](19127.md)) each proclaimed independence.

[Léopold Sédar Senghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor) was elected Senegal's first president in August 1960. Pro-African, Senghor advocated a brand of African socialism.[^cite_note-51]

### 1980 to present

In 1980, Senghor decided to retire from politics. In 1981, he transferred power to his hand-picked successor, [Abdou Diouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdou_Diouf). Former prime minister [Mamadou Dia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamadou_Dia), who was Senghor's rival, ran for election in 1983 against Diouf, but lost.

In the 1980s, [Boubacar Lam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boubacar_Lam) discovered Senegalese oral history that was initially compiled by the [Tuculor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toucouleur_people) noble, [Yoro Dyâo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoro_Dy%C3%A2o), not long after [World War I](4764461.md), which documented migrations into West Africa from the [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley); ethnic groups, from the [Senegal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River) to the [Niger Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_Delta), retained traditions of having an eastern origin.[^cite_note-AfrAm-52]

Senegal joined with [The Gambia](65656.md) to form the nominal [Senegambia Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia_Confederation) on 1 February 1982. However, the union was dissolved in 1989. Despite peace talks, a southern separatist group ([Movement of Democratic Forces of Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Democratic_Forces_of_Casamance) or MFDC) in the [Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance) region has clashed sporadically with government forces since 1982 in the [Casamance conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance_conflict). In the early 21st century, violence has subsided, and President [Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall) held talks with rebels in Rome in December 2012.[^cite_note-53]

Abdou Diouf was president between 1981 and 2000. He encouraged broader political participation, reduced government involvement in the economy, and widened Senegal's diplomatic engagements, particularly with other developing nations. Domestic politics on occasion spilled over into street violence, border tensions, and a violent separatist movement in the southern region of the Casamance. Nevertheless, Senegal's commitment to democracy and human rights strengthened. Abdou Diouf served four terms as president.

In the 1999 presidential election, opposition leader [Abdoulaye Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdoulaye_Wade) defeated Diouf, an election deemed free and fair by international observers. Senegal experienced its second [peaceful transition of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power), and its first from one political party to another. On 30 December 2004, President Wade announced that he would sign a peace treaty with the separatist group in the Casamance region. The [Casamance conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance_conflict), however, has not yet been fully concluded as of 2025 despite several cease fires and treaties between the government and individual factions within the [MFDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Democratic_Forces_of_Casamance).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

In March 2012, the incumbent president, Abdoulaye Wade, lost the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Senegalese_presidential_election), and [Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall) was elected as the new President of Senegal.[^cite_note-57] President Macky Sall was re-elected in [2019 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Senegalese_presidential_election). The presidential term was subsequently reduced from seven years to five years.[^cite_note-\:3-58]



Senegalese President [Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall) with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in Sochi, Russia, 3 June 2022

Since 3 March 2021, Senegal has been rocked by a series of [mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Senegalese_protests) in response to the arrest of [Ousmane Sonko](60022327.md) for alleged rape and mishandling of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). In June 2023, the response to the protests turned increasingly violent, with Amnesty International counting 23 fatalities, most of which were caused by bullets fired by police or armed police collaborators.[^cite_note-police_violence-59]

In March 2024, opposition candidate [Bassirou Diomaye Faye](76426907.md) won Senegal's presidential [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Senegalese_presidential_election) over the ruling coalition's candidate, becoming the youngest president in Senegal's history.[^cite_note-60] In November 2024, Faye announced that France would withdraw its troops from Senegal and close its bases by the end of 2025.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

On 1 July 2025, France handed over the Rufisque joint station to Senegal. Active since 1960, this facility was responsible for communications on the southern Atlantic coast and served as a listening post in efforts to combat maritime trafficking. The handover was conducted with minimal ceremony, involving only the signing of a report. On 17 July 2025, the two military sites were returned to the Senegalese government: the airport base and Camp Geille, a 5-hectare site located in Ouakam. Four villas located in Plateau, near the port, were also transferred to the Senegalese authorities.[^cite_note-63]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Senegal)



[Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall), President of Senegal (2012–2024)



[Abdoulaye Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdoulaye_Wade), President of Senegal (2000–2012)

Senegal is a republic with a presidency; the president is [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Senegal) every five years as of 2016, previously being seven years from independence to 2001, five years from 2001 to 2008, and seven years again from 2008 to 2016, by adult voters. The first president, [Léopold Sédar Senghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor), was a poet and writer, and was the first African elected to the *Académie française*. Senegal's second president, [Abdou Diouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdou_Diouf), later served as the organisation's general secretary. The third president was [Abdoulaye Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdoulaye_Wade), a lawyer. The fourth president was [Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall), elected in March 2012 and reelected in February [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_in_Senegal).[^cite_note-\:3-58] On 25 March 2024, [Bassirou Diomaye Faye](76426907.md) became the fifth democratically elected president.[^cite_note-64]

Senegal has more than 80 political parties. The unicameral parliament consists of the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Senegal), which has 150 seats (a Senate was in place from 1999 to 2001 and from 2007 to 2012).[^cite_note-CIA_Factbook-7] Senegal also has an independent judiciary. The nation's highest courts that deal with business issues are the Constitutional Council and the Court of Justice, both of which have members appointed by the president.

### Political culture

Senegal has a quasi-democratic political culture, one of the more successful post-colonial democratic transitions in Africa. Local administrators are appointed and held accountable by the president. [Marabouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marabout), religious leaders of the various [Muslim brotherhoods of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_brotherhoods_of_Senegal), have also exerted strong political influence in the country, especially during Wade's presidency. In 2009, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) downgraded Senegal's status from "Free" to "Partially Free" due to increased centralisation of power in the executive. By 2014, it had recovered its Free status.[^cite_note-65]

In 2008, Senegal finished in 12th position on the [Ibrahim Index of African Governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Index_of_African_Governance),[^cite_note-ibrahim_index-66] which reflects the success with which governments deliver essential political goods to their citizens. When the Northern African countries were added to the index in 2009, Senegal's 2008 position was retroactively downgraded to 15th place (with Tunisia, Egypt, and Morocco placing ahead of Senegal). As of 2012, Senegal's Ibrahim Index ranking had declined to 16th among 52 African countries.

On 22 February 2011, Senegal severed diplomatic ties with [Iran](14653.md), saying it supplied rebels with weapons which killed Senegalese troops in the [Casamance conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance_conflict).[^cite_note-67]

The [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_presidential_election,_2012) was controversial due to President Wade's candidacy, as the opposition argued that he was not eligible to run again. Several youth opposition movements, including M23 and [Y'en a Marre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y'en_a_Marre), emerged in June 2011. In the end, Macky Sall of the [Alliance for the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_the_Republic_\(Senegal\)) won, and Wade conceded the election to Sall. This peaceful and democratic transition was hailed by many foreign observers, including the EU,[^cite_note-68] as a show of "maturity".

On 19 September 2012, lawmakers voted to abolish the Senate to save an estimated \$15 million.[^cite_note-69]

In August 2017, the ruling party won a landslide victory in the [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Senegalese_parliamentary_election). President Macky Sall's ruling coalition took 125 seats in the 165-seat National Assembly.[^cite_note-70] In 2019 president Macky Sall easily won [re-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Senegalese_presidential_election) in the first round.[^cite_note-71] On 2 April 2024, his successor Bassirou Diomaye Faye was sworn in.[^cite_note-72]

### Leadership in World governance initiatives

Senegal has been one of the signatories to the agreement to convene a convention to draft a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] As a result, in 1968, for the first time, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly) convened to draft and adopt the [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth).[^cite_note-75] [Léopold Sédar Senghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor), then [president of Senegal](450099.md) signed the agreement to convene a World Constituent Assembly.[^cite_note-76]

### Foreign relations

Further information: [Foreign relations of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Senegal)



The African Renaissance Monument was a \$27 million project by Abdoulaye Wade that triggered popular protests.

[^cite_note-79]

Senegal has a high profile in many international organizations and was a member of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) in 1988–1989 and 2015–2016. It was elected to the [UN Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Commission_on_Human_Rights) in 1997. Friendly toward the West, especially the United States, Senegal has vigorously advocated for greater assistance from developed countries. Senegal is well integrated with the central bodies of the international community, including the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS), the [African Union](10634933.md) (AU), and the [Community of Sahel–Saharan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Sahel%E2%80%93Saharan_States).

Historically, Senegal has been close to France, its former colonizer, but this has caused significant tension with the people and was one of the reasons former President Macky Sall lost support. Senegalese often complained that Sall consistently awarded French companies priority contracts to extract Senegal's natural resources rather than giving them to whoever offered Senegal the best deal. They also believed France was pushing Macky Sall to run for an unconstitutional third term. After debating whether to run a third time for months, Sall did not.[^cite_note-80]

Senegal enjoys cordial relations primarily with its neighbors. Despite evident progress on other issues with [Mauritania](18778516.md) (border security, resource management, economic integration, etc.), an estimated 35,000 Mauritanian refugees (of the 40,000 who were expelled from their home country in 1989) remain in Senegal.[^cite_note-81] [Morocco–Senegal relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco%E2%80%93Senegal_relations) have also been courteous and [Bassirou Diomaye Faye](76426907.md) invited the king of Morocco on his inauguration ceremony in 2024.

Senegal is the 84th most peaceful country in the world, according to the 2024 [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index).[^cite_note-82]

### Military



Land mines were widely used in the [Casamance conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance_conflict) between separatist rebels and the central government.



[Senegalese patrol vessel *Poponquine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_patrol_vessel_Poponquine) training with a [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) vessel off the coast of Senegal

The [Armed Forces of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Senegal) consist of about 17,000 personnel in the army, [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_Air_Force), navy, and gendarmerie. The Senegalese military receives most of its training, equipment, and support from France and the United States, and to a lesser extent, Germany.

Military non-interference in political affairs has contributed to Senegal's stability since independence. Senegal has participated in many international and regional peacekeeping missions. Most recently, in 2000, Senegal sent a battalion to the [Democratic Republic of Congo](76762.md) to participate in [MONUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUC), the United Nations peacekeeping mission, and agreed to deploy a US-trained battalion to [Sierra Leone](27308.md) for [UNAMSIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAMSIL), another UN peacekeeping mission.

In 2015, Senegal participated in the Saudi Arabian-led [military intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) against the [Shia](26961.md) [Houthis](17240332.md).[^cite_note-83]

### Law

Senegal is a secular state, as defined in its [Constitution](26244481.md).[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Senegal-84]

To fight corruption, the government has established the National Anti-Corruption Office (OFNAC) and the Commission for the Restitution and Recovery of Illegally Acquired Assets. According to the Business Anti-Corruption Portal, President Sall created the OFNAC to replace the Commission Nationale de Lutte Contre la non-Transparence, la Corruption et la Concussion (CNLCC). It is said that the OFNAC represents a more effective tool for fighting corruption than the CNLCC established under former President Wade.[^cite_note-85] The mission of OFNAC is to fight corruption, embezzlement of public funds, and fraud. OFNAC has the power of self-referral (own initiative investigation). OFNAC is composed of twelve members appointed by decree.

[Homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Senegal) is illegal in Senegal.[^cite_note-86] According to a 2013 survey by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), 96% of Senegalese believe that homosexuality should not be accepted by society.[^cite_note-pewglobal.org-87] LGBTQ community members in Senegal report a strong feeling of being unsafe.[^cite_note-88]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Senegal)



Senegal map of Köppen climate classification



Landscape of [Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance)

Senegal is located on the western coast of Africa. It lies between latitudes [12°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_parallel_north) and [17°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_parallel_north), and longitudes [11°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_meridian_west) and [18°W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_meridian_west).

Senegal is externally bounded by the Atlantic Ocean to the west, [Mauritania](18778516.md) to the north, [Mali](19127.md) to the east, and [Guinea](12176.md) and [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md) to the south; internally it almost completely surrounds [The Gambia](65656.md), namely on the north, east and south, except for Gambia's short Atlantic coastline.

The Senegalese landscape consists mainly of the rolling sandy plains of the western [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel), which rise to foothills in the southeast. Here is also found Senegal's highest point, Baunez ridge situated 2.7 km southeast of [Nepen Diakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepen_Diakha) at 648 m (2,126 ft).[^cite_note-89] The northern border is formed by the [Senegal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River); other rivers include the [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia_River) and [Casamance Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance_River). The capital Dakar lies on the [Cap-Vert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Vert) peninsula, the westernmost point of continental Africa.

The [Cape Verde](18962637.md) islands lie some 560 kilometres (350 mi) off the Senegalese coast, but Cap-Vert ("Cape Green") is a maritime placemark, set at the foot of "Les Mammelles", a 105-metre (344 ft) cliff resting at one end of the Cap-Vert peninsula onto which is settled Senegal's capital Dakar, and 1 kilometre (0.6 mi) south of the "Pointe des Almadies", the westernmost point in Africa.

Senegal contains four terrestrial ecoregions: [Guinean forest-savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest-savanna_mosaic), [Sahelian Acacia savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_Acacia_savanna), [West Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sudanian_savanna), and [Guinean mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_mangroves).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-90]

### Climate

Main article: [Geography of Senegal § Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Senegal#Climate)



Beach at [N'Gor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngor,_Dakar)&#x20;

Senegal has a [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) with pleasant year-round heat and well-defined dry and humid seasons driven by northeast winter and southwest summer winds. The dry season (December to April) is characterized by hot, dry [harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan) winds.[^cite_note-CIA_Factbook-7] Dakar's annual rainfall of about 600 mm (24 in) occurs between June and October when maximum temperatures average 30 °C (86.0 °F) and minimums 24.2 °C (75.6 °F); December to February maximum temperatures average 25.7 °C (78.3 °F) and minimums 18 °C (64.4 °F).[^cite_note-91]

Interior temperatures are higher than along the coast (for example, average daily temperatures in Kaolack and Tambacounda for May are 30 °C (86.0 °F) and 32.7 °C (90.9 °F) respectively, compared to Dakar's 23.2 °C (73.8 °F) ),[^cite_note-92] and rainfall increases substantially farther south, exceeding 1,500 mm (59.1 in) annually in some areas.

In Tambacounda, located in the far interior near the Mali border where the desert begins, temperatures can reach 54 °C (129.2 °F). The northernmost part of the country is the [Lompoul desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lompoul_desert), which has a near-[hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate#Hot_desert_climates), the central part has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates). The southernmost part has a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate). Senegal is mainly a sunny and dry country.



Economic impact of 2°C in Senegal

[Climate change in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Senegal) will have wide reaching impacts on many aspects of life in Senegal. [Climate change](5042951.md) will cause an increase in average temperatures over west Africa by between 1.5 and 4 °C (3 °F and 7 °F) by mid-century, relative to 1986–2005.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:7-93] Projections of rainfall indicate an overall decrease in rainfall and an increase in intense mega-storm events over the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel).[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:8-94][^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:9-95] The sea level is expected to rise faster in [West Africa](67393.md) than the global average.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:10-96][^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:11-97] Although Senegal is currently not a major contributor to global [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md), it is one of the countries most [vulnerable](66204363.md) to climate change.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:2-98][^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:12-99]

Extreme [drought](37335.md) is impacting [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), and causing food and job insecurity. More than 70% of the [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_\(human_biology\)) is employed in the [agricultural sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Senegal). [Sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) and resulting [coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) is expected to cause damage to coastal infrastructure and displace a large percentage of the population living in [coastal](5236.md) areas. Climate change also has the potential to increase [land degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_degradation) that will likely increase [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification) in eastern Senegal, leading to an expansion of the [Sahara](325363.md).[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_Senegal_\:1-100]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Senegal](27283.md)

Senegal has a [developing economy](78449.md) driven by mining, construction, tourism, fishing and agriculture, which are the main sources of employment in rural areas. Natural resources include iron, zircon, gold, phosphates, and now oil and gas. In the past Senegal's economy gained most of its foreign exchange from [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_fishing), [phosphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_phosphate), [groundnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_industry). One of the historically dominant parts of the economy, [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), is highly vulnerable to environmental conditions such as variations in rainfall and climate, and fluctuations in world [commodity prices](48190.md). It is a member of the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization).

The capital of Senegal, [Dakar](44251.md), was the former capital of all of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa). As a result, it remains the home to major banks and other institutions which serve all of Francophonic West Africa and is the hub for shipping and transport into and out of the entire region, which benefits landlocked neighboring [Mali](19127.md).

Senegal has one of the most developed tourist industries in Africa. The main obstacles to the economic development of the country are its great [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Senegal) with inefficient justice, very slow administrative formalities, and a failing education sector.[^cite_note-101]

### Industry and trade

Three trans-African automobile routes pass through Senegal:

- [Cairo-Dakar Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo-Dakar_Highway) (1)

- [Dakar-Ndjamena Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Ndjamena_Highway) (5)

- [Dakar-Lagos Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Lagos_Highway) (7)

The main industries include [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), mining, cement, [artificial fertilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_fertilizer), [chemicals](58721.md), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry), [refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refining) imported petroleum, and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Senegal). Exports include fish, chemicals, cotton, fabrics, groundnuts, and [calcium phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_phosphate). The largest export markets as of 2020 are [Mali](19127.md) (20.4%), Switzerland (12.2%), and India (8.3%).[^cite_note-102]

As a member of the [West African Economic and Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Economic_and_Monetary_Union) (WAEMU), Senegal is working toward greater regional integration with a unified external [tariff](55551.md). Senegal is also a member of the [Organization for the Harmonization of Business Law in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHADA).[^cite_note-ohada.com-103]

Senegal achieved full Internet connectivity in 1996, creating a mini-boom in information technology-based services. Private activity now accounts for 82 percent of its GDP. On the negative side, Senegal faces deep-seated urban problems of chronic high unemployment.[^cite_note-104]

Senegal is a major recipient of international development assistance. Donors include the [United States Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) (USAID), Japan, France, and China. Over 4,000 [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps) Volunteers have served in Senegal since 1963.[^cite_note-peacecorps.gov-105]

### Agriculture

This section is an excerpt from [Agriculture in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Senegal).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agriculture_in_Senegal\&action=edit)]

[Cowpea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowpea) vendors near Thies, Senegal

Agriculture is one of the dominant parts of Senegal's economy, even though Senegal lies within the drought-prone [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region. As only a tiny percentage of the land is [irrigated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation),[^cite_note-106] Senegal continues to rely on rain-fed agriculture. Agriculture occupies about 75% of the workforce. Despite a relatively wide variety of agricultural production, most farmers produce for [subsistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture) needs. [Millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), and [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum) are the primary food crops grown in Senegal. Production is subject to drought and threats of [pests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_\(organism\)) such as [locusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locusts), birds, [fruit flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tephritidae), and [white flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_flies).[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Senegal_fas-107] Moreover, the [effects of climate change in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Senegal) are expected to severely harm the agricultural economy due to [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) such as drought, as well as increased temperatures.[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Senegal_\:0-Sen-108]

Senegal is a net food importer, particularly for rice, which represents almost 75% of cereal imports. [Peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), and [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) are important [cash crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crops), and a wide variety of fruits and vegetables are grown for local and export markets. In 2006, [gum arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gum_arabic) exports soared to \$280 million, making it by far the leading agricultural export. [Green beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_beans), industrial [tomato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), [cherry tomato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_tomato), [melon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon), and [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango) are Senegal's main vegetable cash crops. The [Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance) region, isolated from the rest of Senegal by [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia), is an important agricultural producing area, but without the infrastructure or [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Senegal) links to improve its capacity.[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Senegal_fas-107]

Despite the lack of modernization of [artisanal fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisanal_fishing), the fishing sector remains Senegal's main economic resource and major foreign exchange earner. The livestock and poultry sectors are relatively underdeveloped and have potential for modernization, development and growth. Senegal imports most of its milk and dairy products. The sector is inhibited due to low output and limited investments. The potential production of fauna and forest products is high and diversified, and could, if well organized, benefit poor farmers in rural areas. Although the agricultural sector was impacted by a locust invasion in 2004, it has recovered and gross agricultural production is expected to increase by 6.1% in 2006 and 5.1% in 2007.[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Senegal_fas-107]

### Fishing



Fishing boats in Dakar

Senegal has a 12-nautical-mile (22 km; 14 mi) exclusive fishing zone. Fishing is a sizable sector of the Senegalese economy, as 3.2 percent of Senegal's GDP and around 600,000 jobs come from the fishing industry.[^cite_note-\:0-109] Overfishing and illegal fishing from international vessels are serious issues in Senegal, and have severely impacted the number of fish on Senegalese shores. 57% of fish stocks in Senegal are in a "state of collapse",[^cite_note-110] and some boats—especially [trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler)—owned by other countries fly the Senegalese flag in order to evade the rules and fish in the country's waters, harming artisanal fishermen in the process.[^cite_note-\:0-109][^cite_note-111]

In May 2024, Senegal publicly released the list of all boats with valid licenses allowed to fish in their waters as an effort to reduce [IUU fishing](6346579.md).[^cite_note-112]

### Energy

This section is an excerpt from [Energy in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Senegal).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Energy_in_Senegal\&action=edit)]



Electricity generation in Senegal in terawatt-hours

As of April 2020, the [energy sector in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Senegal) has an installed capacity of 1431 megawatts (MW).[^cite_note-Energy_in_Senegal_\:0-113] Energy is produced by private operators and sold to the [Senelec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senelec) energy corporation. According to a 2020 report by the International Energy Agency, Senegal had nearly 70% of the country connected to the national grid.[^cite_note-Energy_in_Senegal_\:1-114] Current government strategies for electrification include investments in off-grid solar and connection to the grid.[^cite_note-Energy_in_Senegal_\:0-113][^cite_note-Energy_in_Senegal_\:1-114]

Most of the energy production is from [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel), mostly [diesel](81761.md) and gas (733 of 864 MW).[^cite_note-Energy_in_Senegal_\:0-113] An increasing amount of the energy production comes from [sustainable sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy), such as [Manantali Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manantali_Dam) in [Mali](19127.md) and a new wind farm in [Thiès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thi%C3%A8s_Region) opened in 2020—however, it is still a small portion of the total production. Despite increases in production in the 2010s, the economy is frequently hindered by energy shortages compared to demand.

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Senegal)



Senegal's population from 1960 to 2017 (in millions)

Senegal has a population of about 18 million, of whom about 42 percent live in rural areas. Density in these areas varies from about 77 inhabitants per square kilometre (200/sq mi) in the west-central region to 2 per square kilometre (5.2/sq mi) in the arid eastern section.

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Senegal)

French is the [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language), spoken by all those who have spent several years in the educational system, where French is used as the medium of instruction (Koranic schools are also popular, but Arabic is less widely spoken outside the context of recitation). Overall, French speakers were estimated to make up 26% of the population in 2022.[^cite_note-121] During the 15th century, many European territories started to engage in trade in Senegal. In the 19th century, France increased its colonial influence in Senegal, and the number of French-speaking people grew steadily. French was ratified as the official language of Senegal in 1960 when the country achieved independence.

Most people also speak their own ethnic language, while, especially in Dakar, [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language) is the [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca).[^cite_note-122] [Pulaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulaar) is spoken by the Fulas and Toucouleur. The [Serer language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_language) is widely spoken by both Serers and non-Serers (including President Sall, whose wife is Serer); so are the [Cangin languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cangin_languages), whose speakers are ethnically Serers. [Jola languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jola_languages) are widely spoken in the [Casamance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamance). Overall, Senegal is home to around 39 distinct languages. Several have the legal status of "[national languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language)": [Balanta-Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanta-Ganja_language), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Jola-Fonyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jola-Fonyi_language), [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_language), [Mandjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandjak_language), [Mankanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankanya_language), [Noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noon_language) (Serer-Noon), [Pulaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulaar_language), Serer, [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_language), and Wolof.



Ethnolinguistic map of Senegal

English is taught as a [foreign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_as_a_second_or_foreign_language) in secondary schools and many graduate school programs. It is the only subject matter that has a special office in the Ministry of Education.[^cite_note-warwick-123] Dakar is home to a few bilingual schools which offer half of their curriculum in English. The Senegalese American Bilingual School (SABS), Yavuz Selim, and The West African College of the Atlantic (WACA) train thousands of fluent English speakers in four-year programs. English is widely used by the scientific community and in business, including by the *Modou-Modou* (illiterate, self-taught businessmen).[^cite_note-warwick-123]

[Portuguese Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Creole), locally known as Portuguese, is a prominent minority language in [Ziguinchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziguinchor), the regional capital of the Casamance, spoken by local Portuguese creoles and immigrants from Guinea-Bissau. The local Cape Verdean community speaks a similar Portuguese creole, [Cape Verdean Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_Creole), as well as standard Portuguese. Portuguese was introduced in Senegal's secondary education in 1961 in Dakar by the country's first president, [Léopold Sédar Senghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor). It is currently available in most of Senegal and in higher education. It is especially prevalent in Casamance, particularly in relation to local cultural identity.[^cite_note-124]



Aerial view of Yoff Commune, Dakar

A variety of immigrant languages are spoken, such as [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language) (70,000), [Mooré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mossi_language) (37,000), [Kabuverdiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_Creole) (34,000), [Krio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krio_language) (6,100), Vietnamese (2,500), and Portuguese (1,700), mostly in Dakar.[^cite_note-warwick-123]

While French is the sole official language, a rising Senegalese linguistic nationalist movement supports the integration of Wolof, the country's common vernacular, into the national constitution.[^cite_note-125]

Senegalese regions of Dakar, Diourbel, Fatick, Kaffrine, Kaolack, Kedougou, Kolda, Louga, Matam, Saint-Louis, Sedhiou, Tambacounda, Thies, and Ziguinchor are members of the International Association of Francophone regions.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Senegal)

Religion in Senegal ([Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) 2024)

[^cite_note-Pew_Research-128]

1. [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Senegal) (89.0%)

2. [Christianity](5211.md) (10.0%)

3. [Traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Traditional_Religion) (1.00%)

Senegal is a secular state.[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Senegal-84] According to "CIA World Factbook: Senegal" [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Senegal) is the predominant religion in the country, practiced by 97.2% of the country's population; the Christian community, at 2.7% of the population, consists mostly of [Roman Catholics](606848.md) but there are also diverse [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) denominations. Less than 1% hold [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) beliefs, particularly in the southeastern region of the country.[^cite_note-CIA_Factbook-7] Some [Serer people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_people) follow the [Serer religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_religion).[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] According to the Berkley Center, "approximately 95 percent of the population is Muslim and the other five percent is primarily Christian or animist."[^cite_note-131] Marloes Janson, of SOAS, University of London, and other scholars, posit that, in Senegal, [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia), and many African countries where Islam is dominant, Muslim communities tend to [syncretise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism) Islam with [Traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions), leading to a distinctive "African Islam".[^cite_note-132]

According to a 2012 Pew demographic study, 55% of the Muslims in Senegal are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) of the [Maliki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maliki) [madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab) with [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) influences, whilst 27% are [non-denominational Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim).[^cite_note-Pew-133] Islamic communities in Senegal are generally organized around one of several Islamic Sufi orders called [tariqas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqa), headed by a *[khalif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalif)* (*xaliifa* in [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language), from Arabic *khalīfa*), who is usually a direct descendant of the group's founder; the study found that 92% of Senegalese Muslims belonged to a Sufi order. The two most significant and prominent Sufi tariqas in Senegal are the [Tijaniyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijaniyya), whose largest Senegalese sub-groups are based in the cities of [Tivaouane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivaouane) and [Kaolack](1606283.md), and have a broad following in West Africa outside of Senegal, and the [Murīdiyya (Murid)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouride), who are based in the city of [Touba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touba,_Senegal) and has a follower base mostly limited to within Senegal.[^cite_note-Pew-133]



A [mosque](19894.md) in [Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Louis,_Senegal).



[Our Lady of Victories Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Victories_Cathedral,_Dakar), a [Catholic Church](606848.md) in [Dakar](44251.md).

The [Halpulaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halpulaar) ([Pulaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulaar)-speakers), composed of [Fula people](102383.md), a widespread group found along the Sahel from Chad to Senegal, and *[Toucouleurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toucouleur_people)*, represent 23.8 percent of the population.[^cite_note-CIA_Factbook-7] Historically, they were the first to become Muslim. Many of the *Toucouleurs*, or sedentary Halpulaar of the Senegal River Valley in the north, converted to Islam around a millennium ago and later contributed to Islam's propagation throughout Senegal. Most communities south of the Senegal River Valley, however, were not thoroughly [Islamized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization). The Serer people stood out as one of this group, who spent over one thousand years resisting Islamization (see [Serer history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_history_\(medieval_era_to_present\))). Although many Serers are Christians or Muslim, their conversion to Islam in particular is very recent and came of free will rather than by force, after forced conversion had been unsuccessfully tried centuries earlier (see [Battle of Fandane-Thiouthioune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fandane-Thiouthioune)).[^cite_note-134]



The [Mouride brotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouride_brotherhood) in Senegal

As a country with more than 90% Muslims, holidays such as Tabaski, Koriteh, Gamou, and Weri Kor are highly regarded.

The spread of formal Quranic schools (called *daara* in Wolof) during the colonial period was driven mainly by the Tidjâniyya. In Murid communities, which place greater emphasis on the work ethic than on literary Quranic studies, the term *daara* often refers to work groups devoted to serving a religious leader. Today, most Senegalese children study at *daara*s for several years, memorizing as much of the Qur'an as they can. Some of them continue their religious studies at councils (*majlis*) or at the growing number of private Arabic schools and publicly funded Franco-Arabic schools.

Small Catholic communities are mainly found in coastal Serer, [Jola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jola_people), [Mankanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankanya_people), and Balant populations, and in eastern Senegal among the Bassari and Coniagui. Immigrants mainly attend Protestant churches, but during the second half of the 20th century, these churches, led by Senegalese leaders from different ethnic groups, have evolved. In Dakar, Catholic and Protestant rites are practiced by the Lebanese, Cape Verdean, European, and American immigrant populations, by certain Africans from other countries, and by the Senegalese themselves.



An [imam](15240.md) in Senegal leading a prayer

[Serer religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_religion) encompasses a belief in a supreme deity called [Roog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roog) ([Koox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koox) among the [Cangin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cangin_languages)), [Serer cosmogony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_creation_myth), [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_cosmology) and [divination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divination) ceremonies such as the annual *[Xooy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xooy)* (or *Khoy*) ceremony presided over by the Serer [Saltigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltigue) (high priests and priestesses). They were ancient Serer festivals rooted in Serer religion, not Islam.[^cite_note-N.Diouf-135]

The [Boukout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boukout) is one of the Jola's religious ceremonies.

A small number of members of the Bani Israel tribe in the Senegalese bush claim Jewish ancestry. However, this is disputed.[^cite_note-136] The [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) branch of [Buddhism in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Senegal) is followed by a very tiny portion of the [expat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) [Vietnamese community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Vietnamese). The [Bahá'í Faith in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'%C3%AD_Faith_in_Senegal) was established after ['Abdu'l-Bahá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Abdu'l-Bah%C3%A1), the son of the founder of the religion, mentioned Africa as a place that Bahá'ís should more broadly visit.[^cite_note-ToDP8-137] The first Bahá'is to set foot in the territory of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) that would become Senegal arrived in 1953.[^cite_note-hassal-egypt-138] The first Bahá'í [Local Spiritual Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Spiritual_Assembly) of Senegal was elected in 1966 in [Dakar](44251.md).[^cite_note-50th-139] In 1975 the Bahá'í community elected the first [National Spiritual Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Spiritual_Assembly) of Senegal. The most recent estimate, by the [Association of Religion Data Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Religion_Data_Archives) in a 2005 report, details the population of Senegalese Bahá'ís at 22,000.[^cite_note-WCE-05-140]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Senegal)



Development of life expectancy

Life expectancy at birth was estimated to be 66.8 years in 2016 (64.7 years male, 68.7 years female).[^cite_note-141] Public expenditure on health was at 2.4 percent of the GDP in 2004, whereas private expenditure was at 3.5 percent.[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-142] Health expenditure was at US\$72 (PPP) per capita in 2004.[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-142] The fertility rate ranged 5 to 5.3 between 2005 and 2013, with 4.1 in urban areas and 6.3 in rural areas, as official survey (6.4 in 1986 and 5.7 in 1997) point out.[^cite_note-143] There were six physicians per 100,000 persons in the early 2000s (decade).[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-142] [Infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality) in Senegal was 157 per 1,000 live births in 1950., but since then it has declined five-fold to 32 per 1,000 in 2018.[^cite_note-144] In the past five years infant mortality rates of malaria have dropped. According to a 2013 UNICEF report,[^cite_note-UNICEF2013p27-145] 26% of women in Senegal have undergone [female genital mutilation](11408.md).

In March 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic began in Senegal, prompting the imposition of a curfew.[^cite_note-146] [Malaria](20423.md) is [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(epidemiology\)) throughout Senegal, and the entire population is at risk.[^cite_note-147]

In June 2021, Senegal's Agency for Universal Health launched sunucmu.com (SunuCMU), a website that the agency hopes will streamline health care in the country. The website is a part of the Minister of State Mohammad Abdallah Dionne's plan for digitalization. He aims to make Senegal's health care system effective and sustainable. Using SunuCMU, Senegal hopes to achieve 75 percent coverage within two years of the launch.[^cite_note-148]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Senegal)



Students in Senegal

Articles 21 and 22 of the Constitution, adopted in January 2001, guarantee access to education for all children.[^cite_note-ilab-149] Education is compulsory and free up to the age of 16.[^cite_note-ilab-149] The Ministry of Labor has indicated that the public school system is unable to cope with the number of children that must enroll each year.[^cite_note-ilab-149] Portuguese is taught at schools at the secondary high school level, given the large Cape Verdean community, and also from Guinea-Bissau. There are sizeable Portuguese Creole and standard Portuguese-speaking communities in Ziguinchor and Dakar.

[Illiteracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) is high, particularly among women.[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-142] The net primary enrollment rate was 69 percent in 2005. In the fiscal year 2025, 3.5 percent of the budget was allocated for the Ministry of Education.[^cite_note-150] Public expenditure on education was 5.4 percent of the 2002–2005 GDP. Senegal was ranked 89th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

## Culture



A [jingle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_art_in_South_Asia) [share taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi) in [Dakar](44251.md), 2026



Ngor Island



Landscape near Kaolack

Senegal is well known for the West African tradition of storytelling, performed by *[griots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griot)*, who have kept West African history alive for thousands of years through words and music. The *griot* profession is passed down generation to generation and requires years of training and apprenticeship in genealogy, history, and music. *Griots* give voice to generations of West African society.[^cite_note-ross-38]

The *[African Renaissance Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Renaissance_Monument)*, built in 2010 in [Dakar](44251.md), is the [tallest statue in Africa](9830701.md). Dakar also hosts a film festival, Recidak.[^cite_note-153]

The Islamic festival of [Eid al-Adha](358226.md), known locally as Tabaski, is popularly celebrated by Senegalese people. Despite being predominantly Muslim, the Christian celebration of Christmas is also widely observed, with Christmas trees and decorations lining the streets of Dakar.[^cite_note-\:0b-28]

### Cuisine

Further information: [Senegalese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_cuisine)

Because Senegal borders the Atlantic Ocean, [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_as_food) is very important. [Chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_as_food), [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton), [peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pea), [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_\(food\)), and beef are also used in Senegalese cooking, but not pork, due to the nation's essentially [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) population. [Peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), the primary crop of Senegal, as well as [couscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couscous), [white rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_rice), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato), [lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentil), [black-eyed peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-eyed_pea), and various vegetables, are also incorporated into many recipes. Meats and vegetables are typically stewed or marinated in herbs and spices, then served over rice or couscous or with bread.

Popular fresh juices are made from [bissap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiscus), [ginger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginger), *buoy&#x20;*&#x20;(pronounced 'buoy', which is the fruit of the [baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansonia_digitata) tree, also known as "monkey bread fruit"), [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), or other fruit or wild trees (most famously [soursop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soursop), which is called *corossol* in French). Desserts are vibrant and sweet, combining native ingredients with the extravagance and style characteristic of French influence on Senegal's culinary traditions. They are often served with fresh fruit and are traditionally followed by coffee or tea.

### Music

Further information: [Music of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Senegal)



[Kora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kora_\(instrument\)) player from Senegal

Senegal is known across Africa for its musical roots, due to the popularity of [mbalax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbalax), which originated from the Serer [percussive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_instrument) tradition, especially the [Njuup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Njuup), it has been popularized by [Youssou N'Dour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youssou_N'Dour), [Omar Pene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Pene), and others. [Sabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabar) drumming is especially popular. The sabar is mostly used in special celebrations such as weddings. Another instrument, the [tama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_drum), is used in more ethnic groups. Other popular international renowned Senegalese musicians are [Ismael Lô](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismael_L%C3%B4), [Cheikh Lô](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheikh_L%C3%B4), [Orchestra Baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestra_Baobab), [Baaba Maal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baaba_Maal), [Akon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akon) (US-born), [Thione Seck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thione_Seck), [Viviane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviane), Fallou Dieng, [Titi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titi_\(singer\)), [Seckou Keita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seckou_Keita), [Wasis Diop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasis_Diop), and [Pape Diouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pape_Diouf).

### Hospitality

Hospitality, in theory, is highly valued in Senegalese culture and is widely considered part of national identity. The Wolof[^cite_note-Serer_Jom-154] word for hospitality is "teranga" and it is so identified with the pride of Senegal that the national football team is known as [*Les Lions de la Téranga*](1017436.md).[^cite_note-ross-38]

### Sport



Senegalese Wrestling

Senegalese play many sports. [Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_wrestling) and [football](10568.md) are the most popular sports in the country. Senegal will host the [2026 Summer Youth Olympics](64557687.md) in [Dakar](44251.md), making Senegal the first African country to host an Olympic event.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

[Senegalese wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_wrestling) is the country's most popular sport[^cite_note-157] and has become a national obsession.[^cite_note-158] It traditionally serves many young men to escape poverty. It is the only sport recognized as developed independently of Western culture.



Senegalese football fans at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia

Football is a popular sport in Senegal. In [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), the national team beat [Egypt](1022953.md) to win the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) for the first time. They were runners-up in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [2025](57061770.md). They became one of only four African teams to ever reach the quarter-finals of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), after [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) in 1990 and before [Ghana](1029620.md) in 2010, defeating holders [France](250197.md) in their first game in 2002. Senegal qualified for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia, for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar, and for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) in Canada/Mexico/USA.

Senegal has traditionally been one of Africa's dominant basketball powers. The [men's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_national_basketball_team) performed better than any other African nation at the [2014 FIBA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_World_Cup), reaching the playoffs for the first time. The [women's team](13537015.md) won 19 medals at 20 [African Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Africa_Championship_for_Women), more than twice as many medals as any competitor. When the country hosted the [2019 FIBA Women's AfroBasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIBA_Women's_AfroBasket), 15,000 fans flocked to the [Dakar Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Arena) which is registered as a record attendance for basketball in Africa.[^cite_note-159] Senegal was one of the continent's pioneers in basketball as it established one of Africa's first competitive leagues.[^cite_note-160]

In 2016, the [NBA](22093.md) announced the launch of an Elite Academy in Africa, specifically in Senegal.[^cite_note-161]

The country hosted the [Paris–Dakar rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Rally) from [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Paris%E2%80%93Dakar_Rally) until [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Dakar_Rally). The Dakar Rally was an off-road endurance motorsport race that ran from Paris, France, to Dakar, Senegal. The competitors used off-road vehicles to cross the difficult geography. The last race was held in 2007, before the 2008 rally was canceled a day before the event due to security concerns in [Mauritania](18778516.md).[^cite_note-162] The 2021 [Ocean X-Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Ocean_X-Prix) of the electric off-road championship [Extreme E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_E) was also hosted in Senegal.[^cite_note-163]

The [2026 Summer Youth Olympics](64557687.md) are planned to be held in Dakar, Senegal.[^cite_note-164]
