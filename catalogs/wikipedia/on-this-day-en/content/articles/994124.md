An **oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *oblast*, pronounced [\[ˈɔblɐsʲtʲ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b4/Uk-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D1%8C.ogg/Uk-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D1%8C.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b4/Uk-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D1%8C.ogg/Uk-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D1%8C.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; pl. області, *oblasti*), sometimes translated as **region** or **province**, is the main type of first-level [administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) of [Ukraine](31750.md). The country's territory is divided into 24 oblasts, as well as one [autonomous republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_republic_of_Ukraine) and two [cities with special status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_with_special_status).

As Ukraine is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state), oblasts do not have much legal scope of competence other than that which is established in the [Ukrainian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ukraine) and devolved by law. Articles 140–146 of [Chapter XI](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution%20of%20Ukraine#Chapter_IX:_Territorial_Structure_of_Ukraine) of the constitution deal directly with local authorities and their competence.

Oblasts are divided into [raions](6719452.md), between three and ten each, following the [July 2020 reform](6719452.md#July_2020_reform).[^cite_note-2]

In Ukraine, the term *[oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)* denotes a primary [administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division). Under the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and into the 1920s, Ukraine was divided between several [governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guberniya). The term *oblast* was introduced in 1932 by [Soviet authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_regime) when the Ukrainian SSR was divided into seven oblasts, replacing the previous subdivision system based on [okruhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okruhas_of_the_Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) and encompassing 406 raions (districts).[^cite_note-stat-3] The first oblasts were [Vinnytsia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Oblast), [Kyiv Oblast](494884.md), [Odesa Oblast](61438266.md), [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), and [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md). Soon after that, in the summer of 1932, [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) was formed out of eastern parts of Kharkiv and Dnipropetrovsk oblasts; in the fall of 1932 [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast) was formed on the border of Kyiv and Kharkiv oblasts.

Between 1935 and 1938, there were several newly created and self-governed special [border okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okrugs_of_the_Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) (okruhas) located along the western border of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in Ukraine and Belarus. Upon liquidation of the okruhas in 1937–1938, Kyiv, Vinnytsia, Odesa, and Kharkiv oblasts were each split into four additional oblasts ([Zhytomyr Oblast](1567596.md), [Kamianets-Podilsky Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytskyi_Oblast) (later Khmelnytskyi), [Mykolaiv Oblast](494928.md), [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md)). Just before [World War II](32927.md), the [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) was split into Stalino Oblast and [Voroshylovhrad Oblast](494904.md) and the [Kirovohrad Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirovohrad_Oblast) was created out of portions of Kyiv, Mykolaiv and Odesa oblasts.

During World War II, Ukraine added eight more oblasts of the [West Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukraine) and [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia). Upon the occupation of Ukraine by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) the territory was split between [General Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government), [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) and [Reichskommissariat Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) and carried out a completely different administrative division, see [Reichskommissariat Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine). With the re-establishing of Soviet power in the state after the war, the administrative division by oblast resumed, adding one more oblast—[Zakarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast). In 1954, the [Crimean Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Oblast) was [transferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_transfer_of_Crimea) from the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) to the Ukrainian SSR; parts of the surrounding oblasts were incorporated into the [Cherkasy Oblast](494894.md), while [Izmail Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izmail_Oblast) was absorbed by [Odesa Oblast](61438266.md). In 1959, [Drohobych Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drohobych_Oblast) was merged with [Lviv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Oblast).

Most of Ukraine's oblasts are named after their respective [administrative centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center), which are also the largest and most developed cities in the region. Oblast populations range from 904,000 in [Chernivtsi Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi_Oblast) to 4.4 million in the eastern [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md).

## Constitutional provisions and authority

The Ukrainian constitution establishes Ukraine as a unitary state. The specific text of the constitution that refers to the territorial structure is as follows.

> The territorial structure of Ukraine is based on the principles of unity and indivisibility of the state territory, the combination of centralisation and decentralisation in the exercise of state power, and the balanced socio-economic development of regions that takes into account their historical, economic, ecological, geographical and demographic characteristics, and ethnic and cultural traditions.

— [Constitution of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ukraine), [Chapter IX: Territorial Structure of Ukraine, Article 132](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution%20of%20Ukraine#Chapter_IX:_Territorial_Structure_of_Ukraine)



[Election results of the 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Ukrainian_local_elections) regional parliamentarian elections

Each of Ukraine's oblasts has its own legislative and executive authority, most of which is subordinate to the central government authorities in Kyiv. Each region is administered under laws passed by the Ukrainian government and the Constitution of Ukraine. Each region levies its own taxes and, in return, receives a portion of its budget from Kyiv, which gives them a portion of the taxes it levies.[^cite_note-4]

Executive power in each of the oblasts (as well as in other subdivisions of Ukraine) is exercised by local elected administrations.[^cite_note-law-5] The heads of local administrations are in turn appointed and dismissed by the [President of Ukraine](20688479.md) upon nomination by the [Cabinet of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ministers_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-law-5][^cite_note-6] Since Ukraine is a unitary state, there is little true political power and weight that these local administrations actually hold. Carrying out their authority, the heads of local administrations are accountable to the President and are subordinate to higher bodies of executive leadership.[^cite_note-law-5] According to the Constitution the head of the heads of the local Oblast administrations should resign after a [new President is elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_presidential_elections).[^cite_note-7]

Legislative power in the oblast governments is exercised by their respective oblast councils, which in turn supervise the activities of local administrations.[^cite_note-law-5] They also have considerable budgets managed by an oblast council ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): обласна рада) made up of people's deputies (representatives) voted into office in regional elections every four years, the last of which [took place in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Ukrainian_local_elections).

## Nomenclature

The name of each oblast is a [relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_clause) [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective), formed by adding a feminine [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) to the name of respective center city. For example, [Poltava](38581.md) is the administrative center of [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md). Most of them are also sometimes referred to in a feminine noun form, following the convention of traditional regional place names, ending with the suffix "-shchyna", such as Poltava Oblast, which is also called *Poltavshchyna* ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Полтавщина).

Exceptions to this rule include two oblasts, Volyn and Zakarpattia, which retain the names of their respective historical regions, *Volyn* ([Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia)) and *Zakarpattia* ([Transcarpathia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Ruthenia)), whose respective capitals are [Lutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutsk) and [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod).[^cite_note-CIA_W_F-8]

The capital cities of the Dnipropetrovsk Oblast and Kirovohrad Oblast were renamed to [Dnipro](989795.md) and [Kropyvnytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kropyvnytskyi) in 2016 as part of a [process of replacing Soviet toponyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine). As the names of the oblasts are mentioned in the Ukrainian constitution, changing them requires a complicated and lengthy process, thus as of 2024, the two oblasts still formally retain their Soviet-era names.[^cite_note-CIA_W_F-8]

Map of the administrative divisions of the Ukrainian SSR from 1946–1954 shows the [Izmail Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izmail_Oblast) and [Drohobych Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drohobych_Oblast)

### Renamed

- Stalino Oblast was the name of Donetsk Oblast 1938–41 and 1943–61 (created out of the united Donetsk Oblast 1932–38, German occupation 1941–43)

- Akkerman Oblast was the name of Izmail Oblast 1940

- Stanislav Oblast was the name of Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast 1939–41 and 1944–62 (German occupation 1941–44)

- Kamianetsk-Podilska Oblast was the name of Khmelnytskyi Oblast 1937–41 and 1944–54 (German occupation 1941–44, later transfer of administrative center to [Khmelnytskyi](686060.md))

- Voroshylovhrad Oblast was the name of Luhansk Oblast 1938–42, 1943–58 and 1970–90 (German occupation 1942–43)

- Tarnopil Oblast was the name of Ternopil Oblast 1939–41 (renamed soon after World War II)

The Dnipropetrovsk Oblast and Kirovohrad Oblast are pending renaming following the renaming of their capital cities to Dnipro and Kropyvnytskyi.
