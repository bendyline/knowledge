**Leningrad Oblast** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Ленинградская область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Leningradskaya oblastʹ*, IPA: [\[lʲɪnʲɪnˈgratskəjə ˈobləsʲtʲ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian); [Veps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veps_language): *Leningradan agj*; [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language): *Leningradin alue*) is a [federal subject](701438.md) of [Russia](25391.md) (an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)). The oblast has an area of 84,500 square kilometres (32,600 sq mi) and a population of 2,000,997 ([2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2010Census-8] up from 1,716,868 recorded in the [2010 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Russian_census).[^cite_note-2002Census-9] Leningrad Oblast is highly industrialized. Its administrative center and largest city is [Gatchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatchina).[^cite_note-gatchina-10]

The oblast was established on 1 August 1927, although it was not until 1946 that the oblast's borders had been mostly settled in their present position. The oblast was named after the city of [Leningrad](24320051.md). In 1991, the city restored its original name, [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), but the oblast retains the name of Leningrad. It overlaps the historical region of [Ingria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingria), and is bordered by [Finland](10577.md) ([Kymenlaakso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kymenlaakso) and [South Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Karelia)) in the northwest and [Estonia](28222445.md) ([Ida-Viru County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida-Viru_County)) in the west, as well as five federal subjects of Russia: the [Republic of Karelia](199243.md) in the northeast, [Vologda Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda_Oblast) in the east, [Novgorod Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Oblast) in the south, [Pskov Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast) in the southwest, and the federal city of [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) in the west.

The first governor of Leningrad Oblast was [Vadim Gustov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Gustov) (in 1996–1998). The current governor, since 2012, is [Aleksandr Drozdenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Drozdenko).

## Geography



Monrepos Park in [Vyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg)



The Vuoksi in [Priozersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priozersk)

Leningrad Oblast is located around the [Gulf of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Finland) and south of two great freshwater lakes, [Lake Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ladoga) and [Lake Onega](374540.md). The oblast includes the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus) and some islands, including [Gogland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gogland) in the Gulf of Finland and [Konevets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konevets) in Lake Ladoga.

Much of the area of the oblast belongs to the [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of the [Neva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neva_River), which is the only outflow of Lake Ladoga. The Neva, which flows to the Gulf of Finland (the city of Saint Petersburg is located in its [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)) is relatively short, but its drainage basin is very large, including Lake Onega and [Lake Ilmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ilmen). The [Svir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svir_River) and the [Volkhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkhov_River) flow from Lake Onega and Lake Ilmen, respectively, to Lake Ladoga. Other major tributaries of Lake Ladoga include the [Vuoksi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuoksi_River) and the [Syas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syas_River). Rivers in the western part of the oblast flow to the Gulf of Finland; the two biggest rivers there are the [Luga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luga_River) and the [Narva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narva_River), which forms the border of Russia and Estonia. Small areas in the east of the oblast lie within the river basin of the [Chagodoshcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagodoshcha_River), a tributary of the [Mologa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mologa_River), and of the [Suda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suda_River), both within the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) basin. A [ridgeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridgeline) in [Tikhvinsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhvinsky_District) in the eastern oblast forms part of the divide between the Baltic Sea and Caspian Sea basins.

The terrain of Leningrad Oblast is relatively flat and mostly covered with forest and swamps. An exception is the rocky [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus), which contains a lake district, as well as the [Vepsian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vepsian_Upland) in the east. The biggest lakes on the isthmus are [Lake Vuoksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuoksi_River), [Lake Sukhodolskoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sukhodolskoye), and [Lake Otradnoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Otradnoye).

Leningrad Oblast contains two federally protected natural areas, the [Nizhnesvirsky Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhnesvirsky_Nature_Reserve) and [Mshinskoye Boloto Zakaznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mshinskoye_Boloto_Zakaznik), both created to protect the forest and swamp landscapes of northwestern Russia.

### Flora

The most taxonomically diverse vascular plant families are [Asteraceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteraceae), [Cyperaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperaceae), [Poaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae) and [Rosaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosaceae). By far the most diverse genus is *[Carex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carex)* (68 species). The diversity in genera *[Hieracium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieracium)* (with *Pilosella*), *[Ranunculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranunculus)* (with *Batrachium*), *[Alchemilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemilla)*, *[Galium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galium)*, *[Potamogeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potamogeton)*, *[Salix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salix)*, *[Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_\(plant\))*, *[Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola_\(plant\))*, *[Juncus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juncus)*, *[Artemisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemisia_\(plant\))*, *[Potentilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentilla)*, *[Rumex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumex)*, *[Festuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca)*, *[Epilobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epilobium)*, *[Poa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poa)*, *[Trifolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trifolium)*, *[Campanula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanula)*, *[Vicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicia)*, *[Lathyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathyrus)*, *[Geranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geranium)* is also considerable. The territory has no endemic plant taxa. Vascular plant species of Leningrad Oblast listed in the red data book of Russia are *[Botrychium simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botrychium_simplex)*, *[Cephalanthera rubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalanthera_rubra)*, *[Cypripedium calceolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypripedium_calceolus)*, *[Epipogium aphyllum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipogium_aphyllum)*, *[Lobelia dortmanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobelia_dortmanna)*, *[Myrica gale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrica_gale)*, *[Ophrys insectifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophrys_insectifera)*, *[Orchis militaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchis_militaris)*, *[Pulsatilla pratensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsatilla_pratensis)*, *[Pulsatilla vernalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsatilla_vernalis)*.

## History

### Early history



The fortress in [Staraya Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga)



Ivangorod fortress

The territory of present-day Leningrad Oblast was populated shortly after the end of the [Weichselian glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weichselian_glaciation) and now hosts numerous archaeological remnants.[^cite_note-Lapshin1-11][^cite_note-Lapshin2-12][^cite_note-Lebedev-13] The [Volga trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_trade_route) and [trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks) crossed the territory. [Staraya Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga), the first capital of legendary [Rurik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurik), founded in the 8th-9th century, is situated in the east of the oblast, on the [Volkhov River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkhov_River).

In the 12th-15th centuries, the territory was divided between the [Kingdom of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sweden) and [Novgorod Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Republic) (see [Swedish-Novgorodian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish-Novgorodian_Wars)) and populated mostly by various [Baltic Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Finns) people such as [Karelians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelians) (northwest), [Izhorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izhorians) and [Votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votians) (west), [Vepsians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vepsians) (east), as well as [Ilmen Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilmen_Slavs) of Novgorod (south). During the [Russo-Swedish Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Swedish_Wars) of the 15th-17th centuries, the border moved back and forth over the land.

The central part of the territory is known as the historical region of [Ingria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingria) (or the land of Izhora) and in the 17th century, after most of the present-day territory of Leningrad Oblast was captured by Sweden with the [Treaty of Stolbovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Stolbovo) of 1617, became subject to substantial Finnish Lutheran population influx from [Finnish Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Karelia) (which included [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus), the northwestern part of present-day Leningrad Oblast) and [Savonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savonia_\(historical_province\)). Having faced religious pressure from Lutheran pastors and Swedish authorities, the local Orthodox population of Russian and Finnic ancestry massively fled from Ingria to neighbour Russian provinces, so [Ingrian Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrian_Finns) soon became the dominant ethnic group.[^cite_note-14]

During the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) (1700–1721) the territory of what is now Leningrad Oblast was conquered from Sweden by Russia under [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great), who founded [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) amidst the land in 1703, which soon became the capital of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). In 1708, most of the territory was organized into [Ingermanland Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Governorate) under Governor General [Alexander Menshikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Danilovich_Menshikov). It was renamed Saint Petersburg Governorate in 1710 (the borders of that governorate, however, differed very significantly from those of the present-day oblast and included much of the areas of current Novgorod, Pskov, and Vologda Oblasts). In 1721, the territorial concessions of Sweden were confirmed with the [Treaty of Nystad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nystad).

The life of the countryside was greatly influenced by the vicinity of the imperial capital, which became a growing market for its agricultural production as well as the main consumer of its mineral and forest resources. In 1719–1810, [Ladoga Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladoga_Canal) was dug between the [Svir River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svir_River) and the [Neva River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neva_River) as part of the Volga-Baltic waterway to bypass stormy waters of [Lake Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ladoga). Since the advent of rail transport in the late 19th century, the areas in the vicinity of Saint Petersburg had been popular summer resort destinations ([dachas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacha)) for its residents. However, while Saint Petersburg itself was populated mostly by Russians from the very beginning, it was not until the 20th century that its surrounding population was Russified.

In 1914, with the beginning of [World War I](4764461.md), Saint Petersburg was renamed Petrograd and the governorate was accordingly renamed Petrograd Governorate. After the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_\(1917\)), in 1918, the capital was transferred from Petrograd to [Moscow](19004.md), farther from the borders of the country. In 1919, during the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), the Northwestern [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Army) advancing from Estonia and led by [Nikolai Yudenich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Yudenich) tried to capture Petrograd and even managed to reach its southern outskirts, but the attack against the Red Army under [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky) ultimately failed, and Yudenich retreated. The border with Estonia was established in the [Russian-Estonian Treaty of Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tartu_\(Russian%E2%80%93Estonian\)) of 1920. [Finland-backed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heimosodat) Ingrian Finns of [North Ingria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ingria) attempted to secede in 1918–1920, but were incorporated back with the [Russian-Finnish Treaty of Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tartu_\(Russian%E2%80%93Finnish\)), which settled the border between Finland and Soviet Russia. In 1924, Petrograd was renamed Leningrad, and Petrograd Governorate was again renamed accordingly (Leningrad Governorate).

### Leningrad Oblast

Leningrad Oblast was established on 1 August 1927, by the resolutions of the [All-Russian Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Russian_Central_Executive_Committee) "On the Establishment of Leningrad Oblast" and "On the Borders and Composition of the Okrugs of Leningrad Oblast"[^cite_note-ATSBookMurm-1] by merging [Cherepovets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherepovets_Governorate), Leningrad, [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Governorate), [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Governorate), and [Pskov Governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Governorate). The territory of the oblast corresponded to the modern territories of the present-day Leningrad Oblast (with the exception of the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus) and the territories along the border with Estonia), [Novgorod Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Oblast), [Pskov Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast), parts of [Vologda Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda_Oblast), most of [Murmansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast), and the federal city of Saint Petersburg. The total area of the oblast was 360,400 square kilometres (139,200 sq mi);[^cite_note-ATSBookLengr-15] more than four times larger than the modern entity. Administratively, the oblast was divided into nine [okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okrug) ([Borovichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borovichi_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Cherepovets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherepovets_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Okrug), [Lodeynoye Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodeynoye_Pole_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Luga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luga_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Okrug), [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Pskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Velikiye Luki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velikiye_Luki_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1)), each of which was in turn subdivided into [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion).[^cite_note-ATSBookLengr-15]

In 1929, [Velikiye Luki Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velikiye_Luki_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1) was transferred to newly formed [Western Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Oblast). Leningrad was administratively separated from Leningrad Oblast in December 1931. In 1935 five southernmost districts were made part of [Kalinin Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinin_Oblast). In 1936 some parts of the territory of [Leningrad Suburban District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Suburban_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) of Leningrad was returned to Leningrad Oblast and divided into [Vsevolozhsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsevolozhsky_District), [Krasnoselsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoselsky_District,_Saint_Petersburg), [Pargolovsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pargolovsky_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Slutsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slutsky_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) (renamed Pavlovsky District in 1944). [Vologda Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda_Oblast), which has included the easternmost districts of Leningrad Oblast (former Cherepovets Governorate), was created in 1937. [Murmansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast) was split from Leningrad Oblast in 1938.

In the autumn of 1934, the [Forbidden Border Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Security_Zone_of_Russia) along the western border of the Soviet Union was established, where nobody could appear without special permission issued by the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD). It was officially only 7.5 km deep initially, but along the Estonian border it extended to as much as 90 km. The zone was to be cleansed of Finnic and other peoples who were considered "politically unreliable" by the regime.[^cite_note-matley-17][^cite_note-martin-18] Starting from the 1929, the Soviet authorities carried out [mass deportations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportations_of_the_Ingrian_Finns) of the [Ingrian Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrian_Finn) population of the oblast, which constituted the majority in many rural localities as late as in the beginning of the 20th century, to the east, replacing them with people from other parts of the Soviet Union.

On 30 November 1939, the Soviet Union waged the [Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War) against neighbouring Finland and with the [Moscow Peace Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Peace_Treaty) in 1940 gained some territories, including the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus). Their Karelian population was [hastily evacuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_of_Finnish_Karelia) to inner Finland and later replaced with people from other parts of the Soviet Union. A small part of the territory (the municipalities of [Kanneljärvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pobeda,_Leningrad_Oblast), [Koivisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsk,_Leningrad_Oblast) and [Rautu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnovo,_Priozersky_District,_Leningrad_Oblast)) was incorporated into Leningrad Oblast, the rest being included within the [Karelo-Finnish Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelo-Finnish_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).



Map showing the [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) occupation of Leningrad Oblast by the end of 1941

In 1941, Germany invaded the Soviet Union in [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), and shortly thereafter the territory became the site of the [Battle of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leningrad). The Wehrmacht captured the southwestern part of the oblast and reached [Tikhvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhvin) in the east, while Finnish troops [quickly recaptured the ceded territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_reconquest_of_the_Karelian_Isthmus_\(1941\)) in the [Continuation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuation_War), encircling Leningrad from the land. In 1944 Soviet offensives managed to expel the Wehrmacht and [put military pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_strategic_offensive) on Finland, which ceded the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus) again in the [Moscow Armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Armistice) of September 19, 1944. This time the newly acquired territories on the isthmus were incorporated into the Leningrad Oblast ([Vyborgsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborgsky_District,_Leningrad_Oblast) and [Priozersky Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priozersky_District)). In 1947 the territorial gains were confirmed with the [Paris Peace Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Treaty). [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Oblast) and [Pskov Oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast) were formed out of the southern parts of Leningrad Oblast in 1944. In January 1945 a small part of the [Estonian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_SSR) to the east of the [River Narva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Narva) with the town of [Jaanilinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaanilinn) (now Ivangorod) was transferred to the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR) and incorporated into Leningrad Oblast. Since then, the territory of Leningrad Oblast has not changed significantly, although some suburbs of Leningrad (now Saint Petersburg) have been excluded from the oblast and incorporated into the city.[^cite_note-19] In October 1946 Leningrad gained from the oblast some former Finnish territories along the northern coast of the [Gulf of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Finland) divided into [Sestroretsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestroretsky_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Kurortny District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurortny_District), including the town of [Terijoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terijoki).

In 1953, [Pavlovsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlovsky_District,_Leningrad_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the oblast was abolished, and parts of its territory including Pavlovsk were made subordinate to Leningrad. In 1954 the settlements [Levashovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levashovo,_Saint_Petersburg), [Pargolovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pargolovo) and [Pesochny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesochny,_Russia) were also transferred to Leningrad. In 1956 [Boksitogorsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boksitogorsky_District) of Leningrad Oblast gained a small territory of [Novgorod Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Oblast). [Uritsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uritsk) was transferred from the oblast to the city of Leningrad in 1963, [Krasnoye Selo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoye_Selo) and several settlements nearby—in 1973, [Lomonosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov,_Russia)—in 1978.

After a referendum in 1991, the city of Leningrad was renamed back to Saint Petersburg, but Leningrad Oblast retained its name.[^cite_note-20] On 13 June 1996, Leningrad Oblast, alongside [Tver Oblast](463952.md) and [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), signed a power-sharing agreement with the federal government, granting it autonomy.[^cite_note-newsline-21] This agreement was later abolished on 18 April 2002.[^cite_note-demokratiztsiya-22]

## Politics



The building of the Government and the Legislative Assembly of the region on Proletarian Dictatorship Square in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md)

State power in the region is exercised on the basis of the Charter, which was adopted on 27 October 1994.[^cite_note-32]

The highest official of the region is the [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Leningrad_Oblast), who is elected for a term of five years. Since 28 May 2012, he has been [Aleksandr Drozdenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Drozdenko).

The executive power in the region is exercised by the administration, which includes:

- The government of the Leningrad Oblast is the highest executive body of state power, which includes the Governor (Chairman of the Government), vice-governors and chairmen of committees

- sectoral, territorial and other executive authorities

Legislative power in the region is exercised by the [Legislative Assembly of Leningrad Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Leningrad_Oblast), which consists of 50 deputies elected by the region's inhabitants according to a mixed proportional-majority system for a term of 5 years. In 2016, the Legislative Assembly of the sixth convocation was formed, in which there are 4 factions: [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) (40 deputies), [A Just Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Just_Russia) (3 deputies), [Communist Party of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation) (3 deputies), [Liberal Democratic Party of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_of_Russia) (4 deputies). The Chairman of the Legislative Assembly is Sergei Bebenin.

## Administrative divisions

Main article: [Administrative divisions of Leningrad Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Leningrad_Oblast)

[Administratively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), Leningrad Oblast is divided into seventeen [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion) and one [town of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) ([Sosnovy Bor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnovy_Bor,_Leningrad_Oblast)). In terms of area, the largest administrative district is [Podporozhsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podporozhsky_District) (7,706 square kilometers (2,975 sq mi)), and the smallest is [Lomonosovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosovsky_District,_Leningrad_Oblast) (1,919 square kilometers (741 sq mi)).

Lomonosovsky District is the only district in Russia that has its [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) (the town of [Lomonosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov,_Russia)) located within a different [federal subject](701438.md). While the district is a part of Leningrad Oblast, Lomonosov is located within the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md).

## Economy

### Industry

The oblast, particularly the areas adjacent to Saint Petersburg, is heavily industrialized. The major enterprises include the [oil refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinef) in Kirishi, the Ford car assembly plant, Hyundai Russia assembly plant and the Rexam PLC Beverage Can Europe and Asia packaging plant in Vsevolozhsk, the paper mill in Syssstroy, and the paper mill and the plant producing oil platforms in Vyborg, and the [Tikhvin industrial site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhvin_industrial_site) in Tikhvin.

### Agriculture

The main agricultural specializations of the oblast are cattle breeding with meat and milk production and poultry production. The main agricultural lands are in the east and in the southwest of the oblast.

### Transportation



[DT1 multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DT1_multiple_unit) [Diesel train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_locomotive) in Veymarn

Saint-Petersburg is a major railway hub, and all railways running to it cross also Leningrad Oblast. They connect Saint-Petersburg with Moscow ([Saint Petersburg – Moscow Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_%E2%80%93_Moscow_Railway)), [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) via [Vyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg), [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk) via [Petrozavodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk), [Sortavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortavala) via [Priozersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priozersk), [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn) via [Kingisepp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingisepp), [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) via [Pskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov), [Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk) via [Dno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dno), and [Veliky Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliky_Novgorod). There is a network of railways at the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus), in particular, connecting Vyborg and Priozersk, as well as south of Saint-Petersburg. There also railway lines connecting [Veymarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veymarn?action=edit\&redlink=1) with [Slantsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slantsy,_Leningrad_Oblast), Veymarn with [Petergof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petergof) via [Sosnovy Bor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnovy_Bor), [Mga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mga) with [Sonkovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonkovo) via [Kirishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishi), [Volkhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkhov) with [Vologda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda) via [Tikhvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhvin) and [Cherepovets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherepovets), Volkhov with [Chudovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chudovo,_Chudovsky_District,_Novgorod_Oblast), and [Lodeynoye Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodeynoye_Pole) with Sortavala via [Olonets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonets). Most of them support intensive passenger and cargo traffic.

Paved roads well cover the western and the central parts of Leningrad Oblast. The [M10 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M10_highway_\(Russia\)) connects Saint Petersburg with Moscow via Veliky Novgorod in the south and with the Finnish border, continuing across the border to Helsinki, in the north. It is split between European routes [E18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E18) (Saint Petersburg to Finland) and [E105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E105) (Saint Petersburg to Moscow), and much of it within the oblast is built as a dual carriageway. [R21 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R21_highway_\(Russia\)) (also part of E105) runs from Saint Petersburg to Murmansk via Petrozavodsk, and [A180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A180_highway_\(Russia\)) (part of [E20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E20)) connects Saint Petersburg with [Ivangorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivangorod) and continues across the Estonian border to Tallinn. [R23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R23_highway_\(Russia\)) connects Saint Petersburg with Pskov; it is a part of the [E95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E95). [A114](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A114_highway_\(Russia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) runs to Vologda via Cherepovets. A [paid motorway between Saint Petersburg and Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow%E2%80%93Saint_Petersburg_motorway) and the new A121 from Saint-Petersburg to [Sortavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortavala), around the Western coast of Ladoga[^cite_note-36] are under construction. Roads are well served by bus traffic.

The [Luga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luga_River), the [Svir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svir_River), the [Volkhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkhov_River), and the [Neva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neva_River) are all navigable and heavily user for cargo transport, however, there is no scheduled passenger navigation within the oblast, except for seasonal leisure river cruises from Saint-Petersburg. The [Onega Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onega_Canal), shared with Vologda Oblast, serves as a bypass of [Lake Onega](374540.md) from the south. Similarly, the [Ladoga Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladoga_Canal) bypasses [Lake Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ladoga) from the east, from the Svir to the Neva. It is a part of the [Volga–Baltic Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Baltic_Waterway). In contrast, the [Tikhvinskaya water system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhvinskaya_water_system), connecting the [Syas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syas_River) and the [Mologa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mologa_River), which provided access from the Ladoga to the river basin of the Volga, is disused. The trans-border [Saimaa Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saimaa_Canal) connects [Lake Saimaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saimaa) in Finland with the Gulf of Finland, has special status, and is occasionally used for passenger navigation.

[Ust-Luga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ust-Luga), [Vyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg), [Vysotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vysotsk), and [Primorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsk,_Leningrad_Oblast) are the major sea terminals on the Gulf of Finland.

There is a number of [airfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodrome) in Leningrad oblast that are now used only by the [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation). Scheduled and international flights are available exclusively from [Pulkovo airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulkovo_Airport) in Saint-Petersburg.[^cite_note-37]

### Energy



Lock at the [Upper Svir Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Svir_Hydroelectric_Station).

The [Leningrad Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Nuclear_Power_Plant) is located in the city of [Sosnovy Bor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnovy_Bor,_Leningrad_Oblast). There are four major hydroelectric plants in the oblast. The [Volkhov Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkhov_Hydroelectric_Station), on the Volkhov River, was constructed in 1921 and became the first major hydropower station in the Soviet Union. The others are the [Upper Svir Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Svir_Hydroelectric_Station) and the [Lower Svir Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Svir_Hydroelectric_Station), both on the Svir River, and the [Narva Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narva_Hydroelectric_Station) on the Narva River.

### Science and technology

There are many science and high-tech institutions around Saint Petersburg, some of which are located in the oblast. For example, Gatchina is the site of the [Petersburg Nuclear Physics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersburg_Nuclear_Physics_Institute?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-38] and Sosnovy Bor hosts the Research Institute of Optical and Electronic Devices.

## Culture and recreation

### Architecture



The historical center of Vyborg



The Gimreka Ensemble

Since prehistory, the Volkhov and the Neva Rivers were constituents of major trade routes, and archaeological sites dot their banks. [Staraya Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga) has many pre-1700 sites, including two of about a couple of dozens standing [pre-Mongol churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_buildings_of_pre-Mongol_Rus) in Russia. Both of them were built in the 1160s. The [Oreshek Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreshek_Fortress) in [Shlisselburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlisselburg) and the [Koporye Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koporye), both built in the 14th century, and the [Ivangorod Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivangorod_Fortress), originally built in 1492, are excellent examples of Russian fortification art. Podporozhsky District contains the best samples of wooden ecclesiastical architecture in Leningrad Oblast, some of which are collectively referred to as *Podporozhye Ring*: The Resurrection Church in the settlement of [Vazhiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vazhiny), the Saint Nicholas Church in the village of [Soginitsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soginitsy?action=edit\&redlink=1), the Sts Peter and Paul Chapel in the village of [Zaozerye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaozerye,_Podporozhsky_District,_Leningrad_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the Saint Athanasy chapel in the village of [Posad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posad,_Leningrad_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1). The two other notable wooden churches are located in the villages of [Gimreka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimreka) and [Shcheleyki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shcheleyki?action=edit\&redlink=1) close to the Onega Lakeshore. The center of [Vyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg) preserves many examples of medieval Swedish architecture, unique for Russia.

After Saint Petersburg was founded in 1703, many estates and residences were founded around the city. Some of them still stand and are listed as World Heritage sites, aggregated into the site of [Historic Centre of Saint Petersburg and Related Groups of Monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Saint_Petersburg_and_Related_Groups_of_Monuments). includes, among others, estates in [Gostilitsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gostilitsy), [Ropsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ropsha), and [Taytsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taytsy).

Localities in the Karelian Isthmus preserve some fine inter-war Finnish architecture. The best-known example is the [Viipuri Municipal Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg_Library) by [Alvar Aalto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvar_Aalto).
