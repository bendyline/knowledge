**Madison Square Garden**, colloquially known as **the Garden** or by its initials **MSG**, is a multi-purpose indoor [arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena) in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), New York City, U.S. The arena is located between [Seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) and [Eighth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) avenues, extending from [31st to 33rd streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_numbered_streets_in_Manhattan), atop [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)). The current site hosts the fourth venue to bear the name "Madison Square Garden"; the first two, opened in [1879](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1879\)) and [1890](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1890\)), were situated in [Madison Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_and_Madison_Square_Park), on East 26th Street and Madison Avenue, with the [third Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1925\)) (1925) farther uptown at Eighth Avenue and 50th Street.

The Garden hosts professional ice hockey, professional basketball, boxing, mixed martial arts, concerts, ice shows, circuses, professional wrestling, and other forms of sports and entertainment. It is close to other Midtown Manhattan landmarks, including the [Empire State Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_Building), [Koreatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreatown,_Manhattan), and [Macy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy's_Herald_Square) at [Herald Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Square). The arena is currently home to the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL), the [New York Knicks](72855.md) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA), and was home to the [New York Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty) of the [Women's National Basketball Association](57721.md) (WNBA) from 1997 to 2017.

Originally called **Madison Square Garden Center**, the Garden opened on February 11, 1968, on the site of the old [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(1910%E2%80%931963\)). It is the oldest major sporting facility in the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area). It is the oldest arena in the NBA and the second-oldest in the NHL, after Seattle's [Climate Pledge Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Pledge_Arena). As of 2016, MSG is also the second-busiest music arena in the world in terms of ticket sales.[^cite_note-7] Including its two major renovations in 1991 and 2013, the Garden's total construction cost was approximately US\$1.1 billion, and it has been ranked as one of the ten most expensive arena venues ever built.[^cite_note-8] The edifice is part of the [Pennsylvania Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Plaza) office and retail complex, named for the railway station. Several [other operating entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(disambiguation\)) related to the Garden share its name.

## History

### Previous Gardens

Main articles: [Madison Square Garden (1879)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1879\)), [Madison Square Garden (1890)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1890\)), and [Madison Square Garden (1925)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1925\))

[Madison Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_and_Madison_Square_Park) is formed by the intersection of [5th Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue) and [Broadway](4626.md) at [23rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Street_\(Manhattan\)) in Manhattan. It was named after [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison), the fourth [president of the United States](24113.md).[^cite_note-9]

Two venues called Madison Square Garden were located just northeast of the square, [the original Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1879\)) from 1879 to 1890, and [the second Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1890\)) from 1890 to 1925. The first, leased to [P. T. Barnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._T._Barnum),[^cite_note-ballp-10] was demolished in 1890 because of a leaky roof and dangerous balconies that had collapsed, resulting in deaths. The second was designed by noted architect [Stanford White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_White). The new building was built by a syndicate that included [J. P. Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._P._Morgan), [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie), P. T. Barnum,[^cite_note-fednyc-11] [Darius Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_Ogden_Mills), [James Stillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stillman) and [W. W. Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Waldorf_Astor). White gave them a [Beaux-Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaux-Arts_architecture) structure with a [Moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) feel, including a minaret-like tower modeled after [Giralda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giralda), the bell tower of the [Cathedral of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville_Cathedral),[^cite_note-fednyc-11] soaring 32 stories, the city's second-tallest building at the time and dominating [Madison Square Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_and_Madison_Square_Park). It was 200 feet (61 m) by 485 feet (148 m), and the main hall, which was the largest in the world, measured 200 feet (61 m) by 350 feet (110 m) with permanent seating for 8,000 people and floor space for thousands more. It had a 1,200-seat theater, a concert hall with a capacity of 1,500, the largest restaurant in the city, and a roof garden cabaret.[^cite_note-ballp-10] The building cost \$3 million.[^cite_note-ballp-10] Madison Square Garden II was unsuccessful like the first Garden,[^cite_note-12] and the [New York Life Insurance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Life_Insurance_Company), which held the mortgage on it, decided to tear it down in 1925 to make way for a new headquarters building, which would become the landmark [Cass Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cass_Gilbert)-designed [New York Life Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Life_Building).

A [third Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1925\)) opened in a new location, on [Eighth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) between [49th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_numbered_streets_in_Manhattan) and [50th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50th_Street_\(Manhattan\)) streets, from 1925 to 1968. Groundbreaking on the third Madison Square Garden took place on January 9, 1925.[^cite_note-ballp2-13] Designed by the noted theater architect [Thomas W. Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_W._Lamb), it was built at a cost of \$4.75 million in 249 days by boxing promoter [Tex Rickard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex_Rickard);[^cite_note-ballp2-13] the arena was dubbed "The House That Tex Built".[^cite_note-14] The arena was 200 feet (61 m) by 375 feet (114 m), with seating on three levels, and a maximum capacity of 18,496 spectators for boxing.[^cite_note-ballp-10] It notably hosted a 20,000 person rally in 1939 of the [German American Bund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American_Bund), a Nazi organization founded in the United States with covert guidance and financial backing from the Reich.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

Demolition commenced in 1968 after the opening of the current Garden,[^cite_note-18] and was completed in early 1969. The site is now the location of [One Worldwide Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Worldwide_Plaza).

### Current Garden

In February 1959, former automobile manufacturer [Graham-Paige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham-Paige) purchased a 40% interest in the Madison Square Garden for \$4 million[^cite_note-19] and later gained control.[^cite_note-NYTObit-20] In November 1960, Graham-Paige president [Irving Mitchell Felt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Mitchell_Felt) purchased from the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad) the rights to build at [Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)).[^cite_note-MITPlosky-21] To build the new facility, the above-ground portions of [the original Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(1910%E2%80%931963\)) were torn down.[^cite_note-22] Talk of a new Garden dated back as early as 1946. The sight lines in the upper deck of the old Garden’s left much to be desired, a problem exposed by growing attendance at Knicks games.



A 1968 [New York Knicks](72855.md) game at Madison Square Garden

The new structure was one of the first of its kind to be built above the platforms of an active railroad station. It was an engineering feat constructed by [Robert E. McKee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._McKee) of [El Paso, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas) with the contribution of Arq. Leonel Viera's design for the [Cilindro Municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilindro_Municipal).[^cite_note-23] Public outcry over the demolition of the Pennsylvania Station structure—an outstanding example of [Beaux-Arts architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaux-Arts_architecture)—led to the creation of the [New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Landmarks_Preservation_Commission). The venue opened on February 11, 1968. Comparing the new and the old Penn Station, Yale architectural historian [Vincent Scully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Scully) wrote, "One entered the city like a god; one scuttles in now like a rat."[^cite_note-Muschamp_1993-24]

In 1972, Felt proposed moving the [Knicks](72855.md) and [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) to a then incomplete venue in the [New Jersey Meadowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Meadowlands), the [Meadowlands Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Sports_Complex). The Garden was also the home arena for the [NY Raiders/NY Golden Blades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Golden_Blades) of the [World Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association). The Meadowlands would eventually host its own NBA and NHL teams, the [New Jersey Nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) and the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils), respectively. The [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and [Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) also relocated there. In 1977, the arena was sold to [Gulf and Western Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_and_Western_Industries). Felt's efforts fueled controversy between the Garden and New York City over real estate taxes. The disagreement again flared in 1980 when the Garden again challenged its tax bill. The arena has enjoyed tax-free status since the 1980s, under the condition that all Knicks and Rangers home games must be hosted at MSG, lest it lose this exemption. As such, when the Rangers have played neutral-site games—even those in New York City, such as the [2018 NHL Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NHL_Winter_Classic), they have always been designated as the visiting team.[^cite_note-25] The tax agreement includes an [act of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_God) clause, which allowed Knicks and Rangers home games to be played elsewhere during the [2020 NBA Bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble) and [2020 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), respectively, because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-26]

In 1984, the four streets immediately surrounding the Garden were designated as Joe Louis Plaza, in honor of boxer [Joe Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Louis), who had made eight successful title defenses in the [previous Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1925\)).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### 1991 renovation

In April 1986, Gulf and Western announced that they would build a new Madison Square Garden a few blocks away on the site of present-day [Hudson Yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Yards_\(development\)). The plan would cost an estimated \$150 million and included the demolition of the 1964 building to replace it with a new office tower development.[^cite_note-29] After years of planning, Gulf and Western decided against building a new arena in favor of a renovation after estimated costs doubled during the process.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]



Madison Square Garden following its 1991 renovation

Garden owners spent \$200 million in 1991 to renovate facilities and add 89 in place of hundreds of upper-tier seats. The project was designed by [Ellerbe Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellerbe_Becket). The renovation was criticized for perceived corporatization. Additionally, the renovation made bathrooms larger, expanded menus, added a new ventilation system, replaced all of the seats with new cushioned teal and violet seats, and refurbished both home teams' locker rooms.[^cite_note-32]

In 2000, current MSG owner James Dolan was quoted as saying that a new arena was being considered as the current building was starting to show its age.[^cite_note-33]

In 2004–2005, [Cablevision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cablevision) battled with the City of New York over the proposed [West Side arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Stadium), which was canceled. Cablevision then announced plans to raze the Garden, replace it with high-rise commercial buildings, and build a new Garden one block away at the site of the [James Farley Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Farley_Building). Meanwhile, a new project to renovate and modernize the Garden completed phase one in time for the [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_New_York_Rangers_season) and [Knicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_New_York_Knicks_season)' 2011–12 seasons,[^cite_note-34] though the vice president of the Garden says he remains committed to the installation of an extension of Penn Station at the Farley Post Office site. While the Knicks and Rangers were not displaced, the [New York Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty) played at the [Prudential Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudential_Center) in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), during the renovation.

Madison Square Garden is the last of the [NBA](22093.md) and [NHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL) arenas not to be named after a corporate sponsor.[^cite_note-35]

### 2011–2013 renovation

Madison Square Garden's \$1 billion second renovation took place mainly over three off-seasons. It was set to begin after the 2009–10 hockey/basketball seasons, but was delayed until after the 2010–11 seasons. Renovation was done in phases, with the majority of the work done in the summer months to minimize disruptions to the NHL and NBA seasons. While the Rangers and Knicks were not displaced,[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] the Liberty played their home games through the 2013 season at [Prudential Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudential_Center) in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), during the renovation.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

New features include a larger entrance with interactive kiosks, retail, climate-controlled space, and broadcast studio; larger concourses; new lighting and LED video systems with HDTV; new seating; two new pedestrian walkways suspended from the ceiling to allow fans to look directly down onto the games being played below; more dining options; and improved dressing rooms, locker rooms, green rooms, upgraded roof, and production offices. The lower bowl concourse, called the Madison Concourse, remains on the sixth floor. The upper bowl concourse was relocated to the eighth floor and it is known as the Garden Concourse. The seventh floor houses the new Madison Suites and the Madison Club. The upper bowl was built on top of these suites. The rebuilt concourses are wider than their predecessors, and include large windows that offer views of the city streets around the Garden.[^cite_note-40]



The playing surface before a [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) game and the retirement ceremony of [Henrik Lundqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist)

During the three-year, \$1 billion renovation of Madison Square Garden, code consulting and municipal approvals services were provided by Milrose Consultants[^cite_note-41], a building code consulting firm based in New York. Milrose Consultants supported the project with code analysis, place-of-assembly approvals, and assistance with certificate-of-occupancy services as part of the broader renovation effort.

Construction of the lower bowl (Phase 1) was completed in 2011.[^cite_note-42] An extended off-season for the Garden permitted some advance work to begin on the new upper bowl, which was completed in 2012. This advance work included the West Balcony on the tenth floor, taking the place of sky-boxes, and new end-ice 300 level seating. The construction of the upper bowl along with the Madison Suites and the Madison Club (Phase 2) were completed for the 2012–13 NHL and NBA seasons.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Phase 3, which involved the construction of the new lobby known as Chase Square, the Chase Bridges on the 10th floor, and the new scoreboard, was completed for the 2013–14 NHL and NBA seasons.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Penn Station renovation controversy

Madison Square Garden is seen as an obstacle in the renovation and future expansion of [Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Penn_Station),[^cite_note-nycurbed_20130529-47] which expanded in 2021 with the opening of [Moynihan Train Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moynihan_Train_Hall) at the [James Farley Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Farley_Building),[^cite_note-48] and some have proposed moving MSG to other sites in western Manhattan. On February 15, 2013, [Manhattan Community Board 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Community_Board_5) voted 36–0 against granting a renewal to MSG's operating permit in perpetuity and proposed a 10-year limit instead to build a new Penn Station where the arena is currently standing. Manhattan borough president [Scott Stringer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Stringer) said, "Moving the arena is an important first step to improving Penn Station." [The Madison Square Garden Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Sports) responded by saying that "\[i]t is incongruous to think that M.S.G. would be considering moving."[^cite_note-49]



Madison Square Garden in 2024, with the [Empire State Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_Building) in the background

In May 2013, four architecture firms – [SHoP Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHoP_Architects), [SOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skidmore,_Owings_&_Merrill), [H3 Hardy Collaboration Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Hardy), and [Diller Scofidio + Renfro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diller_Scofidio_+_Renfro) – submitted proposals[^cite_note-50] for a new Penn Station. SHoP Architects recommended moving Madison Square Garden to the Morgan Postal Facility a few blocks southwest, as well as removing [2 Penn Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Plaza) and redeveloping other towers, and an extension of the [High Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Line) to Penn Station.[^cite_note-nycurbed_20130529-47] Meanwhile, SOM proposed moving Madison Square Garden to the area just south of the [James Farley Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Farley_Building), and redeveloping the area above Penn Station as a [mixed-use development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-use_development) with commercial, residential, and recreational space.[^cite_note-nycurbed_20130529-47] H3 Hardy Collaboration Architecture wanted to move the arena to a new pier west of [Jacob K. Javits Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javits_Center), four blocks west of the current station and arena. Then, according to H3's plan, four [skyscrapers](29485.md) would be built, one at each of the four corners of the new Penn Station superblock, with a [roof garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof_garden) on top of the station; the Farley Post Office would become an education center.[^cite_note-nycurbed_20130529-47] Finally, Diller Scofidio + Renfro proposed a mixed-use development on the site, with spas, theaters, a cascading park, a pool, and restaurants; Madison Square Garden would be moved two blocks west, next to the post office. DS+F also proposed high-tech features in the station, such as train arrival and departure boards on the floor, and [apps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app) that would inform waiting passengers of ways to occupy their time until they board their trains.[^cite_note-nycurbed_20130529-47] Madison Square Garden rejected the notion that it would be relocated, and called the plans "pie-in-the-sky".[^cite_note-nycurbed_20130529-47]

In June 2013, the [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council) Committee on Land Use voted unanimously to give the Garden a ten-year permit, at the end of which period the owners would either have to relocate or go back through the permission process.[^cite_note-51] On July 24, the City Council voted to give the Garden a 10-year operating permit by a vote of 47–1. "This is the first step in finding a new home for Madison Square Garden and building a new Penn Station that is as great as New York and suitable for the 21st century," said City Council speaker [Christine Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Quinn). "This is an opportunity to reimagine and redevelop Penn Station as a world-class transportation destination."[^cite_note-52]

In October 2014, the Morgan facility was selected as the ideal area for Madison Square Garden to be moved, following the 2014 MAS Summit in New York City. More plans for the station were discussed.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Then, in January 2016, New York Governor [Andrew Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo) announced a redevelopment plan for Penn Station that would involve the removal of [The Theater at Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu_Theater), but would otherwise leave the arena intact.[^cite_note-cumorfp-55][^cite_note-56]



Interior of Madison Square Garden in June 2024 before [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake)'s "[The Forget Tomorrow World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forget_Tomorrow_World_Tour)"&#x20;

In June 2023, nearing the end of the Garden's ten-year permit granted by the city, the [Metropolitan Transportation Authority](384273.md), along with [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) and [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit), filed a report stating that MSG is no longer compatible with Penn Station, with the report saying, "MSG's existing configuration and property boundaries impose severe constraints on the station that impede the safe and efficient movement of passengers and restrict efforts to implement improvements, particularly at the street and platform levels."[^cite_note-57] On September 14, 2023, the New York City Council voted 48–0 to renew the operating permit for Madison Square Garden for five years, the shortest ever granted by the city to the Garden.[^cite_note-58]

In May 2026, Amtrak announced their plans for redeveloping Penn Station. Under the selected plan, Madison Square Garden will remain at its current location and will not be forced to move.[^cite_note-59]

### Regular events

#### Sports

Madison Square Garden hosts approximately 320 events a year. It is the home to the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) of the National Hockey League, and the [New York Knicks](72855.md) of the National Basketball Association. Before 2020, the New York Rangers, New York Knicks, and the Madison Square Garden arena itself were all owned by the Madison Square Garden Company. The MSG Company split into two entities in 2020, with the Garden arena and other non-sports assets spun off into [Madison Square Garden Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Entertainment) and the Rangers and Knicks remaining with the original company, renamed [Madison Square Garden Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Sports). Both entities remain under the voting control of [James Dolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_L._Dolan) and his family. The arena is also host to the [Big East men's basketball tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_East_men's_basketball_tournament) and was home to the finals of the [National Invitation Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Invitation_Tournament) from the beginning of its existence up until 2022.[^cite_note-60] It also hosts select home games for the [St. John's Red Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_Red_Storm_men's_basketball), representing [St. John's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_University_\(New_York\)) in men's [college basketball](768856.md), and almost any other kind of indoor activity that draws large audiences, such as the [Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Kennel_Club_Dog_Show) and the [2004 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Republican_National_Convention).

The Garden was home of the [NBA draft](835946.md) and [NIT Season Tip-Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIT_Season_Tip-Off),[^cite_note-61] as well as the former New York City home of the [Ringling Brothers and Barnum and Bailey Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringling_Bros._and_Barnum_&_Bailey_Circus) and [Disney on Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_on_Ice); all four events are now held at the [Barclays Center](1658814.md) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn). It served the [New York Cosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Cosmos_\(1970%E2%80%9385\)) for half of their home games during the [1983–84 NASL Indoor season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_NASL_Indoor_season).[^cite_note-62]

Many of [boxing](4243.md)'s biggest fights were held at Madison Square Garden, including the [Roberto Durán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Dur%C3%A1n)–[Ken Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Buchanan) affair, [the first Muhammad Ali – Joe Frazier bout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_of_the_Century) and the US debut of [Anthony Joshua](33343240.md) that ended in a huge upset when he was beaten by [Andy Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Ruiz). Before promoters such as [Don King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_King_\(boxing_promoter\)) and [Bob Arum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Arum) moved boxing to [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley), Nevada, Madison Square Garden was a popular location for boxing. The original 18+1⁄2 ft × 18+1⁄2 ft (5.6 m × 5.6 m) ring, which was brought from the second and third generation of the Garden, was officially retired on September 19, 2007, and donated to the [International Boxing Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boxing_Hall_of_Fame) after 82 years of service.[^cite_note-63] A 20 ft × 20 ft (6.1 m × 6.1 m) ring replaced it beginning on October 6 of that same year.[^cite_note-64] The [UFC](169660.md) has hosted many events at Madison Square Garden in recent years and has put on some of the highest grossing PPV events in history.

#### Professional wrestling

Madison Square Garden has hosted many notable [WWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE) (formerly WWF and WWWF) events, and it is affectionally known as WWE's "home office".[^cite_note-65] The Garden has hosted three [WrestleMania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania) events, including the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_I) edition of the annual marquee event for WWE, as well as the [10th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_X) and [20th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_XX) editions. Madison Square Garden is also one of two venues (the other being [Allstate Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allstate_Arena)) to host WrestleMania three times.



[Eddie Guerrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Guerrero) (right) and close friend [Chris Benoit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Benoit) (left) celebrating as reigning World Champions at [WrestleMania XX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_XX) at Madison Square Garden

It also hosted the [Royal Rumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Rumble) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Rumble_\(2000\)) and [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Rumble_\(2008\)); [SummerSlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam) in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam_\(1988\)), [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam_\(1991\)) and [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam_\(1998\)); as well as [Survivor Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_Series) in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_Series_\(1996\)), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_Series_\(2002\)) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_Series_\(2011\)). Multiple episodes of WWE's weekly shows, *[Raw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE_Raw)* and *[SmackDown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE_SmackDown)* have been broadcast from the Arena as well.

[New Japan Pro-Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling) (NJPW) and [Ring of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Honor) (ROH) hosted their [G1 Supercard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G1_Supercard) supershow at the venue on April 6, 2019. A year later it was announced that [New Japan Pro-Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling) would return to Madison Square Garden alone on August 22, 2020, for NJPW Wrestle Dynasty.[^cite_note-66] In May 2020, NJPW announced that the Wrestle Dynasty show would be postponed to 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

#### Concerts



[Billy Joel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Joel), who currently dominates Madison Square Garden's concert performance records, and who in 2013 was recognized as a Madison Square Garden "franchise"



[Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin) guitarist [Jimmy Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Page) performing at Madison Square Garden in 1973

Madison Square Garden hosts more high-profile concert events than any other venue in New York City. It has been the venue for [Michael Jackson](14995351.md)'s [Bad World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_World_Tour) in 1988, [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison)'s [The Concert for Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Concert_for_Bangladesh), [The Concert for New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Concert_for_New_York_City) following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon)'s final concert appearance during an [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John) concert on Thanksgiving Night in 1974 before [his murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_John_Lennon) in 1980, and [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley), who gave four sold-out performances in 1972, his first and last ever in New York City. [Parliament-Funkadelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament-Funkadelic) headlined numerous sold-out shows in 1977 and 1978. [Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_\(band\)), who were formed in the arena's city and three of whose members were city-born, did six shows during their second half of the 1970s main attraction peak or "heyday": four sold out winter shows at the arena in 1977 (February 18 and December 14–16), and another two shows only this time in summer for a decade-ender in 1979 (July 24–25). They played their final two shows at the venue on the December 1 and 2, 2023, the 50th anniversary year of their formation.

[Billy Joel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Joel), another city-born and fellow 1970's pop star, played his first Garden show on December 14, 1978, with that month's follow ups on the 15th, 16th and 18th. Joel would eventually dominate the record for most concerts performed at Madison Square Garden.[^cite_note-msgnonsportrecord-69][^cite_note-70]

[Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin)'s three-night stand in July 1973 was recorded and released as both a [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Song_Remains_the_Same_\(film\)) and [album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Song_Remains_the_Same_\(album\)) titled *The Song Remains The Same*. [The Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Police) played their final show of their reunion tour at the Garden in 2008.

In the summer of 2017, [Phish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phish) held a 13-night series of concerts called "[The Bakers' Dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baker's_Dozen_\(concert_series\))", during which the band played 237 unique songs, repeating none during the entire run. The Garden commemorated "The Bakers' Dozen" by adding a Phish-themed banner to the rafters.[^cite_note-71] With their first MSG show taking place on December 30, 1994, Phish has regularly played annual multi-night runs, typically around New Year's Eve.[^cite_note-72] As of July 2026, Phish has performed 96 times at MSG.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]



[Eric Clapton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Clapton) at the Garden in 2015. Clapton has played 45 concerts at the venue since 1968.

[^cite_note-75]

[Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John) once held the all-time record for the greatest number of appearances at the Garden with 64 shows. In a 2009 press release, John was quoted as saying "Madison Square Garden is my favorite venue in the whole world. I chose to have my 60th birthday concert there, because of all the incredible memories I've had playing the venue."[^cite_note-2009Interview-76] A DVD recording was released as *[Elton 60—Live at Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_60_%E2%80%93_Live_at_Madison_Square_Garden)*.[^cite_note-77]

The current record-holder for most appearances is [Billy Joel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Joel), who as of July 2024 had played the Garden 150 times. That figure includes a "residency" of monthly concerts that he started in 2014 and played nearly continuously for a decade.[^cite_note-78] Joel has stated that the site "has the best acoustics, the best audiences, the best reputation, and the best history of great artists who have played there. It is the iconic, holy temple of rock and roll for most touring acts."[^cite_note-2009Interview-76] Joel would set his first record for most consecutive performances by a music artist at Madison Square Garden in 2006 after performing 12 consecutive concerts at the arena from January to April of that year.[^cite_note-msgnonsportrecord-69][^cite_note-79] In December 2013, Joel was named as Madison Square Garden's first ever music "franchise"; in doing so, Joel joined the [New York Knicks](72855.md), [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) and [New York Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty) as an original Madison Square Garden franchise.[^cite_note-msgnonsportrecord-69][^cite_note-80]



[Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga) performing at the Garden in 2011

The [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead) performed in the venue 53 times from 1979 to 1994, with the first show being held on January 7, 1979, and the last being on October 19, 1994;[^cite_note-81] their longest run was in September 1991.[^cite_note-Madison_Square_Garden_Shows-82]

[The Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Who) have headlined at the venue 32 times, including a four-night stand in 1974, a five-night stand in 1979, a six-night stand in 1996, and four-night stands in 2000 and 2002. They also performed at [The Concert for New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Concert_for_New_York_City) in 2001.[^cite_note-83]

On March 10, 2020, a 50th-anniversary celebration of [The Allman Brothers Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Allman_Brothers_Band) titled 'The Brothers' took place, featuring the five surviving members of the final Allman Brothers lineup and [Chuck Leavell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Leavell). [Dickey Betts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickey_Betts) was invited to participate but his health precluded him from traveling.[^cite_note-rsb50b-84] This was the final concert at the venue before the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) forced its closure. Live shows returned to The Garden when the [Foo Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foo_Fighters) headlined a show there on June 20, 2021. The show was for a vaccinated audience only and was the first 100 percent capacity concert in a New York arena since the start of the pandemic.[^cite_note-85]

The Sirens won 2–1 against the Torrent, [in a shootout](568092.md), in front of 18,006 fans, setting a new attendance record for women's hockey in the United States.[^cite_note-104]

That game was actually the first *with fans* held at the Garden; one of the [Professional Women's Hockey Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Women's_Hockey_Players_Association) "tour" games was held there in February 2021, but due to restrictions related to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), no fans were allowed inside to watch that first-ever professional women's hockey game held at MSG.[^cite_note-105]

## Security and surveillance

### Use of facial recognition

In 2018, under new security chief John Eversole, Madison Square Garden began deploying facial recognition to capture images of patrons entering the building and cross-reference them with a database of gathered information for security purposes, one of the first in the nation to do so.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-\:0-107]

In 2022, it was reported that MSG-owner [James Dolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dolan_\(businessman\)) had instituted a policy of using facial recognition at his venues, including Madison Square Garden, to prevent admission to attorneys whose law firms were engaged in legal disputes with Dolan and his businesses, even when those attorneys had legitimate tickets to the show or event. This led to lawsuits and an investigation into whether Dolan's venues should be stripped of their state-issued liquor licenses.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] By 2025 this had expanded to maintaining watch lists of basketball fans who had previously criticized Dolan's ownership, and banning some of them from entering Madison Square Garden venues—often under falsified pretenses.[^cite_note-\:0-107][^cite_note-110]

Former MSG Vice President of Security Donald Ingrasselino has alleged in a lawsuit that those banned also included a woman who had accused Dolan of sexual abuse and of trafficking her to [Harvey Weinstein](1624086.md), with Eversole allegedly instructing security personnel to find ways to record the woman's phone conversations.[^cite_note-\:1-111][^cite_note-\:2-112][^cite_note-\:3-113]

Former Knicks players also spoke of their rooms being bugged, and Madison Square Garden employees spoke of being followed and surveilled outside of work.[^cite_note-\:0-107]

Ingrasselino and several other current and former security employees have alleged the use of MSG's security service and surveillance apparatus to target marginalized groups in the mid-2020s.[^cite_note-\:1-111][^cite_note-\:2-112][^cite_note-\:3-113]

According to a 2026 [*Wired*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\)) investigation, in one example MSG security teams had been "obsessively" tracking the movements of a trans woman referred to pseudonymously as 'Nina' over a two year period, monitoring her movements in the venue down to the second, which according to one former security staffer was because Eversole had developed a "fixation" on her due to her being a trans woman, which included ordering his staff to compile an [open-source intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_intelligence) dossier on her.[^cite_note-\:2-112][^cite_note-\:1-111][^cite_note-\:3-113][^cite_note-\:4-114] Nina was described by Wired as sitting in the courtside 'Celebrity Row' at games, and by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* as being a personal friend to several of the Knicks players. According to several former employees, the presence of a known trans woman sitting courtside where she might be caught on camera made several MSG executives, including Eversole, uncomfortable for fear of it causing damage to MSG's reputation.[^cite_note-\:1-111] Nina was subsequently banned from entering Madison Square Garden based on what security employees unanimously described as fabricated pretenses.[^cite_note-\:2-112][^cite_note-\:4-114][^cite_note-\:3-113]

In June 2026, Madison Square Garden suffered a data breach. According to research by [404 Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/404_Media), one of the files included in the breach data showed that MSG had been building a dossier on activists who opposed MSG's use of facial recognition.[^cite_note-115]

## Recognition given by Madison Square Garden

### Madison Square Garden Gold Ticket Award

Main article: [Madison Square Garden Gold Ticket Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Gold_Ticket_Award)

In 1977, Madison Square Garden announced [Gold Ticket Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Gold_Ticket_Award) would be given to performers who had brought in more than 100,000 unit ticket sales to the venue. Since the arena's seating capacity is about 20,000, this would require a minimum of five sold-out shows. Performers who were eligible for the award at the time of its inauguration included [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_\(band\)), [John Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Denver), [Peter Frampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Frampton), the [Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones), the [Jackson 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jackson_5), [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John), [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin), [Sly Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sly_Stone), [Jethro Tull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jethro_Tull_\(band\)), [the Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Who), and [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_\(band\)).[^cite_note-Cash_Box_Nov5_77-116][^cite_note-BB_Oct29_1977-117] [Graeme Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graeme_Edge), who received his award in 1981 as a member of [the Moody Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Moody_Blues), said he found his gold ticket to be an interesting piece of memorabilia because he could use it to attend any event at the Garden.[^cite_note-118] Many other performers received Gold Ticket Awards between 1977 and 1994.

### Madison Square Garden Platinum Ticket Award

Madison Square Garden also gave Platinum Ticket Awards to performers who sold over 250,000 tickets to their shows throughout the years. Winners of the Platinum Ticket Awards include: the Rolling Stones (1981),[^cite_note-Central_NJ_Home_News_Jun14_84-119] Elton John (1982),[^cite_note-St._Petersburg_Times_Aug7_82-120] Yes (1984),[^cite_note-Daily_News_May_16_84-121] Billy Joel (1984),[^cite_note-Desert_Sun_Jul7_84-122] the Grateful Dead (1987),[^cite_note-Jaeger_Oct1_87-123] and Madonna (2004).

### Madison Square Garden Hall of Fame

The Madison Square Garden Hall of Fame honors those who have demonstrated excellence in their fields at the Garden. Most of the inductees have been sports figures, however, some performers have been inducted as well. Elton John was reported to be the first non-sports figure inducted into the MSG Hall of Fame in 1977 for "record attendance of 140,000" in June of that year.[^cite_note-BB_Oct29_1977_p3-124] For their accomplishment of "13 sell-out concerts" at the venue, the Rolling Stones were inducted into the MSG Hall of Fame in 1984, along with nine sports figures icons, bringing the hall's membership to 107.[^cite_note-125]

### Madison Square Garden Walk of Fame

Main article: [List of Madison Square Garden Walk of Fame Inductees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Madison_Square_Garden_Walk_of_Fame_Inductees)



Getting the arena ready for a [St. John's Red Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_Red_Storm_men's_basketball) basketball game in 2005

The walkway leading to the arena of Madison Square Garden was designated as the "Walk of Fame" in 1992.[^cite_note-CBS_New_York2010-126] It was established "to recognize athletes, artists, announcers and coaches for their extraordinary achievements and memorable performances at the venue."[^cite_note-Bernstein_May_11,_2015-127] Each inductee is commemorated with a plaque that lists the performance category in which his or her contributions have been made.[^cite_note-CBS_New_York2010-126] Twenty-five athletes were inducted into the MSG Walk of Fame at its inaugural ceremony in 1992, a black-tie dinner to raise money to fight multiple sclerosis.[^cite_note-Seattle_Time_Sept12_92-128] Elton John was the first entertainer to be inducted into the MSG Walk of Fame in 1992.[^cite_note-Cape_Breton_Post_Oct9_2010-129][^cite_note-130] Billy Joel was inducted at a date after Elton John,[^cite_note-Courier-News_May15_2015-131] and the Rolling Stones were inducted in 1998.[^cite_note-BB_Feb14_98-132] In 2015, the Grateful Dead were inducted into the MSG Walk of Fame along with at least three sports-related figures.[^cite_note-Courier-News_May15_2015-131][^cite_note-Bernstein_May_11,_2015-127]

### The Theater at Madison Square Garden

Main article: [The Theater at Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Theater_at_Madison_Square_Garden)

The Theater at Madison Square Garden seats between 2,000 and 5,600 for concerts and can also be used for meetings, stage shows, and graduation ceremonies. It was the home of the [NFL draft](710502.md) until 2005, when it moved to the [Jacob K. Javits Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javits_Center) after MSG management opposed a [new arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Stadium) for the [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets). It also hosted the [NBA draft](835946.md) from 2001 to 2010. The theater also occasionally hosts boxing matches.

The fall 1999 [*Jeopardy!* Teen Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Jeopardy!_tournaments_and_events) as well as [*Celebrity Jeopardy!*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Jeopardy!_tournaments_and_events#Celebrity_Jeopardy!) competitions were held at the theater. *[Wheel of Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Fortune_\(American_game_show\))* taped at the theater twice in 1999 and 2013. In 2004, it was the venue of the *[Survivor: All-Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor:_All-Stars)* finale. No seat is more than 177 feet (54 m) from the 30' × 64' stage. The theater has a relatively low 20-foot (6.1 m) ceiling at stage level[^cite_note-135] and all of its seating except for boxes on the two side walls is on one level slanted back from the stage. There is an 8,000-square-foot (740 m<sup>2</sup>) lobby at the theater.

## Accessibility and transportation



The [7th Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) entrance to Madison Square Garden and [Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)) in 2024

Madison Square Garden sits directly atop a major transportation hub, [New York Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Penn_Station), which is served by [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) and [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit_Rail_Operations) commuter rail, as well as [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak). The Garden is also accessible via the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) at the [34th Street–Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_Street%E2%80%93Penn_Station_\(IND_Eighth_Avenue_Line\)) ([A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), ​[C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), and ​[E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) trains) and the [34th Street–Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_Street%E2%80%93Penn_Station_\(IRT_Broadway%E2%80%93Seventh_Avenue_Line\)) ([1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), ​[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)), and ​[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) trains) stations.[^cite_note-136]
