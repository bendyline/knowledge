**Clomifene**, also known as **clomiphene**, is a medication used to treat [infertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infertility) in women who [do not ovulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anovulation), including those with [polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyendocrine_metabolic_ovarian_syndrome).[^cite_note-AHFS2016-5] It is taken [by mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By_mouth).[^cite_note-AHFS2016-5]

Common side effects include [pelvic pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_pain) and [hot flashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_flashes).[^cite_note-AHFS2016-5] Other side effects can include changes in vision, vomiting, trouble sleeping, [ovarian cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer), and [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizures).[^cite_note-AHFS2016-5][^cite_note-WHO2008-6] It is not recommended in people with [liver disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_disease) or [abnormal vaginal bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abnormal_vaginal_bleeding) of unknown cause or who are [pregnant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnant).[^cite_note-WHO2008-6][^cite_note-FDAlabel-7] Clomifene is in the [selective estrogen receptor modulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_estrogen_receptor_modulator) (SERM) family of medication and is a nonsteroidal medication.[^cite_note-FDAlabel-7][^cite_note-8] It works by causing the release of [GnRH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GnRH) by the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus), and subsequently [gonadotropin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonadotropin) from the [anterior pituitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterior_pituitary).[^cite_note-WHO2008-6]

Clomifene was approved for medical use in the United States in 1967.[^cite_note-AHFS2016-5] It is on the [World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines).[^cite_note-WHO23rd-9] Its introduction began the era of [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology).[^cite_note-Hist-10]

Clomifene (particularly the purified enclomiphene isomer) has also been found to have a powerful ability to boost or restore testosterone levels in [hypogonadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypogonadism) men.[^cite_note-Enclomiphene_citrate_for_the_treatm-11] It can be used to enhance performance in sports and is banned by the [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency).

## Medical uses

### Reproductive medicine

Further information: [Ovulation induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovulation_induction) and [Feminizing hormone therapy § Fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminizing_hormone_therapy#Fertility)

Clomifene is one of several alternatives for inducing [ovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovulation) in those who are infertile due to [anovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anovulation) or [oligoovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligoovulation).[^cite_note-Committee2013-12] Evidence is lacking for the use of clomifene in those who are infertile without a known reason.[^cite_note-Hugh2010-13] In such cases, studies have observed a clinical pregnancy rate 5.6% per cycle with clomifene treatment vs. 1.3%–4.2% per cycle without treatment.[^cite_note-Committee2013-12] Clomifene has also been used with other [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology) to increase success rates of these other modalities.[^cite_note-Induction-14]

Clomifene has been effectively used to restore [spermatogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermatogenesis) in trans women looking to have biological children.[^cite_note-15] The effect of feminizing hormone therapy on fertility is not clear, but it is known that it can prevent sperm production.[^cite_note-16]

#### Testosterone replacement therapy

Clomifene is sometimes used in the treatment of male [hypogonadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypogonadism) as an alternative to [testosterone replacement therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone_replacement_therapy).[^cite_note-BachNajari2016-17] It has been found to increase testosterone levels by 2–2.5 times in hypogonadal men at such dosages.[^cite_note-BachNajari2016-17][^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] Despite the use of questionnaires in testosterone replacement comparator trials being called into question, clomifene's lower cost, therapeutic benefits, and greater value towards hypogonadism improvement have been noted.[^cite_note-19]

Clomifene consists of two [stereoisomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoisomers) in equal proportion: [enclomifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclomifene) and [zuclomifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuclomifene). Zuclomifene has pro-estrogenic properties, whereas enclomifene is pro-androgenic, i.e. it promotes testosterone production through stimulation of the [HPG axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPG_axis). For this reason, purified enclomifene isomer has been found to be twice as effective in boosting testosterone compared to the standard mix of both isomers.[^cite_note-Enclomiphene_citrate_for_the_treatm-11] Additionally, enclomifene has a [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life) of just ten hours,[^cite_note-singledosekinetics-4] but zuclomifene has a half-life on the order of several days to a week, so if the goal is to boost testosterone, taking regular clomifene may produce far longer-lasting pro-estrogenic effects than pro-androgenic effects.[^cite_note-20]

#### Gynecomastia

Clomifene has been used in the treatment of [gynecomastia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynecomastia).[^cite_note-Becker2001-21] It has been found to be useful in the treatment of some cases of gynecomastia but it is not as effective as [tamoxifen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamoxifen) or [raloxifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raloxifene) for this indication.[^cite_note-AgrawalGanie2017-22] It has shown variable results for gynecomastia (probably because the zuclomifene isomer is estrogenic), and hence is not recommended for treatment of the condition.[^cite_note-pmid18622190-23] Pure enclomifene isomer is likely to be more effective than clomifene at treating gynecomastia, because of the lack of the zuclomifene isomer (as noted above).

Due to its long half-life, zuclomifene can be detected in urine for at least 261 days after discontinuation[^cite_note-24] (261 days after discontinuation with a half-life of 30 days, there is still 0.24% of the peak level of zuclomifene being excreted, whereas with a half-life of ten hours, enclomifene reaches the same 0.24% level in less than four days).

## Prohibited use in sports

The [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency) (WADA) prohibits clomifene under category S4 of hormone and metabolic modulators. It can be present as an undeclared ingredient in black market products available online to enhance athletic performance. Like other substances with [anabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabolic_steroid) properties, clomifene leads to increased muscle mass in males.[^cite_note-25]

Because clomifene can enhance [egg production in hens](12116844.md#Egg-laying_chickens), athletes may inadvertently consume the substance through contaminated food.[^cite_note-26] A WADA study found that clomifene given to laying hens migrates into their eggs but was able to develop a method of distinguishing egg ingestion from doping.[^cite_note-27]

## Contraindications

Contraindications include an allergy to the medication, pregnancy, prior liver problems, abnormal vaginal bleeding of unclear cause, ovarian cysts other than those due to polycystic ovarian syndrome, unmanaged adrenal or thyroid problems, and [pituitary tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_tumors).[^cite_note-FDAlabel-7]

## Side effects

The most common [adverse drug reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_drug_reaction) associated with the use of clomifene (>10% of people) is reversible ovarian enlargement.[^cite_note-FDAlabel-7]

Less common effects (1–10% of people) include visual symptoms (blurred vision, [double vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplopia), floaters, [eye sensitivity to light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photophobia), [scotomata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotomata)), headaches, vasomotor flushes (or [hot flashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_flash)), light sensitivity and pupil constriction, abnormal uterine bleeding and/or abdominal discomfort.[^cite_note-FDAlabel-7]

Rare adverse events (<1% of people) include: [high blood level of triglycerides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertriglyceridemia), [liver inflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis), reversible [baldness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alopecia) and/or [ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_hyperstimulation_syndrome).[^cite_note-FDAlabel-7]

Rates of birth defects and miscarriages do not appear to change with the use of clomifene for fertility.[^cite_note-FDAlabel-7] Clomifene has been associated with [liver abnormalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_abnormalities) and a couple of cases of [hepatotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatotoxicity).[^cite_note-CameronFeuer2012-28]

### Cancer risk

Some studies have suggested that clomifene if used for more than a year may increase the risk of [ovarian cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer).[^cite_note-Hugh2010-13] This may only be the case in those who have never been and do not become pregnant.[^cite_note-Trabert-29] Subsequent studies have failed to support those findings.[^cite_note-Committee2013-12][^cite_note-30]

Clomifene has been shown to be associated with an increased risk of malignant [melanomas](716631.md) and [thyroid cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_cancer).[^cite_note-pmid29159661-3] Thyroid cancer risk was not associated with the number of pregnancies carried to viability.[^cite_note-31]

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacodynamics

#### Selective estrogen receptor modulator activity

Clomifene is a [nonsteroidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsteroidal) [triphenylethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triphenylethylene) [derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_derivative) that acts as a [selective estrogen receptor modulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_estrogen_receptor_modulator) (SERM).[^cite_note-Induction-14] It consists of a non-racemic mixture of [zuclomifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuclomifene) (\~38%) and [enclomifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclomifene) (\~62%), each of which has unique [pharmacologic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology) properties.[^cite_note-\:1-32] It is a mixed [agonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agonist) and [antagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_antagonist) of the [estrogen receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen_receptor) (ER). Clomifene activates the [ERα](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ER%CE%B1) in the setting of low baseline [estrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen) levels and partially blocks the receptor in the context of high baseline estrogen levels.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] Conversely, it is an [antagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_antagonist) of the [ERβ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ER%CE%B2).[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] Clomifene has antiestrogenic effects in the [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus).[^cite_note-Goldstein2000-33] There is little clinical research on the influence of clomifene in many target tissues, such as [lipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid), the [cardiovascular system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_system), and the [breasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast).[^cite_note-Goldstein2000-33][^cite_note-Haskell2003-34] Positive effects of clomifene on [bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone) have been observed.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18][^cite_note-Goldstein2000-33][^cite_note-Haskell2003-34] Clomifene has been found to decrease [insulin-like growth factor 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin-like_growth_factor_1) (IGF-1) levels in women.[^cite_note-pmid27704479-35]

Clomifene is a long-acting ER [ligand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand_\(biochemistry\)), with a [nuclear retention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_retention) of greater than 48 hours.[^cite_note-RunnebaumRabe2013-36] Clomifene is a [prodrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodrug) being activated via similar [metabolic pathways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_pathway) as the related triphenylethylene SERMs tamoxifen and [toremifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toremifene).[^cite_note-RocheZito2019-37][^cite_note-pmid23406671-38] The [affinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affinity_\(pharmacology\)) of clomifene for the ER relative to [estradiol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estradiol_\(medication\)) ranges from 0.1 to 12% in different studies, which is similar to the range for tamoxifen (0.06–16%).[^cite_note-WittliffKerr2005-39][^cite_note-pmid10746941-40][^cite_note-pmid11258977-41] 4-Hydroxyclomifene, a major active metabolite of clomifene, and [afimoxifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afimoxifene) (4-hydroxytamoxifen), a major active metabolite of tamoxifen, show 89–251% and 41–246% of the affinity of estradiol for the ER in human [MCF-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCF-7) [breast cancer](70547.md) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_\(biology\)), respectively.[^cite_note-pmid9586957-42][^cite_note-pmid3778464-43] The ER affinities of the [isomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomer) of 4-hydroxyclomifene were 285% for (*E*)-4-hydroxyclomifene and 16% for (*Z*)-4-hydroxyclomifene relative to estradiol.[^cite_note-pmid9586957-42] 4-Hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphene has similar affinity to 4-hydroxyclomifene for the ER.[^cite_note-pmid23406671-38] In one study, the affinities of clomifene and its metabolites for the [ERα](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ER%CE%B1) were \~100 nM for clomifene, \~2.4 nM for 4-hydroxyclomifene, \~125 nM for *N*-desethylclomiphene, and \~1.4 nM for 4-hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphene.[^cite_note-pmid23406671-38]

Even though clomifene has some [estrogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen_\(medication\)) effect, the [antiestrogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiestrogen) property is believed to be the primary source for [stimulating ovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovulation_induction).[^cite_note-AHFS2016-5] Clomifene appears to act mostly in the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus) where it depletes hypothalamic ERs and blocks the [negative feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_feedback) effect of circulating [endogenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endogenous) [estradiol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estradiol), which in turn results in an increase in [hypothalamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus) [gonadotropin-releasing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonadotropin-releasing_hormone) (GnRH) pulse frequency and circulating concentrations of [follicle-stimulating hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follicle-stimulating_hormone) (FSH) and [luteinizing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luteinizing_hormone) (LH).

In normal physiologic female hormonal cycling, at seven days past [ovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovulation), high levels of estrogen and [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone) produced from the corpus luteum inhibit GnRH, FSH, and LH at the hypothalamus and anterior pituitary. If fertilization does not occur in the post-ovulation period the [corpus luteum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_luteum) disintegrates due to a lack of [human chorionic gonadotropin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_chorionic_gonadotropin) (hCG). This would normally be produced by the embryo in the effort of maintaining progesterone and estrogen levels during pregnancy.

Therapeutically, clomifene is given early in the [menstrual cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle) to produce follicles. Follicles, in turn, produce the estrogen, which circulates in serum. In the presence of clomifene, the body perceives a low level of estrogen, similar to day 22 in the previous cycle. Since estrogen can no longer effectively exert negative feedback on the hypothalamus, GnRH secretion becomes more rapidly pulsatile, which results in increased pituitary gonadotropin release. (More rapid, lower amplitude pulses of GnRH lead to increased LH and FSH secretion, while more irregular, larger amplitude pulses of GnRH leads to a decrease in the ratio of LH to FSH.) Increased FSH levels cause the growth of more ovarian follicles, and subsequently rupture of follicles resulting in ovulation. Ovulation occurs most often 6 to 7 days after a course of clomifene.

&#x20;In normal men, 50 mg/day clomifene for eight months has been found to increase [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone) levels by around 870 ng/dL in younger men and by around 490 ng/dL in elderly men.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] [Estradiol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estradiol) levels increased by 62 pg/mL in younger men and by 40 pg/mL in elderly men.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] These findings suggest that the [progonadotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progonadotropic) effects of clomifene are stronger in younger men than in older men.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] In men with [hypogonadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypogonadism), clomifene has been found to increase testosterone levels by 293 to 362 ng/dL and estradiol levels by 5.5 to 13 pg/mL.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18] In a large clinical study of men with low testosterone levels (<400 ng/dL), 25 mg/day clomifene increased testosterone levels from 309 ng/dL to 642 ng/dL after three months of therapy.[^cite_note-pmid27601965-44] No significant changes in [HDL cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDL_cholesterol), [triglycerides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglyceride), fasting [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose), or [prolactin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactin) levels were observed, although [total cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_cholesterol) levels decreased significantly.[^cite_note-TrostKhera2014-18][^cite_note-pmid27601965-44]

#### Other activities

Clomifene is an [inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme_inhibitor) of the conversion of [desmosterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmosterol) into [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol) by the [enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme) [24-dehydrocholesterol reductase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-dehydrocholesterol_reductase).[^cite_note-Zhang2018-45][^cite_note-MaximovMcDaniel2013a-46] Concerns about possible induction of [desmosterolosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmosterolosis) and associated symptoms such as [cataracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataract) and [ichthyosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyosis) with extended exposure precluded the use of clomifene in the treatment of breast cancer.[^cite_note-Zhang2018-45][^cite_note-MaximovMcDaniel2013a-46] Continuous use of clomifene has been found to increase desmosterol levels by 10% and continuous high doses of clomifene (200 mg/day) have been reported to produce [visual disturbances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_disturbance).[^cite_note-Jucker2013-47][^cite_note-48] In 2025, clomifene, along with the structurally related molecules [tamoxifen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamoxifen) and [toremifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toremifene), was reported to interact with [tubulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubulin) and inhibit its polymerization.[^cite_note-49]

### Pharmacokinetics

Clomifene produces *N*-desethylclomiphene, [clomifenoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clomifenoxide) (clomifene *N*-oxide), 4-hydroxyclomifene, and 4-hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphene as [metabolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite).[^cite_note-pmid29516347-2][^cite_note-AcademicPress1998-50] Clomifene is a [prodrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodrug) most importantly of 4-hydroxyclomifene and 4-hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphene, which are the most active of its metabolites.[^cite_note-RocheZito2019-37][^cite_note-pmid23406671-38] In one study, the [peak levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_levels) after a single 50 mg dose of clomifene were 20.37 nmol/L for clomifene, 0.95 nmol/L for 4-hydroxyclomifene, and 1.15 nmol/L for 4-hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphene.[^cite_note-pmid29516347-2]

Clomifene has an [onset of action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onset_of_action) of five to ten days following course of treatment and an [elimination half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_half-life) about four to seven days.[^cite_note-pmid29516347-2][^cite_note-singledosekinetics-4] In one study, after a single 50 mg dose of clomifene, the half-life of clomifene was 128 hours (5.3 days), of 4-hydroxyclomifene was 13 hours, and of 4-hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphenewas 15 hours.[^cite_note-pmid29516347-2] Individuals with the CYP2D6\*10 [allele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) showed longer half-lives for 4-hydroxyclomifene and 4-hydroxy-*N*-desethylclomiphene.[^cite_note-pmid29516347-2] Primarily due to differences in CYP2D6 genetics, steady state concentrations and individual response to clomifene are highly variable.[^cite_note-51]

Most clomifene metabolism occurs in the [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver), where it undergoes [enterohepatic recirculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterohepatic_recirculation). Clomifene and its metabolites are [excreted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excretion) primarily through [feces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feces) (42%), and excretion can occur up to 6 weeks after discontinuation.[^cite_note-\:1-32]

## Chemistry

Clomifene is a [triphenylethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triphenylethylene) derivative. It is a mixture of two [geometric isomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_isomerism), the cis [enclomifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclomifene) (*(E)*-clomifene) form and trans [zuclomifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuclomifene) (*(Z)*-clomifene) form. These two isomers contribute to the mixed estrogenic and antiestrogenic properties of clomifene.[^cite_note-Hist-10] The typical ratio of these isomers after synthesis is 38% zuclomiphene and 62% enclomiphene.[^cite_note-singledosekinetics-4] The United States Pharmacopeia specifies that clomifene preparations must contain between 30% and 50% zuclomiphene.[^cite_note-singledosekinetics-4]

## History

A team at [William S. Merrell Chemical Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Merrell_Chemical_Company) led by Frank Palopoli synthesized clomifene in 1956; after its biological activity was confirmed a patent was filed and issued in November 1959.[^cite_note-Hist-10][^cite_note-52] Scientists at Merrell had previously synthesized [chlorotrianisene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorotrianisene) and [ethamoxytriphetol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethamoxytriphetol).[^cite_note-Hist-10] Clomifene was studied in the treatment of [advanced breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_breast_cancer) during the period of 1964 to 1974 and was found to be effective but was abandoned due to concerns about [desmosterolosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmosterolosis) with extended use.[^cite_note-Zhang2018-45][^cite_note-pmid12796359-53][^cite_note-HowellJordan2013B-54] Short-term use (e.g. days to months) did not raise the same concerns and clomifene continued to be studied for other indications.[^cite_note-MaximovMcDaniel2013a-46][^cite_note-Jucker2013-47]

Clinical studies were conducted under an [Investigational New Drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigational_New_Drug) Application; clomifene was third drug for which an IND had been filed under the 1962 [Kefauver Harris Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefauver_Harris_Amendment) to the [Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Food,_Drug,_and_Cosmetic_Act) that had been passed in response to the [thalidomide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalidomide) tragedy.[^cite_note-Hist-10] It was approved for marketing in 1967 under the brand name Clomid.[^cite_note-Hist-10][^cite_note-56] It was first used to treat cases of [oligomenorrhea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligomenorrhea) but was expanded to include treatment of [anovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anovulation) when women undergoing treatment had higher than expected rates of pregnancy.[^cite_note-57]

The drug is widely considered to have been a revolution in the treatment of female infertility, the beginning of the modern era of [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology), and the beginning of what in the words of [Eli Y. Adashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Y._Adashi), was "the onset of the US multiple births epidemic".[^cite_note-Hist-10][^cite_note-58]

The company was acquired by [Dow Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Chemical) in 1980,[^cite_note-Los_Angeles_Times-59][^cite_note-60] and in 1989 Dow Chemical acquired 67 percent interest of Marion Laboratories, which was renamed Marion Merrell Dow.[^cite_note-Los_Angeles_Times-59] In 1995, Hoechst AG acquired the pharmaceutical business of Marion Merrell Dow.[^cite_note-61] Hoechst in turn became part of [Aventis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aventis) in 1999,[^cite_note-Bris-62]<sup>: 9–11 </sup> and subsequently a part of [Sanofi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanofi).[^cite_note-63] It became the most widely prescribed drug for [ovulation induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovulation_induction) to reverse [anovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anovulation) or [oligoovulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligoovulation).[^cite_note-StraussBarbieri2013-64]

## Research

Clomifene has been used almost exclusively for ovulation induction in [premenopausal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premenopausal) women, and has been studied very limitedly in [postmenopausal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmenopausal) women.[^cite_note-Palacios2007-65]

Clomifene was studied for treatment and prevention of [breast cancer](70547.md), but issues with toxicity led to abandonment of this indication, as did the discovery of [tamoxifen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamoxifen).[^cite_note-66] Like the structurally related drug [triparanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triparanol), clomifene is known to [inhibit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme_inhibitor) the [enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme) [24-dehydrocholesterol reductase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-dehydrocholesterol_reductase) and increase circulating [desmosterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmosterol) levels, making it unfavorable for extended use in breast cancer due to risk of side effects like irreversible [cataracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataracts).[^cite_note-Elsevier2013-67][^cite_note-MaximovMcDaniel2013b-68]
