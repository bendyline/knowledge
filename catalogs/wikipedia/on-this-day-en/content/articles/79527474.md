The **2025–26 Michigan Wolverines men's basketball team** represented the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) during the [2025–26 NCAA Division I men's basketball season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_season). It was the program's 110th season and 109th consecutive year as a member of the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference). The Wolverines were led by second-year head coach [Dusty May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusty_May) and played their home games at [Crisler Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisler_Center) in [Ann Arbor, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan).

On December 8, Michigan achieved the [No. 1 ranking in the Coaches poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_rankings#USA_Today_Coaches_Poll) for the first time since the [Fab Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fab_Five_\(University_of_Michigan\))-led [1992–93 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team), and on February 16, Michigan earned the [No. 1 ranking in the AP poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_rankings#AP_poll) for the first time since the [2012–13 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team). On February 24, Michigan clinched a share of the [2026 Big Ten regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Big_Ten_Conference_men's_basketball_season) championship against [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Minnesota_Golden_Gophers_men's_basketball_team), and secured the outright Big Ten championship against [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Illinois_Fighting_Illini_men's_basketball_team) on February 27. It was the Wolverines' first since the [2021 Big Ten regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Big_Ten_Conference_men's_basketball_season) championship.

Michigan finished the regular season 29–2, and 19–1 in the conference. Their 29 regular-season wins are a program record, and their 19 conference wins are a Big Ten record. The conference wins surpassed the previous program record of 16 wins by the [1976–77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) and [1984–85 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team), and surpassed the previous Big Ten record set by the [1974–75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Indiana_Hoosiers_men's_basketball_team) and [1975–76 Indiana Hoosiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Indiana_Hoosiers_men's_basketball_team) (18–0). Michigan is the only Big Ten team to have ever won all ten conference road games, and the first to finish undefeated on the road since the 1975–76 Hoosiers. It was also the first Michigan team to defeat [rival Ohio State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan%E2%80%93Ohio_State_men's_basketball_rivalry) three times in the same season.

In the [2026 NCAA tournament](76465965.md), Michigan made the Sweet Sixteen for an NCAA-leading ninth time in the last thirteen NCAA tournaments and set a program-record (and tied the [2004–05 Illinois Fighting Illini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_Illinois_Fighting_Illini_men's_basketball_team) for the Big Ten record) for single-season wins, 37, as they won the [national championship](79949085.md) against [UConn](79960295.md). Michigan surpassed the previous single-season wins record of 33 by [John Beilein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Beilein) and the [2017–18 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team). They became the first team to score at least 90 points in five games in a single NCAA tournament, and held their last four opponents to season-lows in [field goal percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_percentage).

It was the second national title in program history and the first since [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). Michigan became the first Big Ten team to win the national title since their [rival Michigan State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan%E2%80%93Michigan_State_men's_basketball_rivalry) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). Michigan also joined the [Florida Gators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Gators) and [Michigan State Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Spartans) as the only programs to win multiple national championships in both basketball and football, and became the first program since Florida in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NCAA_Division_I_FBS_football_season) to win a national title in each sport within three academic years.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## Previous season

The [Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) finished the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_season) with a 27–10 record, 14–6 in [conference play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Big_Ten_Conference_men's_basketball_season) to finish the regular season tied for second place. As the No. 3 seed in the [Big Ten tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Big_Ten_men's_basketball_tournament), they defeated [Purdue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Purdue_Boilermakers_men's_basketball_team) in the quarterfinal, [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Maryland_Terrapins_men's_basketball_team) in the semifinal, and [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Wisconsin_Badgers_men's_basketball_team) in the final, winning their first Big Ten tournament championship since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Big_Ten_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-3] The Wolverines received the Big Ten's automatic bid to the [NCAA tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament) as the No. 5 seed in the South Region, where they defeated [UC San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UC_San_Diego_Tritons_men's_basketball_team), and [Texas A\&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Texas_A\&M_Aggies_men's_basketball_team) to advance to their sixth Sweet Sixteen in the last eight NCAA tournaments. In the regional semifinal, they lost to the No. 1 overall seed [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Auburn_Tigers_men's_basketball_team).[^cite_note-4]

## Offseason

On March 24, 2025, guard [Justin Pippen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Pippen) entered the [NCAA transfer portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_transfer_portal). He did not play or travel with the Wolverines in the postseason.[^cite_note-5] Pippen transferred to the [Cal Golden Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Golden_Bears_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-6] On March 31, forward Jace Howard entered the transfer portal after five seasons with Michigan and forward Sam Walters entered after one season with the program.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Howard would transfer to the [Fordham Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Rams_men's_basketball) and Walters to the [SMU Mustangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMU_Mustangs_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] That same day, the Wolverines received their first transfer portal commitment from junior [Elliot Cadeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Cadeau), a [point guard](583875.md) for the [North Carolina Tar Heels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_men's_basketball). As a sophomore, Cadeau averaged 9.4 points per game and was second in the [Atlantic Coast Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Conference) (ACC) with 6.2 assists per game. He was a former five-star recruit in high school and started 68 games in his first two collegiate seasons.[^cite_note-11] On April 1, point guard [Tre Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tre_Donaldson) entered the transfer portal. He started all 37 games for the Wolverines in his lone season with the program.[^cite_note-12] Donaldson would transfer to the [Miami Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Hurricanes_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-13] That same day, Michigan received its second transfer portal commitment from sophomore forward [Morez Johnson Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morez_Johnson_Jr.) of the [Illinois Fighting Illini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Fighting_Illini_men's_basketball). He started eight games as a freshman.[^cite_note-14]

On April 5, the top-rated player in the transfer portal, forward [Yaxel Lendeborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaxel_Lendeborg) from the [UAB Blazers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAB_Blazers_men's_basketball), committed to the Wolverines as the third incoming transfer. He also entered the [2025 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_draft) process in addition to transferring. Last season Lendeborg became one of only two players in NCAA Division I history to record over 600 points, 400 rebounds and 150 assists in a single season, joining legend [Larry Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Bird), and won the [American Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conference_\(NCAA\)) Defensive Player of the Year for a second consecutive season.[^cite_note-15] He also led the NCAA with 26 double-doubles last season, averaging 17.7 points, 11.4 rebounds, 4.2 assists, 1.8 blocks and 1.7 steals per game.[^cite_note-16] On April 9, guard Phat Phat Brooks entered the transfer portal, Michigan's fifth player to do so.[^cite_note-17] Brooks transferred to the [Central Michigan Chippewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Michigan_Chippewas_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-18] On April 11, the Wolverines secured their fourth incoming transfer commitment, junior [Aday Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aday_Mara), a 7'3" Spanish center from the [UCLA Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Bruins_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-19]

On April 16, junior power forward [Danny Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Wolf) announced he would forgo his remaining eligibility and declared for the [2025 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_draft).[^cite_note-20] On April 21, shooting guard [Nimari Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimari_Burnett) announced he would return for his final season after being granted a sixth year of eligibility.[^cite_note-21] On May 27, Lendeborg withdrew from the NBA draft and chose to play his final season of college basketball in [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor).[^cite_note-22] Lendeborg, Johnson and Mara had all played [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_\(basketball\)) the previous season.[^cite_note-MwtNbDMubtaehtbampt-23]

### Recruiting classes

#### 2025 recruiting class

On October 18, 2024, Michigan landed its first class of 2025 recruit, Winters Grady of [Prolific Prep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolific_Prep) in [Napa, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napa,_California). At the time of his commitment, the four-star [Tualatin, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tualatin,_Oregon) native was ranked No. 83 in the 2025 national class by [247Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/247Sports).[^cite_note-24] On October 23, Michigan received their second commitment from New Zealand native [Oscar Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Goodman_\(basketball\)), who was the [2023 FIBA Under-16 Asian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIBA_Under-16_Asian_Championship) MVP and a [2024 FIBA Under-17 Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIBA_Under-17_Basketball_World_Cup) [All-Tournament first team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Under-17_Basketball_World_Cup_All-Tournament_Team) selection.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] He was rated as a top 100, four-star player by [On3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On3.com), but unrated by 247Sports at the time of his commitment.[^cite_note-27] On November 9, the Wolverines earned their third commitment from [Flint, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint,_Michigan) native and five-star guard from [St. Mary's Preparatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Preparatory), [Trey McKenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_McKenney). McKenney won a gold medal and started every game for the [United States national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_under-18_basketball_team) in the [2024 FIBA Under-18 AmeriCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIBA_Under-18_AmeriCup) in Argentina. At the time of his commitment, he was ranked as the No. 16 overall player in the country by ESPN and No. 19 in 247Sports' composite rankings.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] All three players officially signed their [national letter of intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_letter_of_intent) during the early signing period in November 2024.[^cite_note-30]

On January 7, 2025, Goodman enrolled early, joining the [2024–25 Michigan basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) midseason.[^cite_note-31] As a result of him graduating in November 2024, based on the New Zealand academic calendar, he would remain a member of Michigan's 2025 recruiting class. He used the season to [redshirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redshirt_\(college_sports\)) and was limited to practice only.[^cite_note-32] On January 27, McKenney was named a [2025 McDonald's All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_McDonald's_All-American_Boys_Game). He was the first Michigan signee to be selected since [Moussa Diabaté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Diabat%C3%A9), [Caleb Houstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Houstan) and [Kobe Bufkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bufkin) in 2021.[^cite_note-33] On May 16, Michigan received their fourth commitment in the 2025 class from forward Ricky Liburd, whom flipped from his commitment to play for [Coastal Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Carolina_Chanticleers_men's_basketball). He attended [Sagemont School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagemont_School) near his hometown of [Hollywood, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Florida), and was ranked as a three-star recruit by 247Sports.[^cite_note-34] On June 13, the Wolverines received their fifth commitment from 7'1" center Malick Kordel from [Oberhausen, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberhausen,_Germany). ## Regular season

### November

#### Oakland

On November 3, Michigan began the season with a 121–78 victory over [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Oakland_Golden_Grizzlies_men's_basketball_team), winning a 23rd consecutive season-opening game.[^cite_note-ml-36] For the first time in program history, seven Wolverines scored in double figures. Michigan was led by [Morez Johnson Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morez_Johnson_Jr.) with a career-high 24 points, while [Aday Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aday_Mara) had 12 points, 12 rebounds and five blocks; his first double-double of the season. Five others scored in double figures, including freshman [Trey McKenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_McKenney) scoring 21 points in his collegiate debut on 6-of-8 three-point shooting, [Will Tschetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Tschetter) with 16 points, [Nimari Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimari_Burnett) with 14 points, [Yaxel Lendeborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaxel_Lendeborg) added 12 points, and [Roddy Gayle Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roddy_Gayle_Jr.) had 11 points. [Elliot Cadeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Cadeau) added a career-high 14 assists in his debut.[^cite_note-37] Michigan scored a program-record 69 points in the first half, shooting 71% from the field and surpassing the previous record of 66 points set by the [1987–88 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) against [Eastern Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Michigan_Eagles_men's_basketball). The 121 points were the most scored since the [1989–90 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) scored 127 against [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Iowa_Hawkeyes_men's_basketball_team), and marked the sixth time in program history eclipsing 120 points in a game.[^cite_note-38] The Wolverines finished shooting 64% from the field, tied a program-record with 19 made three-point shots (on 35 attempts: 54%), committed only eight turnovers, and had 55 bench points.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-ml-36]

#### Wake Forest

On November 11, Michigan defeated [Wake Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Wake_Forest_Demon_Deacons_men's_basketball_team), 85–84 in overtime, during the Deacon-Wolverine Challenge at [Little Caesars Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Caesars_Arena). Michigan was led by Mara with 18 points, and career-highs 13 rebounds, six assists, and five blocks; his second double-double of the season. Cadeau had 17 points, a career-high eight rebounds, and seven assists, surpassing 200 career rebounds and 400 career assists. Gayle Jr. had 13 points, and Johnson Jr. added ten points and eight rebounds. With 13 rebounds, Mara surpassed 200 for his career.[^cite_note-40] Michigan led by 13 at halftime, but started 0-for-12 on three-point attempts in the second half, allowing Wake Forest to tie the game and force overtime. Cadeau scored or assisted on all five of Michigan's overtime points.[^cite_note-41]

#### TCU

On November 14, Michigan defeated [TCU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_TCU_Horned_Frogs_men's_basketball_team), 67–63. Michigan was led by double-double performances from Lendeborg with 14 points and ten rebounds, and Johnson Jr. with ten points and 12 rebounds. McKenney scored 11 points and Gayle Jr. added ten points and six assists. With his ten rebounds, Lendeborg surpassed 800 in his NCAA Division I career.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

#### Middle Tennessee

On November 19, Michigan defeated [Middle Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Middle_Tennessee_Blue_Raiders_men's_basketball_team), 86–61. Michigan was led by Lendeborg with a game-high 25 points, and 12 rebounds, his second double-double of the season. Mara added ten points and ten rebounds off the bench, for his third double-double of the season, while Cadeau scored ten points. Tschetter added five points in his first start since March 13, 2024. Ten Wolverines scored during the game, with a season-high 13 players earning minutes. Michigan's defense forced 15 turnovers, the most by a Wolverines opponent in a game this season.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

#### San Diego State

On November 24, Michigan defeated [San Diego State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_San_Diego_State_Aztecs_men's_basketball_team), 94–54, in the first game of the [2025 Players Era Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Players_Era_Festival). Michigan was led by Lendeborg with a game-high 15 points, six rebounds and four assists. Five others scored in double figures, Johnson Jr. and Cadeau with 13 points each, Burnett and Gayle Jr. scored 11 points each, and Tschetter added ten points. Tschetter became the 76th Wolverine in program history to appear in 100 career games.[^cite_note-46]

#### Auburn

On November 25, Michigan defeated No. 21 [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Auburn_Tigers_men's_basketball_team), 102–72, to advance to the Players Era Festival championship game. Michigan was led by Lendeborg with 17 points, five rebounds and four assists, while Gayle Jr. added a season-high 17 points and six rebounds. Four others scored in double figures, Burnett and Johnson Jr. with 15 points each, McKenney scored 11 points and Cason added ten points. Michigan used a 21–4 run late in the first half to extend their lead to 29 points. Michigan shot 51.5% from the field, including 14 of 35 (40%) from three-point range.[^cite_note-47] A season-high 11 players reached the scoresheet, including freshman Winters Grady scoring his first career points. The win marked Michigan's first NCAA-recognized 30-plus-point win over a power-four opponent since a 104–68 win against No. 3 [Purdue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_Boilermakers_men's_basketball) on March 3, 1987. The Wolverines scored 100-plus points against a ranked opponent for the first time since defeating No. 6 [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Duke_Blue_Devils_men's_basketball_team) 113–108 on December 9, 1989.[^cite_note-48]

#### Gonzaga

On November 26, Michigan defeated No. 12 [Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Gonzaga_Bulldogs_men's_basketball_team), 101–61, to win the Players Era Festival championship. Michigan was led by Lendeborg with a game-high 20 points and 11 rebounds, his third double-double of the season, and was named tournament MVP. Four others scored in double figures, McKenney with 17 points, Burnett scored 14 points, Mara had 13 points, and Johnson Jr. added 11 points. Cadeau recorded 13 assists, tied for the seventh most in a single game in program history.[^cite_note-49] The Wolverines won their three games by an average of 36.7 points. Michigan's 40 point win was [Mark Few](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Few)'s worst loss in 902 games as Gonzaga's head coach, and the Bulldogs worst loss since losing 144–100 to [Loyola Marymount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_Marymount_Lions_men's_basketball) in 1990. Gonzaga was held to 33.8% shooting from the floor, including 3-of-22 (13.6%) from three-point range.[^cite_note-50] The win marked the highest margin of victory against an AP ranked opponent in program history, and was the first time in NCAA history that any team outscored AP ranked opponents by 30 or more points in consecutive games.[^cite_note-51]

### December

#### Rutgers

On December 6, Michigan opened the [Big Ten Conference season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Big_Ten_Conference_men's_basketball_season) against [Rutgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Rutgers_Scarlet_Knights_men's_basketball_team), defeating the Scarlet Knights, 101–60. Michigan was led by Johnson Jr. with a game-high 22 points. Five others scored in double figures, Lendeborg with 14 points, McKenney added 13 points, and Cadeau, Cason and Gayle Jr. scored 11 points each. Michigan shot 60% from the field and scored 22 of their first 24 points in the paint, and finished the game with a season-high 52 points in the paint.[^cite_note-52] The Wolverines scored more than 100 points for the third consecutive game, marking the first time this has occurred since the season they won the [national championship in 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_championship_game). Four others scored in double figures, Johnson Jr. with 18 points, Gayle Jr. had 15 points, McKenney added 12 points, and Burnett scored ten points. Michigan swept Michigan State for the first time since the [2013–14 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team). With the win, Michigan earned its fifth regular-season victory against an AP top-10 opponent, tying the program record for a single season with the 1964–65 Wolverines. The win set a Big Ten record for conference wins with 19, surpassing the previous record of 18 set twice by the 1974–75 and 1975–76 Indiana Hoosiers. The victory also set a program record for wins in the regular season with 29, surpassing the previous record of 27 set by the [1985–86 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team).[^cite_note-MSU-113]

## Postseason

### Big Ten tournament

Michigan earned the No. 1 seed in the [2026 Big Ten tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Big_Ten_men's_basketball_tournament) as the defending champions in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Big_Ten_men's_basketball_tournament). They received a triple-bye in the first year of a full 18-team tournament, which took place at the [United Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Center) in [Chicago](6886.md) from March 10 until March 15.[^cite_note-114]

#### Quarterfinal

On March 13, Michigan defeated [Ohio State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Ohio_State_Buckeyes_men's_basketball_team) in the quarterfinal, 71–67. It was the first time the Wolverines ever defeated their [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan%E2%80%93Ohio_State_men's_basketball_rivalry) three times in a season.[^cite_note-Quarterfinal-115] They also became the sixth team in program history to reach 30 wins in a season, and the first since the [2018–19 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team).[^cite_note-116] Michigan was led by Mara with 17 points and seven rebounds. Three others scored in double figures, Cadeau added 15 points, seven assists and three steals, McKenney scored 12 points and Johnson Jr. had 11 points and seven rebounds.[^cite_note-117] Michigan led by as many as 11 points with six and a half minutes remaining, before Ohio State used a 7–0 run to finish the half, giving the Wolverines a 39–35 lead at halftime. The Buckeyes opened the second half with four consecutive field goals to tie the game, 44–44. Ohio State went on to take their first lead of the game on a three-pointer with 5\:40 remaining, capitalizing on a six-minute Michigan field goal drought. Johnson Jr. ended the drought moments later when a driving attempt was ruled a goaltending violation, giving Michigan the lead again. The Buckeyes pulled within two points after a basket with six seconds remaining, however, Lendeborg secured the win with two free throws. Michigan's defense forced Ohio State to miss eight of its final nine field-goal attempts over the closing minutes.[^cite_note-Quarterfinal-115]

#### Semifinal

On March 14, Michigan defeated No. 23 [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Wisconsin_Badgers_men's_basketball_team) in the semifinal, 68–65, officially defeating every team in the Big Ten this season.[^cite_note-Wis-118] The Wolverines advanced to the Big Ten Championship for a seventh time in program history, and for a second consecutive year (fifth time in the last nine tournaments).[^cite_note-Semifinal-119] They also set a program record with their ninth win against an AP ranked opponent.[^cite_note-Champ-120] Michigan was led by Mara with 16 points, eight rebounds and five blocks; the most blocks in program history in a Big Ten tournament game.[^cite_note-Semifinal-119] Three others scored in double figures, Cadeau scored 15 points, Lendeborg had 12 points and McKenney added 10 points. The Wolverines started the first half going down 26–18, before tying the game 28–28 at halftime. Michigan's 28 first-half points were its lowest first-half total this season. Michigan began the second half strong, taking a 54–39 lead with just under ten minutes remaining. Wisconsin answered with a 23–4 run, including six consecutive three-pointers made by Austin Rapp, to lead 62–58 with just under four minutes to go. Mara went on to make two unanswered field goals to tie the game, and Cadeau hit a three-point shot to take the lead at 45 seconds. [Nick Boyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Boyd) of Wisconsin answered with a three-point make of his own to tie the game, 65–65, with 30 seconds remaining. Lendeborg responded with a game-winning three-point shot with 0.3 seconds remaining.[^cite_note-Semifinal-119][^cite_note-Wis-118]

#### Championship

On March 15, Michigan was defeated by No. 18 [Purdue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Purdue_Boilermakers_men's_basketball_team) in the championship, 80–72, snapping a 17-game win streak against Big Ten opponents. Michigan was led by Lendeborg with 20 points and five rebounds. Three others scored in double figures, Mara had 14 points and seven rebounds, [Nimari Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimari_Burnett) added 12 points and Cadeau recorded his second double-double of the season with 10 points and 10 assists. Both teams were tied 38–38 at halftime, before Purdue used an 11–2 run to open the second half; never surrendering the lead.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-Champ-120]

### NCAA tournament

On March 15, Michigan received the 1-seed in the Midwest region of the [2026 NCAA tournament](76465965.md). It was the fourth time in program history earning a 1-seed.[^cite_note-122] Michigan entered with a 31–3 record, including 18 games against NCAA tournament teams, going 15–3.

#### First Round

On March 19, Michigan defeated 16-seed [Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Howard_Bison_men's_basketball_team), 101–80, in the first round at [KeyBank Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KeyBank_Center) in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York). Michigan was led by Johnson Jr. with 21 points and 10 rebounds, for his eighth double-double of the season, while shooting 8-of-8 from the field.[^cite_note-Howard-123] He became only the fifth player in NCAA tournament history to shoot 100% and record at least 20 points and 10 rebounds, and the first to accomplish this feat since 2009.[^cite_note-124] Four others scored in double figures: Mara had 19 points, seven rebounds, six assists and three blocks, Burnett scored 15 points, [Roddy Gayle Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roddy_Gayle_Jr.) added 14 points and McKenney had 10 points. After a tightly contested first half and ten made threes by Howard, Michigan led 50–46 at the intermission. The Wolverines pulled away in the second half, using a 12–1 run at the 15 minute mark and another 21–3 run starting at just under ten minutes, to lead by as many as 31 points with 3\:27 remaining. Michigan shot a program-record for an NCAA tournament game, 67.2% from the field (65.4% in the first half and 69.0% in the second). It was their ninth 100-point performance of the season, and the first in an NCAA tournament game since the [1991–92 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) scored 102 points against [East Tennessee State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_East_Tennessee_State_Buccaneers_men's_basketball_team) in the second round in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-Howard-123][^cite_note-125]

#### Second Round

On March 21, Michigan defeated 9-seed [Saint Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Saint_Louis_Billikens_men's_basketball_team) in the second round, 95–72. They advanced to the Sweet Sixteen for the ninth time in the last 13 NCAA tournaments, tied for the most in that span with [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Blue_Devils_men's_basketball) and [Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_Bulldogs_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-Saint-126] Michigan was led by Lendeborg with 25 points on 9-of-13 shooting. The four other starters each scored in double figures: Mara had 16 points and four blocks, Johnson Jr. added 15 points and eight rebounds, Cadeau had 12 points and eight assists, and Burnett scored 11 points.[^cite_note-Tarpley-127] Cadeau became the seventh Wolverine to surpass 200 assists in a season, and the first since [Zavier Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zavier_Simpson) in 2020.[^cite_note-Saint-126] After each team exchanged early runs and leads, Michigan entered halftime leading 48–39. Saint Louis stayed within striking distance for the first five minutes of the second half, before Michigan's lead was pushed back to double-digits at eleven minutes and over 20 points at eight minutes remaining. It was the first time in program history Michigan scored at least 95 points in back-to-back NCAA tournament games.[^cite_note-S16-128][^cite_note-Saint-126]

#### Sweet Sixteen

On March 27, Michigan defeated 4-seed No. 18 [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Alabama_Crimson_Tide_men's_basketball_team), 90–77. Michigan was led by Lendeborg with 23 points, 12 rebounds and seven assists. Three others scored in double figures: Cadeau had 17 points and seven assists, McKenney scored 17 points and Gayle Jr added 16 points.[^cite_note-Bama-129] Mara recorded two blocks, setting the single-season program record with 98, surpassing [Roy Tarpley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Tarpley)'s record of 97 blocks in 1986.[^cite_note-Tide-130] Alabama opened the game going up 11–2, before Michigan answered and took a 14–13 lead. Both teams traded leads in a tightly contested half, with Michigan using a 14–3 run to go up 47–41, before Alabama closed the half on an 8–0 run to lead 49–47 at halftime. The Crimson Tide made nine threes in the half, and the Wolverines made eight. Michigan came out of the intermission strong, using a 28–11 run to start the first ten plus minutes of the second half, going up 75–60. Michigan never surrendered the lead and advanced to their third Elite Eight in the last eight tournaments, and first since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-Bama-129] Michigan held Alabama to a season-low 30% [field goal percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_percentage).[^cite_note-4TFMNMNCGW-132] Both teams combined 27 three-pointers are tied for the third most in an NCAA Tournament regional game.[^cite_note-Tide-130] The victory marked Michigan's 34th of the season, setting the single-season program record for wins, surpassing the previous record of 33 set by the [2017–18 Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team).[^cite_note-Tide-130]

#### Elite Eight

On March 29, Michigan defeated 6-seed No. 23 [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Tennessee_Volunteers_basketball_team), 95–62, in the largest margin of victory by any team in a regional final since the [Wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_team) in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). As well as the second-largest margin of victory in program-history in any round, behind only that team. It was the worst loss by Tennessee in an NCAA tournament game and tied for the worst loss of [Rick Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Barnes)' career.[^cite_note-133] Michigan was led by Lendeborg with 27 points and seven rebounds. He became the first Michigan player to score at least 20 points in three consecutive NCAA tournament games since [Juwan Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juwan_Howard) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament), and was named the Midwest Regional MVP. Four others scored in double figures: Johnson Jr. and McKenney each had 12 points, Mara scored 11 points, and Burnett had 10 points. Cadeau added 10 assists, and became the first Big Ten player to record at least seven assists in four consecutive NCAA tournament games since [Trey Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Burke) in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). Cadeau joined Lendeborg and Gayle Jr. on the Midwest All-Region team.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] After an evenly scored start to the game, Michigan went on 34–10 run to close the first half, including a 21–0 run from eleven minutes until six minutes remaining, to lead 48–26 at halftime. The Wolverines continued expanding their lead to start the second half, going up 30 points with 14 minutes remaining as they cruised to their 35th win of the season and first Final Four since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). They held Tennessee to a season-low 19% field goal percentage.[^cite_note-4TFMNMNCGW-132] It was Michigan's fourth time defeating an AP ranked opponent by 30 points this season.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-Tennessee-137]

#### Final Four

On April 4, Michigan defeated 1-seed No. 2 [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Arizona_Wildcats_men's_basketball_team), 91–73, to advance to the [national championship game](79949085.md) for the first time since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-Arizona-138] Michigan was led by Mara with a career-high 26 points, nine rebounds, three assists, and two blocks. Four others scored in double figures: McKenney had 16 points on four three-pointers, Cadeau had 13 points, 10 assists, five rebounds and four steals, Lendeborg scored 11 points in 14 minutes, and Johnson Jr. added 10 points. Cadeau recorded the program's first point-assist double-double in the NCAA tournament since [Derrick Walton Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Walton_Jr.) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). The Wolverines opened the game on a 10–1 scoring run, extending their lead to double-digits five and a half minutes into the game, and never trailed. Within the first ten minutes, Michigan led 23–10, giving Arizona their largest deficit of the season. Lendeborg landed on [Motiejus Krivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motiejus_Krivas)' foot, spraining his ankle and [MCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medial_collateral_ligament) in the first half. Following his injury, Arizona used an 11–2 run to reduce Michigan's lead to seven points with 7\:21 remaining in the half. Michigan then used two 7–0 runs to close the half, leading 48–32 at halftime. Lendeborg returned in the second half and made two three-pointers, followed by two three-pointers by McKenney, giving Michigan a 59–39 lead with 14\:35 remaining. From Lendeborg's second three until the 8\:38 mark, Michigan made 11-of-13 shot attempts and held an 81–55 lead. Michigan made three field goals in the final seven and a half minutes of the game and Arizona made four field goals while attempting twice as many shots. The Wolverines shot 57.1 percent from the field, including 63.6 percent in the second half. They held Arizona to a season-low 35% field goal percentage for the game.[^cite_note-4TFMNMNCGW-132] Michigan's 18-point win is tied for the largest between No. 1 seeds in an NCAA men's tournament game since seeding began in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NCAA_Division_I_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-Arizona-138][^cite_note-139] Michigan became the first team in [NCAA tournament](507399.md) history to score at least 90 points in five consecutive games in a single tournament. They won by double-digits and made at least ten threes in each game.[^cite_note-140] In fact, no team had ever scored 90+ points five times in a single tournament, consecutive or not.[^cite_note-MtttNTbragMMroat-141]

#### National Championship

See also: [2026 NCAA Division I men's basketball championship game](79949085.md)

On April 6, Michigan defeated 2-seed No. 7 [UConn](79960295.md), 69–63, to win their second national championship and the first since [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-ESPNNC-142][^cite_note-UConn-143][^cite_note-144] Michigan was the first team to appear in a national championship game with a starting lineup composed entirely of transfers.[^cite_note-145] They were also the first team whose top-four leading scorers during the season were transfers in their first year with the program.[^cite_note-MwtNbDMubtaehtbampt-23] The UConn team included former Wolverine transfer, [Tarris Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarris_Reed).[^cite_note-146] Michigan was led by Cadeau with 19 points, who was awarded the [NCAA tournament's Most Outstanding Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_basketball_tournament_Most_Outstanding_Player). Two others scored in doubles figures: Lendeborg with 13 points and Johnson Jr. had a double-double with 12 points and 10 rebounds. Mara finished his [single-season program record for blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Wolverines_men's_basketball_statistical_leaders#Blocks) with 103 and set an additional single-season record with a 66.8% field goal percentage, passing [Loy Vaught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loy_Vaught)'s mark of 66.1% in 1989.[^cite_note-147] He ranked third in NCAA DI in blocks and fourth in field goal percentage.[^cite_note-148] Mara joined Cadeau and Johnson Jr. on the [Final Four All-Tournament team](76465965.md#Final_Four_all%E2%80%93tournament_team).[^cite_note-ESPNNC-142] Michigan improved upon their program-record, defeating a 13th AP ranked opponent this season and became the first Big Ten team to win the national championship since [Michigan State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Michigan_State_Spartans_men's_basketball_team) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament). Fourteen Big Ten teams reached the Final Four without winning in between championships.[^cite_note-149] Michigan finished the season with a program-record and NCAA-leading 37 wins, and tied the [2004–05 Illinois Fighting Illini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_Illinois_Fighting_Illini_men's_basketball_team) for the most wins in a single-season by any Big Ten team.[^cite_note-150] Michigan held UConn to a season-low 31% field goal percentage, which marked the fourth consecutive game that they held their opponent to a season low.[^cite_note-4TFMNMNCGW-132] Michigan's 541 total points is the third highest tournament total and the second-highest total by a national champion, behind only [UNLV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_UNLV_Runnin'_Rebels_basketball_team) in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-MtttNTbragMMroat-141][^cite_note-151] Michigan's 29-win differential from two seasons prior is the highest since the advent of the NCAA tournament.[^cite_note-MtttNTbragMMroat-141] The improvement made Michigan the first program to go from 10 or fewer wins to a national championship in two seasons since [Michigan State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978-79_Michigan_State_Spartans_men's_basketball_team) in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_tournament).[^cite_note-MwtNbDMubtaehtbampt-23] Michigan finished with a +713 single-season point differential, which is a Big Ten record.[^cite_note-MtttNTbragMMroat-141]

### Preseason

[Yaxel Lendeborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaxel_Lendeborg) was named Preseason All-Big Ten.[^cite_note-155] He was named a preseason first-team All-American by the [Associated Press](18935732.md), [Blue Ribbon Yearbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ribbon_College_Basketball_Yearbook), [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), and [SB Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SB_Nation); as well as a second-team selection by [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports), *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)*, *[The Sporting News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sporting_News)* and *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*.[^cite_note-goblue-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-MocbpAtfm2s-158] He was [Joe Lunardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Lunardi) and SB Nation's preseason National Player of the Year.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-MocbpAtfm2s-158]

### Regular season

Lendeborg was named the [Players Era Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Players_Era_Festival) MVP, and selected as [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) Player of the Week and the [Associated Press](18935732.md) National Player of the Week on December 1–2, 2025.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] [Morez Johnson Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morez_Johnson_Jr.) won the Big Ten Player of the Week on January 5, 2026.[^cite_note-162]

### Postseason

Following the season, [Dusty May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusty_May) was named [Big Ten Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference_Men's_Basketball_Coach_of_the_Year) by the media.[^cite_note-163] Lendeborg was named [Big Ten Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference_Men's_Basketball_Player_of_the_Year) by both the coaches and media, Michigan's first player of the year honoree since [Nik Stauskas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik_Stauskas) in 2014. Lendeborg was also first-team All-Big Ten by the coaches and media, the conference's only unanimous first-team selection by the latter, and joined Johnson Jr. and [Aday Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aday_Mara) on the Big Ten All-Defensive team. In addition, Mara was named the Big Ten Defensive Player of the Year, joining [Gary Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Grant_\(basketball\)) as the second Wolverine to ever earn the award (1986 and 1987), and a third-team All-Big Ten selection by both the coaches and media. Johnson Jr. was a second-team All-Big Ten selection by the media and third team by the coaches, [Elliot Cadeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Cadeau) received an All-Big Ten honorable mention by the media, and [Trey McKenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_McKenney) was selected for the Big Ten All-Freshman team by the coaches (the only selector).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

Nationally, May was selected as the coach of the year, given the [Henry Iba Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Iba_Award) by the [U.S. Basketball Writers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Basketball_Writers_Association) (USBWA). Michigan became the only school to have had three separate coaches win the award, including [Johnny Orr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Orr_\(basketball,_born_1927\)) in 1976 and [Juwan Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juwan_Howard) in 2021.[^cite_note-166] Lendeborg was selected as a [consensus first-team All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NCAA_Men's_Basketball_All-Americans#2026_Consensus_All-America_team) by the four major selectors: [Associated Press](18935732.md), [National Association of Basketball Coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Basketball_Coaches), *[The Sporting News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sporting_News)* and USBWA. He became the ninth consensus All-American in Michigan basketball history, 11th all-time selection, and the first since [Trey Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Burke) in 2013.[^cite_note-167]

### Postseason tournaments

Mara was selected to the [Big Ten All-Tournament team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Big_Ten_men's_basketball_tournament#All-Tournament_team).[^cite_note-168] Cadeau, Lendeborg and [Roddy Gayle Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roddy_Gayle_Jr.) were named to the [NCAA tournament All-Midwest Region team](76465965.md#Midwest_regional_all-tournament_team), with Lendeborg earning the Midwest Region Most Outstanding Player.[^cite_note-169] Following the [national championship game](79949085.md), Cadeau, Johnson Jr. and Mara were selected to the [NCAA tournament All-Final Four team](76465965.md#Final_Four_all%E2%80%93tournament_team) and Cadeau earned the [Final Four Most Outstanding Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_basketball_tournament_Most_Outstanding_Player).[^cite_note-170]
