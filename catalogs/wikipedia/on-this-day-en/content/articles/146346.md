# Medical evacuation



An [AW109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW109) helicopter evacuates a patient from the [Tatra Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatra_Mountains) in [Slovakia](26830.md)

**Medical evacuation**, often shortened to **medevac**[^cite_note-MW-1] or **medivac**,[^cite_note-MW-1] is the timely and efficient movement and en route care provided by medical personnel to patients requiring evacuation or transport using medically equipped [air ambulances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_ambulance), [helicopters](8286923.md) and other means of emergency transport including ground ambulance and maritime transfers.[^cite_note-2]

Examples include civilian [EMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services) vehicles, civilian aeromedical helicopter services, and military air ambulances. This term also covers the transfer of patients from the battlefield to a treatment facility or from one treatment facility to another by medical personnel, such as from a local hospital to another medical facility which has adequate medical equipment.[^cite_note-3]

## History



USAF [Sikorsky R-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_H-5) Helicopter evacuates casualties during the [Korean War](16772.md)



An aeromedical evacuation of injured patients by a [C-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17_Globemaster_III) from [Balad, Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balad,_Iraq) to [Ramstein Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramstein_Air_Base), Germany, in 2007

The first medical transport by air was recorded in [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia) in the autumn of 1915 during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).[^cite_note-4] One of the ill soldiers in that first medical transport was [Milan Rastislav Štefánik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Rastislav_%C5%A0tef%C3%A1nik), a Slovak pilot-volunteer who was flown to safety by French aviator [Louis Paulhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Paulhan).[^cite_note-5]

The [United States Army](32087.md) used this lifesaving technique in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma) toward the end of [World War II](32927.md) with [Sikorsky R-4B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_R-4B) helicopters. The first helicopter rescue was by 2nd Lt [Carter Harman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Harman), in [Japanese-held Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Burma), who had to make several hops to get his Sikorsky YR-4B to the [1st Air Commando Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Air_Commando_Group)'s secret airfield in enemy territory and then made four trips from there between April 25 and 26 to recover the American pilot and four injured British soldiers, one at a time.[^cite_note-6] The first medivac under fire happened in Manila in 1945 when five pilots evacuated 75–80 soldiers one or two at a time.[^cite_note-7]
