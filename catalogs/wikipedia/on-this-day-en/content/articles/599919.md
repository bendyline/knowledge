**Las Américas International Airport** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Aeropuerto Internacional Las Américas*, abbreviation; **AILA**) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **SDQ**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **MDSD**), officially **Las Américas–Dr. José Francisco Peña Gómez International Airport** (Spanish: *Aeropuerto Internacional de Las Américas Dr. José Francisco Peña Gómez*), is an international airport located in [Punta Caucedo](18964877.md), near [Santo Domingo](57638.md) and [Boca Chica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Chica) in the [Dominican Republic](8060.md). The airport is run by Aeropuertos Dominicanos Siglo XXI (AERODOM), a private corporation based in the Dominican Republic, under a 25-year concession to [build, operate, and transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Build%E2%80%93operate%E2%80%93transfer) (BOT) six of the country's airports. Las Américas usually receives a wide variety of long-, mid-, and short-haul aircraft. Santo Domingo's other airport, [La Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Isabela_International_Airport), is much smaller and used by smaller aircraft only.

The airport is the second-busiest in the country, after [Punta Cana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Cana_International_Airport), and one of the largest in the Caribbean, handling 5.490 million passengers in 2023. It is also the busiest cargo hub in the Caribbean and Central America, with 355,000,000 lbs of cargo transported in 2019. The airport has two terminals (A and B). A new terminal is expected to be inaugurated by the end of 2028.[^cite_note-3]

## History

Las Américas Airport opened in 1959 as the official airport of [Ciudad Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Trujillo), the capital of the Dominican Republic, with the city subsequently changing its name to [Santo Domingo](57638.md). The official name of the airport was changed in 2002 to "Aeropuerto Internacional Las Américas- José Francisco Peña Gómez (AIJFPG)" but is known as "Las Américas International Airport", or locally, "Las Américas Airport, AILA or "El Aeropuerto".

In April 1953, [Chicago and Southern Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_and_Southern_Air_Lines), a U.S. based air carrier, was operating a weekly [Lockheed Constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Constellation) service into Ciudad Trujillo with a southbound routing of [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee) - [New Orleans](53842.md) - [Port-au-Prince](57391.md) - Ciudad Trujillo - [San Juan, PR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_PR) and a northbound routing of San Juan - Ciudad Trujillo - Port-au-Prince - New Orleans - Memphis - [St. Louis](27687.md) - [Chicago](6886.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] By 1954, U.S. based [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) (which was known as "Delta C\&S" at the time following its acquisition and merger with Chicago and Southern Air Lines in May 1953) was serving the Dominican Republic on a daily basis via the then-named [General Andrews Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Andrews_Airport) in Ciudad Trujillo with nonstop flights to [San Juan, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico) and [Port au Prince, Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_au_Prince,_Haiti) operated with [Convair 340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_340) twin prop aircraft.[^cite_note-6] The eastbound Delta flights operated a daily routing of [Houston](13774.md) - [New Orleans](53842.md) - [Havana, Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana,_Cuba) - Port-au-Prince - Ciudad Trujillo - San Juan and the westbound Delta flights operated a daily routing of San Juan - Ciudad Trujillo - Port-au-Prince - Havana - New Orleans.[^cite_note-7] Also during the mid-1950s, U.S. based [Pan American World Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_World_Airways) ([Pan Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am)) was serving Ciudad Trujillo with nonstop flights to [New York City](645042.md), [Miami](53846.md) and Port-au-Prince operated with [Douglas DC-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-6) four engine propliners (which the airline called the "Super-6 Clipper").[^cite_note-8] In 1960, locally based [Dominicana de Aviacion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominicana_de_Aviacion), the former flag carrier of the Dominican Republic, was operating scheduled international passenger service nonstop between the airport and Miami with [Douglas DC-4](518813.md) four engine propliners and also nonstop between the airport and San Juan, Puerto Rico with [Douglas DC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) and [Curtiss C-46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtiss_C-46) twin prop aircraft.[^cite_note-9]

By the early and mid-1960s, several airlines were operating international jet service from the airport including [Pan Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am) with [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) service nonstop to [New York City](645042.md), [Miami](53846.md), San Juan, Puerto Rico, [Kingston, Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Jamaica) and [Curaçao](65205.md) as well as direct, no change of plane 707 service to [Montego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montego_Bay), [Caracas](55289.md), [Port of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Spain), [Georgetown, Guyana](56702.md) and [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo) while Brazil-based [VARIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VARIG) was operating [Sud Aviation Caravelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sud_Aviation_Caravelle) and [Convair 990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_990) jetliner flights nonstop to New York City and Miami as well as direct, no change of plane jet service to Port of Spain, [Belém](409573.md), [Fortaleza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza), [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife), [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), [São Paulo](390875.md) and [Porto Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Alegre).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

By 1976, the airport was being served by [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines) with nonstop [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) and [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) flights from New York City, Dominicana de Aviacion with nonstop [Boeing 727-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727-100), [Boeing 727-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727-200), [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707)-399C, Boeing 747 and McDonnell Douglas DC-10 (leased aircraft) service from New York City, Miami and San Juan, Puerto Rico, [Eastern Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Airlines) with nonstop Boeing 727-100 service from San Juan, Puerto Rico as well as direct, no change of plane 727 flights from [Cleveland](5951.md) and Miami, [Pan Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am) with nonstop Boeing 707 service from [Port au Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_au_Prince) as well as direct, one stop 707 service from Miami, [ALM Antillean Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALM_Antillean_Airlines) with nonstop and direct one stop [McDonnell Douglas DC-9-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-9-30) service from Curaçao, [Viasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasa) with nonstop [Douglas DC-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-8) service from Curaçao and direct one stop DC-8 flights from Caracas, and locally based [Aerovias Quisqueyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerovias_Quisqueyana) with nonstop Boeing 707 and Douglas DC-8 service from Miami and San Juan, Puerto Rico.[^cite_note-13] Also in 1976, Aerovias Quisqueyana was operating nonstop Boeing 707 transatlantic service from the airport to [Madrid](41188263.md) with this flight also providing direct one stop service to [Rome](25458.md).[^cite_note-14]

Las Américas was the hub for [Dominicana de Aviación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominicana_de_Aviaci%C3%B3n), [APA International Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APA_International_Air), [PAWA Dominicana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAWA_Dominicana), and a number of other, smaller airlines. Currently [Sky High Dominicana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_High_Aviation_Services) and [Arajet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arajet) are based there.

Las Américas also has served as a hub for airlines such as [Aeromar Líneas Aéreas Dominicanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeromar_L%C3%ADneas_A%C3%A9reas_Dominicanas), [Aero Continente Dominicana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_Continente_Dominicana) and [Queen Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Air).

Recently, the expressway leading from Santo Domingo to the airport (roughly 20 km east of the city center) was expanded and modernised. The airport was also modernised, and two more terminals were added, including 20 more gates. The new expressway crosses a new [suspension bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_bridge) which spans the [Ozama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozama_River), connecting traffic into the city's Elevated Freeway and Tunnel system onto the city's main street, Av. 27 de Febrero. A more scenic route following the coastal shore provides beautiful views of the Caribbean Sea and of the city. This secondary road crosses the Ozama River by means of a floating bridge, connecting traffic onto the Av. George Washington (el Malecón) which leads into the heart of the colonial city.

## Facilities



Terminal exterior

### Terminals

Las Américas has five gates on the main satellite concourse (A), A2 through A6. Other gate facilities are for the flights departing from a parking in the taxiway. back in the 1960s and 70s the airport used to be much smaller, The original building was half the size of today's newest structure but with a still modern look. Concourse B has four gates (B1 through B4) and remote stand (P8). Terminal B now features a co-branded [Copa Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Club) operated jointly by [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) and [Copa Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Airlines), and a Private Lounge exclusive to members and business people. The average number of daily flights in and out of Las Américas ranges between 68 and 84. [JetBlue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue) is the largest airline operating at Las Américas. The construction of a new terminal expansion started in June 2025 and expected to be inaugurated on the end of 2028 with a cost of US\$250 million. The new terminal is expected to increase passenger numbers annually.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Runway

Las Américas Airport's runway direction is north–south (designated 17–35). This runway is the largest in the country, and one of the largest in the Caribbean. With a length of 3,355 m, it is able to support a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747). The runway of SDQ was last renovated in June 2008. The old taxi-way was also renovated and converted into a full runway while the old runway was being renovated, then it was converted back into a taxiway after the normal runway was finished. The runway accommodated the [Antonov An-225](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-225) to supply goods after the [2010 Haiti earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]
