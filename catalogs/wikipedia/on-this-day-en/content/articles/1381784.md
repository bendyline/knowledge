**Elections in Palestine** are held sporadically. Elections for the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) (PNA) were held in Palestinian Autonomous areas from 1994 until their transition into the [State of Palestine](241405.md) in 2013. Elections were scheduled to be held in 2009,[^cite_note-1] but was postponed because of the [Fatah–Hamas conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_conflict). President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) agreed to stay on until the [next election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Palestinian_general_election),[^cite_note-YNN-2] but he was recognized as president only in the West Bank and not by [Hamas](13913.md) in Gaza. The Palestinian National Authority has held several elections in the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories), including elections for president, the legislature and [local councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_\(Palestinian_Authority\)). The PNA has a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system), with numerous [parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties). In this system, [Fatah](11104.md) is the dominant party.

The first [legislative and presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Palestinian_legislative_and_presidential_election) were held in 1996; the first [local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Palestinian_municipal_election) in January–May 2005. Previous (failed) Legislative Council elections were held in [1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Palestinian_Legislative_Council_election) under the [British Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine), and previous municipal elections were held in 1972 and 1976, organized by the [Israeli government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Military_Governorate).[^cite_note-3]

The [Palestinian Legislative Council](40067464.md) passed a law in June 2005 (signed by Abbas on 13 August 2005), to increase the number of members from 88 to 132, with half to be elected using [proportional representation](23298.md) and half by [plurality-at-large voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality-at-large_voting) in traditional constituencies.[^cite_note-pal_2005_elections_law-4] The [January 2005 presidential election](1211733.md) was won by Abbas of Fatah, while the [January 2006 legislative election](38945340.md) was won by [Hamas](13913.md). In 2007, a presidential decree abolished the constituency seats with all seats to be elected from a national list, and prohibited parties which did not acknowledge the Palestine Liberation Organization's right to represent the Palestinian people (specifically Hamas) from contesting the election.[^cite_note-5] An opinion poll suggested that a majority of Palestinians supported the change, while Hamas called it illegal.[^cite_note-6]

## Importance of the elections

See also: [2026 Palestinian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Palestinian_legislative_election) and [2027 Palestinian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Palestinian_presidential_election)

Elections in the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) are held to exercise the Palestinian right to [self-determination](29269.md) in connection with their right to establish their own state, but are held under military occupation.[^cite_note-7] They are held in the framework of the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords), meaning that the power of the PNA was (and is) limited to matters such as culture, education, ID-cards, and the distribution of land and water as per the Oslo Interim Agreement.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In October 2007, 2 ex-ministers and 45 PLC members were in Israeli detention.[^cite_note-CEIRPP_2007-10] In July 2012, there were 4,706 Palestinian prisoners in Israeli prisons. Of these, 22 were PLC members, of which 18 were in administrative detention.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-addameer-12][^cite_note-memo-13] The November 2013 figures of Addameer give about 5,000 prisoners imprisoned by Israel, of which 14 are members of the Palestinian Legislative Council (10 PLC members in [administrative detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_detention)).[^cite_note-14]

Hamas announced its intention to once again boycott local elections and has repeatedly prevented free, local elections since it first took power of Gaza in 2006.[^cite_note-15]

## In the Gaza Strip

Main article: [Gaza Strip under Hamas](38626632.md)

Following the [Fatah–Hamas conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_conflict) that started in 2006, Hamas formed a government ruling the Gaza Strip without elections. Gazan Prime Minister Haniyye announced in September 2012 the formation of a [second Hamas government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas_government_of_2012), also without elections.

## Local elections

### 1972 and 1976 local elections

Following the Israeli victory in the [Six Day War](29328.md), Israel gave limited autonomy to the West Bank, holding mayoral elections in 1972 and [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_West_Bank_local_elections).[^cite_note-Pro-plo_1976-19] The elections were held under an amended version of the 1955 Jordanian electoral law, which granted the right to vote to all Palestinians over the age of 21,[^cite_note-20] the law having previously restricted the franchise to male property owners,[^cite_note-21] although women weren't able to vote until the 1976 elections.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-22] The changes in the franchise were opposed by Jordan, citing the Fourth Geneva Convention, which stated that an occupying power should maintain the status quo in any occupied territories.[^cite_note-23] There were boycotts and threats of violence in the 1972 elections,[^cite_note-24] but by 1976 the PLO changed to actively participating in these elections.[^cite_note-Pro-plo_1976-19] Later elections were not held due to the elections of nationalist mayors in the 1976 elections[^cite_note-nytimes.com-22] out of concerns that further elections would "cause damage to the peace process."[^cite_note-25]

### 2005 local elections

Local elections in 2005 were held in four stages, but were never completed. The last stage was on 23 December 2005, with elections in 26 municipalities that had over 140,000 registered voters in Jericho and 25 villages in the West Bank. The elections were observed by the [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Local_and_Regional_Authorities) of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), with the head of mission, [Christopher Newbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Newbury), commenting "Inside the polling stations, the Congress observed a free and fair election. Outside them, further improvements remain to be made."[^cite_note-26]

Further local elections were planned, as over a quarter of the Palestinian population had had no chance to vote in them, including those in major towns such as Hebron, but they did not take place, due to conflict between Hamas and Fatah after the legislative elections of 2006.

### 2010 and 2012 local elections

Main article: [2012 Palestinian local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Palestinian_local_elections)

Four year term of local councils in Palestinian Authority expired in January 2009. Council of Ministers called for local elections to be held on 17 July 2010, but after Fatah proved incapable of agreeing on list of candidates, the call for elections was canceled on 10 June 2010. The election was postponed and was later held in 2012 after several delays.

## Central Elections Commission

Following the establishment of the Palestinian Authority in 1993, the "Elections Commission" was formed to conduct the [Palestinian presidential and legislative elections in 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Palestinian_general_election), the first elections in the Palestinian Authority. The [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS) was given the task of voter registration.

The Central Elections Commission (CEC) was established in October 2002 as an independent and neutral body under the [General Elections Law of 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Elections_Law_of_1995?action=edit\&redlink=1). The Elections Law, issued in August 2005, stipulated that the CEC is "the supreme body that undertakes the management, supervision, preparation and organization of elections and to take all necessary measures to ensure its integrity and freedom".[^cite_note-cec_about-28] [Hanna Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna_Nasir) has been the chairman of the CEC since 2002.

Under the [Local Council Elections Law No. (10) of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Council_Elections_Law_No._\(10\)_of_2005?action=edit\&redlink=1), the CEC became responsible for organizing local council elections, in addition to organizing elections of the President of the Palestinian National Authority and members of the Palestinian Legislative Council.[^cite_note-cec_about-28]

## External election assistance

The [Elections Reform Support Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_Reform_Support_Group) (ERSG) was formed with support from the [United States](3434750.md) and the [European Union](9317.md) to support Palestinian elections.[^cite_note-IFES_WB\&G-29] One of the leading organizations for the ESRG is the [International Foundation for Electoral Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Foundation_for_Electoral_Systems), which has actively assisted the Central Election Commission in 2004–2005 with the help of [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID).[^cite_note-IFES_WB\&G-29] They continue to support the election commission.[^cite_note-IFES_WB\&G-29]
