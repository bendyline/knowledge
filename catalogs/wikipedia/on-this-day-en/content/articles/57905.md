**Sakha**,[^cite_note-8] officially the **Republic of Sakha (Yakutia)**,[^cite_note-9] is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) of [Russia](25391.md), and the largest [federal subject of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_subject_of_Russia) by area. It is located in the [Russian Far East](173135.md), along the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean), with a population of one million.[^cite_note-2010Census2-10] Sakha comprises half of the area of its governing [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District), and is the world's [largest country subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_area), covering over 3,083,523 square kilometers (1,190,555 sq mi).[^cite_note-area7-11] [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk), which is the world's coldest major city,[^cite_note-12] is its capital and largest city.

The republic has a reputation for an extreme and severe climate, with the second lowest temperatures in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere) being recorded in [Verkhoyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk) and [Oymyakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oymyakon) (second only to [Summit Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_Camp), [Greenland](12118.md)), and regular winter averages commonly dipping below −35 °C (−31 °F) in Yakutsk. The [hypercontinental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) tendencies also result in warm summers for much of the republic.

Sakha was first home to [hunting-gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting-gathering) and [reindeer-herding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer_herding) [Tungusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_peoples) and [Paleosiberian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleosiberian_peoples) such as the [Evenks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenks) and [Yukaghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukaghir). Migrating from the area around [Lake Baikal](56865.md), the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) [Sakha people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakha_people) first migrated to the middle [Lena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River) sometime between the 9th and 16th centuries, likely in several waves, bringing the [pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) economic system of [Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Asia) with them.

The [Russians](59510.md) colonised and incorporated the area as the [Yakutsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk_Oblast) into the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) in the early-mid 17th century, obliging the indigenous peoples of the area to pay [fur tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasak). While the initial period following the Russian conquest saw the Sakha population drop by 70%, the [Imperial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) also saw the expansion of the native [Yakuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts) from the middle Lena along the [Vilyuy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilyuy_River) to the north and the east displacing other indigenous groups. Yakutia saw some of the last battles of the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), and the Bolshevik authorities reorganised Yakutsk Oblast into the autonomous [Yakut ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_ASSR) in 1922. The Soviet era saw the migration of many [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs), specifically [Russians](59510.md) and [Ukrainians](145144.md), into the area.

On 27 September 1990, the area became the Yakutskaya-Sakha Soviet Socialist Republic, and on 27 December 1991, it became the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia).

## Etymology

The [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym) *Yakut* comes from the [Evenk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenks) term *Yako* (also *yoqo*, *ñoqa*, or *ñoka*), which was the term the Evenks used to describe the Sakha. This was in turn picked up by the Russians.[^cite_note-13] The [Yukaghirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukaghirs), another neighbouring people in [Siberia](29639.md), use the exonym *yoqol* \~ *yoqod-* \~ *yoqon-* ([Tundra Yukaghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_Yukaghir_language)) or *yaqal \~ yaqad- \~ yaqan-* ([Kolyma Yukaghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Yukaghir_language)).

The [self-designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym) *Sakha* may be of the same origin (\**jaqa* > *Sakha* following regular sound changes in the course of development of the Yakut language) as the Evenk and Yukaghir exonyms for the Yakuts.[^cite_note-Johanson-14] It is pronounced as *Haka* by the [Dolgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgans), [whose language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgan_language) is a close relative of the [Yakut language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_language).[^cite_note-Krivonogov2013-15][^cite_note-RedBook-16]

- *Maximum E–W distance*: 2,000 km (1,200 mi)

Sakha stretches to the [Henrietta Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrietta_Island) in the far north and is washed by the [Laptev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptev_Sea) and [Eastern Siberian Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Siberian_Sea) of the Arctic Ocean. These waters, the coldest and iciest of all seas in the Northern Hemisphere, are covered by ice for 9–10 months of the year. [New Siberian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Siberian_Islands) are a part of the republic's territory. After [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut) was separated from Canada's [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories) in 1999, Sakha became the [largest subnational entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_country_subdivisions_by_area) ([statoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statoid)) in the world, with an area of 3,083,523 square kilometers (1,190,555 sq mi),[^cite_note-area7-11] slightly smaller than the territory of [India](14533.md) (3.3 million km<sup>2</sup>; 1.3 million sq mi), but still slightly larger than [Argentina](18951905.md).

Sakha can be divided into three great vegetation belts. About 40% of Sakha lies above the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle) and all of it is covered by [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost) which greatly influences the region's ecology and limits forests in the southern region. Arctic and subarctic [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) define the middle region, where [lichen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen) and [moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss) grow as great green carpets and are favourite pastures for [reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer). In the southern part of the tundra belt, scattered stands of dwarf [Siberian pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_pine) and [larch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larch) grow along the rivers. Below the tundra is the vast [taiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga) forest region. Larch trees dominate in the north and stands of [fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir) and [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_tree) begin to appear in the south. Taiga forests cover about 47% of Sakha and almost 90% of the cover is larch.

The Sakha Republic is the site of [Pleistocene Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_Park), a project directed at recreating Pleistocene tundra grasslands by stimulating the growth of grass with the introduction of animals which thrived in the region during the late [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) – early [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) period.

### Mountains



[Verkhoyansk Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk_Range)

Sakha's greatest mountain range, the [Verkhoyansk Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk_Range), runs parallel and east of the Lena River, forming a great arc that begins in the [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk) and ends in the Laptev Sea.

The [Chersky Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersky_Range) runs east of the Verkhoyansk Range and has the highest peak in Sakha, [Peak Pobeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_Pobeda,_Sakha) (3,003 meters or 9,852 feet). The second highest peak is [Peak Mus-Khaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus-Khaya_Mountain) reaching 2,959 meters (9,708 ft).

The [Stanovoy Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanovoy_Range) borders Sakha in the south.



[Ulakhan-Sis Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulakhan-Sis_Range) famous for its unusual [kigilyakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigilyakh) rock formations

### Natural resources



[Udachnaya pipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udachnaya_pipe) diamond mine

Sakha is well endowed with raw materials. The soil contains large reserves of [oil](23195.md), gas, [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [diamonds](8082.md), [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin), [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten) and many others. Sakha produces 99% of all Russian diamonds and over 25% of the diamonds mined in the world.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Climate

Sakha is known for its extremes in [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate), with the [Verkhoyansk Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk_Range) being the coldest area in the Northern Hemisphere. Some of the lowest natural temperatures ever recorded have been here. The [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere)'s [Pole of Cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_of_Cold) is at [Verkhoyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk), where the temperatures reached as low as −67.8 °C (−90.0 °F) in 1892 and 1885, and at [Oymyakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oymyakon), where the temperatures reached as low as −67.8 °C (−90.0 °F) in February 1934.

## History

Further information: [History of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Siberia)

### Prehistory

[Siberia](29639.md), and particularly Sakha, is of [paleontological](23084.md) significance, as it contains bodies of [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) animals from the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) [Epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoch_\(geology\)), preserved in ice or [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost). In 2015, the frozen bodies of [Dina and Uyan the cave lion cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthera_leo_spelaea#Specimens) were found. Bodies of [Yuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuka_\(mammoth\)) and another [woolly mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolly_mammoth) from [Oymyakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oymyakon), a [woolly rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolly_rhinoceros) from the [Kolyma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolyma_River), and [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison) and [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse) from [Yukagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukagir) have also been discovered.[^cite_note-Thesiberiantimes2015-25] In June 2019, the severed but preserved head of a [large wolf from the Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_the_wolf#Pleistocene_wolves), dated to over 40,000 years ago, was found close to the Tirekhtyakh River.[^cite_note-LiveScience_06-2019-26][^cite_note-SiberianTimes_06-2019-27][^cite_note-TheGuardian_06-2019-28]

[Ymyakhtakh culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ymyakhtakh_culture) (c. 2200–1300 BC) was a Late Neolithic culture of Siberia, with a very large archaeological horizon. Its origins were in Sakha, in the [Lena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River) basin. From there it spread both to the east and to the west.[^cite_note-29]

### Early history

The [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) [Sakha people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts) or *Yakuts* may have settled the area as early as the 9th century or as late as the 16th century, though most likely there were several migrations. They migrated northward from around [Lake Baikal](56865.md) to the [middle Lena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River) due to pressure by the Buryats, a Mongolic group.[^cite_note-30]

The Sakha displaced earlier, much smaller populations who lived on hunting and reindeer herding, introducing the [pastoralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) economy of Central Asia. The indigenous populations of [Paleosiberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleosiberian_languages) and [Tungusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_peoples) stock were mostly [assimilated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation) into the Sakha by the 17th century.[^cite_note-31]

### Russian conquest

Main article: [Russian conquest of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia)

The [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) began its conquest of the region in the 17th century, moving east after the defeat of the [Khanate of Sibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Sibir).[^cite_note-\:0-32][^cite_note-\:1-33][^cite_note-\:2-34] [Tygyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tygyn_Darkhan), a king of the [Khangalassky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khangalassky_District) Sakha, granted territory for Russian settlement in return for a military pact that included war against indigenous rebels of all Northeastern Asia ([Magadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan), [Chukotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukotka_Autonomous_Okrug), [Kamchatka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka) and [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin)).[^cite_note-\:0-32][^cite_note-\:1-33][^cite_note-\:2-34] Kull, a king of the [Megino-Khangalassky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megino-Kangalassky_District) Sakha, began a Sakha conspiracy by allowing the first stockade construction.[^cite_note-\:0-32][^cite_note-\:1-33][^cite_note-35]



1821 map of Yakutsk Oblast.

In August 1638, the Moscow Government formed a new administrative unit with the administrative center at Lensky Ostrog (Fort Lensky), the future city of [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk), which had been founded by [Pyotr Beketov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Beketov) in 1632.[^cite_note-\:0-32][^cite_note-\:1-33][^cite_note-\:2-34]

The arrival of Russian settlers at the remote [Russkoye Ustye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russkoye_Ustye) in the [Indigirka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigirka) delta is also believed to date from the 17th century.[^cite_note-36] The [Siberian Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Governorate) was established as part of the Russian Empire in 1708.

Russian settlers began to form a community in the 18th century, which adopted certain Sakha customs and was often called *Yakutyane* (Якутя́не) or Lena Early Settlers (ленские старожилы). However, the influx of later settlers assimilated themselves into the Russian mainstream by the 20th century.[^cite_note-\:0-32][^cite_note-\:1-33][^cite_note-\:2-34]

### Russian Empire

In an administrative reform of 1782, [Irkutsk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irkutsk_Governorate) was created. In 1805, [Yakutsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk_Oblast) was split from Irkutsk Governorate.[^cite_note-37]



British explorer and missionary [Kate Marsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Marsden) in [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk), 1891

Yakutsk Oblast in the early 19th century marked the easternmost territory of the Russian Empire, including such [Far Eastern](173135.md) (Pacific) territories as were acquired, known as Okhotsk Okrug within Yakutsk Oblast. With the formation of [Primorskaya Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorskaya_Oblast) in 1856, the Russian territories of the Pacific were detached from Sakha.



Members of the Siberian Regional Duma from Yakutsk, 1917

The Russians established agriculture in the [Lena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River) basin. The members of religious groups who were exiled to Sakha in the second half of the 19th century began to grow [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat), and [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato). The [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) established a cash economy. Industry and transport began to develop at the end of the 19th century and in the beginning of the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) period. This was also the beginning of [geological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological) prospecting, [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), and local [lead](17747.md) production. The first steam-powered ships and barges arrived.

Sakha's remoteness, compared to the rest of Siberia, made it a place of exile of choice for both Tsarist and Communist governments of Russia. Among the famous Tsarist-era exiles were the democratic writer [Nikolay Chernyshevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Chernyshevsky); [Doukhobors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doukhobor), [conscientious objectors](83003.md) whose story was told to [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) by [Vasily Pozdnyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Nikolaevich_Pozdnyakov); the [Socialist Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Revolutionary_Party) member and writer [Vladimir Zenzinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Zenzinov), who left an account of his Arctic experiences; and Polish socialist activist [Wacław Sieroszewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wac%C5%82aw_Sieroszewski), who pioneered in ethnographic research on the Sakha people.

A Sakha national movement first emerged during the [1905 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Revolution). A Yakut Union was formed under the leadership of a Sakha lawyer and city councilor by the name of Vasily Nikiforov, which criticized the policies and effects of Russian colonialism, and demanded representation in the [State Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Duma_\(Russian_Empire\)). The Yakut Union acted to make the city council of Yakutsk stand down and was joined by thousands of Sakha from the countryside, but the leaders were arrested and the movement fizzled out by April 1906. Their demand for a Sakha representative in the Duma, however, was granted.[^cite_note-38]

### Sakha during the Civil War

After the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), the anti-Bolshevik forces of Sakha created the Committee for the Protection of the Revolution, which supported the idea of convening a Constituent Assembly. On July 1, 1918, the Red Guard detachment of A. S. Rydzinsky [occupied Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Yakutsk?action=edit\&redlink=1). The executive committee of the Soviet of Workers' Deputies was created in the city, headed by M. K. Ammosov. Soviet authorities were also formed in [Vilyuysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilyuysk), in the [Nyurbinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyurbinsky_District) and [Suntarsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suntarsky_District) district, and in other uluses. As a result of the [defeat of the White Guard troops in Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siberian_Ice_March) in late 1919 - early 1920, Soviet power was restored in Sakha.

On April 20, 1920, by the decision of the [Sibrevkom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibrevkom), Yakutsk Oblast was included in the Irkutsk province as a special district. On August 21, 1920, by the decision of the same Sibrevkom, Sakha was given the status of a province.[^cite_note-39] In the summer of 1921, Georgy Lebedev was appointed secretary of the Yakut Bureau of the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks) by the Siberian Committee, and Alexei Kozlov was appointed chairman of the Revolutionary Tribunal. In Yakut historiography, the period of leadership of Georgy Lebedev, Alexei Kozlov and Anton Ageev is usually called the "triumvirate". The "triumvirate" began to pursue a policy of [red terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_terror) and ultra-communism in Sakha.

In September 1921, an anti-Soviet uprising broke out in Sakha. On October 6, the White Guard detachment of Valerian Bochkarev captured [Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhotsk). By 1922, the uprising had engulfed almost all of Sakha. The rebels turned to the Russian émigré circles in [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin) for help, from where a large White Guard detachment was sent to help them. In March 1922, the rebels created the [Provisional Yakut Regional People's Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Yakut_Regional_People's_Administration?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Churapcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churapcha). The rebels approached Yakutsk, a state of siege was introduced in the city. A large detachment of Nestor Kalandarishvili arrived to help the Yakut Bolsheviks, but Kalandarishvili himself, along with his group, died in an ambush near [Tekhtyur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekhtyur?action=edit\&redlink=1). During the Battle of Everstovaya Zaimka near Tulagino and the Battle of Kildyam, the [siege of Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Yakutsk?action=edit\&redlink=1) was lifted.[^cite_note-40]

On March 10, a party meeting headed by Platon Oyunsky accused Lebedev, Kozlov and Ageev of left-wing deviation and serious mistakes that led Sakha to an uprising. It was revealed that Georgy Lebedev had previously been the editor of the ultra-right newspaper "Free Siberia", then as part of [Ignatov's group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Opposition) at the [10th Congress of the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Congress_of_the_Russian_Communist_Party_\(Bolsheviks\)) he opposed the [NEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Economic_Policy). Alexei Kozlov was a [right-wing SR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Revolutionary_Party) and joined the Communist Party only in 1921. Lebedev, Kozlov, and Ageev were removed from the leadership and on the night of March 10-11 they were arrested by the Kalandarishvilists.

On June 21, during the battle near the village of Nikoltsy, the rebels suffered a crushing defeat and, after the [capture of Churapcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Churapcha?action=edit\&redlink=1), began to retreat towards Okhotsk. By October 1, the uprising was generally suppressed, but in the fall, a detachment of Anatoly Pepelyaev arrived to help the Yakut rebels. Pepelyaev hoped to take Yakutsk and begin the seizure of Siberia, thereby rekindling the civil war. The dramatic [siege of Sasyl-Sysy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sasyl-Sysy?action=edit\&redlink=1) was the last major battle of the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War). After the [capture of Amga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Amga?action=edit\&redlink=1) by the Reds, the [battle of Bilistyakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bilistyakh?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the lifting of the siege of Sasyl-Sysy, Pepelyaev's detachment began to retreat towards Okhotsk, where Pepelyaev was arrested by the Reds.&#x20;

The last White Guards in the north of Sakha surrendered by the end of 1923.

#### Subsequent uprisings

In 1924, an [uprising began](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunguska_uprising?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Sakha, which was caused by the actions of the Bolsheviks: the closure of ports for foreign trade, trade restrictions, interruptions in the import of goods from the mainland, the confiscation of reindeer from private owners, the seizure of vast pastures for industrial new buildings. In 1925, the rebels concluded an armistice with the Soviet authorities and laid down their arms.

However, in 1927, a [new uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_uprisings?action=edit\&redlink=1) began under the leadership of the Yakut lawyer Pavel Ksenofontov (a graduate of the Faculty of Law of Moscow University, an employee of the Ministry of Finance of the RSFSR). After its suppression in 1928, 128 people were shot, 130 received various prison terms, some of them were not related to the uprising. Among the repressed were prominent representatives of the intelligentsia who knew nothing about the uprising or even condemned it.

In mid-1929, after the uprising of the confederalists of 1927-1928, on the tip of the center, a wholesale purge of non-party people disloyal to the new government and members of the local Communist Party itself, accused of counterrevolution, began. This became the reason for a new uprising against Soviet power in the north of Sakha, later called the [Bulun uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_uprisings?action=edit\&redlink=1). It was subsequently suppressed in 1930.

### Soviet era

Main article: [Yakut Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)

Sakha was home to the last stage of the Russian Civil War, the [Yakut Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_Revolt). On April 27, 1922, former Yakutsk Oblast was proclaimed the [Yakut ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), although in fact the eastern part of the territory, including the city of Yakutsk, was controlled by the [White Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement).



Platon Oyunsky, who wrote the traditionally-oral [olonkho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonkho) epics

The early Soviet period saw a flourishing of Sakha literature as men such as [Platon Oyunsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platon_Oyunsky) wrote down in writing the traditionally oral and improvised [olonkho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonkho), in addition to composing their own works. Many early Sakha leaders, including Oyunsky, died in the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge).

Map of the Autonomous Yakut SSR, 1928

Autonomous Yakut SSR, 1928

Sakha experienced significant collectivization [between 1929 and 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_five-year_plan_\(Soviet_Union\)), with the number of households experiencing collectivization rising from 3.6% in 1929 to 41.7% in 1932. Policies by which the Sakha were harshly affected resulted in the population dropping from 240,500 in 1926 down to 236,700 at the 1959 census.[^cite_note-41]

Sakha's demographics shifted wildly during the Soviet period as ethnic Russians and Ukrainians, among other groups, settled the area en masse, primarily in Yakutsk and the industrial south. Previously, even Yakutsk had been primarily Sakha and Sakha-speaking. With the end of [korenizatsiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korenizatsiya), usage of the Sakha language was restricted in urban areas such as Yakutsk, which became primarily Russian-speaking.

### Post-Soviet era

Following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in December 1991, the [Yakut Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakut_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) was officially reconstituted as the [Republic of Sakha (Yakutia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk_constituency), a federal subject within the newly formed [Russian Federation](25391.md).[^cite_note-\:3-42] In April 1992, Moscow formally recognized this status, granting Sakha significant autonomy; most notably, a 1992 agreement allowing the republic to retain 20% of its diamond industry profits, a landmark concession deviating from decades of centralized resource extraction.[^cite_note-\:4-43]

During the early 1990s, Sakha saw a rise in ethnic and nationalist activism. Political movements such as *[Sakha Omuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakha_Omuk_\(1990\))* (founded 1990) and the more radical *Sakha Keskile* promoted [Yakut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts) sovereignty, resource self‑management, and cultural revival. These movements led to the republic's 1990 declaration of sovereignty (celebrated each year on September 27) and a gradual shift away from the ethnic suppression of the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-\:3-42][^cite_note-\:4-43][^cite_note-\:5-44]

Economically, Sakha faced the tumultuous transition to a market economy amid systemic [Soviet collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union). The regional government actively supported [privatization](24661.md) of state enterprises, offered tax incentives, subsidies, and direct investment to buffer the population from economic shocks.[^cite_note-\:5-44][^cite_note-\:6-45][^cite_note-46] The republic also passed legislation in the 1990s to protect [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) land use rights and foster the creation of [clan-based communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan), reinforcing traditional livelihoods.[^cite_note-\:4-43]

In 2000, Sakha was incorporated into the newly created [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District), one of eight federal districts established by [President Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Vladimir_Putin) to centralize administrative oversight.[^cite_note-\:4-43][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] While this shift integrated Sakha into [Far East](11672.md) economic development initiatives—including [tax incentives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_incentive), [special economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone), and infrastructure investments—these programs have often favoured industrial and extractive interests, occasionally sidelining Indigenous land rights.[^cite_note-\:4-43]

Under Putin, [federal centralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralisation) increased. Regional autonomy has been curtailed through legal reforms—such as a 2009 removal of sovereignty references from the republic's constitution and renaming the republic's presidential post in 2014—and through restrictions on local veto powers regarding resource projects.[^cite_note-\:4-43] At the same time, Sakha's economy, driven by mining (diamonds, gold, uranium, oil, and natural gas), has shown resilience. Wages in the region now outpace national averages when adjusted for cost of living.[^cite_note-\:6-45] [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk) remains the hub of administrative and economic leadership, buoyed by tourism and essential infrastructure projects, though remote areas still lag behind.[^cite_note-\:5-44][^cite_note-\:6-45][^cite_note-49]

### Languages

The official languages are both Russian and [Sakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakha_language), also known as Yakut, which is spoken by roughly half of the republic's population. In the 2021 census, 95% of Yakuts, 72% of Evenks and 60% of Evens declared Sakha as their native language.[^cite_note-languages2021-58] The Sakha language is a member of the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) language family, belonging to the Siberian branch. It is closely related to the [Dolgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgan_language) language of the former [Taymyr Dolgano-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taymyr_Autonomous_Okrug).

The Sakha Republic is also home to many of the world's speakers of [Tungusic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_languages), primarily of [Evenki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenki_language) and [Even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_language). Additionally, [Chukchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_language) and the dialects of the [Yukaghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukaghir_languages) language family are spoken in the northeast.

Before the arrival of the Russian Empire, the majority of the local population was [Tengrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengrist), similar to the other Turkic people of Central Asia, or in [Paleoasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoasian) indigenous [shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism) with both 'light' (community leading) and 'dark' (healing through spirit journey) shamans. Under the Russians, the local population was converted to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) and required to take Orthodox Christian names, but in practice generally continued to follow traditional religions. During the Soviet era, most or all of the shamans died without successors. In the 1990s, a neopagan shamanist movement called *aiyy yeurekhé* was founded by the controversial journalist Ivan Ukhkhan and a philologist calling himself Téris.[^cite_note-61] This group and others cooperated to build a shaman temple in downtown Yakutsk in 2002.[^cite_note-62]



Transfiguration of Jesus Christ Cathedral in Yakutsk

Currently, while Orthodox Christianity maintains a following (however, with very few priests willing to be stationed outside of Yakutsk), there is interest and activity toward renewing the traditional religions. As of 2008, Orthodox leaders described the worldview of the republic's indigenous population (or, rather, those among the population who are not completely indifferent to religion) as *dvoyeverie* (dual belief system), or a "tendency toward [syncretism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism)", as evidenced by the locals sometimes first inviting a shaman, and then an Orthodox priest to carry out their rites in connection with some event in their life.[^cite_note-63]

According to the Information Centre under the President of Sakha Republic (Информационный центр при Президенте РС(Я)), the religious demography of the republic was as follows:[^cite_note-64] Orthodoxy: 44.9%, Shamanism: 26.2%, Non-religious: 23.0%, New religious movements: 2.4%, Islam: 1.2%, Buddhism: 1.0%, Protestantism: 0.9%, Catholicism: 0.4%.

According to a 2012 survey,[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-59] 37.8% of the population of Sakha adheres to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), 13% to [Tengrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengrism) or Sakha [shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_shamanism), 2% to [Islam](6037917.md), 1% are unaffiliated [Christians](18337522.md), 1% to forms of [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), and 0.4% to [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism). In addition, 26% of the population deems itself [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), 17% is "spiritual but not religious", and 1.8% follows other religions or did not give an answer to the question.[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-59]

## Politics



[Russia Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_Day) celebrations in [Mirny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirny,_Sakha_Republic), 12 June 2014.

The head of government in Sakha is the Head (previously President). The first [Head of the Sakha Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Sakha_Republic) was [Mikhail Yefimovich Nikolayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Nikolayev).[^cite_note-65] As of 2021, the head is [Aysen Nikolayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aysen_Nikolayev), who took office on 28 May 2018.

The supreme legislative body of state authority in Sakha is a unicameral State Assembly known as the *Il Tumen*. The government of the Sakha (Yakutia) Republic is the executive body of state authority.

The republic fosters close cultural, political, economic, and industrial relations with the independent Turkic states through membership in organisations such as the [Turkic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_Council) and the [Joint Administration of Turkic Arts and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Administration_of_Turkic_Arts_and_Culture).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

## Economy

The largest companies in the region include [Alrosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alrosa), [Yakutugol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutugol), [Yakutskenergo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutskenergo?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Yakutia Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutia_Airlines).[^cite_note-regioncompanies-69]

### Mining

The [Mirny diamond mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_mine) and other diamond mines are important sources of exports for the region.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] In addition, tin and gold mining have been major but controversial industries for over 100 years.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

### Transportation

Water transport ranks first for cargo turnover. There are six river ports, two seaports ([Tiksi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiksi) and Zelyony Mys). Four shipping companies, including the Arctic Sea Shipping Company, operate in the republic. The republic's main waterway is the [Lena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River), which links [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk) with the rail station of [Ust-Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ust-Kut) in [Irkutsk Oblast](79477.md).



[Yakutsk Aeroport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk_Airport), the main air-traffic hub of the republic.

Air transport is the most important for transporting people. Airlines connect the republic with most regions of Russia. [Yakutsk Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk_Airport) has an international terminal.

Two federal roads pass the republic. They are Nizhny Bestyakh–[Skovorodino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skovorodino,_Amur_Oblast) ([A360 Lena highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A360_Lena_highway_\(Russia\))) and Nizhny Bestyakh–[Magadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan) ([M56 Kolyma Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M56_Kolyma_Highway)). However, due to the presence of permafrost, use of asphalt was formerly impractical, and therefore the roads were made of clay until being paved by 2014. Prior to the paving of these roads, when heavy rains blew over the region, the roads often turned to mud, sometimes stranding hundreds of travellers in the process.[^cite_note-77] The [Lena Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_Bridge) is under construction across the Lena, which will connect Yakutsk to Nizhny Bestyakh, and thus the rest of the Russian road network, year-round. Construction began in 2024, and is planned to last until 2028.[^cite_note-78]

The [Berkakit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkakit)–[Tommot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommot) railroad is currently in operation. It links the [Baikal-Amur Mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal-Amur_Mainline) with the industrial centres in South Sakha. Construction of the [Amur–Yakutsk Mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur%E2%80%93Yakutsk_Mainline) continues northward; the railway was completed to [Nizhny Bestyakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Bestyakh), across the river from Yakutsk, in 2013. Though this one-track railroad from Tommot to Nizhny Bestyakh was under temporary operation (30% of its full capacity), the federal agency for railways declared that this railroad would be in full operation in fall 2015. Since 2019, there have been passenger trains between Nizhny Bestyakh and the rest of Russia.[^cite_note-79]

## Media

NVK Sakha (national broadcaster company Sakha, Национальная вещательная компания Саха, "Саха" көрдөрөр иһитиннэрэр тэрилтэтэ), the largest media company in the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia). The company owns dozens of TV channels in Yakutia, Russia, and other countries. The main broadcasting languages are Yakut, English, Russian and Evenk. It was founded in 1992 after the collapse of the USSR. 70% of the shares are owned by the Russian VGTRK, 25% are owned by Yakutia, and 5% are in free float. NVK Sakha owns its own animation and film production studios, and some music studios. Since 2018, it has also been streaming 24/7 on YouTube.

## Culture



Sakha dance with traditional clothing

Points of interest in the city of Yakutsk include:

- the State Russian drama theatre named after [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin)

- the Sakha Theater named after [Platon Oyunsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platon_Oyunsky)

- the State Academic Opera and Ballet Theatre named after D. K. Sivtsev

- Suorun Omoloon, the Young Spectator's Theatre

There are a number of museums as well. These include the National Fine Arts Museum of Sakha, the Museum of Local Lore and History named after E. Yaroslavsky, and the Khomus Museum and Museum of Permafrost. In September 2020, the Gagarin Centre for Culture and Contemporary Art was opened in the Gagarin District of Yakutsk.[^cite_note-80]

The Yakuts have fully preserved their native language, which differs significantly from other Turkic languages by the presence of a layer of unique Paleo-Asiatic vocabulary. The Yakut language has a developed literary tradition with many styles and genres, and the ancient Sakha epic [Olonkho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonkho) is recognised by UNESCO as a masterpiece of the oral and intangible heritage of humanity.[^cite_note-81]

In the 2010s, a movie boom began in Yakutia. The local film industry was nicknamed "*Sakhawood*".[^cite_note-82]
