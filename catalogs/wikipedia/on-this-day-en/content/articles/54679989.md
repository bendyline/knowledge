***League of Legends* esports** is the [professional competition](564204.md) of the [multiplayer online battle arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer_online_battle_arena) video game *[League of Legends](21838352.md)*. The game, released in 2009, was the first to be developed and published by [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games). Esports tournaments for the game began as early as 2010, most notably with the [2010 World Cyber Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_World_Cyber_Games) when it was featured as a promotion game. In 2011, the [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends:_Season_1_World_Championship) of the [*League of Legends* World Championship](42058214.md) was held at [DreamHack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamHack) in [Jönköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6nk%C3%B6ping), [Sweden](5058739.md), which was won by [Fnatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fnatic).

*League of Legends* is one of the largest [esports](564204.md) with various annual tournaments taking place worldwide.[^cite_note-1] In terms of esports professional gaming as of June 2016, *League of Legends* has had US\$29,203,916 in prize money, 4,083 Players, and 1,718 tournaments, compared to *Dota 2's* US\$64,397,286 of prize money, 1,495 players, and 613 tournaments.[^cite_note-2]

Former [League Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_Championship_Series_\(esports\)) commissioner Chris Greeley is the current Global Head of *League of Legends* Esports (LoL Esports) for [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games) since 2024.

## History

### 2011–2012: Early years



A match at Final Intel Extreme Masters World Best Gamers League Of Legends in Brazil in 2013

*League of Legends* has 16 years of competitive history, with competitions beginning in 2010. On 30 June of that year, [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games), the game's developer, and World Cyber Games Inc., announced that it would be featured at the [2010 World Cyber Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_World_Cyber_Games) in [Los Angeles](18110.md), United States as one of the promotional game titles, with representatives from China, Europe, North America, and Southeast Asia forming teams of five players.[^cite_note-3] American team [Counter Logic Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_Logic_Gaming) won the tournament, defeating European representatives [SK Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Gaming). Prior to the World Cyber Games, *League of Legends* has built a strong player base, but the introduction of drafts and ranked mode brought competitive experience to the game. As Riot Games was seeing growing viewership, it decided to enter the competitive scene with the first [World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends:_Season_1_World_Championship) in 2011, which would be hosted by [DreamHack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamHack) in [Jönköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6nk%C3%B6ping), [Sweden](5058739.md). The inaugural tournament saw players bringing their own computers and competing in front of fans, while networking with other fellow players [^cite_note-4] Eight teams competed - three each from North America and Europe, and one team each from [Singapore](27318.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-5] European representative [Fnatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fnatic) would win the inaugural World Championship, which saw over 1.6 million accumulated viewers, with a peak viewership of 210,000 concurrent viewers.[^cite_note-6]

In 2012, the first two regional leagues would be established - the [League Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_Championship_Series_\(esports\)) of both North America (NA LCS) and Europe (EU LCS). Both leagues would have their inaugural seasons the following year in 2013.[^cite_note-7] The EU LCS would later be renamed as the [*League of Legends* European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_European_Championship) (LEC) in 2019,[^cite_note-8] later as EMEA Championship in 2023 to incorporate teams from [Turkey](11125639.md), the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) and the [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA) region. The Riot Brazilian Champion League (Brazil) and The Champions (South Korea) would also be established, with both tournaments later becoming professional leagues, renaming as the [Campeonato Brasileiro de *League of Legends*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_de_League_of_Legends) (CBLOL) and the [*League of Legends* Champions Korea](48319339.md) (LCK), respectively. The same year also saw Taiwanese team [Taipei Assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Assassins) winning the [second edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends:_Season_2_World_Championship) of the World Championship - the only team so far to win the title outside teams from China, Europe, and South Korea. Viewership also improved from the first edition at 1.1 million concurrent viewers during the final.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] A special [LAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_area_network)-based client was also developed as a response to delayed matches caused by network issues during the Season 2 World Playoffs, designed for use in tournament environments where the effects of [lag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lag_\(video_games\)) and other network issues can be detrimental to the proper organization of an event. The LAN client was deployed for the first time during the first quarter-final and semi-final matches played following the rescheduled matches, and was in use during the finals.[^cite_note-s2-lanclient-11]

### 2013–2017: Regional leagues, first Korean dominance, and franchising



Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok is widely regarded as one of the greatest esports players of all time.

2013 saw the establishment of the [*League of Legends* Pro League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Pro_League) (LPL) in China, which began with eight teams,[^cite_note-12] and the first Asian team to win the World Championship, with South Korea's [SK Telecom T1](24141237.md), which featured then-rookie [Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faker_\(gamer\)), defeating China's [Royal Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Never_Give_Up) in the [2013 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends:_Season_3_World_Championship), with the final taking place at the [Staples Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staples_Center) in [Los Angeles](18110.md). This would also start a five-year streak of Korean World Championship winners.

On 11 July 2013, Riot Games announced that the [United States Citizenship and Immigration Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Citizenship_and_Immigration_Services) recognized *League of Legends* pro-players as [professional athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_athlete) and that the [P visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P_visa) application process would be more simplified for them.[^cite_note-League_of_Legends-13] These changes allowed professional players to stay in the United States for up to five years.[^cite_note-14] Despite these reforms, there have still been a number of visa problems that have occurred for players in the LCS and other *LoL* tournaments entering the United States.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The [2014 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) was the first edition to be hosted for the first time in multiple countries ([Taiwan](25734.md), [Singapore](27318.md), and [South Korea](27019.md)). This would become a model for future World Championship hostings, specifically in Europe and North America. In the same year, Riot Games introduced the concept of "Championship points", which teams would earn based on performance across both splits and playoffs in order to qualify for the tournament.[^cite_note-17] Between 2014 and 2017, SK Telecom T1 won two world titles, and both Samsung Galaxy White and Samsung Galaxy winning one.[^cite_note-crowned-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] 2015 also saw the creation of a new international tournament - the [Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Season_Invitational). The [first tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Mid-Season_Invitational) was held in 2015 in [Tallahassee, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallahassee,_Florida) in the [United States](3434750.md), and was won by Chinese team [Edward Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Gaming), becoming the first Chinese team to win an international *League of Legends* title.[^cite_note-21] The [2017 *League of Legends* World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) was the first edition to be held in [China](5405.md) and the first to feature a play-in stage.

Franchising in *League of Legends* esports also began in 2017, when the LPL announced in April of that year that it would become a franchised league, removing the promotion and relegation system.[^cite_note-22] Months later, Riot Games announced that the NA LCS would follow suit with ten permanent teams beginning in 2018. Each of the ten teams would have guaranteed spots, which would encourage owners to make substantial investments, including the creation of training facilities and improvements in coaching, scouting, and player well-being. Each team was also mandated to maintain an academy squad for emerging talent development. Riot Games also raised the minimum salary for players to US\$75,000, with an average annual income for League pros around \$150,000. Additionally, a players' association was established to offer financial, legal, and career-building support to players.[^cite_note-23] This franchising model would follow suit in the LEC (2019), CBLOL (2020), and LCK (2021).

### 2018–2021: Growing popularity, Chinese dominance, and COVID-19 impact



The players of G2 Esports lifting the 2019 Mid-Season Invitational trophy. Pictured left to right: [Caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caps_\(gamer\)), [Perkz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perkz), Mikyx, Promisq, [Wunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wunder_\(gamer\)), Jankos

In [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_League_of_Legends_World_Championship), the LPL won its first World Championship, with Chinese team [Invictus Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invictus_Gaming) winning against European representatives [Fnatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fnatic) in a 3-0 sweep.[^cite_note-24] The 2018 edition of the tournament also had a prize pool of \$6,450,000, which remains the highest for any international *League of Legends* competition to date.[^cite_note-25] By December 2018, each major region had its unique name, logo, and brand identities following the re-branding of the NA LCS to simply the LCS alongside an updated logo.[^cite_note-26] 2018 also saw the creation of the [Liga Latinoamérica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Latinoam%C3%A9rica) (LLA), which was a merger between the *Liga Latinoamérica Norte* (LLN, North Latin America League) and *Copa Latinoamérica Sur* (CLS, South Latin America Cup).[^cite_note-27] The league would cease operations six years later in 2024.

In 2019, it was announced that alongside its shift to a franchised league, the LEC's secondary league, the EU Challenger Series (EUCS), was discontinued and replaced with an independent tournament named European Masters (later [EMEA Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMEA_Masters)), which features the top teams from Europe's many regional leagues.[^cite_note-28] Riot Games and Tencent also created a joint venture called "TJ Sports" to oversee all *League of Legends* esports operations in China, including tournament organizing, talent management, and venues.[^cite_note-29] The same year would also see European team [G2 Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G2_Esports) have major international success - winning the [2019 Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Mid-Season_Invitational), becoming the first European team to win the tournament,[^cite_note-30] and reaching the final of the [2019 *League of Legends* World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_League_of_Legends_World_Championship), hosted in [Germany](11867.md), [Spain](26667.md), and [France](5843419.md). The team notably beat SK Telecom T1 in the semifinals, and were on the verge of achieving a "golden road" after winning MSI and both regional splits, but they would lose to the LPL's [FunPlus Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FunPlus_Phoenix), who won the region's second consecutive world title.[^cite_note-31]

The global [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) had a significant impact in *League of Legends* esports, as the 2020 Mid-Season Invitational was cancelled and the [2020 *League of Legends* World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) in China was held in an "isolation bubble" environment in [Shanghai](27643.md),[^cite_note-32] as opposed to a previously planned multi-city hosting of the tournament. MSI was replaced by the [2020 Mid-Season Streamathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Mid-Season_Streamathon), a [live stream event](18996908.md) for a cause, with proceeds going to COVID-19 relief efforts.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Regionally, leagues had to temporarily suspend operations, eventually shift to online play, and to hold games behind closed doors as opposed to having roadshows. For instance, the LCS Spring Finals weekend of the said year were planned to take place at the [Ford Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Center_at_The_Star) in Frisco, Texas,[^cite_note-35] but the event was moved to the LCS's Los Angeles studio.[^cite_note-36] The final of the 2020 World Championship, however, was held with limited fans in attendance at the [Pudong Football Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pudong_Football_Stadium) in [Shanghai](27643.md),[^cite_note-37] with the LCK's [Damwon Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damwon_Gaming) defeating the LPL's [Suning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suning_\(esports\)) to win its first world title. Regional and international play behind closed doors continued in 2021, with all games of the [Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Mid-Season_Invitational) and the [World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) being held at the [Laugardalshöll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laugardalsh%C3%B6ll) in [Reykjavík](25798.md), [Iceland](14531.md),[^cite_note-38] the latter of which was originally scheduled to take place in China, with [Shanghai](27643.md), [Qingdao](250171.md), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), [Chengdu](170375.md), and [Shenzhen](83368.md) serving as host cities.[^cite_note-39] Regional leagues also had to continue play with no spectators in attendance.

### 2022–2024: Return to touring, Korean resurgence, and influencer teams



[Kim "Deft" Hyuk-kyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deft_\(gamer\)) won the 2022 World Championship with DRX, regarded as one of the greatest upsets in *League of Legends* history.

Spectators returned to live events beginning in 2022, with regional leagues offering limited capacities each game day. The [2022 Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mid-Season_Invitational) was held in [Busan](68115.md), [South Korea](27019.md), which was won by the LPL's [Royal Never Give Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Never_Give_Up) despite competing remotely in the team's training facility due to the [COVID-19 pandemic in mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_mainland_China).[^cite_note-40] [Mexico](3966054.md) would host its first [*League of Legends* World Championship in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) alongside the [United States](3434750.md). [Canada](5042916.md) was originally slated to host the semi-final stage of the tournament at the [Scotiabank Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotiabank_Arena), [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-41] However, due to the impact of the [COVID-19 pandemic in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada) on securing multi-entry visas, Riot Games announced the [State Farm Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Farm_Arena) in [Atlanta](3138.md), United States as its replacement venue.[^cite_note-42] The World Championship was won by [DRX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DRX_\(esports\)), defeating [T1](24141237.md), who went 18-0 during the LCK's Spring split of that year, with a 3-2 scoreline and is considered of a [Cinderella run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_\(sports\)) by commentators, with many regarding it as one of the greatest finals in the history of League of Legends.[^cite_note-43] DRX also became the first team to win the world title as a fourth seed and after starting the tournament in the play-in stage. The following year's [Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mid-Season_Invitational) was the first international tournament to be held in Europe after the COVID-19 pandemic, and was won by [JD Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Gaming) after defeating [Bilibili Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili_Gaming) in an all-LPL final.[^cite_note-44] T1 would bounce back from their 2022 defeat, winning the [2023 *League of Legends* World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) on home soil, and would be the start of another streak of international victories for the LCK.

Around this time, teams associated with online influencers began to surface. One example is Disguised, a team formed by content creator Jeremy Wang, also known as [Disguised Toast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disguised_Toast). The team competed in the North American Challengers League (NACL) in 2023 before being selected as a guest team for the 2025 competitive season in the LTA and in the following year for the LCS. In late 2024, former professional player and caster [Marc "Caedrel" Lamont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caedrel), who began full-time streaming in 2023, formed [Los Ratones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Ratones), a team that competed in EMEA Masters and the LEC.



The 2024 World Championship was the most-watched event in esports history, with nearly 7 million peak viewers.

In 2024, Korean team [Gen.G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen.G) would win its first international title, that being the [2024 Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mid-Season_Invitational).[^cite_note-45] Meanwhile, T1 would win back-to-back world championships for the first time since 2016, with Faker winning his fifth world title and second Finals MVP honors.[^cite_note-46] In between MSI and Worlds, *League of Legends* was included in the [Esports World Cup](76626424.md), a multi-title tournament series held primarily in [Riyadh](51554.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), for the first time; it was the first non-Riot major tournament since the [Intel Extreme Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Extreme_Masters) Season XI World Championship in 2017.[^cite_note-47] The [inaugural EWC tournament for the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Esports_World_Cup_%E2%80%93_League_of_Legends) was won by T1 over the LPL's [Top Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Esports).

The same year also saw a major announcement from Riot Games in a blog post entitled "LoL Esports: Building Towards A Brighter Future", that a new split structure and competitive calendar would be introduced starting with the 2025 competitive season,[^cite_note-48] which includes a three-split structure for all Tier 1 leagues, and a third international competition later known as the [First Stand Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Stand_Tournament). The announcement also unveiled plans to merge the LCS, CBLOL and LLA into a Pan-American league that would begin play in 2025,[^cite_note-49] which would eventually be known as the [*League of Legends* Championship of The Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Championship_of_The_Americas) or LTA. A merger between the [Pacific Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Championship_Series) (PCS), [Vietnam Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Championship_Series) (VCS), [*League of Legends* Japan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Japan_League) (LJL) and [*League of Legends* Circuit Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Circuit_Oceania) (LCO) was also planned, later to be known as the [*League of Legends* Championship Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Championship_Pacific) (LCP).[^cite_note-50]

### 2025–present: Restructuring and draft changes

2025 saw the introduction of Fearless Draft. Inspired by China's *League of Legends* Developmental League (LDL), it is a draft format where teams cannot pick a champion that they've already played in a series, even if the opposing team already picked that champion. This was initially planned only for the first split of the 2025 season, but was later announced to be implemented for the rest of the competitive calendar.[^cite_note-fearlessdraft-51] It was later confirmed that Fearless Draft would also remain for the 2026 season.[^cite_note-52]

The inaugural First Stand Tournament (FST) was held in March 2025 in [Seoul](19159283.md), [South Korea](27019.md), with [Hanwha Life Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Life_Esports) of the LCK winning the tournament and its first international title,[^cite_note-53] while Gen.G would win back-to-back [MSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mid-Season_Invitational) titles.[^cite_note-54] The 2025 edition of MSI was the first international *League of Legends* competition to be held in Canada - at the [Pacific Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coliseum) in [Vancouver](32706.md). Both the LCP and LTA would launch their leagues with their first season in 2025, introducing promotion and relegation to *League of Legends* esports for the first time since the start of franchising, with guest teams competing. However, only the LCP is guaranteed to continue in 2026, as on 28 September 2025, Riot Games announced that the LCS and CBLOL would be returning as independent leagues beginning with the 2026 competitive season, discontinuing the LTA.[^cite_note-55] Promotion and relegation will remain in both leagues in 2026, as guest teams will continue to fill up one spot per league. The [2025 *League of Legends* World Championship](78265754.md) saw the first time a team win three consecutive World Championships for the first time in tournament history, with T1 winning its sixth world championship.[^cite_note-56]

In 2026, Riot Games introduced a new draft system called "First Selection", where the team that usually chooses either blue or red side would now have a choice to either select a side or have either the first or second pick in the picking phase of the draft. The system took effect at the first split of all six regional leagues for 2026.[^cite_note-57]

[Bilibili Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili_Gaming) won the second edition of FST, marking the organization's first international title and the LPL's first since the 2023 edition of MSI, and the end of the LCK's streak of six consecutive international titles since the 2023 World Championship. It was also the second international tournament to be held in Brazil after MSI 2017. [Daejeon](284812.md) in [South Korea](27019.md) will host the [2026 Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mid-Season_Invitational), while the [2026 *League of Legends* World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) in the [United States](3434750.md) will see the first international *League of Legends* competition to be held in the state of [Texas](29810.md) in [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen,_Texas), a suburb of the [Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas-Fort_Worth_metroplex), the first tournament to be held in [Los Angeles](18110.md) since 2016, and the first World Championship final to be held in the [East Coast of the United States](89126.md) in [New York City](645042.md),[^cite_note-58]

The 2026 [Esports Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports_Nations_Cup) (ENC) Regional Qualifiers, held in June 2026, determined which national teams would advance to the Esports Nations Cup *League of Legends* tournament in Riyadh. The Asian qualifier featured seven participating countries, with [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Mongolia](19271.md) successfully qualifying for the main event.[^cite_note-59] However, the Esports Nations Cup was postponed to 2027 as a result of [the conflict between the United States and Iran](82533582.md), which also moved the [2026 Esports World Cup](82191810.md) to Paris, France.[^cite_note-60]

## Tournaments

Main article: [List of League of Legends leagues and tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_League_of_Legends_leagues_and_tournaments)

### World Championship

Main article: [League of Legends World Championship](42058214.md)

The *League of Legends* World Championship is the season-ending tournament of the competitive *League of Legends* calendar. It is considered the most important tournament of the entire season, with its [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends:_Season_1_World_Championship) being held in 2011 at [DreamHack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamHack) in [Sweden](5058739.md). The tournament is considered one of the biggest esports events of the year, attracting millions of viewers in every edition. [T1](24141237.md) of the LCK are the defending champions, having won the [2025 edition](78265754.md).

### Mid-Season Invitational

Main article: [Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Season_Invitational)

The [Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Season_Invitational) (MSI) is an annual *League of Legends* tournament hosted by publisher [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games) since 2015. It is the second most important international *League of Legends* tournament aside from the [World Championship](42058214.md).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] [Hanwha Life Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Life_Esports) of the LCK are the defending champions, having won the [2026 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mid-Season_Invitational).

### First Stand Tournament

Main article: [First Stand Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Stand_Tournament)

The First Stand Tournament is third international tournament in the competitive *League of Legends* calendar. The inaugural edition took place on 10–16 March 2025 at LoL Park in [Seoul](19159283.md), [South Korea](27019.md) at the conclusion of the first split. This tournament utilizes *Fearless Draft* (which was popularized by China's LoL Development League), where Champions selected during a game cannot be selected again during the remainder of the series. [Bilibili Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili_Gaming) of the LPL are the defending champions, having won the [2026 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_First_Stand_Tournament).

### Regional leagues

Main article: [List of League of Legends leagues and tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_League_of_Legends_leagues_and_tournaments)

On February 7, 2013, Riot Games created the [*League of Legends* Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_Championship_Series_\(esports\)) (LCS), separate professional leagues for Europe and North America, where initially 8 teams would compete in a league structure similar to those found in other professional sports leagues (Particularly [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX) and other Latin American [association football](10568.md) leagues that have split seasons) with [promotion and relegation](648049.md) with a second-tier Challengers Series; each LCS soon expanded to 10 teams to accommodate demand. In 2018, the NA LCS dropped promotion and relegation in favor of a franchising system similar to American professional sports leagues like the [NFL](21211.md); the European league followed suit in 2019. In late 2018, the European *League of Legends* Championship Series (EU LCS) was renamed to the [*League of Legends* European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_European_Championship) (LEC). The North American *League of Legends* Championship Series (NA LCS) also dropped "North American" from its name,[^cite_note-63] and was renamed to simply the League Championship Series ahead of the [2019 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_LCS_season). In October 2024, the LCS would cease to exist and consolidate with regional leagues in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_de_League_of_Legends) and [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Latinoam%C3%A9rica) to form the [Championship of The Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Championship_of_The_Americas) (LTA).[^cite_note-64]

Equivalent leagues, run by Riot and local publishers, also exist in other regions. This includes the [Pro League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Pro_League) (LPL) in China by [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent), [Champions Korea](48319339.md) (LCK) in South Korea (Originally run by television channel [Ongamenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongamenet) (OGN) with its own group stage and knockout format before being run by Riot), and the [Championship Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Championship_Pacific) (LCP) run by Carry International.

The [Continental League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Continental_League) (LCL) in the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) and [Turkish Championship League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Championship_League?action=edit\&redlink=1) (TCL) in Turkey were also separate leagues that qualified teams for major international events but have since become EMEA regional leagues within the LEC ecosystem, who qualify teams for [EMEA Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMEA_Masters) alongside other leagues from across Europe, the Middle East and Africa. Similarly, the [Pacific Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Championship_Series) (PCS) in Taiwan/Hong Kong/Macau/Southeast Asia, [Vietnam Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Championship_Series) (VCS) in Vietnam, [Japan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Japan_League) (LJL) in Japan and the [Circuit Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Legends_Circuit_Oceania) (LCO) by [ESL Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESL_\(company\)) and Guinevere Capital also sent teams to Worlds but were consolidated into domestic leagues that can promote teams to the LCP; the LCO no longer exists as of 2024.

## Music

Music in *League of Legends* esports tournaments have been used to promote both regional and international competitions. Both the World Championship and the Mid-Season Invitational have had official songs to promote the tournament, with Worlds having a theme song since 2014 and MSI having official songs for the 2019, 2021, 2022, and 2023 editions.

In every best-of-5 series, whenever Game 5 is reached, a song known as "Silver Scrapes" is played. This tradition began during the 2012 World Championship, when it was played repeatedly whenever a technical delay occurred, and fans have since grown to enjoy the music. When the song plays, fans at the venue often wave their mobile phone flashlights to the melody. An orchestral version of the song was released before the 2025 World Championship knockout stages. It was recorded by the Hungarian Studio Orchestra at the Italian Institute in [Budapest](36787.md), [Hungary](13275.md).

## Hall of Legends

In January 2024, [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games) announced the "Hall of Legends", a [hall of fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_halls_and_walks_of_fame) that commemorates active and retired players once a year who have contributed to the game, whilst recognizing their achievements throughout their careers with a trophy and in-game items.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] The criteria for induction includes a player having played in tier-one leagues for five cumulative years, and a group of panelists deciding a players' induction based on their accolades in and out of the game.[^cite_note-67] The first induction in 2024 to the hall was South Korea's [Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faker_\(gamer\)),[^cite_note-68] whilst China's [Jian "Uzi" Zihao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzi_\(gamer\)) was the inductee in 2025.[^cite_note-69] In 2026, Denmark's [Rasmus "Caps" Winther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caps_\(gamer\)) became the first [Western](21208200.md) inductee into the Hall of Legends.
