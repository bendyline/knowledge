**Pernambuco** ([/ˌpɜːrnəmˈb(j)uːkoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PUR-nəm-BEW-koh, -⁠BOO-*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Brazilian Portuguese: [\[peʁnɐ̃ˈbuku\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/49/Pt-br_Pernambuco.ogg/Pt-br_Pernambuco.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/49/Pt-br_Pernambuco.ogg/Pt-br_Pernambuco.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, locally [\[ˌpɛɦʁnɐ̃ˈbuku\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)) is a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil) of [Brazil](3383.md), on the east coast in the [Northeast region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil) of the country. With an estimated population of 9.5 million people as of 2024,[^cite_note-\:0-5] it is the [seventh-most populous state of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_states_by_population) and with around 98,067.877 km<sup>2</sup>, it is the [19th-largest in area among federative units of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_states_by_area). It is also the sixth-most densely populated with around 92.37 people per km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-\:0-5] Its capital and largest city, [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife), is one of the most important economic and urban hubs in the country. Based on 2019 estimates, the [Recife Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife_metropolitan_area) is seventh-most populous in the country, and the second-largest in [northeastern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil).[^cite_note-6] In 2015, the state had 4.4% of the national population and produced 2.8% of the national [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP).[^cite_note-7]

The contemporary state inherits its name from the [Captaincy of Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_of_Pernambuco), established in 1534. The region was originally inhabited by [Tupi–Guarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi%E2%80%93Guarani_languages)-speaking peoples. European colonization began in the 16th century, under mostly [Portuguese rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil) interrupted by a brief period of [Dutch rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Brazil), followed by [Brazilian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil) in 1822. Large numbers of [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Brazil) were brought from Africa during the colonial era to cultivate sugarcane, and a significant portion of the state's population has some amount of African ancestry.

The state has rich cultural traditions thanks to its varied history and peoples. [Brazilian Carnivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Carnival) in Recife and the historic colonial capital of [Olinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olinda) are renowned: the [Galo da Madrugada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galo_da_Madrugada) parade in Recife has held world records for its size.

Historically a center of [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) cultivation due to the favorable climate, the state has a modern economy dominated by the [services sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) today, though large amounts of sugarcane are still grown. The coming of democracy in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Brazil_since_1985) has brought the state progress and challenges in turn: while economic and health indicators have improved, [inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_Brazil) remains high.

## Etymology

The origins of the name *Pernambuco* are debated, though most hypotheses derive the name from the now-extinct [Tupi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi_language).

Some scholars claim that the name comes from the combining of the [Tupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi_language) words *para'nã*, meaning "great river" or "sea" and *buka*, meaning "hole". Thus, *pernambuco* would mean "hole in the sea", possibly referring to the [Canal de Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_de_Santa_Cruz) on the [Island of Itamaracá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilha_de_Itamarac%C3%A1), north of [Olinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olinda), or to an opening in the [reefs](240844.md) between Olinda and Recife.[^cite_note-8] According to others, *pernambuco* was the name of [brazilwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paubrasilia) in local indigenous languages at the time of first contact, as the tree is found widely in the [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambuco_coastal_forests) of the future state. A third hypothesis also derives from a Tupi word, *paranãbuku*, meaning "long river", a possible reference to the [Capibaribe River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capibaribe_River), since primitive maps mark such a "Pernambuco river" north of [Cabo de Santo Agostinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabo_de_Santo_Agostinho), south of Recife.

Another hypothesis, suggested by the academic Jacques Ribemboim, asserts the origins of the name from the Portuguese language. The Canal de Santa Cruz in Recife, at the beginning of the 16th century, was known as *Boca de Fernão* (named after the explorer [Fernão de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_de_Loronha)). The Indians may have pronounced *Fernão* as ***P**ernao* and reversed the [order of the words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_order), giving *Pernão Boca* or *Pernambuka*, leading to the contemporary name of Pernambuco.[^cite_note-9]

The state also has some nicknames, such as *Lion of the North*, *Land of [Frevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frevo) and [Maracatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracatu)* and *Blessed Land*.

## Geography



The [Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha) Islands, 354 km from the mainland, form a "state district" of Pernambuco.

Pernambuco comprises a comparatively narrow coastal zone, a high inland plateau, and an intermediate zone formed by the terraces and slopes between the two.[^cite_note-EB1911-10]

Its surface is much broken by the remains of the ancient plateau which has been worn down by erosion, leaving escarpments and ranges of flat-topped mountains, called [chapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapada), capped in places by horizontal layers of sandstone. Ranges of these chapadas form the boundary lines with three states–the Serra dos Irmãos and Serra Vermelha with [Piauí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piau%C3%AD), the [Serra do Araripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_do_Araripe) with [Ceará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cear%C3%A1), and the [Serra dos Cariris Velhos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariri_Velhos_belt) with [Paraíba](229523.md).[^cite_note-EB1911-10]

### Regions

The coastal area is fertile, and was formerly covered by the humid [Pernambuco coastal forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambuco_coastal_forests), the northern extension of the [Atlantic Forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Forests) (Mata Atlântica) of eastern Brazil. It is now occupied by extensive [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane) plantations. It has a hot, humid climate, relieved to some extent by the south-east trade winds.[^cite_note-EB1911-10]



[Catimbau Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_do_Catimbau_National_Park) - the 2nd-largest Brazilian archeological site

The middle zone, called the *[agreste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreste)* region, has a drier climate and lighter vegetation,[^cite_note-EB1911-10] including the semi-deciduous [Pernambuco interior forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambuco_interior_forests), where many trees lose their leaves in the dry season.

The inland region, called the *[sertão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sert%C3%A3o)* is high, stony, and dry, and frequently devastated by prolonged droughts (secas). The climate is characterized by hot days and cool nights. There are two clearly defined seasons, a rainy season from March to June, and a dry season for the remaining months.[^cite_note-EB1911-10] The interior of the state is covered mostly by the dry thorny scrub vegetation called *[caatinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caatinga)*. The [Rio São Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_S%C3%A3o_Francisco) is the main water source for this area.



[Boa Viagem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boa_Viagem,_Recife) and Pina beaches

The climate is more mild in the [Borborema Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borborema_Plateau) ("Planalto da Borborema"). Some towns are more than 1,000 meters above sea level, and temperatures there can descend to 10 °C (50 °F) and even 5 °C (41 °F) in some cities (i.e., [Triunfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triunfo,_Pernambuco)) during the winter.

The volcanic archipelago of [Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha) in the Atlantic Ocean, 535 km northeast of [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife), has been part of Pernambuco since 1988.

### Hydrology

The rivers of the state include a number of small plateau streams flowing southward to the [São Francisco River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Francisco_River), and several large streams in the eastern part flowing eastward to the Atlantic. The former are the [Moxotó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moxot%C3%B3_River), Ema, [Pajeú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paje%C3%BA_River), [Terra Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Nova_River), Brigida, Boa Vista and Pontai, and are dry channels the greater part of the year.[^cite_note-EB1911-10]

The largest of the coastal rivers are the [Goiana river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goiana_River), which is formed by the confluence of the Tracunhaem and Capibaribe-mirim, and drains a rich agricultural region in the north-east part of the state; the [Capibaribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capibaribe_River), which has its source in the Serra de Jacarara and flows eastward to the Atlantic at [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife) with a course of nearly 300 miles (480 km); the [Ipojuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipojuca_River), which rises in the Serra de Aldeia Velha and reaches the coast south of Recife; the Serinhaen; and the [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_River_\(Pernambuco\)). A large tributary of the Uná, the Rio Jacuhipe, forms part of the boundary line with [Alagoas](165082.md).[^cite_note-EB1911-10]

## History

Main articles: [History of Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pernambuco) and [Captaincy of Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_of_Pernambuco)

### Prehistory

Prior to discovery and colonization by Portugal, Pernambuco was inhabited by numerous tribes of [Tupi-Guarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi%E2%80%93Guarani_languages) speaking indigenous peoples. The [Tupi peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupi_people) were a largely hunter-gatherer culture living in long houses who cultivated some indigenous crops, most notably [manioc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) (*Manihot esculenta*), but lacked any metallic tools. Many elements of the Tupi culture were a shock to Europeans: among these, they bathed frequently, they eschewed wealth accumulation, practiced nudity, and warred frequently, primarily to capture enemies for communal, ritual cannibalism.[^cite_note-11]

### European contact

Main article: [Fernão de Loronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_de_Loronha)

Modern day Pernambuco includes the islands of [Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha), which precedes mainland Pernambuco's history since the islands were granted to Fernão de Laronha by King Manoel in 1502.[^cite_note-12]

Pernambuco was initially valued as a source of [Brazilwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paubrasilia) (*Caesalpinia echinata*) used in Europe for dyes. These Amerindians were eager to harvest and exchange brazilwood for axes, fishhooks and other goods offered by Europeans.[^cite_note-13] The Portuguese crown granted a license to Fernão de Laronha in 1502.[^cite_note-14] After the expiration of the license the trade in brazilwood was a driver of the exploration of Brazil. Brazilwood was highly valued and other European nations, particularly the French, soon sent ships to exploit this new dye wood. The French under [Bertrand d'Ornesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_d'Ornesan) tried to establish a French trading post at Pernambuco in 1531.[^cite_note-15] This fort was at the border of Pernambuco and Itamaracá to the North. The Portuguese King responded by dispatching an armada under the command of Pero Lopes de Sousa. Pero Lopes defeated the French, destroyed their fort and built a new fort.[^cite_note-16]

### Portuguese settlement



The historic centre of [Olinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olinda), a [World Heritage Site](44940.md)

Shortly after the success in dislodging the French from Pernambuco's northern border with Itamaracá the Portuguese began to settle Brazil. King [John III of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal) created the [Hereditary Captaincies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitania) in 1534, Pernambuco was granted to [Duarte Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Coelho), who arrived in *Nova Lusitânia* (or "New [Lusitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitania)") in 1535. Duarte directed military actions against the French-allied [Caetés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caet%C3%A9_people) Indians and upon their defeat in 1537 established a settlement at the site of a former Marin Indian village, henceforth known as Olinda, as well as another village at [Igarassu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igarassu,_Pernambuco). Under his leadership sugar soon replaced Brazilwood as Pernambuco's most profitable export.[^cite_note-17] Due to the cultivation of [sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane) and cotton, Pernambuco was one of the few prosperous captaincies (the other notable one being [São Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_of_S%C3%A3o_Vicente)).

#### Slavery

Main article: [Slavery in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Brazil)

In addition to requiring a lot of capital investment, refining sugar in the 16th century also required a vast amount of labor. Brazilian Indians were very useful to the Portuguese; both free Indians and enslaved Indians performed many useful services for the Portuguese settlers. This included helping with building Engenhos. However, Brazilian Indian culture was not well suited to the operation of sugar engenhos. Indian culture was not oriented to wealth accumulation. Stuart Schwartz expressed it, "Once a man had enough to eat and a few new tools and weapons, why should he want or work for more."[^cite_note-18]

While the [sugar industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_industry) relied at first on the labor of indigenous peoples, especially the [Tupis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupis) and [Tapuyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapuyas), high mortality and economic growth led to the importation of enslaved Africans from the late 17th century onward. Some of these slaves escaped the sugar-producing coastal regions and formed independent inland communities called [mocambos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mocambo_\(settlement\)), including [Palmares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmares,_Pernambuco).

### Dutch conquest

Main article: [Dutch Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Brazil)



Dutch invasions in Brazil



[John Maurice, Prince of Nassau-Siegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maurice,_Prince_of_Nassau-Siegen)

In 1630, Pernambuco, as well as many Portuguese possessions in Brazil, was occupied by the Dutch until 1654.[^cite_note-EB1911-10] The occupation was strongly resisted and the Dutch conquest was only partially successful for these few decades. In the interim, thousands of the [enslaved Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enslaved_Africans) had fled to Palmares, and soon the mocambos there had grown into two significant states. The [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic), which allowed sugar production to remain in Portuguese hands, regarded suppression of Palmares as important, but was unsuccessful in this. [Johan Maurits van Nassau-Siegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Maurits_van_Nassau-Siegen), count of Nassau, was appointed as ruler of the *[Nieuw Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuw_Holland)* (Dutch colonization enterprise in Brazil).

In the 17th century, the [Netherlands](21148.md) was experiencing a surge of freedom and progress, and wanted to expand their colonies in the American continent. An expression of this new economy was the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company), (modeled after the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) which had influence throughout the world and controlled much of the trade between East and West). A Board of nineteen members appointed Prince [Johan Maurits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Maurits), Count of Nassau, Governor of Pernambuco. It was an auspicious choice for Northeast, because he was a lover of the arts with a deep interest in the New World. In 1637 he opened his government guidelines quite different from those of the Portuguese colonists, declaring "Freedom of Religion and Trade". His entourage contained traders, artists, planners, German and Dutch citizens. He was accompanied by six painters, including [Frans Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_Post) and [Albert Eckhout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Eckhout). Nassau also created an environment of Dutch religious tolerance, new to Portuguese America and irritating to his Calvinist associates. Nassau made efforts to reduce the sugar production monoculture by encouraging the cultivation of other crops, particularly foodstuffs.[^cite_note-19]

### Jewish immigration



[Kahal Zur Israel Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahal_Zur_Israel_Synagogue) in Mauritsstad ([Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife)), the first synagogue in the Americas

Under Dutch rule, Jewish culture developed in [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife). Many Jews, having fled the [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Inquisition) in Iberia, sought refuge in the Netherlands. The Jewish community established themselves in Dutch Brazil and would later migrate elsewhere in the Americas. There are records that in 1636 a synagogue was being built in the city. A Jewish scholar from [Amsterdam](844.md), [Isaac Aboab da Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Aboab_da_Fonseca), arrived in [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife) in 1642, becoming the first rabbi on Brazilian soil and on the continent. In 1643, three years after the Portuguese regained the crown in the metropolis, Father [António Vieira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Vieira) – frowned upon, persecuted by the Inquisition and admirer of Aboab – recommended the King of Portugal occupy the capital of the New Christian and Jewish immigrants to help the depressed Portuguese finances[^cite_note-20]

### Portuguese reconquest

The Portuguese reconquered Recife in 1654 and [Olinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olinda) regained its status of political center. However, [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife) remained the commercial port city. Nowadays, it is credited that many inhabitants of Pernambuco's *agreste* region have some Dutch ancestry.[^cite_note-nedbra-21] If the Dutch were gone, however, the threat of the now unified quilombo of Palmares remained. In spite of a treaty negotiated in 1678 with its ruler [Ganga Zumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Zumba), a war between the two remained. [Zumbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumbi) who became ruler following the peace treaty and later repudiated it, fought the Portuguese government until 1694 when soldiers brought from the south eventually defeated him.

### Three centuries of the sugar cycle

Throughout the remainder of the 17th century on to the 20th century much of life in Pernambuco was dominated by the patterns established by monoculture, latifundia, and slavery (until 1888). Sugar and cotton were grown on large plantations and rural society was largely divided into landowning elites and the impoverished poor. In addition, Pernambuco, except for a narrow coastland, is subject to periodic droughts. The boom and bust economy throughout this period is often exemplified as the "sugar cycle" when the international market for sugar is good, the economy booms, when the market is bad, it is hard times for all and particularly for the impoverished. Sugar has always been the principal example of the boom or bust cycle, but there has, from time to time been a similar cycle in cotton. Cotton was profitable during the U.S. War of Independence, the War of 1812, and the U.S. Civil War. Each time the bust in Pernambuco came when U.S. growers resumed their exports.[^cite_note-22]

#### 17th-century class conflict

A sugar mill *engenho* requires a large investment both to build and to operate. Much of the time the money is borrowed. Although there were other sources, one source that was a particular irritant to mill owners were the merchants of Recife. In 1710 this irritant resulted in the [Mascate War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascate_War). This conflict set the *mascates* from Recife against the establishment planters of Olinda It was led by the *Senhores de Engenho* (owners of the sugar mills). It is an example of the continuing tensions between the senhores de engenho (the landed elites) in colonial Brazil and the merchants of Recife. The "War" (there was considerable shooting but little loss of life) has elements of class struggle. Olinda had, before the Dutch, always been the municipal seat. Recife, once merely a port facility for Olinda, had formerly consisted of a few modest dwellings, warehouses, and businesses catering to ships and seamen, but under the Dutch had been developed into a thriving center of commerce populated by wealthy, more recently arrived merchants to whom most of the landed aristocracy of Pernambuco were heavily indebted. After several excesses the king issued a new set of instructions to the governor. In 1715 the crown dispatched a new governor and the residents of Pernambuco finally felt the troubles were ended, though many families of the colony's elites were ruined.[^cite_note-23]

#### 18th century: mining eclipses sugar

The discovery of gold in Minas Gerais late in the Seventeenth Century and the discovery of diamond displaced agriculture. In fact, for all the disruption caused by "gold fever" throughout the mining boom the value of sugar exports always exceeded the value of any other export.[^cite_note-24] Nevertheless, among many other disruptions, gold shifted the focus South. Pernambuco, Bahia, and the entire Northeast were eclipsed by the South of Brazil and that shift in focus has never been reversed.[^cite_note-25]

#### 19th century: a province, then a state

Pernambuco's response to the nationhood of Brazil seems to have been rebellion. Pernambuco was the site of some of the most important rebellions and insurrections in [Brazilian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_history), especially in the 19th century. See Also [Rebellions and revolutions in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellions_and_revolutions_in_Brazil), [Pernambucan revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambucan_revolt), [Cabanada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabanada), [April Revolt (Pernambuco)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Revolt_\(Pernambuco\)) At one point Pernambuco led much of the Northeast region in a very short-lived independent [Confederation of the Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Equator).

#### The end of slavery and the beginning of the republic

In 1888, under the influence of increasingly urban society, and with the advocacy of intellectuals such as Pernambucan politician [Joaquim Nabuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquim_Nabuco), slavery was abolished.[^cite_note-26] However, freedom for the slaves did little or nothing to improve life for the underclass. Economic downturns were used to cut wages, children were paid almost nothing, and violence ruled.[^cite_note-27] In those days before antibiotics there were major epidemics, fourteen between 1849 and 1920.[^cite_note-28]

#### 20th century



Graf Zeppelin over Recife in the 1930s

The twentieth century did bring better communication and transportation which would slowly allow development. But for the poor employed in the sugar industry, as late as the 1960s infant mortality in this labor segment was nearly half of live births.[^cite_note-29] Politically, the century was dominated by two periods of dictatorship, ruled by [Getúlio Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get%C3%BAlio_Vargas) for most of the period from 1930 to 1954,[^cite_note-30] and the military dictatorship from 1964 to 1985.[^cite_note-31]

### Post-dictatorship progress

Since the end of military rule, there is still an underemployed and under-fed underclass. However, quality of life has improved along with industrial development. Pernambuco has also become a major tourist destination. Statistics from the turn of the millennium show a sharp and continuing improvement. According to estimates from the [Global Burden of Disease Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Burden_of_Disease_Study), the [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality) declined 6.2 percent annually between 1990 and 2015: from 90.4 infant deaths per 1000 live births in 1990, to 13.4 deaths/1000 live births in 2015.[^cite_note-32] The homicide rate in Recife, still higher than the average for Brazil, declined by about 6% per annum during the period from 2000 to 2012.[^cite_note-33]

Income inequality remains a problem; in 2000, the state had a Gini coefficient of 0.59,[^cite_note-34] with wealth and resources being concentrated at the top.

## Government and politics

The state government is divided into [three branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers), like all Brazilian states. All the branches are in capital of Recife.

- [Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)): the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Pernambuco), currently [Raquel Lyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raquel_Lyra) of the [PSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Brazil,_2011\)), is responsible for administration. The vice-governor, currently [Priscila Krause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priscila_Krause) of the [PSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Brazil,_2011\)) is first in line of succession to the governorship, but otherwise has few responsibilities.

- [Legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature): the [Legislative Assembly of Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Pernambuco), a unicameral body with 49 state deputies. It meets in the *Edifício Governador Miguel Arraes de Alencar.*

- [Judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary): the [Pernambuco Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribunal_de_Justi%C3%A7a_de_Pernambuco?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*Tribunal de Justiça de Pernambuco*), with 52 justices.

The governor and deputies are elected to four year terms in Brazilian general elections, with the most recent being held in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election).

### Local government

The [185 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Pernambuco) that make up the state have similar structures, though they lack the judicial branch. Each municipality has a chief executive, analogous to mayor, called a *Prefeito/Prefeita*, while the legislative branch is called the *Câmara Municipal*.

Municipal officials also serve four year terms, with the most recent being held in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Brazilian_municipal_elections).

[Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha) is a *[sui generis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_generis)* "State District" (*distrito estadual*), governed directly by a Pernambuco state administrator.[^cite_note-35]

### Federal representation

At the federal level, Pernambuco is represented by 25 deputies in the [Chamber of Deputies](1589485.md), and three senators in the [Federal Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Senate).

### Racial/Ethnic composition

The results of the National Household Sample Survey (PNAD) conducted in 2022 led to the following estimates of [race or skin color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_Brazil): 5,006,802 [Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_people#Pardos_in_Brazil) ([Multiracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial#Brazil)) people (55.3%), 3,043,916 [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Brazilian) people (33.6%), 909,557 [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilian) people (10.0%), 83,667 [Indigenous](55510.md) people (0.9%) and 13,225 [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Brazilian) people (0.1%).[^cite_note-39]

Due to the legacy of slavery and the sugarcane plantations, it has been observed that those of mixed [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilian) and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Brazilian) ancestry are more common on the coast, while [Mamelucos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mameluco) (those of mixed [Amerindian](55510.md) and Portuguese ancestry) are more common in the interior *[Sertão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sert%C3%A3o)* region.[^cite_note-40]

According to a genetic study from 2013, Pernambucans have 56.8% European, 27.9% African and 15.3% Amerindian ancestries.[^cite_note-41]

Local health care training by the [Olinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olinda) local administration

[Castro Alves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castro_Alves) and [Joaquim Nabuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquim_Nabuco), two important Brazilian historical figures are among its alumni.

Many institutions are composed of several autonomous campuses serving the entire state, however Recife remains undeniably the center of education.

### Ethanol

Pernambuco was once the 5th largest producer of [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) in Brazil, but today it ranks 8th, behind [São Paulo](229422.md), [Goiás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goi%C3%A1s), [Minas Gerais](222651.md), [Mato Grosso do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mato_Grosso_do_Sul), [Paraná](229411.md), [Mato Grosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mato_Grosso) and [Alagoas](165082.md).[^cite_note-47] Brazil is the second largest producer of alcohol fuel in the world, typically fermenting ethanol from [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) and [sugar beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet). The country produces a total of 18 billion liters annually, of which 3.5 billion are exported, 2 billion of them to the US. Alcohol-fueled cars started in the Brazilian market in 1978 and became quite popular because of heavy subsidy, but in the 80s prices rose and gasoline regained the leading market share. But from 2004 on, alcohol rapidly increased its market share once again because of new technologies involving [hybrid fuel car engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible-fuel_vehicle) called "Flex" by all major car manufacturers ([Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen), [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), [Ford](30433662.md), [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot), [Honda](13729.md), [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn), [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat), etc.). "Flex" engines work with gasoline, alcohol or any mixture of both fuels. As of February 2007, approximately 80% of new vehicles sold in Brazil are hybrid fuel powered. Because of the Brazilian lead in production and technology, many countries became very interested in importing alcohol fuel and adopting the "Flex" vehicle concept.

### Industry

In 2018, Pernambuco had an industrial GDP of R\$32.4 billion, equivalent to 2.5% of the national industry and employing more than 280,000 workers in the industry. The main industrial sectors are: Construction (20%), Public Utilities Industrial Services, such as Electricity and Water (17%), Food (14.9%), Petroleum derivatives and biofuels (9.3%) and Motor vehicles (8.8%). These 5 sectors concentrate 70% of the state's industry. In only 3 places in the Brazilian Northeast Region, there is some relevant industrial production, one of them being the surroundings of Recife. However, the state continues to have little participation in the Brazilian economy, with the population largely concentrated on the coast.[^cite_note-48]

## Infrastructure

### Airports



[Guararapes International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guararapes_International_Airport), in [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife)

[Recife/Guararapes–Gilberto Freyre International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife/Guararapes%E2%80%93Gilberto_Freyre_International_Airport) has been open since July 2004 and has 52 thousand square meters of area. The largest airport in the North and Northeast regions, Guararapes had its capacity expanded from 5 million to 11 million passengers a year. Now there are 64 check-in counters, versus the former terminal's 24. The shopping and leisure area was also totally remodeled, within the "Aeroshopping" concept, which transforms an airport into a center for business and retail. The commercial spaces will be occupied in steps and the final total will be 142 shops. Since 2000, Recife has had the longest runway in the Northeast, at 3,305 meters. Its extension permits operations with jumbo jets, such as the Boeing 747-400, able to fly nonstop to anywhere in South and Central America, Africa and parts of Europe, the United States and Canada.

Pernambuco is also served by the [Petrolina International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrolina_Airport), which is responsible for the delivery of fresh fruit from the [São Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Francisco_River) valley to Europe and the US. The cargo terminal operates with 6 large coolers[^cite_note-49] with 17000 boxes capacity each plus 2 coolers tunnels. This airports also has daily direct connections between this region (which includes 53 municipalities from the states such as Pernambuco, Piaui and Bahia) to major capitals as [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife) and Salvador. Like the [Recife airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife_airport), it is administered by the Brazilian Federal Agency ([Infraero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infraero)).

Other locally administered airports within the state are the [Fernando de Noronha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha_Airport) and the [Caruaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caruaru) airports. Fernando de Noronhha has daily flights between the [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha) with Recife and Natal, and the second airport connects the textile industry region of Caruaru with São Paulo and local cities.

### Railways

Freight trains are operated by [Transnordestina Logística](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnordestina_Log%C3%ADstica_SA?action=edit\&redlink=1), formerly the *Companhia Ferroviária do Nordeste* (CFN), and mainly hauls iron ore, petroleum, and cement. The [Recife Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife_Metro), opened in 1985, has five lines and is operated by the federally-controlled *Compania Brasileira de Trens Urbanos* (CBTU).

## Festivals

### Carnival



Carnival in [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife)

The four-day period before [Lent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent) leading up to [Ash Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Wednesday) is [carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival) time in [Brazil](3383.md). Rich and poor alike forget their cares as they party in the streets. Pernambuco has large Carnaval celebrations, including the *[frevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frevo)*, typical Pernambuco music. Another carnaval music style from Pernambuco is *[maracatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracatu)*.

The cities of Recife and Olinda hold the most authentic and democratic carnaval celebrations in Brazil. The largest carnaval parade in all of Brazil is *[Galo da Madrugada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galo_da_Madrugada)*, which takes place in downtown Recife in the Saturday of carnaval. Another event is the *Noite dos Tambores Silenciosos*.

Recife's joyous Carnaval is nationally known, attracting thousands of people every year. The party starts a week before the official date, with electric trios "shaking" the Boa Viagem district.

On Friday, people take to the streets to enjoy themselves to the sound of frevo and to dance with maracatu, ciranda, caboclinhos, afoxé, reggae and manguebeat (cultural movement created in Recife during the 1990s) groups. There are still many other entertainment centres around the city, featuring local and national artists.



Giant Dolls - Olinda Carnival

One of the highlights is Saturday when more than one million people follow the Galo da Madrugada group. From Sunday to Monday, there is the Night of the Silent Drums, on the Pátio do Terço, where Maracatus honor slaves that died in prisons.

### Saint John's Day

[Festa Junina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festa_Junina) was introduced to Northeastern [Brazil](3383.md) by the [Portuguese](23033.md), for whom Saint John's day (also celebrated as [Midsummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsummer) Day in several European countries), on 24 June, is one of the oldest and most popular celebrations of the year. Differently, of course, from what happens on the European [Midsummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsummer) Day, the festivities in Brazil do not take place during the [summer solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_solstice), but during the tropical [winter solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_solstice). The festivities traditionally begin after 12 June, on the eve of Saint Anthony's day, and last until the 29th, which is Saint Peter's day. During these fifteen days, there are [bonfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonfires), [fireworks](59493.md), and folk dancing in the streets. Once exclusively a rural festival, today, in Brazil, it is largely a city festival during which people joyfully and theatrically mimic peasant stereotypes and clichés in a spirit of jokes and good times. Typical refreshments and dishes are served, including [canjica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canjica) and [pamonha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamonha). Like during Carnival, these festivities involve costume-wearing (in this case, peasant costumes), dancing, heavy drinking, and visual spectacles (fireworks display and folk dancing). Like what happens on [Midsummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsummer) and Saint John's Day in Europe, [bonfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonfires) are a central part of these festivities in Brazil.

Saint John's Day is celebrated throughout Pernambuco. Nonetheless, the festivities in [Caruaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caruaru) are by far the largest in the state. Saint John's festivals in [Gravatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravat%C3%A1) and [Carpina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpina) are also popular.

### Winter Festival

In the hilly areas of the interior – mainly in areas with a micro-climate of altitude – temperatures that can reach 8 °C in the winter. Every winter, when the weather is milder, tourists from neighboring states and other parts of Pernambuco visit cities such as [Garanhuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garanhuns), [Gravatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravat%C3%A1), [Triunfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triunfo,_Pernambuco), [Taquaritinga do Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taquaritinga_do_Norte) and [Brejo da Madre de Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brejo_da_Madre_de_Deus).

&#x20;The city of Garanhuns holds an annual Winter Festival, in the month of July. The main attractions are concerts, dances, [rural tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_tourism), culinary and the relatively low temperatures for a tropical climate.

## Sports



[Arena Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Pernambuco)

[Football](10568.md) was introduced to Pernambuco in 1902, when English and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutchmen) sailors disembarked in Recife and played a game of football in the beach. The novelty awoke the interest of the people of Pernambuco, that soon adhered to the game. Recife provides visitors and residents with various sport activities; the city has the largest football teams of the state of Pernambuco. There are several football clubs based in Recife, such as [Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_do_Recife), [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Futebol_Clube), and [Náutico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_N%C3%A1utico_Capibaribe).

According to the [Brazilian Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Football_Confederation) in 2008, the [Pernambuco Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federa%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Pernambucana_de_Futebol) was ranked sixth nationwide, behind [São Paulo](390875.md), [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md), [Minas Gerais](222651.md) and [Paraná](229411.md); and first in the [Northeast region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Brazil).[^cite_note-57] The Pernambuco Football Federation organizes the *[Campeonato Pernambucano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Pernambucano)* state championship and the state cup. The first edition of the *Campeonato Pernambucano* was played in 1915, and was won by Sport Club Flamengo, a club since defunct. In 2011 twelve clubs competed, which was won by [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Futebol_Clube).

In 2012 the state was represented in the highest national level of football ([Brazilian Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A)) by [Náutico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_N%C3%A1utico_Capibaribe) and [Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_do_Recife). Also, it was represented in ([Série C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_C)) by [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Futebol_Clube) and [Salgueiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salgueiro_Atl%C3%A9tico_Clube) and in ([Série D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_D)) by [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Sport_Club).

[Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife) was one of the 12 Brazilian cities that hosted the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md).
