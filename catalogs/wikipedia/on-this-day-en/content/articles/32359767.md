# International recognition of Israel

&#x20; States that recognize Israel, but have suspended relations

&#x20; States that do not recognize Israeli statehood

As of February 2026, the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel) is recognized as a [sovereign state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states) by 169 of the other 192 [member states of the United Nations](31969.md), or approximately 88% of all UN members. The State of Israel was formally established by the [Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) on 14 May 1948, and was admitted to the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) as a [full member state](31969.md) on 11 May 1949.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] It also maintains bilateral ties with all of the [UNSC Permanent Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council). Twenty-three member states have either [never formally recognized Israel or have withdrawn their recognition](#States_that_do_not_recognize_Israel); others have severed diplomatic relations without explicitly withdrawing their recognition. Additionally, many non-recognizing countries have [challenged Israel's existence](40229800.md)—predominantly those in the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world)—due to significant animosity stemming from the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md) and the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md).[^cite_note-3]

## History

See also: [1948 Palestine war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_war) and [1948 Palestinian expulsion and flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight)

On 14 May 1948, the [Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence)[^cite_note-4] declared the establishment a [Jewish state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_state) in part of the former [British Mandate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), with borders formally established by the [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)) of the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) at the end of the [1948 Palestine war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_war). The [Arab League](52625.md) was opposed to any partition and to the establishment of [Israel](9282173.md), and an [Arab coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) jointly invaded the territory of the newly formed country one day after its independence, sparking the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War).

Following Israel's establishment, the [Israeli provisional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_government_of_Israel) was established to govern the [Yishuv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishuv); and while military operations were still in progress, it was promptly granted *[de facto](43594.md)* recognition by the [United States](3434750.md),[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-auto-6] followed by [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran) (which had voted against the Partition Plan), [Guatemala](17238567.md), [Iceland](14531.md), [Nicaragua](21362.md), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania), and [Uruguay](31843.md). The [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was the first country to grant *[de jure](43594.md)* recognition to Israel on 17 May 1948,[^cite_note-7] followed by Nicaragua, [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), and [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic).[^cite_note-8] The United States extended *de jure* recognition after the [first Israeli election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Israeli_Constituent_Assembly_election),[^cite_note-9] on 31 January 1949.[^cite_note-10]

By the late 1960s, Israel had established diplomatic relations with almost all of the countries of [Western Europe](33800.md), [North America](21139.md), [South America](26769.md), and [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) combined.

To put additional diplomatic, economic, and military pressure on Israel in the wake of the [1967 Arab–Israeli War](29328.md), oil-producing Arab countries imposed [an oil embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Oil_Embargo) on countries that had bilateral relations with Israel. As a result, many African and Asian countries broke off their ties with Israel. The Soviet Union also [shifted its support in favour of the Arab cause against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_anti-Zionism) during this time, leading most countries of the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) to sever diplomatic ties in 1967; these included the Soviet Union itself, as well as Poland, Czechoslovakia, [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People's_Republic), and [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria). Other countries in the Soviet sphere of influence, such as the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) and [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_People's_Republic), also did not establish relations with Israel. Diplomatic relations with these countries were restored or established following the collapse of the Eastern Bloc in 1989. The Soviet Union restored relations in October 1991, and new countries that had gained independence after the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) also recognised Israel in their own right. China established relations in January 1992.

On 1 September 1967, the then-eight members of the Arab League issued the [Khartoum Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_Resolution), which included three pledges that forbade recognition, peace, and negotiations with Israel. However, [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_peace_treaty), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Jordan_peace_treaty), and [Mauritania](18778516.md) gradually recognized Israel, though Mauritania broke off ties and withdrew recognition in 2010. As part of the [2020 Abraham Accords](64883396.md), the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_Arab_Emirates_normalization_agreement), [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain%E2%80%93Israel_normalization_agreement), and [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Morocco_normalization_agreement) all established normalized bilateral ties with Israel, with [Sudan](27421.md) announcing [its plan (never completed)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Sudan_normalization_agreement) to do so.[^cite_note-WSJ_U.S._Israel_Sudan_Deal-11][^cite_note-12] [Pressure was again exerted by the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) after the [1973 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War), which led [Cuba](5042481.md), [Mali](19127.md), and the [Maldives](19117.md) to break off ties with Israel. [Niger](21373.md) severed bilateral ties with Israel during the [Second Intifada](71717.md), and [Venezuela](32374.md) broke off ties after the [2008–2009 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%932009\)).

Following Israel's recognition of and entering into negotiations with the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO), many African, Asian, and Arab countries either restored or established diplomatic relations with Israel. The [Vatican](32408.md) began a bilateral relationship with Israel in 1994. Some countries[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-19] broke or suspended relations during the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) and after the [blockade of the Gaza Strip](19224310.md). Although [Guinea](12176.md) broke diplomatic ties with Israel in 1967, Israel's extensive support for Guinea during [its fight against an Ebola virus epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_epidemic_in_Guinea) led to the re-establishment of bilateral relations in 2016.[^cite_note-haaretz.com-20] Nicaragua restored relations in March 2017; [Chad](5488.md) did likewise in January 2019.[^cite_note-Chad-Israel-21] The most recent country to establish diplomatic relations with Israel was [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), on 12 December 2020.[^cite_note-22]

## United Nations membership

On 15 May 1948, one day after [its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence), [Israel](9282173.md) applied for membership with the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN), but the application was not acted on by the [Security Council](31956.md). Israel's second application was rejected by the Security Council on 17 December 1948 by a 5-to-1 vote, with 5 abstentions. The [First Syrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Syrian_Republic) was the sole negative vote; the United States, Argentina, Colombia, the Soviet Union, and the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) voted in favour; and Belgium, the United Kingdom, Canada, the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), and France abstained.

Israel's application was renewed in 1949 after the [first Israeli election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Israeli_Constituent_Assembly_election). By [Security Council Resolution 69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_69) on 4 March 1949, the UN Security Council voted 9-to-1 in favour of Israeli membership, with the [Kingdom of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Egypt) voting against and the United Kingdom abstaining.[^cite_note-23] Those voting in favour were China, France, the United States, the Soviet Union, Argentina, Canada, the [Republic of Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Cuba_\(1902%E2%80%931959\)), Norway, and the Ukrainian SSR.

On 11 May 1949, the [UN General Assembly](31957.md), by the requisite two-thirds majority of its then-58 members, approved the application to admit Israel to the UN by [General Assembly Resolution 273](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_273).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The vote in the General Assembly was 37 to 12, with 9 abstentions. Those that voted in favour of Israel were: Argentina, Australia, Bolivia, the [Byelorussian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), Canada, Chile, China, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, France, Guatemala, Haiti, Honduras, Iceland, Liberia, Luxembourg, Mexico, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Nicaragua, Norway, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, the Philippines, [communist Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic), the Ukrainian SSR, South Africa, the Soviet Union, the United States, Uruguay, Venezuela, and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). Those that voted against were six of the then-seven members of the [Arab League](52625.md) (Egypt, the [Kingdom of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq), Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, Syria, and the [Kingdom of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutawakkilite_Kingdom_of_Yemen)) as well as the [Kingdom of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Afghanistan), [post-independence Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-independence_Burma_\(1948%E2%80%931962\)), the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire), the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India), [Pahlavi Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran) and the [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan). Those abstaining were: Belgium, [fourth-republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Brazilian_Republic) Brazil, Denmark, El Salvador, the [Kingdom of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece), Thailand (then known as Siam), Sweden, Turkey, and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-UN11May1949-26] Many of the countries that voted in favour or had abstained had already recognized Israel before the UN vote, at least on a *de facto* basis.

## Present situation

As of February 2026, 169 of the 192 other [member states](31969.md) of the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) recognize Israel. [Several states do not recognize Israel](#States_that_do_not_recognize_Israel), including several members of the [Arab League](52625.md) and other non-Arab members of the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md). In 2002, the Arab League proposed the recognition of Israel by [Arab countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) as a pathway towards a resolution of the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md) under the [Arab Peace Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative). Following the [Abraham Accords](64883396.md), which were signed in September 2020 between Israel and the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain, the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) condemned any Arab agreement with Israel as dishonourable, describing them as a betrayal to the [Palestinian cause](47246228.md) and a blow to their quest for an [independent Palestinian state](241405.md).[^cite_note-27]

The passports of some countries are not valid for travel to Israel, including Bangladesh, Brunei, Iran, Iraq, and Pakistan. Thirteen countries [do not accept Israeli passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_passport#Countries_that_do_not_accept_Israeli_passports): Algeria, Bangladesh, Brunei, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Malaysia, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Syria, and Yemen.[^cite_note-visapol-28] Some of these countries also do not accept passports of other countries whose holder has an Israeli visa or stamp on it. The stamp may be a visa stamp, or a stamp on entry or departure. Because of these issues, Israeli immigration controls do not stamp passports with an entry visa, instead issuing a separate insert which is discarded on departure. However, a stamp of another country which indicates that the person has entered Israel may frustrate that effort. For example, if an Egyptian departure stamp is used in any passport at the [Taba Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Border_Crossing), that is an indication that the person entered Israel, and a similar situation arises for land crossings into Jordan. Some countries also ban direct flights and overflights to and from Israel.[^cite_note-29] In August 2020, the United Arab Emirates permitted direct flights from Israel, and Saudi Arabia and Bahrain authorized overflights for such flights.[^cite_note-30] On 8 October 2020, Israel and Jordan reached an agreement to allow flights to cross over both countries' airspace.[^cite_note-31]
