**Nicușor Daniel Dan**[^cite_note-1] (born 20 December 1969) is a Romanian mathematician and politician serving as the seventh [President of Romania](273659.md) since 2025. He previously served as the [Mayor of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bucharest) from 2020 to 2025 and as a member of the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Romania\)) from 2016 to 2020.

Born in [Făgăraș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C4%83g%C4%83ra%C8%99), [Brașov County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bra%C8%99ov_County), Dan studied mathematics at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest) before continuing his studies in France, where he obtained a [Master's degree](170346.md) from the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_normale_sup%C3%A9rieure_\(Paris\)) and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from [Paris 13 University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_13_University). After returning to Romania, he founded the Școala Normală Superioară București, a research institution in mathematics, and participated in civic initiatives concerning urban governance and heritage preservation.

In 2015, Dan founded the [Save Bucharest Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Bucharest_Union) (USB), a political party focused on anti-corruption and urban development. He co-founded the [Save Romania Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Romania_Union) (USR) in 2016 and resigned in 2017 following internal disagreements regarding the party's ideological direction, subsequently advocating a [big tent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_tent) approach centred on institutional reform. He served in the Chamber of Deputies from 2016 until he was elected mayor of Bucharest in 2020.[^cite_note-2] He was re-elected in 2024 and served until he was elected president. As mayor, his administration oversaw public infrastructure projects, municipal financial policy, and administrative reforms.

Dan was elected President of Romania in 2025 as an independent candidate, with second-round support from the USR, the [National Liberal Party](863906.md) (PNL), and the [Democratic Union of Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Union_of_Hungarians_in_Romania) (UDMR), defeating [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion) of the [Alliance for the Union of Romanians](64666536.md) (AUR).[^cite_note-3] He campaigned on a pro-[European Union](9317.md) and pro-[NATO](21133.md) platform. His presidency has been marked by political instability, including the collapse of a governing coalition led by Prime Minister [Ilie Bolojan](78639092.md), president of the PNL, following a [motion of no confidence](208101.md) in May 2026.

In 2026, some commentators described his contacts with the [Social Democratic Party](407177.md) (PSD) leaders and figures within the American [Republican Party](32070.md) as a political realignment towards [euroscepticism](10037.md), a characterisation he disputed while reaffirming Romania's commitments to the EU and NATO.[^cite_note-4]

## Early life and education

Born in [Făgăraș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C4%83g%C4%83ra%C8%99), Brașov County, Dan attended the [Radu Negru High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radu_Negru_National_College) in his native city, graduating in 1988.[^cite_note-5] He came first in the [International Mathematical Olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Olympiad) in 1987 and 1988 with perfect scores.[^cite_note-IMO-6] Dan moved to Bucharest at the age of 18 and began studying mathematics at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest).[^cite_note-minciuni-7]

In 1992, he moved to France to continue studying mathematics; he attended the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Normale_Sup%C3%A9rieure), one of the most prestigious French *[grandes écoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_%C3%A9cole)*, where he earned a master's degree. In 1998 Dan completed a PhD in mathematics at [Paris 13 University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Sorbonne_Paris_Nord), with thesis "Courants de Green et prolongement méromorphe" written under the direction of [Christophe Soulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Soul%C3%A9) and [Daniel Barsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Barsky?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] He returned to Bucharest that year, giving as reasons the cultural differences and the desire to change Romania.[^cite_note-10ani-10]

Dan was one of the creators and the first administrative director of the [Școala Normală Superioară București](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98coala_Normal%C4%83_Superioar%C4%83_Bucure%C8%99ti?action=edit\&redlink=1), a university set up on the model of the French [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Normale_Sup%C3%A9rieure) within the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy)'s [Institute of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematics_of_the_Romanian_Academy).[^cite_note-10ani-10] As of 2011, he was a professor of mathematics at the institute.[^cite_note-piratul-11]

## Activism

In 1998, Dan founded *Asociația "Tinerii pentru Acțiune Civică"* ("Young People for Civic Action" Association), for which he wanted to gather a thousand young people who wanted to change Romania, which was his stated goal for returning to the country.[^cite_note-10ani-10] Despite failing in its goals, the association did organise two forums for young people who studied abroad, in 2000 and 2002, to which a few hundred people participated. As a result of these forums, the "Ad Astra" Association of Romanian researchers was created in 2000.[^cite_note-10ani-10]

### Save Bucharest Association

Dan founded the *Asociația "Salvați Bucureștiul"* ("Save Bucharest" Association) in 2006 as a reaction to the demolition of architectural heritage houses and the building of high-rise buildings in protected Bucharest neighborhoods, as well as the diminishing number of [green space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_infrastructure) areas in Bucharest.[^cite_note-10ani-10]

In March 2008, the association published the "Bucharest, an urbanistic disaster" Report, which discussed Bucharest's problems and ways to overcome them. In the same year, during the elections, together with other [NGOs](46539.md), the association drafted a *Pact for Bucharest*, which was signed by all the candidates for mayor of Bucharest.[^cite_note-10ani-10] On [April Fools' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Fools'_Day) in 2012, Dan published a list of 100 electoral promises made by elected mayor of Bucharest [Sorin Oprescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorin_Oprescu) which were not kept, including the "Pact for Bucharest".[^cite_note-minciuni-7][^cite_note-12]

The association was involved in many court cases, winning 23 against the local authorities of Bucharest. Among them are the cancellation of a project which would have built a [water park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_park) on 7 hectares of [Tineretului Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tineretului_Park),[^cite_note-minciuni-7] saving from demolition a number of heritage buildings on [Șoseaua Kiseleff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98oseaua_Kiseleff) no. 45, and the cancellation of a project which would have built a glass building on top of Palatul Știrbei on [Calea Victoriei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calea_Victoriei). The association was also able to push for some changes in 2009 to the [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) law.[^cite_note-minciuni-7]

## Early political career

### 2012 local elections



Dan on a bicycle during his electoral campaign for Mayor of Bucharest in 2012



Volunteers in Dan's campaign carrying the signatures to the Electoral Bureau

Dan announced his candidacy for [Mayor of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bucharest) in November 2011 at a café on Arthur Verona Street, with just a few guests, including [Theodor Paleologu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Paleologu), a historian and Member of Parliament.[^cite_note-piratul-11] For gathering the 36,000 signatures needed for his candidacy, having the backing of no party, he relied on a network of volunteers organised on [Facebook](7529378.md). On 22 April 15 bands and musicians performed pro-bono at [Arenele Romane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenele_Romane?action=edit\&redlink=1) for Dan's campaign in order to help him gather the signatures.[^cite_note-13] During the 12-hour-long concert, volunteers gathered 4,000 signatures.[^cite_note-14]

#### Political positions and programme

Among his proposed projects are the creation of a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) infrastructure over the existing rail lines in Bucharest, creating an infrastructure for [prioritising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_signal_preemption) [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) over other traffic in intersections, consolidating buildings that are likely to be affected by earthquakes, protecting the urban green space and clearing illegal buildings from parks.[^cite_note-piratul-11] Dan argues that it is important to incentivise young people to stay in the city, by making it a regional hub in IT, [creative industries](674483.md) and [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education), and attracting investors and skilled people from across the region.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

#### Support and opinions on his candidacy

Dan received support from [Andrei Pleșu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Ple%C8%99u), who argued that Dan was the only one of the candidates who was interested in the architecture of Bucharest and did not support any utopian initiatives.[^cite_note-17] He also received support from political scientist and Member of the European Parliament [Cristian Preda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Preda).[^cite_note-18] Dan gained the support of some journalists who wrote about him in [op-eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) from several newspapers: Andrei Crăciun of *[Adevărul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adev%C4%83rul)* saw in him "a [Don Quijote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Quixote) untouched by the vulgar lard of undeserved riches" and "a person who works against the system".[^cite_note-19] Florin Negruțiu, the editor-in-chief of *[Gândul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A2ndul)* thought he was an "atypical candidate" for Bucharest, the model candidate of the intellectuals; nevertheless, the journalist did not see any chances that Dan would become mayor, because he was "too serious" a candidate, and unlikely to appeal to the masses.[^cite_note-20] [Neculai Constantin Munteanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neculai_Constantin_Munteanu) from *[Radio Free Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty)* wrote that he supported Dan for his unselfish way of caring about Bucharest and that his opponents were "comedians", from whom one could "admire the imposture, ludicrousness, and incompetence".[^cite_note-europalibera-21]

### 2016 local elections

Having registered [Save Bucharest Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Bucharest_Union) (USB) as a political party in 2015, Dan ran again for [Mayor of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bucharest) in 2016.[^cite_note-22] This time, the [2016 Romanian local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Romanian_local_elections) were held in a single round. He gained 30.52% of the total votes, losing to the social-democrat candidate, [Gabriela Firea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Firea), who gained 42.97% of the total votes.[^cite_note-gandul-23] In the election, Dan managed to attract the young electorate, with over half of his voters being under the age of 40.[^cite_note-24] Some of USB's candidates for [sector mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectors_of_Bucharest) have also performed well in their respective races, proving USB's viability as a future political force.[^cite_note-25]

### Save Romania Union

Wanting to capitalise on the momentum that saw him gain a third of the votes in the local elections, Dan announced shortly after the 2016 local election that the Save Bucharest Union would change its name to [Save Romania Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Romania_Union) (USR), shifting its focus to a national stage. He also announced plans for the new party to enter the [2016 Romanian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Romanian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] With Dan at the top of the candidate list, USR gained 8.92% of the vote in the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) race and 8.87% in the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Romania\)), which made them the third largest party in Romania.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The result also meant that Dan became a member of the Chamber of Deputies.

#### Departure from USR

In 2017, anti-same-sex NGO [Coaliția pentru Familie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coali%C8%9Bia_pentru_Familie) managed to raise the necessary number of signatures to organise a referendum that would change the part of the [Romanian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Romania) dealing with marriage, with the hope of redefining it as "between a man and a woman".[^cite_note-30] This created a rift within USR, between the progressive wing, who wanted USR to become the only parliamentary party to oppose the initiative, and Dan, who believed USR should not get involved in the debate and that the party should remain open for both progressives and conservatives.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] An internal referendum within the party followed, in which 52.7% of members voted to position the party against the Constitutional initiative, which led Dan to resign from the party on 1 June 2017.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] As explanation for his opposition to the National Council vote he cited religious matters, the dangers of deviating from the main party issue of [fighting against corruption](41107761.md#Anti-corruption_measures) and his refusal to belong to a party that defines itself as a party of civil liberties.[^cite_note-35]

### Independent

After his resignation from USR, Dan continued to serve as a member of the Chamber of Deputies as an independent. Due to a quirk in the Romanian electoral law, USR required his signature when they attempted to legally register their alliance ([2020 USR-PLUS Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_USR-PLUS_Alliance)) with the [Freedom, Unity and Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom,_Unity_and_Solidarity_Party) (PLUS). In order to help his former party, in March 2019 Dan briefly rejoined USR as a common member, gave the necessary signature and then resigned for a second time.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-\:0-37]

## Mayor of Bucharest (2020–2025)

### 2020 local elections

In May 2019, Dan announced his plans to once again run for Mayor of Bucharest, as an independent. Dan mentioned that while he hoped that his candidacy would be supported by the rest of the opposition parties, he would not run against a different common candidate, unwilling to split the vote of the opposition.[^cite_note-\:0-37] He was ultimately supported by both USR and the [National Liberal Party](863906.md) (PNL). With 95% of votes counted, partial results suggested that he won the mayoral election with 42.8% of votes. Shortly afterwards exit polls showed him winning the race, he announced victory.[^cite_note-38] On 5 October 2020 the Central Electoral Bureau confirmed his status as the new Mayor of Bucharest, winning the elections with a plurality of 42.81% against [Gabriela Firea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Firea) (37.97%), the former Mayor.[^cite_note-hotnews.ro-39]

### 2024 local elections

Following the decision made by the governing alliance between the [National Liberal Party](863906.md) (PNL) and the [Social Democratic Party](407177.md) (PSD) to hold the elections in June 2024, Dan participated once again as an independent for Mayor, for a new term. This time, he was supported by the same USR (Save Romanian Union) party, but also by two other minor parties, the [People's Movement Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Movement_Party) (PMP) and [Force of the Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_of_the_Right) (FD) whose president is former PNL leader [Ludovic Orban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovic_Orban), who left the party in 2021 after losing the presidency of the party to then-prime minister [Florin Cîțu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florin_C%C3%AE%C8%9Bu); all three formed the [United Right Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Right_Alliance) (ADU), an official national opposition to the [National Coalition for Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_for_Romania) (CNR) formed by the PSD and PNL. Additionally, the [Renewing Romania's European Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewing_Romania's_European_Project) (REPER), headed by former PLUS leader [Dacian Cioloș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacian_Ciolo%C8%99), supported Dan, but was not part of ADU. The elections were held on 9 June 2024 together with the [2024 European Parliament election in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Romania), a controversial move done by the CNR earlier that year. Thought to be a close race up until the last moment, the exit polls showed the result was overwhelmingly in favour of Dan, winning with 45% of the total vote, who declared himself the winner of the race. After the vote count, Dan was the clear winner of the elections with approximately 48% of the total votes, more than double the votes given to the same runner-up from 2020, [Gabriela Firea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Firea), who placed second with 22%, followed by then [Sector 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_5_\(Bucharest\)) Mayor, [Cristian Popescu Piedone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Popescu_Piedone) (16%) and PNL candidate and president for the Bucharest branch of the party, [Sebastian Burduja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Burduja) (7.6%).[^cite_note-40]

During his victory speech, Dan declared his intention to organise two referendums for Bucharest, one for centralising more power to the General Mayor of Bucharest regarding building authorisations, a constant theme during his campaign, and another for allocating more financial funds to the General Mayor rather than to the Sector mayors. Both were planned to take place on the same day as the [2024 Romanian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_parliamentary_election) in order to "reduce organisational costs for separate elections" according to Dan [^cite_note-41]

#### Piața Unirii incident

On 14 October 2024, around midnight, [Sector 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_4_\(Bucharest\)) mayor [Daniel Băluță](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_B%C4%83lu%C8%9B%C4%83?action=edit\&redlink=1) (PSD) – with permission from Sector 3 mayor [Robert Negoiță](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Negoi%C8%9B%C4%83) – sent multiple construction workers and Sector 4 local police agents to Unirii Park in order to start proceedings for the Square's foundation's consolidation. The Piața Unirii is shared between Sector 3 and Sector 4. In the morning, Dan went to the square together with his staff, telling the workers present to halt the procedure on the basis of its illegality due to a lack of permits. Allegedly, [Metrorex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest_Metro) and Apa Nova (Bucharest's water and sewage administration institution) had yet to give their approval for the consolidation work.[^cite_note-42]

A conflict erupted between the Sector 4 local police agents (including its director, [Cristian Pîslă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_P%C3%AEsl%C4%83?action=edit\&redlink=1), subsequently suspected of corruption[^cite_note-43]) and Dan and his staff. Eventually, the Bucharest mayor returned with additional documents attesting to the fact that the City Hall of Bucharest was the legal owner of Unirii Square and the sector City Halls had no right to start consolidation proceedings on their own.[^cite_note-44] During the scuffles, local police agents were observed to be especially violent, which the wide public of Bucharest viewed as proof of the agents being members of the Sector 4 [Clanul Sportivilor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clanul_Sportivilor?action=edit\&redlink=1), an organisation of the [Romanian mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_mafia) operating mainly in the southern part of Bucharest who were long suspected to work with Daniel Băluță himself.[^cite_note-45] The then Romanian prime minister [Marcel Ciolacu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Ciolacu) intervened in the matter prompting mayor Băluță to concede; Nicușor Dan launched an investigation into the proceedings. He once again stressed the importance on the referendum held on 9 June 2024 for centralising more power to the Bucharest mayor.

## 2025 presidential campaign

After the Piața Unirii incident, Dan was viewed even more favourably by the general populace of Bucharest,**[^cite_note-46]** being called a bulwark against the widespread corruption of the country and the only one to effectively stand against the PSD-PNL coalition. This led to speculation of a possible presidential candidature in the next elections. On 16 December he announced his candidacy for the [2025 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Romanian_presidential_election),[^cite_note-47] after the annulment of the [2024 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_presidential_election) due to [Russian meddling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusations_of_Russian_interference_in_the_2024_Romanian_presidential_election) in favour of winner of the first round [Călin Georgescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Georgescu).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

&#x20;His announcement came as a surprise to many, as he had previously expressed his intention to serve at least one more term as Mayor of Bucharest before the elections, stating that he "would need at least 2-3 terms to make everything right in Bucharest."[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] This change of plans also led to a falling out with Elena Lasconi, a former supporter of Dan, who came second in the annulled 2024 elections' first round. It is widely believed that Lasconi and Dan appealed to similar voter demographics, with both targeting liberal, progressive, moderate, pro-European, and anti-PSD/anti-PNL camps. As a result, their simultaneous candidacies may have divided this voter base.[^cite_note-auto-53] The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Romania) validated his candidacy on 16 March along with those of [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion) and [Victor Ponta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Ponta).[^cite_note-auto-53] On 22 March, a random draw placed Dan at the [bottom of the candidate list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot_order_effect) on the ballot.[^cite_note-54] Dan came second in the first round of voting on 4 May with 20.99% of the vote. On 18 May, he faced [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion) in a runoff, winning the presidency with 53.6% of the vote.[^cite_note-\:1-55]

## Presidency (2025–present)

Main article: [Presidency of Nicușor Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Nicu%C8%99or_Dan)

Dan was inaugurated as president on 26 May 2025. In his inaugural address, he pledged to deal with Romania's economic problems while acknowledging that "the Romanian state is spending more than it can afford”. He also pledged to become a president “open to the voice of society.”[^cite_note-56] On 20 June 2025, Dan nominated his immediate predecessor as president, [Ilie Bolojan](78639092.md), to become prime minister,[^cite_note-57] and to form the [Bolojan cabinet](80266502.md). During the first weeks of his tenure, Dan was exclusively focused on the formation of the new government. Negotiations were noted to have been a lot longer than in previous years, something that was both praised and criticised by both Romanians and his peers.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Throughout those respective weeks, the newly elected president's image was that of an active and communicative one, oftentimes being greeted by reporters and asked questions in the early morning, right at the gate of the house he and his family were tenants of. Another aspect that painted him in a positive light to the public was that he preferred to take his daughter to school on foot from his house, only accompanied by a [SPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_and_Guard_Service) agent. This was a point of friction between him and the protection service due to complications arising in the security procedures regarding the president.[^cite_note-61]

Internationally, Dan's presidency brought some stability in the European Union and NATO, especially concerning their Eastern border. Many analysts opined that Klaus Iohannis' inactivity and corruption scandals sidelined the state in the last months of his presidency, as well as the internal crisis that was caused by the annulment of the 2024 election, which was marred by suspicions of Russian interference;[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] as such, Romania's internal and international situation was ambiguous and the country was not invited to a security summit in Paris in February 2025.[^cite_note-64] His election also marked a steady increase in appreciation of the Romanian currency against the Euro after weeks of increases due to market uncertainty in late Spring of 2025, together with a decrease in investment risk for foreign firms.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] He signed into law substantial legislation since taking office, concerning health system reform,[^cite_note-68] removing housing subsidies for senators and deputies,[^cite_note-69] foreign official corruption,[^cite_note-70] fiscal and budgetary reforms agreed upon by the government in the context of economic rebound,[^cite_note-71] and environmental and agricultural regulation.[^cite_note-72]

## Political positions

### Geopolitical alignment

Dan has consistently advocated [pro-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Western) views, emphasising Romania's integration into the [European Union](9317.md) and [NATO](21133.md) as cornerstones of national security and economic progress. In his 2025 presidential campaign, Dan positioned himself as a staunch defender of Western democratic values; he supported NATO's presence in Romania, particularly amid the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), contrasting sharply with Simion's [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) and [Euroscepticism](10037.md).[^cite_note-73] Dan is a [Ukrainophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainophilia) and advocates for stronger Romanian support to Ukraine.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Dan faced accusations over his ties with businessman [Matei Păun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matei_P%C4%83un?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was linked to Russian and Belarusian firms and oligarchs. Păun's firm, BAC Financial Advisory SRL, acquired Getica OOH in 2011 from News Corp via Russian [VTB Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTB_Bank) and Alpha Capital Partners. Păun allegedly boasted of financing Belarusian leader [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md) and made statements questioning [2014 Western sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) on [Russia](25391.md), praising [Russian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) "mysticism", and doubting [Ukraine](31750.md)'s [Orange Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Revolution) and [Crimea's annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation).[^cite_note-77]

### LGBT rights

In 2000, Dan published an article in the magazine *[Dilema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilema_veche)* in which he stated his rejection of "homosexual behaviour [in public spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_display_of_affection) in Romania", describing it as "an attack against [traditional values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_conservatism)" and "legitimate [collective identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_identity)". The statements resurfaced after his political career took off, particularly during his much-publicised departure from USR. Dan distanced himself from his previous statements on several occasions, claiming that he is not [homophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobic) and that his opinion on the matter has changed in the following years.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] In 2024, Dan expressed support for [same-sex civil partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_civil_partnerships); however, he also believed that it was not the correct time to have a debate. In the same interview, he did not take a stance on [same-sex marriage](92656.md), and said that it was "a debate that society must have".[^cite_note-80]

### Alleged Securitate collaboration

In May 2024, ahead of local elections, a purported [Securitate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securitate) document from July 1988 emerged, detailing Dan's collaboration with the secret police of the [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu)'s regime. The document contained information provided by Dan about his high school peers who participated in the International Mathematical Olympiads of 1987 and 1988. Dan denied its authenticity, claiming he had minimal contact with authorities of the time. PSD leader and Prime Minister [Marcel Ciolacu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Ciolacu) questioned the document's credibility, noting its unusually polished composition. The National Council for the Study of the Securitate Archives (CNSAS) deemed it a forgery, citing incorrect dates, atypical expressions, and a writing style inconsistent with the typewriters used by the Securitate, including the absence of diacritics typical in genuine documents of the era.[^cite_note-81]

## Personal life

Dan lives with his long-term partner, Mirabela, a [Renault](162292.md) executive. They had a daughter in May 2016 and a son in May 2022.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]
