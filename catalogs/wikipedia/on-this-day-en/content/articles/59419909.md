**Khaled Ahmed El-Enany Ali Ezz** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): خالد العناني; born 14 March 1971) is an Egyptian [Egyptologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptology) and politician who was elected as the 12th Director-General of the [United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Educational,_Scientific_and_Cultural_Organization) (UNESCO) in November 2025. He previously served as [Minister of Tourism and Antiquities](40143408.md) of [Egypt](8087628.md) between 2016 and 2022.

## Early life

Khaled El-Enany was born on 14 March 1971 in [Giza](144514.md), Egypt, and grew up in [Roda Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roda_Island) in Cairo.[^cite_note-forbes-1][^cite_note-report-2] He is the son of an engineer and a French teacher.[^cite_note-report-2] He attended a French-speaking secondary school, where he passed his [baccalauréat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalaur%C3%A9at) in 1988.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-report-2]

He studied [Egyptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptology) at [Helwan University(now Capital university)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_University_\(Egypt\)) and early worked as a [tourist guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_guide).[^cite_note-vanguardia-5][^cite_note-report-2] He later obtained a [MAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Advanced_Studies) in 1996 and PhD at the [Paul Valéry University Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Val%C3%A9ry_University_Montpellier_3) in 2001, both in Egyptology.[^cite_note-auto1-6][^cite_note-report-2]

## Career

### Academic

He became a professor of Egyptology at the University of Helwan.[^cite_note-forbes-1] He also teaches the [Ancient Egyptian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_language) at several universities around the world, including the [University of Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Palermo) and Paul-Valéry-Montpellier University.[^cite_note-\:0-3] In addition, he became a member of several scientific institutions, notably being a corresponding member of the [German Archaeological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Archaeological_Institute) in Berlin, as well as an associate researcher and member of the board of directors of the [French Institute of Oriental Archeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Institute_of_Oriental_Archeology) (IFAO) since 2002.[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-unesco-7][^cite_note-report-2]

In October 2014, he became director-general of the [National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Egyptian_Civilisation) (NMEC).[^cite_note-auto2-8][^cite_note-report-2] Between 2015 and 2016 he was director-general of the [Egyptian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum) in Cairo.[^cite_note-report-2]

### Minister of Antiquities of Egypt (2016–2019)

On 23 March 2016, al-Anani became the Minister of Antiquities in the government of [Sherif Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherif_Ismail).[^cite_note-auto2-8][^cite_note-independent-9]

When the government of [Mostafa Madbouly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Madbouly) was appointed in 2018, he retained his post.[^cite_note-independent-9] [Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism_\(Egypt\)) was added to his portfolio during the reshuffle of 22 December 2019, to become the [Minister of Tourism and Antiquities](40143408.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-independent-9]



Khaled al-Anani at the International Congress of Egyptologists in Giza (November 2019)

During his tenure, he managed to secure funds to build the [Grand Egyptian Museum](5359115.md), and renovate other historical buildings such as the [Baron Empain Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_Empain_Palace), [Graeco-Roman Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graeco-Roman_Museum) and [Eliyahu Hanavi Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliyahu_Hanavi_Synagogue).[^cite_note-auto1-6] He also oversaw projects such as the [Pharaohs' Golden Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaohs'_Golden_Parade) and the reopening of the restored [Avenue of Sphinxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenue_of_Sphinxes) in Luxor in 2021 and had to face the consequences of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Egypt) affecting the Egyptian tourism sector.[^cite_note-report-2][^cite_note-11]

He was replaced by [Ahmed Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Issa_\(Egyptian_politician\)) as Minister of Tourism and Antiquities in a cabinet reshuffle on 13 August 2022.[^cite_note-12]

He also organises press conferences following archaeological discoveries to promote tourism.[^cite_note-13] In addition, he aims to combat [extremism](8734632.md) by encouraging the Egyptian population to take a greater interest in [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) history.[^cite_note-14]

In 2024, he was designated UN Tourism Ambassador for Cultural Tourism.[^cite_note-15] In January 2025, he was designated as the rapporteur of the African World Heritage Fund.[^cite_note-16]

### Director-General of UNESCO (2025–present)

El-Enany was presented as Egypt's candidate for Director-General of [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 2023.[^cite_note-vanguardia-5] His candidacy was supported by the [African Union](10634933.md), the [Arab League](52625.md) and several countries including France, Spain, Turkey, Gabon, Germany and Brazil.[^cite_note-report-2][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

On 6 October 2025, UNESCO's Executive Board proposed him as the new Director-General of UNESCO, receiving 55 votes out of 57 and beating the [Republic of Congo](19599929.md)'s candidate [Firmin Edouard Matoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmin_Edouard_Matoko).[^cite_note-jazeera-19][^cite_note-reuters-20] He was formally elected by the General Conference on 6 November 2025 held in [Samarkand](51491.md), [Uzbekistan](31853.md).[^cite_note-unesco-7][^cite_note-barron-21] He became the first Arab to led the agency and the second from Africa after Senegal's [Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou-Mahtar_M'Bow), and will succeed [Audrey Azoulay](49501745.md) on 15 November 2025.[^cite_note-jazeera-19][^cite_note-vanguardia-5][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-barron-21] Among the challenges he will encounter during his term of office will be the political and financial impact of the [withdrawal of the United States from UNESCO](14863992.md), scheduled to take place in December 2026.[^cite_note-arabweekly-23]

His election was criticised by 50 cultural heritage groups and experts for failing to properly manage Egypt's heritage during his tenure as Minister of Antiquities, for allowing, for example, the demolition of parts of the [City of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_the_Dead_\(Cairo\)) in Cairo and the construction of tourism infrastructure near [Saint Catherine's Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Catherine's_Monastery) in Sinai, which are both UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-modern-24][^cite_note-arabweekly-23] They argued that he was not a fit person to lead UNESCO.[^cite_note-modern-24]
