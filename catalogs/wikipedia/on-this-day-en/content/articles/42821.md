**La Paz** (Spanish: [\[la ˈpas\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); lit. 'the peace'), officially **Nuestra Señora de La Paz** ([Aymara:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_language) **Chuqi Yapu** Aymara pronunciation: [\[ˈtʃoqɛ ˈjapʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)), is the seat of government of [Bolivia](3462.md). With 755,732 residents as of 2024,[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:0-7] it is the [third-most populous city in Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bolivian_cities_by_population). Its metropolitan area, which includes the neighboring city of [El Alto](3475756.md), and other smaller towns, is the second most populous urban area in Bolivia, with a population of 2.2 million, after [Santa Cruz de la Sierra](673987.md) with a population of 2.3 million.[^cite_note-\:0-7] The city is also the capital of the [department of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz_Department,_Bolivia).

Located in west-central Bolivia 68 km (42 mi) southeast of [Lake Titicaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Titicaca), La Paz is set in a canyon created by the [Choqueyapu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choqueyapu_River). It is in a bowl-like depression, part of the Amazon basin, surrounded by the high mountains of the [Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano). Overlooking the city is the triple-peaked [Illimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illimani). Its peaks are always snow-covered and can be seen from many parts of the city. At an elevation of roughly 3,650 m (11,975 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level), La Paz is the [highest Administrative capital city in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capital_cities_by_altitude).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Due to its altitude, the city has an unusual [subtropical highland climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), with rainy summers and dry winters.

La Paz was founded on 20 October 1548, by the Spanish [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) Captain [Alonso de Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_de_Mendoza), at the site of the [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire) settlement of [Laja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laja,_Bolivia) as a connecting point between the commercial routes that led from [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD) and [Oruro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oruro,_Bolivia) to [Lima](85423.md); the full name of the city was originally *Nuestra Señora de La Paz* (meaning *[Our Lady of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Peace)*) in commemoration of the restoration of peace following the [insurrection](513246.md) of [Gonzalo Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Pizarro) and fellow conquistadors against the first [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy) of [Peru](170691.md). The city was later moved to its present location in the valley of Chuquiago Marka.[^cite_note-10] La Paz was under Spanish colonial rule as part of the [Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata), before Bolivia gained independence. Since its founding, the city was the site of numerous revolts. In 1781, the [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people) leader and independence activist [Túpac Katari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BApac_Katari) laid siege to the city for a total of six months, but was finally defeated. On 16 July 1809, the Bolivian patriot [Pedro Domingo Murillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Domingo_Murillo) ignited a revolution for independence, marking the beginning of the [Spanish American Wars of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence), which gained the freedom of South American states in 1821.[^cite_note-11]

As the seat of the government of Bolivia, La Paz is the site of the [Palacio Quemado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_Quemado), the presidential palace. It is also the seat of the Bolivian [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), the [Plurinational Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurinational_Legislative_Assembly), and numerous government departments and agencies. The constitutional capital of Bolivia, [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre), retains the [judicial power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary).[^cite_note-12] The city hosts all the [foreign embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_Bolivia) as well as international missions in the country. La Paz is an important political, administrative, economic, and sports center of Bolivia; it generates 24% of the nation's [gross domestic product](12594.md) and serves as the headquarters for numerous Bolivian companies and industries.[^cite_note-13]

La Paz is also an important cultural center of [South America](26769.md), as it hosts several landmarks dating from colonial times, such as the [San Francisco Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Church_\(La_Paz\)), the [Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Peace,_La_Paz), the [Plaza Murillo](34998500.md) and Jaén Street. La Paz is also situated at the confluence of archaeological regions of the [Tiwanaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwanaku) and [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire). The city is renowned for its markets, particularly the [Witches' Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witches'_Market), and for its nightlife.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Its topography offers views of the city and the surrounding mountains of the [Cordillera Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Real_\(Bolivia\)) from numerous natural [viewing points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlook). La Paz is home to the largest [urban cable car network](298681.md) in the world.[^cite_note-16]

## Name and toponyms



Calle Jaén in the historic center of La Paz, contains well-preserved [Spanish Colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonial_architecture) buildings.

The Spanish conquistadors founded the city in 1548 with the name *Nuestra Señora de La Paz* (meaning *[Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary) of [Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace)*). The name commemorated the restoration of peace following the insurrection of [Gonzalo Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Pizarro) and fellow conquistadors four years earlier against [Blasco Núñez Vela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasco_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_Vela), the first [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy) of [Peru](170691.md).[^cite_note-Crespo-1-17]

In 1825, after the decisive victory of the [republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism) at [Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayacucho) over the Spanish army in the course of the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence), the city's full name was changed to *La Paz de Ayacucho* (meaning *The Peace of Ayacucho*).

The region in which La Paz is located in was known as Chuquiago Marka (*marka* means village;[^cite_note-18] *Chuqi Yapu* means 'gold farm', probably due to the exploitation of gold nuggets in the small rivers of the place;[^cite_note-19] spelling differences due to Aymara writing compared to Spanish transliteration) in the Aymara language, leading former [President of Bolivia](188806.md) [Evo Morales](284163.md) to suggest renaming the city to "Chuquiago Marka" in a speech in 2017.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## History

Main article: [History of La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_La_Paz)



View of La Paz between 1909 and 1920

This area had been the site of an Inca city on a major trading route.

Although the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) [conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) entered the area in 1535, they did not find La Paz until 1548. Originally it was to be at the site of the [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(Americas\)) settlement, [Laja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laja,_Bolivia). The town site was moved a few days later to its present location in the valley of Chuquiago, which is more clement.[^cite_note-Crespo-1-17]

Control over the former [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca) lands had been entrusted to [Pedro de la Gasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_la_Gasca) by the Spanish king (and Holy Roman Emperor) [Emperor Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). Gasca commanded [Alonso de Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_de_Mendoza) to found a new city commemorating the end of the civil wars in Peru; the city of La Paz was founded on 20 October 1548, by Alonzo de Mendoza, with Juan de Vargas appointed as its first mayor.[^cite_note-Crespo-2-22]

In 1549, Juan Gutierrez Paniagua was commanded to design an urban plan that would designate sites for public areas, plazas, official buildings, and a cathedral. These were meant to express the ideals and relationships of Spanish colonial society. La Plaza de los Españoles, which is known today as the Plaza Murillo, was chosen as the location for government buildings as well as the Metropolitan Cathedral.

[Spain](26667.md) controlled La Paz with a firm grip and the Spanish king had the last word in all matters political, but consultation was extended, taking months or longer by sea. Indigenous and other unrest was repeated around the turn of the nineteenth century. In 1781, for a total of six months, a group of [Aymara people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_people) laid siege to La Paz. Under the leadership of [Tupac Katari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac_Katari), they destroyed churches and government property. Thirty years later Indians conducted a two-month siege against La Paz. This incident was the setting for the origin of the legend of the [Ekeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekeko). In 1809, the struggle for independence from the Spanish rule brought uprisings against the royalist forces. On 16 July 1809, [Pedro Domingo Murillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Domingo_Murillo) said that the Bolivian revolution was igniting a lamp that nobody would be able to turn off. This uprising formally marked the beginning of the liberation of South America from Spain. The first open rebellions against the Spanish Crown took place in La Paz and the city of Sucre simultaneously. This event is known as the Primer Grito Libertario de América and brought about the [Bolivian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_War_of_Independence).



[Plaza Murrillo](34998500.md), the city's main square, seen in 1987.

Pedro Domingo Murillo was hanged at the Plaza de los Españoles several months later, on 29 January 1810. After Bolivia gained independence, La Paz named this plaza after him, to commemorate him always. He is remembered as the voice of revolution across South America.

In 1898, La Paz was made the *[de facto](43594.md)* seat of the national government, with Sucre remaining the nominal historical as well as judiciary capital. This change reflected the shift of the Bolivian economy away from the largely exhausted [silver](27119.md) [mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) of [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD) to the exploitation of [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) near [Oruro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oruro,_Bolivia), and resulting shifts in the distribution of economic and political power among various national [elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite).[^cite_note-23]

The [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) of [Cholitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_boliviana?action=edit\&redlink=1) (women of [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean) indigenous background) continued until the 1980s. They were not allowed to enter certain public places and were banned from cinemas and some restaurants, with more emphasis in Bolivian eastern cities like [Santa Cruz De La Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_De_La_Sierra).[^cite_note-24]

## Geography



La Paz is characterized by its mountainous geography, the snow-capped [Illimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illimani) dominates the city's skyline

La Paz is in the valleys of [the Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Andes), close to the Eastern split of the [Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano) region. The metropolitan area is near to the [ Cordillera Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Real_\(Bolivia\)) range, which includes such notable mountains as [Illimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illimani) (guardian of La Paz), [Mururata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mururata), [Huayna Potosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huayna_Potosi), [Chacaltaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chacaltaya), [Kunturiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunturiri_\(Los_Andes\)), [Llamp'u](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llamp'u), [Chachakumani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachakumani), [Chearoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chearoco), and [Ancohuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancohuma).&#x20;

La Paz is built in a canyon created by the [Choqueyapu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choqueyapu_River) (now mostly built over), which runs northwest to southeast. The city's main thoroughfare, which roughly follows the river, changes names over its length, but the central tree-lined section running through the downtown core is called the Prado.

The geography of La Paz (in particular the altitude) is marked by social differences. The more affluent residents live in the lower, central areas of the city southwest of the Prado. Many middle-class residents live in high-rise condos near the center. Lower-income residents live in makeshift brick houses in the surrounding hills. The satellite city of El Alto, where the airport is, is spread over a broad area to the west of the canyon, on the Altiplano.

An [earthquake in July 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Bolivia_earthquake) rated at 8.2 struck 200 miles (322 km) north of La Paz. It could be felt near La Paz and caused damage throughout the villages of the area. In February 2002 the city was subjected to a hail and rainstorm that resulted in [flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_La_Paz_floods) causing serious damage and over 50 deaths.[^cite_note-25]

At more than 4,000 meters (13,000 ft) above sea level, higher parts of La Paz have a [cold subtropical highland climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate#Subpolar_oceanic_and_cold_subtropical_highland_varieties_\(Cfc,_Cwc\)) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cwc*), bordering on a [tundra climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *ET*).[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-26] At this altitude, night-time temperatures range from cold to very cold. [Snow flurries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_flurry) can occur in winter, especially at dawn and usually melts before noon. Despite being located only 16 degrees from the equator, this part of the city has average temperatures similar to that of cities such as [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen), [Norway](21241.md) or [Tórshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B3rshavn), [Faroe Islands](17238607.md).

Temperatures in central La Paz, at 3,600 meters (11,811 feet), and in the *[Zona Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calacoto)* (Southern Zone), at 3,250 m (10,663 ft) above sea level are warmer and are classified as [subtropical highland climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate#Subtropical_highland_variety_\(Cfb,_Cwb\)) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cwb*).[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-26] Heavy precipitation typically occurs throughout summer, and often causes destructive [mudslides](2431323.md). At an altitude of 3,250 meters, in the southern part of the city, the wettest month is January with a monthly average of 114 mm (4.5 in), and the driest is July with 8 mm (0.3 in).

The whole city has rainy summers and dry winters. The city has a relatively dry climate, with rainfall occurring mainly in the slightly warmer months of November to March. Heavy precipitation typically occurs throughout summer, and often causes destructive [mudslides](2431323.md). The wettest month is January with a monthly average of 100–140 mm (3.9–5.5 in) of precipitation, and the driest months are June and July with around 5 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation.

In the highest part of the city, above 4,000 meters, the two cloudiest months are February and March, in late summer, when sunshine averages around 5 hours per day. The two sunniest months are June and July, in winter, when sunshine averages around 8 hours per day.[^cite_note-27]

### Main neighborhoods and zones

San Jorge



Aerial view of San Jorge in the Cotahuma district

Located in the district known as Cotahuma and near [Sopocachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopocachi), is one of the main residential and diplomatic areas of the city. San Jorge was one of the most exclusive neighborhoods of La Paz and the financial center of the metropolis, housing international firms like [Deloitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte), [Bank of America](347756.md), [Ernst & Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_&_Young), [BBVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA) and the famous [Ritz Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ritz-Carlton_Hotel_Company). It is now home of some of Bolivia's tallest building known as Torre Girasoles, Torres del Poeta, and the only [intelligent building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_building) of the country, known as Torre Azul. The neighborhood is also populated with expensive offices, renowned restaurants, museums and bookstores. Its Avenida Arce, one of the main streets of the city, is the highest-priced street in the country and the one with the most upscale boutiques in Bolivia. San Jorge is home to the embassies of the [United States](3434750.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Japan](15573.md), [Germany](11867.md) and [Spain](26667.md). The offices of the [World Bank](36752.md), the [Inter-American Development Bank](382592.md), the [CAF – Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_%E2%80%93_Development_Bank_of_Latin_America_and_the_Caribbean), the [Goethe Institut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_Institut), the [Alliance Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Fran%C3%A7aise) and the [Dante Alighieri Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri_Society) are also located in this modern neighborhood.

[Sopocachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopocachi)



[Plaza Abaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Abaroa) in the neighborhood of Sopocachi.

Located in the 1st District (Cotahuma), Sopocachi is probably one of the oldest residential neighborhoods, 10 minutes from the center of the city. Despite the expansion and development of the area, this quarter maintained its residential property. In the last years, there has been an important commercial expansion, mainly on the surroundings of [Abaroa Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Abaroa), one of the many squares and parks of the zone.

San Pedro

Located in the 1st District (Cotahuma), on the right bank of the Choqueyapu River and built around the "Plaza de San Pedro" (official name: Plaza Sucre, *Sucre Square*), is home to numerous shops, businesses and small enterprises, especially printing, spare parts and auto maintenance and repair shops. San Pedro's "Rodriguez Market" remains as one of the most popular [middle-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-class) and oldest of the city. The [San Pedro prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_prison) is here.

Centro



The [Basilica of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Francisco,_La_Paz) in the city center

The city's downtown area, in the 7th District, comprising the center of La Paz and principal roads of the city, like Arce Avenue, 16 July Avenue (also known as "Prado Avenue"), Mariscal Santa Cruz Avenue and Camacho Avenue – the last one being the home of the headquarters of the principal banks and companies of the country.

Casco Viejo

Located in the 7th District, is the historic and ancient center of La Paz. It now houses museums, hotels, shops and buildings as the Mayor City of La Paz and the [Central Bank of Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Bolivia). In the Old Quarter is the [Plaza Murillo](34998500.md), which is home to the Government Palace and the National Congress.

Miraflores



The Paseo del Prado, one of the main throughfares in the city

In the 7th District, Miraflores district is separated from downtown by a long barrel (*Parque Urbano Central*, "Central Urban Park") and connected by the Bridge of the Americas and two avenues. Originally a residential zone, its growth has led it to become a major recreational center. It houses universities (including the [Universidad Mayor de San Andrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Mayor_de_San_Andr%C3%A9s)'s faculty of medicine), hospitals and the [Estadio Hernando Siles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Hernando_Siles) (capacity of 45,000 people).

Northern District

Located in the 2nd and 3rd districts, it has a significant industrial activity (mainly food), being the Cervecería Boliviana Nacional (Bolivian National Brewery) the most significant industry founded by Germans, and one of the city's biggest companies in the country. It connects La Paz with the city of El Alto by the *autopista* (highway).

Southern District



View of [Calacoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calacoto) in the Southern district

In the 5th district; has less height than the rest of La Paz (3,200 to 2,800 meters). It is referenced as the "Macrodistrito Sur" which has 3 districts with an area of 47.8 km2. The population density reached 3,084 inhabitants per squared km. in 2016. Obrajes, Bolognia, Koani, Achumani, San Miguel, Cota Cota, Irpavi, Següencoma, Ovejuyo, Chasquipampa, La Florida and [Calacoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calacoto) are some neighborhoods that make it up. This city district is the area of greatest expansion and growth, both population and the construction sector in La Paz.

&#x20;This area houses some of the most affluent and exclusive neighborhoods of the city. It has been benefited from steady economic growth and is now the second commercial and financial center of the city, housing international firms like [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's), [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank), [Aon Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aon_Corporation), [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei), [Millicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millicom) International Cellular, [Nissan Motor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motor_Corporation), [Samsung Electronics](46445426.md), [Pan American Silver Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Silver_Corporation), a [Sumitomo Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumitomo_Corporation) branch, [Ernst & Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_&_Young), and the "MegaCenter", La Paz's biggest shopping mall.

## Colonial architecture

The city of La Paz has a consistently decreasing volume of colonial buildings, mostly centered around the vicinity of the Plaza Murillo.[^cite_note-33] Due to a lack of funds and the inability of property owners to pay for restorations to colonial buildings, many have been torn down, or are in a dilapidated state. As historic buildings are more expensive to keep, land owners find it less of a burden to construct more modern buildings as opposed to keeping the old ones. Although there has been an increasing number of projects and propositions to restore some of the city's colonial buildings, the future of these historic edifices remains uncertain.

## Economy



The [Central Bank of Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Bolivia)



The [Ministry of Economy and Public Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Public_Finance_\(Bolivia\)) Building



Headquarters of the [Bolivian Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_Stock_Exchange)

The economy of La Paz has improved greatly in recent years, mainly as a result of improved political stability. Due to the long period of high inflation and economic struggle faced by Bolivians in the 1980s and early 1990s, a large informal economy developed. Evidence of this is provided by the markets found all around the city.

While there are stable markets, almost every street in the downtown area and surrounding neighborhoods has at least one vendor on it.[^cite_note-34]

La Paz remains the principal center of manufacturing enterprises that produce finished-product goods for the country, with about two-thirds of Bolivia's manufacturing located nearby.[^cite_note-35] Historically, industry in Bolivia has been dominated by mineral processing and the preparation of agricultural products. However, in the urban center of La Paz, small plants carry out a large portion of the industry. Food, tobacco products, clothing, various consumer goods, building materials, and agricultural tools are produced. "The tin quotations from London are watched in La Paz with close interest as an index of the country's prosperity; a third of the national revenue and more than half of the total customs in 1925 were derived from tin; in short, that humble but indispensable metal is the hub around which Bolivia's economic life revolves. The tin deposits of Bolivia, second largest in the world, ... invite development."

Further information: [The Federation of Private Entrepreneurs of La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Federation_of_Private_Entrepreneurs_of_La_Paz)

## Sports



The [Hernando Siles Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Hernando_Siles)

The most popular sport in La Paz, as in the rest of the country, is football. Currently, three teams compete in Bolivia’s first division: [Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Bol%C3%ADvar), [The Strongest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strongest), and [Club Always Ready](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Always_Ready). Between Bolívar and The Strongest, the *[Clásico Paceño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1sico_Pace%C3%B1o)* is played, also considered the Bolivian clásico, since this match pits the two most decorated and popular teams in the country against each other.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In 2012, FIFA recognized a list of the 120 official club derbies worldwide; in the case of Bolivia, the *Clásico Paceño* is the only one included on this list.[^cite_note-38]

The year 1964 was a bad year, as it was relegated from the top division, playing the next year in the second division. But it soon returned to the top flight.

In 1968 an airplane accident took the life of almost all the players, but Rafael Mendoza made many efforts that allowed the team to rise again as one of the most important in the country.

* [La Paz F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz_F.C.) : Founded in 1989, the club quickly rose through the ranks to become the third major club in the capital, only behind in popularity to the two well-established city rivals.

## Tourism



The [Witches' Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witches'_Market) in the city center, a popular spot for tourists

La Paz is an important cultural center of Bolivia. The city hosts several cathedrals belonging to the colonial times, such as the San Francisco Cathedral and the Metropolitan Cathedral, this last one located on Murillo Square, which is also home of the political and administrative power of the country. Hundreds of different museums can be found across the city, the most notable ones on Jaén Street, which street design has been preserved from the Spanish days and is home of 10 different museums.

The home of the Bolivian government is located on Murillo Square and is known as "Palacio Quemado" (*Burnt Palace*) as it has been on fire several times. The palace has been restored many times since, but the name has remained untouched.

## Local festivals



The [Alasitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasitas) yearly fair, where people buy miniature gifts and pray to the God of prosperity, [Ekeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekeko)

- January: *[Achocalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achocalla) Festival:* Occurs during the first week of January. The city of La Paz comes together to celebrate this religious festival with traditional dances, music, and a parade. In Bolivia's harvest calendar, Achocalla marks the time when potato fields begin to bloom. People dance the traditional folklore dance known as "tarqueada" during the festival.

## Transportation



[Rush hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_hour) traffic on Mariscal Santa Cruz Avenue in the city center

Automobiles and [public transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transportation) are the main means to get into the city. In March 2012, more than 1.5 million vehicles were registered.[^cite_note-43] Heavy traffic is common in the city center and [traffic jams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) occur on peak hours.

### Highways

The La Paz-El Alto Highway is a [toll road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road) that connects the city of La Paz with the neighboring city of [El Alto](3475756.md). It is the city's main highway. It allows easy access to [El Alto International Airport](2614073.md). The highway runs 11,7 km and crosses the city of El Alto:



The main toll of the La Paz - El Alto highway connects both neighboring cities

- 

  &#x20;Autopista La Paz-El Alto/RN-3 (La Paz-El Alto Highway) – Connects La Paz with El Alto.

- 

  &#x20;Autovía La Paz Oruro/A-1 (La Paz-Oruro Highway) – Connects La Paz with the cities of [Oruro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oruro,_Bolivia), [Patacamaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patacamaya) and [Caracollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracollo). It then connects with Ruta Nacional 1/RN-1 (National Highway 1) heading south to the cities of [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD), [Camargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camargo,_Chuquisaca), [Tarija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarija).

The Southern District, one of La Paz's most affluent and commercial neighborhoods, is relatively separated from the rest of the city, including the [CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Business_District). The Avenida Costanera and Avenida Kantutani (Costanera and Kantutani Avenues) connect the southern district with the rest of the metropolitan area.

### Air



[El Alto International Airport](2614073.md)

[El Alto International Airport](2614073.md) (IATA code: LPB) is La Paz's national and international airport and a principal [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) for [Línea Aérea Amaszonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%ADnea_A%C3%A9rea_Amaszonas) and [Transporte Aéreo Militar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAM_%E2%80%93_Transporte_A%C3%A9reo_Militar). It also serves as a focus city for [Boliviana de Aviación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boliviana_de_Aviaci%C3%B3n), Bolivia's [flag-carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag-carrier) and largest airline. The airport is located in the city of [El Alto](3475756.md) and is (13 km) south-west of La Paz's city center. At an elevation of 4,061 meters (13,323 feet), it is the highest international airport and fifth highest commercial airport in the world.[^cite_note-44] The runway has a length of 4,000 meters (2.5 mi). It is one of Bolivia's three main international gateways, along with [Jorge Wilstermann International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Wilstermann_International_Airport) and [Viru Viru International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viru_Viru_International_Airport).

International carriers serving El Alto International Airport include [Avianca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avianca) and [LATAM Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LATAM_Airlines), which offer direct flights from La Paz to cities such as [Bogotá](211271.md), [Lima](85423.md), and [Santiago](51572.md). However, most international traffic, including flights to [Europe](9239.md), operated out of Viru Viru International Airport in [Santa Cruz de la Sierra](673987.md) which is at a much lower altitude and is capable of handling larger aircraft.

Additionally, the airport supplies travelers with [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) for those who suffer from [altitude sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_sickness).

### Bus



The La Paz Bus Station

La Paz Bus Station, originally a bus and train station, was built by the French architect [Gustave Eiffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Eiffel). It is the main gateway for inter-city buses with several daily departures to all the main Bolivian cities, and routes to [Chile](5489.md) and [Peru](170691.md). The city is connected by road with the city of [Oruro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oruro,_Bolivia) from where there are routes to Sucre, [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD) and the south of the country. Another highway branches off before Oruro to reach [Cochabamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochabamba) and [Santa Cruz](673987.md). Roads to the west go to [Copacabana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copacabana,_Bolivia) and [Tiwanaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwanaku), near [Lake Titicaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Titicaca), and continue to [Cuzco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuzco), Peru via the border town of [Desaguadero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desaguadero_\(Bolivia\)). There are also roads north to get to [Yungas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yungas) crossing the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Mountains).

Departures to smaller cities and towns within the department use informal stations located in Villa Fátima (departures to Los Yungas, [Beni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Beni) and [Pando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Pando)), Upper San Pedro (for [Apolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apolo_\(La_Paz\))) and near the General Cemetery (for Copacabana, Lake Titicaca, or via Tiwanaku to Desaguadero on the Peruvian border).

## Water supply



La Paz gets its main water supply from nearby lakes fed by the glaciers of the Andes Mountains

The [water supply](684928.md) of La Paz is threatened by the impact of [climate change](5042951.md) through the melting of glaciers. The city receives its drinking water from three water systems: El Alto, Achachicala and Pampahasi. La Paz shares the first and largest of these systems with its sister city [El Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Alto,_La_Paz). All three systems are fed by glaciers and rivers in the [Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Real_\(Bolivia\)) mountain range. 20–28% of its water is fed by glaciers, the remainder coming from rainfall and snowmelt. The glaciers recede as a result of climate change, initially increasing water availability during the dry season, but ultimately threatening a substantial decrease in dry season run-off when they completely disappear. A small glacier, the [Chacaltaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chacaltaya) near El Alto, already disappeared in 2008. The El Alto system receives its water from the Tuni Dam and two water channels. These channels divert water that flows from the Zongo Glacier on the slopes of [Huayna Potosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huayna_Potosi) and from [Condoriri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunturiri_\(La_Paz\)) North of El Alto.[^cite_note-SEI-45] The 2.9 km long Zongo glacier retreats at a rate of about 18 meters per year.[^cite_note-46] The Tuni and Condoriri glaciers have lost 39% of their area between 1983 and 2006. According to a study by the [Stockholm Environment Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Environment_Institute) (SEI), the El Alto system is the least resilient against the impact of climate change among the three systems. The study says that reducing water distribution losses is the most effective short-term strategy to deal with [water scarcity](15380061.md).[^cite_note-SEI-45][^cite_note-47] New water sources further to the North in the Cordillera include the Khara Kota and Taypicacha, but they are expensive to develop and their water supply is also affected by glacier melt.
