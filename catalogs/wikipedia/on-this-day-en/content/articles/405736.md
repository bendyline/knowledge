**Perverting the course of justice** is an offence committed when a person interferes with the administration of [justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice). In [England and Wales](275187.md) it is a [common law offence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law_offence), carrying a maximum sentence of [life imprisonment](148681.md). [Statutory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_offence) versions of the offence exist in [Australia](4689264.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Fiji](10707.md), [Ireland](147575.md), and [New Zealand](4913064.md). The Scottish equivalent is **defeating the ends of justice**, although charges of attempting to pervert the course of justice are also raised in Scotland,[^cite_note-1] while the [South African counterpart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_South_Africa) is **defeating or obstructing the course of justice**.[^cite_note-2] A similar concept, [obstruction of justice](240718.md), exists in [United States](3434750.md) law.

## England and Wales

Doing an act tending and intending to pervert the course of public justice[^cite_note-3] is an offence under the [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) of [England and Wales](275187.md).

Perverting the course of justice can include acts such as:

- Fabricating or [disposing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoliation_of_evidence) of evidence

- Intimidating or threatening a [witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness) or [juror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juror)

- Intimidating or threatening a [judge](44719.md)

Also criminal are:

- Conspiring with another to pervert the course of justice, and

- Intending to pervert the course of justice

This offence, and the subject matter of the related forms of [criminal conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_\(criminal\)), have been referred to as:&#x20;

- Perverting the course of justice

- Interfering with the administration of justice

- Obstructing the administration of justice

- Obstructing the course of justice

- Defeating the due course of justice

- Defeating the ends of justice

- [Effecting a public mischief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effecting_a_public_mischief)

  [^cite_note-4]

This proliferation of alternative names has been described as "somewhat confusing".[^cite_note-5]

This offence is also sometimes referred to as "attempting to pervert the course of justice". This is potentially misleading. An attempt to pervert the course of justice is a substantive [common law offence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law_offence) and not an [inchoate offence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inchoate_offence). It is not a form of the offence of [attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempt), and it would be erroneous to charge it as being contrary to section 1(1) of the [Criminal Attempts Act 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Attempts_Act_1981).[^cite_note-6]

This offence is [triable only on indictment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triable_only_on_indictment).[^cite_note-7]

## Canada

In Canada, the equivalent offence is referred to as "obstructing justice". It is set out in s. 139 of the [Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_\(Canada\)):

> **139**. (1) Every one who wilfully attempts in any manner to obstruct, pervert or defeat the course of justice in a judicial proceeding,
>
> (a) by indemnifying or agreeing to indemnify a surety, in any way and either in whole or in part, or
>
> (b) where he is a surety, by accepting or agreeing to accept a fee or any form of indemnity whether in whole or in part from or in respect of a person who is released or is to be released from custody,
>
> is guilty of
>
> (c) an indictable offence and is liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years, or
>
> (d) an offence punishable on summary conviction.
>
> (2) Every one who wilfully attempts in any manner other than a manner described in subsection (1) to obstruct, pervert or defeat the course of justice is guilty of an indictable offence and liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years.
>
> (3) Without restricting the generality of subsection (2), every one shall be deemed wilfully to attempt to obstruct, pervert or defeat the course of justice who in a judicial proceeding, existing or proposed,
>
> (a) dissuades or attempts to dissuade a person by threats, bribes or other corrupt means from giving evidence;
>
> (b) influences or attempts to influence by threats, bribes or other corrupt means a person in his conduct as a juror; or
>
> (c) accepts or obtains, agrees to accept or attempts to obtain a bribe or other corrupt consideration to abstain from giving evidence, or to do or to refrain from doing anything as a juror.
>
> [^cite_note-8]

## Australia

In [New South Wales](21654.md), the equivalent offence is set out in Section 319 of the [Crimes Act 1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimes_Act_1900) (NSW).[^cite_note-9] The maximum penalty is 14 years' imprisonment. In 1985 [Murray Farquhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Farquhar), the former [Chief Stipendiary Magistrate of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Court_of_New_South_Wales), was convicted of attempting to pervert the course of justice to have charges against [Kevin Humphreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Humphreys_\(rugby_league\)) dismissed and sentenced to a maximum of four years in prison.[^cite_note-10] In 2009 [Marcus Einfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Einfeld), a former Judge of the [Federal Court of Australia](4688076.md), was sentenced to a maximum of three years in prison after pleading guilty to making a false statement with intent to pervert the course of justice.[^cite_note-Einfeld_v_R-11]

## International Criminal Court

See also: [International Criminal Court](14880.md)

In 2020, [International Criminal Court (ICC) Prosecutor](33903993.md) [Fatou Bensouda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_Bensouda) described attempted interference with ICC witnesses in the [Kenya investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Kenya) as perverting the course of justice under [Article 70](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Rome%20Statute%20of%20the%20International%20Criminal%20Court#Article_70:_Offences_against_the_administration_of_justice) of the [Rome Statute](68762.md), which defines the legal context of the ICC. Arrest warrants were issued against three people for their alleged attempts to make witnesses withdraw statements or refuse to testify to the court.[^cite_note-ICC_following_transfer_of_Paul_Gicheru-12]
