A **general officer** is an [officer](36301328.md) of high rank in the [armies](1376.md), and in some nations' [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) and [space forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_force), [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines) or [naval infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_infantry).[^cite_note-1]&#x20;

In some usages, the term "general officer" refers to a rank above [colonel](199023.md).[^cite_note-2]&#x20;

The adjective *general* had been affixed to officer designations since the late medieval period to indicate relative superiority or an extended jurisdiction.&#x20;

The various grades of general officer are at the top of the military rank structure. Lower-ranking officers are typically known as [field officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_officer) or field-grade officers, and below them are [company-grade officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company-grade_officer).&#x20;

### Common systems

There are two common systems of general ranks used worldwide. In addition, there is a third system, the Arab system of ranks, which is used throughout the Middle East and North Africa.&#x20;

In the old European system, general officer ranks are named by prefixing "general", as an adjective, with field officer ranks, although in some countries the highest general officers are titled [field marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal) or [marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal).&#x20;

The other is derived from the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), where generals' ranks are named according to the unit they (theoretically) command.&#x20;

In addition to militarily educated generals, there are also generals in medicine and engineering. The rank of the most senior chaplain, (chaplain general), is also usually considered to be a general officer rank.&#x20;

## Specific rank of general

In the old European system, a general, without prefix or suffix (and sometimes referred to informally as a "**full general**"), is usually the most senior type of general, above [lieutenant general](201916.md) and directly below [field marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal) as a [four-star rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-star_rank) (NATO OF-9).&#x20;

Usually it is the most senior peacetime rank, with more senior ranks (for example, field marshal, marshal of the air force, fleet admiral) being used only in wartime or as honorary titles.&#x20;

In some armies, however, the rank of [captain general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_general), [army general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_general) or [colonel general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_general) occupied or occupies this position. Depending on circumstances and the army in question, these ranks may be considered to be equivalent to a "full" general or to a field marshal [five-star rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-star_rank) (NATO OF-10).&#x20;

The rank of general came about as a "captain-general", the captain of an army in general (i.e., the whole army). The rank of captain-general began appearing around the time of the organisation of professional armies in the 17th century. In most countries "captain-general" contracted to just "general".&#x20;

## Air force and naval equivalents

Some countries (such as the United States) use the general officer ranks for both the [army](1376.md) and the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force), as well as their [marine corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_corps); other states only use the general officer ranks for the army, while in the air force they use [air officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_officer) as the equivalent of general officers. They use the air force rank of [air chief marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_chief_marshal) as the equivalent of the specific army rank of general. This latter group includes the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) and many current and former Commonwealth air forces—e.g. [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force), [Indian Air Force](403355.md), [Royal New Zealand Air Force](190903.md), [Nigerian Air Force](538869.md), [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md), etc.&#x20;

In most [navies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy), [flag officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_officer) are the equivalent of general officers, and the naval rank of [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) is equivalent to the specific army rank of general. A noteworthy historical exception was the [Cromwellian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) naval rank "[general at sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_at_sea)". In recent years in the American service there is a tendency to use *flag officer* and *flag rank* to refer to generals and admirals of the services collectively.&#x20;
