**Hendrick Motorsports** is an American professional [auto racing](1022.md) organization that competes in the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md) and the [NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series). The team was founded in 1984 as **All-Star Racing** by [Rick Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hendrick). Hendrick Motorsports has won a [NASCAR](21114.md)-record 324 Cup Series races and 15 Cup Series owners and drivers championships to go with three [Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series) owners and drivers titles and one [O'Reilly Auto Parts Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series) drivers crown. Additionally, the team has 32 O'Reilly Auto Parts Series race wins, 26 Truck Series race wins, and seven [ARCA Menards Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_Menards_Series) race wins.[^cite_note-1]

Hendrick Motorsports fields four full-time Cup Series teams with the [Chevrolet Camaro ZL1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro_\(sixth_generation\)#ZL1); the No. 5 for [Kyle Larson](36163081.md), the No. 9 for [Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott), the No. 24 for [William Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Byron_\(racing_driver\)), and the No. 48 for [Alex Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bowman). The team also fields the No. 17 [Chevrolet Camaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro) team full-time for [Corey Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Day) in the [NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series). Hendrick Motorsports also fielded several trucks in the [NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series), most recently for Elliott in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NASCAR_Camping_World_Truck_Series). The team has fielded cars in the past for many NASCAR drivers, including [Hall of Famers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Hall_of_Fame) [Jeff Gordon](18600829.md), [Mark Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Martin), [Terry Labonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Labonte), [Darrell Waltrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrell_Waltrip), [Benny Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Parsons), [Dale Earnhardt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Earnhardt_Jr.), and seven-time Cup champion [Jimmie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Johnson), and others such as [Geoff Bodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Bodine), [Tim Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Richmond), [Ricky Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Rudd), [Ken Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Schrader), [Ricky Craven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Craven), [Jerry Nadeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Nadeau), [Joe Nemechek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Nemechek), [Kyle Busch](1467805.md), [Casey Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Mears), and [Kasey Kahne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasey_Kahne). Hendrick Motorsports maintains an in-house engine shop, with the team leasing some of its engines to technical partners such as [Haas Factory Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_Factory_Team), [Hyak Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyak_Motorsports) and [Spire Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spire_Motorsports).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## History

See also: [List of NASCAR race wins by Hendrick Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NASCAR_race_wins_by_Hendrick_Motorsports)



Hendrick Motorsports race shop in Concord, North Carolina

What is now Hendrick Motorsports was founded prior to the [1984 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series) by [Rick Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hendrick), a [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md)–based [car dealership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_dealership) owner who currently operates a network of dealerships called Hendrick Automotive Group. The team was formed with crew chief and car builder [Harry Hyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Hyde) as **All-Star Racing**.[^cite_note-Poole2013-5][^cite_note-Fox-Hendrick-Humble-2014-Video-6][^cite_note-Fox-Hendrick-Humble-2014-7] The team, renamed Hendrick Motorsports in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series), was involved with the [GM Goodwrench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GM_Goodwrench) [IMSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_SportsCar_Championship) GTP Corvette and twin-turbo V6 engine development effort and competed in the IMSA GTP series from 1985 through [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series) with drivers Doc Bundy and [Sarel van der Merwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarel_van_der_Merwe). Hendrick Motorsports and GM ceased the project in 1988.

Hendrick Motorsports expanded its NASCAR efforts to two full-time cars in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series), three in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series), and four in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series).[^cite_note-StockCar-MultiCar-Sept2003-8][^cite_note-SunSentinal-HSM-Concert-Feb1987-9][^cite_note-Motorsport-JJohnson-HMS-2000-10] It was one of the first teams in NASCAR to be successful operating multiple entries, partly based on the model used at the Hendrick car dealerships.[^cite_note-Poole2013-5][^cite_note-StockCar-MultiCar-Sept2003-8] The team has also been credited for innovations in engine construction[^cite_note-ESPN-HMS-Dorton-Oct2007-11] and [pit crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pit_stop) training.[^cite_note-BCSN-HMSPitCrew-2014-12][^cite_note-HMS-RainbowWarriors-2010-13][^cite_note-14] In 2020, Hendrick Motorsports partnered with [AdvoCare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AdvoCare) in its performance and fitness teams.[^cite_note-15]

Hendrick Motorsports (as All-Star Racing) won its first race in 1984 at [Martinsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsville_Speedway) with the No. 5 driven by [Geoff Bodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Bodine). At the [2021 Coca-Cola 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Coca-Cola_600), Hendrick Motorsports became the winningest team in [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md) history when it won its 269th race with the No. 5 driven by [Kyle Larson](36163081.md). This eclipsed the record held by [Petty Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_Enterprises) at 268 wins, which had held the record of the winningest team in the series since 1960.[^cite_note-16]

In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NASCAR_Cup_Series), Hendrick worked with NASCAR, [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet), and [Goodyear Tires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Tires) to enter the [24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) with a modified version of the current NASCAR Cup car, in conjunction with the 75th anniversary of NASCAR.

## NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series

Hendrick Motorsports fielded in-house entries in the [Busch Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busch_Series) from 1984 to 1990, and again from 2000 to 2007, primarily the No. 5 entry. Following the conclusion of the 2007 racing season, Hendrick Motorsports and [JR Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Motorsports) (owned by then-Hendrick Motorsports driver [Dale Earnhardt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Earnhardt_Jr.)) officially combined Xfinity Series operations. The No. 5 Chevrolets began running full-time under the JR Motorsports banner in 2008, and the team received engines and technical support from Hendrick Motorsports, with several of its employees moving to JR Motorsports. [Rick Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hendrick) is an equity partner in JRM and continues to be listed as car owner of the No. 5 team.

### Car No. 5 history

Part-time (1985–1986)

The No. 5 car debuted in 1985 with [Brett Bodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Bodine) ran twelve races. The younger Bodine brother would win three races for the team. [Geoff Bodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Bodine) ran four races in the No. 5 car. Bodine won the season opener [Goody's 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_300).

In 1986, Geoff Bodine drove the No. 5 for one race at Bristol. He won the pole and finished sixteenth.

Ricky Hendrick (2002)



[Ricky Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Hendrick)'s No. 5 [GMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Financial) Chevrolet in 2002

The current No. 5 car debuted as the No. 14 of [JG Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JG_Motorsports) in 2000, with Rick Hendrick's son [Ricky Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Hendrick) finishing 39th in the season finale at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead%E2%80%93Miami_Speedway).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The number was switched to No. 5 when the car began competing full-time in 2002. After Ricky Hendrick was injured in a wreck at [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway),[^cite_note-USA2Day-RickyHendrickRising-2004-19][^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20] [Ron Hornaday Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Hornaday_Jr.) took over for the next six races before Hendrick returned at [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Raceway). Toward the end of the season, Hendrick suddenly announced his retirement from driving due to lingering effects from the crash, but he remained as car owner until his death in 2004.[^cite_note-USA2Day-RickyHendrickRising-2004-19][^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20] [David Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Green_\(NASCAR\)) finished out the season for the team.[^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20][^cite_note-21]

Brian Vickers (2003)

Ricky Hendrick selected 19-year-old [Brian Vickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Vickers) to drive the No. 5 car in 2003.[^cite_note-USA2Day-RickyHendrickRising-2004-19][^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20] Vickers won three races and the Busch Series championship, finishing just 14 points ahead of Hendrick test driver and former No. 5 team spotter [David Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Green_\(racing_driver\)).[^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20][^cite_note-Motorsport-Vickers-BGN-2003-22][^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-JRM-2008-23]

Kyle Busch (2004)

When Vickers moved up to the Cup Series, [Kyle Busch](1467805.md) became the No. 5 car's driver after he had run seven races the previous season.[^cite_note-USA2Day-RickyHendrickRising-2004-19][^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20] In his rookie year, Busch won five races and was runner-up to [Martin Truex Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Truex_Jr.) in points.[^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20][^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-HMS-2005-24] He moved up to the Cup Series after the season, but he continued to drive the No. 5 Busch Series car part-time for several more years.

Multiple Drivers (2005–2006)

[Adrián Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adri%C3%A1n_Fern%C3%A1ndez) drove the car for six races in 2005, finishing tenth at [Autódromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aut%C3%B3dromo_Hermanos_Rodr%C3%ADguez), his only top-ten finish of the season.[^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-JRM-2008-23][^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-HMS-2005-24] Hendrick development drivers [Blake Feese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Feese), [Boston Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Reid), and [Kyle Krisiloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Krisiloff) also periodically drove the No. 5 car, running a combined fifteen races.[^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-HMS-2005-24] Busch, Vickers, and [Jimmie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Johnson) ran the rest of the schedule,[^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-HMS-2005-24] with Busch winning at [Lowe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Motor_Speedway) while also scoring one top five and two top tens and scoring a pole at Bristol while Johnson scored only one top five and a pole at Darlington while Vickers in his only start at Watkins Glen finished in third. Fernández had only one top-ten with his tenth place finish in Mexico City along with an average finish of 31.8 and had 3 DNF's. As for Feese, Reid, and Krisiloff, the three drivers driving the 5 car combined for zero top tens with the best finish being a seventeeth by Reid at Nashville along with a combined average finish of 30.5 and had a combined seven DNF's. The season was so bad, all three drivers were released and Rick Hendrick shut down his driver development program until 2008 when he merged with [Dale Earnhardt Jr's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Earnhardt_Jr.) team [JR Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Motorsports). Feese continued to race sporadically in ARCA and the Truck Series before not racing again after 2011. Reid ran 21 races from 2006 to 2007 in the Truck Series. Kyle Krisiloff ran part-time in the Truck Series in 2006 for [Billy Ballew Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Ballew_Motorsports) before returning to the Busch Series doing a full-time season in 2007 for [Carl A. Haas Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_A._Haas_Motorsports) and then running 6 races for [Chip Ganassi Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chip_Ganassi_Racing) in 2008 before retiring at 22 years old. All three never made a start in the Cup Series.

In 2006, Busch drove 34 of 35 races, winning at [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Motor_Speedway) and finishing seventh in points. [Justin Labonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Labonte) drove for one race at [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_International_Raceway).

Part Time (2007)

In 2007, Busch ran the No. 5 on a part-time basis, sharing the ride with [Mark Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Martin), [Landon Cassill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Cassill), [Casey Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Mears), and Adrián Fernández, running a total of 26 races.[^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20][^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-JRM-2008-23] The car carried a number of different sponsors including Lowe's, Delphi, Spectrum, and Hendrick Autoguard. Busch drove the car to victory lane four times in 2007, while Martin finished second twice in three races.

JR Motorsports (2008–2018)

The No. 5 team moved to [JR Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Motorsports) in 2008,[^cite_note-Motorsport-JRM-HMS-2007-25][^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20] and featured eight drivers, including Johnson and Earnhardt Jr., and four primary sponsors in its first year.[^cite_note-Motorsport-AFernandez-JRM-2008-23][^cite_note-26] In 2009, the No. 5 car ran a part-time schedule due to sponsorship limitations.[^cite_note-Bleacher-RickyHendrick-5BGN-2009-20] [Fastenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastenal), [Unilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilever) and [GoDaddy.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoDaddy.com) sponsored seven different drivers over the course of the season. A variety of drivers ran the car in subsequent [NASCAR Xfinity Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Xfinity_Series) seasons until it was shut down for the 2019 season.[^cite_note-JR_Motorsports_shuffles_team_car_numbers_for_2019-27]

### Car No. 15 history

Part-time (1984–1990)

Hendrick Motorsports began competing in the 1984 debut season of the [Busch Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Xfinity_Series), fielding the No. 15 car for seventeen races with sponsorship coming from [Levi Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Garrett), with Cup Series driver [Geoff Bodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Bodine) running 12 of them. Bodine would score Hendrick Motorsports' first win in the Busch Series at [Rockingham Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockingham_Speedway). [Ron Bouchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Bouchard), [Dick Trickle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Trickle), [Glenn Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Jarrett) all ran one race, while [Tim Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Richmond) ran one. In 1985, Brett Bodine ran one race in the No. 15 car. Geoff Bodine also ran one race in the No. 15. Tim Richmond ran two races in the No. 15, winning once at [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Motor_Speedway). In 1986, the team ran seven races, three with Bodine and Richmond, and one with [Rob Moroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Moroso). Richmond would get the team's only win of the year at Charlotte. In 1987, the team ran nine races utilizing the No. 15. Eight of them were driven by Geoff Bodine, and one with team owner [Rick Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hendrick). While Hendrick would DNF in his start, Bodine would once again win the season opener at Daytona. The team ran eight races as the No. 15 in 1988 with Geoff Bodine being the only driver. Bodine would win once at [Darlington Raceway](685467.md). In 1989, the team would only run five races, with Geoff Bodine and [Ken Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Schrader) driving. While Schrader would DNF his two starts, Bodine would once again get a single win at Darlington. In 1990, [Greg Sacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Sacks) drove the No. 15 once at Charlotte, He finished second. After the 1990 season, Hendrick Motorsports shut down its Busch Series operation for the time being.

### Car No. 17 history



[Alex Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bowman) in the No. 17 at [Indianapolis Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway) in 2022

Part-time (2022–2025)

On June 2, 2022, Hendrick Motorsports announced it would field the No. 17 in three Xfinity races in 2022, with [Kyle Larson](36163081.md) running at [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Henry_180), [Alex Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bowman) at [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pennzoil_150), and [William Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Byron_\(racing_driver\)) at [Watkins Glen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sunoco_Go_Rewards_200_at_The_Glen). This marked Hendrick Motorsports' return to the Xfinity Series after [Tony Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stewart) won for the team at [Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_300) in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_NASCAR_Nationwide_Series).[^cite_note-28] Larson dominated at Road America, but eventually lost to [Ty Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Gibbs) on the final lap.[^cite_note-29] Bowman ran the car at the Indianapolis road course, but it again finished second, this time to [A. J. Allmendinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Allmendinger). At Watkins Glen, Byron fiercely battled Gibbs for the lead throughout most of the race until they both spun off-course during the final restart, resulting in Byron finishing 25th.[^cite_note-30] At the [September Darlington race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sport_Clips_Haircuts_VFW_200), Larson finished fifth after engaging in a three-car battle with [Noah Gragson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Gragson) and [Sheldon Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Creed) over the closing laps. Larson attempted a pass on Creed for the lead on the final lap, only for both to be passed by race-winner Gragson.[^cite_note-31]

On March 8, 2023, Hendrick Motorsports announced that, for the second year in a row, it would field the No. 17 in four Xfinity races in 2023, with Byron running at [Circuit of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pit_Boss_250), Larson at [Sonoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_DoorDash_250) and [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sport_Clips_Haircuts_VFW_200), and Bowman at [Watkins Glen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Shriners_Children's_200_at_The_Glen).[^cite_note-32] On July 12, Hendrick Motorsports added a fifth race to their Xfinity schedule, with Elliott driving the No. 17 at [Pocono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Explore_the_Pocono_Mountains_225).[^cite_note-33] On September 26, two further races were added to the No. 17 schedule, with [Boris Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Said) competing at the [Charlotte Roval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Drive_for_the_Cure_250) and [Rajah Caruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Caruth) competing in the season finale at [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NASCAR_Xfinity_Series_Championship_Race).[^cite_note-34]

For [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NASCAR_Xfinity_Series), a ten–race schedule was announced on February 27, 2024, with Cup Byron, Larson, Bowman and [Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott) all competing in at least one race, with Said also competing for the team at [Sonoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Sonoma_250).[^cite_note-35] The organization claimed their first win in the Xfinity Series since 2009 with Larson at the [Circuit of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Focused_Health_250_\(COTA\)) after overtaking a dueling [Shane van Gisbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_van_Gisbergen) and [Austin Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Hill) on the final lap.[^cite_note-36] Elliott took the No. 17 to victory lane at [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BetMGM_300).[^cite_note-37]

In [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NASCAR_Xfinity_Series), the team announced a sixteen-race schedule, with newly signed development driver [Corey Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Day) serving as the anchor driver, competing in ten races for the team, while the remaining six races being split between Byron, Bowman, Larson and Elliott.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Larson took the No. 17 car to victory lane at [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_SciAps_300),[^cite_note-40] while Byron won at [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BetMGM_300).[^cite_note-41] The team added a second race for Elliott at [Pocono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Explore_the_Pocono_Mountains_250).[^cite_note-42] On July 14, the team announced [Jake Finch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Finch) would make his Xfinity Series debut at [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BetRivers_200).[^cite_note-43] Larson made a third start for the team at [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pennzoil_250).[^cite_note-44]

Corey Day (2026–present)



[Corey Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Day) in the No. 17 car at [Las Vegas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway) in 2025

It was announced [Corey Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Day) would move to full time for the 2026 season.[^cite_note-Corey_Day_2026-45] He started the season with a 27th place DNF at [Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_Rentals_300). After a streak of eight consecutive top ten finishes, he scored his first career win at [Talladega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ag-Pro_300).[^cite_note-46] He also won at [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BetRivers_200).[^cite_note-47] On August 18, 2026, it was announced that Day will return to HMS for the 2027 season.[^cite_note-48]

### Car No. 24 History



[Casey Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Mears)' No. 24 [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Guard) Chevrolet at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead%E2%80%93Miami_Speedway) in 2007

JG Motorsports (1999–2000)

The No. 24 team started in 1999 with [Gordon-Evernham Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JG_Motorsports), owned by [Jeff Gordon](18600829.md) and crew chief [Ray Evernham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Evernham). Gordon and [Ricky Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Hendrick) combined to compete in 10 races.[^cite_note-49] In 2000, [Rick Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hendrick) bought out Evernham's share, renaming the team [JG Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JG_Motorsports). Gordon and Ricky Hendrick once again shared the ride, with Hendrick running 15 events.[^cite_note-50] The team also formed an alliance with [Cicci-Welliver Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicci-Welliver_Racing).[^cite_note-51]

Part Time (2001)

Hendrick Motorsports took full control of the team in 2001, with [GMAC Financial Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Financial) sponsoring the No. 24 team in each of its three races. Ricky Hendrick drove in those three races.[^cite_note-52] In 2002, Hendrick moved to the No. 5 Busch Series car and three-time truck series champion [Jack Sprague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sprague) took over the No. 24 full-time.

Jack Sprague (2002)

Sprague ran the full 2002 season, bringing truck series sponsor [NetZero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NetZero) with him.[^cite_note-StockCar-Sprague-Interview-2002-53] He earned three poles and a win at [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Superspeedway) en route to a fifth place points finish. Sprague moved to Hendrick-affiliated [Haas CNC Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_CNC_Racing) in 2003.

Part Time (2007)

In 2007, the No. 24 returned with [Casey Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Mears) and [Landon Cassill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Cassill) as the drivers, with the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Guard) providing sponsorship. After the 2007 season, the team shut down.

### Car No. 48 history

Part Time With Jimmie Johnson (2004–2007)

The 48 car made its debut in the Busch Series in 2004 at [Lowe's Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Motor_Speedway), running a one-race deal with sponsorship from [Lowe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowe's) and [The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_SpongeBob_SquarePants_Movie). Jimmie Johnson drove it to a third-place finish. He drove the car for five races in 2005, winning a pole at [Lowe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowe's_Motor_Speedway). During 2006, he started three races, both Lowe's races and the Ameriquest 300 at California.[^cite_note-54] His best finish was seventh in the first Lowe's race. Johnson drove the 48 car in the same three Busch races for the 2007 races, with a best finish of fourth at California.

### Car No. 57 history

Part Time (2005–2006)

In 2005, Hendrick Motorsports fielded the No. 57, a number taken from the sponsorship of [Heinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz) and its "[57 varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_57)".[^cite_note-HMS-Vickers-Heinz-2005-55] Several drivers piloted the No. 57 in 2005 and 2006, with [Brian Vickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Vickers) competing in the majority of races.[^cite_note-HMS-Vickers-Heinz-2005-55][^cite_note-56] Additional sponsors, including [Lowe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowe's) and [Mountain Dew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Dew), signed deals to sponsor the team for certain races.

### Truck No. 24 history

The No. 24 truck debuted with the Truck Series in 1995 with [Scott Lagasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Lagasse) driving and [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont) sponsoring. Lagasse posted two top-fives and finished ninth in the standings.

In 1996, [Jack Sprague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sprague) drove the No. 24 full-time with Quaker State sponsoring. He won five races and was second in the points. The following season, he won three times and clinched his first NASCAR championship.

The team lost the Quaker State sponsorship after 1997 but signed GMAC Financial as a sponsor after a one-race deal with Big Daddy's BBQ Sauce. He won an additional five races but lost the championship by three points. In 1999, Sprague won the championship again but fell to fifth in 2000. In 2001, NetZero came on board as the team's sponsor, and Sprague won his third championship. After Sprague moved his ride to the Busch Series, [Ron Hornaday Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Hornaday_Jr.) drove the No. 24 in a one-race deal at Daytona, finishing twelfth. The team closed after that race to focus on its Busch Series efforts.

### Truck No. 25 history

In 1995, the team fielded the No. 25 Budweiser Chevrolet part-time with Hendrick Sr. and [Roger Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Mears) driving. Midway through the season, [Jack Sprague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sprague) came on board to finish out the season for the team, winning a pole at [Phoenix International Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_International_Raceway). In seven races, Sprague had three fourth-place finishes and five top-ten finishes, and no finish worse than twentieth.

### Truck No. 94 history



[Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott)'s No. 94 [Aaron's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron's) Chevrolet at [Rockingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockingham_Speedway) in 2013

Hendrick Motorsports revived its truck program in 2013, fielding a part-time entry for [Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott). The team was sponsored by [Aaron's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron's) and ran nine races. The trucks were not built directly by Hendrick Motorsports, but were instead provided by Hendrick-affiliated [Turner Scott Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Scott_Motorsports). However, the trucks were fielded directly by Hendrick, with crew chief [Lance McGrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_McGrew).[^cite_note-ChaseElliottTrucks-61] Elliott made his debut at [Martinsville Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsville_Speedway) on April 6 and finished in the sixth position.[^cite_note-62] Elliott became the youngest pole winner in Truck Series history at the time at Bristol in August,[^cite_note-63] and later the youngest race winner in the Truck Series at the time by winning the inaugural [Chevrolet Silverado 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Silverado_250) at [Canadian Tire Motorsports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Tire_Motorsports_Park).[^cite_note-64] Elliott departed the No. 94 to join JR Motorsports in the Nationwide Series in 2014.[^cite_note-65]

## ARCA Racing Series

Hendrick fielded cars for five ARCA races from 1985 to 1996, twice for [Brett Bodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Bodine) in 1985 and 1986 (who won the pole for both races), and once each for [Tommy Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Ellis) (1988), [Jack Sprague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sprague) (1996), and [Rick Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hendrick) himself. Rick Hendrick drove the No. 15 [Tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide_\(brand\)) car at [Heartland Park Topeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartland_Park_Topeka) in 1991, starting third and finishing 23rd after a braking issue in his only career ARCA start.[^cite_note-racing-reference.info-66] In February 2000, [Ricky Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Hendrick) made his [Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_International_Speedway) stock car in the [ARCA Bondo/Mar-Hyde Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_Bondo/Mar-Hyde_Series), driving the No. 17 [GMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Financial) Chevrolet to a fifth-place finish.[^cite_note-67] Hendrick would run the race again the next year in the renumbered 71 car, finishing 9th.[^cite_note-motorsport_2001-68]

### Car No. 9 history



[Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott)'s No. 9 [Aaron's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron's,_Inc.) Chevrolet at [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America) in 2013

In 2012, Hendrick began fielding the No. 9 Chevrolet for development driver [Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott), with father [Bill Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Elliott) as the [listed owner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Elliott_Racing) and sponsorship from [the Aaron's Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Aaron's_Company). Longtime Hendrick Motorsports crew chief [Lance McGrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_McGrew) served as the team's crew chief. Elliott made his debut at age 16 at [Mobile International Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_International_Speedway), scoring a pole and six top tens in six races.[^cite_note-Aarons-CElliottARCA-2012-73][^cite_note-74]

Elliott returned to the team in 2013, scoring his first career win at [Pocono Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocono_Raceway). Elliott, at the age of seventeen, became the youngest superspeedway winner in [ARCA Racing Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_Racing_Series) history, beating fellow 17-year-old [Erik Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Jones).[^cite_note-USA2Day-CElliottHendrickDevelop-2013-75][^cite_note-76] Elliott scored four top tens, including the win at Pocono, in five races in 2013.[^cite_note-77]

Elliott ran the 2014 ARCA season opener at [Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_International_Speedway), in order to gain [NASCAR](21114.md) approval to run the [Nationwide Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationwide_Series) race the next week. Sponsored by HendrickCars.com and [NAPA Brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Automotive_Parts_Association), Elliott was involved in a fifteen-car crash on the thirteenth lap. In spite of that, Elliott finished ninth, and NASCAR approved him to run on superspeedways; he would go on to win the [Nationwide Series Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NASCAR_Nationwide_Series).[^cite_note-TimesUnion-CElliott-Eligibility-2014-78]

Cassill and Stanley would move to the 88 under the [JR Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Motorsports) banner for 2008.[^cite_note-82]

## 24 Hours of Le Mans

In 2023, Hendrick Motorsports entered the [24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), working with NASCAR, Chevrolet, Goodyear, and [IMSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA) to field a modified version of the [Next Gen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Gen_\(NASCAR\)) car in the experimental Garage 56 category. The [Camaro ZL1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro_\(sixth_generation\)#ZL1) was 'largely unchanged' from the Cup Series car. Modifications included real headlights and taillights, a larger fuel tank, uprated carbon ceramic brakes, as well as new tyres developed by [Goodyear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Tire_and_Rubber_Company).[^cite_note-87]



The No. 24 [Chevrolet Camaro ZL1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Gen_\(NASCAR\)) at the [2023 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans)

The car, bearing long-time Hendrick Motorsports number 24, was driven by long-time Hendrick driver Jimmie Johnson, 2010 Le Mans winner [Mike Rockenfeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Rockenfeller), and 2009 [Formula 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_1) World Champion [Jenson Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenson_Button). The car ultimately finished 39th out of 62 total competitors, and outperformed entries in the [GTE Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LM_GTE) class, the car's closest equivalents.[^cite_note-88]

The car was later featured in the 2023 [Goodwood Festival of Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwood_Festival_of_Speed).

## Plane crash

Main article: [2004 Hendrick Motorsports aircraft crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Hendrick_Motorsports_aircraft_crash)



[Brian Vickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Vickers)' No. 25 Ditech/[GMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Financial) Chevrolet, which pays tribute to the ten people killed in an October 2004 plane crash

On October 24, 2004, ten people associated with Hendrick Motorsports lost their lives in a plane crash while en route from [Concord, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_North_Carolina), to a small airport near the [Martinsville Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsville_Speedway). The plane crashed in heavy fog into Bull Mountain, seven miles (11 km) from the [Blue Ridge Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Airport) in [Stuart, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart,_Virginia), after a failed attempt to land.[^cite_note-89] Ten people aboard the [Beechcraft King Air 200](9023483.md) died. Six were Hendrick family members and/or Hendrick Motorsports employees: John Hendrick, the owner's brother and president of Hendrick Motorsports; Jeff Turner, general manager of Hendrick Motorsports; [Ricky Hendrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Hendrick), a Hendrick Motorsports driver and its owner's son; Kimberly and Jennifer Hendrick, John Hendrick's twin daughters; and [Randy Dorton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Dorton), chief engine builder. Also dead were the plane's pilots, Richard Tracy and Elizabeth Morrison, Joe Jackson, director of the [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont) Motorsports program,[^cite_note-90] and Scott Lathram, who worked for [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md) as a helicopter pilot.[^cite_note-91]

NASCAR officials learned of the crash during that day's [Subway 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Subway_500) race in [Martinsville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsville,_Virginia); they withheld the information from drivers until the end of the race, which was won by Hendrick driver [Jimmie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Johnson). For the rest of the [2004 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_NASCAR_Nextel_Cup_Series), all Hendrick Motorsports cars and the No. 0 [Haas CNC Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_CNC_Racing) car featured pictures of the crash victims on the hood, accompanied by the phrase "Always in our hearts".
