**Jean-Noël Barrot** (French pronunciation: [\[ʒɑ̃nɔɛl baʁo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); born 13 May 1983)[^cite_note-1] is a French-Swiss politician of the [Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Movement_\(France\)) (MoDem) who has been serving as [Minister for Europe and Foreign Affairs](250002.md) in the successive governments of [Prime Ministers](218751.md) [Michel Barnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Barnier), [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou) and [Sébastien Lecornu](54364868.md) since 21 September 2024.[^cite_note-2] He previously served as Minister Delegate for Digital Transition and Telecommunications in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borne_government) of [Élisabeth Borne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lisabeth_Borne) from 2022 to 2024 and Minister Delegate for European Affairs in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attal_government) of [Gabriel Attal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Attal) in 2024.[^cite_note-O'Brien-2022-3][^cite_note-Piquard-2022-4][^cite_note-5]

An academic by occupation, Barrot was elected to represent the [2nd constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines's_2nd_constituency) of the [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines) [department](38523.md) in the [National Assembly](299616.md) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_legislative_election) with the support of [La République En Marche!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_\(French_political_party\)) (LREM), prior to joining the government.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] In 2024, he was elected president of the National Assembly [Committee on Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Committee_\(France\)),[^cite_note-8] a position he held until his appointment as Foreign Minister.

## Early life and career

Barrot was born in the [14th arrondissement of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_arrondissement_of_Paris). He isthe grandson of Noël Barrot, a Christian Democratic resistance fighter and later an MP for [Haute-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Loire).

His mother, Florence Cattani, is [Swiss](7351032.md) and originally from [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne).[^cite_note-Piquard-2022-4][^cite_note-9] His father, [Jacques Barrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Barrot), was a prominent figure in the French Fifth Republic: a ChristianDemocratic MP for [Haute-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Loire) for around thirty years, he served as a minister in several governments, became Vice-President of the European Commission from2004 to 2009, and served as a member of the [French Constitutional Council](1141259.md) from 2010 to 2014. His younger sister, Hélène Barrot, was, in 2022, [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber)'s communications director for Western and Southern European markets.[^cite_note-10]

Barrot followed a *classe préparatoire* at the [Lycée Henri-IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Henri-IV), and graduated from [HEC Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEC_Paris) in 2007 (*[grande école](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_%C3%A9cole)* master's programme) and 2013 (PhD). He also graduated with master's degrees from [Sciences Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po) and the [Paris School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_School_of_Economics), both in 2008.

In 2013, Barrot became a research affiliate at the [Sloan School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_School_of_Management) at the [MIT](18879.md).[^cite_note-11] In 2017, he became an assistant professor at [HEC Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEC_Paris).[^cite_note-12]

## Political career

### Career in local politics

Barrot served in the [Departmental Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_council_\(France\)) of [Haute-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Loire) for the [canton of Yssingeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Yssingeaux) from [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_French_departmental_elections) until his resignation in 2017, a position his father had held until 2004.

In the [2021 regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_regional_elections), he was elected to the [Regional Council of Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_%C3%8Ele-de-France) on the [La République En Marche!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_\(French_political_party\)) list led by [Laurent Saint-Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Saint-Martin).

### Member of Parliament (2017–2022)

In the [2017 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_legislative_election), Barrot was elected to the [National Assembly](299616.md) in the [2nd constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines's_2nd_constituency) of [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines), which encompasses HEC Paris, the *grande école* he taught at. He defeated outgoing [deputy](29334549.md) [Pascal Thévenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Th%C3%A9venot) of [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)) with 58.3% of the second-round vote.[^cite_note-13]

In Parliament, he served as a vice president of the [Committee on Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Committee_\(French_National_Assembly\)).[^cite_note-14] He co-authored with [Bénédicte Peyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9n%C3%A9dicte_Peyrol) draft legislation in 2018 to combat large-scale tax evasion and avoidance schemes through [dividend stripping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividend_stripping) in the wake of the [CumEx Files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CumEx-Files) revelations.[^cite_note-15]

In addition to his committee assignments, Barrot was a member of the French-Uruguayan parliamentary friendship group.

In late 2017, Barrot was appointed by [President of the National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_National_Assembly_of_France) [François de Rugy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Rugy) to chair a ten-member working group on reforming the National Assembly. The group submitted two reports, in 2017 and 2018, respectively.[^cite_note-16]

From February 2018, Barrot served as a Democratic Movement spokesperson, in tandem with [Sarah El Haïry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_El_Ha%C3%AFry).[^cite_note-17] He eventually succeeded [Yann Wehrling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_Wehrling) as Secretary General of the Democratic Movement in December 2018, serving until July 2022 under the leadership of party president [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou).[^cite_note-18]

### Minister for Digital Transition and Telecommunications (2022–2024)

In July 2022, Barrot was appointed Minister Delegate for Digital Transition and Telecommunications in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borne_government) of [Prime Minister](218751.md) [Élisabeth Borne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lisabeth_Borne).[^cite_note-O'Brien-2022-3][^cite_note-Piquard-2022-4]

He won a by-election in October 2022, triggered by the resignation of his substitute Anne Grignon—who had been representing him in the National Assembly after he joined the government—due to a legal incompatibility related to her eligibility.[^cite_note-19]

In 2023, he criticized [ChatGPT](72417803.md) and accused the service of not respecting [privacy law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy_law). However, he also stated being opposed to efforts to ban the service.[^cite_note-20]

### Minister Delegate for European Affairs (2024)

In February 2024, Barrot was appointed Minister Delegate for European Affairs under Foreign Minister [Stéphane Séjourné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A9phane_S%C3%A9journ%C3%A9) in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attal_government) of Prime Minister [Gabriel Attal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Attal).

After being reelected in the [2024 snap legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_legislative_election), he was elected chair of the [Foreign Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Committee_\(France\)) while serving as the caretaker Minister Delegate for European Affairs.

### Minister for Europe and Foreign Affairs (2024–present)

Barrot was appointed [Minister for Europe and Foreign Affairs](250002.md) in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnier_government) of Prime Minister [Michel Barnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Barnier) on 21 September 2024,[^cite_note-21] succeeding Séjourné, who had been proposed as France's new [European Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner) in Brussels by President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md), within the [Von der Leyen Commission II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_Commission_II). He was retained by [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou) when he succeeded Barnier as prime minister.[^cite_note-22] He was also retained by Bayrou's successor, [Sébastien Lecornu](54364868.md) upon the formation of [his first government](81029757.md) and later also in [his second government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Lecornu_government) in October 2025.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

On 29 September, Barrot traveled to Lebanon, two days prior to the start of the [Israeli invasion of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon), stating France "stands with Lebanon", as the country was being pulled into a war "it did not choose".[^cite_note-25] On 8 October, he called Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md)'s rhetoric on the matter a "provocation".[^cite_note-26]

In January 2025, Barrot and his German counterpart [Annalena Baerbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annalena_Baerbock) visited Damascus to meet Syria's *de facto* new leader [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) on behalf of the European Union, thereby becoming the first ministers from the EU to visit the country since the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md).[^cite_note-27]



Barrot with U.S. Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) in Washington, D.C., 1 May 2025

In February 2025 Barrot urged [G20](2206608.md) states to show unambivalent support for the international rules-based order, including the sovereignty of [Ukraine](31750.md). Barrot said the real line of geopolitical division was not between north and south but between those who supported the international rules-based order and those who did not.[^cite_note-28]

On 3 January 2026, Barrot condemned the [United States strikes in Venezuela](82021123.md) as illegal, stating: "The military operation that led to the capture of [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md) violates the principle of not resorting to force, that underpins international law. France reiterates that no lasting political solution can be imposed from the outside and that only sovereign people themselves can decide their future."[^cite_note-29]



Barrot in June 2026, standing behind Emmanuel Macron as US president Donald Trump signs the [Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding](83441543.md) at the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles), with [US Secretary of State](32293.md) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) standing behind Trump.

In April 2025, Barrot wrote to the UN Commissioner for Human Rights to oppose the automatic renewal of [Francesca Albanese](72566699.md) in her role as UN Special Rapporteur. On 11 February 2026, Barrot called for her resignation.[^cite_note-30]

In June 2026, Barrot was present with French President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) at the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles) when US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) signed the [Islamabad Memorandum](83441543.md), a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) between the [United States](3434750.md) and [Iran](14653.md) aimed at ending the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). [^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

## Political positions

In June 2020, Barrot together with fellow party member [Patrick Mignola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Mignola) proposed a law to introduce [mail-in voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_voting) to facilitate voting during the public health crisis caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_France).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]
