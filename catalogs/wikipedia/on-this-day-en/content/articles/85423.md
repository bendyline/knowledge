**Lima**[^cite_note-5] is the [capital and largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_of_Peru) of [Peru](170691.md), as well as a [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city). It is located in the valleys of the [Chillón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chill%C3%B3n_River), [Rímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_River) and [Lurín Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lur%C3%ADn_River), in the desert zone of the central coastal part of the country, overlooking the Pacific Ocean. The city is considered the political, cultural, financial and commercial center of Peru. Jurisdictionally, the metropolis extends mainly within the [province of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lima) and in a smaller portion, to the west, within the [Constitutional Province of Callao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Province_of_Callao), where the seaport and the [Jorge Chávez Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_Airport) are located. Both provinces have had regional autonomy since 2002.&#x20;

The 2023 census projection indicates that the city of Lima has an estimated population of 10,092,000 inhabitants, making it the [second-most populous city in South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_Americas_by_population) after [São Paulo](390875.md) and the largest Spanish-speaking city in the world in terms of [city proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_proper) population.[^cite_note-inei20p33-6][^cite_note-7] The [Lima Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metropolitan_Area), which forms a contiguous urban area with the seaside city of [Callao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_District), has a population of 10,151,200 inhabitants.[^cite_note-inei20p33-6][^cite_note-8] When considering the additional seven districts contained in the [Constitutional Province of Callao](294857.md), the total agglomeration reaches a population of 11,342,100 inhabitants, one of the [most populated urban agglomerations in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_by_population).&#x20;

Lima was named by natives in the agricultural region known by [native Peruvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Peruvians) as *Limaq*. It became the capital and most important city in the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru). Following the [Peruvian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence), it became the capital of the Republic of Peru. Around one-third of the national population now lives in its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metropolitan_Area).&#x20;

As the headquarters of the [Andean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Community), Lima plays a crucial role in regional diplomacy and trade integration. In October 2013, Lima was chosen to host the [2019 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pan_American_Games); these games were held at venues in and around Lima, and were the largest sporting event ever hosted by the country. The city will host the games for a second time in [2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Pan_American_Games).[^cite_note-9] It also hosted the [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC) Meetings three times in 2008, 2016 and 2024; the [Annual Meetings of the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Meetings_of_the_International_Monetary_Fund_and_the_World_Bank_Group) in October 2015, the [United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) in December 2014, and the [Miss Universe 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Universe_1982) contest.&#x20;

## Etymology

Before the [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca) occupation of the area in the 15th century, a famous oracle in the [Rímac Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_Valley) had come to be known by visitors as *Limaq* (**Limaq**, pronounced [\[ˈli.mɑq\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Quechua), which means "talker" or "speaker" in the [coastal Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_II) that was the area's primary language before the Spanish arrival). This oracle was eventually destroyed by the Spanish and replaced with a church, but the name persisted: the chronicles show "Límac" replacing "Ychma" as the common name for the area.[^cite_note-Limaq-10]&#x20;

Modern scholars speculate that the word "Lima" originated as the Spanish pronunciation of the native name Limaq. Linguistic evidence seems to support this theory, as spoken Spanish consistently rejects [stop consonants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_consonant) at the ends of words.&#x20;

The city was founded in 1535 under the name City of [Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Magi) (Spanish: *Ciudad de los Reyes*), because its foundation was decided on January 6, date of the feast of the [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_\(holiday\)). This name quickly fell into disuse, and *Lima* became the city's name of choice. After [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn) reached Lima in 1821 during the [Peruvian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence), the city was granted the title of *Heroica y Esforzada Ciudad de los Libres* ("Heroic and Courageous City of the Free") in September of the same year.[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

The river that flows through Lima is called [Rímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_River), and many people erroneously assume that this is because its original Inca name is "Talking River" (the Incas spoke a highland variety of Quechua, in which the word for "talker" was pronounced [\[ˈrimɑq\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Quechua)).[^cite_note-12] However, the original inhabitants of the valley were not Incas. This name is an innovation arising from an effort by the [Cuzco](72735.md) nobility in colonial times to standardize the toponym so that it would conform to the phonology of [Cuzco Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusco_Quechua).&#x20;

Later, as the original inhabitants died out and the local [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechuan_languages) became extinct, the Cuzco pronunciation prevailed. Nowadays, Spanish-speaking locals do not see the connection between the name of their city and the name of the river that runs through it. They often assume that the valley is named after the river; however, Spanish documents from the colonial period show the opposite to be true.[^cite_note-Limaq-10]&#x20;

## Symbols

### Flag

Main article: [Flag of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Lima)

The [Flag of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Lima) is historically known as "Banner of the City of the Kings of Peru". It is formed by a [golden-colored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_\(color\)) silk canvas and in the center is the embroidered coat of arms of the city.[^cite_note-flag-13]&#x20;

### Coat of arms

Main article: [Coat of arms of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Lima)

The [coat of arms of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Lima) was granted by the [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Spain) on 7 December 1537, through a *[real cédula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_c%C3%A9dula)* signed in [Valladolid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valladolid) by [Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and his mother, Queen [Joanna of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_of_Castile). It is formed by a main field [azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azure_\(heraldry\)), with three [gold crowns of kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_\(heraldry\)) placed in a triangle and above them a gold star that touches the three crowns with its points, and in the [orle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orle_\(heraldry\)) some gold letters that say: *Hoc signum vere regum est (This is the true sign of the kings).* Outside the shield are the initials I and K (Ioana and Karolus), which are the names of Queen Joanna and her son Charles V. A star is placed above the letters and two crowned [sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabre)-faced eagles embracing them, which hold the coat of arms.&#x20;

### Anthem

The anthem of Lima was heard for the first time on 18 January 2008, in a solemn session that was attended by the then [President of Peru](239109.md) [Alan García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Garc%C3%ADa), the mayor of the city [Luis Castañeda Lossio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Casta%C3%B1eda) and various authorities such as city councilors Luis Enrique Tord (author of the lyrics), Euding Maeshiro (composer of the melody) and the musical producer Ricardo Núñez (arranger).[^cite_note-14] It was officially adopted on 7 October 2010.[^cite_note-15]&#x20;

## History

Main articles: [History of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lima) and [Timeline of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Lima)

### Precolonial



[Pachacámac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachac%C3%A1mac), built from 3rd century to 15th century, was one of the most important pre-Columbian centers of pilgrimage on the Peruvian Coast.

[^cite_note-Guide_to_Peru-17]

&#x20;In the image the Temple of the Moon.

Although the history of the city of Lima began with its Spanish foundation in 1535, the territory formed by the valleys of the [Rímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_River), [Chillón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chill%C3%B3n_River) and [Lurín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lur%C3%ADn_River) rivers was occupied by pre-Inca settlements, which were grouped under the [Lordship of Ichma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichma_culture).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConleeDulantoMackayStanish2004218-18] The Maranga culture and the [Lima culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_culture) were the ones that established and forged an identity in these territories. During those times, the sanctuaries of Lati (current [Puruchuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puruchuco)) and [Pachacámac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachac%C3%A1mac) (the main pilgrimage sanctuary during the time of the Incas) were built, it was built from 3rd century to 15th century by several civilizations, and which was used even until the time the [Spanish conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) arrived.&#x20;

These cultures were conquered by the [Wari Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_culture) during the height of its imperial expansion. It is during this time that the ceremonial center of [Cajamarquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cajamarquilla) was built.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-James_Higgins-20] As Wari importance declined, local cultures regained autonomy, highlighting the [Chancay culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancay_culture). Later, in the 15th century, these territories were incorporated into the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire).[^cite_note-21] From this time we can find a great variety of [huacas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca) throughout the city, some of which are under investigation.&#x20;

The most important or well-known huacas are those of [Huallamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Huallamarca), [Pucllana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Pucllana), and Mateo Salado, all located in the middle of Lima districts with very high urban growth, so they are surrounded by business and residential buildings; however, that does not prevent its perfect state of conservation.&#x20;

During the time of the Incas, the valley of Lima was highly populated and organized into an Inca province, or huamani (wamani), called Pachacamac. The colonial Spanish historian [Bernabé Cobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernab%C3%A9_Cobo) mentions that the huamani of Pachacamac was subdivided into three hunu of tributary men, rather than the conventional four hunu. It has also been argued that a fourth hunu may have existed but was not recorded.[^cite_note-\:2-22] The primary meaning of the word hunu in Quechua is 10,000, leading to the assumption that 30,000 families lived in the valley. This assumption has been criticized, including by the historian Åke Wedin, because hunu can also mean countless, and therefore could simply refer to a very large group of men. The scholar [John Rowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howland_Rowe) suggested that the valley had a population of about 150,000 during Inca times.[^cite_note-23]&#x20;

&#x20;Whatever the case, each recorded hunu of Pachacamac had a head town, corresponding to some of the most populated settlements in the valley: Caraguayllo ([Carabayllo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabayllo_District)), Maranga, and Surco (or Sulco, also known as the archaeological site Armatambo).[^cite_note-\:2-22]

> ... this valley was divided, according to the government of the Inca kings, into three 'unos' or governorships of ten thousand families each; the town of Caraguayllo was the head of the first; that of Maranga, which is situated in the middle of the valley, of the second, and the third, that of Surco; this last town was the largest of all ...[^cite_note-24]

The inhabitants of the pre-Columbian town of Surco were relocated to the modern district of Santiago de Surco early in the colonial period. In addition to Aymara and Quechua, the inhabitants of the northern part of the valley, specifically in the hunu of Carabayllo, spoke an additional language believed to be [Quingnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quingnam).[^cite_note-25]&#x20;

Regarding the pre-Hispanic settlement of Lima, it is recorded that this part of the valley, near the Rimac river, was administered by a curaca, or local lord, named Taulichusco. He was a former [yana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanakuna), or servant, of Mama Vilo, one of the wives of Emperor Huayna Capac. Lima was awarded to Taulichusco in recognition of his services to the Inca royalty. Some of Peru's most important buildings were erected on the sites of major constructions of the pre-Hispanic settlement. For example, the residential palace of Taulichusco was located where the modern [Palacio de Gobierno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_Gobierno) of Peru stands today. A temple called Puma Inti once occupied the site where the [Metropolitan Cathedral of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima) is now, and the [Municipal Theater of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Municipal_de_Lima) is situated where a pre-Columbian structure, referred to as Huaca El Cabildo by the Spaniards, once stood. These buildings were centered around a plaza, which was later expanded to become the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima). The Huaca de Aliaga and Huaca Riquelme were other major buildings near the plaza. Other nearby constructions included the temple-oracle of Rímac, one of the main places of worship in the valley, also known as the so-called "huaca grande" that once stood in [Barrios Altos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrios_Altos).[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

### Founding



The founding of Lima, painting by José Effio (1845–1920) in the [National Museum of Archaeology, Anthropology and History of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Archaeology,_Anthropology_and_History_of_Peru).

In 1532, the Spanish and their indigenous allies (from the ethnic groups subdued by the Incas) under the command of [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro) took monarch [Atahualpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atahualpa) prisoner in the city of [Cajamarca](554990.md). Although a ransom was paid, he was sentenced to death for political and strategic reasons. After some battles, the Spanish [conquered their empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Inca_Empire). The [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) named Francisco Pizarro governor of the lands he had conquered. Pizarro decided to found the capital in the Rímac river valley,[^cite_note-27] after a failed attempt to establish it in [Jauja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jauja).&#x20;

He considered that Lima was strategically located, close to a favorable coast for the construction of a [port](294857.md) but prudently far from it in order to prevent attacks by [pirates](50715.md) and foreign powers, on fertile lands and with a suitable cool climate. Thus, on 6 January 1535, Lima was founded with the name "City of the Kings", named in this way in honor of the [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_\(holiday\)),[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] on territories that had been of the [kuraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuraka) [Taulichusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taulichusco). The explanation of this name is due to the fact that "around the same time in January, the Spaniards were looking for the place to lay the foundation for the new city, \[...] not far from the [Pachacámac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachac%C3%A1mac) sanctuary, near the [Rímac river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_River).&#x20;

On the oldest Spanish maps of Peru, both *Lima* and *Ciudad de los Reyes* can be seen together. However, as had happened with the region, initially called [New Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_of_New_Castile) and later [Peru](170691.md), the City of the Kings soon lost its name in favor of "Lima". Pizarro, with the collaboration of [Nicolás de Ribera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_de_Ribera), Diego de Agüero and Francisco Quintero personally traced the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima) and the rest of the city grid, building the Viceroyalty Palace (today transformed into the [Government Palace of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Palace,_Peru), which hence retains the traditional name of *Casa de Pizarro*) and the [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima), whose first stone Pizarro laid with his own hands.[^cite_note-30] In August 1536, the flourishing city was besieged by the troops of the Inca general Quizu Yupanqui under orders from the monarch [Manco Inca Yupanqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manco_Inca_Yupanqui) who was in Cusco, but the Spanish and their indigenous allies managed to defeat them. The [Huaylas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaylas_Province) (Wayllas) army's assistance was of special importance to the Spanish. The army arrived personally led by Contarhuancho[^cite_note-31] (Kuntur-Wanchu), a secondary wife of the deceased Emperor [Wayna Qhapaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayna_Qhapaq) and now a respected kuraka of half the province of Huaylas, the Hanan Huaylas or Upper Huaylas moiety. Contarhuancho came to Lima after receiving a plea for help in a quipu message from her daughter, the Huaylas-Inca princess Doña [Inés Huaylas Yupanqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In%C3%A9s_Huaylas_Yupanqui).&#x20;

In the following years, Lima gained prestige by being designated the capital of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru) and the seat of a *[Real Audiencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Audiencia)* in 1543. Since the location of the coastal city was conditioned by the ease of communications with [Spain](26667.md), a close bond with the port of [Callao](294857.md) was soon established.[^cite_note-32]&#x20;

### Colonial period



[Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) [Metropolitan Cathedral of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima), built between 1602 and 1797 in the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima)

For the next century, it prospered as the center of an extensive trade network that integrated the viceroyalty with the [Americas](29833.md), [Europe](9239.md), and [East Asia](19605700.md). But the city was not without its dangers; violent earthquakes destroyed a large part of it between 1586 and 1687, leading to a great deal of construction activity. It is then when [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_\(water_supply\)), [starlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starling_\(structure\)) and retaining walls appear before the flooding of the rivers, the bridge over the [Rímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_District) is finished, the [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima) is built, and numerous hospitals, convents and monasteries are built. Then we can see that the city is articulated around its neighborhoods. Another threat was the presence of [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate) and [corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer) in the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), which motivated the construction of the [Walls of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Lima) between 1684 and 1687.[^cite_note-James_Higgins-20]&#x20;

The [1687 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1687_Peru_earthquake) marked a turning point in the [history of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lima), since it coincided with a recession in trade due to economic competition with other cities such as [Buenos Aires](255919.md). With the creation of the [Viceroyalty of New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Granada) in 1717, the political demarcations were reorganized, and Lima only lost some territories that actually already enjoyed their autonomy. In 1746 a [strong earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1746_Lima%E2%80%93Callao_earthquake) severely damaged the city and destroyed [Callao](294857.md), forcing a massive reconstruction effort by [Viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy) [José Antonio Manso de Velasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manso_de_Velasco,_1st_Count_of_Superunda).[^cite_note-33]&#x20;

During the 17th and 18th centuries, Lima was considered one of the most cosmopolitan cities in the entire [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), boasting a splendor that some observers considered worthy of a world power, attracting merchants from all over Europe, Asia and the rest of the Americas. The city was well known for being the residence of several members of the Spanish nobility, who resided in sumptuous manors and palaces located within the walls of Lima; the large number of religious orders within the city was also recognized.&#x20;

In the second half of the 18th century, [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) ideas about [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) and social control influenced the development of the city. During this period, the Peruvian capital was affected by the [Bourbon reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_reforms) as it lost its monopoly on foreign trade and its control over the important mining region of [Upper Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Peru). This economic weakening led the elite of the city to depend on the positions granted by the viceregal government and the [Church](606848.md), which contributed to keeping them more linked to the Crown than to the cause of independence.&#x20;

The greatest political-economic impact that the city experienced at that time occurred with the creation of the [Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) in 1776, which changed the course and orientations imposed by the new mercantile traffic. Among the buildings built during this period there is the Coliseo de Gallos, the [Acho Bullring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_toros_de_Acho) and the General Cemetery. The first two were erected to regulate these popular activities, centralizing them in one place, while the cemetery put an end to the practice of burying the dead in churches, considered unhealthy by public authorities.&#x20;

### Independence



[Painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamaci%C3%B3n_de_la_Independencia_del_Per%C3%BA) of [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn) during the Declaration of Independence of Peru in the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima), on July 28, 1821

A combined expedition of Argentine and Chilean independence fighters led by General Don [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn) landed in southern Lima in 1820, but did not attack the city. Faced with a naval blockade and guerrilla action on the mainland, Viceroy [José de la Serna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_la_Serna,_1st_Count_of_the_Andes) was forced to evacuate the city in July 1821 to save the [Royalist army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist_\(Spanish_American_independence\)). Fearing a popular uprising and lacking the means to impose the order, the City Council invited San Martín to enter the city, signing a Declaration of Independence at his request.&#x20;

Once General [San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn) proclaimed the [independence of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence),[^cite_note-40] Lima became the capital of the new [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Peru). Thus, it was the seat of the government of the liberator and also the seat of the first [Constituent Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Congress_of_Peru,_1822) that the country had.[^cite_note-41] The war lasted for two more years, during which the city changed hands many times and suffered abuses from both sides. By the time the war was decided, on 9 December 1824, at the [Battle of Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ayacucho), Lima had been considerably impoverished.&#x20;

### Republican era

After the War of Independence, Lima became the capital of the [Republic of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Peru). Its development was stymied by the country's socioeconomic paralysis until the 1850s, when income from the export of [guano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guano) allowed for rapid expansion. In the following twenty years, the State financed the construction of large public buildings to replace the old viceregal establishments, among these are the Central Market, the General Slaughterhouse, the Mental Asylum, the [Penitentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Penitentiary) and the Hospital Dos de Mayo. There were also improvements in communications; in 1850 a railway line between Lima and Callao was completed from the [Desamparados station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desamparados_station) and in 1870 an iron bridge was inaugurated over the Rímac River, baptized as Puente Balta.&#x20;

In 1872 the [colonial City Walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Lima) were demolished by the US engineer [Henry Meiggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Meiggs) under contract with the Peruvian government,[^cite_note-44] in anticipation of further urban growth in the future. However, this period of economic expansion also widened the gap between rich and poor, producing widespread social unrest. The famous, [Arco del Puente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_of_Lima), the gate of the [colonial wall of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Lima), was consumed by a fire caused by street vendors in 1879.[^cite_note-Arco_Puente2-42]&#x20;

During the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) (1879–1883), the [Chilean army occupied Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Lima) after defeating Peruvian troops and reserves in the battles of [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Juan_and_Chorrillos) and [Miraflores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Miraflores). The city suffered from the invaders, who looted museums, public libraries and educational institutions. At the same time, angry mobs attacked wealthy citizens and the Asian colony, looting their properties and businesses.&#x20;

### 20th century



The [Metropolitan Cathedral of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima) in [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima) in 1910.

At the beginning of the 20th century, the construction of avenues that would serve as a matrix for the development of the city began.[^cite_note-45] The avenues [Paseo de la República](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paseo_de_la_Rep%C3%BAblica), Leguía (today called Avenida Arequipa), Avenida Brasil and the landscaping Salaverry that headed south and Venezuela and Colonial avenues to the west joining the port of [Callao](294857.md).[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

In the 1930s the great constructions began with the remodeling of the [Government Palace of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Palace,_Peru) and the [Palacio Municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_Municipal_de_Lima). These constructions reached their peak in the 1950s, during the government of [Manuel A. Odría](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_A._Odr%C3%ADa), when the great buildings of the [Ministry of Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Finance_\(Peru\)) and the Ministry of Education were built ([Javier Alzamora Valdez Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Alzamora_Valdez_Building), currently the seat of the [Superior Court of Justice of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_District_of_Lima)), the [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_\(Peru\)), the [Ministry of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Labour_and_Promotion_of_Employment_\(Peru\)) and the Hospitals of the Workers' Insurance and of the Employee as well as the [National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stadium_of_Peru) and several large housing units.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]&#x20;

Also in those years a phenomenon began that changed the configuration of the city, which was the massive immigration of inhabitants from the interior of the country, producing the exponential growth of the capital's population and the consequent urban expansion.[^cite_note-Golte-49] The new populations were settling on land near the center which was used as an agricultural area. The current districts of [Lince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lince_District), [La Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Victoria_District,_Lima) to the south were populated; [Breña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre%C3%B1a) and [Pueblo Libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblo_Libre_District,_Lima) to the west; [El Agustino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Agustino), [Ate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ate_District) and [San Juan de Lurigancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_Lurigancho) to the east and [San Martín de Porres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_District) and [Comas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comas_District,_Lima) to the north.&#x20;



[Plaza San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_San_Mart%C3%ADn,_Lima) in 1970

As an emblematic point of this expansion, in 1973 the self-managed community of Villa El Salvador (current district of [Villa El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_El_Salvador)) was created, located 30 km south of the city center and currently integrated into the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-50] In the 1940s, Lima started a period of rapid growth spurred by migration from the Andean region, as rural people sought opportunities for work and education. The population, estimated at 600,000 in 1940, reached 1.9 million by 1960 and 4.8 million by 1980.[^cite_note-51] At the start of this period, the urban area was confined to a triangular area bounded by the city's [historic center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Lima), [Callao](294857.md) and [Chorrillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorrillos_District); in the following decades settlements spread to the north, beyond the Rímac River, to the east, along the Central Highway and to the south.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDietz198035-52] The new migrants, at first confined to [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum) in downtown Lima, led this expansion through large-scale land invasions, which evolved into shanty towns, known as *[pueblos jóvenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblos_j%C3%B3venes)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDietz198036.-53] In the 1980s, terrorist violence added to the disorderly growth of the city and the increase in the number of internally displaced people. Terrorist groups like the [Shining Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shining_Path) and [Túpac Amaru Revolutionary Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BApac_Amaru_Revolutionary_Movement) were centered in Lima during this time. The historic center of the city suffered increasing deterioration and many areas of the city constantly lacked basic services. The city improved and terrorism decreased moving into the 21st century.&#x20;

### 21st century



View of the Bajada Balta in the [Miraflores District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_District,_Lima) at night

The city began to recover from the violence from the [1980s and 90s](9624647.md). The Municipality of Lima began to reorganize the city, remodeling parks, streets, and buildings. The early 2000s and 2010s saw a growth in high rise buildings, especially in the economic center of the [San Isidro District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima). The tallest building in Lima and all of Peru, the [Torre Banco de la Nación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Banco_de_la_Naci%C3%B3n), was completed in 2015. Construction of buildings grew 3.9% in 2024, following the countries economic growth.[^cite_note-54] In 2007, the city established [El Metropolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Metropolitano), a rapid bus transport system operating from the north to the south of the city. The [Lima and Callao Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_and_Callao_Metro) Line 1 was finally completed in 2010, after numerous halts in the 1970s and 90s. The second line of the metro began construction in the early 2020s.&#x20;

The construction of the new terminal of the [Jorge Chávez International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_International_Airport) began in 2019, but was delayed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Peru) and continued in 2021. The terminal was inaugurated on 1 June 2025, allowing transit for an estimated 40 million passengers per year by 2030.[^cite_note-55] The [Port of Chancay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Chancay) in the city of [Chancay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancay), was inaugurated on 14 November, 2024, in a joint construction performed by Peru and China. The port is the new main port of Lima and is one of the largest in South America.&#x20;

Lima was selected to host the [2019 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pan_American_Games) in 2013. The event was the largest sporting event hosted by the country. The city was selected to host the [2027 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Pan_American_Games) for the second time in 2024. The city was also selected to joint host the [2025 Bolivarian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bolivarian_Games) along with [Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayacucho).&#x20;

After the [2022 Peruvian self-coup attempt](72438093.md) by then president [Pedro Castillo](49637220.md), [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_protests_\(2022%E2%80%932023\)) emerged around the city and the country between 2022 and 2023 demanding for the release of Castillo and for the removing of president [Dina Boluarte](67870874.md). Thousands of protesters began on 12 January 2022, in what was known as the *Toma de Lima*, in which the city announced a 4-month state of emergency. The [National Police of Peru](14896441.md) and [Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Peru) were stationed, responsible for the death of 58 people, 1 being from Lima, during the protests between 2022 and 2023. Protests died down towards the end of 2023.&#x20;

## Geography



Lima as seen from the [International Space Station](15043.md)

The urban area covers about 800 square kilometres (310 square miles). It is located on mostly flat terrain in the [Peruvian coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sechura_Desert), within the valleys of the [Chillón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chill%C3%B3n_River), [Rímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_River) and [Lurín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lur%C3%ADn_River) rivers. The city slopes gently from the shores of the Pacific Ocean into valleys and mountain slopes located as high as 1,550 meters (5,090 ft) above sea level. Within the city are isolated hills that are not connected to the surrounding hill chains, such as El Agustino, San Cosme, El Pino, La Milla, Muleria and Pro hills. [San Cristobal hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_Hill,_Lima) in Rímac District, which lies directly north of the downtown area, is the local extreme of an Andean hill outgrowth.&#x20;

[Metropolitan Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_metropolitan_area) covers 2,672.28 square kilometres (1,031.77 sq mi), of which 825.88 km<sup>2</sup> (318.87 sq mi) (31%) comprise the actual city and 1,846.40 km<sup>2</sup> (712.90 sq mi) (69%) the city outskirts.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56] The urban area extends around 60 kilometres (37 miles) from north to south and around 30 km (19 mi) from west to east. The city center is located 15 km (9.3 mi) inland at the shore of the Rímac River, a vital resource for the city, since it carries what will become drinking water for its inhabitants and fuels the hydroelectric dams that provide electricity to the area. While no official administrative definition for the city exists, it is usually considered to be composed of the central 30 of 43 districts of [Lima Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Province), corresponding to an urban area centered around the historic [Cercado de Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_District) district. The city is the core of the [Lima Metro Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metropolitan_Area), one of the ten [largest metro areas in the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_the_Americas). Lima is the world's third largest desert city, after [Karachi](17123.md), Pakistan, and [Cairo](6293.md), Egypt.&#x20;

### Climate



[Parque de la Exposición](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_la_Exposici%C3%B3n)

Lima has a mild climate, despite its location in the tropics and in a [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert).[^cite_note-57] Lima's proximity to the waters of the Pacific Ocean leads to intense maritime moderation of the temperatures, thereby making the climate much milder than those to be expected for a tropical desert, and thus Lima can be classified as a [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_deserts) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BWh*) with [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate) temperature ranges.[^cite_note-Peel-58] Temperatures rarely fall below 12 °C (54 °F) or rise above 30 °C (86 °F).[^cite_note-59] Two distinct seasons can be identified: summer, December through April, and winter from June through September/October. May and October/November are generally transition months, with a more dramatic warm-to-cool weather transition in later May or/and earlier June.[^cite_note-60] Situated onshore from the cold ocean waters, rainfall is extremely rare in Lima.&#x20;

The summers, December through April, are sunny, hot, and muggy.[^cite_note-61] Daily temperatures oscillate between lows of 18 to 22 °C (64 to 72 °F) and highs of 25 to 30 °C (77 to 86 °F). Coastal fogs occur in some mornings and high clouds in some afternoons and evenings. Summer sunsets are colorful, known by locals as "cielo de brujas" (Spanish for "sky of witches"), since the sky commonly turns shades of orange, pink, and red around 7 pm.&#x20;



Weather averages for the [Jorge Chávez International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_International_Airport)

During winter, June through October, the weather is dramatically different. Grey skies, breezy conditions, higher humidity, and cooler temperatures prevail. Long 10 to 15-day stretches of dark overcast skies are common. Persistent morning drizzle ([garúa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gar%C3%BAa)) frequently occurs from June through September, coating the streets with a thin layer of water that generally dries up by early afternoon. Winter temperatures vary little between day and night. They range from lows of 14 to 16 °C (57 to 61 °F) and highs of 16 to 19 °C (61 to 66 °F), rarely exceeding 20 °C (68 °F) except in the easternmost districts.[^cite_note-WMO-62]&#x20;

[Relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) is always very high, particularly in the mornings.[^cite_note-BBC_weather-63] High humidity produces brief morning fog in the early summer and a usually persistent low cloud deck during the winter (generally develops in late May and persists until mid-November or even early December). The predominantly onshore flow makes the Lima area one of the cloudiest among the entire Peruvian coast. Lima has only 1284 hours of sunshine a year, 27.9 hours in August and 183 hours in April, which is exceptionally little for its latitude.[^cite_note-Molina-64] By comparison, London has an average of 1653 hours, and Moscow 1731. Winter cloudiness prompts locals to seek sunshine in Andean valleys 500 meters (1,600 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_mean_sea_level).&#x20;

While relative humidity is high, rainfall is very low due to strong atmospheric stability. The low rainfall impacts the city's water supply, which originates from wells and from rivers that flow from the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes).[^cite_note-65] Inland districts receive anywhere between 10 and 60 mm (0.4 and 2.4 in) of rainfall per year, which accumulates mainly during the winter. Coastal districts receive only 10 to 30 mm (0.4 to 1.2 in). As previously mentioned, winter precipitation occurs as persistent morning drizzle. These are locally called 'garúa', 'llovizna' or '[camanchacas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camanchaca)'. On the other hand, summer rain is infrequent and occurs in the form of isolated light and brief showers. These generally occur during afternoons and evenings when leftovers from Andean storms arrive from the east. The lack of heavy rainfall arises from high atmospheric stability caused, in turn, by the combination of cool waters from semi-permanent coastal upwelling and the presence of the cold [Humboldt Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Current) and warm air aloft associated with the South Pacific anticyclone.&#x20;

Lima's climate (like most of coastal Peru) gets severely disrupted in [El Niño](317311.md) events. Coastal waters usually average around 17–19 °C (63–66 °F), but can get much warmer (as in 1998 when the water reached 26 °C (79 °F)). Air temperatures rise accordingly.&#x20;

## Government



[Government Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Palace_\(Peru\)) of Peru

### National

Lima is the capital city of the [Republic of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Peru) and [Lima Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Province). As such, it is home to the three branches of the [Government of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Peru). In the same way, all Ministries have their main headquarters in the capital. Since mostly all of the country's political, industrial, and financial power is concentrated in Lima, this has led to a severe [centralisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralisation) as evidenced by the disproportionate population and economic size of the capital city compared to other major cities in the country, such as Trujillo, Chiclayo, Piura, Arequipa, and Cuzco.&#x20;

The [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Peru) is headquartered in the [Government Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Palace_\(Peru\)), located in the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima). The Palace, also known as the House of Pizarro, was first constructed in 1535 by [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro). It was renovated in 1937 and serves as the [official residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_residence) of the [president of Peru](239109.md). All national [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Peruvian_Ministries) are located in the city.&#x20;

&#x20;The [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) is headquartered in the [Legislative Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Palace_\(Peru\)) and is home to the [Congress of the Republic of Peru](1571007.md). Built in 1907, it is located on the Ayacucho street in the [historic centre of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_centre_of_Lima) and serves as the seat of the [Congress of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Peru).



[Palace of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Justice,_Lima) of Peru



[Legislative Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Palace_\(Peru\)) of Peru

The [Judicial branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_System_of_Peru) is headquartered in the [Palace of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Justice_\(Peru\)) and is home to the [Supreme Court of Peru](8801058.md). The [Palace of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Justice_\(Peru\)) in Lima is seat of the [Supreme Court of Justice](8801058.md) the highest judicial court in Peru with jurisdiction over the entire territory of Peru.&#x20;

Lima is seat of two of the 28-second highest or [Superior Courts of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Courts_of_Justice_of_Peru). The first and oldest Superior Court in Lima is the *Superior Court of Justice,* belonging to the [Judicial District and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_District_of_Lima). Due to the [judicial organization of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_System_of_Peru), the highest concentration of courts is located in Lima despite the fact that its judicial district has jurisdiction over only 35 of the [43 districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Lima).[^cite_note-71] The *Superior Court of the Cono Norte* is the second Superior Court located in Lima and is part of the [Judicial District of North Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_District_of_Cono_Norte). This judicial district has jurisdiction over the remaining eight districts, all located in northern Lima.[^cite_note-72]&#x20;

### Local



[Lima City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_Municipal_de_Lima)

Main article: [Metropolitan Municipality of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Municipality_of_Lima)

The city is roughly equivalent to the [Province of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lima), which is subdivided into [43 districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Lima). The [Metropolitan Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Municipality_of_Lima) has authority over the entire city, while each district has its own local government. Unlike the rest of the country, the Metropolitan Municipality, although a [provincial municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Peru), acts as and has functions similar to a [regional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Governments_of_Peru), as it does not belong to any of the [25 regions of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Peru). Each of the 43 districts has their own district municipality that is in charge of its own district and coordinate with the metropolitan municipality.&#x20;

The Municipality of Lima is based in the [Palacio Municipal de Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_Municipal_de_Lima), also known as the Lima City Hall. It is located in the Plaza de Armas and was originally built in 1549. However, it was rebuilt in 1939. The current mayor of Lima is [Renzo Reggiardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Reggiardo).&#x20;

### Political system

Unlike the rest of the country, the Metropolitan Municipality has functions of regional government and is not part of any administrative region, according to Article 65. 27867 of the Law of Regional Governments enacted on 16 November 2002, 87 The previous political organization remains in the sense that a Governor is the political authority for the department and the city. The functions of this authority are mostly police and military. The same city administration covers the local municipal authority.&#x20;

Lima has been rocked by corruption scandals: former mayors [Susana Villaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susana_Villar%C3%A1n) (2011–2014) and [Luis Castaneda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Casta%C3%B1eda) (2003–2010 and 2014–2018) were remanded in custody as part of the bribery scandal involving the Brazilian construction company [Odebrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odebrecht). [Jorge Munoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Mu%C3%B1oz_\(Peruvian_politician\)) (mayor from 2019 to 2022), was removed from office for illegally holding several offices and the related allowances.&#x20;

### International organizations

Lima is home to the headquarters of the [Andean Community of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Community_of_Nations) that is a [customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_union) formed in 1973 as the Andean Pact. The union comprises the South American countries of [Bolivia](3462.md), [Colombia](5222.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), and [Peru](170691.md), among other regional and international organizations. Lima is also home to the headquarters of the [South American Council of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Council_of_Health).&#x20;

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lima)



People of Lima

With a population of 10,151,200 for the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metropolitan_Area) and a population density of 3,746 inhabitants per square kilometer (9,700/sq mi) as of 2023,[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-73] Lima ranks as one of the [most populous agglomeration in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_largest_cities), and the second biggest city in South America in terms of population within city limits, after [São Paulo](390875.md).[^cite_note-74]&#x20;

### Ethnic groups

According to the [2017 Peruvian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Peruvian_census), [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) of mixed [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) and [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emigration) (mostly Spanish and [Italians](14532.md)) ancestry are the largest ethnic group, making up 69.9% of the population in 2018.[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-73] [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_people) are the second largest ethnic group, making up 16.8% of the population. [White Peruvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Peruvians) are the third largest group, making up 7.4% of the population.[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-73] Many are of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Peruvian), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peruvian) or [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Peruvian) descent; many others are of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Peruvian), [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Peruvian), or [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Peruvian) descent.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] The minorities in Lima include the [Afro-Peruvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Peruvians), who make up 2.9% of the population, whose African ancestors were initially brought to the region as [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery), and the [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_people), who make up 0.7% of the population. Communities of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Latin_America) of European descent and Middle Easterners are also found in Lima. 2.3% of Lima's population identify with other ethnic groups, including Asians. Lima's [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Peruvian) community is made up primarily of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Peruvian) (Cantonese) and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Peruvian) descendants, whose ancestors came mostly in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The city has, by far, the largest Chinese diaspora in Latin America.[^cite_note-77]&#x20;

### Settlement



*Market in the Plaza of the Inquisition* (Lima) by [Johann Moritz Rugendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Moritz_Rugendas), ca. 1843

[^cite_note-78]

The first settlement in what would become Lima was made up of 117 housing blocks. In 1562, another district was built across the Rímac River and in 1610, the first stone bridge was built. Lima then had a population of around 26,000; blacks made up around 40% and whites made up around 38%.[^cite_note-79] By 1748, the white population totaled 16,000–18,000.[^cite_note-80] In 1861, the number of inhabitants surpassed 100,000 and by 1927, had doubled.&#x20;

During the early 20th century, thousands of immigrants came to the city, including people of European descent. They organized social clubs and built their own schools. Examples are The American-Peruvian school, the [Alianza Francesa de Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_fran%C3%A7aise), the Lycée Franco-Péruvien and the hospital Maison de Sante; [Markham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markham_College), the British-Peruvian school in Monterrico, [Antonio Raymondi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Raymondi_District) Italian School, the Pestalozzi Swiss School and also, several German-Peruvian schools.&#x20;

Chinese and a lesser number of Japanese came to Lima and established themselves in the [Barrios Altos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrios_Altos) neighborhood in downtown Lima. Lima residents refer to their [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Lima) as *Barrio chino* or *Calle Capon* and the city's ubiquitous [Chifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chifa) restaurants – small, sit-down, usually Chinese-run restaurants serving the Peruvian spin on Chinese cuisine – can be found by the dozens in this enclave.&#x20;

### Population

In 2014, the [National Institute for Statistics and Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Nacional_de_Estad%C3%ADstica_e_Inform%C3%A1tica) reported that the population in Lima's districts was 9,752,000 people, including the [Constitutional Province of Callao](294857.md). The city and ([metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_metropolitan_area)) represents around 29% of the national population. Of the city's population 48.7% are men and 51.3% are women. The districts in Metropolitan Lima are divided into 5 areas: [Cono Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Norte) (North Lima), [Cono Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Este) (East Lima), Constitutional Province of Callao, [Cono Centro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Centro) (Central Lima) and [Cono Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Sur_\(Lima\)) (South Lima). The largest areas are Lima Norte with 2,475,432 people and Lima Este with 2,619,814 people, including the largest single district San Juan de Lurigancho, which hosts 1 million people.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;



[Metropolitan Cathedral of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima), seat of the [Archdiocese of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Lima)

Lima is considered a "young" city. According to INEI, by mid 2014 the age distribution in Lima was: 24.3% between 0 and 14, 27.2% between 15 and 29, 22.5% between 30 and 44, 15.4% between 45 and 59 and 10.6% above 60.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

Migration to Lima from the rest of Peru is substantial. In 2013, 3,480,000 people reported arriving from other regions. This represents almost 36% of the entire population of Metropolitan Lima. The three regions that supply most of the migrants are Junin, Ancash and Ayacucho. By contrast only 390,000 emigrated from Lima to other regions.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

The annual population growth rate is 1.57%. Some of the 43 metropolitan districts are considerably more populous than others. For example, San Juan de Lurigancho, San Martin de Porres, Ate, Comas, Villa El Salvador and Villa Maria del Triunfo host more than 400,000, while San Luis, San Isidro, Magdalena del Mar, Lince and Barranco have less than 60,000 residents.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

A 2005 household survey study shows a socio-economic distribution for households in Lima. It used a monthly family income of 6,000 soles (around US\$1,840) or more for socioeconomic level A; between 2,000 soles (US\$612) and 6,000 soles (US\$1,840) for level B; from 840 soles (US\$257) to 2,000 soles (US\$612) for level C; from 420 soles (US\$128) to 1200 soles (US\$368) for level D; and up to 840 soles (US\$257) for level E. In Lima, 18% were in level E; 32.3% in level D; 31.7% in level C; 14.6% in level B; and 3.4% in level A. In this sense, 82% of the population lives in households that earn less than 2000 soles (or US\$612) monthly. Other salient differences between socioeconomic levels include levels of higher education, car ownership and home size.[^cite_note-81]&#x20;

In Metropolitan Lima in 2013, the percentage of the population living in households in poverty was 12.8%. The level of poverty is measured by households that are unable to access a basic food and other household goods and services, such as clothing, housing, education, transportation and health. The level of poverty has decreased from 2011 (15.6%) and 2012 (14.5%). Lima Sur is the area in Lima with the highest proportion of poverty (17.7%), followed by Lima Este (14.5%), Lima Norte (14.1%) and Lima Centro (6.2%). In addition 0.2% of the population lives in extreme poverty, meaning that they are unable to access a basic food basket.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

## Economy



Office buildings in [San Isidro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima)



The [Lima Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Stock_Exchange) building

Lima is the country's industrial and financial center and one of Latin America's most important financial centers,[^cite_note-82] home to many national companies and hotels. It accounts for more than two-thirds of Peru's industrial production[^cite_note-83] and most of its [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy).&#x20;

The metropolitan area, with around 7,000 factories,[^cite_note-studyabroaddomain.com-84] is the main location of industry. Products include textiles, clothing and food. Chemicals, fish, leather and oil derivatives are manufactured and processed.[^cite_note-studyabroaddomain.com-84] The financial district is in [San Isidro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima), while much of the industrial activity takes place in the west of the city, extending to the airport in [Callao](294857.md). Lima has the largest export industry in South America and is a regional center for the cargo industry. Industrialization began in the 1930s and by 1950, through [import substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import_substitution_industrialization) policies, manufacturing made up 14% of [GNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNP). In the late 1950s, up to 70% of consumer goods were manufactured in factories located in Lima.[^cite_note-85] The Callao [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) is one of the main fishing and commerce ports in South America, covering over 47 hectares (120 acres) and shipping 20.7 million metric tons of cargo in 2007.[^cite_note-86] The main export goods are commodities: oil, steel, silver, zinc, cotton, sugar and coffee.&#x20;

The city is home to a variety of national and transnational company headquarters, many of which are located in modern buildings constructed in different sectors, especially in the San Isidro district, which has become the city's financial center in recent decades. The metropolitan area, with nearly eight thousand factories, is also the country's center of industrial development, thanks to the quantity and quality of the available labor force and the infrastructure of the city's internal roads and highways.&#x20;

As of 2003, Lima generated 53% of GDP.[^cite_note-87] Most foreign companies in Peru settled in Lima.&#x20;

In 2007, the Peruvian economy grew 9%, the largest growth rate in South America.[^cite_note-88] The [Lima Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Stock_Exchange) rose 185.24% in 2006[^cite_note-89] and in 2007 by another 168.3%,[^cite_note-90] making it then one of the fastest growing stock exchanges in the world. In 2006, the [Lima Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Stock_Exchange) was the world's most profitable.[^cite_note-proinversion.gob.pe-91]&#x20;

The [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Summit 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC_Peru_2008) and the [Latin America, the Caribbean and the European Union Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America,_the_Caribbean_and_the_European_Union_Summit) were held there.&#x20;

Lima is the headquarters for banks such as [Banco de Crédito del Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_Cr%C3%A9dito_del_Per%C3%BA), [Scotiabank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotiabank) Perú, [Interbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbank), [Bank of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_the_Nation_\(Peru\)), Banco Continental, MiBanco, Banco Interamericano de Finanzas, Banco Financiero, [Banco de Comercio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_Comercio) and CrediScotia. It is a regional headquarters for [Standard Chartered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chartered). Insurance companies based in Lima include Rimac Seguros, Mapfre Peru, Interseguro, Pacifico, Protecta and La Positiva.[^cite_note-92]&#x20;

360° panoramic view of the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima)

As the main entry point to the country, Lima has developed an important tourism industry, among which its [Historic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Lima), its archaeological centers, its nightlife, museums, art galleries, [festivities](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Festivities_of_Peru\&action=edit\&redlink=1) and popular traditions stand out. According to Mastercard's Global Destination Cities Index, in 2014, Lima was the most visited city of Latin America and was the 20th city globally, with 5.11 million visitors.[^cite_note-93] In 2019,[^cite_note-94] Lima is the top destination in South America, with 2.63 million international visitors in 2018 and a growth forecast of 10.00% percent for 2019.&#x20;



The [Catacombs of the Basilica of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_San_Francisco,_Lima) was the Old cemetery of the city during all the colonial times, until 1810. It contains bones of some 70,000 [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonist) people.

[^cite_note-95]

The [Historic Center of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Lima), which includes part of the districts of [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_District) and [Rímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_District), was declared a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 1988 due to the importance that the city had during the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru), leaving as testimony a large number of architectural legacies.[^cite_note-96] Highlights include the [Basilica and Convent of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_San_Francisco,_Lima), the [Plaza Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima), the [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima), the [Basilica and Convent of Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_Santo_Domingo,_Lima), the [Palacio de Torre Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_Torre_Tagle), among others. The tour of the churches of the city is very popular among tourists. In a short walk through the city center we can find many, several of which date from the 16th and 17th centuries.&#x20;



[Basilica of Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_Santo_Domingo,_Lima), built between 1678 and 1766. It holds the [tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb) of the saints [Rose of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_of_Lima), [Martín de Porres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres) and [John Macias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Macias).

[^cite_note-Guide_to_Peru-17]

Among them, the [Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima) and the [Basilica of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_San_Francisco,_Lima) stand out, which are said to be linked by the underground passageways of their catacombs. The [Sanctuary and Monastery of Las Nazarenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_and_Monastery_of_Las_Nazarenas) also stands out, a place of pilgrimage to the *[Señor de los Milagros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_of_Miracles)* (Lord of the Miracles), whose festivities in the month of October constitute the most important religious manifestation of Lima and of all Peruvians. Some sections of the [Colonial Walls of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Lima) can still be seen: such is the case of the Bastion Santa Lucía, remains of the old Spanish fortification built by Viceroy [Melchor de Navarra y Rocafull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchor_de_Navarra,_Duke_of_Palata) around the city center, whose location adjoins the limit of [Barrios Altos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrios_Altos) and [El Agustino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Agustino).&#x20;

Until the 1970s, the hotel offer was characterized by having the best hotels in the city in the [center of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_District), however, since the early 1990s to date, these establishments have positioned themselves in other areas such as the central-southern area of the capital as in [Miraflores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_District,_Lima), Barranco, [Santiago de Surco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Surco), [Surquillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surquillo) and [San Borja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Borja_District); in addition to the [San Isidro district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima) that has the largest hotel building in Peru, the 30-story Westin Libertador.&#x20;

These fine examples of medieval Spanish fortifications were used to defend the city from attacks by [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate) and [corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer). For this, part of the Walls corresponding to the rear area of the Basilica of San Francisco, very close to the [Government Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Palace,_Peru), was recovered, in which a park was built (called Parque de la Muralla) and in which you can see remains of it.[^cite_note-97] Half an hour from the historic center, in the [district of Miraflores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_District,_Lima) is the tourist and entertainment center [Larcomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larcomar) which is located on the [cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff) facing the sea.&#x20;



[Huaca Pucllana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Pucllana), [Lima culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_culture) archaeological site, circa 500-700 CE

[^cite_note-98]

The city has two traditional zoological parks: the main and oldest is the Parque de las Leyendas, located in the [San Miguel district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_District,_Lima), and the other is the Parque Zoológico Huachipa located east of the city in the [Lurigancho-Chosica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurigancho-Chosica) district. On the other hand, the offer of [cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_theater) is wide and has numerous state-of-the-art rooms (4D) that program international film premieres.&#x20;

Exclusive beaches are visited during the summer months, which are located on the [Pan-American Highway](301108.md), to the [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Norte) are the resorts of [Santa Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Rosa_District,_Lima) and [Ancón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anc%C3%B3n_District); Until the 1980s, the latter was the most exclusive in Lima and Peru. Currently, although it maintains its architectural beauty, it is visited by people from all over [Lima North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Norte) and the [Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_District). And to the [south of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Sur_\(Lima\)), the resorts of [Punta Hermosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Hermosa), [Punta Negra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Negra_District), [San Bartolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bartolo_District) and [Pucusana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pucusana). Numerous restaurants, nightclubs, lounges, bars, clubs and hotels have been opened in such places to cater to bathers.&#x20;

The suburban district of [Cieneguilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cieneguilla), the [district of Pachacámac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachac%C3%A1mac_District) and the district of [Chosica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosica) provide important tourist attractions among locals. Due to its elevation (over 500 masl), the sun shines in Chosica during the winter, being very visited by the residents of Lima to escape the urban fog.[^cite_note-99]&#x20;

## Society and culture



[Club Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Nacional_\(Peru\))

Strongly influenced by [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Europe), [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Africa) and [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Asia) culture, Lima is a [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot), due to [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization), immigration and [indigenous influences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-100] The [Historic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Lima) was declared a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1988. The city is known as the *Gastronomical Capital of the Americas,* mixing [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_cuisine), [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) and [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_cuisine) culinary traditions.[^cite_note-mixtura.biz-101]&#x20;



[Gran Hotel Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Hotel_Bol%C3%ADvar) at the [Plaza San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_San_Mart%C3%ADn,_Lima)

Lima's beaches, located along the northern and southern ends of the city, are heavily visited during the summer. Restaurants, clubs and hotels serve the beachgoers.[^cite_note-102] Lima has a vibrant and active theater scene, including classic theater, cultural presentations, modern theater, experimental theater, dramas, dance performances and theater for children. Lima is home to the [Municipal Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Municipal_\(Lima\)), Segura Theater, Japanese-Peruvian Theater, Marsano Theater, British theater, Theater of the PUCP Cultural Center and the Yuyachkani Theater.[^cite_note-103]&#x20;

### Architecture

See also: [Category\:Buildings and structures in Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Buildings_and_structures_in_Lima) and [Category\:Tourist attractions in Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Tourist_attractions_in_Lima)

The architecture of the capital is characterized by a mixture of styles as reflected in the changes between trends throughout various periods of the city's history. Examples of [colonial architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonial_architecture) include structures such as the [Basilica and Convent of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_San_Francisco,_Lima), the [Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_Lima), and the [Palacio de Torre Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_Torre_Tagle). These constructions were generally influenced by the styles of Spanish Neoclassicism, [Spanish Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Baroque_architecture), and the Spanish Colonial styles during the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru).[^cite_note-104]&#x20;



Convento de San Francisco de Asís

In the buildings of the historic center you can see over 1,600 [balconies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balconies_of_Lima) dating from the Viceroyalty and Republican times.[^cite_note-105] The types of balconies that the city presents are open balconies, flat, box, continuous, among others. These balconies are similar to those in Spain as well as [Trujillo](476649.md), Peru's third largest city. After the [Independence of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Peru), a gradual shift towards [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) and [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) styles took place. Many of these constructions were influenced by the French architectural style.&#x20;



[Balconies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balconies_of_Lima) were a common colonial architectural feature in the historic center. In the image the [Palacio de Torre Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_Torre_Tagle) completed in 1735.

In 1940, the census results reflected the city's major urban problems such as sanitation, housing, work, recreation and transportation. During the following years, the Society of Architects, the Institute of Urbanism, the Grupo Espacio, the magazine *El Arquitecto Peruano* and the Department of Architecture at the National School of Engineers were created. These entities tried to promote the improvement of urban conditions based on modern principles. Meanwhile, the State promoted the development of collective housing through organizations such as the National Housing Commission (CNV) and the National Office of Planning and Urban Development (ONPU). With the architect Fernando Belaunde as deputy, in 1945 the Housing Plan based on Neighborhood Units was made official.&#x20;

Some government buildings as well as major cultural institutions were built in this architectural time period. During the 1950s and 1960s, several [Brutalist style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_style) buildings were built on behalf of the [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_Peru) of [Juan Velasco Alvarado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Velasco_Alvarado). Examples of this architecture are the [Museo de la Nación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_de_la_Naci%C3%B3n) and the [Peruvian Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Peru\)). The 20th century saw the appearance of glass [skyscrapers](29485.md), particularly around the [city's financial district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima), San Isidro.There are also several new architectural projects and real estate. The current tallest building in Lima and all of Peru is the [Torre Banco de la Nación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Banco_de_la_Naci%C3%B3n), which has a height of 140 metres.&#x20;

### Language

Known as [Peruvian Coast Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Coast_Spanish), Lima's Spanish is characterized by the lack of strong intonations as found in many other Spanish-speaking regions. It is heavily influenced by [Castilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_\(historical_region\)) Spanish. Throughout the Viceroyalty era, most of the Spanish nobility based in Lima were originally from Castile.[^cite_note-106] Limean Castillian is also characterized by the lack of *[voseo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voseo)*, unlike many other [Hispanic American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Americas) countries. This is because *voseo* was primarily used by Spain's lower socioeconomic classes, a social group that did not begin to appear in Lima until the late colonial era.&#x20;

Limean Spanish is distinguished by its clarity in comparison to other Latin American accents and has been influenced by immigrant groups including Italians, [Andalusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusian_people), West Africans, Chinese and Japanese. It also has been influenced by [anglicisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicism) as a result of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization), as well as by Andean Spanish and [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechuan_languages), due to migration from the Andean highlands.[^cite_note-107]&#x20;

*[El Comercio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Comercio_\(Peru\))* is the largest and oldest newspaper outlet of Peru, headquartered in Lima. It was founded in 1839, by José Manuel Amunátegui y Muñoz and Alejandro Villota. The [National Library of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Peru) is located in the [San Borja District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Borja_District). It was founded in 1821, with its original building being located in [Historic Centre of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Lima). The new building was built in 2006.&#x20;

### Museums

Main article: [Museums in Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museums_in_Lima)



[Lima Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Art_Museum)

Lima is home to the country's highest concentration of museums, most notably the [Museo Nacional de Arqueología, Antropología e Historia del Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Nacional_de_Arqueolog%C3%ADa,_Antropolog%C3%ADa_e_Historia_del_Per%C3%BA) located inside a viceregal mansion and which presents among its most outstanding attractions a fine collection of gold and silver from ancient Peru, the famous collection of erotic art and the deposits where visitors can appreciate 45,000 archaeological objects duly classified, [Lima Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Art_Museum), and the Museo Pedro de Osma. Other museums include the [Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Natural_History,_Lima), the [Museum of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_Nation), The Sala Museo Oro del Perú Larcomar, the Museum of Italian Art, the Museum of Gold and the [Larco Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larco_Museum). These museums focus on art, [pre-Columbian cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era), natural history, science and religion.[^cite_note-108] The [Museum of Italian Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Italian_Art) shows European art.&#x20;

&#x20;Both the Larco Museum and Museum of Peruvian Culture are located in the Pueblo Libre district, and are connected by a blue pedestrian line that makes it easy to visit them together. In the Miraflores district you can visit the Museo Oro del Perú in Larcomar, which has a collection of pre-Columbian gold objects. There are also archaeology site museums of Lima, such as [Pachacamac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachacamac) and [Huaca Pucllana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Pucllana). Not only are there museums dedicated to exhibiting the manifestations of Peruvian pre-Hispanic culture, but there are also art, natural history, science, religious and thematic museums. Of note are the Electricity Museum and the Postal and Philatelic Museum of Peru.&#x20;

### Food

Main article: [Peruvian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_cuisine)



[Causa limeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causa_lime%C3%B1a)

Lima is known as the Gastronomical Capital of the Americas. As a center of immigration and the center of the Spanish Viceroyalty, chefs incorporated dishes brought by the conquistadors and waves of immigrants: African, European, Chinese and Japanese.[^cite_note-mixtura.biz-101] Since the second half of the 20th century, international immigrants were joined by internal migrants from rural areas.[^cite_note-109] Lima cuisines include [Creole food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples), [Chifas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chifa), [Cebicherias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceviche) and [Pollerias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollo_a_la_Brasa).[^cite_note-110]&#x20;

In the 21st century, its restaurants became recognized internationally.[^cite_note-\:0-111]&#x20;

In 2007, the Peruvian Society for Gastronomy was born with the objective of organizing activities to promote Peruvian food and reinforce the Peruvian national identity. The society, called APEGA, gathered chefs, nutritionists, institutes for gastronomical training, restaurant owners, chefs and cooks, researchers and journalists. They worked with universities, food producers, artisanal fishermen and vendors in food markets.[^cite_note-112] One of their first projects (2008) was to create the largest food festival in Latin America, called Mistura ("mixture" in Portuguese). The fair takes place in September every year. The number of attendees has grown from 30,000 to 600,000 in 2014.[^cite_note-113] The fair congregates restaurants, food producers, bakers, chefs, street vendors and cooking institutes from for ten days to celebrate excellent food.[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

Since 2011, several Lima restaurants have been recognized as among [The World's 50 Best Restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World%27s_50_Best_Restaurants).[^cite_note-115] In 2023, [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Restaurante) was named the Best Restaurant in the World.[^cite_note-\:1-116] In 2025, [Maido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maido_\(restaurant\)) was named the Best Restaurant in the World.[^cite_note-117]&#x20;

### Religion



Sacristy of the colonial [Basilica of San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_San_Pedro,_Lima) of Lima. The painting by [Bernardo Bitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Bitti) in the background.

The arrival of the [Spanish conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) in Peru meant the introduction of the [Catholic religion](606848.md) in this area populated by aborigines of various ethnic groups, who followed [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) and [polytheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheism) religions,[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] which produced a [religious syncretism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism#Religious_syncretism). Through a long process of indoctrination and practices among the pre-Columbian settlers, the Spanish [friars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friar) made faith their most important task. The city of Lima, capital of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru), became in the 17th century a city of [monastic](45856.md) life where saints such as [Rose of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_of_Lima) (patron saint of Catholics in Lima, of [National Police of Peru](14896441.md), of the [Republic of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Peru), of the [American continent](29833.md) and of the [Philippines](23440.md)) and [Martín de Porres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres).&#x20;

The Peruvian capital is the seat of the [Archdiocese of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Lima), which was established in 1541 as a [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) and in 1547 as an [archdiocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese).[^cite_note-123] It is one of the oldest Ecclesiastical Provinces in the [Americas](29833.md). Currently the Archdiocese of Lima is in charge of [Cardinal](6221.md) [Juan Luis Cipriani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Luis_Cipriani_Thorne).[^cite_note-124] The city also has two [mosques](19894.md) of the [Muslim religion](6037917.md),[^cite_note-125] three [synagogues](48559.md) of the [Jewish religion](15624.md),[^cite_note-126] a temple of the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) located in [La Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Molina_District),[^cite_note-127] a church of the [Eastern Orthodox religion](10186.md) located in the [district of Pueblo Libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblo_Libre_District,_Lima),[^cite_note-128] five [Buddhist](3267529.md) temples[^cite_note-129] and six [prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayer) rooms of the [Church of God Ministry of Jesus Christ International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_God_Ministry_of_Jesus_Christ_International).[^cite_note-130]&#x20;

According to the [2007 Peru Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Peru_Census), 82.83% of Lima residents over the age of twelve declared they were [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic), while 10.90% profess the [Evangelical religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), 3.15% belong to other religions and 3.13% do not specify any religious affiliation.[^cite_note-131] One of the most prominent Catholic religious manifestations in the capital is the procession of the [Señor de Los Milagros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_of_Miracles) (Lord of Miracles),[^cite_note-132] whose image dating from the [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru) goes out in procession through the streets of the city in the month of October of each year. The *Señor de Los Milagros* was named Patron of the city by the [Cabildo of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabildo_of_Lima) in 1715 and Patron of Peru in 2010.&#x20;

### Sports

Main article: [Sport in Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Lima)



[National Stadium of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stadium_of_Peru) which has a capacity of 50,000.

The city has sports venues for [basketball](3921.md), [golf](19568112.md) and [volleyball](32558.md) among others, many within private clubs. A popular sport among Limenos is *[fronton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleta_Front%C3%B3n)*, a [racquet sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racquet_sport) similar to *[squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\))* invented in Lima. The city is home to seven international-class [golf](19568112.md) links. [Equestrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrianism) is popular in Lima with private clubs as well as the [Hipódromo de Monterrico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip%C3%B3dromo_de_Monterrico) horse racing track. The most popular sport in Lima is [football](10568.md) with professional club teams operating in the city. The [Villa Deportiva Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Deportiva_Nacional) (VIDENA) is the largest sports complex in Peru, located in the district of [San Luis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_District,_Lima).&#x20;

Lima features numerous stadiums. The largest stadium is the [Estadio Monumental "U"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Monumental_%22U%22), which is the largest stadium in Peru and the second largest in South America, with a capacity of 80,093. The [National Stadium of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stadium_of_Peru), also known as the Estadio Nacional, is the country's national stadium. It is multi use, with a running track and field, and holds the Olympic Museum. It is the most used stadium by Peru in national sports. Most of the stadiums in Peru are operated by the [Peruvian Institute of Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Sports_Institute) (IPD). Other stadiums include the [Estadio Alejandro Villanueva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Alejandro_Villanueva), and the [Estadio Universidad San Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universidad_San_Marcos). The city also features many arenas, such as the [Coliseo Amauta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coliseo_Amauta) and [Coliseo Eduardo Dibós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coliseo_Eduardo_Dib%C3%B3s). The [Hipódromo de Monterrico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip%C3%B3dromo_de_Monterrico) is a horse racing track located in the [Santiago de Surco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Surco) district.&#x20;



[Plaza de toros de Acho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_toros_de_Acho); the plaza is classified as a national historic monument. It is the oldest [bullring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullring) in the Americas.

The historic [Plaza de toros de Acho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_toros_de_Acho), located in the [Rímac District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_District), a few minutes from the [Plaza de Armas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Lima), holds [bullfights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfighting) yearly. The season runs from late October to December. It also holds concerts, conventions and small football matches. The bullring is the oldest in the Americas and the second oldest in the world. It has a capacity of 14,000.&#x20;

The [131st IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/131st_IOC_Session) was held in Lima. The meeting saw Paris elected to host the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) and Los Angeles elected to host the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md).&#x20;

Lima would have had 2 venues for the [2019 FIFA U-17 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_U-17_World_Cup) and the [2023 FIFA U-17 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_U-17_World_Cup), however, they were stripped of hosting rights in the two tournaments. The city hosted the [2004 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and [2005 FIFA U-17 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_U-17_World_Championship) and hosted the final of those tournaments. Lima were also the hosts of [2019 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pan_American_Games).[^cite_note-133] In 2024, they were selected once again to host the [2027 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Pan_American_Games). The city will also joint host the [2025 Bolivarian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bolivarian_Games) with [Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayacucho).&#x20;

[Alianza Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alianza_Lima), [Sporting Cristal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_Cristal) and [Universitario de Deportes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universitario) are the most football important teams in the country, which is why they are known as the [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(Peru\)) of Peruvian football. These clubs traditionally compete in the [Peruvian Superclásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Supercl%C3%A1sico) and the [Modern Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Universitario_de_Deportes%E2%80%93Sporting_Cristal_rivalry), and are the most popular in the country. They, along with the also historic [Deportivo Municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_Municipal), are currently the four most popular and traditional teams in the Peruvian capital. [Club de Regatas Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_de_Regatas_Lima) is the largest sports club in Peru and the oldest in the America's. They feature in various sports such as football, volleyball, and rowing. [Lima Cricket and Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Cricket_and_Football_Club) was the first football club established in South America.&#x20;

### Parks



[Friendship Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendship_Park_\(Lima\))

Lima's largest parks are located on the city's outskirts and are known as Zonal Parks. In the historic center are [Parque de la Reserva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_la_Reserva), [Parque de la Exposición](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_la_Exposici%C3%B3n), [El Campo de Marte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Campo_de_Marte), University Park, [Parque de la Muralla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_la_Muralla), and Mariscal Castilla Park. The Parque de la Reserva has the largest pool complex in the country and is known as "The Magic Water Circuit."&#x20;

Other important parks are found throughout the city, such as [El Olivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Olivar,_Peru), [Parque Reducto No. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_Reducto_No._2), [Pantanos de Villa Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantanos_de_Villa_Wildlife_Refuge), [Parque de las Leyendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_de_las_Leyendas), Miraflores Boardwalk, [Friendship Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendship_Park_\(Lima\)), and [Parque Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_Kennedy), to name a few. The city's streets are laid out as a system of plazas, which serve a similar purpose to roundabouts. In addition to this practical purpose, plazas serve as one of Lima's main green spaces and contain a wide variety of different types of architecture, ranging from monuments, statues, and water fountains.&#x20;

## Subdivisions

Main article: [List of districts of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Lima)



**Downtown Lima**\
[**Northern Lima**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Norte)\
[**Southern Lima**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Sur_\(Lima\))\
[**Eastern Lima**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Este)

Lima is made up of thirty-one densely populated districts, each headed by a local mayor and the Mayor of Lima, whose authority extends to these and the twelve outer districts of the Lima province.&#x20;

The city's [historic center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Lima) is located in the [Cercado de Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_District) district, locally known as simply Lima, or as "El Centro" ("Center"). It is home to most of the vestiges the colonial past, the Presidential Palace (Spanish: *Palacio de Gobierno*), the [Metropolitan Municipality and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Municipality_of_Lima) (Spanish: *Consejo municipal metropolitano de Lima*), [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrio_Chino_\(Lima\)) and dozens of hotels, some operating and some defunct, that cater to the national and international elite.&#x20;

The city is marked by a degree of [urban segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_segregation) between poor *[pueblos jóvenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblos_j%C3%B3venes)*, populated in large part by immigrants from the Andean highlands, and wealthy neighborhoods. The upscale [San Isidro District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_District,_Lima) is the city's financial center. It is home to politicians and celebrities. San Isidro has parks, including [Parque El Olivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Olivar,_Peru), which is home to [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) trees imported from Spain during the seventeenth century. The [Lima Golf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Golf_Club), a prominent [golf club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_club), is located within the district.&#x20;

Another upscale district is [Miraflores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_District,_Lima), which has luxury hotels, shops and restaurants. Miraflores has parks and green areas, more than most other districts. Larcomar, a shopping mall and entertainment center built on cliffs overlooking the Pacific Ocean, featuring bars, dance clubs, movie theaters, cafes, shops, boutiques and galleries, is also located in this district. Nightlife, shopping and entertainment center around [Parque Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_Central_Park), a park in the heart of Miraflores.[^cite_note-134]&#x20;

[La Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Molina_District), [San Borja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Borja_District), [Santiago de Surco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Surco) -home to the American Embassy and the exclusive Club Polo Lima – are the other three wealthy districts. The middle class districts in Lima are [Jesús María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Mar%C3%ADa_District,_Lima), [Lince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lince_District), [Magdalena del Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_del_Mar), [Pueblo Libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblo_Libre), [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_District,_Lima) and [Barranco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranco_District).&#x20;

The most densely populated districts lie in Northern and Southern Lima, where the suburbs of the city begin (Spanish: [Cono Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Norte) and [Cono Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Sur_\(Lima\)), respectively) and they are mostly composed of Andean immigrants who arrived during the mid- and late- 20th century looking for a better life and economic opportunity, or as refugees of the country's internal conflict with the [Shining Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shining_Path) during the late 1980s and early 1990s. In the case of Cono Norte (now called [Lima Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cono_Norte)), shopping malls such as Megaplaza and Royal Plaza were built in the [Independencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independencia_District,_Lima) district, on the border with the [Los Olivos District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Olivos_District) (the most residential neighborhood in the northern part). Most inhabitants are [middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class) or [lower middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_middle_class).&#x20;

[Barranco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranco_District), which borders [Miraflores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_District,_Lima) by the Pacific Ocean, is the city's bohemian district, home or once home of writers and intellectuals including [Mario Vargas Llosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vargas_Llosa), [Chabuca Granda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabuca_Granda) and [Alfredo Bryce Echenique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Bryce_Echenique). This district has restaurants, music venues called "peñas" featuring the traditional folk music of coastal Peru (in Spanish, "música criolla") and Victorian-style chalets. Along with Miraflores it serves as the home to the foreign nightlife scene.&#x20;

## Education



Colonial [Casona and Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casona_of_the_National_University_of_San_Marcos) of the [National University of San Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_San_Marcos); it is the [oldest continuously operating university in the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colonial_universities_in_Hispanic_America).

Home to universities, institutions and schools, Lima has the highest concentration of institutions of higher learning on the continent. In Lima is the oldest continuously operating university in the New World, [National University of San Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_San_Marcos), founded in 1551.[^cite_note-135]&#x20;

[Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Nacional_de_Ingenier%C3%ADa) (UNI) was founded in 1876 by Polish engineer [Edward Habich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Habich) and is the country's most important engineering school. Other public universities offer teaching and research, such as the [Universidad Nacional Federico Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Nacional_Federico_Villarreal) (the second largest), the [Universidad Nacional Agraria La Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Nacional_Agraria_La_Molina) (where ex-president [Alberto Fujimori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Fujimori) once taught) and the [National University of Callao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Callao).&#x20;

The [Pontifical Catholic University of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Peru), established in 1917, is the oldest private university. Other private institutions include [Universidad del Pacifico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Pacific_\(Peru\)), [Universidad ESAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_ESAN), [Universidad de Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_de_Lima), [Universidad de San Martín de Porres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres), [Universidad Peruana Cayetano Heredia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Peruana_Cayetano_Heredia), [Universidad Cientifica del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Cientifica_del_Sur), [Universidad San Ignacio de Loyola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_San_Ignacio_de_Loyola), [Universidad Peruana de Ciencias Aplicadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Peruana_de_Ciencias_Aplicadas), [Universidad Privada San Juan Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Privada_San_Juan_Bautista) and Universidad Ricardo Palma.[^cite_note-136]&#x20;

The city and has a total of 8,047 elementary and high schools, both public and private, which educate more than one and a half million students. The number of private schools is much greater than public schools (6,242 vs 1,805) while the average size of private schools is 100 for elementary and 130 for high school. Public schools average 400 students in elementary and 500 in high school.[^cite_note-137]&#x20;



Edificio Ministerio de Educación (Ministry of Education), San Borja

Lima has one of the country's highest levels of enrollment in high school and preschool. 86.1% of high school-age students are in school, vs the national average of 80.7%. In early childhood, the enrollment level in Lima is 84.7%, while the national average is 74.5%. Early childhood enrollment has improved by 12.1% since 2005. In elementary school, the enrollment in Lima is 90.7%, while the national average for this level is 92.9%.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

The dropout rate for Lima is lower than the national average, except for elementary school, which is higher. In Lima, the dropout rate in elementary is 1.3% and 5.7% in high school, while the national average is 1.2% in elementary and 8.3% in high school.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

In Peru, students grade second and fourth students take a test called "Evaluacion Censal de Estudiantes" (ECE). The test assesses skills in reading comprehension and math. Scores are grouped in three levels: Below level 1 means that students were not able to respond to even the most simple questions; level 1 means the students did not achieve the expected level in skills but could respond to simple questions; and level 2 means they achieved/exceeded the expected skills for their grade level. In 2012, 48.7% of students in Lima achieved level 2 in reading comprehension compared to 45.3% in 2011. In math, only 19.3% students achieved level 2, with 46.4% at level 1 and 34.2% less than level 1. Even though the results for Math are lower than for reading, in both subject areas performance increased in 2012 over 2011. The city performs much better than the national average in both disciplines.[^cite_note-umc.minedu.gob.pe-139]&#x20;

The educational system in Lima is organized under the authority of the "Direccion Regional de Educacion (DRE) de Lima Metropolitana", which is in turn divided into 7 sub-directions or "UGEL" (Unidad de Gestion Educativa Local): UGEL 01 (San Juan de Miraflores, Villa Maria del Triunfo, Villa El Salvador, Lurin, Pachacamac, San Bartolo, Punta Negra, Punta Hermosa, Pucusana, Santa Maria and Chilca), UGEL 02 (Rimac, Los Olivos, Independencia, Rimac and San Martin de Porres), UGEL 03 (Cercado, Lince, Breña, Pueblo Libre, San Miguel, Magdalena, Jesus Maria, La Victoria and San Isidro), UGEL 04 (Comas, Carabayllo, Puente Piedra, Santa Rosa and Ancon), UGEL 05 (San Juan de Lurigancho and El Agustino), UGEL 06 (Santa Anita, Lurigancho-Chosica, Vitarte, La Molina, Cieneguilla and Chaclacayo) and UGEL 07 (San Borja, San Luis, Surco, Surquillo, Miraflores, Barranco and Chorrillos).[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

The UGELes with highest results on the ECE 2012 are UGEL 07 and 03 in both reading comprehension and math. UGEL 07 had 60.8% students achieving level 2 in reading comprehension and 28.6% students achieving level 2 in Math. UGEL 03 had 58.5% students achieve level 2 in reading comprehension and 24.9% students achieving level 2 in math. The lowest achieving UGELs are UGEL 01, 04 and 05.[^cite_note-umc.minedu.gob.pe-139]&#x20;

23% of men have completed university education in Lima, compared to 20% of women. Additionally, 16.2% of men have completed non-university higher education along with 17% of women. The average years of schooling in the city is 11.1 years (11.4 for men and 10.9 for women).[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

## Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Lima)

### Air



[Jorge Chávez International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_International_Airport)

Lima is served by [Jorge Chávez International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_International_Airport), located in [Callao](294857.md) (LIM). It is the country's largest airport hosting the largest number of domestic and international passengers. It serves as the fourth-largest hub in the Latin American air network. The airport is the hub for most Peruvian airlines, such as [Atsa Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsa_Airlines), [Star Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Per%C3%BA), [JetSmart Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetSmart_Per%C3%BA), [Sky Airline Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Airline_Peru) and the Peruvian flag carrier, [LATAM Perú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LATAM_Airlines_Per%C3%BA). Lima possesses five other airports: the Las Palmas Air Force Base, Collique Airport and runways in [Santa María del Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_del_Mar_District,_Lima), [San Bartolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bartolo_District) and [Chilca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilca).[^cite_note-140]&#x20;

[Jorge Chávez International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_International_Airport) has undergone an expansion, with a new terminal being constructed along with an additional runway and multiple commercial areas. These new expansions will consist of the Lima Airport City. The airport is the first airport city of Latin America and will increase the current airport capacity of 30 million to 40 million passengers. The expansion project opened on 1 June 2025.[^cite_note-141]&#x20;

### Road

Further information: [Highways in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_in_Peru)



Via Expresa

Lima is a major stop on the [Pan-American Highway](301108.md). Because of its location on the country's central coast, Lima is an important junction in Peru's highway system. Three major highways originate in Lima.&#x20;

- The [Northern Panamerican Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_Highway_1) extends more than 1,330 kilometers (830 mi) to the border with Ecuador connecting the northern districts with many major cities along the northern Peruvian coast.

- The Central Highway (Spanish: *Carretera Central*) connects the eastern districts with cities in central Peru. The highway extends 860 kilometers (530 mi) with its terminus at [Pucallpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pucallpa) near Brazil.

- The [Southern Panamerican Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_Highway_1) connects the southern districts to the southern coast. The highway extends 1,450 kilometers (900 mi) to the border with Chile.

The city has a single major bus terminal next to the mall Plaza Norte. This bus station connects to national and international destinations. Other bus stations serve private bus companies around the city. In addition, informal bus stations are located in the south, center and north of the city.&#x20;

### Maritime



View of the sea from the [Miraflores District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraflores_District,_Lima)

Lima's proximity to the port of [Callao](294857.md) allows Callao to act as the metropolitan area's major port and one of Latin America's largest. Callao hosts nearly all maritime transport for the metropolitan area. A small port in [Lurín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lur%C3%ADn_District) serves oil tankers due to a nearby refinery. Maritime transport inside Lima city limits is relatively insignificant compared to that of Callao.&#x20;

The [Port of Chancay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Chancay) is currently the largest in South America with it commencing operations on 14 November 2024.&#x20;

### Rail



[Desamparados station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desamparados_station)

Lima is connected to the Central Andean region by the [Ferrocarril Central Andino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarril_Central_Andino) of 1435mm gauge which runs from Lima through the departments of [Junín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun%C3%ADn_Region), [Huancavelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancavelica_Region), [Pasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasco_Region) and [Huánuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu%C3%A1nuco_Region).[^cite_note-142] Major cities along this line include [Huancayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancayo), [La Oroya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Oroya), [Huancavelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huancavelica) and [Cerro de Pasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_de_Pasco). Another inactive line runs from Lima northwards to the city of [Huacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huacho).[^cite_note-143] While [Commuter rail](6286.md) services for Lima are planned as part of the larger [Tren de la Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tren_de_la_Costa_\(Peru\)) project, the first official services, beginning 2026, will run from the capital to [Chosica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosica) on the FCCA mainlines and will operate as a diesel powered service using ex-Caltrain EMD F40PH locomotives and 90 Nippon Sharyo Gallery cars, some being cab cars for push-pull configuration, all of the coaches and cab cars being the first of their kind in Peru and western South America. Lima's main railway station is the [Desamparados station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desamparados_station).&#x20;

### Public

Lima's road network is based mostly on large [divided avenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway) rather than [freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway). Lima operates a network of nine freeways – the Via Expresa Paseo de la Republica, Via Expresa Javier Prado, Via Expresa Grau, Panamericana Norte, Panamericana Sur, Carretera Central, Via Expresa Callao, Autopista Chillon Trapiche and the Autopista Ramiro Priale.[^cite_note-arqandina.com-144]&#x20;

According to a 2012 survey, the majority of the population uses public or collective transportation (75.6%), while 12.3% uses a car, taxi or motorcycle.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

The urban transport system is composed of over 300 transit routes[^cite_note-proinversion.gob.pe-91] that are served by buses, microbuses and combis.&#x20;

Taxis are mostly informal and unmetered; they are cheap but feature poor driving habits. Fares are agreed upon before the passenger enters the taxi. Taxis vary in size from small four-door compacts to large vans. They account for a large part of the car stock. In many cases they are just a private car with a taxi sticker on the windshield. Additionally, several companies provide on-call taxi service.[^cite_note-145]&#x20;

### Corredores Complementarios Bus System



Sistema Integrado de Transporte Bus System in [Arequipa Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenida_Arequipa) (Route 301)

Main article: [Corredores complementarios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corredores_complementarios)

The [Integrated Transport System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Transport_System) (SIT), is a bus system developed by the local government to reorganize the current system of routes that has become chaotic. One of the main goals of the SIT is to reduce the number of urban routes, renew the bus fleet currently operating by many private companies and to reduce (and eventually replace) most "combis" from the city.&#x20;

As of July 2020, SIT currently operates 16 routes: [San Martin de Porres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_District) – [Surco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Surco) (107) [Ate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ate_District) – [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_District,_Lima) (201, 202,204,206 and 209), [Rimac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_District) – [Surco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Surco) (301,302,303 and 306), [San Juan de Lurigancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_Lurigancho) – [Magdalena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_del_Mar_District) (404,405,409,412), and [Downtown Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_District) – [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_District,_Lima)(508)&#x20;

### Colectivos

[Colectivos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colectivo) render express service on some major roads. The colectivos signal their specific destination with a sign on their windshield. Their routes are not generally publicitized but are understood by frequent users. The cost is generally higher than public transport; however, they cover greater distances at greater speeds due to the lack of stops. This service is informal and is illegal.[^cite_note-146] Some people in the periphery use so-called "mototaxis" for short distances.&#x20;

#### Metropolitan Transport System



[El Metropolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Metropolitano)

The Metropolitan Transport System or [El Metropolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Metropolitano) is a new, integrated system, consisting of a network of buses that run in exclusive corridors under the Bus Rapid Transit system (BRT). The goal is to reduce passengers' commute times, protect the environment, provide improved security and overall quality of service. Metropolitano was executed with funds from the City of Lima and financing from the [Inter-American Development Bank](382592.md) and the [World Bank](36752.md). Metropolitana is the first BRT system to operate with natural gas, seeking to reduce air pollution.[^cite_note-147] This system links the principal points of the Lima Metropolitan Area. The first phase of this project has 33 kilometers (21 mi) of line (north) to [Chorrillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorrillos_District) (south). It began commercial operations on 28 July 2010. Since 2014, Lima Council operates the "Sistema Integrado de Transporte Urbano" (Urban integrated transport system), which comprises buses over [Avenida Arequipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenida_Arequipa).[^cite_note-148] By the end of 2012, the Metropolitano system counted 244 buses in its central routes and 179 buses in its feeding routes. Weekday use averages 437,148 passengers. Usage increased since 2011 by 28.2% for weekdays, 29.1% for Saturdays and 33.3% for Sundays.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;



[Lima Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metro) Line 1

#### Metro

The [Lima Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Metro) is expected to have six lines. As of 2024, there are two lines running and a third under construction. The other lines are in the Ministry of Transportation's investment portfolio for the next 5 years.&#x20;

Line 1 has twenty six passenger stations, with an average separation of 1.2 kilometers (3,900 ft). It begins in the Industrial Park of Villa El Salvador, south of the city, continuing on to Av. Pachacútec in Villa María del Triunfo and then to Av. Los Héroes in San Juan de Miraflores, then through Av. Tomás Marsano in Surco to reach Ov. Los Cabitos, to Av. [Aviación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenida_Aviaci%C3%B3n) and then cross the river Rimac to finish, after almost 35 km (22 mi), in the east of the capital in [San Juan de Lurigancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_Lurigancho). The system operates 24 trains, each with six wagons. Each wagon has the capacity to transport 233 people. The metro system began operating in December 2012 and transported 78,224 people on average on a daily basis.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

Line 2 of the metro has a first stage already working, the expansion of the line it is being delivered in 2026, along with a portion of line 4 that will be connecting the airport to the metro system. Line 3 and expansion of line 4 are now in the pre bid stage to be done in 2025.&#x20;

### Other transportation issues

avenue

[Traffic Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) in Javier Prado Avenue

Lima has high traffic congestion, especially at peak hours. 1.397 million vehicles were in use by the end of 2012. The region operates 65.3% of the cars in the country.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) offered economic incentives for municipalities to implement bicycle routes in their districts. Recreational bike lanes can be found in 39 districts. The Proyecto Especial Metropolitano de Transporte No Motorizado (PEMTNM) estimates that more than a million and a half people used the bike lanes in 2012. The bike lanes ran for 71 km (44 mi). They estimate that the use of the bike lanes prevented the emission of 526 tons of carbon dioxide in 2012.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

San Borja district was the first to implement a bikeshare program called San Borja en Bici. It supplied 200 bicycles and six stations across the district (two of them connecting with the Metro). By December 2012, the program had 2,776 subscribers.[^cite_note-149]&#x20;

## Challenges

### Environment



Paragliding in Miraflores

#### Air

Lima suffers from significant [air pollution](10934212.md). The microscopic sediment contained within engine exhaust and industrial emissions floats in the air for extended periods of time, either continuing as air pollution or eventually coming to settle as dust upon different urban surfaces. The fine particles are the most dangerous, as if inhaled, are able to damage the human [respiratory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system). The recommended limit of these particles by the [World Health Organization](33583.md) is 5 tons/km2/month. In February 2014, Lima recorded an average of 15.2 tons/km2. The two districts with the highest concentration of sedimentary dust are El Agustino (46.1 tons/km2) and Independencia (25.5 tons/km2) in February 2014.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138][^cite_note-ReferenceA-150]&#x20;

Lima has built [billboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard) which serve as [air purifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_purifier).[^cite_note-151]&#x20;



[Rímac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADmac_River)

#### Water

The permissible limit of lead in the water supply is 0.05 milligrams per liter, according to the Norm ITINTEC. In January 2014, the concentration of minerals in water treatment facilities of [SEDAPAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEDAPAL) was 0.051 iron, 0.005 lead, 0.0012 cadmium and 0.0810 aluminum. These values increased 15.9% and 33.3% in iron and cadmium with respect to January 2013 and a decrease of 16.7% and 12.4% in lead and aluminum. The values are within the recommended limits.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-150]&#x20;

#### Solid waste

The amount of solid waste produced per capita in Lima is about 0.7 kg (2 lb) per day. In 2012, each resident produced 273.36 kg (603 lb) of solid waste. The district municipalities only collect about 67% of the solid waste they generate. The rest ends up in informal landfills, rivers, or the ocean. Three municipalities recycle 20% or more of their waste.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

#### COVID-19

Lima has a population of about 10 million people. This equates to one-third of the overall population of the nation. Lima was subjected to a lengthy period of confinement or quarantine that lasted more than four months. However, in May 2020, the process of restoring activities began as part of the national government's economic reactivation strategy.[^cite_note-\:02-152][^cite_note-\:3-153]&#x20;

Lima's [green recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_recovery) is based on bringing services closer to the most vulnerable people, with the goal of creating a green, wealthy, and equitable city for all. The municipality has an aim of implementing sustainable infrastructure to limit the development of [COVID-19](63030231.md) through the execution of 46 km of rising bike routes.[^cite_note-\:02-152][^cite_note-\:3-153]&#x20;

Lima is one of over 10,000 cities from all around the world that make up the [Global Covenant of Mayors.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Covenant_of_Mayors_for_Climate_%26_Energy)[^cite_note-\:02-152][^cite_note-154]&#x20;

### Access to basic services



[Jesús María District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Mar%C3%ADa_District,_Lima)

In Lima, 93% of households have access to water supply in their homes. In addition, 92% of homes connect with sewage systems. 99.6% of homes have grid electric service. Although most households have water and sewage systems, some are available for only a few hours a day.[^cite_note-limacomovamos.org-138]&#x20;

One and a half million inhabitants have no access to drinking water. The water problem mainly affects the outlying areas of the capital. The story of water in Lima is one of inequality. In the most affluent districts, per capita water consumption stands at 350 liters per day. In the poorest neighborhoods, it's between 20 and 70 liters a day.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]&#x20;

About 1.7 million residents are not connected to the drinking water system and are forced to buy water from tankers, even though it is not always safe to drink. The problem of access to water continues to worsen due to drought, pollution, poor infrastructure, overexploitation by large companies and intensive agriculture.[^cite_note-157]&#x20;

### Safety

The perception of safety varies by district. For example, San Isidro has the lowest perception of being unsafe (21.4%), while Rimac has the highest perception of insecurity (85%), according to a 2012 survey. The five districts with the lowest perception of insecurity are San Isidro, San Borja, Miraflores, La Molina and Jesus Maria. The districts with the highest perception of insecurity are Rimac, San Juan de Miraflores, La Victoria, Comas and Ate.[^cite_note-ciudadnuestra.org-158]&#x20;

Overall, 40% of the population in Lima above 15 years old has been a crime victim. The younger population (ages 15 to 29 years old) has the highest victimization rate (47.9%).[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56] In 2012, citizens reported thefts (47.9%): in homes or establishments (19.4%), robbery or attack (14.9%), gang aggression (5.7%), among others in lesser frequency. The districts with the highest level of victimization are Rimac, El Agustino, Villa El Salvador, San Juan de Lurigancho and Los Olivos. The safest districts by level of victimization are Lurin, Lurigancho-Chosica, San Borja, Magdalena and Surquillo. These districts do not necessarily correspond to the districts with highest or lowest perception of insecurity.[^cite_note-ciudadnuestra.org-158]&#x20;



[Barranco District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranco_District)

While the police force is nationally controlled and funded, each district in Lima has a community policing structure called Serenazgo. The quantity of Serenazgos officials and resources varies by district. For example, Villa Maria del Triunfo has 5,785 citizens per official. Twenty-two districts in Lima have a ratio above 1000 citizens per Serenazgo official, while 14 districts have ratios below 200 citizens per official, including Miraflores with 119 and San Isidro with 57.[^cite_note-inei.gob.pe-56]&#x20;

The satisfaction with the Serenazgos also varies greatly by district. The highest satisfaction rates can be found in San Isidro (88.3%), Miraflores (81.6%), San Borja (77%) and Surco (75%). The lowest satisfaction rates can be found in Villa Maria del Triunfo (11%), San Juan de Miraflores (14.8%), Rimac (16.3%) and La Victoria (20%).[^cite_note-ciudadnuestra.org-158]&#x20;
