# Mass media in the United States

This article is about all forms of mass media. For news media, see [News media in the United States](5392786.md).

"American Media" redirects here. For the publishing company also known as American Media, see [A360media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A360media).



People using [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone), devices associated with young people, but commonly used by people of all ages

There are several types of [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) in the United States: [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_United_States), [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_in_the_United_States), [cinema](418334.md), [newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspapers_of_the_United_States), [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_magazines), [books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book), and [websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Website). The U.S. also has a strong [music industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_in_the_United_States). [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_New_York_City), [Manhattan](45470.md) in particular, and to a lesser extent [Los Angeles](18110.md), are considered the epicenters of American media. Theories to explain the success of such companies include reliance on certain policies of the American [federal government](195149.md) or a tendency to [natural monopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_monopoly) in the industry, with a corporate [media bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_bias_in_the_United_States).

Many media entities are controlled by large for-profit corporations who reap revenue from advertising, [subscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscription), and sale of [copyrighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright) material. American [media conglomerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_conglomerate) tend to be leading global players, generating large revenues as well as large opposition in many parts of the world. With the passage of the [Telecommunications Act of 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_Act_of_1996), further [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation) and [convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_convergence) are under way, leading to mega-mergers, further [concentration of media ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_media_ownership), and the emergence of multinational media conglomerates. These mergers enable tighter control of information.[^cite_note-1] By the early decades of the 21st century, a [handful of corporations control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_cross-ownership_in_the_United_States#Top_Five*) the vast majority of both [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media) and [legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_media) media.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Critics allege that [localism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localism_\(politics\)), local news, and other content at the community level, media spending and coverage of news, and [diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism) of ownership and views have suffered as a result of these processes of media concentration.[^cite_note-5]

The organization [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) compiles and publishes an annual ranking of countries based upon the organization's assessment of their [press freedom records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index). In 2023–24, United States was ranked 55th out of 180 countries (a drop of ten places from the previous year) and was given a "problematic" rating.[^cite_note-6] A 2022 [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)) poll showed that only 11% of Americans trust television news and 16% trust newspapers.[^cite_note-7] On the future of Spanish-language media in the U.S., Alberto Avendaño, ex-director of *[El Tiempo Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Tiempo_Latino)*/*[Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Post)*, claimed that "[Hispanic-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic-American)" news coverage in the English-language media is "absolutely pathetic", but he was optimistic, arguing that demographic shifts would inevitably render the Latino media a significant presence in the context of American media. According to a May 2023 [AP](18935732.md)-[NORC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORC_at_the_University_of_Chicago) poll, 74% of respondents said the media is to blame for increased [political polarization in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarization_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-8] In 2025 a Gallup poll found that only 28% of Americans expressed a "Great deal/Fair amount" of trust and confidence in mass media when it comes to reporting the news fully, accurately and fairl&#x79;*.* With 62% of Republicans stating that have zero trust and confidence in Mass media. Independence being mixed in having no trust, not very much trust, and a Great deal/Fair amount of trust, and 51% of Democrats expressing a "Great deal/Fair amount" of trust in media a 19% decline from 70% who said so in 2022. It also found that people over the age of 65 had generally higher trust in mass media compared to other age groups.[^cite_note-9]

## Newspapers

Main article: [Newspapers in the United States](9030472.md)

See also: [List of newspapers in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_United_States)



*[The New York Times](30680.md)* [Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times_Building) in [Times Square](31389.md), [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan)

After being widely successful in the 20th century, newspapers have declined in their influence and penetration into American households over the years. The U.S. does not have a national paper. *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, and *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)* are the most circulated newspapers in the United States and are sold in most U.S. cities.[^cite_note-10]

Although the primary audience for *The New York Times* (NYT) had initially been the residents of [New York City](645042.md) and its surrounding [metropolitan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), the NYT, nicknamed "the Grey Lady" and which has won the most [Pulitzer Prizes](24230.md) of any publication, has gradually become the dominant "[newspaper of record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_of_record)" for the U.S. media. Apart from its daily nationwide distribution, the term means that back issues are archived on microfilm by every decent-sized public library in the nation, and the Times' articles are often cited by both historians and judges as evidence that a major historical event occurred on a certain date. *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* and *The Wall Street Journal* are also newspapers of record, to a lesser extent. Although *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)* has tried to establish itself as a national paper, it has been widely derided by the academic world as the "McPaper" and is not subscribed to or archived by most libraries.[^cite_note-11]

Apart from the aforementioned newspapers, all major metropolitan areas have their own local newspapers. Most metropolitan areas will generally support one or two major newspapers, with many smaller publications targeted towards particular audiences. Although the cost of publishing has increased over the years, the price of newspapers has generally remained low, forcing newspapers to rely more on advertising revenue and on articles provided by a major [news agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_agency) wire service, such as the [Associated Press](18935732.md), [Bloomberg](3057255.md), and [Reuters](18998750.md),[^cite_note-12] for their national and world coverage.



*[The Washington Post](102226.md)* on Monday, July 21, 1969, stating "'The Eagle Has Landed'—Two Men Walk on the Moon".

With very few exceptions, all the newspapers in the U.S. are privately owned, either by large chains such as [Gannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gannett) or [McClatchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_McClatchy_Company), which own dozens or even hundreds of newspapers; by small chains that own a handful of papers; or in a situation that is increasingly rare, by individuals or families. Most general-purpose newspapers are either being printed one time a week, usually on Thursday or Friday, or are printed daily. Weekly newspapers tend to have much smaller circulation and are more prevalent in rural communities or small towns. Major cities often have "[alternative weeklies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_media)" to complement the mainstream daily papers, for example, New York City's *[Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Voice)* or Los Angeles' *[L.A. Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A._Weekly)*, to name two of the best-known. Major cities may also support a local business journal, trade papers relating to local industries, and papers for local ethnic and social groups.

As competition from other media has evolved, the number of daily newspapers in the U.S. has declined over the past half-century, according to *[Editor & Publisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor_&_Publisher)*, the trade journal of American newspapers. In particular, the number of evening newspapers has fallen by almost one-half since 1970, while the number of morning editions and Sunday editions has grown. For comparison, in 1950, there were 1,772 daily papers (and 1,450 – or about 70 percent – of them were evening papers) while in 2000, there were 1,480 daily papers (and 766—or about half—of them were evening papers.) [Daily newspaper circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_United_States_by_circulation) is also slowly declining in America, partly due to the near-demise of two-newspaper towns, as the weaker newspapers in most cities have folded:

The primary source of newspaper income is [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising) – in the form of "[classifieds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifieds)" or inserted advertising circulars – rather than [circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_circulation) income. However, since the late 1990s, this revenue source has been directly challenged by Web sites like [eBay](130495.md) (for sales of secondhand items), [Monster.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster.com) (jobs), and [Craigslist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigslist) (everything). Additionally, as investigative journalism declined at major daily newspapers in the 2000s, many reporters formed their own non-profit investigative newsrooms. Examples include [ProPublica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProPublica) on the national level, [Texas Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Tribune) at the state level and [Voice of OC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_OC) at the local level. The largest newspapers (by circulation) in the United States are *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, and the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)*.

In August 2019, it was announced that [New Media Investment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Media_Investment_Group) had agreed to buy [Gannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gannett), and operations would continue under the Gannett rather than [GateHouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GateHouse) name, at the Gannett headquarters but under New Media's CEO.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The acquisition of Gannett by New Media Investment Group was completed on November 19, 2019, making the combined company the largest newspaper publisher in the United States.[^cite_note-15] Immediately after the merger was finalized, all GateHouse Media URLs began redirecting to Gannett.com. *[La Opinión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Opini%C3%B3n)* is the most read newspaper website in the United States, reaching more than 6 million readers each month. It is the largest Spanish-language newspaper in the United States and the second-most read newspaper in Los Angeles (after the *Los Angeles Times*).

## Magazines

Main article: [List of United States magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_magazines)

See also: [American comic book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_comic_book)



The first issue of *Time* (March 3, 1923), featuring [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) [Joseph G. Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gurney_Cannon).

Thanks to the huge size of the English-speaking North American media market, the United States has a large magazine industry with hundreds of magazines serving almost every interest, as can be determined by glancing at any newsstand in any large American city. Most magazines are owned by one of the large media conglomerates or by one of their smaller regional brethren. The *[American Society of Magazine Editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Magazine_Editors)* sponsors the annual [National Magazine Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Magazine_Awards) recognizing excellence.

The U.S. has three leading weekly [news magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_magazine): *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))*, *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)*, and *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)*. *Time* and *Newsweek* are center-left while *U.S. News & World Report* tends to be center-right. *Time* is well known for naming a "Person of the Year" each year, while *U.S. News* publishes annual ratings of American colleges and universities. The U.S. also has over a dozen major political magazines, including *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)*, *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)*, *[Harper's Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper's_Magazine)* and *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)* among others. In entertainment the magazines *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))*, [The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter), *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*, [L.A. Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A._Record) and [Billboard](18309966.md) are very popular. In arts [Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_\(magazine\)) and [Art in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_in_America) magazines are major magazines.

In addition, hundreds of specialized magazines serve a variety of interests. General-interest publications include *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_\(magazine\))*, [People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\)), [Maxim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_\(magazine\)), [Consumer Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Reports), [Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated). Fashion magazines include [Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_\(magazine\)), [Glamour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamour_\(magazine\)), [GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ), [InStyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InStyle), [Cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan_\(magazine\)), while publications such as [Motor Trend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Trend), [Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_\(magazine\)), *[AARP the Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AARP_the_Magazine)*, [Good Housekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Housekeeping), [Bon Appétit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_App%C3%A9tit), and [Saveur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saveur) focus on other specific interests. Professional and industry publications include *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* for [scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist), *[Communications of the ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_of_the_ACM)* (for [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) specialists), *[IEEE Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Spectrum)* (for [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineers)), the *[ABA Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABA_Journal)* (for lawyers), [Businessweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Businessweek) and [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes) for [business](39206.md), [Architectural Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_Digest) and [Architectural Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_Record) for [architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architects).

[El Nuevo Cojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Nuevo_Cojo) ([Los Angeles](18110.md)), and *[Two Mundos Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Mundos_Magazine)* ([Miami](53846.md)) are two bilingual (English/Spanish) lifestyle and entertainment magazines. It's not a coordinated exodus for magazines in the United States but the transition from print's primacy to digital's that has started at the turn of the century.

## Radio

Main article: [Radio in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_in_the_United_States)



Fireside chat on government and capitalism (September 30, 1934)

American radio broadcasts in two bands: [FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation) and [AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude_modulation). Some stations are only [talk radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk_radio) – featuring interviews and discussions – while [music radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_radio) stations broadcast one particular type of music: [Top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40), [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music), etc. Radio broadcast companies have become increasingly consolidated in recent years. [National Public Radio](29697232.md) is the nation's primary public radio network, but most radio stations are commercial and profit-oriented. Talk radio as a political medium has also exploded in popularity during the 1990s, due to the 1987 repeal of the [Fairness Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairness_Doctrine), which meant that stations no longer had to "balance" their day by programming alternative points of view.

The [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) (FCC) in 1970 had limited the number of radio station one person or company could own to 1 am and 1 FM locally, and 7 am and 7 FM stations nationally. But due to extensive [concentration of media ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_media_ownership) stemming from the [Telecommunications Act of 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_Act_of_1996), radio companies could own not more than 8 local stations per area market. Most stations are now owned by major radio companies such as [iHeartMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartMedia) (formerly Clear Channel Communications), [Cumulus Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulus_Media), [Townsquare Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsquare_Media) and [Audacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audacy) (Formerly Entercom). See [IBOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBOC) and [HD Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_Radio).

A new form of radio that is gaining popularity is [satellite radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_radio). The two biggest [subscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscriptions) based radio services are [Sirius Satellite Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius_Satellite_Radio) and [XM Satellite Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM_Satellite_Radio), which have recently merged to form [Sirius XM Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius_XM_Radio). Unlike terrestrial radio music channels are commercial free and other channels feature minimal commercials. Satellite radio also is not regulated by the FCC.

During the advent of the internet in the 21st century, [internet radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_radio) and digital streaming services have been emerged. Among popular brands are [Pandora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandora_Radio), [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify) and [iHeartRadio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartRadio). Although, the recording industry also sees Internet radio as a threat and has attempted to impose high royalty rates for the use of recorded music to discourage independent stations from playing popular songs. Spotify listeners can choose the songs they want to play, when they want to play them. ... Pandora is a way for users to discover new music that matches their tastes, while Spotify—even though it offers radio stations, too—is better suited to stream and share music that users already know and love.

[Nielsen Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Audio), formerly known as Arbitron, is consumer research company that provides ratings (similar to the [Nielsen ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_ratings)) for national and local radio stations in the United States. [Digital Audio Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Audio_Broadcasting) goal is to replace FM broadcasting and become the future of radio. Some industry experts are wary of this new transmission method. ... However, this method of transmission could benefit internet radio stations that want to develop local coverage and keep up to speed with FM radio stations.

Several Spanish language broadcast (as well as cable) networks exist, which are the most common form of non-English television broadcasts. These networks are not as widely distributed over-the-air as their English counterparts, available mostly in markets with sizable Latino and Hispanic populations; several of these over-the-air networks are alternatively fed directly to cable, satellite and IPTV providers in markets without either the availability or the demand for a locally based owned-and-operated or affiliate station.

The largest of these networks, [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision), launched in 1986 as a successor to the Spanish International Network. Its major competition is [Telemundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemundo) (est. 1986), a sister network of NBC (which acquired Telemundo in 2001). Founded: 2009 [Estrella TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrella_TV) is another Spanish-language broadcast television network.

[Public television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_television) has a far smaller role than in most other countries. However, a number of states, including West Virginia, Maryland, Kentucky, and South Carolina, among others, do have state-owned public broadcasting authorities which operate and fund all public television stations in their respective states. The income received from the government is insufficient to cover expenses and stations also rely on corporate sponsorships and viewer contributions. [DirecTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirecTV) and [Dish Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dish_Network) are the major satellite television providers, with 20 and 14 million customers respectively as of February 2014.[^cite_note-22] Meanwhile, the major cable television providers are [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) with 22 million customers, [Charter Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_Communications) with 17 million, and [Cox Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_Communications), [AT\&T U-verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_U-verse) and [Verizon Fios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon_Fios) with 5–6 million each.

## Motion pictures

Main article: [Cinema of the United States](418334.md)



[Robert Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rodriguez) directed the 1992 action film *[El Mariachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mariachi)*, which was a commercial success after grossing \$2 million against an initial budget of \$7,000 (before studio production costs). He later launched his own cable television channel, [El Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Rey_Network), thanks to advances in media technology.

[^cite_note-23]

In the 20th century, the motion picture industry rose to become one of the most successful and powerful industries in the U.S. Along with other intellectual property industries, its relative importance to the American economy has strengthened as the importance of manufacturing and agriculture have decreased (due to [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization)).[^cite_note-24]

### Rise of the home video market (1980s–1990s)

The 1980s and 1990s saw another significant development. The full acceptance of [home video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_video) by studios opened a vast new business to exploit. Films such as *[Showgirls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showgirls)*, *[The Secret of NIMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_of_NIMH),* and *[The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shawshank_Redemption)*, which may have performed poorly in their theatrical run, were now able to find success in the video market. It also saw the first generation of filmmakers with access to videotapes emerge. Directors such as [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino) and [Paul Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson) had been able to view thousands of films and produced films with vast numbers of references and connections to previous works. Tarantino has had a number of collaborations with director [Robert Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rodriguez). Rodriguez directed the 1992 action film *[El Mariachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mariachi)*, which was a commercial success after grossing \$2 million against an initial before-production budget of \$7,000. In 2011, *El Mariachi* was inducted into the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) to be preserved as part of its [National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry) for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". The film is further immortalized by [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) as the lowest-budgeted film ever to gross \$1 million at the box office.[^cite_note-25] This was possible thanks to the explosion of [independent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_film) enabled by ever-decreasing costs for filmmaking. With the rise of the DVD in the 21st century, DVDs have quickly become even more profitable to studios and have led to an explosion of packaging extra scenes, extended versions, and [commentary tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_commentary_\(DVD\)) with the films.

### Rise of digital distribution

DVD and high-definition [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray) sales have substantially declined with the rise of [video on demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_on_demand), especially over-the-top [streaming services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_services). The range of viewing devices went through another mass expansion with the popularization of [tablet computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_computer) and [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone).

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), [drive-in theaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive-in_theaters) reported an unexpected surge in attendance in several U.S. states as, unlike with indoor theaters unable to operate because of bans on mass gatherings, these were allowed to operate.

## Internet

Main article: [Internet in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_the_United_States)

The Internet has provided a means for newspapers and other media organizations to deliver news and keep archives public. Revenue is generated through advertising or subscription payments. Aside from web portals and search engines, the top visited website in the United States, include a [wide variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites) of popular websites.

### Online streaming

Online [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming) has made it possible to watch an array of [video-on-demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video-on-demand) options from any device. Everything from live news, sports, classic movies, and TV favorites can be viewed from any device. In the early 21-century many of the most popular over-the-top [video on demand streaming services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_streaming_media_systems) launched. According to Pew, only 5 million American homes had high-speed internet in the year 2000. In only five years, viewers increased to 66 million per day, and by 2011 viewership increases to 3 billion per day. As a result of these large data increases, cable and broadband companies are having to extend offers to compete in this 655 billion dollar digital market.[^cite_note-32]

The [a la carte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_la_carte) style [Virtual MVPDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_MVPD), video streaming services, initially include "TV Everywhere" services, with traditionl cable services. Due to the competitive prices of these on-demand bundles, by 2015 services such as [Sling TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sling_TV), [PlayStation Vue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vue) and [fuboTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FuboTV) were launched. [DirecTV Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirecTV_Now) followed in 2016, and [Philo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philo_\(company\)) in 2017. Streaming soon became integrated into many aspects of society. Influencing local news, magazines, and everyday communication. The *[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_\(magazine\))* magazine began publishing Vulture's streaming guide, listing all the TV Shows and movies available on Netflix, Amazon, HBO, and Hulu. With the continuous fluctuations in new streaming channels the term "[streaming wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_wars)" became coined to discuss the new era of competition between video streaming services such as Netflix, Disney+, Apple TV+, Amazon Prime, HBO Max, Funimation, Crunchyroll, etc.[^cite_note-33]

## Broadcast and entertainment cross-ownership

Most of the current [major film studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_film_studios) have become part of corporate conglomerates, including television broadcast networks, and popular cable television channels. Sony Pictures has been an exception. Fox sold it's film properties to Disney in 2019, retaining its television and radio broadcast operations. All of the major entertainment companies have launched streaming services as they face competition from new media companies. Amazon purchased Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, one of the "big five" studios in 2022, the [Golden Age of Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Hollywood_cinema). Comcast is also a major [Internet service provider](100245.md), representing a high degree of [vertical integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_integration). A deal for Paramount to purchase Warner Bros. is pending regulatory approval.[^cite_note-34]

This conglomeration gives owners the ability to reuse the same content between theaters, broadcast, and streaming. Allowing them to use intellectual property from [media franchises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_franchise) they own, and dispersing it across several businesses without worrying about licensing. Some owners have extended their franchises into in-person [theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park) and [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games). Major entertainment companies are constant looking for fresh sources mayerial and often compete to buy the rights to adapt books and [independent films.s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_film) An example of this includes the Discovery channels content sourcing through it's ownership of the top two comic book publishers within the USA. Some media franchises are also cross-licensed, especially for streaming and theme parks, and the [proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance](81768101.md) is pending, as of April 2026.
