**Chiang Mai**[^cite_note-5] is the largest [province](204637.md) (*changwat*) of [Thailand](30128.md) by area.[^cite_note-area1-6][^cite_note-area2-7] It lies in [upper northern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thailand#Regional_classification_of_northern_Thailand) and has a population of 1.8 million people. It is bordered by [Chiang Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai_province) to the northeast, [Lampang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampang_province) and [Lamphun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamphun_province) to the south, [Tak](223051.md) to the southwest, [Mae Hong Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Hong_Son_province) to the west, and [Shan State](493376.md) of [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) to the north. The capital, [Chiang Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Mai), is 685 kilometres (426 mi) north of Bangkok. Older towns stood on the plain before it. The city was the capital of the [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na) kingdom from its founding in 1296.

## Geography

Chiang Mai province is about 685 km (426 mi) from Bangkok in the [Mae Ping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_River) River basin and is on average at 300 m (1,000 ft) elevation. Surrounded by the mountain ranges of the [Thai highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_highlands), it covers an area of approximately 22,311 km<sup>2</sup> (9,000 sq mi). The [Daen Lao Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daen_Lao_Range) (ทิวเขาแดนลาว) lies at the north end of the province and the [Khun Tan Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khun_Tan_Range) in the east. The [Thanon Thong Chai Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanon_Thong_Chai_Range) (เทือกเขาถนนธงชัย) runs from north to south and holds the highest mountain in Thailand, [Doi Inthanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_Inthanon), at 2,565 metres (8,415 ft). All three ranges are covered by [rain forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_forest). The Mae Ping, one of the major tributaries of the [Chao Phraya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Phraya_River), originates in the [Daen Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daen_Lao_Range) mountains. Several [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_\(Thailand\)) are in the province: [Doi Inthanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_Inthanon_National_Park),[^cite_note-8] [Doi Suthep-Pui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_Suthep-Pui_National_Park),[^cite_note-9] [Ob Luang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ob_Luang_National_Park),[^cite_note-10] [Sri Lanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanna_National_Park),[^cite_note-11] [Huai Nam Dang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai_Nam_Dang_National_Park),[^cite_note-12] [Mae Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Wang_National_Park),[^cite_note-13] and [Pha Daeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pha_Daeng_National_Park).[^cite_note-14] The total forest area is 15,404 km<sup>2</sup> (5,948 sq mi) or 69.6 percent of provincial area.[^cite_note-15]

## Climate

Chiang Mai has a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*), tempered by the low latitude and moderate elevation, with warm to hot weather year-round. Nighttime conditions during the dry season can be cool and are much lower than daytime highs. The maximum temperature ever recorded is 42.4 °C (108.3 °F) in May 2005.[^cite_note-MaxTemperature-19]

Of the population, 13.4 percent in the province are members of [hill tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_tribe_\(Thailand\)).

- **[Northern Thai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_people)** or **[Tai Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_people)** native to nine provinces in [Northern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thailand), principally in the area of the former kingdom of [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na).

- **[Tai Lue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_people)** live in dwellings of usually only a single room wooden house built on high poles. They are skilled in weaving.

- **[Tai Yai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people)**, Burmese in origin, harvest rice, farm, raise cattle and trade. Their craftsmanship lies in weaving, pottery, wood carving and bronze ware.

- **[Akha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akha_people)** the largest population of any hill tribe in the region. Originating from Tibet and Southern China, they dwell on high ground around 1,200 metres (3,937 ft) above sea-level. Within their villages they build a spirit gateway to protect them from evil spirits.

- **[Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people)** from southern China. Prefer higher elevations. They raise livestock and grow rice, corn, tobacco, and cabbage. Known for their embroidery and silver.

- **[Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people)** occupy valleys and riverbanks.

- **[Lahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahu_people)** from southern China and live in high areas. They are known as hunters and planters.

- **[Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people)** from southern China and Tibet are renowned for their colorful dress and also build their dwellings on high poles. They harvest rice and corn and their men are skilled in hunting.

- **[Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\))** reside on mountainsides and grow corn and other crops. They are skilled blacksmiths, silversmiths and embroiders.

- **[Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_people)**, native to [Haripuñjaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haripu%C3%B1jaya), whose capital was at [Lamphun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamphun).

## Symbols

The [seal of the province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seals_of_the_provinces_of_Thailand) shows a [white elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_elephant) in a glass pavilion. The white elephant is a royal symbol in Thailand, and it is depicted to remember the offering of a white elephant by [Thammalangka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thammalangka), a ruler of Chiang Mai, to his overlord, King [Rama II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_II_of_Siam) of Bangkok. The pavilion stands for the prosperity of Buddhism in Chiang Mai, and particularly for the review of the [Tripitaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripitaka) there in 1477.[^cite_note-RoySoc-20]

The provincial flower and [tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Thai_provincial_trees) is the "flame of the forest" (*[Butea monosperma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butea_monosperma)*).[^cite_note-RoySoc-20] The edible [cyprinid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprinid) fish "[black sharkminnow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_sharkminnow)" (*[Labeo chrysophekadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labeo_chrysophekadion)*) is the provincial aquatic life.

The provincial slogan is "*In the shadow of Mount Doi Suthep, blessed with rice customs and traditions, beautiful wild flowers, magnificent Nakhon Phing"*.[^cite_note-RoySoc-20]

## History

Main articles: [Kingdom of Chiang Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Chiang_Mai) and [Lan Na Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na_Kingdom)

Older towns stood on the plain before Chiang Mai. At [Wiang Tha Kan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiang_Tha_Kan?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [San Pa Tong district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pa_Tong_district), surveys found antiquities in the Lopburi style, and Sujit Wongthes compared the name with the Wiang Takan of the *[Mūlasāsanā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C5%ABlas%C4%81san%C4%81?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.[^cite_note-sujit1983-21]<sup>: 58 </sup> The province has also yielded Old Mon inscriptions. Christian Bauer listed five that are absent from H. L. Shorto's dictionary of the Mon inscriptions, one of them Jm.45 from Wiang Mano in the same district. Bp. 422/2524 comes from Wiang Tho, and two votive images from [Saraphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraphi_district), registered 23/2523 and 24/2523, are now in the National Museum, [Lopburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopburi). The fifth is an unregistered tablet thought to come from the same site, now in the Los Angeles County Museum of Art. All five, Bauer held, antedate the inscriptions from the [Lamphun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamphun) sites. He read the epigraphy as showing that Mon use persisted longer in the region south of Lamphun and Chiang Mai than in the [Chao Phraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Phraya_River) basin. Mon inscriptions there are attested from the 12th to the 13th centuries, after which the language gave way to Thai.[^cite_note-bauer1993-22]<sup>: 535 </sup>

The city of [Chiang Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Mai), the capital of Chiang Mai province, was also capital of the [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na) kingdom after its founding in 1296, during the same period of time as the establishment of the [Sukhothai Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhothai_Kingdom).[^cite_note-TAT-23] Chiang Mai was thereafter the capital of Lan Na and the centre of Buddhism in northern Thailand. [Mangrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrai) built many temples in the region.

In 1558, Chiang Mai became a colony of the [First Toungoo Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Toungoo_Empire). Chiang Mai remained its colony for more than 200 years, until the [Burmese–Siamese War (1775–1776)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese%E2%80%93Siamese_War_\(1775%E2%80%931776\)). In 1774 the Burmese colonial regime was driven out of Chiang Mai by a coalition of Lan Na and Siamese forces. It then became a tributary state of [Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siam), which later installed a [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na) chieftain ally, [Kawila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawila), to independently rule over Lampang and Chiang Mai region as a monarch.

During the reign of [King Rama V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Rama_V) of Siam, under his administrative centralization policy, Chiang Mai lost its independence, was annexed and became a second-level subdivision of Siam.[^cite_note-24]

Chiang Mai has had the status of a province since 1933.[^cite_note-25]

### Local government

As of 26 November 2019 there are:[^cite_note-27] one Chiang Mai Provincial Administration Organisation (*_ongkan borihan suan changwat_*) and 121 municipal (*thesaban*) areas in the province. Chiang Mai has city (*[thesaban nakhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_nakhon)*) status. Mae Jo, Mae Hia, Mueang Kaen Phatthana and Ton Pao have town (*[thesaban mueang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_mueang)*) status. A further 116 are subdistrict municipalities (*[thesaban tambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_tambon)*). The non-municipal areas are administered by 89 Subdistrict Administrative Organisations - SAO (*ongkan borihan suan tambon*).

Chiang Mai province is the tourist hub of the north and one of the country's main tourist destinations. It is regarded as scenic for its mountain ranges, valleys, flora and fauna, and the [Hang Dong Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Dong_Canyon) is among its visitor attractions. In some months the climate in Chiang Mai and the north is cool, unlike most of Thailand.

## Health

Each amphoe of Chiang Mai has its own hospital. The largest, [Maharaj Nakorn Chiang Mai Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaj_Nakorn_Chiang_Mai_Hospital) and [Nakornping Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakornping_Hospital), are in Mueang Chiang Mai district.

## Local products

Chiang Mai is a handicrafts centre, with a variety of antiques, silver jewellery, and embroidery, Thai silks and cottons, basketry, celadon, silverware, furniture, lacquerware, woodcarvings, and parasols.[^cite_note-29]

## Local culture

The culture of northern Thailand is Lan Na in origin. The region has distinctive food, music, arts, way of life and language. Chiang Mai is home to various [hill tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_tribe_\(Thailand\)) and their own distinctive cultures.

### Local food



*Khao soi*

See also: [Thai cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_cuisine)

- **[Nam phrik ong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik_ong)** is a type of *[Nam phrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik)* chili paste which is made of minced pork and tomatoes. It is usually eaten with soft-boiled vegetables, pork crackling, or deep-fried crunchy rice cakes.

- **[Nam phrik num](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik_num)** meaning "chili paste young man", is another kind of paste which can be eaten with pork crackling.

- **[Sai ua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_ua)** is a local sausage that is very aromatic and spicy and is usually eaten with sticky rice.

- **Kaeng** meaning "curry", is not made with coconut milk in the north.&#x20;

  - [Kaeng hang-le](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_hangle?action=edit\&redlink=1) is northern-style pork curry

  - Kaeng om is a spicy curry consisting of intestines

  - [Kaeng khae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_khae) is a spicy curry consisting mainly of vegetables.

- **[Khanom chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanom_chin) [nam ngiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_ngiao)** is a traditional northern noodle dish with chicken or pork.

- **[Khao soi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_soi)** is a noodle dish which can be made from chicken, pork, or beef made with coconut milk and garnished with chopped fresh shallots, pickled cabbage, chilli paste to taste, and a slice of fresh lime.
