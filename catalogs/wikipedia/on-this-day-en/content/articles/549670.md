**Córdoba** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[ˈkoɾðoβa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish), [Spanish](26825.md): *Departamento de Córdoba*) is a [Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) of [Colombia](5222.md) located in the [Caribbean Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Region,_Colombia) in the north. Córdoba borders the Caribbean Sea to the north, [Sucre Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre_Department) to the northeast, [Bolívar Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar_Department) to the east, and [Antioquia Department](185251.md) to the south. As of 2018, the population of Córdoba was estimated to be 1,784,783. Córdoba's capital is the city of [Montería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monter%C3%ADa).

## History

The department of Cordoba was established when the Congress of Colombia passed Law 9 of December 18, 1951[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]. This separated the new department from the existing [Bolívar Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar_Department). The official inauguration came 6 months later on June 18, 1952, as Montería was inaugurated as the department’s capital and its first governor, Remberto Burgos Puche[^cite_note-7] was installed.&#x20;

The department takes its name from [José María Córdova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_C%C3%B3rdova)[^cite_note-8], an independence fighter and military leader who played a crucial role in the liberation of Colombia. The surname Córdova itself traces its roots to the Spanish Andalusian city, [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain).&#x20;
