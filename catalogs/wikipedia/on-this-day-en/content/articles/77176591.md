**Raphaël Archenault** (French pronunciation: [\[ʁafaɛl aʁʃəno\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); born 8 January 1995), known as **Raphaël Arnault** ([\[aʁno\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), is a French [far-left](78703256.md) activist and politician who has represented [Vaucluse's 1st constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaucluse's_1st_constituency) in the [National Assembly](299616.md) since 2024.[^cite_note-assemblee-nationale.fr-1][^cite_note-Guéron-Gabrielle-2] A member of [La France Insoumise](52520313.md) (LFI), he co-founded the [Jeune Garde Antifasciste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeune_Garde_Antifasciste) collective in 2018 and was one of its spokespersons. The collective grew close to LFI before it was outlawed for its violence by the government in 2025.[^cite_note-Le_Monde-3][^cite_note-Lyon_Capitale-4] In 2022, Arnault was found guilty of violent behaviour following an assault in 2021.[^cite_note-E1-5]

## Early life and activism

A native of Lyon, Arnault became politically active during the 2016 protests against the [El Khomri law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Khomri_law).[^cite_note-6] He was a member of the [New Anticapitalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Anticapitalist_Party) and ran under its label in [Rhône's 2nd constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne's_2nd_constituency) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_legislative_election), placing fourth in the first round.[^cite_note-7] In 2018, he co-founded the anti-fascist collective [Jeune Garde Antifasciste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeune_Garde_Antifasciste) in Lyon and became one of its spokespersons until the group's dissolution on 12 June 2025.[^cite_note-Le_Monde-3][^cite_note-Lyon_Capitale-4][^cite_note-8]

In February 2022 Arnault was convicted in first instance by a Lyon court of voluntary grouped violence ("*violences volontaires en réunion*") for an assault that occurred in April 2021 in Lyon; he was handed a four-month suspended prison sentence. He initially appealed the judgment but later withdrew his appeal in 2025.[^cite_note-E1-5][^cite_note-9]

In May 2024 Arnault was questioned by police regarding an October 2023 communiqué that initially referred to [Hamas](13913.md) as a "resistance movement" and was later withdrawn the same day.[^cite_note-10]

## Political career

In the [2024 French legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_legislative_election), Arnault was elected to the National Assembly in [Vaucluse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaucluse)'s 1st constituency, defeating incumbent [Catherine Jaouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Jaouen?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [National Rally](67119.md) with 54.9% of the second-round vote.[^cite_note-Guéron-Gabrielle-2] In Parliament, he sits with the LFI–NFP ([La France Insoumise](52520313.md)–[New Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Popular_Front)) group and has served on the Committee on Cultural Affairs and Education.[^cite_note-assemblee-nationale.fr-1]

### "Fiche S" reporting

During the 2024 legislative campaign multiple outlets described Arnault as listed under a "[Fiche S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiches_S)" by the government, meaning he has been flagged as a threat for [national security](240468.md).[^cite_note-11] According to [Europe 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_1), he was subject to three such listings; Arnault criticised proposals to render "Fiché S" individuals ineligible for office.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### Killing of Quentin Deranque

Main article: [Killing of Quentin Deranque](82434853.md)

On 16 February 2026, Jacques-Élie Favrot, a parliamentary assistant to Arnault, had his access to the National Assembly suspended by President of the National Assembly [Yaël Braun-Pivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya%C3%ABl_Braun-Pivet) after being suspected of involvement in the killing of Quentin Deranque, an activist with the far-right [Collectif Némésis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectif_N%C3%A9m%C3%A9sis) in Lyon two days prior.[^cite_note-14] On 17 February, Favrot was arrested by police in connection to the death, alongside another staffer for Arnault, Robin Chalendard. Media reported that both are also "Fiché S" by the government.[^cite_note-15] On 1 April 2026, Arnault returned to the National Assembly for the first time since the incident. National Assembly President Yaël Braun-Pivet stated that, while legally permitted, "morally it is difficult for him to represent the Nation given this type of behaviour" and left the decision to Arnault's conscience.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

In an April 2026 interview with Blast, Arnault broke his media silence, explaining it as a desire "to take some height in this moment". He expressed emotion over the events but placed "immense responsibility" on the French state and police, citing "failures at all levels" and a clear "laissez-faire" attitude, while noting that "a 23-year-old also died". Arnault defended his parliamentary collaborator Favrot as "not someone who wishes the death of another" and insisted the violence had nothing to do with the now-dissolved Jeune Garde, describing the circulated images as "antinomical" to the group's proposals and confirming that all activities had ceased after the dissolution decree.[^cite_note-19]
