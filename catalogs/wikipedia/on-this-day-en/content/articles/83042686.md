On 28 March 2026, the United States-Israel coalition launched airstrikes on the [Iran University of Science and Technology](2073099.md) (IUST), a scientific and engineering national university in [Narmak](5651550.md), Tehran.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The airstrike damaged six buildings and caused about 500 billion [tomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_toman) (approximately US\$3 million) in damage. No casualties were reported.

## Background

Since the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) in 2025, Iranian universities and academics have been targeted. IUST professor [Saeed Shamghadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Shamghadri) was killed in an airstrike on his home a few days before on 24 March.[^cite_note-4]

## Damage

The airstrikes damaged six buildings. The Aerospace Research Center was reduced to rubble.[^cite_note-5] In addition to the main target, structures, laboratories, classrooms, and professors' offices in Physics Department Building No.1 and No. 2, the Mathematics Department, the Basic Science Department, the Islamic Knowledge Department, the Foreign Language Department, and the Mechanical Engineering Research Center were also affected.[^cite_note-6] According to Helyeh Doutaghi, a researcher based in Tehran familiar with the incident, the attack occurred without warning.[^cite_note-7]

According to the university's administrative and financial VP, at least 500 billion [tomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_toman) (approximately US\$3 million) was sustained in damage due to the airstrike on the IUST campus.[^cite_note-AbanDaily-1]

## Reaction

Both the Iranian and international academic community reacted strongly to the attacks on Iran University of Science and Technology and, generally, attacks on scientific sites across Iran.

## Aftermath

Following the attack on IUST, the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) said that it would target American and Israeli universities in the region as a response.[^cite_note-13] The statement shared by Tasnim stated that "the United States should be aware that all Israeli and American universities located in the West Asia region would become legitimate targets until two universities are struck in retaliation for the destroyed Iranian universities".[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] As a result, the [American University of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_of_Beirut) shifted to online classes for two days, while the [American University of Madaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_of_Madaba) in Jordan halted in-person instruction. The [U.S. embassy in Iraq](4754335.md) warned that American universities in Baghdad, Sulaymaniyah, and Dohuk could be targeted.[^cite_note-16]

While Western media outlets considered IRGC threats legitimate,[^cite_note-17] the Dean of the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran), Mohammad Hossein Omid, published an open letter asking Iran's armed forces to refrain from retaliation against American universities in the region.[^cite_note-18] Omid came under intense criticism in Iran's domestic media for what was described as "appeasement and currying favor with the West", forcing Omid and the University of Tehran to issue another statement, siding with retaliation after the attacks on Pasteur Institute of Iran and [Shahid Beheshti University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Beheshti_University).[^cite_note-19]
