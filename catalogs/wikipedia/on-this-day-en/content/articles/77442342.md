The **Gen Z protests** are a series of youth-led protests and riots that have occurred in some parts of the world during the [2020s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020s). The [protests in Bangladesh in 2024](78109234.md) are widely cited as the first successful [Gen Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Z) revolution,[^cite_note-IND-1][^cite_note-CNN-2][^cite_note-REU-3][^cite_note-NAB-4] inspiring other Gen Z-led protests, including [Nepal](81013780.md), [Indonesia](79252449.md), [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2025_Philippine_protests), [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Timor-Leste_protests), [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Maldivian_protests), [Mongolia](19271.md), [Madagascar](18964.md), [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Moroccan_Gen_Z_protests), [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_anti-corruption_protests), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Gen_Z_protests), [Albania](83313353.md), [South Korea, Taiwan, and Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightstick_protests)[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-au-6][^cite_note-KP-7][^cite_note-NYT20250919-8][^cite_note-AJ-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] as well as in other parts of the world. The protests began in Asia, although by 2025 the protests had spread to other continents. To date, the earliest known use of the term was in an [opinion piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_piece) by French market research company [Ipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipsos) published on 8 December 2019, referring to the [Fridays for Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridays_for_Future) and [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests) 2019 protests,[^cite_note-ob-12][^cite_note-13] which have generally been considered the beginning of the movement.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

Although the causes of the protests are different in each country, they have generally been in response to [inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality), declining [standards of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standards_of_living), [corruption](3608404.md), [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding) and [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism). As the first true digital-native generation, [social media](5897742.md) has been a common tool for activism and coordination and is widely believed by non-members of the generation to have caused these protests. An ML–generated study by Bloomberg Economics in late 2025, assessing 22 million data points on the dynamics surrounding Gen Z protests, found a correlation between social media penetration and a low average age, on the one hand, and increased social discontent over issues such as socio-economic inequality, unemployment, and corruption, on the other.[^cite_note-16]

Some protests, like Bangladesh's 2024 [July Uprising](78109234.md) and Nepal, have resulted in the overthrow of national governments. As a result, many deaths have occurred from the overthrows. In particular, it saw the emergence of the world's first Gen Z [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party), [National Citizen Party](79340683.md) and [Rastriya Swatantra Party](72312845.md),[^cite_note-17] whose candidates became the world's first Gen Z leaders elected as [members of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_Parliament) of a government.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## Background

See also: [Political views of Generation Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_views_of_Generation_Z)

Some of the first unrest [Generation Z](4015968.md) experienced was related to the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession) and, later into adulthood of some, issues surrounding the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-yp-21] Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, as of March 2026, there have been at least 120 "significant anti-government protests" in no less than 73 countries over the last 12 months, the largest figures since 2017.[^cite_note-22]

### Causes

See also: [Youth bulge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_bulge)

The demonstrations have had several causes, varying from country to country. However, they generally relate to dissatisfaction with ruling governments, economic turmoil, claims of corruption, and a large, disaffected, often unemployed youth population[^cite_note-23] that experienced declining [standards of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) due to difficulty accessing basic utilities and resources.[^cite_note-au-6][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Rising [social inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) has also been described as forming the common thread of the protests and riots caused by Gen Z around the world,[^cite_note-yp-21][^cite_note-sg-26]

The Bloomberg Economics study examined possible impetuses for Gen Z protests on a country-by-country basis, finding income inequality to be a leading cause of youth mobilisation in Peru and Mexico. Political corruption was a key driver of unrest in the Philippines, while extreme poverty and inadequate access to basic sanitation and clean water drove actions in Madagascar.[^cite_note-27] Carnegie's Global Protest Tracker likewise identified state corruption as a primary catalyst for protests, with anti-democratic excesses and economic difficulties as secondary ones.[^cite_note-28]

Gen Z political mobilisation started to be recorded as early as in 2010. The earliest known example is during the [2010 United Kingdom student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_student_protests) when the police reportedly stopped and searched two protesters who were 11 year old twins in an unlawful manner,[^cite_note-29] and it was reported that a significant percentage of other people at the protests were also part of Gen Z.[^cite_note-30] The [British police](347149.md) also confessed detaining numerous children who were part of the protests for up to 6 hours, some, once again as young as 11 years old.[^cite_note-31] One year later, during the [2011 UK riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_England_riots), a 11-year-old boy was arrested over the riots, and a 11-year-old girl was also arrested for her own involvement in the riots.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In 2011 during the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) in Syria a 13-year-old boy named [Hamza al-Khatib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamza_al-Khatib) was killed by security forces.[^cite_note-34]

In 2013, during the [Gezi Park protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezi_Park_protests), [Berkin Elvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Berkin_Elvan), a 15 year old, was struck in the head by a tear-gas canister fired by a police officer in Istanbul leading to his death.[^cite_note-35] Following his death, various protests by children were observed; for example, During [National Sovereignty and Children's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sovereignty_and_Children's_Day), four kids who held banners reading "Berkin Elvan is Immortal" and chanted slogans were taken into custody by police officers.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] It is worth noting however, that the average age during the Gezi Park protests was 28.[^cite_note-40]

In October 2015, reports by the [U.S Embassy in Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Lima) reported a Gen Z group planned, organised and pursued a protest, culminating in a March to Congress. The action was a part of the [2015 Peruvian protests against Las Bambas mining project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Peruvian_protests_against_Las_Bambas_mining_project).[^cite_note-41]

In December 2017, in an article by [Americas Quarterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas_Quarterly), it was noted that, whilst the main drives of political change within Paraguayan politics at the time were from [Generation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X), a presence of younger activists was noted.[^cite_note-42]

During the [2017–2018 Russian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%932018_Russian_protests), Gen Z became more politically active and actively participated to the protests, despite not leading it.[^cite_note-43]

In April 2018 (the 19th anniversary of the [Columbine High School massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbine_High_School_massacre)), [The National School Walkout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_School_Walkout) was a student-led protest in the United States against gun violence.[^cite_note-44]

During the [protests against Daniel Ortega](47244332.md) there were 325 fatal victims of the repression in Nicaragua, 29 were minors including a one year child. There are also 22 adolescents among the political prisoners.[^cite_note-45]

#### Movements (2018–2023)

Since the late 2010s, members of Generation Z have led protests around the world in what some have called the "Gen Z Movement".[^cite_note-46] The first recorded Gen-Z led movement was [March for Our Lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_for_Our_Lives), a Gen-Z, [student led](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_activism) movement and organisation which saw its formation in March 2018.[^cite_note-wp-47] Other Gen Z Movements around this time include #NoBoundaries5, a [sexual movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_revolution) which begun in 2019.[^cite_note-48] [Fridays for Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridays_for_Future), which started on 20 August 2018,[^cite_note-wp-47] was the first instance in which a series of protests was ever defined as "Gen Z protests", specifically by an article, published on 8 December 2019 called "[Ok Boomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ok_Boomer)!" and published by [Ipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipsos),[^cite_note-ob-12] coining the term.

Nationwide Gen Z protests are recorded as early as in 2020, with the term being used to describe the [2020–2021 Thai protests](64637075.md).[^cite_note-dd-49] In 2022, the [Aragalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragalaya) riots occurred, leading to the overthrowal of the Sri Lankan government. While the term was not used to describe the [Black Lives Matter protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter_protests) beginning in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests), 41% of protesters were between the ages of 18–29.[^cite_note-50]

#### Protests (2024–2026)

See also: [Gen Z protests in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Z_protests_in_Asia)

The [protests in Bangladesh in 2024](78109234.md) are widely cited as the first successful Gen Z revolution in the world,[^cite_note-IND-1][^cite_note-CNN-2][^cite_note-REU-3][^cite_note-NAB-4] inspiring similar Gen Z protests in other Asian countries including [Nepal](81013780.md), [Indonesia](79252449.md), [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2025_Philippine_protests), [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Timor-Leste_protests), and the [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Maldivian_protests).[^cite_note-sa-51][^cite_note-au-6][^cite_note-NYT20250919-8][^cite_note-AJ-9] The term was later used to describe the 2024 [Kenya Finance Bill protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Finance_Bill_protests), a largely Gen Z lead mass rioting movement against a controversial tax hike.[^cite_note-52]

The term garnered wider global popularity following the [ousting of the Nepali government](81013780.md) in 2025 and a string of concurrent protests that occurred around and after it.[^cite_note-sa-51] The global wave of demonstrations then reached further with similar youth-led protests springing up in [Madagascar](81184334.md) and [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Moroccan_Gen_Z_protests).[^cite_note-53] In September, an unpopular pension reform and corruption scandal further sparked a self-styled "Gen Z march" in Lima, Peru.[^cite_note-pc-54] The Gen Z protests had thus by that month reached the continents of Asia, Africa, and South America.[^cite_note-ka-55] At the end of September, [Italian](14708.md) media noted the large involvement of Gen Z protesters in the country's [general strikes and protests for Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_general_strikes_and_protests_for_Gaza).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] By the beginning of October, the year 2025 was even described as a potential "year of the protest", a title that was previously applied to the year [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:2019_protests).[^cite_note-sg-26]

## Methods

### Social media

Members of [Generation Z](4015968.md), who [grew up in the age of the Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_native),[^cite_note-59] have commonly used [social media](5897742.md) as a platform to organize and coordinate protests and riots.[^cite_note-ka-55][^cite_note-Vision_of_Humanity-60][^cite_note-61] Protestors in Morocco and Nepal frequently exchanged plans via the messaging platform [Discord](49271581.md),[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] and other apps like [Instagram](31591547.md), [TikTok](56822861.md) and [Telegram](41501305.md) have also been noted as platforms for communication and spreading their agenda.[^cite_note-64]

While previous protests in Nepal were initially peaceful or online, a government ban on social media in September 2025 spurred [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action) as Gen Z protestors claimed the ban as censorship. The protestors spread [short videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_videos) on Facebook and TikTok alleging corruption and nepotism.[^cite_note-65] In Morocco, the "GenZ 212" Discord server surged from 3,000 members to over 150,000 by 2 October, showing the rapid spread of the movement among youth.[^cite_note-66] Online coordination, in particular using Discord, was also used for a follow-up political process in the Nepalese case: the [online election](81013780.md#Digital_coordination) of a temporary prime minister for a transitionary period.[^cite_note-AJE_GenZ_Discord_PM-67][^cite_note-NYT_politics_chat_room-68]

### Symbols

See also: [Straw Hats' Jolly Roger § Use as a political symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_Hats'_Jolly_Roger#Use_as_a_political_symbol)



The [Straw Hat Pirates' Jolly Roger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_Hat_Pirates'_Jolly_Roger) from *[One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)*

In various Gen Z protests, the [Straw Hats' Jolly Roger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_Hats'_Jolly_Roger) from the manga *[One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)* has been used as an international symbol of the protests.[^cite_note-jm-69][^cite_note-af-70] The flag was used in the protests in [Indonesia](14579.md), [Madagascar](18964.md), [Nepal](171166.md) and [Bulgaria](3415.md).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-pf-72]

The first and the earliest use of the Straw Hat Pirate flag in protest activity in real-life world, was in [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta), Indonesia, in October 2023 when thousands of [Indonesians](4885606.md) protested the Gaza war and Gaza crisis.[^cite_note-73] Almost two years later, student protesters in Indonesia had begun flying the flag again in February 2025.[^cite_note-74] The flag came into prominence during the 2025 Indonesian anti-government protests which started in the weeks leading up to Indonesia's Independence Day on 17 August.[^cite_note-75]

In Nepal, when the government of [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md) was toppled in Nepal, protesters hung the flag at the gates of the [Singha Durbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singha_Durbar) palace as it burned.[^cite_note-jm-69][^cite_note-76] It has also been used in the Philippines,[^cite_note-pm-77] Peru,[^cite_note-pf-72] and Madagascar.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-Vision_of_Humanity-60] The response to the use of the Straw Hat flag has been harsh, with an Indonesian lawmaker saying it was an attempt to divide national unity and another suggesting it could amount to [treason](31292.md),[^cite_note-pm-77] drawing criticism from [Amnesty International](18947898.md).[^cite_note-af-70]

In the United States, many protesters utilised the design of [Manny Heffley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Heffley) from the *[Diary of a Wimpy Kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_a_Wimpy_Kid)* book series during the [George Floyd protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests) to oppose [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality_in_the_United_States) and support the [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) movement. On June 8, 2020, a satirical [TikTok](56822861.md) post of someone drawing the symbol during a protest went viral. It then began to trend alongside the phrase "[the Manny will not be televised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Revolution_Will_Not_Be_Televised)."[^cite_note-79] Users created a flag parodying that of the US, featuring black and yellow stripes and the drawing of Manny in the canton.[^cite_note-80] Graffiti featuring Manny alongside anti-police slogans including [All cops are bastards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_cops_are_bastards) and [Fuck 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuck_12).[^cite_note-81]

In Southeast Asian countries, other pop culture symbols have been used to signal defiance as well, among them references to *[Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter)* and the [three-finger salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-finger_salute_\(pro-democracy\)) from *[The Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_\(franchise\))*.[^cite_note-pm-77] [Raqib Naik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raqib_Hameed_Naik), director of the Center for the Study of Organised Hate, said "I think we are entering a new era of organising that draws heavily from digital, pop and [gaming culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_culture), creating a common vocabulary".[^cite_note-82]
