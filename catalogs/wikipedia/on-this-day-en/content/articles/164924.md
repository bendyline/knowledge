**Norovirus**, also known as **Norwalk virus**, is the most common cause of [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis).[^cite_note-NHS2018-1][^cite_note-CDC2017Glob-7] The infection, sometimes referred to as the **winter vomiting disease**, is characterized by non-bloody [diarrhea](53951.md), [vomiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vomiting), and [stomach pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_pain).[^cite_note-CDC2016Sym-3][^cite_note-Yel2017-4] Fever or headaches may also occur.[^cite_note-CDC2016Sym-3] Symptoms usually develop 24 hours after being exposed, and recovery typically occurs within one to three days.[^cite_note-CDC2016Sym-3] Complications are uncommon, but may include [dehydration](155056.md), especially in the young, the old, and those with other health problems.[^cite_note-CDC2016Sym-3]

The [virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus) is usually spread by the [fecal–oral route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecal%E2%80%93oral_route).[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] This may be through contaminated food or water or person-to-person contact.[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] It may also spread via contaminated surfaces or through [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosols) from the vomit of an infected person.[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] Risk factors include unsanitary food preparation and sharing [close quarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_quarters).[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] Diagnosis is generally based on symptoms.[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] Confirmatory testing is not usually available but may be performed by public health agencies during outbreaks.[^cite_note-Yel2017-4]

Prevention involves proper [hand washing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_washing) and [disinfection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinfection) of contaminated surfaces.[^cite_note-CDC2017Pre-5] There is no [vaccine](32653.md) or specific treatment for norovirus.[^cite_note-CDC2017Pre-5][^cite_note-CDC2017Tx-6] Management involves [supportive care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supportive_care) such as drinking sufficient fluids or [intravenous fluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous_fluids).[^cite_note-CDC2017Tx-6] [Oral rehydration solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_rehydration_solutions) are the preferred fluids to drink, although other drinks without [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine) or [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\)) can help.[^cite_note-CDC2017Tx-6] [Hand sanitizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_sanitizers) based on [alcohols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(chemistry\)) tend to be ineffective against noroviruses due to their being [non-enveloped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope), although some virus genotypes are more susceptible.[^cite_note-park-9]

Norovirus results in about 685 million cases of disease and 200,000 deaths globally a year.[^cite_note-CDC2017Glob-7][^cite_note-GBD2017-8] It is common both in the [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_world) and [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world).[^cite_note-Yel2017-4][^cite_note-10] Those under the age of five are most often affected, and in this group it results in about 50,000 deaths in the developing world.[^cite_note-CDC2017Glob-7] Norovirus infections occur more commonly during winter months.[^cite_note-CDC2017Glob-7] It often occurs in [outbreaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outbreaks), especially among those living in close quarters.[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] In the United States, it is the cause of about half of all [foodborne disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foodborne_disease) outbreaks.[^cite_note-Yel2017-4] The virus is named after the city of [Norwalk, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwalk,_Ohio), where an outbreak occurred in 1968.[^cite_note-Vesikari2021-11]

## Signs and symptoms

Norovirus infection is characterized by [nausea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nausea), [vomiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vomiting), watery [diarrhea](53951.md), abdominal pain, and in some cases, loss of taste. A person usually develops symptoms of [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis) 24 hours after being exposed to norovirus.[^cite_note-CDC2016-12] General lethargy, weakness, muscle aches, headaches, and low-grade fevers may occur. The disease is usually [self-limiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-limiting_\(biology\)), and severe illness is rare. Although having norovirus can be unpleasant, it is not usually dangerous, and most who contract it make a full recovery within two to three days.[^cite_note-NHS2018-1]

Norovirus can establish a long-term infection in people who are [immunocompromised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunocompromised), such as those with [common variable immunodeficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_variable_immunodeficiency) or with a suppressed immune system after [organ transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplantation).[^cite_note-chronic_noro-13] These infections can be with or without symptoms.[^cite_note-chronic_noro-13] In severe cases, persistent infections can lead to norovirus‐associated [enteropathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enteropathy), [intestinal villous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestinal_villous) [atrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrophy), and [malabsorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabsorption).[^cite_note-chronic_noro-13]

### Transmission

Noroviruses are transmitted directly from person to person (62–84% of all reported outbreaks)[^cite_note-15] and indirectly via contaminated water and food. Transmission can be [aerosolized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosolized) when those stricken with the illness vomit or by a toilet flush when vomit or diarrhea is present; infection can follow eating food or breathing air near an episode of vomiting, even if cleaned up.[^cite_note-16] The viruses continue to be [shed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_shedding) after symptoms have subsided, and shedding can still be detected many weeks after infection.[^cite_note-17]

Vomiting, in particular, transmits infection effectively and appears to allow [airborne transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_transmission). In one incident, a person who vomited spread the infection across a restaurant, suggesting that many unexplained cases of food poisoning may have their source in vomit.[^cite_note-18] In December 1998, 126 people were dining at six tables; one person vomited onto the floor. Staff quickly cleaned up, and people continued eating. Three days later, others started falling ill; 52 people reported a range of symptoms, from fever and nausea to vomiting and diarrhea. The cause was not immediately identified. Researchers plotted the seating arrangement: more than 90% of the people at the same table as the sick person later reported becoming ill. There was a direct correlation between the risk of infection of people at other tables and how close they were to the sick person. More than 70% of the diners at an adjacent table fell ill; at a table on the other side of the restaurant, the infection rate was still 25%. The outbreak was attributed to a Norwalk-like virus (norovirus). Other cases of transmission by vomit were later identified.[^cite_note-19]

In one outbreak at an international scout [jamboree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamboree_\(Scouting\)) in the Netherlands, each person with gastroenteritis infected an average of 14 people before increased hygiene measures were put in place. Even after these new measures were enacted, an ill person still infected an average of 2.1 other people.[^cite_note-20] A US [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) study of 11 outbreaks in New York State lists the suspected [mode of transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(medicine\)) as person-to-person in seven outbreaks, foodborne in two, waterborne in one, and one unknown. The source of waterborne outbreaks may include water from municipal supplies, wells, recreational lakes, swimming pools, and ice machines.[^cite_note-pmid8395330-21]

[Shellfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellfish) and salad ingredients are the foods most often implicated in norovirus outbreaks. Ingestion of shellfish that has not been sufficiently heated – under 75 °C (167 °F) – poses a high risk for norovirus infection.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Foods other than shellfish may be contaminated by infected food handlers.[^cite_note-pmid11479930-24] Many norovirus outbreaks have been traced to food that was handled by only one infected person.[^cite_note-pmid14672828-25]

From March to August 2017, in Quebec, Canada, there was an outbreak of norovirus that sickened more than 700 people. According to an investigation by Canada's CFIA Food Control Agency, the culprit was frozen raspberries imported from Harbin Gaotai Food Co Ltd, a Chinese supplier. Canadian authorities subsequently issued a recall on raspberry products from Harbin Gaotai.[^cite_note-26]

According to the CDC, there was a surge in norovirus cases on thirteen [cruise ships](314855.md) in 2023, which marks the highest number of outbreaks since 2012.[^cite_note-27]

### Classification

Noroviruses (NoV) are a genetically diverse group of single-stranded [positive-sense RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-sense_RNA), non-[enveloped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus_envelope) viruses belonging to the family *[Caliciviridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliciviridae)*.[^cite_note-ICTV-28][^cite_note-health-29] According to the [International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Taxonomy_of_Viruses), the genus *Norovirus* has one species: Norwalk virus (*Norovirus norwalkense*).[^cite_note-ICTV-28][^cite_note-ictvhistory-14]

Noroviruses can genetically be classified into at least seven different genogroups (GI, GII, GIII, GIV, GV, GVI, and GVII), which can be further divided into other genetic clusters or [genotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype).[^cite_note-30]

Noroviruses commonly isolated in cases of acute gastroenteritis belong to two genogroups: genogroup I (GI), which includes Norwalk virus, Desert Shield virus, and Southampton virus; and II (GII), which includes Bristol virus, Lordsdale virus, Toronto virus, Mexico virus, Hawaii virus, and Snow Mountain virus.[^cite_note-health-29]

Most noroviruses that infect humans belong to genogroups GI and GII.[^cite_note-pmid10752550-31] Noroviruses from genogroup II, genotype 4 (abbreviated as GII.4), account for the majority of adult outbreaks of gastroenteritis and often sweep across the globe.[^cite_note-32]

Recent examples include the US95/96-US strain, associated with global outbreaks in the mid- to late-1990s; [Farmington Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmington_Hills) virus associated with outbreaks in Europe and the United States in 2002 and in 2004; and the Hunter virus, which was associated with outbreaks in Europe, Japan, and Australasia. In 2006, there was another large increase in NoV infection around the globe.[^cite_note-pmid18177226-33] Reports have shown a link between the expression of human histo-[blood group antigens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_group_antigen) (HBGAs) and the susceptibility to norovirus infection. Studies have suggested the [capsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsid) of noroviruses may have evolved from [selective pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_pressure) of human HBGAs.[^cite_note-pmid21519121-34] HBGAs are not, however, the receptor or facilitator of norovirus infection. Co-factors such as [bile salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bile_salts) may facilitate the infection, making it more intense when introduced during or after the initial infection of the host tissue.[^cite_note-\:0-35] Bile salts are produced by the liver in response to eating fatty foods, and they help with the absorption of consumed [lipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid). It is not yet clear at what specific point in the Norovirus replication cycle bile salts facilitate infection: penetration, uncoating, or maintaining capsid stability.[^cite_note-\:0-35]

The protein [MDA-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFIH1) may be the primary immune sensor that detects the presence of noroviruses in the body.[^cite_note-36] Some people have common variations of the MDA-5 gene that could make them more susceptible to norovirus infection.[^cite_note-37]

Viruses in Norovirus are non-enveloped, with [icosahedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral) geometries. [Capsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsid) diameters vary widely, from 23 to 40 [nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meter#SI_prefixed_forms_of_metre) in diameter. The larger capsids (38–40 nm) exhibit [T=3 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsid#Triangulation_number) and are composed of 180 [VP1 proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_capsid_protein_VP1). Small capsids (23 nm) show T=1 symmetry, and are composed of 60 VP1 proteins.[^cite_note-ViralZone-38] The virus particles demonstrate an amorphous surface structure when visualized using [electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscopy).[^cite_note-pmid11444031-39]

### Genome

Noroviruses contain a linear, non-segmented,[^cite_note-ViralZone-38] [positive-sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_\(molecular_biology\)) [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome) of approximately 7.5 [kilobases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabases), encoding a large polyprotein which is cleaved into six smaller non-structural proteins (NS1/2 to NS7)[^cite_note-40] by the viral [3C-like protease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3C-like_protease) (NS6), a major structural protein ([VP1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_capsid_protein_VP1)) of about 58\~60 [kDa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDa) and a minor capsid protein ([VP2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_capsid_protein_VP2)).[^cite_note-pmid10804143-41]

The most variable region of the viral capsid is the P2 [domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_\(protein\)), which contains [antigen-presenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigen_presentation) sites and carbohydrate-receptor binding regions.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Evolution

Groups 1, 2, 3, and 4 last shared a [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ancestor) in AD 867.[^cite_note-Kobayashi2016-47] The group 2 and group 4 viruses last shared a common ancestor in approximately AD 1443 (95% highest posterior density AD 1336–1542). Several estimates of the evolution rate have been made, varying from 8.98 × 10<sup>−3</sup> to 2.03 × 10<sup>−3</sup> substitutions per site per year.[^cite_note-Ozaki2018-48]

The estimated [mutation rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_rate) (1.21×10<sup>−2</sup> to 1.41 ×10<sup>−2</sup> substitutions per site per year) in this virus is high even compared with other RNA viruses.[^cite_note-Victoria2009-49]

In addition, a [recombination hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recombination_hotspot) exists at the ORF1-ORF2 (VP1) junction.[^cite_note-50]

### Replication cycle

[Viral replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_replication) is cytoplasmic. Entry into the host cell is achieved by attachment to host receptors, which mediates [endocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis). Positive-stranded RNA virus [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_transcription) is the method of replication. Translation takes place by [leaky scanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaky_scanning) and RNA termination-reinitiation. Humans and other mammals serve as the [natural host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_host). Transmission routes are fecal-oral and contamination.[^cite_note-ViralZone-38]

## Pathophysiology

When a person becomes infected with norovirus, the virus replicates within the [small intestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_intestine). The principal symptom is acute [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis), characterized by nausea, forceful vomiting, watery [diarrhea](53951.md), and abdominal pain, that develops 12 to 48 hours after exposure and lasts for 24–72 hours.[^cite_note-cdcfactsheet-51] Sometimes there is loss of taste, general [lethargy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethargy), weakness, muscle aches, [headache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headache), and [fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever). The disease is usually self-limiting.[^cite_note-52]

Severe illness is rare; although people are frequently treated at the emergency ward, they are rarely admitted to the hospital. The number of deaths from norovirus in the United States is estimated to be around 570–800[^cite_note-53] each year, with most of these occurring in the very young, the elderly, and persons with weakened immune systems. Symptoms may become life-threatening in these groups if [dehydration](155056.md) or [electrolyte imbalance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolyte_imbalance) is ignored or left untreated.[^cite_note-pmid16968608-54]

## Diagnosis

Specific diagnosis of norovirus is routinely made by [polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction) (PCR) assays or [quantitative PCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_PCR) assays, which give results within a few hours. These assays are very [sensitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_and_specificity) and can detect as few as 10 virus particles.[^cite_note-pmid17175887-55] Tests such as [ELISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELISA) that use [antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody) against a mixture of norovirus strains are available commercially, but lack specificity and sensitivity.[^cite_note-pmid17391396-56]

## Prevention

After [infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection), [immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunity_\(medical\)) to the *same* strain of the virus – the genotype – protects against reinfection for six months to two years.[^cite_note-pmid25490691-57] This immunity does not fully protect against infection with the other diverse genotypes of the virus.[^cite_note-pmid25490691-57]

In [Canada](5042916.md), norovirus is a [notifiable disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notifiable_disease).[^cite_note-58] In both the US and the UK it is not notifiable.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

### Hand washing and disinfectants

Hand washing with soap and water is an effective method for reducing the transmission of norovirus pathogens. Alcohol rubs (≥62% [isopropyl alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isopropyl_alcohol)) may be used as an adjunct, but are less effective than hand-washing, as norovirus lacks a [lipid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid) [viral envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope).[^cite_note-pmid16765204-61] [Hand sanitizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_sanitizers) based on [alcohols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(chemistry\)) tend to be ineffective against noroviruses due to their being [non-enveloped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope), although some virus genotypes were found in *[in vitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro)* tests with [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) and [isopropyl alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isopropyl_alcohol) to be more susceptible. Alcohol susceptibility patterns between different norovirus genotypes were found to vary widely, and virolysis data for a single strain or genotype were not representative for all noroviruses.[^cite_note-park-9][^cite_note-62] Another study found that alcohol in combination with acid (1% [citric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citric_acid), e.g. from 15% concentrated lemon juice) produced an effective disinfectant of noroviruses.[^cite_note-63]

Surfaces where norovirus particles may be present can be sanitised with a solution of 1.5% to 7.5% of household bleach in water, or other disinfectants effective against norovirus.[^cite_note-cdcfactsheet-51][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Healthcare facilities

In healthcare environments, the prevention of [nosocomial infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nosocomial_infection) involves routine and [terminal cleaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_cleaning). [Nonflammable alcohol vapor in CO<sub>2</sub> systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonflammable_alcohol_vapor_in_CO2_systems) is used in health care environments where medical electronics would be adversely affected by aerosolized chlorine or other caustic compounds.[^cite_note-pmid10833336-66]

In 2011, the CDC published a clinical practice guideline addressing strategies for the prevention and control of norovirus gastroenteritis outbreaks in healthcare settings. Based on a systematic review of published scientific studies, the guideline presents 51 specific [evidence-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_medicine) recommendations, which were organized into 12 categories: 1) patient cohorting and isolation precautions, 2) hand hygiene, 3) patient transfer and ward closure, 4) food handlers in healthcare, 5) diagnostics, 6) [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment), 7) environmental cleaning, 8) staff leave and policy, 9) visitors, 10) education, 11) active case-finding, and 12) communication and notification. The guideline also identifies eight high-priority recommendations and suggests several areas in need of future research.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

### Vaccine trials

LigoCyte announced in 2007 that it was working on a vaccine and had started phase 1 trials.[^cite_note-Norovac-69] The company has since been taken over by [Takeda Pharmaceutical Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeda_Pharmaceutical_Company).[^cite_note-70] As of 2019, a bivalent (NoV GI.1/GII.4) intramuscular vaccine had completed phase 1 trials.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] In 2020 the phase 2b trials were finished.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] The vaccine relies on using a [virus-like particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus-like_particle) that is made of the norovirus capsid proteins to mimic the external structure of the virus. Since there is no RNA in this particle, it is incapable of reproducing and cannot cause an infection.[^cite_note-Norovac-69]

### Persistence

The norovirus can survive for long periods outside a human host depending on the surface and temperature conditions: it can survive for weeks on hard and soft surfaces,[^cite_note-75] and it can survive for months, maybe even years in contaminated still water.[^cite_note-76] A 2006 study found the virus remained on surfaces used for food preparation seven days after contamination.[^cite_note-77]

### Detection in food

Routine protocols to detect norovirus in clams and oysters by [reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_transcription_polymerase_chain_reaction) are being employed by governmental laboratories such as the [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) in the US.[^cite_note-pmid10804149-78]

## Treatment

There is no specific medicine to treat people with norovirus illness. Treatments aim to avoid complications by measures such as the [management of dehydration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_of_dehydration) caused by fluid loss in vomiting and diarrhea,[^cite_note-CDC2017Tx-6] and to mitigate symptoms using [antiemetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiemetics) and [antidiarrheals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidiarrheals).[^cite_note-79]

## Epidemiology

Norovirus causes about 18% of all cases of acute gastroenteritis worldwide. It is relatively common in developed countries and in low-mortality developing countries (20% and 19%, respectively) compared to high-mortality developing countries (14%). Proportionately, it causes more illness in people in the community or in hospital outpatients (24% and 20% respectively) as compared with hospital inpatients (17%), in whom other causes are more common.[^cite_note-FERG-81] Norovirus is considered to be an extremely contagious virus, with a [basic reproduction number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_reproduction_number) (     R  0     {\displaystyle R\_{0}}  {\displaystyle R\_{0}}) estimated around 7.2; the estimated R0 also ranges from 2.1 to as high as 14, based on different conditions.[^cite_note-Norovirus_transmission_dynamics-82] As few as 10 Norovirus virions are needed to infect a person.[^cite_note-Norovirus-83]

Age and emergence of new norovirus strains do not appear to affect the proportion of gastroenteritis attributable to norovirus.[^cite_note-FERG-81]

In the United States, the estimated annual number of norovirus cases in 2023 was 21 million,[^cite_note-84] representing a rate of 6,270 cases per 100,000 individuals.

Norovirus is a common cause of epidemics of gastroenteritis on [cruise ships](314855.md). The CDC, through its Vessel Sanitation Program, records and investigates outbreaks of gastrointestinal illness – mostly caused by norovirus – on cruise ships with both a US and foreign itinerary;[^cite_note-CDC-85] there were 12 in 2015, and 10 from 1 January to 9 May 2016. An outbreak may affect over 25% of passengers, and a smaller proportion of crew members.[^cite_note-86]

## Human genetics

Epidemiological studies have shown that individuals with different ABO(H) (histo-[blood group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_group)) phenotypes are infected with NoV strains in a [genotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype)-specific manner.[^cite_note-Infect-87][^cite_note-pmid20053852-88] GII.4 includes global epidemic strains and binds to more histo-blood group antigens than other genogroups.[^cite_note-Infect-87] [FUT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FUT2) [fucosyltransferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fucosyltransferase) transfers a [fucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fucose) sugar to the end of the [ABO(H)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_group) precursor in [gastrointestinal cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_cell?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [saliva glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saliva_gland). The ABH-antigen produced is thought to act as a receptor for human norovirus: A non-functional [fucosyltransferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fucosyltransferase) FUT2 provides high protection from the most common norovirus strain, GII.4.[^cite_note-89]

Homozygous carriers of any nonsense mutation in the FUT2 gene are called *[non-secretors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-secretors)*, as no ABH-antigen is produced. Approximately 20% of [Caucasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_race) are non-secretors due to G428A and C571T nonsense mutations in FUT2 and therefore have strong – although not absolute – protection from the norovirus GII.4.[^cite_note-Rydell2011-90] Non-secretors can still produce ABH antigens in erythrocytes, as the precursor is formed by FUT1.[^cite_note-Infect-87] Some norovirus genotypes (GI.3) can infect non-secretors.[^cite_note-Nordgren2010-91]

## History

The norovirus was originally named the "Norwalk agent" after [Norwalk, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwalk,_Ohio), in the United States, where an outbreak of acute gastroenteritis occurred among children at Bronson Elementary School in November 1968. In 1972, electron microscopy on stored human stool samples identified a virus, which was given the name "Norwalk virus". Numerous outbreaks with similar symptoms have been reported since. The [cloning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning) and sequencing of the Norwalk virus genome showed that these viruses have a genomic organization consistent with viruses belonging to the family [Caliciviridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliciviridae).[^cite_note-pmid9015097-92] The name "norovirus" (*Norovirus* for the genus) was approved by the [International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Taxonomy_of_Viruses) (ICTV) in 2002.[^cite_note-93] In 2011, however, a press release and a newsletter were published by ICTV, which strongly encouraged the media, national health authorities, and the scientific community to use the virus name Norwalk virus, rather than the genus name Norovirus when referring to outbreaks of the disease. This was also a public response by ICTV to the request from an individual in Japan to rename the Norovirus genus because of the possibility of negative associations for people in Japan and elsewhere who have the family name "Noro". Before this position of ICTV was made public, ICTV consulted widely with members of the Caliciviridae Study Group and carefully discussed the case.[^cite_note-94]

In addition to "Norwalk agent" and "Norwalk virus", the virus has also been called "Norwalk-like virus", "small, round-structured viruses" (SRSVs), Spencer flu, and "Snow Mountain virus".[^cite_note-pmid3036438-95] Common names of the illness caused by noroviruses still in use include "Roskilde illness", "winter vomiting disease",[^cite_note-96] "winter vomiting bug",[^cite_note-bbc_sussex-97][^cite_note-bbc_norfolk-98] "viral gastroenteritis", and "acute nonbacterial gastroenteritis".[^cite_note-pmid16968608-54]
