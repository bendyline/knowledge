**West Azerbaijan province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان آذربایجان غربی)[^cite_note-11] is one of the 31 [provinces](448160.md) of [Iran](14653.md), whose capital and largest city is [Urmia](615663.md).[^cite_note-West_Azerbaijan_Province_Structure-12]

It is in the [northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_\(Iran\)) of the country, bordered by [Turkey](11125639.md) ([Ağrı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C4%9Fr%C4%B1_Province), [Hakkâri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakk%C3%A2ri_Province), [Iğdır](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C4%9Fd%C4%B1r_Province) and [Van Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Province)), [Iraq](7515928.md) ([Erbil](463860.md) and [Sulaymaniyah Governorates](752619.md)) and [Azerbaijan](746.md)'s [Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic](385358.md), as well as the Iranian provinces of [East Azerbaijan](530348.md), [Zanjan](448108.md), and [Kurdistan](448112.md). West Azerbaijan province is part of [Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran) [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_3,_Iran).[^cite_note-13] It forms parts of the areas unofficially known as [Iranian Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_\(Iran\)) and [Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md). It is separated from [Armenia](10918072.md) by Turkey's short border with the [Azerbaijan Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Republic). The province covers an area of 39,487 km<sup>2</sup>, or 43,660 km<sup>2</sup> including [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia).

## History

See also: [Matiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matiene), [Kingdom of Armenia (antiquity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)), [Persarmenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persarmenia), [Azerbaijan (Iran) § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_\(Iran\)#History), and [History of the name Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_name_Azerbaijan)



The region of the modern province as Matiene, as opposed to Atropatene to the east

The major known ancient civilization in the region was that of [Mannaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannaeans), a buffer state between [Urartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) and [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo_Assyrian_Empire) sphere of influence. Mannaeans in turn spoke a language related to Urartian. After the fall of Assyria, the region was known as [Mantiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantiene) (or Matiene) in Greek sources. Matiene bordered on [Atropatene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropatene) situated east of [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia).

The region is known as [Vaspurakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaspurakan) and [Nor Shirakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor_Shirakan) in [Armenian history](87368.md) and made up an important part of [historical Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)), functioning as one of the cradles of [Armenian](387816.md) civilisation.[^cite_note-ArmVan-14] On 26 May 451 AD, a very important battle was fought that would prove pivotal in Armenian history. On the [Avarayr Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avarayr_Plain), at what is modern-day [Churs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churs) in West Azerbaijan province, the Armenian army under [Vardan Mamikonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vardan_Mamikonian) clashed with the [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) one. Although the Persians were victorious on the battlefield itself, the battle proved to be a major strategic victory for Armenians, as Avarayr paved the way to the [Nvarsak Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvarsak_Treaty) (484 AD), which affirmed Armenia's right to practice [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Armenia) freely.[^cite_note-Hewsen-15][^cite_note-Pattie-16]

In the late 4th century AD the [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) incorporated the area into the neighbouring [Adhurpadagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adurbadagan) satrapy to the east.[^cite_note-17] The name Adhurpadagan, later Arabicized to Azerbaijan, derives from [Atropates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropates),[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] an [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples) [satrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap) of [Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes) under the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), who later was reinstated as the satrap of Media under [Alexander of Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great).[^cite_note-ISBN_2-20]

In the 7th century this area was under Islamic rule. After [Babak Khorramdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babak_Khorramdin) revolted, the grip of the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) weakened, allowing some native dynasties to rise. By the first half of the 11th century, the [Byzantine emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) were actively trying to round off their eastern territories, in an attempt to absorb the unstable Armenian dynasties. In 1021–1022, emperor [Basil II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_II) led his army as far as [Khoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoy) within 175 km of [Dvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvin_\(ancient_city\)), and obtained the surrender of royalty from the [Artsruni dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artsruni_dynasty) of [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van,_Turkey).[^cite_note-21] The [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) Turkic tribes, who the local [Hadhabani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadhabani) Kurds initially resisted, eventually conquered the region in the 11th and early 12th centuries. During [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire) rule in the 14th century, [Khoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoy) gained an important role in the region.

After Hadhabanis, three other Kurdish principalities, [Mukriyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukriyan) in the southern part, [Bradosti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradosti) in the middle, and [Donboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donboli_tribe) in the northern part ruled the region for centuries, who temporarily sided with either the [Ottomans](22278.md) or the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran). The [Siege of Dimdim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Dimdim) between the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids) and local Bradosti Kurds took place in this region. After a long and bloody siege led by the Safavid grand vizier [Hatem Beg Ordubadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatem_Beg_Ordubadi), which lasted from November 1609 to the summer of 1610, the [Castle of Dimdim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_of_Dimdim) was captured. All the defenders were killed and [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_the_Great) ordered a general massacre in Bradost and Mukriyan (reported by [Eskandar Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskandar_Beg), Safavid historian in the book *[Alam Aray-e Abbasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alam_Aray-e_Abbasi)*) and resettled the [Afshar tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshar_people) in the region while deporting many Kurdish tribes to the [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan) region, where many of their descendants still reside as of today.

The Safavid control was firmly restored by [Shah Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_the_Great) but during the [Afghan invasion (1722–1728)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Isfahan) more than a century later, the Ottomans captured the northwestern regions of Iran, until [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) expelled them and reasserted Iranian suzerainty over the region and far beyond. The Russian (Tsarist) army occupied the region in 1909, and again in 1912–1914 and 1915–1918 period. The Ottomans occupied the region in 1914–1915 and 1918–1919 periods. The Soviet forces occupied the region in 1941, resulting in the establishment of a short-lived, Soviet-supported [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) called the [Republic of Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mahabad), from November 1945 to November 1946.

The districts of Maku, Khoy, Salmas, and Arasbaran, and the region of Urmia, according to 19th-century administrative division became a part of the northwestern Iranian province of Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-22] In 1937, the province was renamed to *Shomal-e gharb* (Northwestern Province). Shortly after it the province of Azerbaijan was divided into a western and eastern part which were renamed to *Chaharom* (Fourth Province) and *sevom* (Third Province), respectively. In 1961, Fourth province was renamed *West Azerbaijan* by the Iranian authorities.

### Zoroaster claim

Some Muslim researchers[^cite_note-26] have proclaimed that the birth of the prophet [Zoroaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroaster) was in this area, in the vicinity of [Lake Orumieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia), Chichest or Ganzak; recent scholarship, however, indicates that sites in Central Asia are more likely.[^cite_note-27]

## Demographics

### Language and ethnicity

There are no official statistics on the ethnic or linguistic makeup of Western Azerbaijan. Most of the population of the province consists of [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis) and [Kurds](17068.md), with smaller populations of [Armenians](387816.md), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), and [Jews](25955086.md). On the question of linguistic majority of the province, linguist Anonby argued in 2019 that:[^cite_note-Anonby2019-6]

> As is the case for most other parts of Iran, there are no reliable or detailed data on language distribution in West Azerbaijan Province. A number of districts in the province are majority Azerbaijani-speaking, including the capital city of Orumieh (Urmia). Because of this – and perhaps also because of the province's name – it is often assumed that Azerbaijani is the main language of the province as a whole. However, our own preliminary investigations of this topic, which are based on district-by-district calculations... suggest that Kurdish may in fact be the mother tongue of a slight majority of the province's population.

#### Distribution

The counties of [Bukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukan_County),[^cite_note-\:0-28] [Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad_County),[^cite_note-29] [Oshnavieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshnavieh_County),[^cite_note-30] [Piranshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piranshahr_County)[^cite_note-31] and [Sardasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardasht_County)[^cite_note-32] are populated by Kurds, while [Chaldoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldoran_County),[^cite_note-33] [Maku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maku_County),[^cite_note-34] [Miandoab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miandoab_County),[^cite_note-35] [Naqadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naqadeh_County),[^cite_note-36] [Salmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmas_County)[^cite_note-\:1-37][^cite_note-38] and [Takab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takab_County)[^cite_note-39] have a mixed population of both Azerbaijanis and Kurds. Salmas moreover has a Christian minority.[^cite_note-\:1-37]

## Geography

### Location

With an area of 43,660 square kilometers, including [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia), the province of West Azerbaijan is located on the northwest of Iran.

### Climate

Cold northern winds affect the province during winter and cause heavy snow.[^cite_note-48] According to existing meteorological data, local temperatures vary within the province. Average temperature differs from 9.4 °C in [Piranshahr](30865712.md) to 11.6 °C in Mahabad, while it is 9.8 °C in [Urmia](615663.md), 10.8 °C in Khoy, 9.4 °C in [Piranshahr](30865712.md), and in Mahabad 11.6 °C. According to the same data, the highest temperature in the province reaches 34 °C in July, and the lowest temperature is –16 °C in January. The maximum change of temperature in summer is 4 °C and in winter 15 °C.[^cite_note-49]

## Archaeology

Permanent settlements were established in the province as early as the 6th millennium BC as excavation at sites such as [Teppe Hasanlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teppe_Hasanlu) establish. In Hasanlu, a famous Golden Vase was found in 1958. The province is the location of [Tepe Hajji Firuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajji_Firuz_Tepe), site of some of the world's earliest evidence of [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) production.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] *Gooy Teppe* is another significant site, where a metal plaque dating from 800 BC was found that depicts a scene from the *[Epic of Gilgamesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_Gilgamesh)*.

Ruins such as these and the [UNESCO](21786641.md) world heritage site at the [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) compound of [Takht-e Soleymān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takht-e_Soleym%C4%81n) illustrate the strategic importance and tumultuous history of the province through the millennia. Overall, the province enjoys a wealth of historical attractions, with 169 sites registered by the [Cultural Heritage Organization of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Heritage_Organization_of_Iran).

## Higher education

[Urmia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_University) was first built by an American [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian) missionary in 1878. A [medical faculty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_faculty) was also established there, headed by [Joseph Cochran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cochran) and a team of American medical associates. Cochran and his colleagues were buried in an old cemetery in the vicinity of [Urmia](615663.md). Urmia University website says this about them:

"There they lie in peace away from their homeland, and the testimonial epitaphs on their tombs signify their endeavor and devotion to humanity."
