**Commuter rail** or **suburban rail** is a [passenger rail](15461460.md) service that primarily operates within a [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), connecting the [central city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) to its [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) and [commuter towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3] Commuter rail systems can use locomotive-hauled trains or [multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_unit), using electric or diesel propulsion.[^cite_note-\:0-2] Distance charges or zone pricing may be used.

The term can refer to systems with a wide variety of different features and service frequencies, but typically refers to [mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_line_\(railway\)) rail services connecting suburban communities with [central business districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) over medium distances; it is distinguished from [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) systems, which operate inside the urban core.&#x20;

Some services blur the line between suburban rail and [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit); examples include German [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn) in some cities, the [Réseau Express Régional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_Express_R%C3%A9gional) (RER) in Paris, the [S Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_S_Lines) in Milan, many Japanese commuter systems, the [East Rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rail_line) in Hong Kong, and some Australasian suburban networks, such as [Sydney Trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Trains) and [Metro Trains Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Trains_Melbourne). Many commuter rail systems share tracks with other passenger services and [freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo).

In North America, commuter rail sometimes refers only to systems that primarily operate during [rush hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_hour) and offer little to no service for the rest of the day, with [regional rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail) being used to refer to systems that offer all-day service.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

[GO Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit) serves the Greater [Golden Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horseshoe) region surrounding Toronto. Its train services are transitioning from a peak direction commuter railway to a [Regional Express Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit_Regional_Express_Rail).

Most commuter (or suburban) trains are built to main line rail standards,[^cite_note-7] differing from [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) or [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) (metro rail) systems by:

- being larger

- providing more seating and less standing room, owing to the longer distances involved

- having (in most cases) a lower frequency of service

- having scheduled services (i.e., trains run at specific times rather than at specific intervals)

- serving lower-density suburban areas, typically connecting [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) to the city center

- sharing track or [right-of-way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-of-way_\(railroad\)) with intercity and/or [freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight) trains

- not fully grade separated (containing at-grade crossings with crossing gates)

- being able to skip certain stations as an express service due to normally being driver-controlled

### Train schedule

Compared to [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) (or metro rail), commuter/suburban rail often has lower [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headway), following a schedule rather than running to a headway, and wider station spacing. Such services primarily serve lower-density suburban areas (outside the inner city), generally only having one or two stops in a city's central business district, and often share [right-of-way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-of-way_\(railroad\)) with [inter-city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) and [freight trains](70161362.md).[^cite_note-2022_NTD_Glossary-8] Often, service is concentrated in the [peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_hour), with fewer trains per hour running during the day; service late into the evening and on weekends may be reduced or not run at all. Average speeds are usually higher than rapid transit, often 50 km/h (31 mph) or higher. These higher speeds better serve the longer distances involved. Some suburban networks include [skip-stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skip-stop) or [express trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_train) which serve only high-demand [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_station), [interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_station) and other major stations.[^cite_note-9]

The general range of commuter trains' travel distance varies between 15 and 200 km (9.3 and 124.3 mi). Still, longer distances can be covered when trains run between two or more cities (e.g., the S-Bahn in the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr) area of Germany). Distances between stations may vary, but are usually much longer than those of urban rail systems. In the city center, the train may terminate, or [run through](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_service) the city center to serve suburbs on the other side before returning.&#x20;

### Track

Suburban trains' ability to coexist with freight or intercity services in the same [right-of-way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-of-way_\(railroad\)) can drastically reduce system construction costs. However, as lines converge near the city center and the number of trains per hour increases, dedicated tracks may be built or existing tracks set aside for suburban trains only; commonly, two tracks of a [quadruple-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadruple-track_railway) line running through a city center are used for suburban services only.&#x20;

Most such trains run on the [local standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_track_gauges) track. Some systems may run on a narrower or broader gauge. Examples of [narrow-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railway) systems are found in some systems in Sweden, Austria, Switzerland, and on the [Genoa-Casella line](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovia%20Genova-Casella) in Italy. Some countries and regions, such as [San Francisco](49728.md) ([BART](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Area_Rapid_Transit)) in the US and the [Keikyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikyu) system in Japan, use wider track gauges than their national standards.

### Distinction between other modes of rail

#### Metro

Metro rail and [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) usually cover smaller inner-urban areas within 12 to 20 km (7 to 12 mi) of city centers, with shorter stop spacing, use rolling stocks with larger standing spaces, lower top speed and higher acceleration, designed for short-distance travel. They also run more frequently, with headways rather than a published timetable, and use dedicated tracks (underground or elevated). In contrast, commuter rail often shares tracks, technology, and the legal framework within mainline railway systems, and uses rolling stocks with more seating and higher speed for comfort on longer city-suburban journeys.

However, classifying a line as metro or rapid rail can be difficult; many highly developed suburban networks run over dedicated rights-of-way in the city center and may primarily serve densely built-up [inner suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_suburbs). The fact that the terminology is not standardized across countries (even across English-speaking countries) further complicates matters. This distinction is most easily made when there are two (or more) systems such as New York's [subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) and the [LIRR](37139.md) and [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad), Paris' [Métro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_M%C3%A9tro) and [RER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_Express_R%C3%A9gional) along with [Transilien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transilien), Washington D.C.'s [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Metro) along with its [MARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARC_Train) and [VRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Railway_Express), London's tube lines of the [Underground](17839.md) and the [Overground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Overground), [Elizabeth line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_line), [Thameslink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thameslink) along with other commuter rail [operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_London#Heavy_rail), Madrid's [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Metro) and [Cercanías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas_Madrid), Barcelona's [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Metro) and [Rodalies](27342438.md), and Tokyo's [subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_subway) and the [JR lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Japan_Railway_Company#Greater_Tokyo_Area) along with various privately owned and operated commuter rail systems.

#### Regional rail

[Regional rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail) usually provides [rail services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) between towns and cities, rather than solely linking major population hubs as [inter-city rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) does. Regional rail operates outside major cities. Unlike inter-city, it stops at most, if not all, stations between cities. It provides service to smaller communities along the line, which are often byproducts of [ribbon development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_development). Also, it connects to long-distance services at interchange stations located at junctions, terminals, or larger towns along the line. Alternative names are "local train" or "stopping train". Examples include the former [BR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail)'s [Regional Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Railways), France's [TER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_express_r%C3%A9gional) (*Transport express régional*),[^cite_note-10] Germany's [Regionalexpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalexpress) and [Regionalbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalbahn), and South Korea's [Tonggeun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonggeun) and [Mugunghwa-ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugunghwa-ho) services.[^cite_note-11]

#### Inter-city rail

See also: [Inter-city rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail)



A [New South Wales D set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales_D_set) with an upper and lower deck

In some European countries, the distinction between commuter trains and long-distance/intercity trains is subtle, due to the relatively short distances involved. For example, [Nederlandse Spoorwegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen) runs more than 80 direct trains a day over some 50 km between [Amsterdam](844.md) and [The Hague](30269.md), used daily by numerous commuters, despite notionally being an intercity route (albeit one within the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad) conurbation). Likewise, many suburban services in the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md) in Japan connect downtown Tokyo with cities which are substantial in their own right, such as [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) (population 3.7 million), [Saitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_\(city\)) (1.3 million), and [Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_\(city\)) (983,000) but are less than 50 km from the centre of Tokyo.

The United Kingdom has a [largely privatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_privatisation_in_the_UK) rail system (though it is in the process of [renationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_British_Railways)), with different routes and services covered by different private operators. The distinction between commuter and intercity rail is not as clear as it was before privatization (when InterCity existed as a brand in its own right). However, usually it is still possible to tell them apart. Some operators, for example [Thameslink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thameslink), focus solely on commuter services. Others, such as [Avanti West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avanti_West_Coast) and [LNER](57431689.md), run solely intercity services. Others still, such as [GWR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Railway_\(train_operating_company\)) and [EMR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Railway), run a mixture of commuter, regional, and intercity services. Some of these operators use different branding for different types of service (for example, EMR brands its trains as either "InterCity", "Connect" for London commuter services, and "Regional"), but even for those operators that do not, the type of train, amenities offered, and stopping pattern usually tell the services apart.

[Russian commuter trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektrichka), on the other hand, frequently cover areas larger than Belgium itself, although these are still short distances by Russian standards. They have a different ticketing system from long-distance trains, and in major cities, they often operate from a separate section of the train station.

Some consider "inter-city" service to be an express service operating between two main city stations, bypassing intermediate stations. However, this term is used in Australia (Sydney, for example) to describe the regional trains operating beyond the boundaries of the suburban services, even though some of these "inter-city" services stop at all stations, similar to German regional services. In this regard, the German service delineations and naming conventions are clearer and better used for academic purposes.

#### High-speed rail



A Tokyo-bound [E4 Series Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E4_Series_Shinkansen) train. As of October 2021, these have since been retired.

Sometimes [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) can serve commuters' daily needs. The Japanese [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen) high speed rail system is heavily used by commuters in the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md), who commute between 100 and 200 km (62 and 124 mi) by Shinkansen.[^cite_note-12] To meet the demand of commuters, [JR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Railways_Group) sells commuter discount passes. Before 2021, they operated 16-car [bilevel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilevel_car) [E4 Series Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E4_Series_Shinkansen) trains at rush hour, providing a capacity of 1,600 seats.[^cite_note-13] Several lines in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_China), such as the [Beijing–Tianjin Intercity Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Tianjin_Intercity_Railway) and the [Shanghai–Nanjing High-Speed Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Nanjing_High-Speed_Railway), serve a similar role with many more under construction or planned.[^cite_note-14]

In [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_South_Korea), some sections of the high-speed rail network are also heavily used by commuters, such as the section between [Gwangmyeong Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangmyeong_Station) and [Seoul Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Station) on the [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX) network ([Gyeongbu HSR Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_High-speed_railway?action=edit\&redlink=1)), or the section between [Dongtan Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongtan_Station) and [Suseo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suseo_Station) on the [SRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Rapid_Train) Line.

The high-speed services linking [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), and [Basel](4911.md) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Switzerland) (200 km/h (120 mph)) have brought the Central Business Districts (CBDs) of these three cities within 1 hour of each other. This has resulted in unexpectedly high demand for new commuter trips between the three cities and a corresponding increase in suburban rail passengers accessing the high-speed services at the main city-center stations (*Hauptbahnhof*). The [Regional-Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional-Express) commuter service between [Munich](19058.md) and [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) in Germany runs at 200 km/h (120 mph) on the 300 km/h (186 mph) [Nuremberg–Ingolstadt high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg%E2%80%93Ingolstadt_high-speed_railway).

The regional trains [Stockholm](26741.md)–[Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala), Stockholm–[Västerås](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4ster%C3%A5s), Stockholm–[Eskilstuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskilstuna) and [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg)–[Trollhättan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trollh%C3%A4ttan) in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Sweden) reach 200 km/h (120 mph) and have many daily commuters.

In [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain), the [HS1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HS1) domestic services between London and Ashford run at a top speed of 225 km/h, and in peak hours the trains can be full with commuters standing.

The [Athens Suburban Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens_Suburban_Railway) in Greece consists of five lines, 4 of which are electrified. The [Kiato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiato_railway_station)–[Piraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piraeus) line and the [Aigio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aigio_railway_station)–[Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens_Airport_Station) lines reach speeds of up to 180 km/h (112 mph). The [Athens](1216.md)–[Chalcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis) line is also expected to attain speeds of up to 200 km/h (124 mph) upon upgrading of the SKA–Oinoi railway sector. These lines also serve many daily commuters, with the number expected to rise further upon the full completion of the [Acharnes Railway Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acharnes_Railway_Center).

The [Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eski%C5%9Fehir)-[Ankara](802.md) and [Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya)-[Ankara](802.md) high-speed train routes serve as commuter lines in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Turkey).

## Train types

Commuter/suburban trains are usually optimized for maximum passenger volume, in most cases without sacrificing too much comfort and [luggage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luggage) space. However, they seldom have all the amenities of long-distance trains. Cars may be single- or [double-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilevel_car), and aim to provide seating for all. Compared to intercity trains, they have less space, fewer amenities, and limited baggage areas.

### Multiple unit type



An electric multiple unit at Treviglio, on [Milan suburban line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_S_Lines) S5

Commuter rail trains are usually composed of [multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_unit), which are self-propelled, bidirectional, articulated passenger rail cars with driving motors on each (or every other) [bogie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogie). Depending on local circumstances and tradition, they may be powered either by [diesel engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engines) located below the passenger compartment ([diesel multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_multiple_unit)) or by electricity picked up from [third rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_rail) or [overhead lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_lines) ([electric multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_multiple_unit)). Multiple units are almost invariably equipped with control cabs at both ends, which is why they are so frequently used to provide commuter services, owing to the associated short turnaround time.

### Locomotive hauled services



An [Altamont Corridor Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altamont_Corridor_Express) train operating along the [San Francisco Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay); a [MPI F40PH-2C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMD_F40PH) locomotive hauls a consist of [Bombardier BiLevel Coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_BiLevel_Coach).

Locomotive-hauled services are used in some countries or locations. This is often a case of asset-sweating, using a single large combined fleet for intercity and regional services. Loco-hauled services are usually run in [push-pull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push-pull_train) formation, that is, the train can run with the locomotive at the "front" or "rear" (pushing or pulling). Push-pull trains are often equipped with a [control car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_car) at the end of the train, allowing the train operator to control the train from either end. The motive power for locomotive-hauled commuter trains may be either electric or [diesel–electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel%E2%80%93electric_powertrain), although some countries, such as Germany and some of the former Soviet-bloc countries, also use diesel–hydraulic locomotives.

### Seat plans

In the US and some other countries, a three-and-two seat plan is used. Middle seats on these trains are often less popular because passengers feel crowded and uncomfortable.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

In Japan, South Korea, and Indonesia, longitudinal (sideways window-lining) seating is widely used on many commuter trains to increase capacity during rush hours. Carriages are usually not organized to increase [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) (although in some trains at least one carriage would feature more doors to facilitate easier boarding and alighting and bench seats so that they can be folded up during rush hour to provide more standing room) even in the case of commuting longer than 50 km and commuters in the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md), [Seoul metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_metropolitan_area), and [Jabodetabek area](1340014.md) have to stand in the train for more than an hour.

### Asia

#### East Asia



An [CRH6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railway_CRH6) train operating a through service on the [Beijing Suburban Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Suburban_Railway) [Sub-Center line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Center_line) in China.

In Japan, commuter rail systems have extensive networks, frequent service, and high ridership. In many cases, Japanese commuter rail is operationally more like a typical metro system (frequent trains, an emphasis on [standing passengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_passenger), short station spacings) than it is like commuter rail in other countries. Japanese commuter rail commonly interlines with city-center subway lines, with commuter trains continuing into the subway network and then out onto other commuter rail systems on the city's other side. Many Japanese commuter systems operate various stopping patterns to reduce travel time to distant destinations, often using station [passing loops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_loop) instead of dedicated express tracks. Notably, the larger Japanese commuter rail systems are owned and operated by for-profit [private railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_railway) companies, without public subsidy.

[East Japan Railway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Japan_Railway_Company) operates a large suburban train network in [Tokyo](30057.md) with various lines connecting the suburban areas to the city center. While the [Yamanote Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamanote_Line), [Keihin Tohoku Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihin_Tohoku_Line), [Chūō–Sōbu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%AB%C5%8D%E2%80%93S%C5%8Dbu_Line) services arguably are more akin to [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) with frequent stops, simple stopping patterns (relative to other JR East lines) no branching services and largely serving the inner suburbs; other services along the [Chūō Rapid Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%AB%C5%8D_Line_\(Rapid\)), [Sōbu Rapid Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%8Dbu_Line_\(Rapid\))/[Yokosuka Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka_Line), [Ueno–Tokyo Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueno%E2%80%93Tokyo_Line), [Shōnan–Shinjuku Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dnan%E2%80%93Shinjuku_Line) etc. are mid-distance services from suburban lines in the outer reaches of Greater Tokyo through operating into these lines to form a high frequency corridor though central Tokyo.

Other commuter rail routes in Japan include:

- [Hanshin Namba Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshin_Namba_Line) and [Kintetsu Namba Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Line_\(Kintetsu\)#Kintetsu_Namba_Line) have a busy east west underground section that allow trains from both [Hanshin Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshin_Electric_Railway) and [Kintetsu Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintetsu_Railway) to access [Namba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namba), a major commercial center of Osaka, and service destinations east and west of Osaka.

- [Osaka Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Metro) [Sakaisuji Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakaisuji_Line) is a north south line that allows [Hankyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankyu) services from the [Senri Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankyu_Senri_Line), [Kyoto Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankyu_Kyoto_Main_Line) and [Arashiyama Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankyu_Arashiyama_Line) to enter Osaka city center.

- [JR West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_West) [Tozai Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tozai_Line_\(JR_West\)) is an underground east-west corridor allowing trains from the [Kobe Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Kobe_Line), [Takarazuka Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Takarazuka_Line) and [Gakkentoshi Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakkentoshi_Line) to access [Umeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umeda) in central Osaka.

- JR West [Osaka Loop Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Loop_Line) is a mostly elevated loop line that allows for services from the [Yamatoji Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamatoji_Line), [Hanwa Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwa_Line), and [Sakurajima Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakurajima_Line) to loop around central Osaka.

- JR West [Kobe Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Kobe_Line)/[Kyoto Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Kyoto_Line) is a four track corridor allowing [Biwako Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biwako_Line), [Kosei Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosei_Line), [Takarazuka Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Takarazuka_Line), [San'yō Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San'y%C5%8D_Main_Line) and [Akō Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C5%8D_Line) services to service [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto), [Osaka](52230.md) and [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe).

- A special private railway [Kōbe Rapid Transit Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dbe_Rapid_Transit_Railway) owns two underground corridors (a north-south and east-west line) that allow for [Sanyo Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanyo_Electric_Railway), [Hankyu railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankyu), [Hanshin Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshin_Electric_Railway), and [Kobe Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Electric_Railway) services to enter and cross Kobe city center.

- Most of the trains on the [Meitetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitetsu) network operate on a high-frequency trunk line on the [Meitetsu Nagoya Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitetsu_Nagoya_Main_Line), branching out to other lines on the other side of [Nagoya](61720.md).

Commuter rail systems have been inaugurated in several cities in China such as [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Suburban_Railway), [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Suburban_Railway), [Zhengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Plain_Metropolitan_Intercity_Rail), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_Metropolitan_Area_Intercity_Railway), [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha%E2%80%93Zhuzhou%E2%80%93Xiangtan_Intercity_Railway) and the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta_Rapid_Transit). With plans for large systems in the northeastern [Zhejiang](96602.md), [Jingjinji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingjinji), and [Yangtze River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_Delta) areas. The level of service varies considerably from line to line, ranging from [high](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_speed_rail) to near high speeds. More developed and established lines, such as the [Guangshen Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou%E2%80%93Shenzhen_Railway), offer more frequent metro-like service.

The two [MTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTR) lines which are owned and formerly operated by the [Kowloon-Canton Railway Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon-Canton_Railway_Corporation) ([East Rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rail_line) and [Tuen Ma line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuen_Ma_line) which is integrated from the former [West Rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Rail_line) and [Ma On Shan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_On_Shan_line) in 2021), then the ["KCR"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon%E2%80%93Canton_Railway)), and MTR's own [Tung Chung line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tung_Chung_line) connect the new towns in [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories) and the city centre [Kowloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon) with frequent intervals, and some New Territories-bound trains terminate at intermediate stations, providing more frequent services in Kowloon and the towns closer to Kowloon. They use rolling stock with a higher maximum speed and have longer stop spacing than other lines, which only run in the inner urban area. Still, to maximize capacity and throughput, these rolling stocks have longitudinal seating, 5 pairs of doors in each carriage with large standing spaces like the urban lines, and run as frequently as well. Most of the four lines are overground, and some sections of the East Rail Line share tracks with intercity trains to [mainland China](55079.md). The three KCR lines have been integrated into the MTR network since 2008. Most passengers do not need to exit and re-enter the system through separate fare gates and purchase separate tickets to transfer between such lines and the rest of the network (the exceptions are between the Tuen Ma line's [East Tsim Sha Tsui station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tsim_Sha_Tsui_station) and the Tsuen Wan line's [Tsim Sha Tsui station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsim_Sha_Tsui_station).

In Taiwan, the [Western line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Trunk_line) in the [Taipei](57648.md)-[Taoyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoyuan_City) Metropolitan Area, [Taichung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taichung) Metropolitan Area and [Tainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tainan)-[Kaohsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaohsiung) Metropolitan Area as well as the [Neiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiwan_line)-[Liujia line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liujia_line) in the [Hsinchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsinchu) Area are considered commuter rail.

In South Korea, the [Seoul Metropolitan Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Subway) has 22 lines, some of which serve suburban areas. This is especially the case for lines operated by [Korail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korail), such as the [Gyeongui-Jungang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongui-Jungang_Line), the [Gyeongchun Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongchun_Line), the [Suin-Bundang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suin-Bundang_Line), or the [Gyeonggang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonggang_Line). Even some lines not operated by Korail, such as the [AREX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AREX) Line, the [Seohae Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seohae_Line), or the [Shinbundang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinbundang_Line), mostly function as commuter rail. Lastly, even for the "numbered lines" (1–9) of the Seoul Metropolitan Subway which mostly travel in the dense parts of Seoul, some track sections extend far outside of the city, and operate large sections at ground level, such as on the [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_1), [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_3) and [Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_4). In Busan, the [Donghae Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghae_Line), while part of the [Busan Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busan_Metro) system, mostly functions as a commuter rail line.

The [KRL Commuterline](15039992.md) set [Seri 205 (Marchen face)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/205_series) serves the [Bogor Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogor_Line) in Indonesia

In [Indonesia](14579.md), the [KRL Commuterline](15039992.md) is the largest commuter rail system in the country, serving the [Greater Jakarta](1340014.md). In Malaysia, there are three commuter services operated by [Keretapi Tanah Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keretapi_Tanah_Melayu). They are the [KTM Komuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTM_Komuter) that serves [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) and the surrounding [Klang Valley area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klang_Valley), the [KTM Komuter Northern Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTM_Komuter_Northern_Sector) that serves the [George Town Conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Town_Conurbation), [Perak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perak), [Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah) and [Perlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlis) in the northern region of Peninsular Malaysia and the [KTM Komuter Southern Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTM_Komuter_Southern_Sector) which operates within Johor.

In Thailand, the [Greater Bangkok Commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Bangkok_Commuter_rail) and the [Airport Rail Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_Rail_Link_\(Bangkok\)) serve the [Bangkok Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Metropolitan_Region). The [SRT Red Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRT_Red_Lines), a new commuter line in Bangkok, started construction in 2009. It opened in 2021.

Another commuter rail system in Southeast Asia is the [Yangon Circular Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon_Circular_Railway) in [Myanmar](19457.md).

### Europe

See also: [Commuter rail in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_rail_in_the_United_Kingdom) and [List of suburban and commuter rail systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_suburban_and_commuter_rail_systems)

#### Western Europe



[Stadler KISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadler_KISS) and [Re 450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBB-CFF-FFS_Re_450) hauled double-decker cars of [Zürich S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_S-Bahn)

[London](17867.md) has multiple commuter rail routes:

- The [Elizabeth line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_line) runs on a 22-kilometre (14-mile) east–west twin tunnel under central London ([Crossrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossrail) project) as its central core section.

- [Thameslink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thameslink) brings together several branches from northern and southern suburbs and satellite towns into a high-frequency central tunnel underneath London.

- The [London Overground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Overground), by contrast, skirts through the inner suburbs with lines mostly independent of each other, although there are several branches. The [Watford DC line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_DC_line), partly shared with underground trains, uses a third rail, but parallels a main line using overhead wires. The [East London line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London_line) and [North London line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_London_line) run at metro-like frequencies in inner London, making them nearly indistinguishable from metro systems, except that the tracks are shared with freight trains.

- The [Metropolitan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_line), despite being part of the [London Underground](17839.md), is a commuter rail route as it links the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London) to commuter towns outside [Greater London](45367389.md) such as [Rickmansworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickmansworth), [Amersham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amersham) and [Chesham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesham), where it runs to a timetable, being the only [London Underground](17839.md) line with a public timetable published. It also shares tracks with [Chiltern Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiltern_Railways) main line services between London and [Aylesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aylesbury).

The [Merseyrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyrail) network in [Liverpool](18081.md) consists of two commuter rail routes powered by third rail, both of which branch out at one end. At the other, the [Northern line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_line_\(Merseyrail\)) continues out of the city centre to a mainline rail interchange, while the [Wirral line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirral_line) has a city-centre loop.

[Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) has four suburban routes that operate from [Birmingham New Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_New_Street) & [Birmingham Moor Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Moor_Street) stations, one of which uses diesel trains.

The [Tyneside Electrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyneside_Electrics) system in [Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne) operated from 1904 to 1967, using a DC third rail. [British Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail) did not have the budget to maintain the aging electrification system. The [Riverside Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Branch) was closed, while the remaining lines were de-electrified. 13 years later, they were re-electrified using DC overhead wires, and now form the [Tyne & Wear Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyne_&_Wear_Metro) Yellow Line.

Many of the rail services around [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) are branded as [Strathclyde Partnership for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strathclyde_Partnership_for_Transport). The network includes most electrified Scottish rail routes.

The [West Yorkshire Passenger Transport Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Passenger_Transport_Executive) operates 11 services that feed into Leeds, connecting the city with commuter areas and neighboring urban centers within the [West Yorkshire Built-up Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Built-up_Area).

[MetroWest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroWest_\(Bristol\)) is a proposed network in [Bristol](36741.md), northern [Somerset](51763.md) & southern [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire). The four-tracking of the line between [Bristol Temple Meads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Temple_Meads_railway_station) and [Bristol Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Parkway_railway_station) stations will enable local rail services to be separated from long-distance trains.

The [Réseau express régional d'Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_Express_R%C3%A9gional) (RER) is a commuter rail network in the Paris agglomeration. In the center, the RER has high-frequency underground corridors where several suburban branches feed, similar to a [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system.

Commuter rail systems in [German-speaking regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) are called [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn). While in some major cities S-Bahn services run exclusively on separate lines, other systems use existing regional rail tracks.

[Randstadspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstadspoor?action=edit\&redlink=1) is a network of [Sprinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen#Types_of_train_service) train services in and around the city of [Utrecht](50333.md) in the Netherlands. To realize this network, new stations were opened. Separate tracks have been built for these trains, so they can call frequently without disturbing high-frequency Intercity services parallel to these routes.[^cite_note-20] Similar systems are planned for [The Hague](30269.md) and [Rotterdam](26049.md).

#### Northern Europe

In Sweden, electrified commuter rail systems known as *Pendeltåg* are present in the cities of [Stockholm](26741.md) and [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg). The [Stockholm commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_commuter_rail) system, which began in 1968, shares railway tracks with intercity and freight trains but, for the most part, runs on its own dedicated tracks. It is primarily used to transport passengers from nearby towns and other suburban areas into the city center, not for transportation inside the city center. The [Gothenburg commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg_commuter_rail) system, which began in 1960, is similar to the Stockholm system, but does fully share tracks with long-distance trains.

In Norway, the [Oslo commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Commuter_Rail) system has been more limited since 2022, but the remaining commuter lines run on tracks mostly unused by other trains. From 2022, several lines with an hourly frequency and travel times to endpoints of over 1 hour are reclassified as regional trains. Before 2022, Oslo had the largest commuter rail system in the Nordic countries by line length and number of stations. Also, [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_Commuter_Rail?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A6ren_Commuter_Rail), and [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%B8nderbanen) have commuter rail systems. These have only one or two lines each, and they share tracks with other trains.

In [Finland](10577.md), the [Helsinki commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_commuter_rail) network runs on dedicated tracks from [Helsinki Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Central_railway_station) to [Leppävaara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepp%C3%A4vaara_railway_station) and [Kerava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerava). The [Ring Rail Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_Rail_Line) serves [Helsinki Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Airport) and the northern suburbs of [Vantaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vantaa) and is exclusively used by the commuter rail network. On 15 December 2019, the [Tampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampere) region got its own commuter rail service, with trains running from Tampere to [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia,_Finland), [Lempäälä](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemp%C3%A4%C3%A4l%C3%A4), and [Orivesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orivesi).

### Americas

#### North America

Main article: [Commuter rail in North America](1395672.md)

In the United States, Canada, Costa Rica, El Salvador, and Mexico, regional passenger rail services are provided by government or quasi-governmental agencies, with the busiest and most extensive rail networks in the Northeastern US, California, and Eastern Canada. Most North American commuter railways utilize [diesel locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_locomotive) propulsion, except for services in New York City, Philadelphia, Chicago, Denver, San Francisco, and Mexico City; New York's commuter rail lines use a combination of third rail and overhead wire power generation, while Chicago only has two out of twelve services that are electrified. Many newer and proposed systems in Canada and the United States are often geared toward serving peak-hour commutes rather than the all-day systems of Europe, East Asia, and Australia.

### Oceania

See also: [Rail transport in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Australia)



A [Siemens Nexas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Nexas) used on the [Metro Trains Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Trains_Melbourne) network

The five major cities in Australia have suburban railway systems in their metropolitan areas. These networks have frequent services, with frequencies varying from every 10 to every 30 minutes on most suburban lines, and up to 3–5 minutes in peak on bundled underground lines in the city centers of Sydney, Brisbane, Perth, and Melbourne. The networks in each state developed from mainline railways and have never been completely operationally separate from long-distance and freight traffic, unlike [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) systems. The suburban networks are almost completely electrified.

The main suburban rail networks in Australia are:

- The [Sydney Trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Trains) suburban rail network consists of nine lines converging in the underground [City Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Circle) with frequencies as high as three minutes in this section, 5–10 minutes at most major stations all day, and 15 minutes at most minor stations all day.

- The [Sydney rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Sydney) operated by [Sydney Trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Trains) in [Sydney](27862.md) (with connected suburban services in [Newcastle](52665.md) and [Wollongong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollongong) run by its counterpart intercity operator, [NSW TrainLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSW_TrainLink)).

- [Melbourne's rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Melbourne) features sixteen electrified commuter rail lines traversing the city center in the underground [City Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Loop), providing a metro-like service in the central core. A second underground core, the [Metro Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Tunnel), opened in 2025. [V/Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/Line) operates some commuter services between Melbourne and surrounding towns, as well as between Melbourne and some locations within the Melbourne metropolitan area.

- Commuter rail services in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) are provided under the [Queensland Rail City network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Rail_City_network) brand, featuring twelve electrified lines converging in the city center. [Cross River Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_River_Rail) is an under-construction underground cross-city tunnel to relieve pressure on this network.

- [Railways in Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Perth) fall under the [Transperth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transperth) network, which are operated by the [Public Transport Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Transport_Authority_\(Western_Australia\))

- The [Adelaide rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Adelaide) operated by [Adelaide Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_Metro) in [Adelaide](1148.md).

New Zealand has two frequent suburban rail services comparable to those in Australia: the [Auckland rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_in_Auckland) is operated by [Auckland One Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_One_Rail), and the [Wellington rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_in_the_Wellington_Region) is operated by [Transdev Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdev_Wellington).

## Hybrid systems

Hybrid [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_rail)-[suburban rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban_rail) systems that exhibit characteristics of both rapid transit and commuter rail, serving a [metropolitan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_region), are common in German-speaking countries, where they are known as [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn). Other examples include: [Lazio regional railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio_regional_railways) in [Rome](25458.md), the [RER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_Express_R%C3%A9gional) in France and the [Elizabeth line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_line), [London Underground](17839.md) [Metropolitan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_line), [London Overground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Overground) and [Merseyrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyrail) in the UK. Comparable systems can be found in Australia, such as [Sydney Trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Trains) and [Metro Trains Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Trains_Melbourne), and in Japan, where many urban and suburban lines are operated by JR East/West and third-party companies and run at metro-style frequencies. In contrast, comparable systems of this type are generally rare in the United States and Canada, where peak-hour frequencies are more common.

In Asia, the construction of higher-speed urban-suburban rail links has gained traction in various countries, such as in India, with the [Delhi RRTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Regional_Rapid_Transit_System), in China, with the [Pearl River Delta Metropolitan Region intercity railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta_Metropolitan_Region_intercity_railway), and in South Korea, with the [Great Train eXpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Train_eXpress) system. These systems usually run on dedicated elevated or underground tracks for most of their route and have features comparable to [Higher-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-speed_rail).
