The **French Navy**[^cite_note-4] is the [maritime arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) of the [French Armed Forces](10724.md) and one of the four military service branches of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_France). It is among the largest and most powerful [naval forces in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_navies) recognised as being a [blue-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] The French Navy is capable of operating globally and conducting expeditionary missions, maintaining a significant [overseas presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_French_Navy_Deployments). The French Navy is one of eight naval forces currently operating [fixed-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft) [aircraft carriers](2219.md),[^cite_note-8] with its flagship [*Charles de Gaulle*](639127.md) being the only [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion) aircraft carrier outside the [United States Navy](20518076.md), and one of two non-American vessels to use [catapults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_catapult) to launch aircraft.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Founded in the 17th century, the French Navy is one of the oldest navies still in continuous service, with precursors dating back to the Middle Ages. It has taken part in key events in French history, including the Napoleonic Wars and both world wars, and played a critical role in establishing and securing the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) for over 400 years. The French Navy pioneered several innovations in naval technology, including the first steam-powered [ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line), first seagoing [ironclad warship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship), first mechanically propelled [submarine](28825.md), first steel-hulled warship, and first [armoured cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_cruiser).

The French Navy consists of six main components: the [Naval Action Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Action_Force), the [Submarine Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_forces_\(France\)) ([FOST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_oc%C3%A9anique_strat%C3%A9gique) and [ESNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_forces_\(France\))), [French Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Naval_Aviation), the [Navy Riflemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusiliers_marins) (including [Naval Commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Commandos_\(France\))), the [Marseille Naval Fire Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Marine_Fire_Battalion), and the [Maritime Gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Gendarmerie). As of 2021, the French Navy employed 44,000 personnel (37,000 military and 7,000 civilian), more than 180 ships, 200 aircraft, and six commandos units;[^cite_note-11] as of 2014, its [reserve element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_forces_of_France) numbered roughly 48,000.[^cite_note-Key_figures_in_2014-12]

It operates a wide range of fighting vessels, including various aeronaval forces, [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN_\(hull_classification_symbol\)) and [ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_submarines_of_France), [frigates](11464.md), [patrol boats](536773.md) and support ships, with aircraft carrier *Charles de Gaulle* serving as the centrepiece of most expeditionary forces.

## Origins

The history of French naval power dates back to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), and had three loci of evolution:

- The [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), where the *[Ordre de Saint-Jean de Jérusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller)* had its own navy, the [Levant Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant_Fleet), whose principal ports were [Fréjus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9jus), [Marseille](40888948.md), and [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon). The *Ordre*, which was both a religious and military order, recruited knights from the families of [French nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nobility). Members who had fulfilled their service at sea were granted the rank of [Knights Hospitaller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller), elites who served as the officer corps. The *Ordre* was one of the ancestors of modern French naval schools including the [French Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Navale).

- The [Manche](9230.md) along [Normandy](21724.md) which, since [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror), always tendered capable [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines) and [sailors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailors) from its numerous active seaports.

- The [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), where the navy of the [Duchy of Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brittany) eventually constituted the nucleus of the royal *[Flotte du Ponant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flotte_du_Ponant),* which projected French naval power across the Atlantic and the Americas.

## Names and symbols



The historic "Golden Anchor" symbol.

The first true French Royal Navy ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *la Marine Royale*) was established in 1626 by [Cardinal Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu), chief minister to King [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII). During the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), *la Marine Royale* was formally renamed *la Marine Nationale*. Under the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) and the [Second French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire), the navy was designated as the [Imperial French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_French_Navy) (*la Marine impériale française*). Institutionally, however, the navy has never lost its short familiar nickname, *la Royale*.

The original symbol of the French Navy was a golden [anchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor), which, beginning in 1830, was interlaced by a [sailing rope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_\(sailing\)); this symbol was featured on all naval vessels, arms, and uniforms.[^cite_note-13] Although anchor symbols are still used on uniforms, a new naval logo was introduced in 1990 under [Naval Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_French_Navy) Bernard Louzeau, featuring a modern design that incorporates the [tricolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_France)—by flanking the bow section of a white warship with two ascending red and blue spray foams—and the inscription "*Marine nationale*".

## History

Main article: [History of the French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_French_Navy)

### 17th century

Cardinal Richelieu personally supervised the Navy until his death in 1643.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] He was succeeded by his protégé, [Jean Baptiste Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Colbert), who introduced the first code of regulations of the French Navy and established the original naval dockyards in [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_France) and [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] Colbert and his son, the [Marquis de Seignelay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Colbert,_Marquis_de_Seignelay), between them administered the Navy for twenty-nine years.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14]



[Battle of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lagos_\(1693\)), 1693.

During this century, the Navy cut its teeth in the [Anglo-French War (1627–1629)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_War_\(1627%E2%80%931629\)), the [Franco-Spanish War (1635–59)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Spanish_War_\(1635%E2%80%9359\)), the [Second Anglo-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Dutch_War), the [Franco-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Dutch_War), and the [Nine Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Years'_War). Major battles in these years include the [Battle of Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Augusta), [Battle of Beachy Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beachy_Head_\(1690\)), the [Battles of Barfleur and La Hougue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Barfleur_and_La_Hougue), the [Battle of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lagos_\(1693\)), and the [Battle of Texel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Texel_\(1694\)).

### 18th century

The 1700s opened with the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession), over a decade long, followed by the [War of the Austrian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession) in the 1740s. Principal engagements of these wars include the [Battle of Vigo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vigo_Bay) and two separate [Battles of Cape Finisterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Finisterre_\(disambiguation\)) in 1747. The most grueling conflict for the Navy, however, was the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), in which it was virtually destroyed.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] Significant actions include the [Battle of Cap-Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cap-Fran%C3%A7ais), the [Battle of Quiberon Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Quiberon_Bay), and another [Battle of Cape Finisterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Finisterre_\(1761\)).



[Battle of the Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Chesapeake), 1781.

The Navy regrouped and rebuilt, and within 15 years it was eager to join the fray when [France intervened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_American_Revolutionary_War) in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] Though outnumbered everywhere, the French fleets held the British at bay for years until victory.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] After this conflict and the concomitant [Anglo-French War (1778–1783)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_War_\(1778%E2%80%931783\)), the Navy emerged at a new height in its history.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] Major battles in these years include the [Battle of the Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Chesapeake), the [Battle of Cape Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Henry), the [Battle of Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grenada), the [invasion of Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Dominica_\(1778\)), and three separate [Battles of Ushant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ushant_\(disambiguation\)).

Within less than a decade, however, the Navy was decimated by the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) when large numbers of veteran officers were dismissed or executed for their noble lineage.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] Nonetheless, the Navy fought vigorously through the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars) as well as the [Quasi-War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-War). Significant actions include a fourth Battle of Ushant (known in English as the [Glorious First of June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_First_of_June)), the [Battle of Groix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Groix), the [Atlantic campaign of May 1794](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_campaign_of_May_1794), the [French expedition to Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_expedition_to_Ireland_\(1796\)), the [Battle of Tory Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tory_Island), and the [Battle of the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Nile).

### 19th century

See also: [French Imperial Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Imperial_Navy)

Other engagements of the Revolutionary Wars ensued in the early 1800s, including the [Battle of the Malta Convoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Malta_Convoy_\(1800\)) and the [Algeciras Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras_Campaign). The Quasi-War wound down with single-ship actions including [USS *Constellation* vs *La Vengeance*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constellation_vs_La_Vengeance) and [USS *Enterprise* vs *Flambeau*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Enterprise_vs_Flambeau).

When [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) was crowned Emperor in 1804, he attempted to restore the Navy to a position that would enable his plan for an invasion of England.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] His dreams were dashed by the [Battle of Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar) in 1805, where the British all but annihilated a combined Franco-Spanish fleet, a disaster that confirmed British naval superiority for the rest of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars). Still, the Navy did not shrink from action: among the engagements of this time were the [Battle of the Basque Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Basque_Roads), the [Battle of Grand Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grand_Port), the [Mauritius campaign of 1809–1811](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_campaign_of_1809%E2%80%931811), and the [Battle of Lissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lissa_\(1811\)).



[Battle of Navarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Navarino), 1827.

After Napoleon's fall in 1815, the long era of Anglo-French rivalry on the seas began to close, and the Navy became more of an instrument for expanding the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] Under King [Charles X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_X_of_France), the two nations' fleets fought side by side in the [Battle of Navarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Navarino), and throughout the rest of the century they generally behaved in a manner that paved the way for the [Entente Cordiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_Cordiale).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14]



[Battle of the Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Tagus_\(1831\)), 1831.

Charles X sent a large fleet to execute the [invasion of Algiers in 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Algiers_in_1830). The next year, his successor, [Louis Philippe I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Philippe_I), made a show of force against Portugal at the [Battle of the Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Tagus_\(1831\)), and in 1838 conducted another display of [gunboat diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_diplomacy), this time in Mexico at the [Battle of Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Veracruz_\(1838\)). Beginning in 1845, a five-year [Anglo-French blockade of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_blockade_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) was imposed on Argentina over trade rights.

The Emperor [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) was determined to follow an even stronger foreign policy than his predecessors, and the Navy was involved in a multitude of actions around the world. He joined in the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) in 1854; major actions for the Navy include the [siege of Petropavlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Petropavlovsk) and the [Battle of Kinburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kinburn_\(1855\)). The Navy was heavily involved in the [Cochinchina Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochinchina_Campaign) in 1858, the [Second Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War) in China, and the [French intervention in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_intervention_in_Mexico). It took part in the [French expedition to Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_expedition_to_Korea) and the [Shimonoseki campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimonoseki_campaign). In the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) in 1870, the Navy imposed an effective blockade of Germany, but events on land proceeded at such a rapid pace that it was superfluous. Isolated engagements between French and German ships took place in other theaters, but the war was over in a matter of weeks.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The Navy continued to protect colonial safety and expansion under the [French Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic). The [Sino-French War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-French_War) saw considerable naval action including the [Battle of Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fuzhou), the [Battle of Shipu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shipu), and the [Pescadores Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pescadores_campaign_\(1885\)). In Vietnam, the Navy helped wage the [Tonkin Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkin_Campaign) which included the [Battle of Thuận An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thu%E1%BA%ADn_An), and it later participated in the [Franco-Siamese conflict of 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Siamese_conflict_of_1893).



Steam-powered ship of the line *[Napoléon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_ship_Napol%C3%A9on_\(1850\))*, 1852.

The 19th century French Navy brought forth numerous new technologies. It led the development of [naval artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_artillery) with its invention of the highly effective [Paixhans gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paixhans_gun). In 1850, [*Napoléon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_ship_Napol%C3%A9on_\(1850\)) became the first steam-powered [ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line) in history, and [*Gloire*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_ironclad_Gloire) became the first seagoing [ironclad warship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship) nine years later. In 1863, the Navy launched [*Plongeur*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_submarine_Plongeur), the first submarine in the world to be propelled by mechanical power. In 1876, [*Redoutable*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_ironclad_Redoutable) became the first steel-hulled warship ever. In 1887, [*Dupuy de Lôme*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_cruiser_Dupuy_de_L%C3%B4me) became the world's first [armoured cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_cruiser).

During the latter part of the century, French officers developed the so-called *[Jeune École](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeune_%C3%89cole)* (Young School) theory that emphasized the use of small, cheap [torpedo boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat) to destroy expensive battleships, coupled with long-range commerce raiders to attack an opponent's merchant fleet.

### 20th and 21st centuries



A [Caudron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudron) seaplane being craned on [*Foudre*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_seaplane_carrier_Foudre), 1914.

The first [seaplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane), the French [Fabre Hydravion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabre_Hydravion), was flown in 1910, and the first [seaplane carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane_carrier), [*Foudre*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_seaplane_carrier_Foudre), was christened in the following year.[^cite_note-17] Despite that innovation, the general development of the French Navy slowed down in the beginning of the 20th century as the [Anglo-German naval arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-German_naval_arms_race) grew in intensity.

It entered [World War I](4764461.md) with relatively few modern vessels, and during the war few warships were built because the main French effort was on land. While the British held control of the North Sea, the French held the Mediterranean, where they mostly kept watch on the [Austro-Hungarian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Navy).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] The largest operations of the Navy were conducted during the [Dardanelles Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_operations_in_the_Dardanelles_Campaign).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] In December 1916, during the [Noemvriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noemvriana) events, French warships also bombarded Athens, trying to force the pro-German government of Greece to change its policies.[^cite_note-18] The French Navy also played an important role in countering Germany's [U-boat campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat_Campaign_\(World_War_I\)) by regularly patrolling the seas and escorting convoys.[^cite_note-Auphan1-14]



Battleship [*Richelieu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_battleship_Richelieu), 1943.

Between the World Wars, the Navy modernized and expanded significantly, even in the face of limitations set by the 1922 [Washington Naval Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Treaty).[^cite_note-Auphan1-14] New additions included the heavy and fast [*Fantasque*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasque-class_destroyer) "super-[destroyers](8779.md)", the [*Richelieu*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richelieu-class_battleship) battleships, and the submarine [*Surcouf*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_submarine_Surcouf) which was the largest and most powerful of its day.

During the first two years of [World War II](32927.md), the French Navy participated in the [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic), the [Norwegian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_campaign), the [Dunkirk evacuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation) and, briefly, the [Battle of the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Mediterranean). However, after the [fall of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_France) in June 1940, the Navy was obligated to remain neutral under the terms of the armistice that created [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France). Approximately 100 French warships around the world joined the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French) under [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle), but the bulk of the French Navy, including all its [capital ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_ship), declared their allegiance to Vichy France. On 3 July 1940, [Force H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_H) of the Royal Navy launched an [attack on Mers-el-Kébir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Mers-el-K%C3%A9bir) in Algeria to prevent the French fleet there from falling into German hands, destroying or damaging several French warships. In 1942, the Vichy Navy fought the [Naval Battle of Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Battle_of_Casablanca) when the Allies [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch) [French North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_North_Africa), but the confrontations were set aside once the Germans [occupied Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Anton). The capital ships were a primary target for the Germans, but before they could be seized they were [scuttled by their own crews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuttling_of_the_French_fleet_in_Toulon). A few small ships and submarines managed to escape in time, and these joined the [Free French Naval Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_Naval_Forces), an arm of [Free France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) that fought as an adjunct of the Royal Navy until the end of the war. In the [Pacific theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) as well, Free French vessels operated until the Japanese capitulation; [*Richelieu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_battleship_Richelieu) was present at the [Japanese Instrument of Surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender).

The Navy later provided fire support and troop transport in the [Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina_War), the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War), the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), and the [Kosovo War](16760.md).

Since 2000, the Navy has given logistical support to the [War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md) as well as the global [war on terror](13425800.md). In 2011, it assisted [Opération Harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Harmattan) in Libya.

The chief of the naval staff is Amiral [Nicolas Vaujour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Vaujour?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-19] and as of November 2024 the Navy has an active strength of 37,965 military personnel and 3,000 civilian staff.[^cite_note-20] The Navy is organised into four main operational branches:

- The [Force d'Action Navale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_d'Action_Navale) (Naval Action Force) – Surface fleet.

- The [Forces Sous-marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_Sous-marines) (Submarine forces) – Nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines and fleet submarines.

- The [Aviation Navale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Navale) (Naval air force) – Ground and sea-based aircraft.

- The [Fusiliers Marins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusiliers_Marins) (Naval riflemen) – Protection force and infantry including the Navy special forces ([Commandos Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandos_Marine)).

In addition, the [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md) of France maintain a maritime force of patrol boats that falls under the operational command of the French Navy:

- The [Gendarmerie maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie_maritime) – The coast guard of France.

During most of the Cold War, the Navy was organised in two squadrons based in Brest and Toulon, commanded by ALESCLANT (*Amiral commandant l'escadre de l'Atlantique*) and ALESCMED (*Amiral commandant l'escadre de la Méditerranée*) respectively. Since the post-Cold War restructuring process named Optimar '95, the two components have been divided into the Naval Action Force (commanded by ALFAN) and the Antisubmarine Group (commanded by ALGASM).[^cite_note-21]

### Main naval bases



French navy facilities in metropolitan France (status 2015).

As of 2014, the largest French naval base is the [military port of Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_port_of_Toulon). Other major bases in metropolitan France are the [Brest Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest_Arsenal) and [Île Longue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_Longue) on the Atlantic, and [Cherbourg Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherbourg_Naval_Base) on the English Channel. Overseas French bases include [Fort de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint_Louis_\(Martinique\)) and [Degrad des Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrad_des_Cannes) in the Americas; [Port des Galets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_des_Galets) and [Dzaoudzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzaoudzi) in the Indian Ocean; and [Nouméa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noum%C3%A9a) and [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete) in the Pacific. In addition, the navy shares or leases bases in foreign locales such as [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md), [Dakar](44251.md) and [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_\(city\)).

### Ships and submarines

Main article: [List of active French Navy ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_French_Navy_ships)



The [BRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A2timent_ravitailleur_de_forces) *Jacques Chevalier* conducting replenishment at sea with *[Charles de Gaulle](639127.md)* and [*Alsace*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Alsace) FREMM-DA.

Although French [naval doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_doctrine) calls for two [aircraft carriers](2219.md), the French only have one, [*Charles de Gaulle*](639127.md). Originally a planned order for [French aircraft carrier PA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_aircraft_carrier_PA2) was based on the design of the British [*Queen Elizabeth*-class aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth-class_aircraft_carrier) recently constructed and launched for the Royal Navy. However, the French programme had been delayed several times for budgetary reasons and the result was priority being given to the more exportable [FREMM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FREMM) project. In April 2013 it was confirmed that the second aircraft carrier project would be abandoned due to defence cuts announced in the [2013 French White Paper on Defence and National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_White_Paper_on_Defence_and_National_Security).

The French Navy operates three [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship), eleven [destroyers](8779.md) (classified in the French Navy as surface combatants of the "first rank"), five general purpose frigates and has a commitment to six [fleet submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN_\(hull_classification_symbol\)) (SSNs). These vessels, with the aircraft carrier *Charles de Gaulle*, constitute the French Navy's main ocean-going war-fighting force, while the four [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) ([SSBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSBN)) of the navy's [Strategic Oceanic Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_oc%C3%A9anique_strat%C3%A9gique) provide the backbone of the French [nuclear deterrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_deterrent).

### Application requirement

#### Seamen

Seamen must be at least 17 but no more than 30 years old, with no minimum level of schooling.

#### Petty officers

Petty officers must be at least 17 but no more than 30 years old, with at least a high school diploma giving access to university studies.

Petty officer candidates begin training with five months at the Petty Officer School of Maistrance at [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_France).

#### Contract officers

Contract officers serve on an initial eight-year contract, renewable up to 20 years.

- Operational officers must be 21 to 26 years old, with at least a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree, or having passed a [classe préparatoire aux grandes écoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classe_pr%C3%A9paratoire_aux_grandes_%C3%A9coles) in engineering or business.

- Staff officers have to be 21 to 29 years old, with an honors degree or master's degree in a field corresponding to the military occupational specialty.

#### Career officers

- Less than 22 years old, having passed a *classe préparatoire* in science. After four years at the [École Navale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Navale) (naval academy) a cadet will graduate as a commissioned [Enseigne de Vaisseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranks_in_the_French_Navy#Officers) with an engineering degree.

- Less than 25 years old, having an honors degree in science. After three years at the naval academy a cadet will graduate as Enseigne de Vaisseau with an engineering degree.

- Less than 27 years old, having a master's degree. After two years at the naval academy a cadet will graduate as an Enseigne de Vaisseau.

## Customs and traditions

### Ranks

Main article: [Ranks in the French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranks_in_the_French_Navy)

The [rank](150237.md) insignia of the French Navy are worn on shoulder straps of shirts and white jackets, and on sleeves for navy jackets and mantels. Until 2005, only [commissioned officers](36301328.md) had an anchor on their insignia, but [enlisted personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlisted_personnel) are now receiving them as well. Commanding officers have titles of *capitaine*, but are called *commandant* (in the army, both *capitaine* and *commandant* are ranks, which tends to stir some confusion among the public). The two highest ranks, *vice-amiral d'escadre* and *amiral* ([admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral)), are functions, rather than ranks. They are assumed by officers ranking *vice-amiral* ([vice admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral)). The only *amiral de la flotte* ([Admiral of the Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_Fleet)) was [François Darlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Darlan) after he was refused the dignity of *amiral de France* ([Admiral of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_France)). Equivalent to the dignity of [Marshal of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_of_France), the rank of *amiral de France* remains theoretical in the [Fifth Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fifth_Republic); it was last granted in 1869, during the [Second Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire), but retained during the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) until the death of its bearer in 1873. The title of *amiral de la flotte* was created so that Darlan would not have an inferior rank than his counterpart in the British [Royal Navy](26061.md), who had the rank of [Admiral of the Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_Fleet_\(Royal_Navy\)).

### Military music



The [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_France) band before its deactivation in 2012.

The main military musical unit of the French Navy is the Military Band of the [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon) Fleet ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *La musique des équipages de la flotte de Toulon*), founded on 13 July 1827.[^cite_note-27] The [Bagad Lann Bihoue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagad_Lann_Bihoue), based on the [bagad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagad) bands in [Bretagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany), is currently the sole [pipe band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_band) in the service of the French Navy, which uses [bagpipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagpipe) and [bombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombard_\(music\)), and thus is affiliated to the band.

In [Canada](5042916.md), French naval music has affected the traditions of [Canadian navy bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_bands_in_Canada). French navy bands in the country date back to the era of [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France).[^cite_note-28] Musical units were primarily attached to the [Compagnies Franches de la Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnies_Franches_de_la_Marine) and the [Troupes de la marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troupes_de_la_marine), the former of which maintained two drums (tambour) and a [fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fife_\(instrument\)).

## Future

Main article: [Future of the French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_French_Navy)



[FREMM multipurpose frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FREMM_multipurpose_frigate).



[EDA-R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engin_de_d%C3%A9barquement_amphibie_rapide) landing craft.



[*Barracuda*-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Barracuda_class_submarine).

France's [financial problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone_crisis) have affected all branches of her military. The [2013 French White Paper on Defence and National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_White_Paper_on_Defence_and_National_Security) cancelled the long-planned [new aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_aircraft_carrier_PA2) and a possible fourth [*Mistral*-class amphibious assault ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral-class_amphibious_assault_ship).[^cite_note-29] The backbone of the fleet will be the *Aquitaine*-class [FREMM anti-submarine frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FREMM_multipurpose_frigate), replacing the [*Georges Leygues* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Leygues-class_frigate), but plans to buy a possible seventeen FREMMs were cut back to eleven and then to eight. The cancellation of the third and fourth Horizon destroyers meant that the last two FREMM hulls, which entered service between 2021 and 2023, are fitted out as FREDA air-defence ships to replace the [*Cassard* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassard-class_frigate).[^cite_note-30] DCNS has shown a FREMM-ER concept to meet this requirement, emphasising [ballistic missile defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_defence) with the Thales Sea Fire 500 AESA radar.[^cite_note-31] Industrial considerations mean that the funds for FREMMs 9-11 are now being spent on five more exportable *Frégate de Défense et d'Intervention* ([*FDI*, "intermediate size frigates"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_and_intervention_frigate)) from 2024 to supplement, and ultimately replace, the *La Fayette* class, three of which are being upgraded with new sonars to operate into the early 2030s.[^cite_note-32] With respect to support ships, the [*Durance* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durance-class_tanker) are being replaced under the FLOTLOG project by up to four derivatives of Italy's [*Vulcano*-class logistic support ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcano-class_logistic_support_ship), with three being delivered from 2023 to 2027.[^cite_note-33] A fourth potential ship is delayed until after 2030.[^cite_note-34]

Construction has started on the first of six [*Barracuda*-class nuclear attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Barracuda-class_submarine); commissioning of *Suffren* took place in 2020. These nuclear attack submarines are to be followed in the 2030s by the incremental introduction of a [new class of nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNLE_3G) (SSBNs) whose construction began in 2024.

The first MM40 [Exocet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet) Block 3 missile was test-fired in 2010 to be produced. Naval versions of the [*SCALP EG*](306908.md) land-attack cruise missile are under development, along with a planned [Aster Block 1NT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aster_\(missile_family\)) with greater capabilities against ballistic missiles.

In October 2018, the French Ministry of Defence launched an 18-month study for €40 million for the eventual future replacement of the aircraft carrier *Charles de Gaulle* beyond 2030. A decision to build the new carrier was taken by President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) in 2020[^cite_note-35] and once it enters service it is anticipated to remain in service until beyond 2080.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Construction of the new carrier is to begin in around 2025 with service entry anticipated in the latter 2030s.

### Other important French naval officers

- [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [Florent de Varennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florent_de_Varennes): first admiral of France

- [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [Jean de Vienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_Vienne): admiral of the French fleet during the Hundred Years' War

- [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [de Grasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Joseph_Paul_de_Grasse): commander of the French fleet at [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Chesapeake) during the American Revolutionary War

- [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) [Courbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9d%C3%A9e_Courbet): commander of the Far East Squadron

- [Vice-Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Admiral) [Duquesne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Duquesne): commander of the French fleet at the [Battle of Agosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Agosta)

- [Vice-Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Admiral) [Jean-Paul de Saumeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalier_Paul), often called Chevalier Paul: served in several Mediterranean campaigns

- [Vice-Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Admiral) [Tourville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hilarion_de_Tourville): commander of the French fleet at the [Battle of Beachy Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beachy_Head_\(1690\))

- [Vice-Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Admiral) [Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Charles_Villeneuve): commander of the French and [Spanish](26667.md) fleets at the [Battle of Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar)
