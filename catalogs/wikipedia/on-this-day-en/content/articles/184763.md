**Málaga** ([/ˈmæləɡə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/12/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-M%C3%A1laga.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-M%C3%A1laga.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/12/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-M%C3%A1laga.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-M%C3%A1laga.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; Spanish: [\[ˈmalaɣa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/98/Pronunciation_of_M%C3%A1laga_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_M%C3%A1laga_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/98/Pronunciation_of_M%C3%A1laga_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_M%C3%A1laga_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_in_Spain) of [Spain](26667.md) and the capital of the [province of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_M%C3%A1laga), in the [autonomous community](55002.md) of [Andalusia](2736.md). With a population of 592,346 in 2024, it is the 2nd-largest city in Andalusia and the [6th-largest in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_lists_of_Spanish_municipalities#By_population).[^cite_note-population-7] It lies in Southern [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) on the [Costa del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_del_Sol) ('Coast of the Sun') of the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean), primarily on the left bank of the [Guadalhorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalhorce). The urban core originally developed in the space between the [Gibralfaro Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro) and the [Guadalmedina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalmedina).

Málaga's history spans about 2,800 years, making it one of the [oldest continuously inhabited cities in Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_time_of_continuous_habitation#Europe). According to most scholars, it was founded about 770 BC by the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) from [Tyre](30796.md) as *Malaka*.[^cite_note-WawnWood2000-8] From the 6th century BC the city was under the hegemony of [Ancient Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage), and from 218 BC, it was under [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) rule, economically prospering owing to [garum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garum) production.[^cite_note-9] In the 8th century, after a period of Visigothic and Byzantine rule, it was placed under Islamic rule. In 1487, the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) gained control in the midst of the [Granada War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_War). In the 19th century, the city underwent a period of industrialisation followed by a decay in all socioeconomic parameters in the last third of the century.[^cite_note-10]

The most important business sectors in Málaga are tourism, construction and technology services, but other sectors such as transportation and [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) are beginning to expand. Málaga has consolidated as a tech hub, with companies mainly concentrated in the [Málaga TechPark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia_Technology_Park) (Technology Park of Andalusia).[^cite_note-11] It hosts the headquarters of the region's largest bank, [Unicaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicaja), and it is the fourth-ranking city in Spain in terms of [economic activity](27393.md).[^cite_note-anuarieco.lacaixa.comunicacions.com-12] Regarding transportation, Málaga is served by the [Málaga–Costa del Sol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Airport) and the [Port of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_M%C3%A1laga), and the city [was connected to the high-speed railway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93M%C3%A1laga_high-speed_rail_line) in 2007.

## History

Main article: [History of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_M%C3%A1laga)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_M%C3%A1laga).



Clay amphora from the [Cerro del Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_del_Villar) site, near the mouth of the [Guadalhorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalhorce) (6th century BC).

[Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) from [Tyre](30796.md) founded a [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_colony) named *Malake*[^cite_note-Huss1985-13] about 770 BC ([Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_language): 𐤌𐤋𐤊𐤀, mlkʾ).[^cite_note-Huss1985-13] The town controlled access to the [Guadalmedina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalmedina) and served as a waypoint on trade routes between [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) and the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar). Like other Phoenician colonies, it fell under [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage) rule during the 6th or 5th century BC. The Phoenician and Later Roman urban core developed around an area running from the [Gibralfaro Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro) to the mouth of the *Malaca flumen* ([Guadalmedina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalmedina)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECorrales_Aguilar2003377-14]

After the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars), the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) took control of the town known to them as *Malaca*. By the 1st century BC, [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) alluded to its Phoenician profile, in contrast to the hellenised characteristics of the neighbouring settlement of [*Mainake*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainake_\(Greek_settlement\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECorrales_Aguilar2003381-15]

Transformed into a confederated city, it was under a special law, the *[Lex Flavia Malacitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Flavia_Malacitana)*. A [Roman theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_theatre_\(structure\)) was built at this time.[^cite_note-16] After the fall of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), it was ruled first by the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths). The city was taken c. 552 by the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECollado_Campaña20122-17] either Malaca or [Carthago Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthago_Nova) possibly then becoming the capital of the province of [Spania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spania).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECollado_Campaña20123-18] The Byzantines restored and expanded the docks, thus consolidating the fishing and trading tradition the city already enjoyed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECollado_Campaña20123-18] The city was retaken by the Visigoth King [Sisebut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisebut) in 615.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECollado_Campaña20125-19] The Islamic conquest of Málaga (rendered as مالقة—*Mālaqah*—in Arab sources) by Arab and Berber forces took place in 711 or perhaps 713.[^cite_note-20] Following a period of diminished importance during the early stages of the [emiral period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) already in force since before the conquest, Málaga was fully [Islamised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam) by the end of the aforementioned period in the wake of [Muhammad I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_I_of_C%C3%B3rdoba)'s attributed intervention in the urban configuration as a *[medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina_quarter)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERuiz_Povedano201743–44-21]



A 19th-century reconstruction of Islamic Malaqah

The consolidation of the city's importance after 930 (under the [Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba)) ran parallel to the diminishing fortune of [Archidona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archidona), the latter of which Málaga replaced as the capital of the corresponding *kura* of Rayya.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] The early 10th-century chronicle of [Aḥmad al-Rāzī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%E1%B8%A5mad_ibn_Mu%E1%B8%A5ammad_ibn_M%C5%ABsa_al-R%C4%81z%C4%AB) mentions the vineyards of Málaga, extolling the unparalleled quality of its [raisins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin).[^cite_note-24] In the 11th century, following the unravelling of Umayyad authority across the caliphate, Málaga became a centre of power of the [Hammudids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammudid_dynasty), who established a petty kingdom (nominally also a caliphate) in the city, the [taifa of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_M%C3%A1laga), complemented by the also Hammudid [sister dominion in Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Ceuta) across the Strait of Gibraltar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalero_Secall1993–199448-25] The city was seized away from the Hammudids by the [Granadan Zirids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirid_dynasty) in 1056 or 1057, and also underwent an ephemeral spell under the [Sevillian Abbadids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbadid_dynasty) by 1066 before returning to the former.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERuiz_Povedano201746–47-26] By the late 11th century, the Zirids lost the city to the North African [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravids).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERuiz_Povedano201746–47-26]

See also: [Taifa of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_M%C3%A1laga)

The traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta), who passed through around 1325, characterised it as "one of the largest and most beautiful towns of Andalusia \[uniting] the conveniences of both sea and land, and... abundantly supplied with foodstuffs and fruits". He praised its grapes, figs, and almonds; "its ruby-coloured Murcian [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate) have no equal in the world." Another exported product was its "excellent gilded pottery". The town's mosque was large, with "exceptionally tall orange trees" in its courtyard.[^cite_note-27]



Ceramic plate from Málaga (14th century)

After the formation of the [Nasrid Kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrid_Kingdom_of_Granada) in the 13th century, Málaga became a part of it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorres_Balbás1974329-28] The export-oriented harbour traded silk fabrics, [dry nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\)) (raisins, almonds and the famous Rayya figs, reportedly exported to as far as China), wine, cutlery, leather and the famous regional [lustreware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lustreware).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorres_Balbás1974329-28]

In the 15th century, Málaga was the main Nasrid port (followed by Almería),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFábregas_García2003–200479,_89-29] featuring a notable presence of [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) merchants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFábregas_García2003–200479-30] It played a role both as stopover of the Atlantic international trade (as part of the routes connecting the Central Mediterranean to the North Atlantic) and as regional trading cog of the Kingdom of Granada.[^cite_note-31] By the last rales of Nasrid rule, the city had a population of about 15,000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Ruiz201883-32]

Málaga was seized by Christian forces on 18 August 1487,[^cite_note-33] after a 3-month 11 days siege,[^cite_note-34] in what was the most violent episode of the [Granada War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_War). The Muslim inhabitants resisted assaults and artillery bombardments before hunger forced them to surrender; practically the entire remaining population (around 11,000 people) became war captives and were sold into slavery in other Andalusian cities as well as Valencia and Barcelona.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The Jewish population was also taken prisoner and held in [Carmona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmona,_Spain), and a ransom was imposed on them, partly covered by their own assets and partly collected through emissaries.[^cite_note-\:02-37] Only a minority of around 50 people led by merchant Alí Dordux were allowed to remain in the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Ruiz201877-38]

See also: [Siege of Málaga (1487)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_M%C3%A1laga_\(1487\))

The city was swiftly repopulated by Christian settlers coming from different locations of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Ruiz201877-38] Málaga became an exporting centre for Andalusia via the link of the city with Antequera and Córdoba, maintaining its trading character despite the nearly complete replacement of the population.[^cite_note-39] The city did not escape a series of [typhus fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus_fever) outbreaks following its annexation to the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile).[^cite_note-40]

Following the death of regent [Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon) the city rose in revolt in 1516 on the occasion of the installment of a new court controlled by the Admiral of Castile.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReder_Gadow2017323–334-41] It was only on 2 December 1530 when Málaga was freed from the influence of the Admiralty for good, confirming the privileges granted in the past by the Catholic Monarchs.[^cite_note-42]



Málaga in 1572: Castle of [Gibralfaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro) (center)

As of 1625, Málaga may have had a population of around 36,000.[^cite_note-43]

On 24 August 1704 the indecisive [Battle of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_M%C3%A1laga_\(1704\)), the largest naval battle in the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession), took place in the sea south of Málaga.[^cite_note-44]



Street fighting in Málaga during the [Glorious Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution_\(Spain\)) in 1868

The city's economy profited from an early industrialisation in the first third of the 19th century and the population steadily increased until the last years of the century,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIbáñez_Linares2018232-45] when the population decreased between 1887 and 1897 due to [the economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylloxera_plague_in_M%C3%A1laga?action=edit\&redlink=1) induced by the [Phylloxera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylloxera) grapevine pest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIbáñez_Linares2018233-46] The century saw the [accumulation of capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_accumulation) in an enriched bourgeoisie class, that invested in the incipient industrial development.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOrdóñez_Vergara1993163-47]

The municipality of Málaga annexed the coastal town of [Torremolinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torremolinos) in 1924.



Republican refugees fleeing Málaga during the *Desbandá* (8 February 1937).

After the [coup of July 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_coup_of_July_1936) the government of the [Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) retained control of Málaga. Its harbour was a base of the [Republican navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_Navy) at the beginning of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War). It suffered heavy bombing by [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_under_Fascism_\(1922%E2%80%931943\)) [warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_navy) which took part in breaking the Republican navy's [blockade](293054.md) of Nationalist-held [Spanish Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Morocco) and took part in naval bombardment of Republican-held Málaga.[^cite_note-48] After the Battle of Málaga and the [Francoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist) takeover in February 1937, over seven thousand people were killed,[^cite_note-49] as they were trying to flee the city through the road to [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almer%C3%ADa).[^cite_note-50]

See also: [Málaga–Almería road massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga%E2%80%93Almer%C3%ADa_road_massacre)

Torremolinos—originally a small coastal town—greatly developed in the late 1950s and early 1960s, becoming an international tourist centre.[^cite_note-51] The first gay bar in Spain was opened in Torremolinos in 1962 (and the first lesbian club in 1968),[^cite_note-gomez-52] and the place acquired a lively LGBT life, to the point of being described as "the most 'cosmopolitan' and gay-friendly place in all of Spain".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFernández_Galeano201610-53] Nearly a decade after, in 1971, a police crackdown seeking to curb "offences against public morality and decency" largely put an end to the appeal of the place, only regaining its status as hub of LGBT leisure and tourism after the death of the dictator.[^cite_note-gomez-52]

Torremolinos became independent from the municipality of Málaga in September 1988.[^cite_note-54]

## Geography



Satellite view centered on Málaga

Málaga is located in the south of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), on the [Costa del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_del_Sol) (*Coast of the Sun*) on the northern side of the [Alboran Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alboran_Sea) (the westernmost portion of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md)). It lies about 100 kilometres (62 miles) east of the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) and about 130 kilometres (81 miles) east of [Tarifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarifa) (the southernmost point of continental Europe) and about 130 km (81 miles) to the north of Africa.

The [Montes de Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montes_de_M%C3%A1laga) mountain range (part of the [Penibaetic System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penibaetic_System)) is located in the northeast of the municipality. The highest point in the range (and in the municipality) is the *Pico Reina*, rising up to 1,031 m (3,383 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-55]

The city centre is located around the mouth of the [Guadalmedina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalmedina) and close to the [Guadalhorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalhorce)'s mouth (where the airport is located). The Totalán Creek constitutes the eastern boundary of Málaga with the municipality of [Rincón de la Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinc%C3%B3n_de_la_Victoria).[^cite_note-56]

The [Gibralfaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro) is a 130 m (427 ft) high foothill from which the [Gibralfaro Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro_Castle) and the [Alcazaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba_de_M%C3%A1laga) fortress overlook the city.[^cite_note-57]

### Climate

Málaga's climate is a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate)[^cite_note-58] ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Csal*)[^cite_note-59] with mild winters, during which most of the year's rainfall occurs, and hot summers with very little rainfall. Summer to early-autumn tends to be moderately humid, due to the evaporation of warm water off the adjacent [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) being blown on shore by a [sea breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_breeze). This humidity is most pronounced at this time of year as the sea water is at its warmest in relation to the rest of the year. On particularly humid days, which occasionally occurs, the [apparent temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_temperature) feels higher than the actual temperature would suggest, especially when the wind is light. When the wind is stronger, this effect is lessened somewhat, and the heat feels more manageable.[^cite_note-malaga.com/geo-60] Málaga enjoys plenty of sunshine throughout the year, with an average of about 300 days of sunshine and only about 40–45 with precipitation annually.[^cite_note-61]

Málaga experiences the warmest winters of any European city with a population over 500,000. The average maximum temperature during the day in the period from December to February is 17–18 °C (63–64 °F). During the winter, the [Málaga Mountains (*Montes de Málaga*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montes_de_M%C3%A1laga) block the passage of cold winds from the north.[^cite_note-malaga.com/geo-60] Its average annual temperature is 23.6 °C (74.5 °F) during the day and 14.2 °C (57.6 °F) at night. In the coldest month, January, the temperature ranges from 14 to 20 °C (57 to 68 °F) during the day, 5 to 10 °C (41 to 50 °F) at night and the average sea temperature is 16 °C (61 °F). In the warmest month, August, the temperature ranges from 26 to 34 °C (79 to 93 °F) during the day, above 20 °C (68 °F) at night and the average sea temperature is 23 °C (73 °F).[^cite_note-62]

Large fluctuations in temperature are rare. The highest temperature ever recorded at the airport was 44.2 °C (111.6 °F) on 18 July 1978, equalled on 19 July 2023. In August 1881, the average reported daytime maximum temperature was a record 34.8 °C (94.6 °F). The lowest temperature ever recorded was −3.8 °C (25.2 °F) on 4 February 1954.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] The highest wind speed ever recorded was on 16 July 1980, measuring 119 km/h (73.94 mph). Snowfall is virtually unknown; since the beginning of the 20th century, Málaga city has only recorded snow on one day, on 2 February 1954.[^cite_note-65]

Annual average [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) is 65%, ranging from 58% in June to 72% in December.[^cite_note-aemet.es-66] Yearly sunshine hours average between 2,800 and 3,000 per year, from 5–6 hours of sunshine per day in December to average 11 hours of sunshine per day in July.[^cite_note-aemet.es-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

At [Málaga Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Airport) [weather station](206018.md), annual wind speeds average from 14 km/h (8.70 mph) in December, January and February, to 10 km/h (6.21 mph) in September and October. Atmospheric pressure averages from 1015 mbar in July and August to 1023 mbar in January. Visibility averages either 11 or 12 km in all months.[^cite_note-69] The strongest gust of wind recorded at this station was 130 km/h (80.78 mph) on 27 January 1948 at 02\:30.[^cite_note-70] On 12 December 2023, Málaga broke [Spain](26667.md)'s and [Europe](9239.md)'s all-time December temperature record, setting a new record of 29.9 °C (85.8 °F) at the [AEMET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEMET) station of Málaga and 29.6 °C (85.3 °F) at the airport of Málaga.[^cite_note-71]

## Main sites



View of the old [Alcazaba of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba_\(M%C3%A1laga\))



The [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Cathedral) of the Incarnation



The Concepción viewpoint



The historic [Anglican Cemetery of St. George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Cemetery,_M%C3%A1laga) is the oldest non-Roman Catholic Christian cemetery established on mainland Spain (in 1831).

The old historic centre of Málaga reaches the harbour to the south. In the north it is surrounded by mountains, the [Montes de Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montes_de_M%C3%A1laga) (part of the [Baetic Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baetic_Cordillera)) lying in the southern base of the Axarquía hills, and two rivers, the [Guadalmedina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalmedina) – the historic center is located on its left bank – and the [Guadalhorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalhorce), which flows west of the city into the Mediterranean, in the Churriana district.

The oldest architectural remains in the city are the walls of the Phoenician city, which are visible in the cellar of the [Museo Picasso Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Picasso_M%C3%A1laga).

The Roman theatre of Málaga, which dates from the 1st century BC, was rediscovered in 1951.[^cite_note-81]

The Moors left posterity the dominating presence of the [Castle of Gibralfaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro), which is connected to the [Alcazaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba_of_M%C3%A1laga), the lower fortress and royal residence. Both were built during the [Taifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_M%C3%A1laga) period (11th century) and extended during the [Nasrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrid_dynasty) period (13th and 14th centuries). The Alcazaba stands on a hill within the city. Originally, it defended the city from the incursions of pirates. Later, in the 11th century, it was completely rebuilt by the [Hammudid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammudid_dynasty).[^cite_note-82] Occupying the eastern hillside that rises from the sea and overlooks the city, the Alcazaba was surrounded by palms and pine trees.

Like many of the military fortifications that were constructed in Islamic Spain, the Alcazaba of Málaga featured a quadrangular plan. It was protected by an outer and inner wall, both supported by rectangular towers, between which a covered walkway led up the slope to the Gibralfaro (this was the only exchange between the two sites). Due to its rough and awkward hillside topography, corridors throughout the site provided a means of communications for administrative and defensive operations, also affording privacy to the palatial residential quarters.

The entrance of the complex featured a grand tower that led into a sophisticated double bent entrance. After passing through several gates, open yards with gardens of pine and eucalyptus trees, and the inner wall through the Puerta de Granada, one finds the 11th- and 14th-century Governor's palace. It was organised around a central rectangular courtyard with a triple-arched gateway and some of the rooms have been preserved to this day. An open 11th-century mirador (belvedere) to the south of this area affords views of the gardens and sea below. Measuring 2.5 square metres (27 square feet), this small structure highlighted scalloped, five-lobed arches. To the north of this area were a waterwheel and a Cyclopean well (penetrating forty metres or 130 feet below ground), a hammam, workshops and the monumental *Puerta de la Torre del Homenaje*, the northernmost point of the inner walls. Directly beyond was the passage to the Gibralfaro above.

The [Church of Santiago](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia%20de%20Santiago%20Ap%C3%B3stol%20\(M%C3%A1laga\)) ([Saint James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee)) is an example of Gothic vernacular [Mudéjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud%C3%A9jar), the hybrid style that evolved after the [Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista) incorporating elements from both Christian and Islamic tradition. Also from the period is the *Iglesia del Sagrario*, which was built on the site of the old mosque immediately after the city fell to Christian troops. It boasts a richly ornamented portal in the Isabeline-Gothic style, unique in the city.[^cite_note-83]

The [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Cathedral) and the [Episcopal Palace](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio%20Episcopal%20de%20M%C3%A1laga) were planned with Renaissance architectural ideals but there was a shortfall of building funds and they were finished in Baroque style.

The *[Basílica y Real Santuario de Santa María de la Victoria](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santuario%20de%20la%20Victoria%20\(M%C3%A1laga\))*, built in the late 17th century, has a chapel in which the vertical volume is filled with elaborate Baroque plasterwork.[^cite_note-84]

## Politics and administration



[Málaga City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_City_Hall)

Málaga is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Spain), the basic local administrative division in Spain. The [Ayuntamiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayuntamiento_\(Spain\)) is the body charged with the municipal government and administration. The Plenary of the *ayuntamiento* is formed by 31 elected municipal councillors, who in turn invest the [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcalde). The last municipal election took place on 26 May 2019. The current mayor is [Francisco de la Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_la_Torre_\(politician\)) ([People's Party](404727.md)), who has won several mandates since becoming mayor in 2000.[^cite_note-92] The [city hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_City_Hall) is located at the [Casona del Parque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casona_del_Parque?action=edit\&redlink=1), a [Neo-Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Revival_architecture) building inaugurated in 1919.[^cite_note-93]

## Economy



Trade Fair and Congress in Málaga (*Palacio de Ferias y Congresos de Málaga*)

Málaga is the fourth-ranking city in [economic activity](27393.md) in Spain behind [Madrid](41188263.md), [Barcelona](4443.md) and [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Spain).[^cite_note-anuarieco.lacaixa.comunicacions.com-12]

See also: [Economy of Spain](27393.md)



Aerial view of the [Andalusia Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia_Technology_Park).

The most important business sectors in Málaga are tourism, construction and technology services, but other sectors such as transportation and [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) are beginning to expand. The [Andalusia Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia_Technology_Park) (PTA) (In Spanish, "*Parque Tecnológico de Andalucía*"), located in Málaga, has enjoyed significant growth since its inauguration in 1992 by the [King of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Spain). Málaga TechPark closed 2025 with 29,018 employees, an increase of 3.89% from the previous year, while companies and institutions based in the park recorded combined revenue of €4.896 billion.[^cite_note-94]

In line with the city's strategic plan, the campaign "Málaga: Open for Business" is directed towards the international promotion of the city on all levels but fundamentally on a business level. The campaign places a special emphasis on new technologies as well as innovation and research in order to promote the city as a reference and focal point for many global business initiatives and projects.[^cite_note-95]

Málaga is a city of commerce and tourism has been a growing source of revenue, driven by the presence of a major airport, the improvement of communications, and new infrastructure such as the AVE and the maritime station, and new cultural facilities such as the Picasso Museum, the Contemporary Art Centre and Trade Fair and Congress, which have drawn more tourists.[^cite_note-96]

The city hosts the International Association of Science and Technology Parks (IASP) (*Asociación Internacional de Parques Tecnológicos*), and a group of IT company executives and business leaders has launched an information sector initiative, Málaga Valley e-27, which seeks to make Málaga the Silicon Valley of Europe. Málaga has had strong growth in new technology industries, mainly located in the Technological Park of Andalusia, and in the construction sector. The city is home to the largest bank in Andalusia, [Unicaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicaja), and such local companies as *Mayoral*, *Charanga*, *Sando*, *Vera*, *Ubago*, *Isofoton*, *Tedial*, *Novasoft*, *Grupo Vértice* and *Almeida viajes*, and other multinationals such as [Fujitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujitsu) Spain, [Pernod Ricard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernod_Ricard) Spain, [Accenture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accenture), [Epcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epcos), [Oracle Corporation](22591.md), [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei) and [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahou-San_Miguel_Group).[^cite_note-97] In February 2021, [Google](1092923.md) decided to install a centre of excellence in [cybersecurity](7398.md) in the city, slated for a 2023 opening.[^cite_note-98] Also in 2021, [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone) chose Málaga for the installment of a research, development and innovation centre.[^cite_note-99]

## Culture

### Feasts and festivals

Holy Week

Main article: [Holy Week in Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_M%C3%A1laga)



*Nazarenos* during the 2016 [Holy week in Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_week_in_M%C3%A1laga).

Holy Week has been observed for five centuries in Málaga.[^cite_note-101] Processions start on Palm Sunday and continue until Easter Sunday. Images depicting scenes from the Passion are displayed on huge ornate *tronos* (floats or thrones), some weighing more than 5,000 kilograms (11,000 pounds). Famous is the royal [archbrotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confraternity) of Our-Lady of Hope *Nuestra Señora de la Esperanza*. They have more than 5,000 members and 600 [*nazarenos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareno_\(Holy_Week\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). These *tronos* highlight the processions that go through the streets led by penitents dressed in long robes, with [capirote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capirote), followed by women in black carrying candles. Drums and trumpets play music and occasionally someone spontaneously sings a mournful saeta dedicated to the floats as they make their way slowly round the streets. Some Holy Week *tronos* are so huge that they must be housed in places outside the churches, as they are taller than the entrance doors. Famous is the military procession of "[la legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Legion)" (Royal congregation of Mena) playing marches and singing their anthem (El Novio de la Muerte) during procession.

Feria de Agosto

Main article: [Feria de Agosto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feria_de_Agosto)



Children during the *[Feria de Agosto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feria_de_Agosto)*.

During the celebration of the *Feria de Málaga* in August, the streets are transformed into traditional symbols of Spanish culture and history, with sweet wine, tapas, and live flamenco shows. The day events consist of dancing, live music (such as [flamenco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamenco) or *[verdiales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdiales)*, traditional music from Málaga) and bullfights at *La Malagueta*, while the night fair is moved to the Recinto Ferial, consisting of restaurants, clubs, and an entire fair ground with rides and games.[^cite_note-102]

Málaga Film Festival

Main article: [Málaga Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Film_Festival)

The Málaga Film Festival (*Festival de Málaga Cine Español*; FMCE), dedicated exclusively to films produced in Spain, is one of the most important film festivals in the country. It is held annually during a week in March or April.

Other

The *Fiesta Mayor de Verdiales* takes place every year on 28 December during which Spain's April Fools' Day is celebrated.[^cite_note-Málaga_Festivals-103]

The *Fiestas de Carnaval*, in which people dress in all types of costumes, takes place prior to the holy 40 days of Lent every February. A contest is held in the *Teatro Cervantes* between groups of singers, quartets and choirs who compete in the singing of ironic songs about social and political issues. The Carnival takes to the streets of Málaga on the week before Ash Wednesday, ending on Malagueta beach with the [burial of the anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entierro_de_la_Sardina) (*entierro de la sardina*).[^cite_note-Málaga_Festivals-103]

### Gastronomy



[Espetos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espeto_\(food\))

The cuisine of Málaga and the wider Costa del Sol is known for its *[espetos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espeto_\(food\))*, fish[^cite_note-104] (most often [sardines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardines_as_food)) grilled over open fires in the [chiringuitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiringuito) located near the beaches.[^cite_note-105] The *espeto* has been proposed as a candidate for designation by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as an [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage).[^cite_note-106]

### Religion

Most of the population of Málaga professes [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Spain) as its religion, although not many are practising Catholics. [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Spain) also have a presence in Málaga: one of seven congregations of the [Reformed Churches in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Churches_in_Spain) is based in the city and is the only one that permits [paedocommunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paedocommunion).

[Islam](6037917.md) is represented by a growing number of [immigrants](4599312.md) and a mosque, while the [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism_in_Spain) (primarily [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi)) is represented by its synagogue and the Jewish Association.

### Tourism



Tourists near the Sagrario Church.

The city is an important tourist destination, known as "the capital of the [Costa del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_del_Sol)". An estimated 6 million tourists visit the city each year.[^cite_note-109] Tourists usually visit the birthplace of [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso) and the [Museo Picasso Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Picasso_M%C3%A1laga), the Carmen Thyssen Museum, the old town or the beaches. The Málaga harbour is also the second busiest cruise port of the Iberian Peninsula.[^cite_note-110]

A popular walk leads up the hill to the [Gibralfaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibralfaro) castle (a [Parador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradores)), offering panoramic views over the city. The castle is next to the [Alcazaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba), the old Muslim palace, which in turn is next to the inner city of Málaga. Other nearby attractions are the Roman Theatre, the old Jewish quarter (or [judería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_quarter_\(diaspora\))), the cathedral, and the Church of Santiago in [mudéjar style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud%C3%A9jar_style). A popular walk follows the *Paseo del Parque* (a promenade that runs alongside a grand park with many palm trees and statues) to the harbour, ending in *Calle Larios*, the main commercial street of the city. There is also a curious museum, the Museum of the Holy Week (Museo de Arte Cofrade), which includes an impressive display of Baroque ecclesiastical items.[^cite_note-111]

## Education

### Bilingual education in schools

Since the launch of the 'Plan de Fomento del Plurilingüismo' in 2005, 169 schools in Málaga have included bilingual education in their programmes.[^cite_note-117] Although English is the most usual second language, many other primary and secondary schools in Málaga offer the choice of French, German, Arabic, Portuguese or Chinese. This first action has been followed by a second project run by the Junta de Andalucia. The so-called "Plan Estratégico de Desarrollo de las Lenguas en Andalucía" intends to provide pupils with a basic level (B1) of at least one foreign language.[^cite_note-118]

## Transport

### Airport

Main article: [Málaga Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Airport)



Málaga Airport

The city is served by [Málaga–Costa del Sol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga%E2%80%93Costa_del_Sol_Airport), located approximately 8 kilometres (5.0 mi) from the city centre. In 2025, the airport handled 26,760,549 passengers and 186,990 aircraft movements, both record annual totals, making it the fourth-busiest airport in Spain by passenger traffic.[^cite_note-127] It is the international airport of Andalusia, accounting for 85 percent of its international traffic. The airport, connected to the Costa del Sol, has a daily link with twenty cities in Spain and over a hundred cities in Europe (mainly in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), Central Europe and the Nordic countries but also the main cities of Eastern Europe: [Bucharest](36877.md), [Budapest](36787.md), [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) and [Warsaw](32908.md)), North Africa, Middle East ([Riyadh](51554.md), [Jeddah](277718.md) and [Kuwait](7515890.md)) and North America ([New York City](645042.md), [Toronto](64646.md) and [Montreal](7954681.md)).

The airport is connected to the city centre and surrounding areas through a transport hub, which includes the bus system and suburban trains[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] and car parks.

### Seaport

Main article: [Port of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_M%C3%A1laga)



Port of Málaga

The Port of Málaga is the city's seaport, operating continuously at least since 600 BC. In 2025, the Port of Málaga handled 5.6 million tonnes of total traffic, an increase of 24.3% from 2024 and a record for the port. The Málaga–Melilla regular passenger route carried 365,503 passengers, while cruise traffic reached 570,481 passengers on 331 ship calls.[^cite_note-131]

The port has a [ferry](50771.md) connection to the [Port of Melilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Melilla), playing a role in the so-called *[Operación paso del estrecho](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operaci%C3%B3n%20paso%20del%20estrecho)* ("Operation Pass of the Strait"), the planned seasonal transit of passengers during the summer months from Europe to North-Africa (and back to Europe).[^cite_note-132]

### Roads and highways

The [A45 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A45_road_\(Spain\)) leads north to [Antequera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antequera) and [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain). ### Public transportation



Málaga Metro

Urban bus

Empresa Malagueña de Transportes[^cite_note-133] buses are the main form of transport around the city.[^cite_note-134] Málaga's [bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station) is connected with the city by the bus line number 4, although it is only ten minutes' walk to the Alameda from there. In 2025, EMT buses carried 53,058,202 passengers, the highest annual total in the operator's history and a 4.83% increase from 2024.[^cite_note-135]

Metropolitan bus

The buses of the [Málaga Metropolitan Transport Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Metropolitan_Transport_Consortium) (*Consorcio de Transporte Metropolitano del Área de Málaga*)[^cite_note-136] are the main means of transportation around the city of Málaga and the surrounding municipalities.

Mass transit

The city has two commuter train lines [Cercanías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas_M%C3%A1laga) departing from the [Centro-Alameda station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Centro-Alameda_railway_station) and the [Málaga Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Metro), a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) system.[^cite_note-137] The city is served by two [Cercanías Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas_M%C3%A1laga) commuter rail lines and the [Málaga Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Metro), whose two lines extend 13.6 kilometres (8.5 mi) and serve 19 stations and stops. The Metro carried 19,222,912 passengers in 2025, a 5.3% increase from 2024.[^cite_note-138] The Cercanías Málaga network carried 15,542,731 passengers in 2025.[^cite_note-139]
