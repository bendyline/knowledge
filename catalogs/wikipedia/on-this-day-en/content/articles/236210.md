A **crown prince** or **hereditary prince** is the [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent) to the [throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throne) in a royal or imperial [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy).[^cite_note-1] The female form of the title, **crown princess**, is held by a woman who is heir apparent or is married to the heir apparent.[^cite_note-2]

*Crown prince* as a descriptive term has been used throughout history for the prince who is first-in-line to a throne and is expected to succeed (i.e. the heir apparent), barring any unforeseen future event preventing this. In certain monarchies, a more specific [substantive title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantive_title) may be accorded and become associated with the position of heir apparent (e.g. [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Prince of Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Asturias) in the [Kingdom of Spain](26667.md) and formerly the [Dauphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauphin_of_France) in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France)). In these monarchies, the term crown prince may be used less often than the substantive title (or never).

Until the late twentieth century, no modern monarchy adopted a system whereby women would be guaranteed to succeed to the throne (i.e. [absolute primogeniture](136535.md)). A crown princess would therefore be more likely to refer to the [spouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spouse) of a crown prince. She would be styled crown princess, not in her [own right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantive_title) but by [courtesy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtesy_title). Many European countries have now abolished male primogeniture; a notable exception is Spain.[^cite_note-3]

## Description

The term *crown prince* is not used in European monarchies if the hereditary sovereign holds a title below that of king/queen or emperor/empress (such as [grand duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_duke) or [prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince)), although it is sometimes used as a synonym for heir apparent.

In Europe, where [primogeniture](136535.md) governed succession to all monarchies except those of the [papacy](23056.md) and [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra), the eldest son or (more recently) eldest child of the current monarch fills the role of crown prince or princess, depending upon whether women of the [dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty) enjoy personal [succession rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_hereditary_succession). Male precedence has been abolished in [Belgium](3343.md), [Denmark](76972.md), [Luxembourg](17515.md), [Norway](21241.md), [Sweden](5058739.md) and the [Netherlands](21148.md), as well as in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and other [Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm) pursuant to the [Succession to the Crown Act 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_to_the_Crown_Act_2013). The eldest living child of a monarch is sometimes not the heir apparent or crown prince, because that position can be held by a descendant of a deceased older child who, by "right of representation", inherits the same place in the line of succession that would be held by the ancestor if he or she were still living (for example, [Carl Gustaf, Duke of Jämtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf) was the crown prince of Sweden from 1950 to 1973, as the senior grandson by male primogeniture of King [Gustaf VI Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf), although the former [Prince Sigvard, Duke of Uppland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigvard_Bernadotte) was Gustaf VI Adolf's eldest living son, and [Prince Bertil, Duke of Halland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Bertil,_Duke_of_Halland) his eldest living [dynastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty) son during those years).

In some monarchies, those of the [Middle East](19323.md) for example, in which primogeniture is not the decisive factor in dynastic succession, a person may not possess the title or status of crown prince by right of birth, but may obtain (and lose) it as a result of an official designation made on some other legal or traditional basis, such as former crown prince [Hassan bin Talal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Hassan_bin_Talal) of [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemite_Kingdom_of_Jordan).

Compare [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent) and [heir presumptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_presumptive). In Scandinavian kingdoms, the heir presumptive to the crown may hold a different title from an [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent): hereditary prince (German: *Erbprinz*, French: *prince héréditaire*). It is also the title borne by the heir apparent of [Liechtenstein](17810.md), as well as the heir apparent or presumptive of [Monaco](19261.md). In [Luxembourg](17515.md), the heir apparent bears the title of hereditary grand duke ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Erbgroßherzog*, [Luxembourgish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourgish): *ierfgroussherzog*); along with hereditary prince, it was also the title borne by the heirs apparent to the thrones of the grand duchies, sovereign [duchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke) and [principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince), and of [mediatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_mediatisation) princely families in the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) [monarchies abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolished_monarchies) in 1918.

- [Earl of Carrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Carrick) ([Kingdom of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland)), currently one of the titles of the Prince of Wales when in Scotland

Some monarchies have used (although not always *[de jure](43594.md)*) a territorial title for heirs apparent which, though often perceived as a crown princely title, is not automatically hereditary. It generally requires a specific conferral by the sovereign, which may be withheld.

Current and past titles in this category include:

- [Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar_\(title\)#Minor_dynastic_title) or Kaisar ([Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and early [Byzantine Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire)) in honor of [Gaius Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar), distinguished from the senior [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_\(title\))

- [Aetheling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetheling) ([Anglo-Saxon England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England)) and [edling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edling) ([Welsh kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_kingdoms)), lit. *of the royal family*

- [Duke of Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Estonia) and Lolland (Denmark; during, at least, reigns of [Christopher II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_II_of_Denmark) and [Valdemar IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_IV))

- [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales) and [Earl of Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Chester) (England, Great Britain, United Kingdom)

- [King of the Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Romans) ([Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire)) – an elective, rather than an inherited title, for the designated successor—usually the son, but sometimes the brother—of the Emperor

- [King of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Romans) ([First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire))

- [Duke of Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Sparta) ([Kingdom of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece_\(Gl%C3%BCcksburg\))); used briefly, *within* Greece, only by [Prince Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I_of_Greece), during the reign of his father, King [George I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_I_of_Greece)

- [Duke of Valentinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Valentinois), used by several heirs to the [Monégasque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Monaco) throne

- [Marquess of Baux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquess_of_Baux), used by several heirs to the [Monégasque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Monaco) throne

- [Prince of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Brazil) (title of the [Portuguese](23033.md) heir from 1645 to 1815)

- [Tupoutoʻa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupouto%CA%BBa) ([Tonga](30158.md))

- Duke of Scania (Sweden during the time when [Magnus IV of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_IV_of_Sweden) also was King of [Terra Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Scania))

- Prince of Ani ([Kingdom of West Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_West_Armenia))

- Grand Voivode of [Alba Iulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alba_Iulia) ([Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) from 1930 to 1940)

- Grand Voivode of Grahovo ([Kingdom of Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Montenegro))

- Prince of Venice (see [Prince Eugène de Beauharnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Beauharnais)); for the heir presumptive to [Napoleon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I) in his kingdom of Italy

- [Duke of Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Calabria) ([Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples) and [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies)); prior to the accession of [King Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert,_King_of_Naples) the title of the Neapolitan heir was [Prince of Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Princes_of_Salerno)

- Pangeran Adipati Anom ([House of Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Mataram))

## Other specific traditions

In Islamic tradition, the title is the Arabic term [Wali al-Ahd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali_al-Ahd).

- In Persia ([Iran](14653.md)), during the [Pahlavi dynasty](338883.md) and [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty), the full style was *Vala Hazrat-i-Humayun [Vali Ahd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vali_Ahd), [Shahzada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahzad_\(disambiguation\))* (given name) (meaning "His August Imperial Highness the Heir Apparent, Prince ...");

- The title was adopted by many [oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental) monarchies, even some non-Muslim, e.g. "Walet" as alternative title for the [Nepali](171166.md) (Hindu) royal heir apparent; first used by Crown Prince [ Trailokya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailokya,_Crown_Prince_of_Nepal) in the middle of the nineteenth century, taken from the Mughal title 'Vali Ahd'.

[Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) tradition (Indian subcontinent):

- [Yuvaraja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuvaraja) was part of the full title in many princely states of India, e.g. in [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)), the heir apparent was styled *Maharaj Kumar Shri Yuvaraj* (personal name) *Singhji Bahadur*

- [Nepal](171166.md), where the King was styled [Maharajadhiraja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharajadhiraja):&#x20;

  - the heir apparent was styled: *[Sri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri) Sri Sri Sri Sri* Yuvarajadhiraj ('Young King of Kings', i.e. Crown Prince) (personal name) *Bir Bikram Shah Deva*;

  - the eldest son of the heir apparent was styled: *Sri Sri Sri Sri Sri* Nava Yuvaraj ('Young Crown Prince') (personal name) *Bir Bikram Shah Deva*

East Asian traditions:

- The cognates of Chinese *Huang [Taizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taizi)* (皇太子, "Great Imperial Son") – if a son of the reigning emperor, and *Huang Taisun* (皇太孫, Great Imperial Grandson) – if a grandson of the emperor:

| *if the heir apparent is a:* | son | grandson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chinese | Huang [Taizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taizi) | Huang Taisun |
| Japanese | Kōtaishi | Kōtaison |
| Korean | Hwangtaeja (황태자) | Hwangtaeson (황태손) |
| Vietnamese | Hoàng Thái Tử | Hoàng Thái Tôn |

- The crown prince of an emperor was sometimes referred as Dong-gong (東宮, 'East Palace') due to the location of his residence from the main palace.

- If the crown prince is the son of a king, he was called 世子 (Shizi).

- The crown prince was not necessarily the first-born son.

- During the [Joseon dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon_dynasty) in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), the crown prince was often referred as Dong-gung (동궁, 東宮, 'East Palace') or wangseja (王世子 왕세자); The first-born son was called wonja (元子 원자).

- In Japan, the Imperial Crown Prince is the son or grandson of the current emperor and is referred to as kōtaishi or kōtaison respectively. As of 2025, Japan has a crown prince, [Prince Fumihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Fumihito) who holds the title of "kōshi" as he is not the imperial crown prince (due to him being the Emperor's brother). In the past, this title was gender neutral and has been conferred upon one woman, [Princess Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_K%C5%8Dken).

Southeast Asian traditions:&#x20;

- [Siam Makutrajakuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siam_Makutrajakuman) (สยามมกุฎราชกุมาร) in [Thailand](30128.md) since 1886.

- [Krom Phrarajawangboworn Sathanmongkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krom_Phrarajawangboworn_Sathanmongkol) or Phra Maha [Uparaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uparaja) or commonly called Wang Na (or [Front Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_Palace)) in [Thailand](30128.md) prior to 1886.

- Kanjeng Gusti Pangeran Adipati Anom in [Surakarta sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta), [Indonesia](14579.md).

- Raja Muda or Tengku Mahkota in the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_people) sultanates of [Malaysia](3607937.md).

- Pengiran Muda Mahkota in [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei)

Equivalents in other cultures:

- [Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguars_in_Mesoamerican_culture) Prince ([Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica))

- Ka Haku O Hawaiʻi or "The Lord of Hawaii" in the [Hawaiian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_language).

- Aremo, "First Son and Heir" in the [Yoruba language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language) of [West Africa](67393.md), used as a royal title in many of the kingdoms of the region.

- [Lee Jae-yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jae-yong_\(businessman\)), South Korean billionaire and Chairman of [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung) referred to as the "Crown Prince of Samsung"
