**Titanosaurs** (or titanosaurians; members of the group **Titanosauria**) were a diverse group of [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda) dinosaurs, including genera from every continent. The titanosaurs were the last surviving group of long-necked sauropods, with taxa still thriving at the time of the [extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous%E2%80%93Paleogene_extinction_event) at the end of the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous). This group includes some of the [largest land animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_land_animal) known to have ever existed, such as *[Patagotitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagotitan)*, estimated at 37 m (121 ft) long[^cite_note-15] with a mass of 69 tonnes (76 tons),[^cite_note-Carballido2017-16] and the comparably sized *[Argentinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentinosaurus)* and *[Puertasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puertasaurus)* from the [same region](65433.md).

The group's name alludes to the mythological [Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_\(mythology\)) of ancient [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), via the type [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) (now considered a *[nomen dubium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomen_dubium))* *[Titanosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosaurus)*. Together with the [brachiosaurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosauridae) and relatives, titanosaurs make up the larger sauropod [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade) [Titanosauriformes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauriformes). Titanosaurs have long been a poorly known group, and the relationships among titanosaur species are still not well understood.

## Fossil record



Excavation of titanosaur fossils at the Lo Hueco fossil site in Spain

Due to the near-global distribution of titanosaurs during the Cretaceous, titanosaur fossils have been found on every continent, including Antarctica.[^cite_note-Cerda2011-17] However, titanosaurs have the least complete fossil record of any major sauropodomorph group.[^cite_note-Cashmore2020-18] No complete titanosaur skeletons are known, and many species are only known from a few bones. Titanosaur skulls are especially rare. Though fragmentary cranial remains are known for several titanosaur genera, nearly complete skulls have been described for only four: *[Nemegtosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemegtosaurus)*, *[Rapetosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapetosaurus)*, *[Sarmientosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmientosaurus)*, and *[Tapuiasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapuiasaurus)*.[^cite_note-Martínez2016-19] As is the case in most other sauropod groups, there are few titanosaur specimens with complete necks preserving all of the [cervical vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_vertebrae) in sequence.&#x20;

Only three complete titanosaur necks are known: the holotype of *[Futalognkosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futalognkosaurus)* and two undescribed specimens from Argentina. A fourth specimen, of an unidentified titanosaur from Brazil, preserves a nearly complete neck, with only the [atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\(anatomy\)), the tiny vertebra forming the joint between the skull and neck, missing.[^cite_note-Taylor2022-20] Only five titanosaur specimens preserve complete, articulated hind feet.[^cite_note-GonzálezRiga2022-21] This incompleteness is especially significant for giant titanosaurs, which are generally known from disarticulated and fragmentary remains.[^cite_note-GonzálezRiga2022-21]

Titanosaurs are one of the few groups of dinosaurs for which fossil eggs are known.[^cite_note-Norell2020-22] The fossil site of Auca Mahuevo preserves a titanosaur nesting ground. Some titanosaur eggs have been found containing fossil [embryos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryos), which even preserve fossil skin.[^cite_note-CoriaChiappe2007-23] These fossil embryos are among the few titanosaur specimens to preserve complete skulls.[^cite_note-Kundrát2020-24]

## Description

Titanosauria have the largest range of body size of any sauropod clade, and includes both the largest known sauropods and some of the smallest.[^cite_note-Wilson2006-25] One of the largest titanosaurs, *[Patagotitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagotitan)*, had a body mass estimated to be 69 tonnes (76 tons), whereas one of the smallest, *[Magyarosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyarosaurus)*, had a body mass of approximately 900 kilograms (2,000 lb).[^cite_note-Carballido2017-16][^cite_note-Stein2010-26] Even relatively closely related titanosaurs could have very different body sizes, as the small [rinconsaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinconsauria) were closely related to the gigantic [lognkosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lognkosauria).[^cite_note-Carballido2017-16] Fossils from perhaps the largest dinosaur ever found were discovered in 2021 in the Neuquén Province of northwest Patagonia, Argentina. It is believed that they are from a titanosaur.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Some of the smallest titanosaurs, such as *[Magyarosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyarosaurus)*, inhabited Europe, which was largely made up of islands during the Cretaceous, and were likely island dwarfs. Another taxon of tiny titanosaurs, *[Ibirania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibirania)*, lived a non-insular context in Upper Cretaceous Brazil, and is an example of [nanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarfism) resultant from other ecological pressures.[^cite_note-29]

### Head and neck



Unnamed titanosaur from Japan labelled "[Xinghesaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_informally_named_dinosaurs#Xinghesaurus)"

The heads of titanosaurs are poorly known. However, several different cranial morphologies are apparent. In some species, such as *[Sarmientosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmientosaurus)*, the head resembled that of [brachiosaurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosauridae).[^cite_note-Martínez2016-19] In others, such as *[Rapetosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapetosaurus)* and *[Nemegtosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemegtosaurus)*, the head resembled that of [diplodocids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplodocidae). In some titanosaurs, the skull was especially diplodocid-like due to square-shaped jaws;[^cite_note-apesteguia2004-30] the titanosaur *[Antarctosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctosaurus)* is especially similar to the [rebbachisaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebbachisauridae) *[Nigersaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigersaurus)*.[^cite_note-wilson02-31] Titanosaurs had small heads, even when compared with other sauropods. The head was also wide, similar to the heads of *[Camarasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarasaurus)* and *[Brachiosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosaurus),* though somewhat more elongated. Titanosaurian nostrils were large ("[macronarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macronaria)") and all had crests formed by the nasal bones. Their teeth were either somewhat spatulate (spoon-like) or like pegs or pencils, but were always very small.

Titanosaur necks were of average length for sauropods, and their [tails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail) were whip-like though not as long as in the [diplodocids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplodocid). While the [pelvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvis) was slimmer than some sauropods, the pectoral (chest) area was much wider, giving them a uniquely "wide-legged" stance. As a result, the [fossilized trackways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichnite) of titanosaurs are distinctly broader than those of other sauropods. Their forelimbs were also stocky, and often longer than their hind limbs. Unlike other sauropods, some titanosaurs had no digits, walking only on horseshoe-shaped "stumps" made up of the columnar metacarpal bones.[^cite_note-apesteguia2005-32][^cite_note-dayetal2004-33] Their [vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebra) (back bones) were solid (not hollowed-out), which may be a reversal to more basal [saurischian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurischia) characteristics. Their spinal column was relatively flexible, likely making them more agile than other sauropods, though at the expense of rearing on their hind legs compared to the Diplodocoids. One of the most characteristic features shared by most titanosaurs were their procoelous caudal vertebrae, with ball-and-socket articulations between the vertebral centra.

### Torso and limbs

Photo of a Futalognkosaurus hand

Photo of a Futalognkosaurus foot

Hand (left) and hindfoot (right) of a typical titanosaur

The [dorsal vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsal_vertebrae) of titanosaurs show multiple derived features among sauropods. Similarly to the Rebbachisauridae, titanosaurs lost the [hyposphene-hypantrum articulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyposphene-hypantrum_articulation), a set of surfaces between vertebrae that prevent additional rotation of the bones. *[Andesaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesaurus)*, one of the most basal titanosaurs, shows a normal hyposphene. The same area is reduced in *[Argentinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentinosaurus)* to only two ridges, and is fully absent in taxa like *[Opisthocoelicaudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthocoelicaudia)* and *[Saltasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurus)*. Both *Argentinosaurus* and *[Epachthosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epachthosaurus)* bear similar intermediate "hyposphenal ridges", which suggests they represent a more primitive form of dorsal vertebrae.[^cite_note-apesteguia05-34]

Sauropod hands already are highly derived from other dinosaurs, being reduced into columnar [metacarpals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacarpal) and blocky [phalanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanges) with fewer claws. However, titanosaurs evolved the manus even further, completely losing the phalanges and heavily modifying the metacarpals. *[Argyrosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyrosaurus)* is the only titanosaur with preserved [carpals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpal). Other taxa like *Epachthosaurus* show a reduction of phalanges to one or two bones. *Opisthocoelicaudia* shows even more reduction of the hand than other titanosaurs, with both carpals and phalanges completely absent.[^cite_note-apesteguia05b-35] However, *[Diamantinasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamantinasaurus)*, while lacking carpals, preserves a manual formula of 2–1–1–1–1, including a thumb claw and phalanges on all other digits. This, coupled with the preservation of a single phalanx on digit IV of *Epachthosaurus* and potentially *Opisthocoelicaudia* (further study is necessary), show that preservation biases may be responsible for the lack of hand phalanges in these taxa. This suggests that *Alamosaurus*, *[Neuquensaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuquensaurus)*, *Saltasaurus* and *Rapetosaurus -* all known from imperfect or disarticulated remains previously associated with a lack of phalanges - may have had phalanges but lost them after death.[^cite_note-poropat14-36]

Titanosaurs have a poor fossil record of their [pedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot) (feet), only being complete in five definitive titanosaurs. Among these, *[Notocolossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notocolossus)* is the largest, and also has the most specialized pes: like all titanosaurs, its pes is composed of short, thick [metatarsals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metatarsal) of approximately the same lengths; however, metatarsals I and V are notably more robust than in other taxa.[^cite_note-gonzalez16-37]

### Integument



*Ampelosaurus*, a titanosaur with osteoderms, depicted with the osteoderms arranged in a pair of rows

From skin impressions found with [fossils](10958.md), it has been determined that the skin of many titanosaurs was [armored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour_\(zoology\)) with a small mosaic of small, bead-like [scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_\(zoology\)) surrounding larger scales.[^cite_note-CoriaChiappe2007-23] While most titanosaurs were very large animals, many were fairly average in size compared to other giant dinosaurs. Some island-dwelling dwarf titanosaurs, such as *[Magyarosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyarosaurus)*, were probably the result of [allopatric speciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopatric_speciation) and [insular dwarfism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_dwarfism).

Some titanosaurs had [osteoderms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoderm). Osteoderms were first confirmed in the genus *[Saltasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurus)* but are now known to have been present in a variety of titanosaurs within the clade Lithostrotia.[^cite_note-Demic2012-38] The exact arrangement of osteoderms on the body of a titanosaur is not known, but some paleontologists consider it likely that the osteoderms were arranged in two parallel rows on the animal's back, an arrangement similar to the plates of [stegosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stegosaurs).[^cite_note-Vidal2014-39] Several other arrangements have been proposed, such as a single row along the midline, and it is possible that different species had different arrangements. The osteoderms were certainly far more sparse than those of [ankylosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankylosaurs), and did not completely cover the back in scutes. Because of their sparse arrangement, it was unlikely that they served a significant role in defense. However, they may have played an important role in nutrient storage for titanosaurs living in highly seasonal climates and for female titanosaurs laying eggs.[^cite_note-CurryRogers2011-40][^cite_note-Vidal2017-41] Osteoderms were present on both large and small species, so they were not solely used by smaller species as protection against predators.[^cite_note-Carrano2015-42] New evidence published in 2021 suggests there were indeed some defensive purposes in titanosaur osteoderms; simulated bite marks from both baurusuchid crocodylomorphs and abelisaurids on titanosaurid osteoderms suggest they could be useful for protecting the animals as well as functioning in mineral storage.[^cite_note-43]

Titanosaurs are classified as [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda) [dinosaurs](8311.md). This highly diverse group forms the dominant clade of Cretaceous sauropods.[^cite_note-GonzalezRiga2019-45] Within Sauropoda, titanosaurs were once classified as close relatives of [Diplodocidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplodocidae) due to their shared characteristic of narrow teeth, but this is now known to be the result of convergent evolution.[^cite_note-salgado97-46] Titanosaurs are now known to be most closely related to [euhelopodids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhelopodidae) and [brachiosaurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosauridae); together they form a clade named Titanosauriformes.[^cite_note-mannion13-47]

For much of the 20th century, most known species of titanosaurs were classified in the family Titanosauridae, which is no longer in widespread use.[^cite_note-wilson03-11] Titanosauria was first proposed in 1993 as a taxon to encompass titanosaurids and their close relatives.[^cite_note-bonaparte93-48] It has been phylogenetically defined as the clade composed of the most recent common ancestor of *[Saltasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurus)* and *[Andesaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesaurus)* and all of its descendants.[^cite_note-salgado97-46][^cite_note-wilson03-11][^cite_note-curryrogers05-49][^cite_note-calvo07a-50][^cite_note-Demic2012-38][^cite_note-mannion13-47] The relationships of species within Titanosauria remain largely unresolved, and it is considered one of the most poorly-understood areas of dinosaur classification. One of the few areas of agreement is that the majority of titanosaurs except *Andesaurus* and some other basal species form a clade called [Lithostrotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithostrotia), which some researchers consider equivalent to the deprecated Titanosauridae.[^cite_note-wilson03-11][^cite_note-upchurch04-51][^cite_note-calvo07a-50] Lithostrotians include titanosaurs such as *Alamosaurus*, *Isisaurus*, *Malawisaurus*, *Rapetosaurus*, and *Saltasaurus*.[^cite_note-upchurch04-51]

### Early history



[Lectotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lectotype) of *[Titanosaurus indicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosaurus_indicus)*, the name-bearing genus of Titanosauria

*[Titanosaurus indicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosaurus_indicus)* was first named by British [paleontologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontologist) [Richard Lydekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lydekker) in [1877](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1877_in_paleontology), as a new taxon of dinosaur based on two caudals and a femur collected on different occasions at the same location in [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-lydekker77-52] While it was later given a position as a sauropod within [Cetiosauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetiosauridae) by Lydekker in [1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1888_in_paleontology),[^cite_note-lydekker88-53] he named the new [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropod) family [Titanosauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauridae) for the genus in [1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_in_paleontology), which included only *Titanosaurus* and *[Argyrosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyrosaurus)*, united by [procoelous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#centrum) caudals, [opisthocoelous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#centrum) presacrals, a lack of pleurocoels and open chevrons.[^cite_note-lydekker93-54] Following this, Austro-Hungarian paleontologist [Franz Nopcsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Nopcsa) reviewed reptile genera in [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_in_paleontology), and provided a short classification of Sauropoda, where he placed the [Titanosaurinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosaurinae) (a reranking of Lydekker's Titanosauridae) in [Morosauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morosauridae), and included the genera *Titanosaurus*, *[Hypselosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypselosaurus)* and *[Macrurosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrurosaurus)* because they all had strongly procoelous caudals.[^cite_note-nopcsa28-55]

German paleontologist [Friedrich von Huene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_von_Huene) provided a significant revision of Titanosauridae the following year in [1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_in_paleontology), where he reviewed the dinosaurs of [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) [Argentina](18951905.md), and named multiple new genera. Huene included multiple species of *Titanosaurus* from India, [England](9316.md), [France](5843419.md), [Romania](25445.md), [Madagascar](18964.md) and Argentina, *Hypselosaurus* and *[Aepisaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aepisaurus)* from France, *Macrurosaurus* from England, *[Alamosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamosaurus)* from [United States](3434750.md), and *Argyrosaurus*, *[Antarctosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctosaurus)*, and *[Laplatasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplatasaurus)* from Argentina. The material between them represented almost all regions of the skeleton, which showed they were derived sauropods Huene interpreted as closest to *[Pleurocoelus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleurocoelus)* of the various non-titanosaurid genera.[^cite_note-huene29-56]



Skeletal mount of *[Neuquensaurus australis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuquensaurus_australis)*

For his [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_in_paleontology) thesis, Argentinian paleontologist [Jaime Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Powell) described and classified many new genera of South American titanosaurs. Using the family Titanosauridae to include them all, he grouped the genera into Titanosaurinae, [Saltasaurinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurinae), [Antarctosaurinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctosaurinae?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Argyrosaurinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyrosaurinae?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Titanosauridae indet. Titanosaurinae included *Titanosaurus* and the new genus *[Aeolosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolosaurus)*, united by multiple features of the caudal vertebrae; the new clade Saltasaurinae was created to include *[Saltasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurus)* and the new genus *[Neuquensaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuquensaurus)*, united by very distinct dorsals, caudals, and ilia; the new clade Antarctosaurinae was created to include *Antarctosaurus*, distinguished by large size, a different form of [braincase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braincase), more elongate girdle bones, and more robust limb bones; and Argyrosaurinae was created for *Argyrosaurus*, bearing a more robust forelimb and hand and more primitive dorsals. The new genus *[Epachthosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epachthosaurus)* was named for a more basal titanosaurid classified as Titanosauridae indet al.ong with unnamed specimens, *[Clasmodosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clasmodosaurus)* and *[Campylodoniscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campylodoniscus)*.[^cite_note-powell86-57]

[John Stanton McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stanton_McIntosh) provided a synopsis of sauropod relationships in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_in_paleontology), using Titanosauridae as the group to contain all taxa like previous authors. *Opisthocoelicaudia* was placed in [Opisthocoelicaudiinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthocoelicaudiinae) within [Camarasauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarasauridae), following its original description and not later works, and *[Nemegtosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemegtosaurus)* and *[Quaesitosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaesitosaurus)* were placed within [Dicraeosaurinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicraeosaurinae). Titanosauridae included many previously named genera, plus taxa like *[Tornieria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornieria)* and *[Janenschia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janenschia)*.[^cite_note-mcintosh90-58] *Saltasaurus* included the species previously known as *Titanosaurus australis* and *T. robustus*, which were named *Neuquensaurus* by Powell in 1986.[^cite_note-wilson03-11] McIntosh provided a large diagnosis of the family: "dorsals with irregularly shaped pleurocoels and spines directed strongly backward; transverse processes directed dorsally as well as laterally, very robust in shoulder region; a second dorsosacral, its rib fused to ilium; caudals strongly procoelous with a prominent ball on distal end of centrum throughout tail; caudal arches on front half of centrum; sternal plates large; preacetabular process of ilium swept outward to become almost horizontal", but stressed that the relationships of titanosaurids to other sauropod groups couldn't be determined due to a lack of cranial material.[^cite_note-mcintosh90-58]

A brief review of putative titanosaurids from Europe was authored by [Jean Le Loeuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Le_Loeuff?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_in_paleontology), and covered the supposed genera known so far. The [Barremian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barremian) (middle Early Cretaceous) species *Titanosaurus valdensis*, named decades previous by Huene, was kept as the oldest of the titanosaurid and given the new genus name *[Iuticosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iuticosaurus)*. The French taxon *Aepisaurus* was removed from the family and placed in undetermined Sauropoda. *Macrurosaurus* was considered a [chimaera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimaera) of titanosaurid and non-titanosaurid material because of the presence of both procoelous and [amphicoelous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#centrum) caudals. Huene's species *Titanosaurus lydekkeri* was left as a [nomen dubium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomen_dubium), but left within Titanosauridae. [Maastrichtian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastrichtian) fossils from France and [Spain](26667.md) were removed from *Hypselosaurus* and *Titanosaurus*, with *Hypselosaurus* being declared dubious like *T. lydekkeri*. The variety of Romanian fossils named as *Magyarosaurus* by Huene were also moved into the same species again, *M. dacus* as originally named by Nopcsa.[^cite_note-loeuff93-59]

### Titanosauria named



*Argentinosaurus* dorsal and sauropod paleontologist [Matt Wedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Wedel)

José Bonaparte and Rodolfo Coria in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_in_paleontology) concluded that a new clade of derived sauropods was necessary because *[Argentinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentinosaurus)*, *Andesaurus* and *Epachthosaurus* were distinct from Titanosauridae as they possessed [hyposphene-hypantrum articulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#hyposphene-hypantrum_articulations), but were still very closely related to the titanosaurids. The taxa that possessed the articulations were united within the new family [Andesauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesauridae), and the two families were grouped together within the new clade Titanosauria. The titanosaurs were diagnosed by possessing small [pleurocoels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#pleurocoel) centered within an anteroposteriorly elongate depression and the presence of two well defined depressions on the posterior face of the neural arch. The entire group was compared favourably with [cetiosaurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetiosauridae) like *[Patagosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagosaurus)* and *[Volkheimeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkheimeria)*.[^cite_note-bonaparte93-48]

Overlooking the naming of Titanosauria, [Paul Upchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Upchurch?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_in_paleontology) named the clade [Titanosauroidea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauroidea), to include *[Opisthocoelicaudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthocoelicaudia)* and the more derived Titanosauridae (*[Malawisaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawisaurus)*, *[Alamosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamosaurus)* and *[Saltasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurus)*). United by: caudals with anteriorly-shifted neural spines, extremely robust forearm bones, a prominent concavity on the [ulna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulna) for articulation with the humerus, a laterally flared and flattened [ilium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilium_\(bone\)), and a less robust pubis; Upchurch considered the clade sister taxon to [Diplodocoidea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplodocoidea), because of their shared dental anatomy, although he noted that peg-like teeth might have been independently evolved.[^cite_note-upchurch95-60] This was followed up by Upchurch's [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_in_paleontology) study on sauropod phylogenetics, which additionally recovered *[Phuwiangosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuwiangosaurus)* and *Andesaurus* within Titanosauroidea and resolved *Opisthocoelicaudia* as the sister of *Saltasaurus* instead of the most basal titanosauroid. This result places Titanosauroidea in a group with *[Camarasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarasaurus)* and *[Brachiosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosaurus)*, although [Nemegtosauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemegtosauridae) (*[Nemegtosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemegtosaurus)* and *[Quaesitosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaesitosaurus)*) was still classified as the basalmost family of diplodocoids. Upchurch chose to use Titanosauroidea as a replacement name for Titanosauria due to the recommended use of [Linnean taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnean_taxonomy) and ranks.[^cite_note-upchurch98-61]

In [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_in_paleontology), Leonardo Salgado *et al.* published a phylogenetic study on [Titanosauriformes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauriformes), including relationships within Titanosauria. They provided a definition for the clade of "including the most recent common ancestor of *Andesaurus delgadoi* and Titanosauridae and all of its descendants". Titanosauria resolved including the same two subclades as Bonaparte & Coria (1993), where Andesauridae was monotypic, only including the name genus, and Titanosauridae was all other titanosaurs. Titanosauria was additionally rediagnosed, with eye-shaped pleurocoels, forked infradiapophyseal [laminae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#laminae), centro-parapophyseal laminae, procoelous anterior caudals, and a significantly longer [pubis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pubis_bone) than [ischium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischium). Titanosauridae was less strongly defined because of the polytomy between *Malawisaurus* and *Epachthosaurus*, so some diagnostic features couldn't be resolved. Saltasaurinae was defined as the most recent ancestor of *Neuquensaurus*, *Saltasaurus* and its descendants, and diagnosed by short cervical [prezygapophyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#prezygapophysis), vertically compressed anterior caudals, and a posteriorly shifted anterior caudal neural spine.[^cite_note-salgado97-46]

Contributing additional work to the systematics of titanosaurs, Spanish paleontologist [José Sanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Sanz_\(paleontologist\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) *et al.* published an additional study in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_in_paleontology), utilizing both the names Titanosauria and Titanosauroidea in displaying their results. Similar to Upchurch (1995), Sanz *et al.* recovered *Opisthocoelicaudia* as a titanosauroid outside Titanosauria, while Titanosauria was redefined to include only the taxa classified by their study. [Eutitanosauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutitanosauria) was proposed as a name for the titanosaurs more derived than *Epachthosaurus*, and noted the presence of [osteoderms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoderms) as a probable [synapomorphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapomorphy) of this clade. *Aeolosaurus*, *Alamosaurus*, *[Ampelosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampelosaurus)* and *[Magyarosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyarosaurus)* were looked at using their character list, but were considered too incomplete to add to the final study.[^cite_note-sanz99-62]

Argentinian paleontologist Jaime Powell published his 1986 thesis in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_in_paleontology), with revisions to bring his old work up to date, including the addition of more phylogenetics and the recognition of Titanosauria as a clade name. Using the datamatrix of Sanz *et al.* (1999) and modifying it to include additional taxa and some character changes, Powell found that titanosaurs formed mostly a single gradual radiation beginning with *Epachthosaurus* as the most basal titanosaur, and *[Ampelosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampelosaurus)* and *[Isisaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isisaurus)* as the most derived. Titanosauroidea (following Upchurch 1995), was distinguished by pre- and post-spinal laminae in anterior caudals, a laterally flared ilium, a lateral expansion of the upper femur, and strongly opisthocoelous posterior dorsals. Less inclusive, Titanosauria was diagnosed by horizontally facing dorsal [diapophyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#diapophysis), prominent procoelous anterior caudals, and a ridge on the [sternal plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternum). Within Titanosauria, Eutitanosauria was characterized by the absence of a hyposphene-hypantrum, no femoral fourth trochanter, and osteoderms. A small clade of *Alamosaurus*, *[Lirainosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lirainosaurus)* and the "Peirópolis titanosaur" (*[Trigonosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonosaurus)*) was resolved, and diagnosed by only a rotation of the tibia so the proximal end is perpendicular to the distal end. More derived clades, while resolved, were only weakly supported, or characterized by reversions of diagnostic traits of larger groups (below and left).[^cite_note-powell2003-63]

*[Rapetosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapetosaurus)* was described in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_in_paleontology) by [Kristina Curry-Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristina_Curry-Rogers?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Catherine Forster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Forster), who additionally provided a new phylogenetic analysis of [Titanosauriformes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauriformes) (above and right). Titanosauria was strongly supported, distinguished by up to 20 characters depending on unknown traits in basal taxa. Similarly, Saltasaurinae was characterised by up to 16 traits, and the clade of *Rapetosaurus* and related taxa possessed four unique features. *Nemegtosaurus* and *Quaesitosaurus* were resolved within Titanosauria for the first time, after being placed in Diplodocoidea by multiple other analyses, because *Rapetosaurus* provided the first significant titanosaur cranial material with associated postcrania. All three genera were resolved in a clade together, although Curry-Rogers & Forster noted that it was possible the group was only resolved because no other titanosaurs had comparable cranial material. *Opisthocoelicaudia* was also nested deeply in Saltasaurinae, though a further investigation of titanosaur interrelationships was proposed.[^cite_note-curryrogers01-64]



Mounted skeleton of a juvenile *[Rapetosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapetosaurus)*

American paleontologist Jeff Wilson presented another revision of overall sauropod phylogeny in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_in_paleontology), resolving strong support for most groups, and a similar result to Upchurch (1998) although with *[Euhelopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhelopus)* closest to titanosaurs instead of outside [Neosauropoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neosauropoda). More internal clades were resolved for Titanosauria, with *Nemegtosaurus* and *Rapetosaurus* united within Nemegtosauridae, and [Saltasauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasauridae) including two subfamilies, Opisthocoelicaudiinae and Saltasaurinae. Saltasauridae was defined as a node-stem triplet, where everything descended from the common ancestor of *Opisthocoelicaudia* and *Saltasaurus* was within Saltasauridae, and the subfamilies Saltasaurinae and Opisthocoelicaudiinae were for every taxon on one branch of the saltasaurid tree or the other.[^cite_note-wilson02-31]

Wilson and Paul Upchurch followed this study up in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_in_paleontology) with a significant revision of the type genus *Titanosaurus*, and revisited all the material that had been assigned to the genus while reviewing titanosaur inter-relationships. Because they found *Titanosaurus* to be a [dubious name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomen_dubium), they proposed that [Linnaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus)-named groups Titanosauridae and Titanosauroidea should be considered invalid as well. Wilson & Upchurch (2003) supported the definition of Salgado *et al.* (1997) for Titanosauria, since it was oldest and most similar to the original content of the group when named by Bonaparte & Coria (1993). [Lithostrotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithostrotia) (Upchurch *et al.* 2004) was defined to be *[Malawisaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawisaurus)* and all more derived titanosaurs, and the clade [Eutitanosauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutitanosauria) (Sanz *et al.* 1999) was considered a possible synonym of [Saltasauridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasauridae). Wilson & Upchurch (2003) presented a reduced cladogram of Titanosauria, including only the most commonly-analyzed taxa from previous studies, resulting in a tree similar to that of Wilson (2002) but with *Rapetosaurus* and *Nemegtosaurus* excluded and *Epachthosaurus* included. *Alamosaurus* and *Opisthocoelicaudia* were united within Opisthocoelicaudiinae, *Neuquensaurus* and *Saltasaurus* formed Saltasaurinae, and *Isisaurus* placed as the next most derived titanosaurid.[^cite_note-wilson03-11]



Holotype skeleton of *[Opisthocoelicaudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthocoelicaudia)*

At the same time as Wilson & Upchurch redescribing the species of *Titanosaurus*, Saldago (2003) looked over the potential invalidity of the family Titanosauridae and redefined the internal clades of Titanosauria.[^cite_note-wilson03-11][^cite_note-salgado03-65] Titanosauria was defined as more inclusive than Titanosauroidea, contrasting with earlier used by Upchurch (1995) and Sanz *et al.* (1999), as all taxa in [Somphospondyli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somphospondyli) closer to *Saltasaurus* than *[Euhelopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhelopus)*. In order to create additional stability, Saldago also defined [Andesauroidea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesauroidea) for only *Andesaurus*, as every titanosaur closer to that genus than *Saltasaurus*, and also it's opposite Titanosauroidea as every titanosaur closer to *Saltasaurus* than *Andesaurus*. Next most inclusive, Salgado revitalised Titanosauridae to include everything descended from the ancestor of *Epachthosaurus* and *Saltasaurus*, and to replace the node-stem triplet of Saltasauridae, defined the clades [Epachthosaurinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epachthosaurinae) and Eutitanosauria as *Epachthosaurus*>*Saltasaurus* and *Saltasaurus*<*Epachthosaurus* respectively. Saltasaurinae and Opisthocoelicaudiinae were retained with their original definitions, but Lithostrotia was considered a synonym of Titanosauridae, and Titanosaurinae was considered a [paraphyletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraphyletic) clade of unrelated titanosaurids.[^cite_note-salgado03-65]



Life restoration of *[Rinconsaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinconsaurus)*, a derived titanosaur possessing unique caudals that significantly change articular surfaces throughout the tail

Following the clade definitions proposed in previous Salgado studies, [Bernardo González-Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Gonz%C3%A1lez-Riga?action=edit\&redlink=1) published two papers in 2003 describing new taxa in Titanosauria: *[Mendozasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendozasaurus)*, and *[Rinconsaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinconsaurus)* (with [Jorge O. Calvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_O._Calvo)). In both studies, the new taxa formed clades within Titanosauridae, although neither were named, and new diagnostic features were proposed for the family.[^cite_note-gonzalez03-66][^cite_note-calvo03-67] For *Mendozasaurus*, the new genus grouped with *Malawisaurus* as basal within Titanosauridae, but because of the features of caudal vertebrae in these basal taxa, González-Riga recommended revising the diagnosis of the family, instead of changing the content.[^cite_note-gonzalez03-66] The situation of caudals in *Rinconsaurus* also suggested procoelous caudals were no longer diagnostic, because in the tail of *Rinconsaurus* the vertebrae regularly changed their articular surfaces, being from procoelous caudals interspersed with amphicoelous, opisthocoelous and biconvex vertebrae.[^cite_note-calvo03-67] *Rinconsaurus* was then included in [Aeolosaurini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolosaurini), a clade named the following year by Aldirene Franco-Rosas *et al.* containing everything closer to *Aeolosaurus* and *Gondwanatitan* than *Saltasaurus* or *Opisthocoelicaudia*. Only the three genera and various intermediate specimens were included in Aeolosaurini in their [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_in_paleontology) paper, with the tribe being considered to be within Saltasaurinae.[^cite_note-franco04-68]

The second edition of *[The Dinosauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dinosauria)*, published in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_in_paleontology), included newly described titanosaurs and other taxa reidentified as titanosaurs. Written by Upchurch, [Paul Barrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Barrett_\(palaeobiologist\)) and [Peter Dodson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Dodson), a review of Sauropoda included a more expansive Titanosauria for sauropods more derived than brachiosaurids. Titanosauria, defined as everything closer to *Saltasaurus* than *[Brachiosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosaurus)*, included a very large variety of taxa, and the new clade [Lithostrotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithostrotia) was named for a large number of more derived taxa, although Nemegtosauridae was placed in Diplodocoidea following earlier publications of Upchurch.[^cite_note-upchurch04-51] Lithostrotia adopted the distinguishing feature of strongly procoelous caudals, previously used for Titanosauria.[^cite_note-wilson03-11][^cite_note-upchurch04-51]

### New phylogenetic frameworks

In [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_in_paleontology), Curry-Rogers proposed a new phylogenetic analysis that focused on the inter-relationships of Titanosauria and included the most expansive character and taxon list of any study before it. 364 characters were selected from all previous phylogenetic analyses and scored across 29 probable titanosaurs, ranging from the [Late Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Jurassic) African *Janenschia* to the large variety of Late Cretaceous global genera. Proposing her analysis as the basis for a new phylogenetic framework of Titanosauria, Curry-Rogers recommended only using named for clades that were very strongly supported. For the strict consensus, every taxon more derived than *Brachiosaurus* was in an unresolved polytomy except for a clade of *Rapetosaurus* and *Nemegtosaurus*, and one of Saltasaurinae. Within the recommended results, she only named Titanosauria, Lithostrotia, Saltasauridae, Saltasaurinae and Opisthocoelicaudiinae, because of the weakness of support (below and left).[^cite_note-curryrogers05-49]

Another form of composite matrix was created by Calvo, González-Riga and [Juan Porfiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Porfiri?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_in_paleontology), based upon multiple previous studies between 1997 and 2003. The final analysis included 15 titanosaurs and 65 characters, and the typical titanosaur subclades were resolved, Titanosauridae being used over Lithostrotia following Salgado (2003), and the new clade [Rinconsauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinconsauria) for the clade of *[Rinconsaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinconsaurus)* and *[Muyelensaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muyelensaurus)*. The new clade (defined as *Rinconsaurus* and *Muyelensaurus*) was placed as the sister taxon of Aeolosaurini, which together grouped with *Rapetosaurus* as sister to Saltasauridae.[^cite_note-calvo07a-50] In the same year, Calvo *et al.* published another paper, describing the basal titanosaur *[Futalognkosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futalognkosaurus)*. The only difference in the resulting phylogeny, based on the matrix of the Calvo, González-Riga & Porfiri (2007), was the addition of *Futalognkosaurus* as the sister taxon to *[Mendozasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendozasaurus)* in a clade Calvo *et al.* named [Lognkosauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lognkosauria), defined by the two genera classified within it.[^cite_note-calvo07b-69] A very similar result was also recovered by González-Riga *et al.* in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_in_paleontology) in a phylogenetic analysis based partially on that of Calvo *et al.* (2007), although *Epachthosaurus* was nested with *Rapetosaurus* outside the clades of aeolosaurines.[^cite_note-gonzalez09-70] Further updates and modifications were then made by Palbo Gallina & Apesteguía in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_in_paleontology), with the additions of *[Ligabuesaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligabuesaurus)*, *Antarctosaurus*, *Nemegtosaurus* and *[Bonitasaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonitasaura)* and character updates to match, bringing the total to 77 characters and 22 taxa. Significantly contrasting the earlier results, internal relationships of Titanosauria were rearranged. *Malawisaurus* nested with *Andesaurus* in a clade of the basalmost titanosaurs outside Titanosauroidea, where *Lirainosaurus*, instead of being the basal member of the saltasaur-branch was instead basalmost titanosauroid. Lognkosauria moved to be within rinconsaurs, while Nemegtosauridae was resolved as the sister of *Aeolosaurus* and *Gondwanatitan*, and the rinconsaur-lognkosaur branch. *Antarctosaurus* was unstable, but placed in a polytomy with the lognkosaurs and rinconsaurs before being excluded. Saltasaurinae and its relationship with *Opisthocoelicaudia* remained the same.[^cite_note-gallina11-71]



Skull of *[Tapuiasaurus macedoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapuiasaurus_macedoi)*

Nemegtosauridae was additionally revised by Hussam Zaher *et al.* (2011) with the description of *[Tapuiasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapuiasaurus)*, which nested closer to *Rapetosaurus* than *Nemegtosaurus*, with all three forming a clade of derived lithostrotians. Using the matrix of Wilson (2002), following the additions of a few cranial characters and *[Diamantinasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamantinasaurus)*, *[Tangvayosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangvayosaurus)* and *[Phuwiangosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuwiangosaurus)*, remained the same as originally found by Wilson but with *Diamantinasaurus* sister to Saltasauridae and the other two genera as basal titanosaurs outside Lithostrotia, since Titanosauria, while undefined, was labelled to include all taxa closer to *Saltasaurus* than *[Euhelopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhelopus)*.[^cite_note-zaher11-72] Following a revision of the skull of *Tapuiasaurus*, Wilson *et al.* ([2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_in_paleontology)) rescored the analysis of Zaher *et al.* and recovered similar results for everything but Nemegtosauridae, where the family dissolved into a more basal *Tapuiasaurus* outside Lithostrota and *Nemegtosaurus* outside Saltasauridae. While non-titanosaur phylogeny remained identical in every single result, the topology within Titanosauria was very labile and prone to change with minor adjustments.[^cite_note-wilson16-73]



Mounted replica skeleton of *[Futalognkosaurus dukei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futalognkosaurus_dukei)*, [Royal Ontario Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ontario_Museum)

Also following the 2002 analysis of Wilson, José Carballido and colleagues published a redescription of *[Chubutisaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubutisaurus)* in 2011, and utilized an updated Wilson matrix, expanded to 289 characters across 41 taxa, including 15 titanosaurs. The primary focus of the analysis was on the basal titanosauriform taxa, but Titanosauria was defined, as the most recent common ancestor of *Andesaurus delgadoi* and *Saltasaurus loricatus*, and all its descendants, although the only autapomorphy of the group recovered was the absence of a prominent ventral process on the scapula.[^cite_note-carballido11-74] This same matrix and basis of characters was further utilized and expanded for analyses on *[Tehuelchesaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehuelchesaurus)*, *[Comahuesaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comahuesaurus)* and related rebbachisaurs, *[Europasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europasaurus)*, and *[Padillasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padillasaurus)*, before being expanded upon once again in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_in_paleontology) by Carballido *et al.* during the description of *[Patagotitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagotitan)* to 405 characters and 87 taxa, including 28 titanosaurs (above and right).[^cite_note-carballido11b-75][^cite_note-carballido12-76][^cite_note-carballido14-77][^cite_note-carballido15-78][^cite_note-carballido17-79] The definition of Titanosauria was preserved following Salgado *et al.* (1997) as *Andesaurus* plus *Saltasaurus*. Eutitanosauria (closer to *Saltasaurus* than *Epachthosaurus*) was resolved as a very inclusive clade composed of two distinct branches, one leading to the larger-bodied lognkosaurs and the other to the smaller-bodied saltasaurs. On the lognkosaur branch of Eutitanosauria, there is a branch of lognkosaurs and one of Rinconsauria. Following Calvo, González-Riga and Porfiri (2007), Rinconsauria was defined as *Muyelensaurus* plus *Rinconsaurus*, and Lognkosauria was defined as *Mendozasaurus* plus *Futalognkosaurus*. Rinconsauria included taxa typically found within Aeolosaurini as well, so Aeolosaurini was redefined as *Aeolosaurus rionegrinus* plus *Gondwanatitan* to preserve the original restricted content, otherwise the entire rinconsaur-lognkosaur branch would be classified within Aeolosaurini. Lithostrotia, Saltasauridae and Saltasaurinae had their definitions preserved from earlier studies, and included their typical content.[^cite_note-carballido17-79]

Philip Mannion and colleagues redescribed *[Lusotitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusotitan)* in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_in_paleontology), creating a new analysis of 279 characters drawn from significant previous analyses by Upchurch and Wilson supplemented by other studies. 63 sauropods were included, focusing on non-titanosaurian sauropods, although 14 probable titanosaurs were included. Unique to Mannion *et al.*, continuous characters were distinguished in a run of the matrix, which resolved almost all of [Somphospondyli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somphospondyli) within Titanosauria because of *Andesaurus* placing very basal in a large group of Andesauroidea. Titanosauroidea was tentatively retained as the opposite clade of titanosaurs, which included all other traditional titanosaurs, although it was noted because of the invalidity of *Titanosaurus*, Titanosauroidea should be considered an invalid name as well.[^cite_note-mannion13-47] While the original analysis didn't focus on titanosaurs, it was utilised during the descriptions of *[Savannasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannasaurus)* and *[Diamantinasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamantinasaurus)*, *[Yongjinglong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongjinglong)*, an osteology of *Mendozasaurus*, and redescribing *[Tendaguria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendaguria)*.[^cite_note-poropat14-36][^cite_note-poropat16-80][^cite_note-li14-81][^cite_note-gonzalez18-82][^cite_note-mannion19-44] From these updates, an analysis of 548 characters and 124 taxa was published by Mannion *et al.* in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_in_paleontology) for a redescription of *[Jiangshanosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangshanosaurus)* and *[Dongyangosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongyangosaurus)*, and additional revisions of *[Ruyangosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruyangosaurus)* were made. No differentiation between continuous and discrete characters was made like performed by Mannion *et al.* (2013), but a large clade of Andesauroidea was still resolved with implied weights. Both redescribed Asian taxa, as well as *Yongjinglong*, previously considered derived titanosaurs related to Saltasauridae, were removed to outside the clade.[^cite_note-mannion19b-83]

## Paleobiology

### Diet

[Fossilized dung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprolite) associated with late Cretaceous titanosaurids from India has revealed [phytoliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoliths), silicified plant fragments, that offer clues to a broad, unselective plant diet. Besides the plant remains that might have been expected, such as [cycads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycad) and [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer), discoveries published in 2005[^cite_note-85] revealed an unexpectedly wide range of [monocotyledons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocotyledon), including [palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae) and grasses ([Poaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae)), including ancestors of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and [bamboo](39029.md), which has given rise to speculation that herbivorous dinosaurs and grasses [co-evolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-evolution).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

### Nesting



Diagram showing titanosaur nest excavation and egg laying

A large titanosaurid [nesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nest) ground was discovered in Auca Mahuevo, in [Patagonia](65433.md), Argentina and another colony has reportedly been discovered in Spain. Several hundred female [saltasaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltasaurus) dug holes with their back feet, laid eggs in clutches averaging around 25 eggs each, and buried the nests under dirt and vegetation. The small [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_\(biology\)), about 11–12 centimetres (4.3–4.7 in) in [diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diameter), contained [fossilised](10958.md) [embryos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo), complete with [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin) impressions. The impressions showed that titanosaurs were covered in a mosaic armour of small bead-like scales.[^cite_note-CoriaChiappe2007-23] The huge number of individuals gives evidence of [herd](969684.md) behavior, which, along with their armor, could have helped provide protection against large predators such as *[Abelisaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelisaurus)*.[^cite_note-90]

### Range



*[Patagotitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagotitan)* skeleton cast on display at the [American Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Museum_of_Natural_History)

The titanosaurs were the last great group of sauropods, which existed from about 136[^cite_note-\:0-91] to 66 [million years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_years_ago), before the [Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous%E2%80%93Paleogene_extinction_event), and were the dominant [herbivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbivore) of their time. The fossil evidence suggests they replaced the other sauropods, like the [diplodocids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplodocid) and the [brachiosaurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosaurid), which died out between the late [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) and the mid-[Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) Periods.

Titanosaurs were widespread. In December 2011, Argentine scientists announced titanosaur fossils had been found on Antarctica[^cite_note-Cerda2011-17]—meaning that titanosaur fossils have been found on all continents. They are especially numerous in the southern continents (then part of the [supercontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercontinent) of [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana)). Australia had titanosaurs around 96 million years ago: fossils have been discovered in [Queensland](59051.md) of a creature around 25 metres (82 ft) long.[^cite_note-austitan-92][^cite_note-carpenterandtidswell2005-93] Remains have also been discovered in New Zealand.[^cite_note-94] One of the largest ever titanosaur footprints was discovered in the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert) in 2016.[^cite_note-95] One of the oldest remains of this group was described by [Ghilardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aline_Ghilardi) et al. (2016).[^cite_note-96] It was found from the [Valley of the Dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Dinosaurs,_Para%C3%ADba), [Paraíba](229523.md) state of [Brazil](3383.md), representing a 136-million-year-old subadult individual.[^cite_note-\:0-91]

### Paleopathology

*[Ibirania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibirania)*, a nanoid titanosaur fossil from Brazil suggests that individuals of various genera were susceptible to diseases such as [osteomyelitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteomyelitis) and parasite infestations. The specimen hails from the late cretaceous [São José do Rio Preto Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamantina_Formation), [Bauru Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauru_Group), and was described in the journal *Cretaceous Research* by [Aureliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito_Aureliano) et al. (2021).[^cite_note-97] Examination of the titanosaur's bones revealed what appear to be parasitic blood worms similar to the prehistoric *[Paleoleishmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoleishmania)* but are 10-100 times larger, that seemed to have caused the osteomyelitis. The fossil is the first known instance of an aggressive case of osteomyelitis being caused by blood worms in an extinct animal.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]
