# Government of the 33rd Dáil

&#x20; There were three governments of the [33rd Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_D%C3%A1il), being [coalition governments](6036.md) of [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il), [Fine Gael](11252.md) and the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Ireland\)). This followed the [2020 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election) to [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann) held on 8 February, and negotiations on a [programme for government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election#Government_formation) that lasted till June. The parties agreed on a [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_government#Ireland), with the two major party leaders alternating as Taoiseach.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-agreed17dec-2] The makeup of the parties resulted in a [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right) coalition.[^cite_note-3] It was the first time that [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il) and [Fine Gael](11252.md) have participated in the same government, which Leo Varadkar described as the end of what has often been referred to as [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Civil_War) politics.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The **32nd government of Ireland** (27 June 2020 to 17 December 2022) was led by [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin), [leader of Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_Fianna_F%C3%A1il), as Taoiseach, and [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar), [leader of Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_Fine_Gael), as [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste). It was in office for 2 years, 175 days and ended as planned with the rotation of the office of Taoiseach.

The **33rd government of Ireland** (17 December 2022 to 9 April 2024) was led by Varadkar as Taoiseach and Martin as Tánaiste. It lasted 1 year, 114 days. Varadkar resigned as leader of Fine Gael on 20 March 2024 and was succeeded on 24 March by [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris). Varadkar resigned as Taoiseach on 8 April.[^cite_note-April8-6]

The **34th government of Ireland** (9 April 2024 to 23 January 2025) was led by Simon Harris as Taoiseach and Martin as Tánaiste. It lasted 289 days. Harris resigned as Taoiseach on 18 December 2024 on the morning of the first meeting of the [34th Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_D%C3%A1il) after the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election). Harris and the other members of the government continued to carry out their duties until the appointment of their successors on 23 January 2025.

### Nomination of Taoiseach

The [33rd Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_D%C3%A1il) first met on 20 February 2020. [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar), [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach) and [Fine Gael leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael_leader), [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin), [Fianna Fáil leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il_leader), [Mary Lou McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Lou_McDonald), [Sinn Féin leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Sinn_F%C3%A9in), and [Eamon Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eamon_Ryan), [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Ireland\)) leader, were each [proposed for nomination as Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_vote_for_Taoiseach). None of the four motions were successful. Varadkar announced that he would resign as Taoiseach but that under the provisions of Article 28.11 of the Constitution, the members of the government would continue to carry out their duties until their successors were appointed.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

On 27 June, the Dáil again debated nominations for the position of Taoiseach. The nomination of Martin was approved by the Dáil.[^cite_note-9] Martin was then appointed as Taoiseach by [President](24676.md) [Michael D. Higgins](559753.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-tapp32-11]

### Attorney General

[Paul Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gallagher_\(barrister\)) [SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_counsel) was appointed by the president as [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Ireland) on the nomination of the Taoiseach, a role he had previously served in from 2007 to 2011.[^cite_note-tapp32-11][^cite_note-mapp32-17]

### Events affecting the government

[Minister for Agriculture, Food and the Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Agriculture,_Food_and_the_Marine) [Barry Cowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Cowen) was sacked on 14 July 2020 because of driving offences which he had committed but not disclosed to Micheál Martin prior to his appointment to cabinet.[^cite_note-rte14jul20-44] He was replaced by Dara Calleary.

Legislation was passed to allow each of three [Ministers of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_\(Ireland\)) who attend cabinet meetings to receive an allowance, as previous legislation had provided an allowance for two only.[^cite_note-87] After public dissatisfaction with the proposal, the three ministers of state agreed on 28 July 2020 to share the existing allowance between them, rather than accept the increase.[^cite_note-88]

Minister for Agriculture, Food and the Marine [Dara Calleary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dara_Calleary) resigned on 21 August 2020 after the fallout from the [Oireachtas Golf Society scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas_Golf_Society_scandal).[^cite_note-callres-47] [Phil Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Hogan) resigned as [European Commissioner for Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Trade) on 26 August 2020 in response to the same events.[^cite_note-89]

In October 2020, *[Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_\(magazine\))* magazine published a claim that [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar) had provided a copy of a confidential document to the head of the National Association of General Practitioners that had been part of negotiations with the [Irish Medical Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Medical_Organisation) in April 2019 while Taoiseach.[^cite_note-90] Fine Gael issued a statement which described the article as "both inaccurate and grossly defamatory", and while accepting that the provision of the agreement by private channels was "not best practice", said there was nothing unlawful about what had occurred.[^cite_note-91] Sinn Féin tabled a motion of no confidence in the Tánaiste. In response, the Taoiseach moved a motion of confidence.

At a cabinet meeting in July 2021, [Minister for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade) [Simon Coveney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Coveney) announced the appointment of [Katherine Zappone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Zappone), former [Minister for Children and Youth Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Children,_Disability_and_Equality), to the newly created position of Special Envoy to the UN for Freedom of Opinion and Expression. It emerged that the proposed appointment had not been flagged by Coveney with the Taoiseach in advance of the meeting.[^cite_note-92] Zappone declined the appointment after the [Merrion Hotel controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrion_Hotel_controversy) arose, in which the *[Irish Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Independent)* reported that six days prior to the announcement of her appointment, Zappone had hosted a gathering for 50 guests, including Tánaiste Leo Varadkar, at the [Merrion Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrion_Hotel) while the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland) was ongoing. Comparisons were made between the gathering and the [Golfgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas_Golf_Society_scandal) scandal earlier in the pandemic.[^cite_note-Striking_similarities-93] Sinn Féin tabled a motion of no confidence in Coveney, to be debated on 15 September on the return of the Dáil from the summer recess. In response, the Taoiseach moved a motion of confidence.[^cite_note-94]

On 6 July 2022, the government lost its majority after Fine Gael TD [Joe McHugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_McHugh) voted against legislation underpinning a €2.7 billion [mica redress scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mica_scandal) and subsequently resigned the Fine Gael [party whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_\(politics\)#Ireland).[^cite_note-95] Sinn Féin tabled a motion of no confidence in the government, to be debated on 12 July before the summer recess.[^cite_note-96] In response, the Taoiseach moved a motion of confidence.[^cite_note-97]

On 24 August 2022, [Robert Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Troy) resigned as [Minister of State at the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Enterprise,_Tourism_and_Employment) after his failure to declare property interest was revealed by [The Ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ditch_\(website\)).[^cite_note-troyresign-83][^cite_note-98] He was succeeded by former Minister for Agriculture [Dara Calleary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dara_Calleary).[^cite_note-99]

In December 2022, [People Before Profit–Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Before_Profit%E2%80%93Solidarity) tabled a motion of no confidence in Minister for Housing [Darragh O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darragh_O'Brien), to be debated on 13 December, four days before a new government was formed. The group claimed the worsening housing and homelessness crisis under O'Brien was "tearing apart the social fabric of Irish society and leading to the scapegoating of refugees". In response, the Taoiseach moved a motion of confidence.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

### Motions of confidence

On 10 November 2020, a [motion of confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_confidence) in the [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste) and [Minister for Enterprise, Trade and Employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Enterprise,_Tourism_and_Employment), [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar), proposed by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, was approved with 92 votes in favour to 65 against.[^cite_note-102]

On 15 September 2021, a motion of confidence in the [Minister for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade) and [Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Defence_\(Ireland\)), [Simon Coveney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Coveney), proposed by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, was approved with 92 votes in favour to 59 against.[^cite_note-103]

On 12 July 2022, a motion of confidence in the government, proposed by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, was approved with 85 votes in favour to 66 against, with one abstention.[^cite_note-104]

On 13 December 2022, a motion of confidence in the [Minister for Housing, Local Government and Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Housing,_Local_Government_and_Heritage), [Darragh O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darragh_O'Brien), proposed by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, was approved with 86 votes in favour to 63 against, with one abstention.[^cite_note-105]

### Resignation

Micheál Martin resigned as Taoiseach on Saturday 17 December 2022 to allow the appointment of Leo Varadkar as Taoiseach and the formation of a new government, a continuation of the coalition agreement between Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael and the Green Party.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] The date agreed in the Programme for Government had been Thursday 15 December, but this date was put back to facilitate Martin's attendance at a meeting of the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council).[^cite_note-agreed17dec-2]

### Events affecting the government

A month after the government was formed, in January 2023, news website *[The Ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ditch_\(website\))* published a story claiming [Minister of State for Employment Affairs and Retail Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Enterprise,_Tourism_and_Employment) [Damien English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_English) failed to declare ownership of an existing home in his planning application for a new property in 2008. It also claimed he neglected to declare such ownership in the Dáil register of interests.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] He resigned as Minister of State on 12 January 2023.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] He was succeeded by [Neale Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neale_Richmond).[^cite_note-richmond-145]

Also in January, the *[Sunday Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Independent_\(Ireland\))* revealed that [Minister for Public Expenditure, National Development Plan Delivery and Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Public_Expenditure,_Infrastructure,_Public_Service_Reform_and_Digitalisation) [Paschal Donohoe](12464309.md) failed to properly declare a donation from a company in 2016. The [Standards in Public Office Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standards_in_Public_Office_Commission) (SIPO) made a complaint against Donohoe that the Designer Group engineering firm used two company vans and six employees to erect and later remove election posters for Donohoe in his [Dublin Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Central) constituency during the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election) campaign.[^cite_note-153] On 14 January, Donohoe began conducting a review of his election expenses statements amid the allegations which he had denied.[^cite_note-154] The next day, on 15 January, he apologised for making incorrect declarations of election expenses and donations during his campaign and said he would recuse himself from any decision making around ethics legislation while the SIPO investigated him, but refused to resign as minister.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] The controversy intensified on 20 January when Donohoe identified a new issue over expenses from the [2020 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election).[^cite_note-157]

In March 2023, the government ended a ban on evictions, with effect from 31 March. Sinn Féin tabled a motion calling on the government to extend the ban until the end of January 2024. A government amendment to this motion was carried by a vote of 83 to 68.[^cite_note-158] In a series of votes, Green Party TD [Neasa Hourigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neasa_Hourigan) broke with the government whip, leading to her suspension from the parliamentary party.[^cite_note-159] The [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)) tabled a [motion of no confidence](208101.md) in the government.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

On 23 November 2023, [a riot broke out in Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dublin_riot) following an attack on three children and a care assistant by a male [immigrant](15497819.md) of [Algerian origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerians). Following the riot, opposition politicians called for the resignations of the [Minister for Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Justice,_Home_Affairs_and_Migration) [Helen McEntee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_McEntee) and the [Garda Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_Commissioner) [Drew Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Harris). On 1 December, a week after the riot, [Sinn Féin](28175.md) announced that they would bring a motion of no confidence in McEntee. In response, the Taoiseach moved a vote of confidence in McEntee on 5 December.

### Constitutional referendums

Main article: [2024 Irish constitutional referendums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_constitutional_referendums)

The government held two referendums on 8 March 2024 on proposed [amendments to the Constitution of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Ireland). The [Thirty-ninth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_constitutional_referendums#Thirty-ninth_Amendment) proposed to expand the constitutional definition of family to include durable relationships outside marriage. The [Fortieth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_constitutional_referendums#Fortieth_Amendment) proposed to replace a reference to women's "life within the home" and a constitutional obligation to "endeavour to ensure that mothers shall not be obliged by economic necessity to engage in labour to the neglect of their duties in the home" with a gender-neutral article on supporting care within the family.[^cite_note-162]

Voters comprehensively rejected both bills, with 67.69 percent voting No to the proposed Thirty-ninth Amendment on the Family and 73.93 percent voting No to the proposed Fortieth Amendment on Care.[^cite_note-163] These were the highest and third-highest percentage votes for No in the history of Irish constitutional referendums.[^cite_note-164]

### Nomination of Taoiseach

Following the resignation of Leo Varadkar as Taoiseach on 8 April, the Dáil reconvened on 9 April.

[Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris) and [Michael Healy-Rae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Healy-Rae) were [proposed for the nomination of the Dáil for the position of Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_vote_for_Taoiseach). The motion proposing the nomination of Harris was approved with 88 for and 69 against the motion and Harris was appointed by [President](24676.md) [Michael D. Higgins](559753.md). This appointment made Harris Ireland's youngest Taoiseach to date and also marked the first time three Taoisigh were appointed within a single Dáil term.

### Resignation

On 8 November 2024, Taoiseach Simon Harris sought a dissolution of the Dáil which was granted by the [president](24676.md), with the new Dáil to convene on 18 December.[^cite_note-191] The [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election) took place on 29 November. On 18 December, before the first meeting of the [34th Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_D%C3%A1il), Harris submitted his resignation to the president.[^cite_note-192] The other members of the government were also deemed to have resigned, but the taoiseach and the other members of the government continued to carry on their duties until the appointment of their successors on 23 January 2025.
