**Agadez Region&#x20;**&#x20;is one of the seven [regions of Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Niger). At 667,799 square kilometres (257,839 mi<sup>2</sup>), it covers more than half of Niger's land area, and is the largest region in the country, as well as the [largest African state subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_country_subdivisions_by_area). The capital of the region is [Agadez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadez).

## History

The region is a centre for [palaeontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeontology), with numerous dinosaur skeletons being found here, including the *[Ouranosaurus nigeriensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouranosaurus_nigeriensis)*.[^cite_note-Bradt-3] Cave painting and the remains of ancient human settlements are also located here.[^cite_note-Bradt-3] Tuareg peoples began migrating to the region from the mid-8th century.[^cite_note-Bradt-3] From the mid-15th century to the early 20th, much of the region was under the control of the [Sultanate of Agadez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Agadez), except for a period when the area came under the rule of the [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire) in the 1500s.[^cite_note-Bradt-3]

The region suffered with the advent of French colonialism as power shifted away to the southwest; Tuareg disaffection with French rule resulted in the [Kaocen revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaocen_revolt) in 1916–17.[^cite_note-Bradt-3] This process continued following Niger's independence in 1960; local Tuareg saw little recompense from the [uranium](31743.md) mining boom in Arlit in the 1970s, and Agadez Region was struck by repeated droughts and famines.[^cite_note-Bradt-3] Since then there have been two Tuareg rebellions: from [1990–95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(1990%E2%80%931995\)) and [2007–09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2007%E2%80%932009\)).[^cite_note-Bradt-3] In recent years the region has also been affected by [an insurgency in the Maghreb](13153771.md) by Islamist groups.

## Geography

The Agadez Region borders [Algeria](358.md) ([Tamanrasset Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamanrasset_Province) and [Illizi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illizi_Province)) and [Libya](17633.md) ([Murzuq District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murzuq_District)) to the north, [Chad](5488.md) ([Tibesti Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti_Region)) to the east, [Diffa Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffa_Region), [Zinder Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinder_Region), [Tahoua Region](2000318.md) and [Maradi Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maradi_Region) to the south, and [Mali](19127.md) ([Kidal Region](2214311.md)) to the west. It is by far the largest region of the country, representing 52% of the total area of Niger. The region is dominated by the [Sahara desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_desert), and includes the vast [Ténéré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9n%C3%A9r%C3%A9) portion of that desert, as well as dune seas such as the [Erg of Bilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_of_Bilma).[^cite_note-Bradt-3] The [Aïr Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFr_Mountains), the tallest peaks in Niger, are also located here.[^cite_note-Bradt-3] The [Djado Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djado_Plateau) is located in the far north.

### Settlements

[Agadez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadez) is the regional capital. Other major settlements include [Aderbissinat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aderbissinat), [Arlit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlit), [Assamakka](3819829.md), [Bilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilma), [Dirkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirkou), [Iferouane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iferouane), [In-Gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-Gall), [Madama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madama), [Séguédine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gu%C3%A9dine), [Tchirozerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchirozerine), [Tegguiada In Tessoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegguiada_In_Tessoum) and [Timia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timia).[^cite_note-4] The historically important city of [Tchintoulous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchintoulous) also lies in the region, but today is only a sparsely populated village.[^cite_note-5]

## Demographics

&#x20;Agadez has a large area but is sparsely populated. Its 487,620 inhabitants (as per the 2012 census)[^cite_note-Niger_at_Geohive-1] account for only 2.8% of the total population of Niger, with a population density of 0.73 inhabitants per square kilometre (1.9/sq mi). Much of its population comprises [nomadic or semi-nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) peoples, including [Arabs](2185.md), [Fulani](102383.md), [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people), [Dazaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazaga) [Toubou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubou) and various [Tuareg](103054.md) groups. The [Tagdal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagdal_language) and [Tasawaq language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasawaq_language), thought to be mixed [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhay_languages)-Tuareg languages, are also spoken.[^cite_note-7]

## Economy

See also: [Oil and mining industry of Niger § Uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_mining_industry_of_Niger#Uranium)



The Aïr Mountains near Timia

Traversed for centuries by the [Trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade) routes, the oasis towns of the Aïr and the eastern [Kaouar Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaouar) are known for their gardens, salt manufacture, and [date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) cultivation. [Arlit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlit) is the centre of Niger's [uranium](31743.md) industry, a prominent economic sector in the region with uranium pits and mines operated by foreign companies providing substantial revenue for the country. The French discovered Niger's first uranium deposits in the Tim Mersoi Basin of the Agadez Region in 1958 and since then French companies such as [Areva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orano) have maintained a large footprint in the region, employing a large quantity of locals.[^cite_note-Klare2012-9][^cite_note-USA2008-10] Depressed uranium prices since the 1980s have hit the region hard, though uranium remains one of the main foreign exchange earners for the country and a main export.[^cite_note-11] The region is less poor than other regions of the country, having the second-highest [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) and the second-lowest incidence of [extreme poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty) according to [World Bank](36752.md) data from 2018.[^cite_note-12]

Agadez had historically been one of the main centres of tourism in Niger, with visitors attracted by the desert scenery, archaeological sites and the Air Mountains. However Tuareg rebellions and activities of Islamist militants in Agadez region have severely reduced the numbers of tourists, with most third party governments advising against travel to the region.[^cite_note-13]
