**Juan Carlos Muñoz Abogabir** (born 22 June 1970) is a [Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_people) [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician) and [civil engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineer) who currently serves as [Minister of Transportation and Telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transportation_and_Telecommunications_\(Chile\)).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## Family and education

He is the son of Juan Carlos Muñoz Frías and María Ximena Abogabir Scott.[^cite_note-latercera-3] He completed his primary and secondary education at Craighouse School in the commune of [Lo Barnechea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Barnechea).[^cite_note-4] He later pursued higher education in industrial civil engineering, with a specialization in transportation systems, at the [Pontifical Catholic University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Chile) (PUC). He subsequently earned a doctorate in civil and environmental engineering from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), in the [United States](3434750.md).

## Professional career

With an extensive academic background, he has worked in research areas focused on transportation operations, public transport systems, logistics and traffic flow theory, among others.[^cite_note-gob-5]

He served as a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in the Department of Transport and Logistics Engineering at the [Pontifical Catholic University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Chile) from 1995 to 2022.[^cite_note-gob-5] He was director of the Centro de Desarrollo Urbano Sustentable (CEDEUS), an institution promoted by the PUC and the [University of Concepción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Concepci%C3%B3n) aimed at contributing to sustainable development from multiple perspectives, including territorial planning, transportation systems and land use. He also served as the first director of the Institute for Sustainable Development at the PUC.[^cite_note-6]

Between 2003 and 2004, he served as an adviser to [Javier Etcheberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Etcheberry), then [Minister of Transport and Telecommunications](32175180.md), for the [Transantiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transantiago) public transport plan. In 2008, he was also a member of an expert committee established by the same ministry to improve the Transantiago project. He served on the board of directors of Metro Regional de Valparaíso (Merval), was a director of [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Metro) in Santiago, and acted as an adviser to the Metropolitan Public Transport Directorate. He founded and directed for eight years the Bus Rapid Transit Centre of Excellence, a research center funded by the Volvo Educational and Research Foundation.

During the second government of [Sebastián Piñera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Pi%C3%B1era), he served on the Scientific Committee on Climate Change of the [Ministry of Science, Technology, Knowledge and Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science,_Technology,_Knowledge_and_Innovation) and participated in various international scientific committees related to his field of expertise.[^cite_note-pauta-7]

On 21 January 2022, he was appointed [Minister of Transport and Telecommunications](32175180.md) by then president-elect [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md).[^cite_note-latercera-3] He assumed office on 11 March 2022.

In 2026, the [US Department of State](31975.md) revoked the US visas of Muñoz and his family in a dispute over a planned undersea fiber optic cable connecting Chile and [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island) with China. The US claimed these were "activities that compromised critical telecommunications infrastructure and eroded regional security".[^cite_note-8] After the project was shelved, the US reinstated the visas.[^cite_note-9]
