**Troy Edward Meink**[^cite_note-1] ([/mink/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*MEENK*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is an American government official who has served as the 27th [secretary of the Air Force](305210.md) since 2025.[^cite_note-2] He previously served as the principal deputy director of the [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office) (NRO).

## Early life

Meink is from [Lemmon, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemmon,_South_Dakota).[^cite_note-auto-3] He attended [South Dakota State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_State_University), where he received a degree in mechanical engineering in 1988.[^cite_note-auto1-4] He later attended [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), earning a [master's degree](170346.md) in aeronautical and astronautical engineering in 1995 and a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in the field in 1999.[^cite_note-auto1-4]

## Career

Meink entered the [United States Air Force](32090.md) in 1988 through the [Reserve Officers' Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps) (ROTC) program at South Dakota State University.[^cite_note-auto2-5] He started his career as a KC-135 tanker navigator and instructor and then a lead test engineer for the design and evaluation of ballistic missile test vehicles for the [Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Defense_Agency).[^cite_note-auto2-5] While a KC-135 tanker navigator, he was stationed at [Grissom Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grissom_Air_Force_Base) and served during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in Iraq.[^cite_note-auto1-4]

Meink subsequently led development for the Military Satellite Communications Joint Program Office and served as program director for the [Transformational Satellite Communications System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformational_Satellite_Communications_System).[^cite_note-auto2-5] He was a director in the office of the assistant secretary of defense and a director for signal intelligence systems acquisition for the [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office) (NRO) from 2006 to 2013.[^cite_note-auto2-5]

Meink then served as the [Deputy Under Secretary of the Air Force for Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_the_Air_Force_for_Space_Acquisition_and_Integration) from November 2013 to June 2014.[^cite_note-auto2-5] Afterwards, he was assistant director of national intelligence for systems and resource analyses from 2014 to 2017, before becoming the director of Geospatial Intelligence Systems Acquisition at the NRO.[^cite_note-auto2-5] In October 2020, he was appointed the principal deputy director of the NRO by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md).[^cite_note-6] In this position, he oversaw a budget of over \$15 billion to develop satellite capabilities.[^cite_note-auto-3]

On January 16, 2025, President-elect Trump announced Meink as the nominee to serve as [United States Secretary of the Air Force](305210.md).[^cite_note-auto-3] Meink's appointment had been quietly encouraged by [Elon Musk](909036.md).[^cite_note-nyt-swan-musk-feb-25-7] His nomination was confirmed by the Senate in a 74–25 vote on May 13,[^cite_note-8] and he was sworn in on May 16, 2025.[^cite_note-9]

Meink disclosed at an [Air & Space Forces Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_&_Space_Forces_Association) conference on September 14, 2026 that the [United States](3434750.md) had already deployed via the [U.S. Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Space_Force) active "[on-orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_orbits#Common_abbreviations) space control [weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weapon) capable of defending the [joint force](32212.md) against hostile adversary action".[^cite_note-AFA_Meink_2026-09-14-10]
