The **British Army** is the [land warfare force](1376.md) of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) responsible for defending the UK, the [British Overseas Territories](207114.md) and [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies). The British Army, founded in [1707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707), has [seen involvement in most of the world's major wars throughout history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_Kingdom), including both [world wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_wars).

The British Army traces back to 1707 and the [formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707) of the united [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) which joined the Kingdoms of [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England) and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland) into a [single state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_union) and, with that, united the [English Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Army) and the [Scots Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_Army) as the British Army.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England) [Bill of Rights 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_of_Rights_1689) and [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_the_Estates) [Claim of Right Act 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claim_of_Right_Act_1689) require parliamentary consent for [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown) to maintain a peacetime [standing army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army).[^cite_note-10] Members of the British Army swear allegiance to the [monarch](125248.md) as their [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief).[^cite_note-\:2-11] The army is administered by the [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) and commanded by the [Chief of the General Staff](691314.md).[^cite_note-\:3-12]

At its inception, being composed primarily of cavalry and infantry, the British Army was one of two *Regular* Forces (there were also separate *Reserve Forces*) within the British military (those parts of the British Armed Forces tasked with land warfare, as opposed to the naval forces),[^cite_note-13] with the other having been the *Ordnance Military Corps* (made up of the [Royal Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Artillery), [Royal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Engineers), and the [Royal Sappers and Miners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Sappers_and_Miners)) of the [Board of Ordnance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Ordnance), which along with the originally civilian [Commissariat Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissariat#19th_century), stores and supply departments, as well as barracks and other departments, were absorbed into the British Army when the Board of Ordnance was abolished in 1855. Various other civilian departments of the board were absorbed into the [War Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Office).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

The British Army has seen action in major wars between the world's [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power), including the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) and the [First](4764461.md) and [Second World Wars](32927.md). Britain's victories in most of these decisive wars allowed it to influence world events and establish itself as one of the world's leading [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) and [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy) powers.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Since the end of the [Cold War](325329.md), the British Army has been deployed to a number of conflict zones, often as part of an [expeditionary force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare), a [coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition) force or part of a [United Nations peacekeeping](15652027.md) operation.[^cite_note-18]

## History

Main article: [History of the British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_British_Army)

### Background

Until the [Wars of the Three Kingdoms (1639–1653)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Three_Kingdoms), neither England nor Scotland had a [standing army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army) with professional officers and career corporals and sergeants. England relied on [militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(English\)) organised by local officials or private forces mobilised by the nobility, or on hired mercenaries from Europe.[^cite_note-19] From the [later Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Middle_Ages) until the Wars of the Three Kingdoms, when a foreign expeditionary force was needed, such as the one that King [Henry V of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V_of_England) took to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) and that fought at the [Battle of Agincourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt) (1415), the army, a professional one, was raised for the duration of the expedition.[^cite_note-20]





Lord Protector [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) (left) Lord General [Thomas Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fairfax), the first commander of the New Model Army (right)

During the Wars of the Three Kingdoms, the members of the English [Long Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Parliament) realised that the use of county militia organised into regional associations (such as the [Eastern Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Association)), often commanded by local members of Parliament (both from the House of Commons and the House of Lords), while more than able to hold their own in the regions which [Parliamentarians ('Roundheads")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentarians_\(English_Civil_War\)) controlled, were unlikely to win the war. So Parliament initiated two actions. The [Self-denying Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-denying_Ordinance) forbade members of Parliament (with the notable exception of [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell), then a member of parliament and future [Lord Protector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Protector)) from serving as officers in the Parliamentary armies. This created a distinction between the civilians in Parliament, who tended to be [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism) and conciliatory to the Royalists ("Cavaliers") in nature, and a corps of professional officers, who tended to be Independent ([Congregational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational)) in theology. The second action was legislation for the creation of a Parliamentary-funded army, commanded by Lord General [Thomas Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fairfax), which became known as the [New Model Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army) (originally phrased "new-modelled Army").[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERogers1968207–211-21]

While this proved to be a war-winning formula, the New Model Army, being organised and politically active, went on to dominate the politics of the [Interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum_\(England\)) and by 1660 was widely disliked. The New Model Army was paid off and disbanded at the later [Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_\(1660\)) of the monarchy in 1660 with the accession of King [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England). For many decades the alleged excesses of the New Model Army under [the Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protectorate) / Commonwealth under Oliver Cromwell were used as propaganda (and still feature in Irish folklore)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEÓ_Siochrú20081–2-22] and the [Whig Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_\(England\)) element recoiled from allowing a standing army to continue with the agreed-upon rights and privileges under the return of a king.[^cite_note-23] The militia acts of 1661 and 1662 prevented local authorities from calling up militia and oppressing their own local opponents. Calling up the militia was possible only if the king and local elites agreed to do so.[^cite_note-24]

King Charles II and his "[Cavalier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalier)" / Royalist supporters favoured a new army under royal control, and immediately after the Restoration of 1660 to 1661 began working on its establishment.[^cite_note-25] The first [English Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Army#Restoration) regiments, including elements of the disbanded [New Model Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army), were formed between November 1660 and January 1661[^cite_note-26] and became a standing military force for England (financed by [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England)).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Clayton2014-28] The [Royal Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Scots_Army) and [Irish Armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Army_\(Kingdom_of_Ireland\)) were financed by the parliaments of [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Scotland) and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Ireland).[^cite_note-Glozier2007-29] Parliamentary control was established by the [Bill of Rights 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_of_Rights_1689) and [Claim of Right Act 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claim_of_Right_Act_1689), although the monarch continued to influence aspects of army administration until at least the end of the 19th century.[^cite_note-30]

After the Restoration, King Charles II pulled together four regiments of infantry and cavalry, calling them his guards, at a cost of £122,000 from his general budget. This became the foundation of the permanent English Army. By 1685, it had grown to number 7,500 soldiers in marching regiments, and 1,400 men permanently stationed in garrisons. A [Monmouth Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouth_Rebellion) in 1685 allowed successor King [James II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England) to raise the forces to 20,000 men. There were 37,000 in 1678, when England played a role in the closing stage of the cross-channel [Franco-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Dutch_War). After [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) dual Monarchs [William III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_England), formerly William of the Dutch [House of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Orange), and his wife [Mary II's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_II_of_England) joint accession to the throne after a short constitutional crisis with Parliament sending Mary's father, predecessor King James II, (who remained a Catholic) during his brief controversial reign, off the throne and into exile. England then involved itself in the [War of the Grand Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Grand_Alliance) on the Continent, primarily to prevent a possible French Catholic monarch organizing an invasion restoring the exiled James II (Queen Mary's father and still a [Roman Catholic](606848.md)).[^cite_note-31] Later in 1689, William III to solidify his and Mary's hold on the monarchy, expanded the new English army to 74,000, and then a few years later to 94,000 in 1694. Parliament was very nervous and reduced the cadre to 70,000 in 1697. Scotland and Ireland had theoretically separate military establishments, but they were unofficially merged with the English Crown force.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Oil-on-canvas portrait

[John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Churchill,_1st_Duke_of_Marlborough), was one of the first generals in the new British Army and fought in the War of the Spanish Succession. He was a noted ancestor of Sir [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), later famous [Prime Minister](24150.md) during [World War II](32927.md).

### Formation

By the time of the 1707 [Acts of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707), many regiments of the English and Scottish armies were already combined under one operational command and stationed in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) for the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession). Although all the regiments were now part of the new British military establishment,[^cite_note-Chandler2003-3] they remained under the old operational-command structure and retained much of the institutional ethos, customs and traditions of the standing armies created shortly after the [Restoration of the Monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_\(England\)) 47 years earlier. The order of seniority of the most-senior British Army line regiments is based on that of the earlier English army. Although technically the Scots [Royal Regiment of Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Regiment_of_Foot) was raised in 1633 and is the oldest Regiment of the Line,[^cite_note-34] Scottish and Irish regiments were only allowed to take a rank in the English army on the date of their arrival in England (or the date when they were first placed on the English establishment). In 1694, a board of general officers was convened to decide the rank of English, Irish and Scots regiments serving in the Netherlands; the regiment which became known as the [Scots Greys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_Greys) were designated the [4th Dragoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Royal_Irish_Dragoon_Guards) because there were three English regiments raised prior to 1688 when the Scots Greys were first placed in the English establishment. In 1713, when a new board of general officers was convened to decide the rank of several regiments, the seniority of the Scots Greys was reassessed and based on their June 1685 entry into England. At that time there was only one English regiment of dragoons, and the Scots Greys eventually received the British Army rank of 2nd Dragoons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyal_Scots_Greys184056–57-35]

### British Empire (1707–1914)

Main articles: [British Army during the Georgian Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Georgian_Era), [British Army during the American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_American_Revolutionary_War), [British Army during the Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Napoleonic_Wars), and [British Army during the Victorian Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Victorian_Era)

After 1707, British continental policy was to contain expansion by competing powers such as France and Spain. Although Spain was the dominant global power during the previous two centuries and the chief threat to England's early trans-Atlantic colonial ambitions, its influence was now waning. The territorial ambitions of the French, however, led to the War of the Spanish Succession[^cite_note-36] and the later [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars).[^cite_note-ma165-37]

Although the [Royal Navy](26061.md) is widely regarded as vital to the rise of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), the British Army played an important role in the formation of colonies, [protectorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) and [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) in the [Americas](29833.md), [Africa](5334607.md), [Asia](689.md), [India](14533.md) and [Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia).[^cite_note-38] British soldiers captured strategically important sites and territories, with the army involved in wars to secure the empire's borders, internal safety and support friendly governments and princes. Among these actions were the French and Indian War / Seven Years' War,[^cite_note-ma106-39] the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War),[^cite_note-40] the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars),[^cite_note-ma165-37] the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Opium_War) and [Second Opium Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War),[^cite_note-ma102-41] the [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion),[^cite_note-boxer-42] the [New Zealand Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Wars),[^cite_note-\:0-43] the [Australian frontier wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_frontier_wars),[^cite_note-44] the [Sepoy Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857),[^cite_note-ma210-45] the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Boer_War) and [second Boer Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War),[^cite_note-ma257-46] the [Fenian raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenian_raids),[^cite_note-fenian-47] the [Irish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence),[^cite_note-ma282-48] interventions in [Afghanistan](737.md) (intended to maintain a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state) between [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) and the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire))[^cite_note-49] and the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) (to keep the Russian Empire to the north on the [Black Sea](3386.md) at a safe distance by aiding the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md)).[^cite_note-ma195-50] Like the [English Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Army), the British Army fought the kingdoms of Spain, France (including the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire)) and the Netherlands ([Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic)) for supremacy in [North America](21139.md) and the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies). With native and provincial and colonial assistance, the Army conquered [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) in the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War) (North American theatre) of the parallel [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War)[^cite_note-ma106-39] and suppressed a [Native / Indian North Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) uprising in [Pontiac's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War) around the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes).[^cite_note-51] The British Army was defeated in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), losing the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) but retaining [The Canadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadas) and [The Maritimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes) as in [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America), including [Bermuda](3460.md) (originally part of the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia), and which had been originally strongly sympathetic to the American colonial rebels early in the war).[^cite_note-52]

[Halifax, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) and [Bermuda](3460.md) were to become [Imperial fortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) (although Bermuda, being safer from attack over water and impervious to attack overland, quickly became the most important in British North America), along with [Malta](19137.md) and [Gibraltar](7607314.md), providing bases in the eastern [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) and [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) for [Royal Navy](26061.md) squadrons to control the oceans and trade routes, and heavily garrisoned by the British Army both for defence of the bases and to provide mobile military forces to work with the Navy in amphibious operations throughout their regions.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

Panoramic painting of the Battle of Waterloo

The [Duke of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington) and [Field Marshal von Blücher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal_von_Bl%C3%BCcher)'s triumph over [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I_of_France) at the [Battle of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Waterloo_\(painting,_Sadler_II\))

The British Army was heavily involved in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), participating in a number of campaigns in Europe (including continuous deployment in the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War)), the [Caribbean](18956035.md), North Africa and [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812). The war between the British and the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) of [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) stretched around the world; at its peak in 1813, the regular army contained over 250,000 men. A coalition of Anglo-Dutch and Prussian armies under the [Duke of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Wellington) and [Field Marshal von Blücher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal_von_Bl%C3%BCcher) finally defeated Napoleon at [Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo) in 1815.[^cite_note-58]

The English were involved politically and militarily in Ireland. The campaign of English republican Protector [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) involved uncompromising treatment of the Irish towns (most notably [Drogheda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drogheda) and [Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wexford)) which supported the Royalists during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War). The English Army (and the subsequent British Army) remained in Ireland primarily to suppress Irish revolts or disorder. In addition to its conflict with Irish nationalists, it was faced with the prospect of battling Anglo-Irish and [Ulster Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_people) in Ireland who were angered by unfavourable taxation of Irish produce imported into Britain. With other Irish groups, they raised a volunteer army and threatened to emulate the American colonists if their conditions were not met. Learning from their experience in America, the British government sought a political solution. The British Army fought Irish rebels—Protestant and Catholic—primarily in [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster) and [Leinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster) ([Wolfe Tone's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theobald_Wolfe_Tone) [United Irishmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Irishmen)) in the [1798 rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rebellion_of_1798).[^cite_note-59]

Painting of the Battle of Rorke's Drift, with a building burning

In the 1879 [Battle of Rorke's Drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rorke's_Drift), a small British force repelled an attack by overwhelming [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_Empire) forces; eleven [Victoria Crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Cross) were awarded for its defence.

In addition to battling the armies of other European empires (and its former colonies, the United States, in the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812)),[^cite_note-60] the British Army fought the Chinese in the First and Second [Opium Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_Wars)[^cite_note-ma102-41] and the [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion),[^cite_note-boxer-42] [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) tribes in the first of the New Zealand Wars,[^cite_note-\:0-43] [Nawab Shiraj-ud-Daula's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siraj_ud-Daulah) forces and [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) mutineers in the [Sepoy Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857),[^cite_note-ma257-46] the Boers in the first and second Boer Wars,[^cite_note-ma257-46] Irish [Fenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenian) in Canada during the [Fenian raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenian_raids)[^cite_note-fenian-47] and [Irish separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republican_Army) in the [Anglo-Irish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish_War).[^cite_note-ma102-41] The increasing demands of imperial expansion and the inadequacy and inefficiency of the underfunded British Army, [Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(United_Kingdom\)), Ordnance Military Corps, [Yeomanry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeomanry) and [Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Force_\(Great_Britain\)) after the Napoleonic Wars led to series of reforms following the failures of the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War).[^cite_note-61]



Establishment and strength of the British Army, excluding Indian native troops stationed in India, prior to August 1914

Inspired by the successes of the [Prussian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Army) (which relied on short-term conscription of all eligible young men to maintain a large reserve of recently discharged soldiers, ready to be recalled on the outbreak of war to immediately bring the small peacetime regular army up to strength), the [*Regular Reserve*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\)#Army_Reserve_\(Regular\)) of the British Army was originally created in 1859 by [Secretary of State for War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War) [Sidney Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Herbert,_1st_Baron_Herbert_of_Lea), and re-organised under the [Reserve Force Act 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Force_Act_1867). Prior to this, a soldier was generally enlisted into the British Army for a 21-year engagement, following which (should he survive so long) he was discharged as a Pensioner. Pensioners were sometimes still employed on garrison duties, as were younger soldiers no longer deemed fit for expeditionary service who were generally organised in invalid units or returned to the regimental depot for home service. The cost of paying pensioners, and the obligation the government was under to continue to employ invalids as well as soldiers deemed by their commanding officers as detriments to their units were motivations to change this system. The long period of engagement also discouraged many potential recruits. The long service enlistments were consequently replaced with short service enlistments, with undesirable soldiers not permitted to re-engage on the completion of their first engagement. The size of the army also fluctuated greatly, increasing in war time, and drastically shrinking with peace. Battalions posted on garrison duty overseas were allowed an increase on their normal peacetime establishment, which resulted in their having surplus men on their return to a *Home* station. Consequently, soldiers engaging on short term enlistments were enabled to serve several years with the colours and the remainder in the Regular Reserve, remaining liable for recall to the colours if required. Among the other benefits, this thereby enabled the British Army to have a ready pool of recently trained men to draw upon in an emergency. The name of the Regular Reserve (which for a time was divided into a *First Class* and a *Second Class*) has resulted in confusion with the *Reserve Forces*, which were the pre-existing part-time, local-service home-defence [forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_the_British_Army#British_Army_units_in_other_areas_of_the_British_Armed_Forces) that were auxiliary to the British Army (or *Regular Force*), but not originally part of it: the [Yeomanry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeomanry), [Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(United_Kingdom\)) (or *Constitutional Force*) and [Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Force). These were consequently also referred to as *Auxiliary Forces* or *Local Forces*.[^cite_note-62]

The late-19th-century [Cardwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardwell_Reforms) and [Childers Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childers_Reforms) gave the army its modern shape and redefined its [regimental system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regimental_system).[^cite_note-63] The 1907 [Haldane Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldane_Reforms) created the [Territorial Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Force) as the army's volunteer reserve component, merging and reorganising the Volunteer Force and Yeomanry, while the Militia was replaced by the Special Reserve.[^cite_note-64]

### World Wars (1914–1945)

Main articles: [British Army during the First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_First_World_War) and [British Army during the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Second_World_War)

Early First World War tank, with soldiers in a trench next to it

British First World War [Mark I tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_I_tank); the guidance wheels behind the main body were later scrapped as unnecessary. Armoured vehicles of the era required considerable infantry and artillery support. *(Photo by [Ernest Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Brooks_\(photographer\)))*

Bagpiper leading a line of soldiers though thigh-high growth

Led by their piper, men of the 7th Battalion, [Seaforth Highlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaforth_Highlanders) (part of the [46th (Highland) Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_Infantry_Brigade_\(United_Kingdom\))), advance through Normandy during [Operation Epsom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Epsom) on 26 June 1944

Great Britain was challenged by other powers, primarily the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and [Nazi Germany](21212.md), during the 20th century. A century earlier it vied with Napoleonic France for global pre-eminence, and [Hanoverian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hanover) Britain's natural allies were the kingdoms and principalities of [northern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Germany). By the middle of the 19th century, Britain and France were allies in preventing Russia's appropriation of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), although the fear of French invasion led shortly afterwards to the creation of the Volunteer Force. By the first decade of the 20th century, the United Kingdom was allied with France (by the [Entente Cordiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_Cordiale)) and Russia (which had a secret agreement with France for mutual support in a war against the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia)-led German Empire and the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire)).[^cite_note-65]

When the First World War broke out in August 1914 the British Army sent the [British Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Expeditionary_Force_\(World_War_I\)) (BEF), consisting mainly of [regular army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army) troops, to [France and Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)).[^cite_note-66] The fighting bogged down into static [trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare) for the remainder of the war. In 1915 the army created the [Mediterranean Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Expeditionary_Force) to invade the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) via [Gallipoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_Campaign), an unsuccessful attempt to capture [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) and secure a sea route to [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm19113-67]

The First World War was the most devastating in [British military history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_Kingdom), with nearly 800,000 men killed and over two million wounded. Early in the war, the BEF was virtually destroyed and was replaced first by [volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchener's_Army) and then by a [conscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_the_United_Kingdom) force. Major battles included those at [the Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme) and [Passchendaele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Passchendaele).[^cite_note-68] Advances in technology saw the advent of the [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank)[^cite_note-69] (and the creation of the [Royal Tank Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Tank_Regiment)) and advances in aircraft design (and the creation of the [Royal Flying Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Flying_Corps)) which would be decisive in future battles.[^cite_note-70] Trench warfare dominated Western Front strategy for most of the war, and the use of [chemical weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_weapons_in_World_War_I) (disabling and poison gases) added to the devastation.[^cite_note-71]

The [Second World War](32927.md) broke out in September 1939 with the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(Wehrmacht\))'s [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland).[^cite_note-ma335-72] British [assurances to the Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Polish_alliance#British_assurance_to_Poland) led the British Empire to declare war on [Germany](21212.md). As in the First World War, a relatively small [BEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Expeditionary_Force_\(World_War_II\)) was sent to France[^cite_note-ma335-72] but then hastily evacuated from [Dunkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation) as the German forces [swept through the Low Countries and across France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France) in May 1940.[^cite_note-ma342-73]

After the British Army recovered from its earlier defeats, it defeated the Germans and Italians at the [Second Battle of El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein) in [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Campaign) in 1942–1943 and helped drive them from Africa. It then fought through [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Campaign_\(World_War_II\))[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETaylor1976157-74] and, with the participation of American, Canadian, Australian, New Zealand, Indian and Free French forces,[^cite_note-75] was one of the principal organisers alongside the U.S. Army and participant in the [D-Day invasion of Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_landings) on 6 June 1944; nearly half the Allied soldiers were British.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGilbert2005301-76] In the [Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Asian_theatre_of_World_War_II), the British Army rallied against the Japanese in the [Burma Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign) and regained the British Far Eastern colonial possessions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETaylor1976210-77]

### Postcolonial era (1945–2000)



1945 Order of Precedence of the British Army

After the Second World War the British Army was significantly reduced in size, although [National Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Service) continued until 1960.[^cite_note-M_384-78] This period saw [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation) begin with the [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) and [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) of India and Pakistan, followed by the independence of British colonies in Africa and Asia.

The [Corps Warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_Warrant), which is the official list of which bodies of the British Military (not to be confused with *naval*) Forces were to be considered Corps of the British Army for the purposes of the [Army Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Act), the Reserve Forces Act, 1882, and the Territorial and Reserve Forces Act, 1907, had not been updated since 1926 (Army Order 49 of 1926), although amendments had been made up to and including Army Order 67 of 1950. A new Corps Warrant was declared in 1951.

Although the British Army was a major participant in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Commonwealth_Division) in the early 1950s[^cite_note-M_384-78] and [Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis) in 1956,[^cite_note-79] during this period Britain's role in world events was reduced and the army was downsized.[^cite_note-80] The [British Army of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_of_the_Rhine), consisting of [I (BR) Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Corps_\(United_Kingdom\)), remained in Germany as a bulwark against Soviet invasion.[^cite_note-81] The [Cold War](325329.md) continued, with significant technological advances in warfare, and the army saw the introduction of new weapons systems.[^cite_note-82] Despite the decline of the British Empire, the army was engaged in [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Emergency),[^cite_note-ma401-83] [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konfrontasi), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EOKA),[^cite_note-ma401-83] [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mau_Mau_Uprising)[^cite_note-ma401-83] and [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency).[^cite_note-84] In 1982, the British Army and the [Royal Marines](93985.md) helped liberate the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) during the [conflict with Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) after that country's invasion of the British territory.[^cite_note-85]

In the three decades following 1969, the army was heavily deployed in [Northern Ireland](21265.md)'s [Operation Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Banner) to support the [Royal Ulster Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ulster_Constabulary) (later the [Police Service of Northern Ireland](294068.md)) in their conflict with republican paramilitary groups.[^cite_note-86] The locally recruited [Ulster Defence Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Defence_Regiment) was formed, becoming home-service battalions of the [Royal Irish Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Irish_Regiment_\(27th_\(Inniskilling\)_83rd_and_87th_and_Ulster_Defence_Regiment\)) in 1992 before it was disbanded in 2006. Over 700 soldiers were killed during [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles). Following the 1994–1996 [IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army) ceasefires and since 1997, demilitarisation has been part of the peace process and the military presence has been reduced.[^cite_note-87] On 25 June 2007 the 2nd Battalion of the [Princess of Wales's Royal Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_of_Wales's_Royal_Regiment) left the army complex in [Bessbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles_in_Bessbrook), [County Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Armagh), ending the longest operation in British Army history.[^cite_note-88]

#### Persian Gulf War

Main articles: [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and [Operation Granby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Granby)

An armoured personnel carrier flying the Union Jack

British APC passing by wrecked and abandoned vehicles along the "[Highway of Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_of_Death)" in 1991.

The British Army contributed 50,000 troops to the coalition which fought [Iraq](7515928.md) in the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War),[^cite_note-89] and British forces controlled [Kuwait](7515890.md) after its liberation. Forty-seven British military personnel died during the war.[^cite_note-90]

#### Balkan conflicts



British Army vehicles in a staging area before being deployed to Bosnia

Main article: [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars)

The army was deployed to former [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) in 1992. Initially part of the [United Nations Protection Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Protection_Force),[^cite_note-91] in 1995 its command was transferred to the [Implementation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implementation_Force) (IFOR) and then to the [Stabilisation Force in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilisation_Force_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (SFOR);[^cite_note-92] the commitment rose to over 10,000 troops. In 1999, British forces under SFOR command were sent to [Kosovo](17391.md) and the contingent increased to 19,000 troops.[^cite_note-93] Between early 1993 and June 2010, 72 British military personnel died during operations in the former Yugoslavian countries of Bosnia, Kosovo and Macedonia.[^cite_note-\:6-94]

#### The Troubles

Although there have been permanent garrisons in Northern Ireland throughout its history, the British Army was deployed as a peacekeeping force from 1969 to 2007 in [Operation Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Banner).[^cite_note-95] Initially, this was (in the wake of [unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionism_in_Ireland) attacks on nationalist communities in [Derry](9055.md)[^cite_note-96] and [Belfast](5046.md))[^cite_note-97] to prevent further loyalist attacks on Catholic communities; it developed into support of the [Royal Ulster Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ulster_Constabulary) (RUC) and its successor, the [Police Service of Northern Ireland](294068.md) (PSNI) against the [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army) (PIRA).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKernan200517-98] Under the 1998 [Good Friday Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Agreement), there was a gradual reduction in the number of soldiers deployed.[^cite_note-99] In 2005, after the PIRA declared a ceasefire, the British Army dismantled posts, withdrew many troops and restored troop levels to those of a peacetime garrison.[^cite_note-100]

Operation Banner ended at midnight on 31 July 2007 after about 38 years of continuous deployment, the longest in British Army history.[^cite_note-analysis-101] According to an internal document released in 2007, the British Army had failed to defeat the IRA but made it impossible for them to win by violence. Operation Helvetic replaced Operation Banner in 2007, maintaining fewer service personnel in a more-benign environment.[^cite_note-analysis-101][^cite_note-BBC-102] Of the 300,000 troops who served in Northern Ireland since 1969, there were 763 British military personnel killed[^cite_note-103] and 306 killed by the British military, mostly civilians.[^cite_note-104] An estimated 100 soldiers committed suicide during Operation Banner or soon afterwards and a similar number died in accidents. A total of 6,116 were wounded.[^cite_note-105]

**Sierra Leone**

Main article: [British military intervention in the Sierra Leone Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_military_intervention_in_the_Sierra_Leone_Civil_War)

The British Army deployed to Sierra Leone for [Operation Palliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Palliser) in 1999, under United Nations resolutions, to aid the government in quelling violent uprisings by militiamen. British troops also provided support during the 2014 [West African Ebola virus epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Ebola_virus_epidemic).[^cite_note-106]

### Recent history (2000–present)

#### War in Afghanistan

Main article: [War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md)

Armed soldiers in and around a military vehicle

[Royal Anglian Regiment](1306783.md) in Helmand Province

In November 2001, as part of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) with the United States, the United Kingdom deployed forces in [Afghanistan](737.md) to topple the [Taliban](30635.md) in [Operation Herrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Herrick).[^cite_note-107] The [3rd Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Division_\(United_Kingdom\)) were sent to [Kabul](16826.md) to assist in the liberation of the capital and defeat Taliban forces in the mountains. In 2006 the British Army began concentrating on fighting Taliban forces and bringing security to [Helmand Province](457235.md), with about 9,500 British troops (including marines, airmen and sailors) deployed at its peak[^cite_note-108]—the second-largest force after that of the US.[^cite_note-109] In December 2012 Prime Minister [David Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cameron) announced that the combat mission would end in 2014, and troop numbers gradually fell as the [Afghan National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Army) took over the brunt of the fighting. Between 2001 and 26 April 2014 a total of 453 British military personnel died in Afghan operations.[^cite_note-110] Operation Herrick ended with the handover of [Camp Bastion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Bastion) on 26 October 2014,[^cite_note-111] but the British Army maintained a deployment in Afghanistan as part of [Operation Toral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Toral).[^cite_note-toral-112]

Following an announcement by the US Government of the end of their operations in the Afghanistan, the Ministry of Defence announced in April 2021 that British forces would withdraw from the country by 11 September 2021.[^cite_note-113] It was later reported that all UK troops would be out by early July.[^cite_note-auto-114] Following the collapse of the Afghan Army, and the completion of the withdrawal of civilians, all British troops had left by the end of August 2021.[^cite_note-115]

#### Iraq War

Main articles: [Iraq War](5043324.md) and [Operation Telic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Telic)

Two soldiers with a mortar gun—one loading and the other aiming

British soldiers from the [1st Battalion, Royal Regiment of Fusiliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Regiment_of_Fusiliers) [battlegroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlegroup_\(army\)) engage Iraqi positions with an [81mm mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81mm_mortar) [south of Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Basra_\(2003\))

In 2003, the United Kingdom was a major contributor to the [invasion of Iraq](201936.md), sending a force of over 46,000 military personnel. The British Army controlled southern Iraq, and maintained a peace-keeping presence in [Basra](57580.md).[^cite_note-116] All British troops were withdrawn from Iraq by 30 April 2009, after the Iraqi government refused to extend their mandate.[^cite_note-117] One hundred and seventy-nine British military personnel died in Iraqi operations.[^cite_note-\:6-94] The [British Armed Forces](31730.md) returned to Iraq in 2014 as part of [Operation Shader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shader) to counter the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (ISIL).[^cite_note-118]

#### Recent military aid

Main articles: [Operation Rescript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rescript), [Operation Temperer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Temperer), and [Military Aid to the Civil Authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Aid_to_the_Civil_Authorities)

The British Army maintains a standing liability to support the civil authorities in certain circumstances, usually in either niche capabilities (e.g. explosive ordnance removal) or in general support of the civil authorities when their capacity is exceeded.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] In recent years this has been seen as army personnel supporting the civil authorities in the face of the [2001 United Kingdom foot-and-mouth outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_foot-and-mouth_outbreak), the 2002 firefighters strike, widespread flooding in 2005, 2007, 2009, 2013 and 2014, [Operation Temperer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Temperer) following the [Manchester Arena bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Arena_bombing) in 2017 and, most recently, [Operation Rescript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rescript) during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-OpRESCRIPT-122]

#### Baltic states

Since 2016, the British Army has maintained a presence in the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) in support of the [NATO Enhanced Forward Presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Enhanced_Forward_Presence) strategy which responded to the [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md). The British Army leads a multinational armoured battlegroup in [Estonia](28222445.md) under Operation Cabrit and contributes troops to another military battle group in [Poland](22936.md).[^cite_note-efp-123] As part of the NATO plans, Britain has committed a full mechanized infantry [brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) to be on a high state of readiness to defend Estonia.[^cite_note-124]

#### Ukraine

Between 2015 and 2022, the British Army deployed Short Term Training Teams (STTTs) to [Ukraine](31750.md) under [Operation Orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Orbital) to help train the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) against further Russian aggression.[^cite_note-125] This operation was succeeded by [Operation Interflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Interflex) in July 2022.[^cite_note-politico1-126]

## Modern army

### Personnel



The [Blues and Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_and_Royals) [Trooping the Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trooping_the_Colour) in 2013

The British Army has been a volunteer force since national service ended during the 1960s.[^cite_note-M_384-78] Since the creation of the part-time, reserve [Territorial Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Force) in 1908 (renamed the Territorial Army in 1921 and the Army Reserve in 2014), the full-time British Army has been known as the Regular Army. In July 2020 there were just over 78,800 Regulars, with a target strength of 82,000, and just over 30,000 [Army Reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\)), with a target strength of 30,000.[^cite_note-statistics2021-127] All former Regular Army personnel may also be recalled to duty in exceptional circumstances during the 6-year period following completion of their Regular service, which creates an additional force known as the [Regular Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\)#Army_Reserve_\(Regular\)).[^cite_note-128]

As of January 2025, the British Army had 73,847 regular soldiers, 4,127 Gurkhas, and 25,742 volunteer reserve personnel.[^cite_note-stats-7] Under the recommendations of the 2025 [Strategic Defence Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_Review_\(2025\)), the number of regular soldiers is planned to stop declining and eventually increase to 76,000.[^cite_note-129]

## Equipment

Main article: [List of equipment of the British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_British_Army)

### Infantry

The British Army's basic weapon is the [5.56 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5.56%C3%9745mm_NATO) [L85A2 or L85A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA80) assault rifle, with some specialist personnel using the L22A2 carbine variant (pilots and some tank crew). The weapon was traditionally equipped with either [iron sights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_sights) or an optical [SUSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUSAT), although other optical sights have been subsequently purchased to supplement these.[^cite_note-148] The weapon can be enhanced further utilising the [Picatinny rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picatinny_rail) with attachments such as the [L17A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L17A2_UGL) under-barrel grenade launcher.[^cite_note-\:8-149] In 2023, the [Army Special Operations Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Special_Operations_Brigade), which includes the [Ranger Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranger_Regiment_\(United_Kingdom\)), began using the [L403A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L403A1), an [AR-pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M16_rifle) rifle also used by the [Royal Marines](93985.md).[^cite_note-150] An initiative to find a replacement for the SA80 family, known as [Project Grayburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Grayburn), was in the concept phase in 2025,[^cite_note-151] and is expected to enter the assessment phase in 2026.[^cite_note-152]

Some soldiers are equipped with the 7.62mm [L129A1 sharpshooter rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L129A1),[^cite_note-153] which in 2018 formally replaced the [L86A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L86_LSW) Light Support Weapon. Support fire is provided by the [L7 general-purpose machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L7_\(machine_gun\)) (GPMG),[^cite_note-154] and indirect fire is provided by [L16 81mm mortars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L16_81mm_mortar). Sniper rifles include the [L118A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accuracy_International_Arctic_Warfare) 7.62 mm, [L115A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L115A3) and the [AW50F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AW50F), all manufactured by [Accuracy International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accuracy_International).[^cite_note-155] The British Army utilises the [Glock 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glock) as its side arm.[^cite_note-\:8-149]

Anti tank guided weapons include the [Javelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FGM-148_Javelin), the medium range anti-tank guided weapon replacement for [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MILAN), with overfly and direct attack modes of operation, and the [NLAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLAW). The Next-generation Light Anti-tank Weapon ([NLAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLAW)) is the first, non-expert, short-range, anti-tank missile that rapidly knocks out any main battle tank in just one shot by striking it from above.[^cite_note-156]

### Armour

The army's [main battle tank](26595097.md) is the [Challenger 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_2), which is being upgraded to [Challenger 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_3).[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] It is supported by the [Warrior tracked armoured vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrior_tracked_armoured_vehicle) as the primary [infantry fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle),[^cite_note-159] (which will soon be replaced by the [Boxer 8x8 armoured fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_\(armoured_fighting_vehicle\))) and the [Bulldog armoured personnel carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FV430_series).[^cite_note-160] The [Ajax armoured fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_Ajax) is also being brought into service. Light armoured units often utilise the [Supacat "Jackal" MWMIK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MWMIK) and [Coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal_\(vehicle\)#Coyote) tactical support vehicle for reconnaissance and fire support.[^cite_note-161]

### Artillery

The army has three main artillery systems: the [M270 multiple launch rocket system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_multiple_launch_rocket_system) (MLRS), the [Archer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_artillery_system) and the [L118 light gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L118_light_gun).[^cite_note-162] The MLRS, first used in [Operation Granby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Granby), has an 85-kilometre (53 mi) standard range, or with the [PrSM](71176097.md), up to 500 km.[^cite_note-163] The Archer is a 155 mm self-propelled armoured gun with a 50-kilometre (31 mi) range. The L118 light gun is a 105 mm towed gun, which is typically towed by a [Pinzgauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steyr-Puch_Pinzgauer) all-terrain vehicle.[^cite_note-164] The army plans to replace the Archer with the [RCH 155](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCH_155) in the near future.[^cite_note-165]

To identify artillery targets, the army operates the TAIPAN artillery detection radar[^cite_note-166] and utilises [artillery sound ranging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_sound_ranging).[^cite_note-167] For air defence it uses the new [Sky Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sabre) system, which in 2021 replaced the [Rapier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapier_\(missile\)).[^cite_note-whatdotheyknow\.com-168] It also deploys the Very Short-Range Air Defence (VSHORAD) [Starstreak HVM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starstreak_\(missile\)) (high-velocity missile) launched by a single soldier or from a [Stormer HVM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvis_Stormer) vehicle-mounted launcher.[^cite_note-169]

### Protected mobility

Where armour is not required or mobility and speed are favoured the British Army utilises protected patrol vehicles, such as the Panther variant of the [Iveco LMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveco_LMV), the [Foxhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocelot_\(vehicle\)), and variants of the [Cougar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar_\(MRAP\)) family (such as the Ridgeback, Husky and Mastiff).[^cite_note-170] For day-to-day utility work the army commonly uses the [Land Rover Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover_Wolf), which is based on the [Land Rover Defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover_Defender).[^cite_note-171]

### Engineers, utility and signals

Specialist engineering vehicles include bomb-disposal robots such as the [T7 Multi-Mission Robotic System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L3Harris) and the modern variants of the [Armoured Vehicle Royal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_Vehicle_Royal_Engineers), including the Titan bridge-layer, [Trojan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Armoured_Vehicle_Royal_Engineers) armoured engineer vehicle, [Terrier armoured digger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrier_armoured_digger).[^cite_note-172] Day-to-day utility work uses a series of support vehicles, including six-, nine- and fifteen-tonne [MAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Truck_&_Bus) trucks, [Oshkosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh_Corporation) heavy-equipment transporters (HET), close-support tankers, quad bikes and ambulances.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] Tactical communication uses the [Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowman_\(communications_system\)) radio system, and operational or strategic communication is controlled by the [Royal Corps of Signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Corps_of_Signals).[^cite_note-175]

### Aviation

The [Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_Air_Corps) (AAC) provides direct aviation support, with the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) providing support helicopters. The primary attack helicopter is the [Boeing AH-64E Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-64E_Apache) which replaced the [AgustaWestland Apache AH-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_Apache) in the anti-tank, anti-air defence, and anti-armour role.[^cite_note-176] The [AgustaWestland AW159 Wildcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW159_Wildcat) is a dedicated [intelligence, surveillance, target acquisition, and reconnaissance](650971.md) (ISTAR) helicopter.[^cite_note-177] The [Eurocopter AS 365N Dauphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Dauphin) is used for [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations) aviation, primarily [counter terrorism](83392.md) operations, within the UK.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERipley200810-178] The army operates [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) in a surveillance role, such as the small [Lockheed Martin Desert Hawk III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_Desert_Hawk_III).[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

[Army Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Headquarters_\(United_Kingdom\)) is located in [Andover, Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover,_Hampshire), and is responsible for providing forces at operational readiness for employment by the [Permanent Joint Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Joint_Headquarters).[^cite_note-\:3-12] The command structure is hierarchical, with overall command residing with the [Chief of the General Staff](691314.md) (CGS), who is immediately subordinate to The Chief of Defence Staff, the head of the British Armed Services. The CGS is supported by the [Deputy Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chief_of_the_General_Staff_\(United_Kingdom\)). Army Headquarters is further organised into two subordinate commands, [Field Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Army_\(United_Kingdom\)) and [Home Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_Home_Command), each commanded by a [lieutenant general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-general_\(United_Kingdom\)).[^cite_note-\:4-195] These two Commands serve distinct purposes and are divided into a structure of [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)) and [brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade), which themselves consist of a complex mix of smaller units such as [Battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion). British Army units are either full-time 'Regular' units, or part-time [Army Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\)) units.[^cite_note-196]

### Home Command

Main article: [Home Command (British Army)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Command_\(British_Army\))

Home Command is the British Army's supporting command; a generating, recruiting and training force that supports the Field Army and delivers UK resilience.[^cite_note-\:4-195] It comprises

- Army Personnel Centre, which deals with personnel issues and liaises with outside agencies.

  [^cite_note-198]

- Army Personnel Services Group, which supports personnel administration

  [^cite_note-\:4-195]

- [Army Recruiting and Initial Training Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Recruiting_and_Initial_Training_Command), which is responsible for all recruiting and training of Officers and Soldiers.

  [^cite_note-\:4-195]

- [London District Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_District_\(British_Army\)), which is the main headquarters for all British Army units within the M25 corridor of London. It also provides for London's ceremonial events as well as supporting operational deployments overseas.

When the [English Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Empire) was established in [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America) (including Bermuda), and the West Indies in the early 17th century there was no standing English Army, only the [Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(English\)), [Yeomanry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeomanry), and [Royal bodyguards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign's_Bodyguard), of which the Militia, as the primary home-defence force, was immediately extended to the colonies. [Colonial militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(British_Dominions_and_Crown_Colonies\)) defended colonies single-handedly at first against indigenous peoples and European competitors. Once the standing English Army, later the British Army, came into existence and began to garrison the colonies, the colonial militias fought side by side with it in a number of wars, including the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War). Some of the colonial militias rebelled during the [American War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). The militia fought alongside the regular British Army (and native allies) in defending [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America) from their former countrymen during the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812).[^cite_note-207]

Locally raised units in strategically located [Imperial fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) colonies (including: [Nova Scotia](21184.md) before the military defence, other than of the dockyard, passed to the new Dominion Government on [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) and the Royal Naval Dockyard there was transferred to the Dominion Government in 1905; [Bermuda](3460.md) – which was treated as part of [The Maritimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes) under the [Commander-in-Chief at Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_North_America#Commanders-in-Chief,_maritime_provinces,_1783%E2%80%931875) until the [Confederation of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_Canada), with its garrison, formerly commanded by a Major-General, becoming the [Bermuda Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Garrison) thereafter, under a General or Lieutenant General as [General Officer Commanding-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Officer_Commanding); [Gibraltar](7607314.md); and [Malta](19137.md)) and the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands) were generally maintained from army funds and more fully integrated into the British Army as evident from their placements in British Army lists, unlike units such as the [King's African Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_African_Rifles).[^cite_note-208]

The larger colonies (Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa, etc.) mostly achieved [Commonwealth Dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) status before or after the First World War and were granted full legislative independence in 1931. While remaining within the British Empire, this placed their governments on a par with the British government, and hence their military units comprised separate armies (e.g. the [Australian Army](2795.md)), although Canada retained the term "militia" for its military forces until the Second World War. From the 1940s, these dominions and many colonies chose full independence, usually becoming [Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm) (as member states of the Commonwealth are known today).[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

Units raised in [self-governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governing_colony) and [Crown colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony) (those without local elected [Legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislatures), as was the case with [British Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong)) that are part of the British realm remain under British Government control. ### Levels of Command

The structure of the British Army beneath the level of Divisions and Brigades is also hierarchical and command is based on rank. The table below details how many units within the British Army are structured, although there can be considerable variation between individual units:[^cite_note-\:4-195]

Whilst many units are organised as Battalions or Regiments administratively, the most common fighting unit is the combined arms unit known as a Battlegroup. This is formed around a combat unit and supported by units (or sub-units) from other capabilities. An example of a battlegroup would be two companies of armoured infantry (e.g. from the 1st Battalion of the [Mercian Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercian_Regiment)), one squadron of heavy armour (e.g. A Squadron of the [Royal Tank Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Tank_Regiment)), a company of engineers (e.g. B Company of the 22nd Engineer Regiment), a Battery of artillery (e.g. D Battery of the 1st Regiment of the [Royal Horse Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Horse_Artillery)) and smaller attachments from medical, logistic and intelligence units. Typically organised and commanded by a battlegroup headquarters and named after the unit which provided the most combat units, in this example, it would be the 1 Mercian Battlegroup. This creates a self-sustaining mixed formation of armour, infantry, artillery, engineers and support units, commanded by a lieutenant colonel.[^cite_note-SAS-219]

## Recruitment

Main article: [Recruitment in the British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruitment_in_the_British_Army)

World War I recruiting poster, with Lord Kitchener pointing at the viewer

One of the most recognisable recruiting posters of the British Army; from [World War I](4764461.md), with [Lord Kitchener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Kitchener,_1st_Earl_Kitchener)

The British Army primarily recruits from within the United Kingdom, but accept applications from all British citizens. It also accepts applications from Irish citizens and [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) citizens, with certain restrictions.[^cite_note-\:5-220] Since 2018 the British Army has been an equal-opportunity employer (with some legal exceptions due to medical standards), and does not discriminate based on race, religion or sexual orientation.[^cite_note-221] Applicants for the Regular Army must be a minimum age of 16, although soldiers under 18 may not serve in operations, and the maximum age is 36. Applicants for the Army Reserve must be a minimum of 17 years and 9 months, and a maximum age of 43. Different age limits apply for Officers and those in some specialist roles. Applicants must also meet several other requirements, notably regarding medical health, physical fitness, past-criminal convictions, education, and regarding any tattoos and piercings.[^cite_note-\:5-220]

Soldiers and officers in the Regular Army now enlist for an initial period of 12 years, with options to extend if they meet certain requirements. Soldiers and officers are normally required to serve for a minimum of 4 years from date of enlistment and must give 12 months' notice before leaving; soldiers who joined before the age of 18 years old are normally required to serve for a minimum of 6 years.[^cite_note-222]

### Oath of allegiance

All soldiers and commissioned officers must take an oath of allegiance upon joining the Army, a process known as attestation. Those who wish to swear by [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Abrahamic_religions) use the following words:[^cite_note-\:2-11]

> I, \[soldier's or commissioned officer's name], swear by Almighty God that I will be faithful and bear true allegiance to [His Majesty King Charles III](125248.md), his heirs and successors and that I will as in duty bound honestly and faithfully defend His Majesty, his heirs and successors in person, [crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Crown) and dignity against all enemies and will observe and obey all orders of His Majesty, his heirs, and successors and of the generals and officers set over me.[^cite_note-223]

Others replace the words "swear by Almighty God" with "solemnly, sincerely and truly declare and affirm".[^cite_note-\:2-11]

### Training

Red-brick buildings with large windows

New College buildings at Royal Military Academy Sandhurst

See also: [Selection and Training in the British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selection_and_Training_in_the_British_Army) and [List of British Army installations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_Army_installations)

Candidates for the Army undergo common training, beginning with [initial military training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training), to bring all personnel to a similar standard in basic military skills, which is known as Phase 1 training. They then undertake further specialist trade-training for their specific Regiment or Corps, known as Phase 2 training. After completing Phase 1 training a soldier is counted against the Army's trained strength, and upon completion of Phase 2 are counted against the Army's fully trained trade strength.[^cite_note-boot-224]

Soldiers under the age of 17 and six months will complete Phase 1 training at the [Army Foundation College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Foundation_College).[^cite_note-225] Infantry Soldiers will complete combined Phase 1 & 2 training at the [Infantry Training Centre, Catterick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_Training_Centre_\(British_Army\)), whilst all other Soldiers will attend Phase 1 training at the [Army Training Centre Pirbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Training_Centre_Pirbright) or [Army Training Regiment, Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_training_regiment), and then complete Phase 2 training at different locations depending on their specialism.[^cite_note-boot-224] Officers conduct their initial training at the [Royal Military Academy Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst) (RMAS),[^cite_note-226] before also completing their Phase 2 training at multiple different locations.[^cite_note-boot-224]

## Reserve forces

Main article: [Army Reserve (United Kingdom)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Reserve_\(United_Kingdom\))

The oldest of the Reserve Forces was the [Militia Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(Great_Britain\)) (also referred to as the *Constitutional Force*),[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] which (in the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England), prior to 1707) was originally the main military defensive force (until the 1645 creation of the [New Model Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army), there otherwise were originally only Royal bodyguards, including the [Yeomen Warders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeomen_Warders) and the [Yeomen of the Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeomen_of_the_Guard), with armies raised only temporarily for expeditions overseas), made up of civilians embodied for annual training or emergencies, which had used various schemes of compulsory service during different periods of its long existence. From the 1850s it recruited volunteers who engaged for terms of service. The Militia was originally an all-infantry force, though [Militia coastal artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_Artillery_units_of_the_United_Kingdom_and_Colonies), field artillery, and engineers units were introduced from the 1850s.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-249]

[Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Force) units were also frequently raised during wartime and disbanded upon peace. This was re-established as a permanent (i.e., in war and peace) part of the Reserve Forces in 1859. It differed from the Militia in a number of ways, most particularly in that volunteers did not commit to a term service, and were able to resign with fourteen days notice (except while embodied). As volunteer soldiers were originally expected to fund the cost of their own equipment, few tended to come from the labouring class among whom the Militia primarily recruited.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251]

The [Yeomanry Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeomanry) was made up of mounted units, organised similarly to the Volunteer Force, first raised during the two decades of war with France that followed the French Revolution. As with the Volunteers, members of the Yeomanry were expected to foot much of the cost of their own equipment, including their horses, and the make-up of the units tended to be from more affluent classes.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

Although Militia regiments were linked with British Army regiments during the course of the Napoleonic Wars to feed volunteers for service abroad into the regular army, and volunteers from the Reserve Forces served abroad either individually or in contingents, service companies, or battalions in a succession of conflicts from the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) to the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War), personnel did not normally move between forces unless re-attested as a member of the new force, and units did not normally move from the Reserve Forces to become part of the Regular Forces, or vice versa. There were exceptions, however, as with the *New Brunswick Regiment of Fencible Infantry*, raised in 1803, which became the [104th (New Brunswick) Regiment of Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/104th_\(New_Brunswick\)_Regiment_of_Foot) when it was transferred to the British Army on 13 September 1810.[^cite_note-254]

Another type of reserve force was created during the period between the French Revolution and the end of the Napoleonic Wars. Called [Fencibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fencibles), these were disbanded after the Napoleonic Wars and not raised again, although the [Royal Malta Fencible Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Malta_Fencible_Regiment), later the *Royal Malta Fencible Artillery*, existed from 1815 until the 1880s when it became the [Royal Malta Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Malta_Artillery),[^cite_note-255] and the [Royal New Zealand Fencible Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Fencible_Corps) was formed in 1846.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257]

The Reserve Forces were raised locally (in Britain, under the control of [Lords-Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord-Lieutenant) of counties, and, in [British colonies](207114.md), under the [colonial governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor#United_Kingdom_overseas_territories), and members originally were obliged to serve only within their locality (which, in the United Kingdom, originally meant within the county or other recruitment area, but was extended to anywhere in Britain, though not overseas). They have consequently also been referred to as *Local Forces*. As they were (and in some cases *are*) considered separate forces from the British Army, though still within the British military, they have also been known as *Auxiliary Forces*. The Militia and Volunteer units of a colony were generally considered to be separate forces from the *Home* Militia Force and Volunteer Force in the United Kingdom, and from the Militia Forces and Volunteer Forces of other colonies. Where a colony had more than one Militia or Volunteer unit, they would be grouped as a Militia or Volunteer Force for that colony, such as the Jamaica Volunteer Defence Force. Officers of the Reserve Forces could not sit on Courts Martial of regular forces personnel. The [Mutiny Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutiny_Act) did not apply to members of the Reserve Forces. The *Reserve Forces* within the British Isles were increasingly integrated with the British Army through a succession of reforms (beginning with the [Cardwell Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardwell_Reforms)) of the British military forces over the last two decades of the Nineteenth Century and the early years of the Twentieth Century, whereby the Reserve Forces units mostly lost their own identities and became numbered Militia or Volunteer battalions of regular British Army corps or regiments.[^cite_note-258]

In 1908, the Yeomanry and Volunteer Force were merged to create the [Territorial Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Force) (changed to *Territorial Army* after the First World War), with terms of service similar to the army and Militia, and the Militia was renamed the *Special Reserve*,[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261] After the First World War the Special Reserve was renamed the Militia, again, but permanently suspended (although a handful of Militia units survived in the United Kingdom, its colonies, and the Crown Dependencies). Although the Territorial Force was nominally still a separate force from the British Army, by the end of the century, at the latest, any unit wholly or partly funded from Army Funds was considered part of the British Army. Outside the United Kingdom-proper, this was generally only the case for those units in the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands) or the [Imperial fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) colonies ([Nova Scotia](21184.md), before [Canadian confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_confederation); [Bermuda](3460.md); [Gibraltar](7607314.md); and [Malta](19137.md)).[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264]

The [Bermuda Militia Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Militia_Artillery), [Bermuda Militia Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Militia_Infantry), [Bermuda Volunteer Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Volunteer_Engineers), and the [Bermuda Volunteer Rifle Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Volunteer_Rifle_Corps),[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-autogenerated1-249] by example were paid for by the War Office and considered part of the British Army, with their officers appearing as such in the *Army List* unlike those of many other colonial units deemed auxiliaries. Today, the British Army is the only Home British military force, including the various other forces it has absorbed, though British military units organised on Territorial Army lines remain in British Overseas Territories that are still not considered formally part of the British Army, with only the [Royal Gibraltar Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gibraltar_Regiment) and the [Royal Bermuda Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bermuda_Regiment) (an amalgam of the old Bermuda Militia Artillery and Bermuda Volunteer Rifle Corps) appearing on the [British Army order-of-precedence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_order_of_precedence) and in the [Army List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_List), as well as on the [Corps Warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_Warrant) (the official list of those British military forces that are considered corps of the British Army).[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273]

In October 2012 the Ministry of Defence announced that the Territorial Army was to be renamed the Army Reserve.[^cite_note-274]

## Uniforms

Further information: [Uniforms of the British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniforms_of_the_British_Army)

The British Army uniform has sixteen categories, ranging from ceremonial uniforms to combat dress to evening wear. No. 8 Dress, the day-to-day uniform, is known as "Personal Clothing System – Combat Uniform" (PCS-CU)[^cite_note-275] and consists of a [Multi-Terrain Pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Terrain_Pattern) (MTP) windproof smock, a lightweight jacket and trousers with ancillary items such as [thermals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_underwear) and [waterproofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raincoat).[^cite_note-dresscodes-276] The army has introduced [tactical recognition flashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_recognition_flash) (TRFs); worn on the right arm of a combat uniform, the insignia denotes the wearer's regiment or corps.[^cite_note-277] In addition to working dress, the army has a number of parade uniforms for ceremonial and non-ceremonial occasions. The most-commonly-seen uniforms are No. 1 Dress (full ceremonial, seen at formal occasions such as at the changing of the guard at Buckingham Palace) and No. 2 Dress (Service Dress), a brown [khaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaki) uniform worn for non-ceremonial parades.[^cite_note-dresscodes-276][^cite_note-278]
