**Tripolitania** [/trɪpɒlɪˈteɪniə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): طرابلس), historically known as the **Tripoli region**, is a historic region and former [province of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Libya).

The region had been settled since antiquity, first coming to prominence as part of [the Carthaginian empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage). Following the defeat of Carthage in the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars), [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) organized the region (along with what is now modern day [Tunisia](30188.md) and eastern [Algeria](358.md)), into a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) known as [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_\(Roman_province\)), and placed it under the administration of a [proconsul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proconsul). During the [Diocletian reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian#Reforms) of the late 3rd century, all of North Africa was placed into the newly created [Diocese of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Africa), of which Tripolitania was a constituent province.

After the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire) in the 5th century, Tripolitania changed hands between the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) and the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), until it was taken during the [Muslim conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb) in the 8th century. It was part of the region known to the Islamic world as [Ifriqiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifriqiya), whose boundaries roughly mirrored those of the old Roman province of [Africa Proconsularis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_\(Roman_province\)). Though nominally under the suzerainty of the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), local dynasties such as the [Aghlabids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghlabids) and later the [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate) were practically independent. The native [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers), who had inhabited the area locally for centuries before the arrival of the [Arabs](2185.md), established their own native [Hafsid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafsid_dynasty) in Ifriqiya in the 13th century, and they controlled the region until it was conquered by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in the 15th century, who established [Ottoman Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Tripolitania) as a distinct province. Tripolitania became [an Italian colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Tripolitania) in 1911.&#x20;

After the 1934 formation of Libya, the [Tripolitania province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripolitania_\(province_of_Libya\)) was designated as one of the three primary [provinces of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Libya), alongside [Cyrenaica province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrenaica_province) to the east and [Fezzan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan_province) to the south.

## Definition



1818 Pinkerton map of Northern Africa, excerpt

Historically, the name Tripoli designated a region rather than a city, just as today in Arabic the same word Tarablus (طرابلس) is used for both the city and the region. The Arabic word used alone would be understood to mean only the city; in order to designate Tripolitania in Arabic, a qualifier such as "state", "province" or "sha'biyah" is required.

The region of Tripoli or Tripolitania derives from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) name Τρίπολις (Tripolis) "three cities", referring to [Oea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oea), [Sabratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabratha) and [Leptis Magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptis_Magna). Oea was the only one of the three cities to survive antiquity, and became known as [Tripoli](31232.md). Today Tripoli is the capital city of Libya and the northwestern portion of the country.

In addition to [Tripoli](31232.md), the following are among the largest and most important cities of Tripolitania: [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata), [Zawiya](3105551.md) (near ancient Sabratha), [Gharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharyan), [Khoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoms,_Libya) (near ancient Leptis Magna), [Tarhuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarhuna) and [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte).

## History

Further information: [History of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya)

### Antiquity

Further information: [Tripolitania (Roman province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripolitania_\(Roman_province\)), [Ancient Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Libya), [Libya in the Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_in_the_Roman_era), [Diocese of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Africa), [Praetorian prefecture of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorian_prefecture_of_Africa), and [Exarchate of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exarchate_of_Africa)

The city of *Oea*, on the site of modern [Tripoli](31232.md), was founded by the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) in the 7th century BC. It was conquered for a short time by the Greek colonists of [Cyrenaica](75451.md), who were in turn displaced by the [Punics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punics) of [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage). The [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) captured Tripolitania in 146 BC, and the area prospered during the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) period. The Latin name *Regio Tripolitania* dates to the 3rd century. The [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) took over in 435, and were in turn supplanted by the counter offensive of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) in the 530s, under the leadership of [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors) [Justinian the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_the_Great) and his general [Belisarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisarius).

### Middle Ages

Main article: [Islamic Tripolitania and Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Tripolitania_and_Cyrenaica)

In the 7th century, Tripolitania was [conquered by the Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb), and its successors, the [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) and [Abbasid Caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), inherited it.

The [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate), founded by [Isma'ili Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma'ilism) in 909 in [Raqqada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raqqada), Tunisia, ruled the area from Tunisia to Syria. In the 1140s, the [Italo-Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Normans) invaded Tripoli and created the brief [Kingdom of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Africa), which the [Almohad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate) destroyed in 1156. [Abu Zakariya Yahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Zakariya_Yahya), a [vassal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal) of the Almohads, established an independent state in Tunisia in 1229 and took control of Tripolitania shortly after. He founded the [Hafsid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafsid_dynasty), which controlled the region until the 16th century. During that century, wars between the Ottomans and the states ruled by the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) repeatedly led to the region changing alliances, although the Hafsids continued to rule. Hafsid rule ended when the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) brought [Abu Abdallah Muhammad VI ibn al-Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Abdallah_Muhammad_VI_ibn_al-Hasan) to Constantinople in 1574 and executed him.

### Modern history

[Ottoman Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Tripolitania) ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): ایالت طرابلس غرب) extended beyond the region of Tripolitania proper, also including Cyrenaica. Tripolitania became effectively independent under the rulers of the [Karamanli dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamanli_dynasty) in 1711 until Ottoman control was re-imposed by [Mahmud II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_II) in 1835. Ottoman rule persisted until the region was captured by [Italy](14532.md) in the 1911–1912[Italo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War). Italy officially granted autonomy after the war but gradually occupied the region.

&#x20;After [World War I](4764461.md), an [Arab republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism), *Al-Jumhuriya al-Trabulsiya*, or "[Tripolitanian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripolitanian_Republic)", declared the independence of Tripolitania from [Italian Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya). Its proclamation in autumn 1918 was followed by a formal declaration of independence at the 1919 [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919), which drafted the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles). It was the first formally declared [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) form of government in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), but it gained little support from international powers and had disintegrated by 1923. Italy, under the fascist dictator [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), had managed to re-establish full control over Libya by 1930.

Originally administered as part of a single colony, Italian Tripolitania was a separate colony from 26 June 1927 to 3 December 1934, when it was merged into Libya. The Italian fascists constructed the [Marble Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Arch_\(Libya\)) as a form of an imperial triumphal arch at the border between Tripolitania and Cyrenaica near the coast.

Tripolitania experienced a huge development in the late 1930s, when the Italian [Fourth Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Shore) was created with the [Province of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli_Province), with Tripoli as a modern "westernized" city. The Tripoli Province ("Provincia di Tripoli" in Italian) was established in 1937, with the official name being *Commissariato Generale Provinciale di Tripoli*. It was considered a province of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) and lasted until 1943.

&#x20;During [World War II](32927.md), several back-and-forth campaigns with mobile armour vehicles ebbed and flowed across the North African coastal deserts between first Fascist Italians and the British, with the Italians joined by the [Nazi Germans](21212.md) in 1941. Libya was finally occupied by the western [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_\(World_War_II\)), with the British moving west from [Egypt](8087628.md) after their victory at [El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Alamein) in October 1942 against German Field Marshall [Erwin Rommel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Rommel) and his [Afrika Korps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrika_Korps) and the Americans and British from the west after landings in [Operation Torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch) in [Morocco](19291.md) and [Algeria](358.md) in November 1942. From 1942 and past the end of the war in 1945 to 1951, when Libya gained independence, Tripolitania and the region of [Cyrenaica](75451.md) were administered by the [British Military Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Military_Administration_\(Libya\)). Italy formally renounced its claim upon the territory in 1947.

Tripolitania retained its status as a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Libya) in the [Kingdom of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Libya) from 1951 to 1963, when it was replaced by a new system of [governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorates_of_Libya), which divided Tripolitania into the governorates of [Khoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoms,_Libya), [Zawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zawiya_District), [Jabal al Gharbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jabal_al_Gharbi_Governorate), [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata_Governorate), and [Tarabulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarabulus_Governorate).

The Modern Latin missionary jurisdiction was called the [Apostolic Vicariate of Tripolitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Vicariate_of_Tripolitana) but was later renamed after its [episcopal see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see), Benghazi.
