# Information technology in Russia

&#x20; The **information technology sector in Russia** employed around 300,000 people in 2012,[^cite_note-cloudyfuture-1] and contributed 1.2% of the country's GDP in 2015.[^cite_note-schneider16-2] The sector is concentrated in the cities of [Moscow](19004.md) and [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md).[^cite_note-cloudyfuture-1]

## History

See also: [History of computing in the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_computing_in_the_Soviet_Union)

The Russian IT sector drew comparatively little from Soviet-era institutions.[^cite_note-wilsonc-3] Russian IT companies were started in the early 1990s by founders with an academic background seeking to find a place in the new market economy.[^cite_note-wilsonc-3] Piracy was widespread in the country, with an estimated 90% of all software in Russia being pirated in 1997.[^cite_note-4]

In the 1990s, companies such as [Vist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vist_\(company\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) began assembling computers out of foreign-made components, targeting small businesses and families who could not afford foreign brands like [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) and [Compaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compaq).[^cite_note-5] [DVM Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVM_Computer?action=edit\&redlink=1) gained some traction in the laptop market with its RoverBook brand.[^cite_note-6] The Russian Computer Association (Российская компьютерная ассоциация) was the trade association representing the sector.[^cite_note-7] In 1997 [Yandex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yandex) was established in Moscow.[^cite_note-8]

In 1999 [MCST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCST) developed the [Elbrus 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbrus_2000) processor, which was initially hyped as an [Itanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itanium) killer,[^cite_note-9] but the project was hampered by a chronic lack of funding.[^cite_note-10]

Over time, Russian companies moved to software development, an activity which enjoyed higher margins.[^cite_note-cloudyfuture-1] Local companies cater to the specific needs of the Russian market, such as [ERP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_resource_planning) software developed by [1C Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1C_Company) with a focus on Russian accounting rules.[^cite_note-11] [Kaspersky Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspersky_Labs) is described as the flagship company of the Russian IT industry.[^cite_note-12] Exports of software and IT services from Russia reached \$7 billion in 2015, up from \$2.8 billion in 2009.[^cite_note-13]



[MCST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCST) Elbrus HT-R1000 laptop

In 2012 MCST launched the [NT-ElbrusS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NT-ElbrusS?action=edit\&redlink=1), a rugged laptop for military applications.[^cite_note-14]

After the start of the [War in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas) and the [Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), the Ukrainian government banned a number of Russian IT companies from conducting business in the country.[^cite_note-15]

In June 2015 the Russian parliament passed a law to establish a preference system for software developed in Russia.[^cite_note-16]

Worsening relations between the United States and Russia have led some to advocate a purge of Russian software.[^cite_note-zdnet17-17]
