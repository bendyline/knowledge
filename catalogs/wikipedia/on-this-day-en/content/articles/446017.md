[Pakistan](23235.md) is administratively divided into four [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province), one [federal territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_territory), and two [disputed territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_dispute): the provinces of [Punjab](24751.md), [Sindh](28972.md), [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), and [Balochistan](4687.md); the [Islamabad Capital Territory](15294.md); and the administrative territories[^cite_note-1] of [Azad Jammu and Kashmir](2745.md) and [Gilgit–Baltistan](18807336.md).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] As part of the [Kashmir conflict](3030955.md) with neighbouring [India](14533.md), Pakistan has also claimed sovereignty over the Indian-controlled territories of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and [Ladakh](303611.md) since the [First Kashmir War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947%E2%80%931948) of 1947–1948. It also has a territorial dispute with India over [Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junagadh_State),[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] but has never exercised administrative authority over either regions. All of Pakistan's provinces and territories are subdivided into [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Pakistan), which are further subdivided into [districts](639402.md), and then [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tehsils_in_Pakistan), which are again further subdivided into [union councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_council_\(Pakistan\)).[^cite_note-12]

[West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) (pale yellow) as it was at the time of independence, with the independent princely states of 1947 in purple

[Pakistan](23235.md) inherited the territory comprising its current provinces from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) following the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) on [14 August 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Pakistan\)). Two days after independence, the [Muslim](19541.md)-majority [Murshidabad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshidabad_district) in [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) was moved out of the [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan) and put within the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India) due to a boundary adjustment by the [Radcliffe Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Line) which was aimed at keeping the [Hooghly River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooghly_River) entirely within India.[^cite_note-Murshidabad_Govt_Website-13][^cite_note-14] At its inception, Pakistan consisted of two wings, which were separated from each other by around 1,600 kilometres (990 mi) of Indian territory. The western wing consisted of a merger of the [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province), [West Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Punjab), [Sind Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Province_\(1936%E2%80%9355\)), and [Baluchistan CCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_\(Chief_Commissioner's_Province\)). The eastern wing consisted of [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal). What later became the [Princely states of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states_of_Pakistan) chose at first to remain independent.

In 1948, [Karachi](17123.md) was separated from Sind Province to form the [Federal Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Capital_Territory_\(Pakistan\)). In 1950, the North-West Frontier Province absorbed the princely states of [Amb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amb_\(princely_state\)) and [Phulra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phulra) while West Punjab (designated 'West' to distinguish it from India's [Punjab in the east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Punjab)) was renamed to simply [Punjab](24751.md). In 1952, the four princely states in the southwest formed the [Baluchistan States Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_States_Union).

In 1955, the *[One Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Unit)* policy was launched by then-[Prime Minister](461896.md) [Muhammad Ali Bogra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Ali_Bogra), whereby all the provinces and princely states of the western wing were merged to form the provincial wing of [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan), with [Lahore](125315.md) serving as its provincial capital. Simultaneously, East Bengal was redesignated as [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan), with [Dacca](56656.md) serving as its provincial capital. The One Unit policy aimed to reduce expenditure and to eliminate provincial prejudices, but the [military coup of 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) brought difficulties when the [first military President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan), [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(general\)), abolished the office of the [Chief Minister of West Pakistan in favour of Governor rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan#Governor_and_chief_minister).

On 7 September 1958, after four years of negotiations (including six months of intense negotiations), Pakistan purchased the [enclave](4848945.md) of [Gwadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar) from [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_and_Oman) for [₨.](588111.md)5.5 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)3 million; approximately \$22,410,311.42 in 2017).[^cite_note-15] Gwadar formally became a part of Pakistan on 8 December 1958, ending 174 years of Omani rule. In 1960, the federal capital was moved from Karachi to [Rawalpindi](343524.md) and in 1961, the Federal Capital Territory was also merged into West Pakistan. In 1966, the capital was again moved to the newly constructed city of [Islamabad](51153.md). In 1962, Dacca was made the legislative capital of the country due to East Pakistan's high population.[^cite_note-16] Following the [1963 Sino–Pakistan Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Pakistan_Agreement), a part of the [Gilgit Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit_Agency) (controlled by Pakistan since the [First Kashmir War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947%E2%80%931948)) was formally relinquished by Pakistan to the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (the [Trans-Karakoram Tract/Shaksgam Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Karakoram_Tract) in northeastern [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir)) with the provision that the settlement was subject to the final solution of the [Kashmir dispute](3030955.md) between [India](14533.md) and Pakistan.

### Since 1970

In 1970, the second military [President](419460.md), [Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan), abolished the political structure of West Pakistan and established four new provinces: [Sindh](28972.md), Punjab, [Balochistan](4687.md) and the North-West Frontier Province. In 1971, the [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis)-majority wing of East Pakistan [seceded](144732.md) from the Pakistani union following the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War), consequently forming the independent [People's Republic of Bangladesh](3454.md). In 1974, the remaining princely states of [Hunza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunza_\(princely_state\)) and [Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_\(princely_state\)) were abolished and their territories merged into the [Gilgit Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit_Agency), following which the [Northern Areas](18807336.md) were formed. In 1975, portions of the districts of [Peshawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_District) and [Dera Ismail Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ismail_Khan_District) were separated to form the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas). In 1981, the region surrounding Islamabad was separated from Punjab province, and renamed to the [Islamabad Capital Territory](15294.md).

In August 2000, [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Pakistan) were abolished as part of a plan to restructure [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Pakistan), followed by elections in 2001. Many of the functions previously handled at a provincial level had been transferred to individual [districts](639402.md) and [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil). In 2008, the government restored the former divisions and appointed commissioners.

In 2009, the Northern Areas were renamed to Gilgit-Baltistan, and retained its formal status as an [autonomous territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] In 2010, the North-West Frontier Province was formally renamed to [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md).[^cite_note-19] In 2018, the [National Assembly of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan) and [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Provincial Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) passed the historic *FATA Merger Bill* with the [Twenty-Fifth Constitutional Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan). On 31 May 2018, the final step in the merger of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa was completed, as then-President [Mamnoon Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamnoon_Hussain) signed the 25th Constitutional Amendment Bill into law. The amendment's signing abolished the Federally Administered Tribal Areas as a separate political entity and merged them into the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

The Provinces and administrative territories of Pakistan are subdivided into administrative "divisions", Divisional Commissioner is the administrative head of a division. Divisional Commissioner is appointed by the government of Pakistan from [Pakistan Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Administrative_Service)

### District

Main article: [Districts of Pakistan](639402.md)

The District Coordination Officer is the administrative head of the District Administration. They have wide-ranging responsibility for overseeing, improving and directing the approved plans of the District Government.[^cite_note-23]

The Zila Nazim used to be the executive head of the District Administration until 2010 when the government gave their powers to the District Coordination Officers also. Their role is similar to district [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor) or [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect), with responsibility for implementing government strategy and developing initiatives arising out of it.[^cite_note-24]

In order to decentralize administrative and financial authority to be accountable to Local Governments, for good governance, effective delivery of services, and transparent decision making through institutionalized participation of the people at grassroots level, elections to the local government institutions are held after every four years on none party basis by the Chief Election Commissioner of Pakistan.

### Tehsil

Main article: [Tehsils of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsils_of_Pakistan)

Among the three tiers of local government, tehsil government is the second tier. It is where the functions, responsibilities, and authorities of districts government are divided into smaller units, these units are known as "tehsils". The tehsils are used all over Pakistan except Sindh province where the word "taluka" is used instead, although the functions and authorities are the same. The head of the Tehsil government is "Tehsil Nazim" who is assisted by the tehsil Naib-Nazim. Every tehsil has a [Tehsil Municipal Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil_Municipal_Administration), consisting of a tehsil council, Tehsil Nazim, tehsil/taluka municipal officer (TMO), chief officer and other officials of the local council.[^cite_note-25]

Members of the union council including Union Administrator and Vice Union Administrator are elected through direct elections based on adult franchise and on the basis of joint electorate. However, for the election to the reserved seats for women in Zila Council proportionately divided among tehsils or towns shall be all members of the union councils in a tehsil or town. It is the responsibility of the Chief Election Commissioner to organize and conduct these elections.
