The **Bangsamoro Parliament** ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Parlamento ng Bangsamoro*; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): البرلمان بانجسامورو) is the [unicameral](204304.md) [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of [Bangsamoro](37260549.md), an [autonomous region](45522517.md) of the [Philippines](23440.md). It is currently led by the [Bangsamoro Transition Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority), the region's interim governing body. The inaugural session of the Parliament was held on March 29, 2019. The first session of the first regular Bangsamoro Parliament is expected to convene in late 2026.

The Parliament consists of 80 members, known as [members of parliament](98066.md) (MPs), who are currently appointed by the [president of the Philippines](271126.md). Under the current composition, 41 seats are held by nominees of the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](51673.md) (MILF), while 39 are held by nominees of the [national government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Philippines).

The Bangsamoro Parliament succeeded the [ARMM Regional Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARMM_Regional_Legislative_Assembly), the legislature of the former [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao).

## History



Old plenary hall at the [Shariff Kabunsuan Cultural Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shariff_Kabunsuan_Cultural_Complex) auditorium

The [1987 Constitution](19554336.md) provided for the creation of an autonomous region in Muslim Mindanao comprising provinces, cities, municipalities, and geographical areas that share a common and distinctive historical and cultural heritage, socioeconomic structures, and other relevant characteristics according to the framework of the Constitution, national sovereignty, and territorial integrity of the Philippines.[^cite_note-2]

On July 27, 2018, President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) signed Republic Act 11054, known as the "Organic Law for the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao," replacing the [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao) (ARMM) with the new Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao.[^cite_note-3] It provided that the region would have greater fiscal autonomy, a regional government, a Parliament, and its own justice system.

Article VII, Section 2 of the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law) states that:

the powers of government shall be vested in the Parliament which shall exercise those powers and functions expressly granted to it in this Organic Law, and those necessary for, or incidental to, the proper governance and development of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region. It shall set policies, legislate on matters within its authority, and elect a Chief Minister who shall exercise executive authority on its behalf.

The first Bangsamoro Parliament is an interim legislature headed by the [Bangsamoro Transition Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority) (BTA). The first set of members of the BTA took their oath on February 22, 2019.[^cite_note-4] The effective abolishment of the precursor autonomous region, [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao) (ARMM) took place following the official turnover of the ARMM to the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region occurred on February 26, 2019.[^cite_note-5]

[Pangalian Balindong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangalian_Balindong) was the first speaker of the Parliament, serving until his death from an undisclosed illness on October 2, 2025.[^cite_note-6] He was succeeded by [Mohammad Yacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Yacob) on October 21, 2025. [Sha Elijah Dumama-Alba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha_Elijah_Dumama-Alba) served as the [floor leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor_Leader_of_the_Bangsamoro_Parliament) of the second interim Parliament until October 21, 2025, when she was elected one of the deputy speakers, and was succeeded by John Anthony Lim.

In the first interim Parliament, there was a majority leader ([Lanang Ali Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanang_Ali_Jr.), an MILF nominee) and a minority leader ([Laisa Alamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laisa_Alamia), a national government nominee), but these positions were scrapped in the second interim Parliament.[^cite_note-ditches-7][^cite_note-nomajority-8]

The [first interim Bangsamoro Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority_Parliament) had its inaugural session on March 29, 2019, and passed four resolutions, two of which involved the budget for the Bangsamoro region.[^cite_note-9] The interim Bangsamoro Parliament's mandate was supposed to end on June 30, 2022, as per the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law), but this was extended to 2025 by law passed by President Rodrigo Duterte whose presidency ended on the same day the interim Parliament is supposed to be dissolved.[^cite_note-10]

The [second interim Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority_Parliament) had its inaugural session on September 15, 2022.[^cite_note-11] President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) appointed a new set of members for the interim Parliament. The MILF nominees include people associated with the MNLF—eight from the Sema-Jikiri faction and seven from the [Nur Misuari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Misuari)'s faction. Misuari's group became part of the Parliament for the first time.[^cite_note-marcos-no-extension-12]

The [2026 parliamentary election](68697708.md), which elected members of the [first regular parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Bangsamoro_Parliament), resulted in a [hung parliament](652214.md), after the [Bangsamoro Federalist Party](83436978.md) topped the election, winning 31 seats, short of the number required to form a majority. The MILF-affiliated [United Bangsamoro Justice Party](46652799.md) placed second, winning 30 seats. The first regular parliament is expected to convene after October 30, 2026, when members formally assume office.[^cite_note-13]

### Seat



The Bangsamoro Parliament building in 2022 which formerly hosted the [ARMM Regional Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARMM_Regional_Legislative_Assembly).

The Parliament first met at the auditorium of the [Shariff Kabunsuan Cultural Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shariff_Kabunsuan_Cultural_Complex) located within the BARMM Complex in [Cotabato City](298955.md) during the first interim Parliament from 2019 to 2022. Sessions were then regularly held at the Parliament Building, the former seat of the [ARMM Regional Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARMM_Regional_Legislative_Assembly), within the same complex since 2022.

The chamber's current design features traditional Islamic geometric patterns and cultural motifs, such as the [okir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okir). The speaker's rostrum has the [Philippine national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Philippines) and the [Bangsamoro flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Bangsamoro) on display, as per the Bangsamoro Organic Law.[^cite_note-14]

On April 16, 2026, the four-storey Regional Assembly Building or the Sheikh Salamat Hashim Building was inaugurated. The building is meant to host committee hearings and policy deliberations of the Bangsamoro Parliament.[^cite_note-15]

## Membership

As per law, the legislature should be composed of at least 80 members,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] who in turn are led by the [speaker of the Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Bangsamoro_Parliament) which was appointed from among the members of the legislature.[^cite_note-18] Until June 30, 2019, 24 elective officials of the defunct Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao could have served as additional members.

40 percent of the Parliament seats are allotted to the representatives of Bangsamoro's [parliamentary districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_districts_of_Bangsamoro), although the districts are yet to be constituted. The Bangsamoro parliamentary districts will exist independently from the [legislative districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_the_Philippines) used to determine representation in the national [House of Representatives](293605.md).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Only the national [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) has the power to increase the composition of the Parliament.

There are also legal provisions to deter members of the Parliament to [switch political party allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_floor). Changing political party affiliation within the term of a member of Parliament would mean the forfeiture of their seat. Changing of affiliation within six months prior to a parliamentary election renders the person ineligible as a nominee of a political party seeking representation in the Parliament.[^cite_note-21]

## Seal



Parliament seal from 2019 to 2021

The current seal of the Bangsamoro Parliament is in use since 2021 and its specifications is defined under Bangsamoro Autonomy Act No. 16.[^cite_note-barmmAct16-26] It is a circular symbol with a green, red, white, and yellow color scheme patterned after the [Bangsamoro Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Flag) and bears the name of the Parliament. Its central element is a shield bearing the Bangsamoro flag and is partially surrounded by a semicircle [parliament diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_apportionment_diagram) with 80 blocks signifying the number of seats in the Parliament. The book on top of the shield which represents the "living adherence of the Parliament to the rule of law," while the text "2019" represents the foundation year of Bangsamoro. The Bangsamoro Autonomy Act No. 16, as Parliament Bill No. 24 was passed by the Bangsamoro Parliament on January 19, 2021.

A previous version of the seal without the book and foundation year was used prior to the passage of the bill.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The modified seal was formally adopted after the Chief Minister signed the bill into law on February 12, 2021.[^cite_note-barmmAct16-26]
