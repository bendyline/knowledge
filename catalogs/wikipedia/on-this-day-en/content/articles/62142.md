The **Horn of Africa** (**HOA**), also known as the **Somali Peninsula**,[^cite_note-Montana_State_University2-4][^cite_note-Egerton_University2-5][^cite_note-Brock_Millman2-6] is a large [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) and [geopolitical](163225.md) region located in the easternmost part of mainland [Africa](5334607.md).[^cite_note-Stock22-7] It is the fourth largest peninsula in the world and is composed of  [Somalia](27358.md), [Djibouti](17207794.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), [Eritrea](17238590.md), and the partially recognised state of [Somaliland](51425.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Although not common, some broader geopolitical definitions include parts or all of [Kenya](188171.md) and [Sudan](27421.md).[^cite_note-auto12-10][^cite_note-Joireman222-11][^cite_note-BritannicaOnline2-12] It has been described as a region of significant geopolitical and strategic importance, since it is situated along the southern boundary of the [Red Sea](26191.md); extending hundreds of kilometers into the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md), [Guardafui Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardafui_Channel), and [Indian Ocean](14580.md). It shares a maritime border with the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The Horn of Africa commonly is referred to simply as "the Horn," while inhabitants are sometimes colloquially termed Horn Africans or Horners.[^cite_note-Teklehaimanot,_Hailay_Kidu_2015-17][^cite_note-\:0-18] The term Horn Africans or Horners, aside from its geographic context, is considered a coherent population label.[^cite_note-\:1-19] Anthropologically, "Horn Africans" is used in a stricter sense, reserved for the Cushitic and Ethio‑Semitic speaking peoples, who constitute the core historical and demographical population of the Horn, rather than all groups living within the peninsula's borders.[^cite_note-\:1-19][^cite_note-\:2-20][^cite_note-Joireman22-21] Regional studies on the Horn of Africa are carried out in the fields of [Ethiopian studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_studies) and [Somali studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_studies).

## Names

The peninsula has been known by various names. The [Ancient Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) referred to it as [Regio Aromatica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromata) or Regio Cinnamonifora after the aromatic plants found there, or as Regio Incognita for its poor coverage by classical Mediterranean cartography. In ancient and medieval times, the Horn of Africa was referred to as [Bilad al Barbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_\(region\)) ("Land of the Berbers").[^cite_note-auto2-22][^cite_note-auto-23][^cite_note-auto12-10] It is also known as the Somali Peninsula, or in the Somali language as *Geeska Afrika or* *Jasiiradda Soomaali*.[^cite_note-24] In other local languages it is called the direct equivalent of "Horn of Africa" or "African Horn": in [Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic_language): የአፍሪካ ቀንድ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Amharic): *yäafrika qänd*, in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القرن الأفريقي, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-qarn al-'afrīqī*, in [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_language): *Gaanfaa Afrikaa*, and in [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language): ቀርኒ ኣፍሪቃ, romanized: *q'ärnī afīrīqa*.[^cite_note-Teklehaimanot,_Hailay_Kidu_2015-17][^cite_note-\:0-18]

The region geographically consists of [Djibouti](17207794.md), [Eritrea](17238590.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), [Somalia](27358.md), and the partially recognised state of [Somaliland](51425.md).[^cite_note-auto-23][^cite_note-auto12-10][^cite_note-Joireman22-21][^cite_note-25] These nations protrude from the continent in the shape resembling a [horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_\(anatomy\)). Geopolitically, the region consists of these same nations.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The term is occasionally used to be inclusive of neighbouring [southeast African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Africa) countries to distinguish the broader geopolitical definition of the Horn of Africa from narrower peninsular definitions.[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-Schreck,_Carl_J._2004-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-BritannicaOnline2-12][^cite_note-32]

### Population

In genetics, linguistic and anthropological studies, the term is used as a label for those with a shared, distinct, commonality in their genetic structure, historical trajectories, cultural traits, and Afroasiatic languages.[^cite_note-Joireman22-21] Genome‑wide studies of these populations identify two ancestry components largely restricted to the Horn: a differentiated African component termed 'Ethiopic' and a differentiated non‑African component termed 'Ethio‑Somali', which represent East African plus West Eurasian related admixture.[^cite_note-\:1-19][^cite_note-\:2-20]

### Demographics and linguistics



Map showcasing the distribution of the Afroasiatic languages in the horn

The main groups are the [Cushitic-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages) [Cushitic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_peoples), traditionally centered in the lowlands, and the [Ethio-semitic-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Semitic_languages) [Habesha peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habesha_peoples), who inhabit the [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands) and [Eritrean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Highlands) highlands. The most spoken Cushitic languages are: [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_language) with \~30 million speakers, [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language) with \~28 million speakers,[^cite_note-33][Sidama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidama_language) with \~3 million speakers,[^cite_note-34] and [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_language) with \~3 million speakers.[^cite_note-35] The most spoken [Ethio-semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethio-Semitic_languages) languages are: [Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic) with \~32 million speakers,[^cite_note-e25-36] and [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language) with \~10 million speakers.[^cite_note-37] Other Afroasiatic languages with a significant number of speakers include the Cushitic [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_language), [Hadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadiyya_language), and [Agaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaw_languages) languages; the Semitic [Tigre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_language), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language), [Gurage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurage_language), [Harari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harari_language), [Silt'e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt'e_language) and [Argobba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argobba_language) tongues [^cite_note-Ethnologue-38]

Languages belonging to the [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages) language family are spoken in some areas by [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_peoples) ethnic minorities in [Ethiopia](187749.md) and [Eritrea](17238590.md). These tongues include the Nilo-Saharan [Me'en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me'en_language) and [Mursi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursi_language) languages used in southwestern Ethiopia, and [Kunama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_language) and [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_language) idioms spoken in parts of southern Eritrea. [Omotic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omotic_languages) are also spoken by Omotic communities inhabiting Ethiopia's southern regions. Among these languages are [Aari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aari_language), [Dizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizi_language), [Gamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamo_language), [Kafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafa_language), [Hamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamer_language) and [Wolaytta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolaytta_language).[^cite_note-Tspahcoe-39]

The Horn has, both modern and historically, utilised several indigenous writing systems. Among these is [Ge'ez script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge'ez_script) (ግዕዝ *_Gəʿəz_*) (also known as *Ethiopic*), which has been written in for at least 2000 years.[^cite_note-40] It is an [abugida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abugida) script that was originally developed to write the [Ge'ez language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge'ez_language). In speech communities that use it, such as the Amharic and Tigrinya, the script is called *_fidäl_* (ፊደል), which means "script" or "alphabet". The Somali language was, for centuries, written using an adapted Arabic script known as [wadaad's script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadaad's_writing). In the early 20th century, in response to a national campaign to settle on a writing script for the [Somali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language), which had since lost its ancient script,[^cite_note-41] [Osman Yusuf Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Yusuf_Kenadid), a Somali poet and cousin of the [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) [Yusuf Ali Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Ali_Keenadid), devised a phonetically sophisticated alphabet called [Osmanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmanya_script) (also known as *far soomaali*; Osmanya: 𐒍𐒖𐒇 𐒈𐒝𐒑𐒛𐒐𐒘) for representing the sounds of Somali.[^cite_note-42] Though no longer the official writing script in Somalia, the Osmanya script is available in the [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) range 10480-104AF \[from U+10480 – U+104AF (66688–66735)].

### Genetics

#### Autosomal



Admixture analysis of the Horn

Modern studies consistently show that Horn of Africans, those speaking Cushitic and Ethio‑Semitic, form a distinct cluster, deriving their genomes from a mix of local East African ancestry dubbed "Ethiopic" and substantial non‑African, West Eurasian, ancestry which Hodgson et al dub "Ethio-Somali"".[^cite_note-\:1-19][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-Pagani_83–96-44]

> "The Semitic-Cushitic Ethiopians stand out as a relatively uniform set of individuals characterized by a strong (40%–50%) non-African component and an African component split between a broad East African and an apparently Ethiopia-specific component"

— Pagani et al, Ethiopian Genetic Diversity Reveals Linguistic Stratification and Complex Influences on the Ethiopian Gene Pool

Genome‑wide analyses indicate that HOAs carry 40–60 % or more of this non‑African component, detectable in ADMIXTURE and principal component analyses setting them apart from other sub‑Saharan African populations. This non‑African component is often represented in analyses as a distinct "Ethio‑Somali" ancestral component, interpreted as an ancient back‑migration into northeast Africa, tied to the Afro‑Asiatic linguistic dispersal. This back‑migration signal shows affinities to ancestries typical of the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) and [North Africa](21714.md); in combination with the "Ethiopic" African component, this places Horn populations in an intermediate genetic position between sub‑Saharan Africans and Middle Eastern/European populations in PCA space.[^cite_note-\:1-19]

In addition to this Cushitic, Ethio-Somali, Eurasian component, HOAs carry a secondary southern Arabian related component that entered via the Red Sea corridor. This component is detectable in autosomal studies as a distinct West Eurasian ancestry not overlapping with the older Ethio‑Somali signal.[^cite_note-\:1-19]

> "Thus, it is more likely that the genetic-geographic HOA-Arabia distance gradient reflects secondary admixture of Arabian migrants into HOA populations already carrying substantial non-African ancestry"

— Hodgson et al, Early Back-to-Africa Migration into the Horn of Africa

#### Unipaternal

##### Y-DNA

[Haplogroup E-M35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M35) has the highest frequency in Horners, particularly its descendent [E-M78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-M78) with a high diversity of subclades suggesting an ancient presence in the region, followed by various lineages belonging to [Haplogroup J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J_\(Y-DNA\)), specifically [J1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M267) and [J2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M172), as well as [Haplogroup T-M184](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_T-M184). Cruciani et al found [E-M35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M35) in approximately 57% of both Oromo and Amhara individuals.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The second most prevalent lineage [Haplogroup J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J_\(Y-DNA\)), has been found to exist at levels as high as 35.4% in the Amhara, of which 33.3% is of the type [J1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M267), while 2.1% is of [J2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M172) type. These lineages are also found in the neighbouring [Tigrayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayans).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-Seminoone-51] Amongst [Somalis](1571696.md), [Haplogroup E-M78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-M78), specifically E-V32 is found in roughly 2/3 of the population [^cite_note-52] with most of the remaining belonging either to T-M70[^cite_note-53] or [J-P58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-P58).[^cite_note-54]

##### mtDNA

Maternal ancestry in northern [Ethiopian](187749.md) populations, including Tigrayans, is dominated by [African L haplogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_L_\(mtDNA\)), which comprise about 60% of lineages. Consistent with other regions and groups like Somalis where it also comprises around 60% of lineages. The remaining 40% belong primarily to Eurasian-associated lineages such as [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_M1_\(mtDNA\)) and subclades of [haplogroup N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_N_\(mtDNA\)).[^cite_note-Pagani_83–96-44][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

### Prehistory

Some of the earliest *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)* fossils, the [Omo remains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omo_remains) (from ca. 233,000 years ago) and the [Herto skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herto_Man) (from ca. 160,000 ago) have been found in the region, both in Ethiopia.[^cite_note-Vidal22-57]

The findings of the Earliest Stone Tipped Projectiles from the [Ethiopian Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley,_Ethiopia) dated to more than 279,000 years ago "in combination with the existing archaeological, fossil and genetic evidence, isolate East Africa as a source of modern cultures and biology."[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

According to the [Southern Dispersal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dispersal) scenario, the Southern route of the Out of Africa migration occurred in the Horn of Africa through the [Bab el Mandeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_el_Mandeb). Today at the Bab-el-Mandeb straits, the Red Sea is about 12 miles (19 kilometres) wide, but 50,000 years ago it was much narrower and sea levels were 70 meters lower. Though the straits were never completely closed, there may have been islands in between which could be reached using simple rafts. Shell [middens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden) 125,000 years old have been found in Eritrea,[^cite_note-pmid10811218-61] indicating the diet of early humans included seafood obtained by [beachcombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachcombing).

Ethiopian and Eritrean [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) established the earliest known use of the seed grass [teff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teff) (*Poa abyssinica*) between 4000 and 1000 BCE.[^cite_note-62] Teff is used to make the flatbread [injera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injera)/taita. [Coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) originated in Ethiopia and has become a worldwide beverage.[^cite_note-63]

Historian [Christopher Ehret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Ehret), cited genetic evidence which had identified the Horn of Africa as a source of a genetic marker "[M35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M35)/[215](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M215)" Y-chromosome lineage for a significant population component which moved north from that region into Egypt and the Levant. Ehret argued that this genetic distribution paralleled the spread of the [Afrasian language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) with the movement of people from the Horn of Africa into Egypt and added a new demic component to the existing population of Egypt 17,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Ancient_Africa:_A_Global_History,_t-64]

[King Ezana's Stela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Ezana's_Stela) at [Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksum), symbol of the [Aksumite civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum)

The area comprising [Somaliland](51425.md), [Somalia](27358.md), [Djibouti](17207794.md), the [Red Sea](26191.md) coast of [Eritrea](17238590.md) and [Sudan](27421.md) is considered the most likely location of the land known to the ancient [Egyptians](31912046.md) as *[Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt)* (or "Ta Netjeru", meaning god's land), whose first mention dates to the 25th century BCE.[^cite_note-65]

[Dʿmt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%CA%BFmt) was a kingdom located in Eritrea and northern [Ethiopia](187749.md), which existed during the 8th and 7th centuries BCE. With its capital probably at [Yeha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeha), the kingdom developed [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) schemes, used [plows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plow), grew [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), and made [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) tools and weapons. After the fall of Dʿmt in the 5th century BCE, the plateau came to be dominated by smaller successor kingdoms, until the rise of one of these kingdoms during the 1st century, the [Aksumite Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum), which was able to reunite the area.[^cite_note-66]

The [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum) (also known as the Aksumite Empire) was an ancient state located in the [Eritrea](17238590.md) and [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands), which thrived between the 1st and 7th centuries CE. A major player in the commerce between the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Ancient India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_India), Aksum's rulers facilitated trade by minting their own [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksumite_currency). The state established its [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony) over the declining [Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush) and regularly entered the politics of the kingdoms on the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), eventually extending its rule over the region with the conquest of the [Himyarite Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himyarite_Kingdom). Under [Ezana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezana) (fl. 320–360), the kingdom of Aksum became the first major empire to adopt [Christianity](5211.md), and was named by [Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_\(prophet\)) as one of the four great powers of his time, along with [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty).



Ancient trading centers in the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula according to the *[Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea)*

Somalia was an important link in the Horn, connecting the region's commerce with the rest of the ancient world. Somali sailors and merchants were the main suppliers of [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense), [myrrh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrrh) and spices, all of which were valuable luxuries to the [Ancient Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptians), [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians), [Mycenaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece), [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonians) and [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The Romans consequently began to refer to the region as *Regio Aromatica*. In the [classical era](252905.md), several flourishing [Somali city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesea) such as [Opone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opone), [Mosylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosylon) and [Malao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malao) competed with the [Sabaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans), [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) and [Axumites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axumite_Empire) for the rich [Indo](14533.md)-[Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman) trade.[^cite_note-69]

The [birth of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Islam) opposite the Horn's Red Sea coast meant that local merchants and sailors living on the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) gradually came under the influence of the new religion through their converted [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) Muslim trading partners. With the migration of Muslim families from the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world) to the Horn in the early centuries of Islam, and the peaceful conversion of the local population by Muslim scholars in the following centuries, the ancient city-states eventually transformed into Islamic [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera), [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila), [Barawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barawa) and [Merka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merka), which were part of the *[Barbara civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_\(region\))*.[^cite_note-Laitin-70][^cite_note-71] The city of Mogadishu came to be known as the "City of Islam"[^cite_note-72] and controlled the East African gold trade for several centuries.[^cite_note-73]

### Middle Ages and Early Modern era

Main articles: [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate), [Ajuran Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate), [Isaaq Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_Sultanate), [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire), [Sultanate of Showa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Showa), [Sultanate of Ifat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ifat), [Sultanate of the Geledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_the_Geledi), [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty), [Sultanate of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Mogadishu), [Aussa Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aussa_Sultanate), [Majeerteen Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majeerteen_Sultanate), [Sultanate of Hobyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Hobyo), [Khedivate's Somali Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khedivate's_Somali_Coast), and [Ottoman Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Zeila)

Further information: [History of Africa § Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Africa#Horn_of_Africa)



A copy of 17th century philosophical and [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical) treatise by [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian) philosopher [Zara Yaqob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zara_Yaqob)

During the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), several powerful empires dominated the regional trade in the Horn, including the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate), the [Ajuran Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate), the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire), the [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty), and the [Sultanate of the Geledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_the_Geledi).

The [Sultanate of Showa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Showa), established in 896, was one of the oldest local [Islamic](6037917.md) states. It was centered in the former [Shewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa) province in central Ethiopia. The polity was succeeded by the [Sultanate of Ifat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ifat) around 1285. Ifat was governed from its capital at [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila) in Somaliland and was the easternmost district of the former Shewa Sultanate.[^cite_note-C1-74]

The [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate) was a medieval multi-ethnic [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) state centered in the Horn region. At its height, it controlled large parts of Somalia, Ethiopia, Djibouti and Eritrea. Many of the historic cities in the region, such as [Amud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amud), [Maduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maduna), [Abasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abasa,_Somalia), [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera), [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila) and [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar), flourished during the kingdom's golden age. This period that left behind numerous [courtyard houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard), [mosques](19894.md), [shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine) and [walled enclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall). Under the leadership of rulers such as [Sabr ad-Din II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabr_ad-Din_II), [Mansur ad-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansur_ad-Din_of_Adal), [Jamal ad-Din II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_ad-Din_II), [Shams ad-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams_ad-Din_ibn_Muhammad), General [Mahfuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahfuz) and [Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Ibrahim_al-Ghazi), Adalite armies continued the struggle against the [Solomonic dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomonic_dynasty), a campaign historically known as the [Conquest of Abyssinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian%E2%80%93Adal_War) or *Futuh al Habash*.

Through a strong centralized administration and an aggressive military stance towards invaders, the [Ajuran Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate) successfully resisted an [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_people) invasion from the west and a [Portuguese](23033.md) incursion from the east during the [Gaal Madow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaal_Madow) and the [Ajuran-Portuguese wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate#Ajuran-Portuguese_battles). Trading routes dating from the ancient and early medieval periods of [Somali maritime enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_history_of_Somalia) were strengthened or re-established, and the state left behind an extensive [architectural legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_architecture). Many of the hundreds of ruined castles and fortresses that dot the landscape of Somalia today are attributed to Ajuran engineers,[^cite_note-75] including a lot of the [pillar tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillar_tomb) fields, [necropolises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necropolis) and ruined cities built during that era. The royal family, the [House of Gareen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Gareen), expanded its territories and established its hegemonic rule through a combination of warfare, trade linkages and alliances.[^cite_note-76]

The [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty) ruled many parts of modern Ethiopia and Eritrea from approximately 1137 to 1270. The name of the dynasty comes from the [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages)-speaking [Agaw people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaw_people) of northern Ethiopia. From 1270 onwards for many centuries, the [Solomonic dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomonic_dynasty) ruled the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire).



The [citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel) in [Gondershe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondershe), an important city in the medieval [Ajuran Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate)

In 1270, the [Amhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharas) nobleman [Yekuno Amlak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekuno_Amlak), who claimed descent from the last [Aksumite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum) king and ultimately the [Queen of Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Sheba), overthrew the [Agaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaw_people) [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty) at the [Battle of Ansata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ansata), ushering his reign as [Emperor of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Ethiopia). While initially a rather small and politically unstable entity, the empire managed to expand significantly during the [crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) of [Amda Seyon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amda_Seyon_I) (1314–1344) and his successors, becoming the dominant force in [East Africa](260987.md).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

In the early 15th century, Ethiopia sought to make diplomatic contact with European kingdoms for the first time since Aksumite times. A letter from King [Henry IV of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_England) to the Emperor of Abyssinia survives.[^cite_note-79] In 1428, the Emperor [Yeshaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshaq_I_of_Ethiopia) sent two emissaries to [Alfonso V of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_V_of_Aragon), who sent return emissaries who failed to complete the return trip.[^cite_note-Beshah-80]

The first continuous relations with a European country began in 1508 with Portugal under Emperor [Lebna Dengel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawit_II_of_Ethiopia), who had just inherited the throne from his father.[^cite_note-81] This proved to be an important development, for when Abyssinia was subjected to the attacks of the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate) General and [Imam](15240.md) [Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Ibrahim_al-Ghazi) (called "*Gurey*" or "*Grañ*", both meaning "the Left-handed"), Portugal assisted the Ethiopian emperor by sending weapons and four hundred men, who helped his son [Gelawdewos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelawdewos_of_Ethiopia) defeat Ahmad and re-establish his rule.[^cite_note-82] This [Ethiopian–Adal War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian%E2%80%93Adal_War) was one of the first proxy wars in the region as the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), and Portugal took sides in the conflict.



The [Lalibela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalibela) churches carved by the [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty) in the 12th century

When Emperor [Susenyos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susenyos_of_Ethiopia) converted to [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) in 1624, years of revolt and civil unrest followed resulting in thousands of deaths.[^cite_note-83] The [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) missionaries had offended the Orthodox faith of the local Ethiopians. On 25 June 1632, Susenyos's son, Emperor [Fasilides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasilides_of_Ethiopia), declared the state religion to again be [Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Orthodox_Tewahedo_Church), and expelled the Jesuit missionaries and other Europeans.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

During the end of 18th and the beginning of 19th century the Yejju dynasty (more specifically, the Warasek) ruled north Ethiopia changing the official language of Amhara people to Afaan Oromo, including inside the court of Gondar which was capital of the empire. Founded by [Ali I of Yejju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_I_of_Yejju) several successive descendants of him and [Abba Seru Gwangul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abba_Seru_Gwangul) ruled with their army coming from mainly their clan the [Yejju Oromo tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yejju_Oromo_tribe) as well as Wollo and Raya Oromo.[^cite_note-86]

The [Sultanate of the Geledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_the_Geledi) was a Somali kingdom administered by the Gobroon dynasty, which ruled parts of the Horn of Africa during the 18th and 19th centuries. It was established by the Ajuran soldier [Ibrahim Adeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Adeer), who had defeated various [vassals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal) of the Ajuran Empire and established the *House of Gobroon*. The [dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty) reached its apex under the successive reigns of Sultan [Yusuf Mahamud Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Mahamud_Ibrahim), who successfully consolidated Gobroon power during the [Bardera wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardera), and Sultan [Ahmed Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yusuf_\(Gobroon\)), who forced regional powers such as the [Omani Empire](22316.md) to submit [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute).



Engraving of the 13th century [Fakr ad-Din Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakr_ad-Din_Mosque) built by Fakr ad-Din, the first Sultan of the [Sultanate of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Mogadishu)

The [Isaaq Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_Sultanate) was a [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people) kingdom that ruled parts of the Horn of Africa during the 18th and 19th centuries. It spanned the territories of the [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq) clan, descendants of the [Banu Hashim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Hashim) clan,[^cite_note-Lewisapd-87] in modern-day [Somaliland](51425.md) and [Ethiopia](187749.md). The sultanate was governed by the [Reer Guled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guled_Dynasty) branch of the [Eidagale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidagale) sub-clan established by the first sultan, Sultan [Guled Abdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guled_Abdi_\(Sultan\)). The sultanate is the pre-colonial predecessor to the modern [Somaliland](51425.md).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

According to oral tradition, prior to the Guled dynasty the [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq) clan-family were ruled by a dynasty of the Tolje'lo branch starting from, descendants of Ahmed nicknamed Tol Je'lo, the eldest son of [Sheikh Ishaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishaaq_bin_Ahmed)'s [Harari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harari_people) wife. There were eight Tolje'lo rulers in total, starting with Boqor Harun ([Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language): *Boqor Haaruun*) who ruled the Isaaq Sultanate for centuries starting from the 13th century.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] The last Tolje'lo ruler [Garad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garad) Dhuh Barar ([Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language): *Dhuux Baraar*) was overthrown by a coalition of Isaaq clans. The once strong Tolje'lo clan were scattered and took refuge amongst the [Habr Awal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habr_Awal) with whom they still mostly live.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

&#x20;The [Majeerteen Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majeerteen_Sultanate) (Migiurtinia) was another prominent Somali sultanate based in the Horn region. Ruled by [King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boqor) [Osman Mahamuud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Mahamuud) during its golden age, it controlled much of northeastern and central Somalia in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The polity had all of the organs of an integrated modern state and maintained a robust trading network. It entered into treaties with foreign powers and exerted strong centralized authority on the domestic front.[^cite_note-HOA-95][^cite_note-NEAS-96] Much of the Sultanate's former domain is today coextensive with the autonomous [Puntland](233569.md) region in northern Somalia.[^cite_note-IIA-97]



[Hobyo Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Hobyo) cavalry and fort, 1924

The [Sultanate of Hobyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Hobyo) was a 19th-century Somali kingdom founded by Sultan [Yusuf Ali Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Ali_Kenadid). Kenadid's goal was to seize control of the neighboring Majeerteen Sultanate, which was then ruled by his cousin Boqor Osman Mahamuud. He was unsuccessful, and was forced into exile in [Yemen](350939.md). A decade later, in the 1870s, Kenadid returned from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) with a band of [Hadhrami](325752.md) [musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musketeer) and a group of devoted lieutenants with whom he established the kingdom of Hobyo, which ruled much of northern and central Somalia during the early modern period.[^cite_note-Metz-98]

### Modern history

The Horn of Africa before the Scramble for Africa (c. 1880)

In the period following the opening of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1869, when European powers [scrambled for territory in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa) and tried to establish [coaling stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaling_station) for their ships, Italy invaded and occupied [Eritrea](17238590.md). On 1 January 1890, Eritrea officially became a colony of [Italy](14532.md). In 1896 further Italian incursion into the horn was decisively halted by Ethiopian forces. By 1936, Eritrea became a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Ethiopia) of [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) (Africa Orientale Italiana), along with [Ethiopia](187749.md) and [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland). By 1941, Eritrea had about 760,000 inhabitants, including 70,000 Italians.[^cite_note-99] The Commonwealth armed forces, along with the Ethiopian patriotic resistance, expelled those of Italy in 1941,[^cite_note-100] and took over the area's administration. The British continued to administer the territory under a [UN Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Mandate) until 1951, when Eritrea was federated with Ethiopia, per UN resolution 390 A (V) adopted December 1950.

The strategic importance of Eritrea, due to its [Red Sea](26191.md) coastline and mineral resources, was the main cause for the federation with Ethiopia, which in turn led to Eritrea's annexation as Ethiopia's 14th province [in 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea#Aftermath). This was the culmination of a gradual process of takeover by the Ethiopian authorities, a process which included a 1959 edict establishing the compulsory teaching of [Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic), the main language of Ethiopia, in all Eritrean schools. The lack of regard for the Eritrean population led to the formation of an independence movement in the early 1960s (1961), which erupted into a [30-year war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) against successive Ethiopian governments that ended in 1991. Following a UN-supervised [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_independence_referendum,_1993) in Eritrea (dubbed [UNOVER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOVER)) in which the Eritrean people overwhelmingly voted for independence, Eritrea declared its independence and gained international recognition in 1993.[^cite_note-Britannica_Eritrea-page20-101] In 1998, a border dispute with Ethiopia led to the [Eritrean-Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean-Ethiopian_War).[^cite_note-102]

From 1862 until 1894, the land to the north of the [Gulf of Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tadjoura) situated in modern-day [Djibouti](17207794.md) was called *Obock* and was ruled by [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people) and [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) [Sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan), local authorities with whom [France](5843419.md) signed various treaties between 1883 and 1887 to first gain a foothold in the region.[^cite_note-Uwechue-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-Apcoatf-105] In 1894, [Léonce Lagarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9once_Lagarde) established a permanent French administration in the [city of Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_\(city\)) and named the region *Côte française des Somalis* ([French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Somaliland)), a name which continued until 1967.

In 1958, on the eve of neighboring [Somalia](27358.md)'s independence in 1960, a [referendum](51582.md) was held in the territory to decide whether to join the Somali Republic or to remain with France. The referendum favoured continued association with France, partly due to a combined yes vote by the sizable Afar ethnic group and resident Europeans.[^cite_note-Barrington2006-106] There were reports of widespread [vote rigging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_rigging), with the French expelling thousands of Somalis before the polls.[^cite_note-107] The majority of those who voted no were Somalis who were strongly in favour of joining a united Somalia, as had been proposed by [Mahmoud Harbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Harbi), Vice President of the Government Council. Harbi was killed in a plane crash two years later.[^cite_note-Barrington2006-106] Djibouti finally gained its independence from France in 1977. [Hassan Gouled Aptidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Gouled_Aptidon), a Somali politician who had campaigned for a yes vote in the referendum of 1958, became the nation's first president (1977–1999).[^cite_note-Barrington2006-106] In early 2011, the Djiboutian citizenry took part in a [series of protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Djiboutian_protests) against the long-serving government, which were associated with the larger [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) demonstrations. The unrest eventually subsided by April of the year, and Djibouti's ruling [People's Rally for Progress](622709.md) party was re-elected to office.



[Eritrean Ascaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Ascari), colonial troops of the Italian Army, in a 1898 wood engraving

The [Dervish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dervish_movement_\(Nugaal\)) existed for 25 years, from 1895 until 1920. The [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) named Hassan [Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of the Somali nation,[^cite_note-108] and the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) promised to officially recognize any territories the Dervishes were to acquire.[^cite_note-109] After a quarter of a century of holding the British at bay, the Dervishes were finally defeated in 1920 as a direct consequence of Britain's new policy of [aerial bombardment](323167.md).[^cite_note-110] As a result of this bombardment, former Dervish territories were turned into a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) of Britain. [Italy](14532.md) faced similar opposition from Somali [Sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) and armies, and did not acquire full control of modern Somalia until the [Fascist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) in late 1927. This occupation lasted until 1941, and was replaced by a [British](31717.md) [military administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_administration). Former British Somaliland would remain, along with Italian Somaliland, a [trusteeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Trusteeship) of Italy, between 1950 and 1960. The Union of the two countries in 1960 formed the Somali Republic. A civilian government was formed, and on 20 July 1961, through a popular [referendum](51582.md), the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) drafted in 1960 was ratified.[^cite_note-111]

Due to its longstanding ties with the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), the Somali Republic was accepted in 1974 as a member of the [Arab League](52625.md).[^cite_note-Frankel-112] During the same year, the nation's former [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) administration also chaired the [Organization of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_African_Unity), the predecessor of the [African Union](10634933.md).[^cite_note-Yang-113] In 1991, the [Somali Civil War](3070332.md) broke out, which saw the dissolving of the union and Somaliland regaining its independence, along with the collapse of the central government and the emergence of numerous autonomous polities, including the [Puntland](233569.md) administration in the north.[^cite_note-NYT-114] Somalia's inhabitants subsequently reverted to local forms of conflict resolution, either [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)), [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_law) or [customary law](218798.md), with a provision for appeal of all sentences. A [Transitional Federal Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Federal_Government) was subsequently created in 2004.[^cite_note-2009factbook-115] The [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md) was established on 20 August 2012, concurrent with the end of the TFG's interim mandate.[^cite_note-Fcacsunesinpshm-116] It represents the first permanent central government in the country since the start of the civil war.[^cite_note-Fcacsunesinpshm-116] The [Federal Parliament of Somalia](36817385.md) serves as the government's [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) branch.[^cite_note-Gttsdpc-117]

&#x20;Modern Ethiopia and its current borders are a result of significant territorial reduction in the north and expansion in the east and south toward its present borders, owing to several migrations, commercial integration, treaties as well as conquests, particularly by [Emperor Menelik II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelik_II_of_Ethiopia) and [Ras Gobena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Gobena).[^cite_note-Young-118] From the central province of Shoa, Menelik set off to subjugate and incorporate 'the lands and people of the South, East and West into an empire.'[^cite_note-Young-118][^cite_note-119] He did this with the help of Ras Gobena's Shewan Oromo militia, began expanding his kingdom to the south and east, expanding into areas that had not been held since the invasion of [Ahmad ibn Ibrihim al-Ghazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Ibrihim_al-Ghazi), and other areas that had never been under his rule, resulting in the borders of Ethiopia of today.[^cite_note-120] Menelik had signed the [Treaty of Wichale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Wichale) with Italy in May 1889, in which Italy would recognize Ethiopia's sovereignty so long as Italy could control a small area of northern Tigray (part of modern Eritrea).[^cite_note-121] In return, Italy was to provide Menelik with arms and support him as emperor.[^cite_note-Tekeste-122] The Italians used the time between the signing of the treaty and its ratification by the Italian government to further expand their territorial claims. Italy began a state funded program of resettlement for landless Italians in Eritrea, which increased tensions between the Eritrean peasants and the Italians.[^cite_note-Tekeste-122] This conflict erupted in the [Battle of Adwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Adwa) on 1 March 1896, in which Italy's colonial forces were defeated by the Ethiopians.[^cite_note-123]



Somali engineers repair a captured Ethiopian [T-34/85 Model 1969 tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-34_tank) for use by the [Western Somali Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Somali_Liberation_Front) during the [Ogaden War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_War), March 1978.

The early 20th century in Ethiopia was marked by the reign of Emperor [Haile Selassie I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie_I_of_Ethiopia), who came to power after [Iyasu V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyasu_V_of_Ethiopia) was deposed. In 1935, Haile Selassie's troops fought and lost the [Second Italo-Abyssinian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italo-Abyssinian_War), after which Italy annexed Ethiopia to [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa).[^cite_note-124] Haile Selassie subsequently appealed to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations), delivering an address that made him a worldwide figure and 1935's [*Time* magazine Man of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-125] Following the entry of Italy into World War II, [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) forces, together with patriot Ethiopian fighters, liberated Ethiopia during the [East African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Campaign_\(World_War_II\)) in 1941.[^cite_note-126]

Haile Selassie's reign came to an end in 1974, when a Soviet-backed [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism-Leninism) [military junta](216815.md), the [Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg) led by [Mengistu Haile Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam), deposed him, and established a one-party [communist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state), which was called the [People's Democratic Republic of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Republic_of_Ethiopia). In July 1977, the [Ogaden War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_War) broke out after the Somalia government of [Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre) sought to incorporate the predominantly Somali-inhabited [Ogaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden) region into a Pan-Somali [Greater Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Somalia). By September 1977, the [Somali army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Somalia) controlled 90 percent of the Ogaden, but was later forced to withdraw after Ethiopia's Derg received assistance from the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR), [Cuba](5042481.md), [South Yemen](718749.md), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany)[^cite_note-127] and [North Korea](21255.md), including around 15,000 Cuban combat troops.

In 1989, the [Tigrayan Peoples' Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayan_Peoples'_Liberation_Front) (TPLF) merged with other ethnically based opposition movements to form the [Ethiopian Peoples' Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Peoples'_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF), and eventually managed to overthrow Mengistu's dictatorial regime in 1991. A transitional government, composed of an 87-member Council of Representatives and guided by a national charter that functioned as a transitional constitution, was then set up. The first free and democratic election took place later in 1995, when Ethiopia's longest-serving prime minister [Meles Zenawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meles_Zenawi) was elected to office. As with other nations in the Horn region, Ethiopia maintained its historically close relations with countries in the Middle East during this period of change.[^cite_note-128] Zenawi died in 2012, but his [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF) party remains the ruling political coalition in Ethiopia.

## Geography

### Geology and climate



The Horn of Africa as seen from the [NASA](18426568.md) [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) in May 1993. The orange and tan colors in this image indicate a largely arid to semiarid climate.

The Horn of Africa is almost [equidistant](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/equidistant) from the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) and the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer). It consists chiefly of mountains uplifted through the formation of the [Great Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley_\(geographical_concept\)), a fissure in the Earth's [crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crust_\(geology\)) extending from [Turkey](11125639.md) to [Mozambique](19301.md) and marking the separation of the African and Arabian [tectonic plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics). Mostly mountainous, the region arose through faults resulting from the Rift Valley.

Geologically, the Horn and [Yemen](350939.md) once formed a single landmass around 18 million years ago, before the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md) rifted and separated the Horn region from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md).[^cite_note-Ranrestar-129][^cite_note-Rrloageappabh-130] The [Somali Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Plate) is bounded on the west by the East African Rift, which stretches south from the [triple junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_junction) in the [Afar Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_Depression), and an undersea continuation of the rift extending southward offshore. The northern boundary is the [Aden Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Ridge) along the coast of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). The eastern boundary is the [Central Indian Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Indian_Ridge), the northern portion of which is known as the [Carlsberg Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Ridge). The southern boundary is the [Southwest Indian Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Indian_Ridge).

Extensive [glaciers](12463.md) once covered the [Simien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simien_Mountains) and [Bale Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bale_Mountains) but melted at the beginning of the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene).[^cite_note-131] The mountains descend in a huge [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) to the [Red Sea](26191.md) and more steadily to the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra) is a small island in the Indian Ocean off the coast of Somalia. Its size is 3,600 km<sup>2</sup> (1,400 sq mi) and it is a territory of Yemen.

The [lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland) of the Horn are generally arid in spite of their proximity to the equator. This is because the winds of the tropical [monsoons](57630.md) that give seasonal rains to the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) and the [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_\(region\)) blow from the west. Consequently, they lose their moisture before reaching Djibouti and northern part of Somalia, with the result that most of the Horn receives little rainfall during the monsoon season.[^cite_note-Desert-132]



The Horn of Africa. [NASA](18426568.md) image

In the mountains of Ethiopia, many areas receive over 2,000 mm (79 in) per year, and even [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) receives an average of 570 mm (22 in). This rainfall is the sole source of water for many areas outside Ethiopia, including [Egypt](8087628.md). In the winter, the northeasterly [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_wind) do not provide any moisture except in mountainous areas of northern Somalia, where rainfall in late autumn can produce annual totals as high as 500 mm (20 in). On the eastern coast, a strong [upwelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upwelling) and the fact that the winds blow parallel to the coast means annual rainfall can be as low as 50 mm (2.0 in).[^cite_note-Desert-132]

The climate in Ethiopia varies considerably between regions. It is generally hotter in the lowlands and temperate on the plateau. At [Addis Ababa](52593.md), which ranges from 2,200 to 2,600 m (7,218 to 8,530 ft), maximum temperature is 26 °C (78.8 °F) and minimum 4 °C (39.2 °F). The weather is usually sunny and dry, but the short (*belg*) rains occur from February to April and the big (*meher*) rains from mid-June to mid-September. The [Danakil Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Desert) stretches across 100,000 km<sup>2</sup> of arid terrain in northeast Ethiopia, southern Eritrea, and northwestern Djibouti. The area is known for its volcanoes and extreme heat, with daily temperatures over 45 °C and often surpassing 50 °C. It has a number of lakes formed by lava flows that dammed up several valleys. Among these are [Lake Asale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Karum) (116 m below sea level) and [Lake Giuletti/Afrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Afrera) (80 m below sea level), both of which possess cryptodepressions in the [Danakil Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Depression). The Afrera contains many active volcanoes, including the Maraho, [Dabbahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabbahu_Volcano), [Afdera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afdera_\(volcano\)) and [Erta Ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erta_Ale).[^cite_note-Stobin-133][^cite_note-Etpame-134]

In Somalia, there is not much seasonal variation in climate. Hot conditions prevail year-round along with periodic [monsoon](57630.md) winds and irregular rainfall. Mean daily maximum temperatures range from 28 to 43 °C (82 to 109 °F), except at higher elevations along the eastern seaboard, where the effects of a cold offshore current can be felt. Somalia has only two permanent rivers, the [Jubba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubba_River) and the [Shabele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebelle_River), both of which begin in the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands).[^cite_note-Hadden-135]

### Ecology



*[Oryx beisa beisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryx_beisa_beisa)* is found throughout the Horn of Africa.

About 220 [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) are found in the Horn of Africa. Among threatened [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) of the region, there are several [antelopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope) such as the [beira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beira_\(antelope\)), the [dibatag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibatag), the [silver dikdik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_dikdik) and the [Speke's gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speke's_gazelle). Other remarkable species include the [Somali wild ass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_wild_ass), the [desert warthog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_warthog), the [hamadryas baboon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadryas_baboon), the [Somali pygmy gerbil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia_gerbil), the [ammodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammodile), and the [Speke's pectinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundi). The [Grevy's zebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grevy's_zebra) is the unique wild [equid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equidae) of the region. There are predators such as [spotted hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_hyena), [striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena) and [African leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard). The [endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered) [painted hunting dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painted_hunting_dog) had populations in the Horn of Africa, but pressures from human exploitation of habitat along with warfare have reduced or extirpated this canid in this region.[^cite_note-136]

Some important bird species of the Horn are the [black boubou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_boubou), the [golden-winged grosbeak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden-winged_grosbeak), the [Warsangli linnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsangli_linnet), and the [Djibouti spurfowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_spurfowl).

The Horn of Africa holds more [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(ecology\)) [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile) than any other region in Africa, with over 285 species total and about 90 species which are found exclusively in the region. Among endemic reptile genera, there are *[Haackgreerius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haackgreerius)*, *[Haemodracon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemodracon)*, *[Ditypophis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditypophis)*, *[Pachycalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachycalamus)* and *[Aeluroglena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeluroglena)*. Half of these genera are uniquely found on Socotra. Unlike reptiles, [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian) are poorly represented in the region.

There are about 100 species of [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater) [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) in the Horn of Africa, about 10 of which are endemic. Among the endemic, three [cavefishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavefish), [Somali blind barb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_blind_barb), *[Phreatichthys andruzzii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreatichthys_andruzzii)* and *[Uegitglanis zammaranoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uegitglanis_zammaranoi)* can be found.



[Myrrh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrrh), a common resin in the Horn

It is estimated that about 5,000 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant) are found in the Horn, about half of which are endemic. Endemism is most developed in Socotra and northern Somalia. The region has two endemic plant [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_\(taxonomy\)): the [Barbeyaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbeyaceae) and the [Dirachmaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirachmaceae). Among the other remarkable species, there are the [cucumber tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucumber_tree) found only on Socotra, the [Bankoualé palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankoual%C3%A9_palm), the [yeheb nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordeauxia_edulis), and the Somali [cyclamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclamen).

Due to the Horn of Africa's [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) and [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) climate, droughts are not uncommon. They are complicated by climate change and changes in agricultural practices. For centuries, the region's [pastoral groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) have observed careful [rangeland management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangeland_management) practices to mitigate the effects of drought, such as avoiding overgrazing or setting aside land only for young or ill animals. However, population growth has put pressure on limited land and led to these practices no longer being maintained. Droughts in 1983–85, 1991–92, 1998–99 and 2011 have disrupted periods of gradual growth in herd numbers, leading to a decrease of between 37 and 62 percent of the cattle population. Initiatives by [ECHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate-General_for_European_Civil_Protection_and_Humanitarian_Aid_Operations) and [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID) have succeeded in reclaiming hundreds of hectares of pastureland through rangeland management, leading to the establishment of the Dikale Rangeland in 2004.[^cite_note-ODI_2009-137] 2023 began with the continuation of a severe multiyear drought across the Horn of Africa, affecting over 36 million people. Consecutive failed rainy seasons led to the death of over 13 million livestock in Somalia, Ethiopia and Kenya.[^cite_note-138]

As of 2023, the Horn of Africa is affected by a severe [ongoing drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%93present_Horn_of_Africa_drought) and famine that has been going on for six consecutive years, especially in Somalia and in the months from March to May during which 60 percent of the annual rainfall occurs. It is estimated that the lives of a number of people ranging from 22[^cite_note-139] to 58 million[^cite_note-140] are at risk.

## Economy



Coffee beans from Ethiopia

According to the IMF, in 2010 the Horn of Africa region had a total GDP (PPP) of \$106.224 billion and nominal of \$35.819 billion. Per capita, the GDP in 2010 was \$1061 (PPP) and \$358 (nominal).[^cite_note-imf.org-141][^cite_note-ReferenceA-142][^cite_note-ReferenceB-143][^cite_note-cia.gov-144]

Over 95 percent of cross-border trade within the region is unofficial and undocumented, carried out by pastoralists trading livestock.[^cite_note-ODI_2010-145] The unofficial trade of live cattle, camels, sheep and goats from [Ethiopia](187749.md) sold to other countries in the Horn and the wider Eastern Africa region, including [Somalia](27358.md) and [Djibouti](17207794.md), generates an estimated total value of between US\$250 and US\$300 million annually (100 times more than the official figure), with the towns of [Burao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burao) and [Yirowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yirowe) in Somaliland being home to the largest livestock markets in the Horn of Africa, with as many as 10,000 heads of sheep and goats sold daily from all over the Horn of Africa, many of which are shipped to [Gulf states](3088555.md) via the [port](41240501.md) of [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera).[^cite_note-ODI_2010-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] This trade helps lower [food prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_prices), increase food security, relieve border tensions and promote regional integration.[^cite_note-ODI_2010-145] The unregulated and undocumented nature of this trade runs risks, such as allowing disease to spread across national borders, and governments are unhappy with lost tax revenue and foreign exchange revenues.[^cite_note-ODI_2010-145]
