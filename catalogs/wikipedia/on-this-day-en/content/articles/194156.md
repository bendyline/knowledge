**Villahermosa** ([/ˌviː(j)əɛərˈmoʊsə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*VEE-(y)ə-air-MOH-sə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Spanish: [\[ˌbiʝaeɾˈmosa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/97/Villahermosa.ogg/Villahermosa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/97/Villahermosa.ogg/Villahermosa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; "beautiful town") is the capital and largest city of the [Mexican state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_state) of [Tabasco](194150.md), and serves as the [municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Mexico) seat (governing county) of the state. Located in Southeast Mexico, Villahermosa is known for its cultural history, natural resources, commercial development, and modern industrialization.

Coined *La Esmeralda del Sureste* ("The [Emerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald) of the Southeast"), Villahermosa is a modern city with history dating back to the early 1500s. Its natural resources like cacao, sugarcane, bananas, tobacco, rice, and hardwood trees have made Villahermosa attractive to domestic investors.[^cite_note-3] The city has become a hub for oil and gas operations in Southern Mexico and is referred to as the "Energy City of Mexico". The most recent oil discovery, at the Ogarrio oil field[^cite_note-4] just 107 km west of Villahermosa, make it an important city in the production of hydrocarbons. Commercially, the city is popular with major retailers like [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_\(store\)), [Fábricas de Fráncia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A1bricas_de_Francia), [Palacio de Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_Hierro_\(store\)), [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart), [City Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Club_\(wholesale_club\)), [Costco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costco), [Soriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soriana), [Chedraui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chedraui), [Comercial Mexicana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comercial_Mexicana) and [Home Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Depot). National banks [Santander Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_M%C3%A9xico) and [Banamex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Financiero_Banamex) serve the city's financial needs.

## Etymology

In 1598, King [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) named the city "Villa Hermosa of San Juan Bautista", and granted it use of a royal shield on its coat of arms, which is still in use today. During the city's long history, its name has changed many times. In 1916, governor [Francisco J. Múgica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_J._M%C3%BAgica) decreed to eliminate the name of "San Juan Bautista", leaving only "Villahermosa".[^cite_note-5]

Throughout the city's history, Villahermosa has had many names:

- *San Juan Bautista* (1565): in honor of the city's patron saint.

- *Villa Felipe II:* in honor of the Spanish monarch.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorruco_Saravia198731-7]

## Symbols of the city

### Coat of arms

On July 24, 1598, King [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) granted the village of San Juan Bautista the title of *Villa Hermosa de San Juan Bautista*, and afforded it a royal [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms). In 1892, State Congress adopted the city's coat of arms as the official shield of the State of [Tabasco](194150.md).

The shield incorporates a quarterly heraldry design with a center [inescutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inescutcheon). The coat of arms depicts the Spanish Imperial Crown for King [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), who served as Holy Roman Emperor and King.

The first quarter [gules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gules) features four castle towers representing the [Kingdom of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile), the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon), the [Nasrid Kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrid_Kingdom_of_Granada), and the Mediterranean Sea, four territories of land and sea. The four castles refer to the four lordships that initially carved up the area after it was conquered by [Alfonso X of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile).[^cite_note-13]

The second quarter [argent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argent) features a military arm wielding a two-edged sword, representing Spanish power over a province.

The third quarter argent features an [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) woman wearing a feathered skirt in shades of sinople red and green, ribbons on her forearms, and flower bouquets in each hand.

The fourth quarter gules features a crowned lion with a protruding tongue representing the ancient [Kingdom of León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Le%C3%B3n).[^cite_note-14]

The center inescutcheon features a crowned [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary) dressed in [gules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gules) and [azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azure_\(heraldry\)) and the [Pillars of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Hercules) tipped with globes. Written on the Pillars of Hercules is the motto “Plus Ultra“ reflecting overseas expansion. Charles V incorporated this symbolism in reference to the then-recent [European discovery of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_discovery_of_America).[^cite_note-15]

## Geography

Villahermosa is located approximately 50 kilometres (31 mi) south of the Gulf Coast and about 904 kilometres (562 mi) southeast of [Mexico City](18987.md). The municipality is in the center of [Tabasco](194150.md), which adjoins the northern municipalities of [Nacajuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacajuca) and [Centla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centla), and the southern municipalities of [Jalapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalapa_Municipality_\(Tabasco\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Teapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teapa_\(municipality\)) and the south-central municipality of [Macuspana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macuspana). It also borders the northwestern part of the State of [Chiapas](6787.md). The geographical coordinates of the municipality are north 18-20', south 17-43' north latitude; 92-35', west 93-15' west.

The urban area of Villahermosa occupies 61,177 km2 while the territorial extension of the municipality occupies 1,612 km2, which corresponds to 6.9% of the total state territory, occupying 7th place on the municipal extension scale. The territorial division of the [municipality of Centro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Centro_\(Tabasco\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) consists of a city, 7 villas, 1 village, 132 rancheries, and 117 boroughs (colonial) and fractions. The municipality contains 13 regional development centers (CORs) where most economic and social activities are carried out, including [Villa Ocuiltzapoltán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocuiltzapolt%C3%A1n?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Macultepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macultepec), [Villa Parrilla 1a. Section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Parrilla_1a._Section?action=edit\&redlink=1), Villa Subteniente García, [Rosario Beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario_Beaches?action=edit\&redlink=1), Villa [New People of Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People_of_Roots?action=edit\&redlink=1), Poblado [Dos Montes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dos_Montes?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Los Boquerones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Boquerones), [Villa Luis Gil Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Luis_Gil_P%C3%A9rez?action=edit\&redlink=1), and Villa [Tamulte de Las Sabanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamulte_de_Las_Sabanas?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Today, Villahermosa is a modern, industrialized city and one of the most important commercial points in the long stretch of territory between [Mexico City](18987.md) and [Cancún](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn)—perhaps second only to [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Yucat%C3%A1n) in [Yucatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n).

### Natural resources



Museo de Antropología Carlos Pellicer

Villahermosa is located in the southeast of Mexico, between the rivers [Grijalva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grijalva_River) and Carrizal. The average elevation of the city is 10 meters above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). The city also has several lagoons, the largest and most important of them being *La Laguna de las Ilusiones* ("Lagoon of Illusions").

The most important city thoroughfares are Paseo Tabasco, Gregorio Méndez Avenue, the Periférico, Ruiz Cortines Avenue, and Pino Suárez Avenue.

### Climate

Like most of Tabasco, Villahermosa has a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) (*Am*). Temperatures during the spring and summer seasons can reach upwards of 40 °C (104 °F), with humidity levels hovering around 30% during the same period (for total humidity-adjusted temperatures in the high forties). During its short "winter", Villahermosa's climate is very humid but daytime temperatures decrease to around 28 °C (82 °F).

In October 2007, Villahermosa suffered [its worst flood in recorded history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Tabasco_flood). Several hundred thousand people were displaced because of flooded homes.

## Demographics

According to the World Population Review 2021, there are 913,606 inhabitants in the Metropolitan area of Villahermosa, a 1.69% growth rate.[^cite_note-21] Although the first inhabitants settled in 1557, the official occupation occurred on June 24, 1564.

## History

See also: [Timeline of Villahermosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Villahermosa)



Main square in the Historic Center

Founded officially on 24 June 1564 by the [Spanish](26667.md) explorer [Diego de Quijada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Quijada?action=edit\&redlink=1) on the banks of the [Grijalva River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grijalva_River) under the name of *Villa Hermosa*, in 1826 the village was raised to the rank of city under the name of *San Juan Bautista de la Villa Hermosa* (Saint [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist)). During the [French intervention in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_intervention_in_Mexico), French troops [occupied the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Juan_Bautista) in 1863. In 1916, the governor of Tabasco, [Francisco J. Múgica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_J._M%C3%BAgica), ordered the restoration of the city's name to *Villahermosa*.[^cite_note-22]

### Colonial Age

The Villa Carmona was later christened San Juan Bautista and was divided into neighborhoods. The oldest of them was the neighborhood of Esquipulas, on the ridge of the hill of Esquipulas and around the Church of Our Lord of Esquipulas, with his image of Christ black, which had been brought from [Esquipulas, Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquipulas).

By 1596, [Lazaro Suárez de Córdova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazaro_Su%C3%A1rez_de_C%C3%B3rdova?action=edit\&redlink=1) was Mayor of Tabasco and continued to dispatch in [Santa Maria de la Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_de_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_la_Victoria) as the capital, and because the pirates had been attacking much of the coast of Tabasco, it commands to build in San Juan Bautista (today Villahermosa) the Fort House or Royal Warehouse. This was in order to defend the population and safeguard the flows Real. The Royal Warehouse was located on what are the current streets of Juárez, Reforma, Madero and Lerdo.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorruco_Saravia198731-7]

On July 24, 1598, the King granted the city of San Juan Bautista the title of *Villa Hermosa de San Juan Bautista* as well as a royal shield, which currently identifies the state of [Tabasco](194150.md) and is one of the oldest in the Americas.

In 1604, the Mayor of Tabasco [Juan de Miranda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Miranda?action=edit\&redlink=1) requested the Viceroy [Juan de Mendoza and Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Mendoza_and_Luna?action=edit\&redlink=1) to authorize the powers to be changed from [Santa Maria de la Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_de_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_la_Victoria) to Saint John the Baptist (now Villahermosa) in addition to requesting that the name of the city be changed to *San Juan de Villahermosa*, authorizing the change of name of the city, not so the transfer of powers.



Philip II of Spain

King [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) gave the city the title of **Villa Hermosa** as well as a [Coat of Tabasco's Royal Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Tabasco's_Royal_Shield?action=edit\&redlink=1). As the pirate attacks did not cease, and they continually destroyed and raided the village of [Santa María de la Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_de_Santa_Maria_de_la_Victoria), in the year 1619, the change of the powers of the province to San Juan Bautista before the Marquis de Guadalcázar Diego Fernández de Córdoba, authorizing it on December 3 of that same year. However, was re-established. The move was not made because many residents did not want to leave Santa Maria de la Victoria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorruco_Saravia198734-23]

### Capital of Tabasco for the first time

In 1640, due to the continuous pirate attacks on the village of [Santa María de la Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_de_Santa_Maria_de_la_Victoria), the authorities of the Province requested the Viceroy [Diego López Pacheco and Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_L%C3%B3pez_Pacheco_and_Portugal?action=edit\&redlink=1) to change the capital of the Province to San Juan de Villahermosa, which the Viceroy authorized on 3 February 1641. The transfer of powers took effect on June 24 of that same year, making Villahermosa the new capital of the Province of [Tabasco](194150.md). The "Fortín de la Encarnación" was immediately built in what is now the park of "the birds" on the corner that forms the current streets of May 5 and Zaragoza at the foot of the hill of "La Encarnación".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorruco_Saravia198732-24]

In 1677, as Mayor [Diego de Loyola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Loyola?action=edit\&redlink=1), the privateers fiercely attacked the capital Villahermosa de San Juan Bautista, forcing the authorities to leave the city and transfer the powers to the village of [Tacotalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacotalpa), which served as capital of the province for the next 118 years. On June 21, 1677, the Mayor of Tabasco informed the Viceroy that the fear of pirate invasions had caused the depopulation of entire villages such as Cunduacán, and that Villahermosa, which was the capital, had depopulated, too.

In 1688, lookouts, trenches and fortifications were built in Villahermosa to protect the desolated city from pirate attacks. However, in 1711, Villahermosa was again fiercely attacked by English pirates, who set fire to the Royal Warehouse that was the Government House, and the defenders had to take refuge in the Fortín de la Encarnación, which withstood the attack, though the pirates burned down many houses, leaving the town in ruins.



Church of the Conception, built in 1800 in front of the [Plaza de Armas de Villahermosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_Armas_de_Villahermosa?action=edit\&redlink=1)

It was not until July 16, 1717, when the then-Mayor of Tabasco [Alonso Felipe Andrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_Felipe_Andrade?action=edit\&redlink=1), at the head of an army of Tabasco and Veracruz forces, attacked the pirates on the Island of Tris (now the island of Carmen) which at the time belonged to Tabasco. Though Andrade was killed, the Tabasco forces expelled the pirates and founded that same day the military post of *Nuestra Señora del Carmen* (today [City of Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Carmen?action=edit\&redlink=1)). However, in 1757, the English pirates counterattacked the military post of Nuestra Señora del Carmen, setting it on fire and destroying it, and then re-attacking the Tabasco coasts.

Finally, in 1785, the Tabasco militias, commanded by Captain [Juan de Amestoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Amestoy?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Lieutenant Francisco Interiano, defeated and permanently evicted the English from Carmen Island.[^cite_note-25]

### Capital of Tabasco for the second time

In January 1795 the Viceroy [Miguel de la Grúa Talamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_de_la_Gr%C3%BAa_Talamanca?action=edit\&redlink=1) authorized the change of the powers of the Province of [Tabasco](194150.md) from [Tacotalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacotalpa) to Villa Hermosa de San Juan Bautista, taking effect on Monday, August 15 of that year. Thus, after 118 years, Villahermosa is again the capital of Tabasco.

The neighborhood of La Punta or La Concepción was the next growth step of San Juan that transcended the first natural boundaries of the site. Developed around the church of the Immaculate Conception - "La Conchita"-, the neighborhood was also known as La Punta for being at the southern end of the city, on a hillside of the hill of La Eminence, between the Grijalva River, the lagoon of La Pólvora and the primary trace. The last district dating from the late colonial era, from the early years of the nineteenth century, was the neighborhood of La Santa Cruz, with its center in the church of the same name. This neighborhood was developed on marshy and grassland land north of the city center.

The city lived peacefully during the last years of the Colony, being an important river port.

#### Garridism

This time began in 1928 with the arrival of [Tomás Garrido Canabal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_Garrido_Canabal) in the state government. Several times Governor of Tabasco, he created the organization "red shirts" which, in addition to his political activity of support, deployed a paramilitary battle against the Catholic Church in the state. As authority he expelled the bishop, the priests and decreed several prohibitions concerning worship, religious feasts, Catholic funeral rites, including domestic worship and even the mere mention of God. He closed the temples and gave them a new use, turning them into "rationalist" schools, military barracks or libraries. Later, the temples, the center of the old quarters, were demolished. The images were burned, but the Christians hid several of them in the "mountain", including the black Christ of Esquipulas and also the image of the Virgin Mary brought by [Hernán Cortés' Courtesy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s'_Courtesy?action=edit\&redlink=1). Life linked to worship and other religious celebrations virtually disappeared in Garrido's years of authority, whose influence he left feeling even after his last governorship in 1934.

#### Contemporary era

In the 1940s and 1950s, the expansion of Villahermosa was made up of developments by state governments and landowners who prepared and fractioned land that had previously been agricultural, grassland or grassland use forest, thus creating the first boroughs (colonial). Additionally, the installation of drains, stormwater ducts, etc. began to change also the physiognomy of some old streets. Juan Perez Arrollave, an Infantry Lieutenant who participated in the Mexican Revolution, was as municipal president from 1946 to 1950 and a personal friend of Plutarco Elias Calles.

The old Saint John the Baptist now became the "center" of a larger city. The babbling of a new urban phenomenon began to manifest itself: metropolization.

When Tabasco entered the "age of capital" – fully integrating into the country's economic development through oil exploitation and the construction of road, hydro-agricultural and hydroelectric infrastructure in the 1950s-1960s – Villahermosa began an important commercial development. Towns such as Tamulté, Atasta, and Tierra Colorada became populous boroughs (colonias) of the new city.

By 1970, Villahermosa was considered a modern city due to its architectural dynamism that began in the last century, when the state of [Tabasco](194150.md) became one of the main oil regions of the country, which changed the urban geography of Villahermosa.

Buildings and large recreation centers changed the physiognomy of the state capital, being the area called Tabasco 2000, the main area of progress, the best example of the rejuvenated city. Places located in Tabasco 2000 include the Municipal Powers, the Administrative Center of Government, office buildings, banks, hotels, restaurants, shopping squares, and residential areas.

Congress of the State; the High Court of Justice, the headquarters of the Judiciary, and the Church of the Conception, opened on 8 December 1800 but destroyed by the American bombing sprees in 1846 and 1847 and by the French in 1863 and 1864.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorruco_Saravia198747-49] There are other buildings of historical value in the area, such as the House of Tiles (now the Museum of History), the ancient Juarez Institute (now house of culture of the [UJAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UJAT?action=edit\&redlink=1)), the museum house of the poet [Carlos Pellicer Camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Pellicer_Camera?action=edit\&redlink=1) and others buildings that are now art galleries or businesses.

The heart of the [Historic Center of Villahermosa's historic center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Villahermosa's_historic_center?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the so-called *Light Zone'*, formed by Juarez, Lerdo, Aldama, and Sáenz streets, Reforma, which are the oldest in the city and which have been transformed into pedestrian streets, where there are cafes, restaurants, neverías, clothing stores, etc., and they constitute the commercial heart of the city. Also within the [Historic Center of Villahermosa's old town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Center_of_Villahermosa's_old_town?action=edit\&redlink=1) are located the Park of the Corregidora, the Juarez Park, the Square of the Eagle and the Square Bicentennial, with a metallic [obelísco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obel%C3%ADsco?action=edit\&redlink=1) of 30 meters high.

Outside the historic center, the city features modern architecture. Its rapid development since the 1970s caused it to overflow into areas that were formerly formed by lagoons, grasslands and swamps, and to absorb other communities, such as [Atasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atasta?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tamulté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamult%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Red Land, now populous boroughs (colonias). Its most important roads that link the city of Villahermosa between different points are: Av.[Tabasco Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabasco_Walk?action=edit\&redlink=1): from the Gaviotas Bridge to the Peripheral Arco Noreste, crossing sites such as the Main Cemetery, the [Cathedral of the Lord of Tabasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Lord_of_Tabasco?action=edit\&redlink=1), crosses the [Laguna de las Ilusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_las_Ilusions?action=edit\&redlink=1), the tourist complex [Tabasco 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabasco_2000?action=edit\&redlink=1), Gregorio Mendez Magaña Av., Av. 27 De Febrero, Av. [Paseo Usumacinta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paseo_Usumacinta?action=edit\&redlink=1), Av. Francisco J. Mina, El [Boulevard Adolfo Ruiz Cortines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_Adolfo_Ruiz_Cortines?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Inner circuit Carlos Pellicer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_circuit_Carlos_Pellicer?action=edit\&redlink=1) communicate to the different points of importance of the city.



Indoor Circuíto "Carlos Pellicer Camera", expanded to 8 lanes, surrounds the urban area of the city.

In the west of the city is located the most important commercial and financial area called Tabasco 2000, urban complex built in the 1980s as a result of the strong oil activity, there are located important commercial squares, banks, restaurants, hotels and high-class residential developments. Tall and modern office buildings, they account for the important economic development of the city.

To the north is the Industrial City with its two stages, and housing areas of social interest and middle class. To the south, the mountains and higher areas have led to the proliferation of shopping centers and housing and residential developments.

Over the past 10 years, the lack of suitable land for the construction of housing and the new reordering that prohibits construction in low and flood areas of the city, has led to the growth of housing developments to the outskirts of the city, promoting the growth of the urban spot towards nearby towns, now integrated into the so-called [Metropolitan Area of Villahermosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Area_of_Villahermosa?action=edit\&redlink=1), promoting the development of populations such as Villa Parrilla, Macultepec, Ocuiltzapotlán, Playas del Rosario, Río Viejo, Buenavista, Ixtacomitán and Boquerón where most housing fractions currently take place.

### Tourism



The Zoological Park "Yumká" is one of the main tourist attractions of the city.

In addition to this, Villahermosa is one of the main congress and convention destinations in the country. In 2010 alone, 61 congresses and conventions were held with a total of 40,933 attendees,[^cite_note-50] highlighting that the city is the permanent seat of the "Mexico's International Petroleum Congress and Conference"' which is held each year and brings together just over 3,000 attendees from all over the world.

The city has more than 4,000 hotel rooms,[^cite_note-51] large shopping, entertainment, dining and infrastructure.

It has an international airport, which has flights to and from the main cities of the country; by land, roads link it to the center and south of the country; and by sea, the commercial port of [Two Mouths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Mouths?action=edit\&redlink=1) (80 km from the city), has the capacity to receive cargo ships and cruise ships up to 10 m in line, in addition to having port infrastructure.
