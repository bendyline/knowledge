Beit furic

**Beit Furik** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بيت فوريك) is a town located nine kilometers southeast of [Nablus](233369.md), in the [Nablus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Governorate) of the northern [West Bank](33209.md), [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories). According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics), the town had a population of 13,477 inhabitants in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1]

## Location

Beit Furik is located 8.24 km south east of [Nablus](233369.md). It is bordered by [Al Jiftlik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jiftlik) to the east, [Ar Rajman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ar_Rajman), [Yanun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanun), and ['Awarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Awarta) to the south, [Rujeib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rujeib) and Nablus to the west, and [Beit Dajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Dajan,_Nablus), [Salim](18049378.md) and [Deir al Hatab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_al_Hatab) to the north.[^cite_note-\:1-3]

## History

Bayt Furik was an ancient [Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans) village.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Old [tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb) have been found here.[^cite_note-Dauphin848-5]

The place is mentioned in the Samaritan [Tolidah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolidah) and in the *Continuatio of the Samaritan Chronicle of Abu l-Fath*.[^cite_note-\:0-4] It has also been suggested that this place is mentioned in the [Samaritan Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_Chronicle).[^cite_note-6] [Neubauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Neubauer), and others, suggested that it was the place called *Ferka* in the Talmud,[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] but [Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix-Marie_Abel) suggested locating that at [Farkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farkha).[^cite_note-Dauphin848-5][^cite_note-9]

Samaritan author [Benyamim Tsedaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benyamim_Tsedaka?action=edit\&redlink=1) lists two Samaritan families, Maarhib and Qaakai, who resided in Beit Furik before their eventual disappearance through destruction or conversion.[^cite_note-10]

In the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states) era, it was known as *Bethflori*,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-Finkelstein814-12] and in 1241 CE there was fought a battle here, according to [Ibn el-Jawzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibt_ibn_al-Jawzi).[^cite_note-Finkelstein814-12]

### Ottoman era

In 1517, the village was included in the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) with the rest of [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Palestine#Ottoman_period), and in the 1596 [tax-records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) it appeared as *Bayt Furik*, located in the *[Nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* of Jabal Qubal, part of [Nablus Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak). The population was 68 households, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). They paid a fixed tax rate of 33,3% on agricultural products, such as wheat, barley, summer crops, olive trees, goats and beehives, a press for olive oil or grape syrup, in addition to occasional revenues and a fixed tax for people of Nablus area; a total of 16,665 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-13]

In 1838, [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) noted it on his travels in the area,[^cite_note-14] and as part of the *El-Beitawy* district, east of Nablus.[^cite_note-15]

In 1870, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) noted *Beit Foureik* sitting on the slopes of a hill, with a belt of olives surrounding it.[^cite_note-16]

In 1882, the [Palestine Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* described it as: "A small village in a nook of the hills near the plain of [Salim](18049378.md). It has a [well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_well) to the east."[^cite_note-17]

During the 19th century and mid-20th century, Beit Furik was the main supplier of lime to the [Nabulsi soap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabulsi_soap) industry based in Nablus.[^cite_note-18]

### British Mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), *Bait Furik* had a population of 744 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim)[^cite_note-Census1922a-19] increasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine), where Beit Furik (together with the smaller location Kh. Beita) had a population of 867 Muslims, in a total of 262 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-20]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) Beit Furik (including Kh. Kafr Beita) had a population of 1,240, all Muslims,[^cite_note-1945p18-21] with 36,663 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land, according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-22] Of this, 2,645 dunams were plantations and irrigable land, 12,453 used for cereals,[^cite_note-23] while 53 dunams were built-up land.[^cite_note-24]

### Jordanian era

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Beit Furik came under [Jordanian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank).

In 1961, the population of Beit Furik was 1,997 persons.[^cite_note-25]

### 1967, aftermath

Since the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Beit Furik has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md) along with the rest of the Palestinian territories. The population in the 1967 census conducted by Israel was 2,416, of whom 7 originated from the Israeli territory.[^cite_note-67census-26]

Under the interim [Oslo Peace Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Peace_Accords), areas of the West Bank were divided into various categories. According to [ARIJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Research_Institute_-_Jerusalem), 45% of the village land is in [Area B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves), while the remaining 55% is in [Area C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_C_\(West_Bank\)).[^cite_note-ARIJ17-27]



Entrance to Beit Furik



A monument in Beit Furik in memory of the villagers who have been killed since 1967

Israel has confiscated 441 dunums from Beit Furik for the two [Israeli settlements](15123.md) of [Itamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itamar_\(settlement\)) and [Michola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michola). In addition it has confiscated land for Israeli military bases, Israeli settlement roads, and Israeli fence.[^cite_note-ARIJ17-27]

## Demography

The inhabitants of Beit Furik belong to various families, such as the Al-Haj Mohammad, Hanani, Khatatbah, Mletat, and Nasasrah families.[^cite_note-\:1-3]
