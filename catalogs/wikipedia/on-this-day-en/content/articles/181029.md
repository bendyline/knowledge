**Ibaraki Prefecture** (茨城県, *Ibaraki-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[i.baꜜ.ɾa.kʲi, i.ba.ɾa.kʲi̥ꜜ.keɴ, i.ba.ɾaꜜ.kʲi̥.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-3]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-4] Ibaraki Prefecture has a population of 2,828,086 (1 July 2023) and has a geographic area of 6,097.19 square kilometres (2,354.14 square miles). Ibaraki Prefecture borders [Fukushima Prefecture](211914.md) to the north, [Tochigi Prefecture](180960.md) to the northwest, [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture) to the southwest, [Chiba Prefecture](179796.md) to the south, and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) to the east.

[Mito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito,_Ibaraki), the capital, is the largest city in Ibaraki Prefecture. Other major cities include [Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba,_Ibaraki), [Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi,_Ibaraki), and [Hitachinaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachinaka,_Ibaraki).[^cite_note-5] Ibaraki Prefecture is located on Japan's eastern [Pacific](23070.md) coast to the northeast of [Tokyo](30057.md), and is part of the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md), the most populous [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the world. Ibaraki Prefecture features [Lake Kasumigaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kasumigaura), the second-largest lake in Japan; the [Tone River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tone_River), Japan's second-longest river and largest [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin); and [Mount Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tsukuba), one of the most famous mountains in Japan. Ibaraki Prefecture is also home to [Kairaku-en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairaku-en), one of the [Three Great Gardens of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Great_Gardens_of_Japan), and is an important center for the [martial art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_art) of [Aikido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aikido).

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Ibaraki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Ibaraki_Prefecture)

Ibaraki Prefecture was previously known as [Hitachi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi_Province). In 1871, the name of the province became Ibaraki, and in 1875 it became its current size, by annexing some districts belonging to the extinct [Shimōsa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shim%C5%8Dsa_Province).



[Kamitakatsu Shell Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamitakatsu_Shell_Mound) in [Tsuchiura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuchiura)

### Paleolithic

In the [Japanese Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Paleolithic), humans are believed to have started living in the present-day prefecture area before and after the deposition of the volcanic ash layer from the [Aira Caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aira_Caldera) about 24,000 years ago. At the bottom of this layer are local tools of polished stone and burnt pebbles.

### Asuka period

During the [Asuka period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asuka_period) the provinces of Hitachi and [Fusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusa_Province) were created. Later Fusa was divided, among them, the Shimōsa Province.

### Muromachi period

At the beginning of the [Muromachi period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muromachi_period), in the 14th century, [Kitabatake Chikafusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitabatake_Chikafusa) made of the [Oda Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oda_Castle) his field headquarters for over a year, and wrote the [Jinnō Shōtōki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinn%C5%8D_Sh%C5%8Dt%C5%8Dki) (Chronicles of the Authentic Lineages of the Divine Emperors), while he was at castle.



Lake Kasumigaura in Ushibori Village (Hitachi Province), [Mount Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fuji) in the background; 19th century of the Edo period. [Hokusai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokusai), painter and printmaker

### Edo period

During the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), one of the three houses or clans originating from [Tokugawa Ieyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Ieyasu) (Gosanke 御 三家, three houses), settled in the [Mito Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito_Domain), the clan is known as the [Mito Tokugawa family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito_Tokugawa_family) or simply the Mito clan. Mito Domain, was a Japanese domain of the Edo-period [Hitachi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi_Province).

In 1657, a *[Mitogaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitogaku)* was created when [Tokugawa Mitsukuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Mitsukuni), head of the Mito Domain, commissioned the compilation of the *[Dai Nihonshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Nihonshi)*, a book on the history of Japan.

### Meiji era

During the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), old provinces were converted or merged to create the current prefectures, in this case Ibaraki Prefecture.

Ibaraki Prefecture is the northeastern part of the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region), stretching between [Tochigi Prefecture](180960.md) and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) and bounded on the north and south by [Fukushima Prefecture](211914.md) and [Chiba Prefecture](179796.md). It also has a border on the southwest with [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture). The northernmost part of the prefecture is mountainous, but most of the prefecture is a flat plain with many lakes and is part of [Kantō Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_Plain).

### Natural parks

As of 1 April 2012, 15% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely [Suigo-Tsukuba Quasi-National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suigo-Tsukuba_Quasi-National_Park), and nine Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-6] Also, Ibaraki has one Prefectural Geopark. The Suigo-Tsukuba Quasi-National Park, also includes the northeast area of Chiba Prefecture.

### Mountains

The northern third of the prefecture is mountainous and in the center is the Tsukuba Mountains (筑波 山地). Its main mountains are: [mount Yamizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Yamizo) with an elevation of 1022 m on the border with Fukushima and Tochigi prefectures ([tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint)), mount Takasasa with 922 m, [mount Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tsukuba) with two peaks Nyotai-San at 877 m and Nantai-San at 871 m, mount Osho at 804 m, mount Hanazono at 798 m, and [mount Kaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kaba_\(Ibaraki\)) at 709 m.

### Water system

The main rivers that flow through the prefecture include the [Tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tone_River), [Naka (Ibaraki)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naka_River_\(Tochigi_Ibaraki\)), and [Kuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuji_River_\(Ibaraki\)) rivers, all of which flow into the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). Before the seventeenth century, the lower reaches of the Tone were different from its current layout, and the Tone ran south and emptied into [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay), and tributaries such as the Watarase and Kinu rivers had independent water systems.

The main tributaries of the Tone River basin are the [Kinu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinugawa_River) and Kokai River, which flow from north to south in the western part of the prefecture. The Shintone and Sakura rivers flow into Lake Nishiura.

The [Edo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_River) flows into Tokyo Bay; its source currently rises as an arm of the Tone River. In the past, the course of the Edo River was different, its source was corrected and diverted to the Tone River in the 17th century by the Tokugawa shogunate to protect the city of [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo) (now Tokyo) from flooding.

The Tone River, in addition to the Edo River, is part of the southern border of Ibaraki Prefecture with Chiba Prefecture, and the [Watarase River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watarase_River), Tone River, Gongendō River, and [Naka River (Saitama)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naka_River_\(Saitama_Tokyo\)) in the southwestern border of Ibaraki with Saitama Prefecture. The Watarase River has become a small boundary of the southern border between Ibaraki and Tochigi prefectures.

From ancient times to the beginning of the Edo period, the lower reaches of the Tone River did not exist and the mouth of the Tone was in Tokyo Bay. On the plain was the [Katori Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katori_Sea), which existed in ancient times,[^cite_note-7] the [Lake Kasumigaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kasumigaura) and other lagoons in present-day Chiba prefecture are remnants of that sea. Katori Sea was connected to the Kashima-nada (Pacific Ocean).

Lake Kasumigaura is currently divided into three lakes: Nishiura, Kitaura, Sotonasakaura. In addition, in the prefecture there are freshwater lagoons such as Hinuma, [Senba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Senba), and Ushiku.

[Fukuoka Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Dam), is a dam that spans the Kokai River in [Tsukubamirai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukubamirai,_Ibaraki), it is one of the three largest dams in the Kantō region. Ryūjin Dam in [Hitachiōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi%C5%8Dta,_Ibaraki), is a beautiful dam on the Ryūjin River with a large pedestrian suspension bridge above the dam lake.

## Economy

Ibaraki's economy is based on energy production (particularly nuclear energy), chemical and precision machining industries, research institutes, and tourism. Agriculture, fishing, and livestock are also important sectors in the prefecture.[^cite_note-8]

Ibaraki's vast flat terrain make it highly suitable for industrial development. This complements its proximity to the Tokyo metropolitan area, giving it a high reputation as an industrial base. The prefecture is also home to [Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba), Japan's most extensive research and academic city, and the birthplace of [Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi), Ltd.[^cite_note-9]

### Agriculture

With extensive flat lands, abundant water, and suitable climate, Ibaraki is among the prefectures with the highest agricultural production in Japan. It plays an important role in supplying food to the Tokyo metropolitan area. Its main products include melons, pears, peppers, various varieties of rice and sugar cane, as well as flowers and ornamental plants.

It also supplies other food crops to the rest of the country. As of March 2011, the prefecture produced 25% of Japan's bell peppers and Chinese cabbage.[^cite_note-10]

### Fishing

It is one of the prefectures with the highest fish production in the country; in the Pacific Ocean, Lake Kasumigaura, other lagoons and rivers, various species of fish are obtained.

### Cattle

The Hitachigyū cattle (常 陸 牛 - ひたちぎゅう - Hitachi-gyū, Hitachi-ushi), which is a prefectural bovine breed, is noteworthy in livestock. The name comes from the kanji 常 陸 (Hitachi), the name of the ancient Hitachi Province and 牛 (ushi or gyū, beef).[^cite_note-11]

Background. In 1833 Tokugawa Nariaki (徳川 斉昭) established the breeding of black cattle in the present Migawa-chō (見川 町) of the city of Mito. Originally it remained mainly in the northern part of the prefecture, but later it spread throughout the prefecture.

### Industrial centers

- Hitachi area. Grouping of industries, such as electrical, electronic and machinery. More than 1,300 companies; many of them hired by the [Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi) company, which was founded in Sukegawa ([Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi,_Ibaraki) City) in 1910.

- Tōkai area. Atomic Energy Research Organization Grouping. [J-PARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-PARC), Proton Accelerator Research Complex.

- Tsukuba area. 32 institutes for education and research. Manipulation of matter at the level of atoms (nanotechnology). Robotic security center for support in daily life. Space center.

- Kashima area. Grouping of materials industries, such as steel and petrochemicals, around 160 companies.

## Culture

Ibaraki is known for [nattō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natt%C5%8D), or fermented soybeans, in [Mito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito,_Ibaraki), watermelons in [Kyōwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%8Dwa,_Ibaraki) (recently merged into [Chikusei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikusei,_Ibaraki)), and [chestnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnuts) in the [Nishiibaraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishiibaraki_District,_Ibaraki) region.[^cite_note-13]

Ibaraki is famous for the martial art of [Aikido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aikido) founded by [Morihei Ueshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morihei_Ueshiba), also known as [Osensei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensei). Ueshiba spent the latter part of his life in the town of [Iwama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwama,_Ibaraki), now part of [Kasama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasama,_Ibaraki), and the [Aiki Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiki_Shrine) and [dojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dojo) he created still remain.[^cite_note-14]

Kasama is famous for [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) ([Kasama Inari Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasama_Inari_Shrine)), Ibaraki Ceramic Art Museum, house museum of the calligrapher and ceramist [Kitaōji Rosanjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosanjin), [Kasama Nichidō Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasama_Nichid%C5%8D_Museum_of_Art), residence of [Morihei Ueshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morihei_Ueshiba), founder of the martial art Aikidō.[^cite_note-15]

The capital [Mito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito,_Ibaraki) is home to [Kairakuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairakuen), one of Japan's three most celebrated gardens, and famous for its over 3,000 [Japanese plum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ume) trees of over 100 varieties.

[Kashima Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashima_Shrine) (Jingū) Ibaraki's cultural heritage.

[Mito Tōshō-gū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito_T%C5%8Dsh%C5%8D-g%C5%AB), is the memorial shrine of [Tokugawa Ieyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Ieyasu) in Mito.

[Seizansō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizans%C5%8D) was the retirement villa of [Tokugawa Mitsukuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Mitsukuni).

[Mito Municipal Botanical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito_Municipal_Botanical_Park), is a botanical garden in Mito.

Park Ibaraki Nature Museum in [Bandō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band%C5%8D,_Ibaraki).

There are castle ruins in many cities, including [Mito Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito_Castle), Yūki Castle, [Kasama Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasama_Castle), [Tsuchiura Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuchiura_Castle), [Oda Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oda_Castle).

[Hitachi Fūryūmono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi_Furyumono), a puppet float theater festival, Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Makabe Hina Doll Festival - [Hinamatsuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinamatsuri) - (Sakuragawa City).

[Yūki-tsumugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%ABki-tsumugi) (silk weaving technique) Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, [Kasama ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasama_ware), Makabe Stone Lamp, Kagami Crystal Glass Factory, old glass factory in Ryūgasaki City.

## Pronunciation

The prefecture is often alternatively pronounced "Ibara*gi*" by those who speak the regional dialect known as Ibaraki-ben. However, the standard pronunciation is "Ibara*ki*". According to the author of "Not Ibara*gi*, Ibara*ki*",[^cite_note-16] this is most likely due to a mishearing of the softening of the "k" sound in Ibaraki dialect.
