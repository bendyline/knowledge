The **State Corporation for Space Activities**,[^cite_note-2] commonly known as **Roscosmos** (Роскосмос), is a [state corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_corporation_\(Russia\)) of the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) responsible for [space flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_science), [cosmonautics programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_space_agencies), and [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) research.[^cite_note-3]

Roscosmos inherited the bulk of the vast [Soviet space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_space_program) following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991. Established in 1992 as the **Russian Space Agency**,[^cite_note-\:0-4] its modern form resulted from a 2015 merge with the [United Rocket and Space Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation), re-nationalizing the [space industry of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_industry_of_Russia).[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-Avaneesh_Pandey-6][^cite_note-financialexpress.com-7] Roscosmos is headquartered at the [National Space Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Space_Centre_\(Moscow\)) in [Moscow](19004.md); [Mission Control Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RKA_Mission_Control_Center) and [Star City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_City,_Russia) are located in [Moscow Oblast](372315.md). Its [cosmodromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmodrome), jointly operated with the [Russian Space Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces), comprise [Baikonur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome) in [Kazakhstan](16642.md), the world's first and largest spaceport, [Plesetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesetsk_Cosmodrome) in [Arkhangelsk](472779.md), and [Vostochny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostochny_Cosmodrome) in [Amur](87627.md). Its [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Heads_of_Roscosmos) since February 2025 is [Dmitry Bakanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Bakanov).[^cite_note-8]

Roscosmos utilizes four rockets: the [heavy-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) [Proton-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton-M) and [Angara A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara_A5), [medium-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-lift_launch_vehicle) [Soyuz-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-2) variants, and [small-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-lift_launch_vehicle) [Angara-1.2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara-1.2). Roscosmos constructed [seven modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_International_Space_Station) of the [Russian Orbital Segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orbital_Segment) on the [International Space Station](15043.md) (ISS), founding the station with [*Zarya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarya_\(ISS_module\)) in 1998. It inherited the [Soyuz program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_programme), the most flown [crewed spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewed_spacecraft) in history. Its derivations, most recently [Soyuz MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS) and [Progress MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS), [service](31023441.md) the ISS. Roscosmos extended operations aboard *[Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir)*, the first [modular space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_space_station), ten years beyond its design life; [its last crewed mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_TM-30) departed in 2000.[^cite_note-9] The [Russian Orbital Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orbital_Station) is planned to be constructed from the ISS's [*Nauka*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauka_\(ISS_module\)) module alongside the [ISS deorbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_Deorbit_Vehicle) from 2028. Roscosmos is developing the medium-lift [Soyuz-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-5_\(rocket\)) and [partially reusable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reusable_spacecraft) [methalox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methalox) [Soyuz-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-7).&#x20;

The [Luna-Glob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna-Glob) program aims to succeed [Soviet missions to the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_programme). The [Luna 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_25) lander failed in 2023, future plans include [an orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_26), [polar landers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_27), and [a sample return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_28). Mars missions [Mars 96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_96) and [Fobos-Grunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fobos-Grunt) failed, while its joint mission with the [European Space Agency](10363.md) (ESA), [Trace Gas Orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_Gas_Orbiter), entered orbit in 2016; its [Venera-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venera-17) orbiter/lander [Venus mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_missions) launch was postponed beyond 2034.[^cite_note-10] Roscosmos plans crewed Moon missions, developing the [Orel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orel_\(spacecraft\)) spacecraft, [Yenisei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisei_\(rocket\)) [super heavy-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) rocket, and planning the [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station), with the [Chinese space program](343674.md) and 11 other countries.&#x20;

[Reactions to the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactions_to_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) isolated the agency internationally: Roscosmos ended the [Soyuz at the Guiana Space Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_at_the_Guiana_Space_Centre) program with ESA, and its supply of [NK-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NK-33) and [RD-181](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RD-191#RD-181) engines to the US [Orbital Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_Sciences_Corporation) [Antares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antares_\(rocket\)) rockets; ESA suspended cooperation on the [ExoMars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExoMars) program.

## Name

Roscosmos began as the Russian Space Agency,[^cite_note-11] established 1992[^cite_note-\:0-4] and restructured in 1999 and 2004 as the Russian Aviation and Space Agency[^cite_note-12] and the Federal Space Agency (Roscosmos),[^cite_note-13] respectively.[^cite_note-\:0-4] In 2015, the Federal Space Agency (Roscosmos) was merged with the [United Rocket and Space Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation), a government corporation, to re-nationalize the [space industry of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_industry_of_Russia), leading to Roscosmos in its current form.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-Avaneesh_Pandey-6][^cite_note-financialexpress.com-7]

The [Soviet space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_space_program) did not have central executive agencies. Instead, its organizational architecture was multi-centered; it was the design bureaus and the council of designers that had the most say, not the political leadership. The creation of a central agency by the Russian Federation after the dissolution of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was therefore a new development. The Russian Space Agency was formed on 25 February 1992, by a decree of President [Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin). [Yuri Koptev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Koptev), who had previously worked with designing Mars landers at [NPO Lavochkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPO_Lavochkin), became the agency's first director.[^cite_note-harvey-14]

In the early years, the agency suffered from lack of [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority) as the powerful design bureaus fought to protect their own spheres of operation and to survive. For example, the decision to keep Mir in operation beyond 1999 was not made by the agency, but by the private shareholder board of the Energia design bureau. Another example is that the decision to develop the new [Angara rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara_rocket) was rather a function of [Khrunichev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrunichev)'s ability to attract resources than a conscious long-term decision by the agency.[^cite_note-harvey-14]

### Crisis years

The 1990s saw serious financial problems due to the decreased cash flow, which encouraged the space agency to improvise and seek other ways to keep space programs running. This resulted in the agency's leading role in commercial satellite launches and [space tourism](67896.md). Scientific missions, such as interplanetary probes or astronomy missions during these years played a very small role, and although the agency had connections with the Russian aerospace forces, its budget was not part of Russia's defense budget; nevertheless, the agency managed to operate the [Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir) space station well past its planned lifespan, contributed to the [International Space Station](15043.md), and continued to fly [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_spacecraft) and [Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_spacecraft) missions.

In 1994, Roscosmos renewed the lease on its [Baikonur cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_cosmodrome) with the government of [Kazakhstan](16642.md).[^cite_note-eura1-15]

### 2000: Start of ISS cooperation

On 31 October 2000, a Soyuz spacecraft lifted off from the Baikonur Cosmodrome at 10\:53 a.m. Kazakhstan time. On board were Expedition One [Commander William M. (Bill) Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shepherd) of NASA and cosmonauts [Sergei Krikalev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Krikalev) and [Yuri Gidzenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gidzenko) of Roscosmos. The trio arrived at the International Space Station on 2 November, marking the start of an uninterrupted human presence on the orbiting laboratory.[^cite_note-16]

### 2004–2006: Improved situation

In March 2004, the agency's director [Yuri Koptev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Koptev) was replaced by [Anatoly Perminov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Perminov), who had previously served as the first commander of the Space Forces.[^cite_note-harvey-14][^cite_note-spacecorp.ru-17]

The [Russian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_economy) boomed throughout 2005 from high prices for exports, such as oil and gas, the outlook for future funding in 2006 appeared more favorable. This resulted in the Russian Duma approving a budget of 305 billion [rubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble) (about US\$11 billion) for the Space Agency from January 2006 until 2015, with overall space expenditures in Russia total about 425 billion rubles for the same time period.[^cite_note-18] The budget for 2006 was as high as 25 billion rubles (about US\$900 million), which is a 33% increase from the 2005 budget. Under the current 10-year budget approved, the budget of the Space Agency shall increase 5–10% per year, providing the space agency with a constant influx of money. In addition to the budget, Roscosmos plans to have over 130 billion rubles flowing into its budget by other means, such as industry investments and commercial space launches. It is around the time US-based *[The Planetary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Planetary_Society)* entered a partnership with Roscosmos.

### 2006–2012



Cosmonaut [Anton Shkaplerov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Shkaplerov) on EVA (February 2012)

The federal space budget for the year 2009 was left unchanged despite the global economic crisis, standing at about 82 billion rubles (\$2.4 billion). In 2011, the government spent 115 billion rubles (\$3.8 bln) in the national space programs.

The proposed project core budget for 2013 to be around 128.3 billion rubles. The budget for the whole space program is 169.8 billion rubles. (\$5.6 bln). By 2015, the amount of the budget can be increased to 199.2 billion rubles.[^cite_note-spacecorp.ru-17]

Priorities of the Russian space program include the new [Angara rocket family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara_rocket_family) and development of new communications, navigation and remote Earth sensing spacecraft. The [GLONASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLONASS) global navigation satellite system has for many years been one of the top priorities and has been given its own budget line in the federal space budget. In 2007, GLONASS received 9.9 billion rubles (\$360 million), and under the terms of a [directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Government_of_Russia) signed by [Prime Minister](339344.md) Vladimir Putin in 2008, an additional \$2.6 billion will be allocated for its development.

Space station funding issues

Due to [International Space Station](15043.md) involvements, up to 50% of Russia's space budget is spent on the crewed space program as of 2009. Some observers have pointed out that this has a detrimental effect on other aspects of space exploration, and that the other space powers spend much lesser proportions of their overall budgets on maintaining human presence in orbit.[^cite_note-observer-20]

Despite the considerably improved budget, attention of legislative and executive authorities, positive media coverage and broad support among the population, the Russian space program continues to face several problems.[^cite_note-mdb_industry-21] Wages in the space industry are low; the average age of employees is high (46 years in 2007),[^cite_note-mdb_industry-21] and much of the equipment is obsolete. On the positive side, many companies in the sector have been able to profit from contracts and partnerships with foreign companies; several new systems such as new rocket upper stages have been developed in recent years; investments have been made to production lines, and companies have started to pay more attention to educating a new generation of engineers and technicians.[^cite_note-harvey-14]

2011 New director

On 29 April 2011, Perminov was replaced with [Vladimir Popovkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Popovkin) as the director of Roscosmos. The 65-year-old Perminov was over the legal age for state officials, and had received some criticism after a failed [GLONASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLONASS) launch in December 2010. Popovkin is a former commander of the [Russian Space Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces) and First Deputy Defense Minister of Russia.[^cite_note-mt_popovkin_appointment-22] Also in 2011, the [Fobos-Grunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fobos-Grunt) Mars mission was lost in low Earth orbit and crashed back to earth in 2012.[^cite_note-23]

### 2013–2016: Reorganization of the Russian space sector

As a result of a series of reliability problems, and proximate to the failure of a July 2013 [Proton M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_M) launch, a major reorganization of the Russian space industry was undertaken. The [United Rocket and Space Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation) was formed as a [joint-stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock_company) corporation by the [government](9787373.md) in August 2013 to consolidate the Russian [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) sector. Deputy Prime Minister [Dmitry Rogozin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Rogozin) said "the failure-prone space sector is so troubled that it needs state supervision to overcome its problems."[^cite_note-pa20130830-24] Three days following the Proton M launch failure, the Russian government had announced that "extremely harsh measures" would be taken "and spell the end of the \[Russian] space industry as we know it."[^cite_note-rbth20130703-25] Information indicated then that the government intended to reorganize in such a way as to "preserve and enhance the Roscosmos space agency."[^cite_note-pa20130830-24]

More detailed plans released in October 2013 called for a [re-nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) of the "troubled space industry", with sweeping reforms including a new "unified command structure and reducing redundant capabilities, acts that could lead to tens of thousands of layoffs."[^cite_note-pa20131009-26] According to Rogozin, the Russian space sector employs about 250,000 people, while the United States needs only 70,000 to achieve similar results. He said: "Russian space productivity is eight times lower than America's, with companies duplicating one another's work and operating at about 40 percent efficiency."[^cite_note-pa20131009-26]

Under the 2013 plan, Roscosmos was to "act as a federal executive body and contracting authority for programs to be implemented by the industry."[^cite_note-pa20130830-24]

Despite Russian state efforts in the reorganization, two more Proton launch vehicle failures occurred in 2014 and 2015.[^cite_note-pm20140516-27][^cite_note-sn20150516-28]

The government reorganized all of Russia's [rocket engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine) companies into a single entity in June 2015. [NPO Energomash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPO_Energomash), as well as all other engine companies, became a part of [United Rocket and Space Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation).[^cite_note-mt20160624-29]

The decree to actually abolish Roscosmos as a [state agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_agency) was signed by [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in December 2015, which was replaced by a [state-run corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_corporation) effective 1 January 2016.[^cite_note-ti20151228-30]

In 2016, the [state agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_agency) was dissolved and the Roscosmos brand moved to the [state corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_corporation), which had been created in 2013 as the [United Rocket and Space Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rocket_and_Space_Corporation), with the specific mission to renationalize the Russian space sector.[^cite_note-ti20151228-30]

In May 2018, Putin selected Rogozin to be the head of the Russian state space corporation Roscosmos.[^cite_note-sn20180524-31]

### 2017–2021

In 2018, [Russian](9787373.md) President Vladimir Putin said "it 'is necessary to drastically improve the quality and reliability of space and launch vehicles' ... to preserve Russia's increasingly threatened leadership in space."[^cite_note-32] In November 2018 [Alexei Kudrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Kudrin), head of Russian financial audit agency, named Roscosmos as the public enterprise with "the highest losses" due to "irrational spending" and outright [theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theft) and [corruption](27352290.md),[^cite_note-33] under the leadership of [Igor Komarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Komarov) who was terminated in May 2018 in favour of Rogozin.

In 2020 Roscosmos under Rogozin reneged on its participation in [Lunar Gateway](53648310.md), a [NASA](18426568.md)-led project that will see a lunar orbiter spaceport for the moon. It had previously signed an agreement in September 2017 with the Americans.[^cite_note-ars1-34][^cite_note-snews1-35]

In March 2021, Roscosmos signed a memorandum of cooperative construction of a lunar base called the [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station) with the [China National Space Administration](344618.md)."[^cite_note-36]

In April 2021, Roscosmos announced that it will be departing the ISS program after 2024. In its place, it was announced that a new space station ([Russian Orbital Service Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orbital_Service_Station)) will be constructed starting in 2025.[^cite_note-37]

In June 2021 Rogozin complained that sanctions imposed in the wake of the [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md) were hurting Roscosmos.[^cite_note-bbg1-38]

In September 2021, Roscosmos announced its revenue and net income, losing 25 billion roubles and 1 billion roubles respectively in 2020, due to the reduction of profit from foreign contracts, an increase in show-up pay, stay-at-home days and personnel health expenses due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). According to Roscosmos, these losses would also impact the corporation for the next two years.[^cite_note-39] In October, Roscosmos placed the tests of rocket engines in the engineering bureau of chemical automatics in [Voronezh](467218.md) on hold for one month to deliver 33 tons of oxygen to local medical centers, as part of aid for the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-40]

In December 2021, the [Government of Russia](9787373.md) confirmed determination of the agreement with Roscosmos for development of next-gen space systems, the document been provided for the officials in July 2020.[^cite_note-41]

### 2022–present



2022 alternate logo of Roscosmos

Since the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) on 24 February 2022, Roscosmos launched nine rockets in 2022 and seven in the first half of 2023.

In early March 2022, Roscosmos under Rogozin suspended its participation in the ESA's spaceport in [Kourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourou), [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) in a tit-for-tat move over the sanctions imposed in the wake of the Russian invasion.[^cite_note-wired1-42] As well Rogozin said he would suspend delivery of the [RD-181](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RD-181) engine which is used for the [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman) [Antares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antares_\(rocket\))-[Cygnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_\(spacecraft\)) space cargo delivery system.[^cite_note-wired1-42]

In late March 2022, the [European Space Agency](10363.md) (ESA) suspended cooperation with Roscosmos in the [ExoMars rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExoMars_rover) (now named *Rosalind Franklin*) mission because of the Russian invasion, and British satellite venture [OneWeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneWeb) signed contracts with [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO) and [SpaceX](832774.md) to launch its satellites after friction had developed "with Moscow" and Roscosmos, its previous orbit service provider.[^cite_note-euron1-43] The friction had developed over Rogozin's command that OneWeb needed to ditch its venture capital investment from the UK government.[^cite_note-yah1-44][^cite_note-cnbc1-45]

On 2 May 2022, Rogozin announced that Roscosmos would terminate its involvement in the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS) with 12 months' notice as stipulated in the international contract that governs the satellite.[^cite_note-indy1-46] This followed the 3 March 2022 announcement that Roscosmos would cease cooperation on scientific experiments at the Spacelab,[^cite_note-yah1-44] and the 25 March 2022 announcement by Rogozin that "cooperation with Europe is now impossible after sanctions over the Ukraine war."[^cite_note-itin1-47]

Rogozin was removed from his job as CEO in July 2022,[^cite_note-ft1-48] and replaced with [Yury Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Borisov), who seemed to stabilize the relationship with the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS) partners, especially NASA. One complaint against Rogozin was his risky words about terminating the ISS agreement over the war in Ukraine,[^cite_note-eura1-15] which he broadcast as early as April 2022.[^cite_note-sky1-49][^cite_note-euron1-43] At one point in time NASA had bought 71 return trips on [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_\(spacecraft\)) for almost \$4 billion over six years.[^cite_note-eura1-15]

The global space-launch services market was valued at \$12.4 billion in 2021 and was forecast to reach \$38 billion by decade's end. An American academic wrote that in the wake of the Russian invasion, Roscosmos' share of that market was likely to decline in favour of new entrants such as Japan and India, as well as commercial entrants like [SpaceX](832774.md) and [Blue Origin](887418.md).[^cite_note-waro1-50]

In June 2023, Roscosmos held a campaign to recruit volunteers for the [Uran Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uran_Battalion?action=edit\&redlink=1), a militia for the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-ft1-48]

In October 2023, Borisov announced the need for 150 billion rubles to build the Russian space station in the next three years. At completion in 2032, it will have absorbed 609 billion rubles.[^cite_note-ifx2-51]

In February 2024, at the 2023 [AGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_general_meeting), Borisov announced the loss of 180 billion rubles in export revenues, chiefly engine sales and launch services, because of the Western hostility to the Russian invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-ifx1-52] Roscosmos had lost 90% of its launch service contracts since the advent of the war.[^cite_note-nik1-53][^cite_note-eat1-54]

In late 2025, Roscosmos launched three Iranian satellites into orbit aboard a [Soyuz-2.1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-2.1b) rocket from Vostochny Cosmodrome. Analysts described the mission as part of Russia's ongoing cooperation with non-Western partners.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

Roscosmos and Russia's space industry are facing significant challenges. The country is on track to conduct its fewest orbital launches since 1961. As of August 15, 2024, only nine launches had occurred, a sharp decline partly attributed to the loss of Western customers following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Roscosmos has reported financial losses of 180 billion rubles (\$2.1 billion) due to canceled contracts. The agency's first deputy director indicated it may not achieve profitability until 2025.[^cite_note-58]

From 2025 on Roscosmos headquarters are located in the new [National Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Space_Centre_\(Moscow\)) in the Moscow district of [Fili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fili_\(Moscow\)).[^cite_note-59]

## Current programs

### Rockets

Roscosmos uses a family of several launch rockets, the most famous of them being the [R-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-7_family), commonly known as the [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_\(rocket_family\)) rocket that is capable of launching about 7.5 tons into [low Earth orbit](47568.md) (LEO). The [Proton rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_\(rocket\)) (or UR-500K) has a lift capacity of over 20 tons to LEO. Smaller rockets include [Rokot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokot) and other Stations.

Currently rocket development encompasses both a new rocket system, [Angara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara_\(rocket\)), as well as enhancements of the Soyuz rocket, [Soyuz-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-2_\(rocket\)) and [Soyuz-2-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-2-3?action=edit\&redlink=1). Two modifications of the Soyuz, the Soyuz-2.1a and Soyuz-2.1b have already been successfully tested, enhancing the launch capacity to 8.5 tons to LEO. Future projects include the [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_spacecraft) successor launch rocket.

### Gecko mating experiment

On 19 July 2014, Roscosmos launched the [Foton-M4 satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foton-M4_satellite?action=edit\&redlink=1) containing, among other animals and plants, a group of five [geckos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geckos).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The five geckos, four females and one male, were used as a part of the [Gecko-F4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko-F4?action=edit\&redlink=1) research program aimed at measuring the effects of weightlessness on the lizards' ability to procreate and develop in the harsh environment. However, soon after the spacecraft exited the atmosphere, mission control lost contact with the vessel which led to an attempt to reestablish communication that was only achieved later in the mission. When the satellite returned to Earth after its planned two-month mission had been cut short to 44 days, the [space agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_agency) researchers reported that all the geckos had perished during the flight.

The exact cause that led to the deaths of the geckos was declared unknown by the scientific team in charge of the project. Reports from the [Institute of Medical and Biological Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medical_and_Biological_Problems?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Russia have indicated that the lizards had been dead for at least a week prior to their return to Earth. A number of those connected to the mission have theorized that a failure in the vessel's heating system may have caused the cold blooded reptiles to freeze to death.

Included in the mission were a number of fruit flies, plants, and mushrooms which all survived the mission.[^cite_note-69]

### ISS involvement

See also: [Russian Orbital Segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orbital_Segment)



The [Zarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarya_\(ISS_module\)) module was the first module of the ISS, launched in 1998.

Roscosmos is one of the partners in the [International Space Station program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station_programme). It contributed the core space modules [Zarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarya_\(ISS_module\)) and [Zvezda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvezda_\(ISS_module\)), which were both launched by [Proton rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_\(rocket\)) and later were joined by NASA's [Unity Module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_\(ISS_module\)). The [Rassvet module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rassvet_\(ISS_module\)) was launched aboard [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) [*Atlantis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Atlantis)[^cite_note-70] and is primarily used for cargo storage and as a docking port for visiting spacecraft. The [Nauka module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauka_\(ISS_module\)) is the final planned component of the ISS, launch was postponed several times from the initially planned date in 2007,[^cite_note-ria-20200523-71] but attached to ISS in July 2021.[^cite_note-nsf20210729-72]

Roscosmos is responsible for expedition crew launches by [Soyuz-TMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_spacecraft) spacecraft and resupplies the space station with [Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_spacecraft) space transporters. After the initial ISS contract with NASA expired, Roscosmos and NASA, with the approval of the US government, entered into a space contract running until 2011, according to which Roscosmos will sell NASA spots on Soyuz spacecraft for approximately \$21 million per person each way, thus \$42 million to and back from the ISS per person, as well as provide Progress transport flights, at \$50 million per Progress as outlined in the [Exploration Systems Architecture Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_Systems_Architecture_Study).[^cite_note-73] Roscosmos announced that according to this arrangement, crewed [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_spacecraft) flights would be doubled to 4 per year and [Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_spacecraft) flights doubled to 8 per year beginning in 2008.

Roscosmos has provided [space tourism](67896.md) for fare-paying passengers to ISS through the [Space Adventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Adventures) company. As of 2009, six space tourists have contracted with Roscosmos and have flown into space, each for an estimated fee of at least \$20 million ([USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD)).

Continued international collaboration in ISS missions has been thrown into doubt by the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) and related sanctions on Russia,[^cite_note-74] although resupply missions continued in 2022 and 2023.

### Suffa Space Observatory

In 2018, Russia agreed to help build the Suffa observatory in Uzbekistan. The observatory was started in 1991, but stalled after the fall of the USSR.[^cite_note-75]

### New piloted spacecraft

One of Roscosmos's projects that was widely covered in the media in 2005 was [Kliper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kliper), a small [lifting body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifting_body) reusable spacecraft. While Roscosmos had reached out to [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA) and [JAXA](716184.md) as well as others to share development costs of the project, it also stated that it will go forward with the project even without the support of other space agencies. This statement was backed by the approval of its budget for 2006–2015, which includes the necessary funding of [Kliper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kliper). However, the Kliper program was cancelled in July 2006,[^cite_note-flightglobal-76] and has been replaced by the new Prospective Piloted Transport System. (Orel) project. As of August 2023, the first uncrewed and crewed test flights of [Orel spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orel_\(spacecraft\)) are expected to occur in 2028.[^cite_note-kp-20230815-77]

## Launch control

Main article: [Russian Space Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces)

The [Russian Space Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces) is the military counterpart of the Roscosmos with similar mission objectives as of the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force). The Russian branch was formed after the merging of the space components of the [Russian Air Force](1526308.md) and the [Aerospace Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Aerospace_Defence_Forces) (VKO) in 2015. The Space Forces controls Russia's [Plesetsk Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesetsk_Cosmodrome) launch facility. Roscosmos and the Space Forces share control of the [Baikonur Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome), where Roscosmos reimburses the VKO for the wages of many of the flight controllers during civilian launches. Roscosmos and the Space Forces also share control of the [Yuri Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin_Cosmonaut_Training_Center). It has been announced that Russia is to build another spaceport in [Tsiolkovsky, Amur Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsiolkovsky,_Amur_Oblast).[^cite_note-78] The [Vostochny Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostochny_Cosmodrome) was scheduled to be finished by 2018 having launched its first rocket in 2016.
