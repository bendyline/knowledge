**Khartoum**, also spelled **Khartum**,[^cite_note-7] is the [capital](181337.md) and most populous city of [Sudan](27421.md) as well as [Khartoum State](767612.md). With an estimated population of 7.1 million people, Greater Khartoum is the largest urban area in Sudan.[^cite_note-khartoum-urban-3]

Khartoum is located at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile)[^cite_note-8] – flowing north from [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria) – and the [Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile), flowing west from [Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana) in [Ethiopia](187749.md). Divided by these two parts of the Nile, the Khartoum [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) is a tripartite metropolis consisting of Khartoum proper and linked by bridges to [Khartoum North](3145079.md) (الخرطوم بحري *al-Kharṭūm Baḥrī*) and [Omdurman](100414.md) (أم درمان *Umm Durmān*) to the west. The place where the two Niles meet is known as *al-Mogran* or *al-Muqran* (المقرن; English: "The Confluence").

Khartoum was founded in 1821 by [Muhammad Ali Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt),[^cite_note-9] north of the ancient city of [Soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soba_\(city\)). In 1882, the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [took control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_War) of the Egyptian government, leaving the administration of Sudan in the hands of the Egyptians. At the outbreak of the [Mahdist War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdist_War), the British attempted to evacuate Anglo-Egyptian garrisons from Sudan but the [Siege of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Khartoum) in 1884 resulted in the capture of the city by [Mahdist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdist_State) forces and a massacre of the defending Anglo-Egyptian garrison. In 1898 it was reoccupied by British forces and was the seat of Anglo-Egyptian Sudan's government until 1956.[^cite_note-10]

In 1956, the city was designated as the capital of an [independent Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Sudan_\(1956%E2%80%931969\)). Three hostages were killed during the [attack on the Saudi Embassy in Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_the_Saudi_Embassy_in_Khartoum) in 1973.

In 2008, the [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) engaged in [combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_attack_on_Omdurman_and_Khartoum) in the city with the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) as part of the [War in Darfur](5043637.md). The [Khartoum massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_massacre) occurred in 2019 during the [Sudanese Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Revolution). Between 2023 and 2025, the city saw [extensive combat](73563331.md) during the [civil war](73556699.md) involving the armed forces and the [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF), affecting [Khartoum International Airport](4306874.md) and other critical sites.[^cite_note-11] In 2025, the [Sudanese armed forces](27429.md) recaptured Khartoum from the RSF, leaving widespread destruction.[^cite_note-12]

Khartoum is an economic and trade center in [North Africa](21714.md), with rail lines from [Port Sudan](213572.md) and [El Obeid](1481380.md). It is served by [Khartoum International Airport](4306874.md) with the [New Khartoum International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Khartoum_International_Airport) under construction. Several national and cultural institutions are in Khartoum and its metropolitan area, including the [National Museum of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Sudan), the [Khalifa House Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_House_Museum), the [University of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Khartoum), and the [Sudan University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_University_of_Science_and_Technology).

## Etymology

One [folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology) is that it is derived from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) *khurṭūm* (خرطوم 'trunk' or 'hose'), probably referring to the narrow strip of land extending between the Blue and White Niles.[^cite_note-13]

Captain [J.A. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Augustus_Grant), who reached Khartoum in 1863 with [Captain Speke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hanning_Speke)'s expedition, thought the name was most probably from the Arabic *qurṭum* (قرطم 'safflower', i.e., *[Carthamus tinctorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthamus_tinctorius)*), which was cultivated extensively in Egypt for its oil to be used as fuel.[^cite_note-14] Some scholars speculate that the word derives from the [Nubian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_language) word *Agartum*, meaning "the abode of [Atum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atum)", Atum being the Nubian and Egyptian god of creation. Other [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_people) scholars suggest *Khartoum* is derived from the [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_language) word *hartoom*, "meeting".[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Sociologist Vincent J. Donovan notes that in the [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_languages) [Maa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maa_languages) language of the [Maasai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_people), *khartoum* means "we have acquired" and that the geographical location of Khartoum is where Maasai oral tradition claims that the ancestors of the Maasai first acquired [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle).[^cite_note-17]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Khartoum)

### 19th century



In the [slave-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_auction) at Khartoum

In 1821, Khartoum was established 24 km (15 mi) north of the ancient city of [Soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soba_\(city\)), by [Isma'il Kamil Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma'il_Kamil_Pasha), the third son of Egypt's ruler, [Muhammad Ali Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt), who had just incorporated Sudan into his realm. Originally, Khartoum served as an outpost for the [Egyptian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Army). Egypt shifted the seat of the colonial government from [Wad Madani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wad_Madani) to Khartoum in 1823, which became a permanent settlement and underwent rapid development in the next decades. With its elevation to capital status, Khartoum quickly grew into a regional center of trade, serving as a [rest area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_area) on the caravan route from [Ethiopia](187749.md) to [Egypt](8087628.md), but also becoming a major focal point for the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Sudan).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

A significant change took place in 1854, when most of the city was destroyed by heavy rains and floods. It was rebuilt with houses made out of mud and stones, replacing those made out of thatch and straw. Khartoum also became the seat of several European consulates and the [Apostolic Vicariate of Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Khartoum). European pressure and influence forced Egypt to close the city's public slave market in 1854, although slaves continued to be sold and trafficked in large numbers, specifically from the [Blue Nile](767625.md) region and the [Nuba Mountains](1139195.md), as well as down the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) (the [Dinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinka_people) and [Shilluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilluk_people) territories).[^cite_note-20] According to the British explorer [Samuel Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Baker), who visited Khartoum in 1862, slavery was the industry "that kept Khartoum going as a bustling town".[^cite_note-21]

On 13 March 1884, troops loyal to the [Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi) [Muhammad Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ahmad) began the [siege of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Khartoum) against the Egyptian garrison led by the British General [Charles George Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_George_Gordon). Despite being fortified by trenches and a wall connecting the Blue and White Niles, the city was conquered by the Mahdists on 26 January 1885 and the entire garrison was annihilated. Many of the inhabitants were massacred or enslaved and the survivors were deported to the newly established [Omdurman](100414.md), while Khartoum was largely destroyed and abandoned.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

With the reconquest of Sudan by Anglo-Egyptian forces in 1898, Khartoum was established as the capital of [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan). [Herbert Kitchener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Kitchener,_1st_Earl_Kitchener) became [Governor-General of the Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Sudan) in September 1898, and began a programme of reconstruction, Khartoum was rebuilt according to a street plan in the shape of the [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack). Khartoum Bahri was established as a garrison comprising a dockyard and a railhead to [Egypt](8087628.md), while Omdurman, remained the most populous part and largely kept its old shape. Kitchener ordered the mosques of Khartoum rebuilt and guaranteed freedom of religion to all citizens. He also prevented Christian missionaries from trying to proselytize the local Muslims.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### 20th century



Khartoum in 1888

The city rapidly grew and developed under British administration. Under [Reginald Wingate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Wingate) (1899–1916), the [Governor-General's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Palace,_Khartoum) in Khartoum was rebuilt using red bricks, with sandstone used for the corners. The buildings in Khartoum proper were modeled after Victorian architecture, with a distinct Middle Eastern influence as well as Mediterranean-style windows and balconies. The Gordon Memorial College (now the [University of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Khartoum)) was opened in 1903, and a new railway to [Port Sudan](213572.md) was completed in 1919. These developments prompted large migration from rural areas, resulting in an increase in the city's population. By 1930, Khartoum was estimated to have a population of over 50,000.[^cite_note-26] Khartoum continued to grow after it was declared the capital of the newly independent [Republic of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sudan_\(1956%E2%80%931969\)) in 1956. Within the following years, the [Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_Stadium), the [Khartoum American School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_American_School) and the [Bank of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Sudan) would be established. This large scale development would spurred rural to urban migration, by 1964 Khartoum's population had ballooned to 185,000. In the 1973 census Khartoum would have a population of 334,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-27]

In 1973, the city was the site of a [hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_the_Saudi_Embassy_in_Khartoum) in which members of [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_Organization) held 10 hostages at the Saudi Arabian embassy, five of them diplomats. The US ambassador, the US deputy ambassador, and the Belgian *chargé d'affaires* were murdered. The remaining hostages were released. A 1973 [United States Department of State](31975.md) document, declassified in 2006, concluded: "The Khartoum operation was planned and carried out with the full knowledge and personal approval of [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat)."[^cite_note-28]

In 1977, the first oil pipeline between Khartoum and [Port Sudan](213572.md) was completed.[^cite_note-29] The [Organization of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) summit of 18–22 July 1978 was held in Khartoum, during which Sudan was awarded the [OAU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) presidency.[^cite_note-30]



Government House (1936); now the Presidential Palace

Throughout the 1970s and 1980s, Khartoum was the destination of hundreds of thousands of refugees fleeing conflicts in neighboring nations such as [Chad](5488.md), [Eritrea](17238590.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md) and [Uganda](31816.md). Many Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees assimilated into society, while others settled in large slums on the city's outskirts. Since the mid-1980s, large numbers of refugees from [South Sudan](32350676.md) and [Darfur](605837.md) – fleeing the violence of the [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War) and [Darfur conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfur_conflict) – have settled around Khartoum.

In 1991, [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) purchased [a house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden's_house_in_Khartoum) in the affluent [al-Riyadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyad,_Khartoum) neighborhood of the city and another in [Soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soba_\(city\)). He lived there until 1996, when he was banished from the country. Following the [1998 U.S. embassy bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_U.S._embassy_bombings), the United States accused bin Laden's [al-Qaeda](1921.md) group and, on 20 August, launched [cruise missile](6590.md) attacks on the [al-Shifa pharmaceutical factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shifa_pharmaceutical_factory) in [Khartoum North](3145079.md). The factory's destruction created diplomatic tension between the U.S. and Sudan. The factory ruins are now a tourist attraction.[^cite_note-31]

In November 1991, the government of President [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) sought to remove half the population from the city. The residents, deemed [squatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting_in_Sudan), were mostly southern Sudanese whom the government feared could be potential rebel sympathizers. Around 425,000 people were placed in five "Peace Camps" in the desert an hour's drive from Khartoum. The camps were watched over by heavily armed security guards, many relief agencies were banned from assisting, and "the nearest food was at a market four miles away, a vast journey in the desert heat". Many residents were reduced to having only burlap sacks as housing. The intentional displacement was part of a large urban renewal plan backed by the housing minister, Sharaf Bannaga.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### 21st century

The sudden death of [SPLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_People's_Liberation_Army) head and vice-president of Sudan [John Garang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Garang) in late July 2005, was followed by three days of violent [riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Khartoum_Riots) in the capital. Order was finally restored after southern Sudanese politicians and tribal leaders sent strong messages to the rioters. The death toll was at least 24, as youths from southern Sudan attacked northern Sudanese and clashed with security forces.[^cite_note-35]

The [African Union](10634933.md) summit of 16–24 January 2006 was held in Khartoum;[^cite_note-36] as was the [Arab League](52625.md) summit of 28–29 March 2006, during which they elected Sudan the Arab League presidency.[^cite_note-37]

On 10 May 2008, the [Darfur](605837.md) rebel group [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) attacked the city with the goal of toppling [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md)'s government. The Sudanese government held off the assault.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

On 23 October 2012, an [explosion at the Yarmouk munitions factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarmouk_munitions_factory_explosion) killed two people and injured another person. The Sudanese government claimed that the explosion was the result of an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-aj25-41]

On 3 June 2019, Khartoum was the site of the [Khartoum massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_massacre), where over 100 dissidents were murdered (the government said 61 were killed), hundreds more injured and 70 women [raped](7980471.md) by [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) soldiers in order to forcefully disperse the [peaceful protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Sudanese_protests) calling for a civilian government.[^cite_note-42]

On 1 July 2020, activists demanded that al-Zibar Basha street in Khartoum be renamed. [Al-Zubayr Rahma Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Zubayr_Rahma_Mansur) was a slave trader and the al-Zibar Basha street leads to the military base where the 2019 Khartoum massacre took place.[^cite_note-43]

On 26 October 2021, the city was locked down following [a military coup](69110231.md) that left at least 7 dead, triggering protests and calls for a general strike. Prime minister [Abdalla Hamdok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok) was arrested during the coup, and held along with other cabinet members in an unknown location.[^cite_note-44]

On 15 April 2023, [fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the RSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_civil_war_\(2023-present\)) broke out across Sudan, [including in Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khartoum_\(2023\)). Fighting was reported at the presidential palace, the RSF's headquarters, [Khartoum International Airport](4306874.md) and [Merowe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merowe_Airport), which the RSF claimed to have captured. The Sudanese Armed Forces regained full control of Khartoum on 26 March 2025.[^cite_note-45]

## Geography



Khartoum (*center*) is near the middle of the Nile river system

### Location

Khartoum is located at the confluence of the Blue Nile and the White Nile.[^cite_note-46]

Khartoum is relatively flat, at elevation 385 m (1,263 ft),[^cite_note-dto-47] as the Nile flows northeast past Omdurman to [Shendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shendi), at elevation 364 m (1,194 ft)[^cite_note-48] about 163 km (101 mi) away.

### Climate

Khartoum features a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) [BWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWh)) with a dry season occurring during winter, typical of the Saharo-Sahelian zone, which marks the progressive passage between the [Sahara Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_Desert)'s vast arid areas and the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel)'s vast semi-arid areas. The climate is extremely dry for most of the year, with about eight months when average rainfall is lower than 5 mm (0.20 in). The very long dry season is itself divided into a warm, very dry season between November and February, as well as a very hot, dry season between March and May. During this part of the year, hot, dry continental [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds) from deserts, such as the [harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan), sweep over the region; the weather is stable and very dry.

The very irregular, very brief, rainy season lasts about 1 month as the maximum rainfall is recorded in August, with about 48 mm (1.9 in). The rainy season is characterized by a seasonal reverse of wind regimes, when the [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone) goes northerly. Average annual rainfall is very low, with only 121.3 mm (4.78 in) of precipitation. Khartoum records on average six days with 10 mm (0.39 in) or more and 19 days with 1 mm (0.039 in) or more of rainfall. The highest temperatures occur during two periods in the year: the first at the late dry season, when average high temperatures consistently exceed 40 °C (104 °F) from April to June, and the second at the early dry season, when average high temperatures exceed 39 °C (102 °F) in September and October. Temperatures cool off somewhat during the night, with Khartoum's lowest average low temperature of the year, in January, just above 15 °C (59 °F). Khartoum is one of the hottest major cities on Earth, with annual mean temperatures hovering around 30 °C (86 °F). The city also has very warm winters. In no month does the average monthly high temperature fall below 30 °C (86 °F). This is something not seen in other major cities with hot desert climates, such as [Riyadh](51554.md), [Baghdad](4492.md) and [Phoenix](49121.md).[^cite_note-49]

Almost 250,000 Syrians lived in Khartoum as of 2019, representing 5% of the total population of the city. Most are young men who have fled war in Syria. Sudan was the only country in the world to accept travelers carrying a Syrian passport who lacked a visa.[^cite_note-55]

## Economy



Development in Khartoum in 2009, with the [PDOC Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDOC_Headquarters) on right and the under-construction [GNPOC Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNPOC_Tower) on left

After the signing of the historic [Comprehensive Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Peace_Agreement) between the Government of Sudan and the [Sudan People's Liberation Movement](2691327.md) (SPLA), the Government of Sudan began a massive development project.[^cite_note-ST_ECON-56][^cite_note-BBC_ECON-57] In 2007, the biggest projects in Khartoum were the [Al-Mogran Development Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mogran_Development_Project), two five-star hotels, a new airport, [El Mek Nimr Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mek_Nimr_Bridge) (finished in October 2007) and the [Tuti Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuti_Bridge) that links Khartoum to [Tuti Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuti_Island).

In the 21st century, Khartoum developed based on Sudan's oil wealth (although the independence of [South Sudan](32350676.md) in 2011 affected the economy of Sudan negatively[^cite_note-eisourcebook-58]). The center of the city has tree-lined streets. Khartoum has the highest concentration of [economic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_activity) in the country. This has changed as major economic developments take place in other parts of the country, like [oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration) in the south, the [Giad Industrial Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giad) in [Al Jazirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazirah,_Sudan) state and White Nile Sugar Project in Central Sudan, and the [Merowe Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merowe_Dam) in the North.

Among the city's industries are printing, glass manufacturing, food processing, and textiles. Petroleum products are now produced in the far north of Khartoum state, providing fuel and jobs for the city. One of Sudan's largest refineries is located in northern Khartoum.[^cite_note-eisourcebook-58]

### Retailing



Africa road tunnel near the [Khartoum International Airport](4306874.md)

The [Souq al Arabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souq_al_Arabi) is Khartoum's largest open air market. The [souq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souq) is spread over several blocks in the center of Khartoum proper just south of the Great Mosque (Mesjid al-Kabir) and the minibus station. It is divided into separate sections, including one focused entirely on gold.[^cite_note-59]

Al Qasr Street and Al Jamhoriyah Street are considered the most famous [high streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Street) in [Khartoum State](767612.md).

Afra Mall is located in the southern suburb of Arkeweet. The Afra Mall has a supermarket, retail outlets, coffee shops, a bowling alley, movie theaters, and a children's playground.

In 2011, Sudan opened the Hotel Section and part of the food court of the new, [Corinthia Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthia_Hotel_Khartoum) Tower. The Mall/Shopping section is still under construction.

## Education

Main article: [Education in Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Khartoum)



The [University of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Khartoum)

Khartoum is the main location for most of Sudan's top educational bodies. There are four main levels of education:

1. [Kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten) and day-care. It begins in the age of 3–4, consisting of 1-2 grades, (depending on the parents).

2. Elementary school. The first grade pupils enter at the age of 6–7. It consists of 8 grades, after which, at 13–14 years old, students are ready to take the certificate exams and enter high school.

3. Upper second school and high school. In these three the school methods add some main academic subjects such as chemistry, biology, physics, and geography. There are three grades in this level. The students' ages are about 14–15 to 17–18.

4. Higher education. There are several universities and colleges in Khartoum, including the [University of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Khartoum)

   [^cite_note-60]

   &#x20;and [Sudan University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_University_of_Science_and_Technology).

## Transportation

Khartoum Public Transportation

Khartoum public bus

Khartoum is home to the largest airport in Sudan, [Khartoum International Airport](4306874.md). It is the main hub for [Sudan Airways](383994.md), Sudan's main carrier. A new airport was planned for the southern outskirts of the city, but with Khartoum's rapid growth and consequent [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl), the airport is still located in the heart of the city.

Khartoum's transportation is limited to the vehicular road system, with buses and personal vehicles comprising the main types of vehicles. As with many cities in the continent, parts of Khartoum are connected through privately owned buses.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]



A boat on the [Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile) before the [Al Mansheiya Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mansheiya_Bridge).

Khartoum has a number of bridges across both tributaries of the Nile. The [Mac Nimir Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_Nimir_Bridge), the [Blue Nile Road & Railway Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile_Road_&_Railway_Bridge), the [Cooper Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Bridge) (also known as the Armed Forces Bridge), and the [Elmansheya Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmansheya_Bridge) span the Blue Nile, connecting Khartoum to Khartoum North. The [Omdurman Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omdurman_Bridge), the Victory Bridge, and the [Al-Dabbasin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Dabbasin_Bridge?action=edit\&redlink=1) span the White Nile, connecting Khartoum to Omdurman. The [Tuti Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuti_Bridge) connects Tuti Island with Khartoum. Prior to the construction of the Tuti Bridge in 2008, residents of Tuti Island relied on [water taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_taxi) to cross the Blue Nile into Khartoum.

Khartoum has rail lines from [Wadi Halfa](1126657.md), Port Sudan on the [Red Sea](26191.md), and [El Obeid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ubayyid). All are operated by [Sudan Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Railways).

## Architecture

The architecture of Khartoum reflects the city's history since the early 1820s and is marked by both native Sudanese, Turkish, British and modern buildings. In general, the [architecture of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Sudan) reflects a wide diversity in its shapes, materials, and use.

Since independence, the people of Sudan have introduced new infrastructure and technology, which has led to new and innovative building concepts, ideas and construction techniques.[^cite_note-64]

## Culture



[National Museum of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Sudan)

### Museums

The largest museum in Sudan is the [National Museum of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Sudan).[^cite_note-65] Founded in 1971, it contains works from different epochs of Sudanese history. Among the exhibits are two [Egyptian temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_temple) of [Buhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhen) and [Semna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semna),[^cite_note-66] originally built by Pharaoh [Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatshepsut) and Pharaoh [Tuthmosis III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuthmosis_III), respectively, but relocated to Khartoum upon the flooding of [Lake Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nasser).

The [Republican Palace Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Palace_Museum,_Khartoum),[^cite_note-67] opened in 2000, is located in the former Anglican All Saints' cathedral[^cite_note-68] on Sharia al-Jama'a, next to the historical [Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Palace,_Khartoum).

The Ethnographic Museum[^cite_note-69] is located on Sharia al-Jama'a, close to the Mac Nimir Bridge.

## In popular culture

### Literature

Khartoum's unique history and cultural significance have inspired literary works that explore its past, present, and future. For example, in "Reading Khartoum", the city is depicted as a space shaped by movement, political instability, and socio-cultural changes, resulting in underlying layers of meanings and ambiguity. Arabic-written poetry also offers a personalized glimpse of the city, reflecting its distinct cultural appearance and setting it apart from other Arab and African cities.[^cite_note-77]
