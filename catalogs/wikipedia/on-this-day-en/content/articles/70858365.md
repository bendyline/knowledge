# Extraordinary court

"Special court" redirects here. For courts with specialized subject-matter jurisdiction, see [specialized court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialized_court).



*[Khmer Rouge Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge_Tribunal)* in Cambodia is contemporary example of extraordinary court

An **extraordinary court**, or **special court**, is a type of [court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court) that is established outside of ordinary [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), composed of irregularly selected judges or applying irregular procedure for judgment. Since extraordinary courts can be abused to infringe fundamental rights of individuals, most modern countries ban such courts by constitution or statutes. Usually, modern [military courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_courts) judged by [courts-martial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts-martial) are regarded as examples of extraordinary courts.[^cite_note-1]

## By country

See also: [List of special tribunals and courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_special_tribunals_and_courts)

### Cambodia

Further information: [Khmer Rouge Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge_Tribunal)

An extraordinary court is the [Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_Chambers_in_the_Courts_of_Cambodia), which is basically a chamber in the national court of [Cambodia](334751.md) that is specially designed to judge crimes of the [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge) such as the [Cambodian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide), but its judges are not of the ordinary Cambodian judiciary but are selected among international candidates nominated by [Secretary-General of the United Nations](162415.md), according to an agreement between [United Nations](31769.md) and the Cambodian government.[^cite_note-2]

### Germany

Further information: [Sondergericht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sondergericht) and [German military law § Current law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_law#Current_law)

In modern [Germany](11867.md), the establishment of extraordinary courts (*[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Ausnahmegerichte**) is strictly prohibited by article 101(1) of the [Constitution of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Germany#Other_courts),[^cite_note-3] in reflection of [judicial murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_murder) by [People's Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Court_\(Germany\)) (*[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Volksgerichtshof**), which was a kind of *_Sondergericht_* that was established during [Nazi Germany](21212.md). The term *_Sondergericht_* itself means just the concept of 'special court', but the use of that term is discouraged, as it can refer to the legacy of the Nazis. As a result, contemporary courts in Germany with special jurisdiction (such as '[Federal Social Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Social_Court)' in cases of social security matters) are called a kind of '[specialized court](265708.md#Specialized_courts)' (*[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Fachgerichte**), composed of ordinary judges.

In that way, Article 101(1) of the Constitution is explained as forbidding establishment of both extraordinary courts and special courts, which are substantially the same concept.[^cite_note-4] As the Constitution bans judgments by irregularly-composed judges, courts in the City of [Kempten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempten) with special jurisdiction on [military justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_justice) in Germany are also constituted by ordinary judges, according to Section 11a of [German Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Criminal_Code).[^cite_note-5]

### South Korea

Further information: [Judiciary of South Korea § Military courts](63660454.md#Military_courts)

In contemporary [South Korea](27019.md), the establishment of special courts (*[Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 특별법원, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Korean): *Teugbyeolbeob-won**), meaning extraordinary courts (*[Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 예외법원, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Korean): *Ye-oebeob-won**) is exceptionally allowed only for a [military court](58625.md), according to Article 110(1) of the [Constitution of South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_South_Korea).[^cite_note-Korcon-6] It is notable that [Justices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_of_the_Constitutional_Court_of_Korea) at the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Korea) must have qualification as judges, which includes Justices in the [Supreme Court of Korea](4899540.md), in ordinary courts, but Article 111(2) of the Constitution does not require military judges in the [Military Court of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Court_of_Korea?action=edit\&redlink=1) to have competence as ordinary court judges by 110(3) of the Constitution.[^cite_note-Korcon-6] The [Constitutional Court of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Korea) explains that constitutional difference of qualification among [ordinary court judges](63660454.md#Judges) (including Supreme Court Justices), military judges and Constitutional Court Justices as constitutional grounds for non-qualified senior military officers to participate in military court judgments as *adjudicators* (*[Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 심판관, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Korean): *Simpangwan**), together with qualified military judges (*[Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 군판사, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Korean): *Gunpansa**) in exceptional cases,[^cite_note-7] according to article 22(3) of the Military Court Act.[^cite_note-8]

### United States

Further information: [FISA court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FISA_court), [military tribunals in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tribunals_in_the_United_States), and [combatant status review tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatant_status_review_tribunal)

The [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Court) has the authority to issue [warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_\(law\)) authorizing surveillance of suspected [foreign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-citizen) [spies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy) or [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist). FISA warrant requests are rarely denied. If an application is denied by one judge of the court, the federal government is not allowed to make the same application to a different judge of the court but may appeal to the United States Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court of Review. Such appeals are rare: the first appeal from the FISC to the Court of Review was made in 2002 ([In re Sealed Case No. 02-001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_re_Sealed_Case_No._02-001)), 24 years after the founding of the court.

The [Alien Terrorist Removal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_Terrorist_Removal_Court)[^cite_note-9] similarly allows the government to present classified evidence in a sealed application to a judge for the purpose of designating a person as an "alien terrorist" and effecting their [removal](45009959.md) from the country. Created in 1996, the court lied dormant until it received its first case in 2026.[^cite_note-10]

There are also [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) [courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court) designed to [judicially try](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_\(law\)) members of enemy forces during [wartime](33158.md), operating outside the scope of conventional [criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law) and [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) proceedings. The judges are military [officers](36301328.md) and fulfill the role of [jurors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_jury). Military tribunals are distinct from [courts-martial](58625.md). These were most recently created under the [George W. Bush](3414021.md) administration and authorized by the [Military Commissions Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Commissions_Act_\(disambiguation\)) under the Bush and [Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) administrations to assert jurisdiction over terror suspects designated as [unlawful enemy combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlawful_enemy_combatants).
