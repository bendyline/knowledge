An **intermediate-range ballistic missile** (**IRBM**) is a [ballistic missile](38025.md) with a [range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_\(aeronautics\)) between 3,000 and 5,500 kilometres (1,900–3,400 mi), categorized between a [medium-range ballistic missile](478272.md) (MRBM) and an [intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBM).[^cite_note-1] Classifying ballistic missiles by range is done mostly for convenience. In principle there is little difference between a high-performance IRBM and a low-performance ICBM, because decreasing payload mass can increase the range over the ICBM threshold. The range definition used here is used within the U.S. [Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Defense_Agency).

## History

The progenitor for the IRBM was the [A4b rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregate_\(rocket_family\)#A4b/A9), winged for increased range and based on the famous [V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket), Vergeltung, or "Reprisal", officially called [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregate_series), rocket designed by [Wernher von Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wernher_von_Braun). The V-2 was widely used by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) at the end of World War II to bomb English and Belgian cities. The A4b was the prototype for the upper stage of the [A9/A10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregate_\(rocket_family\)#A9) rocket. The goal of the program was to build a missile capable of hitting New York, when launched from France or Spain (see [Amerika Bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerika_Bomber#Winged_rockets)).[^cite_note-v2werk-oberraderach-2]

A4b rockets were tested a few times in December 1944 and January and February 1945.[^cite_note-v2werk-oberraderach-2] All of these rockets used [liquid propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_propellant). The A4b used an [inertial guidance system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_guidance_system), while the A9 would have been controlled by a pilot. They started from a non-mobile [launch pad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_pad).

Following World War II, von Braun and other lead [Nazi scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_scientists) were secretly transferred to the United States, to work directly for the U.S. Army through [Operation Paperclip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Paperclip), developing the V-2 into the weapon for the United States.

As of 2026, IRBMs are operated by the People's Republic of China, India,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Israel, North Korea,[^cite_note-5] and Russia.[^cite_note-bbcrussiairbm-6][^cite_note-7] The United States, USSR, Pakistan, United Kingdom, and France are former operators.

## Nomenclature

There is no clearly agreed-upon distinction between an intermediate-range and a medium range ([MRBM](478272.md)) missile, and the categories overlap. Different sources classify missiles in different ways. They are both distinct from ICBMs, in that they have a range that is less than intercontinental, and hence must be based relatively close to the target. An IRBM, in general, is intended as a strategic weapon, while a MRBM, in general, is intended as a [theatre ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_ballistic_missile).
