The **Philippines women's national football team** represents the [Philippines](23440.md) in international [women's association football](191466.md) competitions. It is managed by the [Philippine Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Football_Federation) (PFF), the sport's governing body in the country.

The Philippine women's national football team was established in the 1980s and has been a regular participant in the [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md) since their first appearance in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_AFC_Women's_Championship), back when the tournament was known as the AFC Women's Championship. The Philippines hosted the tournament in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_AFC_Women's_Championship), with matches played in [Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo_City) and [Bacolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod). The team took a hiatus from the continental tournament after competing in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_AFC_Women's_Championship). Following the [AFC](715249.md)'s introduction of a qualification process from the [2006 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), the Philippines successfully returned to the AFC Women's Asian Cup in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), having [qualified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification) the previous year, and achieved a historic milestone by advancing beyond the group stage for the first time. Their success continued into the [2022 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), where they reached the [semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Semi-finals) and secured their qualification for the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](45271353.md), marking the national team's debut at the tournament. They subsequently secured their second consecutive appearance by qualifying for the [2027 FIFA Women's World Cup](60983910.md) as the sole representative from Southeast Asia.

In Southeast Asian football, the Philippine women's team won their first [AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFF_Women's_Championship) title in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFF_Women's_Championship). In the [Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Games), the team achieved a historic breakthrough in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Southeast_Asian_Games) by winning their first-ever gold medal. Prior to this victory, their notable accomplishments in the regional meet included bronze medal finishes in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Southeast_Asian_Games) and in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Southeast_Asian_Games).

From December 2023 to March 2024, the team achieved their highest-ever position, 38th, in the [FIFA Women's World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Rankings).

## History

### Early years

The Philippines women's national football team was formally organized after the Philippine Ladies' Football Association (PLFA) was established in 1980 by footballer [Cristina Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Ramos-Jalasco), who later became a member of the team.[^cite_note-speaksup-3] The Philippines took part in the [1983 Asian Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_AFC_Women's_Championship) in [Thailand](30128.md) although the tournament at the time was not sanctioned by [FIFA](11049.md) or the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC). In order for the Philippines to be eligible to participate in FIFA tournaments, the PLFA and, in extension, the women's national team would have to be an affiliate of the PFF, the Philippines' national sports association for football. The PLFA later became part of the PFF.[^cite_note-4]

The Philippines was among the teams which competed in the [1985 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1985_SEA_Games#Women's_tournament) in [Thailand](30128.md), the first-ever edition of the games to host women's football.[^cite_note-5] The team clinched its first podium finish in a tournament by finishing in third place.[^cite_note-speaksup-3] However, the football event of the tournament was only contested by two other teams, [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_women's_national_football_team) and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_women's_national_football_team), with the Philippines not winning a single match.

### 2000s

[Marlon Maro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Maro), a former [defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_\(association_football\)) for the [Philippines men's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_national_football_team), coached the women's national football team as early as 2001 when he guided the national team through the [2001 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2001_SEA_Games#Women's_tournament).[^cite_note-6] He was head coach of the national team until 2007, coaching the Malditas for the last time at the [2007 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2007_Southeast_Asian_Games#Women's_tournament).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-gmanews-8]

### 2010s

Supporters of the Philippine national team with flags

Fans of the Philippines national team at the [Bangabandhu National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangabandhu_National_Stadium) in [Dhaka](56656.md)

The Philippines national team participated at the [2011 AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFF_Women's_Championship) in [Laos](17752.md) on October 16–25, after being inactive for the last 21 months.[^cite_note-9]

The national team participated in a training camp in the United States in 2012. The team management scouted for players with [Filipino heritage in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Americans) for the national team. Part of the training camp was participation in the 2012 LA Viking Cup, which saw the national team play against American club sides California Cosmos, Metro Stars, and Leon. The Malditas won the tournament by beating the California Cosmos in the final 4–3 on [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_time_\(football\)) following a 1–1 draw, earning the team their first-ever trophy, albeit in a minor tournament.[^cite_note-laviking-10]

Philippine national team players in blue posing as a team

The Philippines national football team after their away match against Bangladesh on May 25, 2013, in Dhaka.

The national team attempted to [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification) for the [2014 AFC Women's Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup). The Philippines was grouped with [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_women's_national_football_team), [Iran](9896170.md) and [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_women's_national_football_team) at the [qualifiers single group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification#Group_B) with the winner advancing to the Asian Cup finals. The Malditas fell short of qualifying after losing to eventual group winners Thailand by a single goal despite winning convincingly over its other group opponents, Iran and Bangladesh.[^cite_note-fallshort-11]

At the [2013 AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_AFF_Women's_Championship), the Philippines was grouped with [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos_women's_national_football_team), Indonesia, hosts [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_women's_national_football_team), and the [Japan under-23 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_women's_national_under-23_football_team), who were invited to the tournament. The Philippines failed to proceed to the knockout stage after placing third in the group, with only the top two teams proceeding to the next phase of the tournament. The Malditas lost to Japan U23 and Myanmar and won against the other two teams.[^cite_note-12]

At the [2013 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2013_Southeast_Asian_Games#Women's_tournament),[^cite_note-seagames-13] the women's national football team failed to get past the group stage, losing against the two other nations grouped with the country and failing to score a single goal.[^cite_note-14]

In February 2015, PFF general secretary [Ed Gastanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Gastanes) said that the head coach position for the women's national team was vacant after its previous holder, [Ernie Nierras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Nierras), was not an A license coach, meaning he was not able to continue his coaching stint. Nierras led the team in his last competitive match as coach in 2013.[^cite_note-undervalued-15] The PFF announced in April 2015 that former footballer [Buda Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda_Bautista) was appointed as head coach and was first tasked to lead the team at the [2015 AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFF_Women's_Championship).[^cite_note-aff2015-16] Bautista also became the first female coach of the national team.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-freshstart-18] The Malditas failed to get past the group stage of the [2015 AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFF_Women's_Championship), only winning a game against [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_women's_national_football_team) and losing the other two matches.

A fan waves the Philippine flag as support for the national team playing against Jordan in the Asian Cup

Jordan v. Philippines; 2018 AFC Asian Cup

2017 would mark the entry of businessman [Jefferson Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Cheng) as a sponsor of the Philippine national team.[^cite_note-19] who also was appointed the team manager of the team in 2017.[^cite_note-financial-20]

Still under Bautista, the Philippines [qualified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification) for the [2018 AFC Women's Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) in Jordan, the first time the national team qualified for the [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md) since a qualification phase was introduced starting from the [2006 competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup); prior to the inception of qualifications, the Philippines had [participated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_at_the_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) in every iteration of the tournament except for three, failing to reach the knockout stages in each participation. The team finished second in their group in the qualifiers, in which each of the group's winners qualify for the Asian Cup. [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_women's_national_football_team) won the group, but since it had already qualified as hosts of the 2018 edition of the tournament, the virtue of qualification went to the group's runners-up.[^cite_note-21]

In 2017, the PFF launched the "Project Jordan" task force to help the Malditas [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_qualification) for the [2019 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) through the 2018 Asian Cup. The PFF secured major sponsorship and hired United States–based English coach, Richard Boon; an identification camp was held in the United States participated by national team players and prospects.[^cite_note-22] A three-month training camp in the United States was later set up in late December 2017.[^cite_note-23] In March 2018, Boon was replaced by French coach [Rabah Benlarbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabah_Benlarbi)[^cite_note-24] as the national team held a camp at the [PFF National Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFF_National_Training_Centre) in [Carmona, Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmona,_Cavite).[^cite_note-25] The national team held their last camp under "Project Jordan" in Japan from March 20 to 27, 2018.[^cite_note-26]

At the 2018 Asian Cup, the Philippines failed to reach the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup#Knockout_stage) leading to the championship, by which doing so would have resulted in automatic qualification for the 2019 World Cup. However, by finishing third in [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup#Group), the Malditas qualified for a special [fifth place match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup#Fifth_place_match), of which the winner qualifies for the World Cup; it was the first time the Philippines advanced beyond the group stage of the Asian Cup in their participation history. The country, then ranked 72nd in the [FIFA Women's World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Rankings), failed to qualify for the World Cup after losing 5–0 to the 16th-ranked [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_football_team) in the fifth place match of the tournament.[^cite_note-27]

The Philippines competed at the [2019 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2019_Southeast_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament), which was hosted at home, but were denied a bronze medal by Myanmar.[^cite_note-28]

The Philippines did not play any games since the 2019 Southeast Asian Games due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_association_football). Despite this, they attained their highest-ever world ranking then at 65th place by the end of 2020.[^cite_note-womenteamgearsup-29]

Guided by Marlon Maro, who returned as head coach of the team, the Philippines qualified for the [2022 AFC Women's Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) in [India](14533.md) after featuring in the [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification) held in September 2021 despite almost a year of inactivity after beating [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_women's_national_football_team) and [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_women's_national_football_team) in [Tashkent](57533.md), [Uzbekistan](31853.md).[^cite_note-womenteamgearsup-29][^cite_note-30] [Alen Stajcic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alen_Stajcic) was appointed as head coach in October 2021.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] At the [2022 Asian Cup group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_Group_B), they defeated [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_women's_national_football_team) 1–0, ending a 13-match losing streak against their Southeast Asian rivals.[^cite_note-33] The Malditas advanced to the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage) for only the second time in their Asian Cup participation, beating [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_women's_national_football_team) in the [quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Chinese_Taipei_vs_Philippines) on [penalties](511302.md) following a 1–1 draw and qualifying for the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](45271353.md). It was the first time that the Philippines qualified for the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md), and the first time the country qualified for a FIFA World Cup of any gender or age level.[^cite_note-34] The national team also improved their Asian Cup record by reaching the [semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#South_Korea_vs_Philippines) of the tournament, where they lost 2–0 to South Korea. This consequently moved them 10 places up the [FIFA Women's World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Rankings) to 54th place, setting a new peak rank. Stajcic also had his contract with the team extended to after the 2023 World Cup.[^cite_note-35]

At the [2021 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2021_Southeast_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) in Vietnam, which was held in May 2022 due to the pandemic, the Filipinas repeated their success from the previous edition by reaching the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2021_Southeast_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament#Knockout_stage). Despite losing to [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_women's_national_football_team) 3–0 in the semifinals, the Philippines defeated [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_women's_national_football_team) 2–1 to win the bronze medal in a rematch of the 2019 edition. The team achieved their best finish at the tournament in 37 years.[^cite_note-36]

On June 23, 2022, the Filipinas recorded their first win on European soil after defeating [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_women's_national_football_team) 3–0 in a friendly in [Brežice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre%C5%BEice), [Slovenia](27338.md).[^cite_note-37]

#### 2022 AFF Women's Championship



The Philippine national team celebrating their [2022 AFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFF_Women's_Championship) title.

The Philippines hosted the [2022 AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFF_Women's_Championship). They secured their first-ever final appearance in a FIFA-sanctioned tournament and guaranteed a podium finish by [upsetting](1169845.md) defending champions [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_women's_national_football_team) 4–0 in the [semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_AFF_Women's_Championship#Semi-finals), recording their best finish at the [AFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFF_Women's_Championship) yet.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] It also marked the Filipinas' first win against Vietnam after 16 matches against each other, who, alongside Thailand, have been their most-matched opponents in their 42-year history.[^cite_note-40] The team went on to defeat [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_women's_national_football_team) 3–0 in the final, which was attended by 8,257 spectators at the [Rizal Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Memorial_Stadium) in [Manila](184334.md), [Philippines](23440.md), and clinched their first-ever title in any major tournament.[^cite_note-41]

#### Road to World Cup

The Philippines was invited to participate in the [2023 Pinatar Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pinatar_Cup) in [San Pedro del Pinatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_del_Pinatar), [Spain](26667.md), to prepare for its maiden Women's World Cup bid. The Philippines were the first non-[European](32332.md) team to join the [Pinatar Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinatar_Cup). The team played against [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_women's_national_football_team), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_women's_national_football_team), and [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_women's_national_football_team), making them the lowest-ranked team in the tournament. They lost all of their matches and finished in fourth place with zero points having scored only one goal in the tournament, a 90th-minute [free kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_kick_\(association_football\)) by [Meryll Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryll_Serrano) against Scotland.[^cite_note-42]

The Filipinas played the first round of the [2024 AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament) in [Dushanbe](56658.md), [Tajikistan](30108.md), defeating all three opponents without a single goal conceded, and at the [2023 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Southeast_Asian_Games) in [Cambodia](334751.md).[^cite_note-43] At the latter tournament, they failed to reach the semifinals after finishing third in the group behind Myanmar, whom they lost to 1–0.[^cite_note-44]

#### 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup



Fans supporting the Philippines at the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup.



Players of the national team on a motorcade in [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig) shortly after the conclusion of their World Cup campaign.

Debutants Philippines were drawn into [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Group_A) alongside [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_women's_national_football_team), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_women's_national_football_team), and tournament co-hosts [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_women's_national_football_team). The Filipinas were selected from pot 4, thus were the lowest-ranked team in Group A.[^cite_note-45]

The Philippines played their first group stage match at the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](45271353.md) in [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin), New Zealand, on July 20, 2023, suffering a 2–0 defeat against [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_women's_national_football_team). On July 25, 2023, in [Wellington](33804.md), the Filipinas won 1–0 against [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_women's_national_soccer_team), their first match victory at a [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md). [Sarina Bolden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarina_Bolden) scored the winning goal, which was the Philippines' first-ever goal in the competition, in the 24th minute through a [header](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Header_\(association_football\)) from [Sara Eggesvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Eggesvik)'s [cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_\(association_football\)). Goalkeeper [Olivia McDaniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_McDaniel) was awarded the [player of the match](906698.md) for her performance in the historic win, registering numerous [saves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_\(association_football\)) against the co-hosts and keeping a [clean sheet](313346.md).[^cite_note-46] However, the Philippines lost 6–0 in their final group match against [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_women's_national_football_team), the highest-ranked team in their group, with [Alicia Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicia_Barker) committing an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal), [Sofia Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Harrison) being [sent off](3090014.md) for a careless [dive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_\(association_football\)), and the Filipinas conceding a [hat-trick](411541.md#Association_football) to Norway's [Sophie Román Haug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Rom%C3%A1n_Haug) at [Eden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Park) in [Auckland](18660332.md). They finished last in Group A with [three points](4960100.md) thanks to their win against the Football Ferns, but failed to reach the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_knockout_stage) in their debut World Cup.[^cite_note-47] Stajcic's contract as head coach expired thereafter.[^cite_note-48]

#### Post-World Cup tournaments

In August 2023, [Mark Torcaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Torcaso) was appointed as the head coach of the Philippines women's team under a two-year contract. Until December, Torcaso concurrently coached [Western United FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_FC_\(A-League_Women\)) in the [A-League Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-League_Women).[^cite_note-49] The Filipinas played in the [football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) of the postponed [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asian_Games) in China in September 2023. It was their first ever participation in the [tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Asian_Games).[^cite_note-50] They were able to advance to the quarterfinals[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] where eventual champions [Japan](3190583.md) ended their run.[^cite_note-53]

The Philippines took part in the second round of the [2024 AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament) in [Perth](24355.md), [Australia](4689264.md), later that year.[^cite_note-54] Their 8–0 defeat against hosts [Australia](30864460.md) on the second matchday took place in front of a sold-out crowd of 59,155 at the [Perth Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Stadium), having been relocated from the much smaller [Perth Rectangular Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Rectangular_Stadium) to meet the strong demand.[^cite_note-55] The Philippines failed to advance to the third round and thus qualify for the [women's football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) of the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) after finishing as the second-best runners-up among the three groups in the qualifying tournament, wherein only the best-ranked runners-up would advance.[^cite_note-56]

There was uncertainty after [John Gutierrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gutierrez)'s election as PFF president in November 2023. However in January 2024, it was announced that sponsor [Jefferson Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Cheng), whose best feat was to help the team qualify for the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md), would be retained as team manager.[^cite_note-57]

In February 2024, the Philippines played in the [2024 Pinatar Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pinatar_Cup) against [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_women's_national_football_team) and [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_women's_national_football_team).[^cite_note-58] They finished the tournament in fourth place after losing 1–0 to Slovenia in the third-place playoff.[^cite_note-59]

In June 2024, Cheng stepped down from the role, citing that the situation was "not workable" due to an "incompatibility" and "misalignment" of goals with the current organization.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] Friendlies, especially with teams from other confederations, became less frequent after this.[^cite_note-stability-62]

In June 2025, the Philippines started their campaign to secure a berth at the [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) via the [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_qualification) in [Cambodia](334751.md).[^cite_note-stability-62] The Philippines qualified on July 5, 2025, after their 1–0 win against Hong Kong. They also won their two prior games against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_women's_national_football_team) (3–0) and hosts [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia_women's_national_football_team) (6–0).[^cite_note-63]

#### 2025 Southeast Asian Games

In December 2025, the Philippines competed in the [2025 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Southeast_Asian_Games) held in [Thailand](30128.md). The team was drawn into Group B alongside defending champions [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_women's_national_football_team), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_women's_national_football_team), and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_women's_national_football_team). The campaign began with a 1–2 loss to Myanmar, putting the team's knockout stage hopes in jeopardy. However, they recovered in their second match to defeat Vietnam 1–0, courtesy of a stoppage-time goal from rookie [Mallie Ramirez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallie_Ramirez).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-ABS-CBN-Semis-65] A decisive 6–0 victory over Malaysia, highlighted by an [Alexa Pino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Pino) hat-trick, secured the Philippines' progression to the semi-finals as group runners-up.[^cite_note-ABS-CBN-Semis-65]

In the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2025_SEA_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament_%E2%80%93_Knockout_stage#Thailand_vs_Philippines) at the [Chonburi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonburi_Stadium), the Filipinas faced the hosts, [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_women's_national_football_team). After conceding the opening goal in the 53rd minute, the Philippines equalized late in the match when [Jael Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jael_Guy) converted a penalty in the 87th minute following a handball violation. With the score deadlocked at 1–1 after extra time, the Philippines won the ensuing [penalty shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout) 4–2, with Guy, [Alex Carpio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandrea_Carpio), [Sara Eggesvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Eggesvik), and [Hali Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hali_Long) converting their spot kicks to book the team's first-ever appearance in the gold medal match.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

The [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2025_SEA_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament_final) saw a rematch against Vietnam. After a goalless draw following 120 minutes of regulation and extra time, the match was decided by a penalty shootout. The Philippines defeated Vietnam 6–5 on penalties, with goalkeeper [Olivia McDaniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_McDaniel) making the winning save to secure the country's first SEA Games gold medal in women's football.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

#### 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup qualification

In March 2026, the Philippines participated in the [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) in Australia, which served as the Asian qualification tournament for the [2027 FIFA Women's World Cup](60983910.md) in [Brazil](3383.md). After advancing to the knockout stage as one of the best third-placed teams, the Filipinas were defeated 0–7 by [Japan](3190583.md) in the quarter-finals, sending them to the play-in round. On March 19, 2026, the Philippines defeated [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan_women's_national_football_team) 2–0 at the [Robina Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robina_Stadium) on the [Gold Coast](59251.md), with second-half goals from [Angela Beard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Beard) and [Jaclyn Sawicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaclyn_Sawicki), both assisted by crosses from [Jael-Marie Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jael-Marie_Guy).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-PFF_2027WWC-71] This victory secured their second consecutive appearance at the FIFA Women's World Cup. They became the sixth nation overall to qualify for the 2027 edition, and the only team from Southeast Asia to secure a direct berth.[^cite_note-PFF_2027WWC-71]

### Nicknames

The Philippines women's national football team was historically nicknamed the "Malditas".[^cite_note-seminar-72] Although the term lacks a direct English equivalent, translations range from a mild epithet ("damned" or "accursed") to an expression of reverence or fear ("badasses"). The nickname was adopted by the team during the [2005 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2005_SEA_Games#Women's_tournament) under head coach [Ernest Nierras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Nierras), who intended it to reflect the players' *palaban* (combative or gutsy) nature and symbolize a resilient squad that opponents would fear.[^cite_note-73] In Octorber 2021, head coach [Marlon Maro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Maro) proposed dropping the nickname, arguing that the word could be interpreted pejoratively as "bratty".[^cite_note-74] However, following Maro's departure in late 2021, the proposal did not advance, as players at the time expressed a preference for retaining the moniker.[^cite_note-75]

In March 2022, the [Philippine Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Football_Federation) (PFF) announced that they would be officially adopting the nickname "Filipinas" for the team, which is the standard demonym for female citizens of the Philippines.[^cite_note-76] Team manager [Jefferson Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Cheng) explained the change by noting that while "malditas" is unique, it translates to "damned" in Spanish and Portuguese and carries a pejorative connotation in Tagalog. He added that it had become increasingly problematic to repeatedly explain the nickname's intended meaning to international audiences. Cheng also clarified that "Filipinas" is intended to be used as a standalone moniker and should not be preceded by a geographic modifier (e.g., the Philippine Filipinas).[^cite_note-whats-inaname-77]

## Competitive record

### FIFA Women's World Cup

Further information: [Philippines at the FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_at_the_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup)

The Philippines first qualified for the FIFA Women's World Cup during the 2023 edition. It did not attempt to qualify for the inaugural [FIFA Women's World Cup in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) with its non-participation at the [1991 AFC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_AFC_Women's_Championship), which served as the Asian qualifiers of the World Cup. The national team first attempted to qualify for the succeeding editions of the tournament from [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) except for the [2011 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).

### Olympic Games

The Philippines entered a qualification tournament for the Olympics. At the first two editions of the Olympics where women's football was played, the standings at the preceding [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) were used. With the country failing to qualify for the final tournament of the [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and [1999 FIFA Women's World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) the country failed to qualify for the [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) and [2000 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament).
