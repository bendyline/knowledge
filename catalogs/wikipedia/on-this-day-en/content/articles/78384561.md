**Shenzhou 20** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 神舟二十号; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Shénzhōu èrshí-hào*; lit. 'Divine Boat Number 20') was a Chinese spaceflight mission to the [*Tiangong* space station](2481401.md) launched on 24 April 2025.[^cite_note-2] It carried three [taikonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taikonaut) on board a [Shenzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_\(spacecraft\)) spacecraft.

Shenzhou 20 was expected to return to Earth in early November 2025 after the arrival of the [Shenzhou 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_21) crew. However, due to suspected [space debris](266344.md) damage to the spacecraft, the Shenzhou 20 crew returned to Earth on 14 November using the Shenzhou 21 spacecraft instead.[^cite_note-Yeung_2025-3] The Shenzhou 20 spacecraft returned to Earth uncrewed on 19 January 2026.

The mission was the 15th crewed Chinese spaceflight and the 20th flight overall of the [Shenzhou program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program).

## Background

Shenzhou 20 was launched on 24 April 2025, prior to the end of the previous mission, [Shenzhou 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_19). It was the ninth flight to the *Tiangong* space station and was planned to last approximately six months, departing following the arrival of the [Shenzhou 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_21) crew in late 2025.

## Mission

The mission was launched from [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center) on board a [Long March 2F](340934.md) rocket. The Shenzhou spacecraft docked with the nadir docking port on the [*Tianhe* core module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianhe_core_module) of the station, where the crew entered to take over operations from the departing crew of Shenzhou-19.

The [Tianzhou 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianzhou_9) cargo ship docked with the *Tiangong* station in October during a planned resupply mission.[^cite_note-4]

On 31 October 2025, [Shenzhou 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_21) arrived at the space station in preparation for the return of Shenzhou 20. Members of both crews were pictured eating "barbecued" chicken wings aboard the station in a video shared by Chinese state media.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

### Space debris incident

On 5 November 2025, just hours before it was scheduled to undock, minor cracks were detected in a window of the docked Shenzhou 20 spacecraft, attributed to a suspected debris impact.[^cite_note-Jones_2025-6][^cite_note-Yeung_2025-3] Taikonauts Chen Dong and Wang Jie found a small triangular crack on the outer pane of the re-entry capsule window. Further examination with a small microscope revealed long, thin cracks in the windowpane.[^cite_note-7] As a precaution, the crew's scheduled return was postponed, and further assessment determined that Shenzhou 20 should not be used for reentry. Its three-person crew returned to Earth on 14 November aboard the already-docked Shenzhou 21 spacecraft.[^cite_note-Jones_2025-6][^cite_note-Yeung_2025-3]

On 25 November, [Shenzhou 22](81552219.md) launched uncrewed—six months earlier than planned—and docked with *Tiangong* later the same day. Its arrival restored a flightworthy return vehicle for the station crew.[^cite_note-Jones_2025-6] It also meant that both docking ports were in use on the space station and Shenzhou 20 needed to be removed before [Shenzhou 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_23) could dock in 2026.[^cite_note-8]

Shenzhou 22 carried food, clothing, and other consumables to replace supplies used by the crew during the extended Shenzhou 20 mission. It also delivered equipment intended to assess whether the cracked window on Shenzhou 20 could be repaired.[^cite_note-Jones_2025-6]

CMSA stated that Shenzhou 20 would remain in orbit to support follow-on experiments related to the damage event.[^cite_note-Jones_2025-6]

In December 2025, CMSA confirmed that Shenzhou 20 would return to Earth uncrewed after repair work using equipment delivered by Shenzhou 22.[^cite_note-9] On 9 December, Shenzhou 21 astronauts [Wu Fei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Fei_\(taikonaut\)) and [Zhang Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Lu_\(taikonaut\)) conducted an 8-hour spacewalk that inspected the damage to the Shenzhou 20 spacecraft.[^cite_note-10]

Shenzhou 20 undocked on 18 January 2026 and landed in the deserts of [Inner Mongolia](55064.md) on 19 January at 01\:34 UTC. The spacecraft was uncrewed but carried cargo from *Tiangong* back to Earth.[^cite_note-11]

## Crew

In keeping with China's past practice, the names of the crew were not announced until the day before launch. The mission was commanded by Chen Dong, who previously flew on [Shenzhou 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_11)—the only crewed mission to the [Tiangong-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong-2) space laboratory in 2016—and later commanded [Shenzhou 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_14) to the *Tiangong* space station in 2022. He will take his second flight as commander taking responsibility for the whole mission. He was joined by operator [Chen Zhongrui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Zhongrui), a fighter pilot with the [People's Liberation Army Air Force](381445.md), he is tasked with completing mission operations and [Wang Jie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jie_\(taikonaut\)), an engineer with the [China Academy of Space Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Academy_of_Space_Technology). He will maintain the space station platform keeping care of the sub systems.[^cite_note-XH-22042025-12]
