**Miroslav Lajčák&#x20;**&#x20;(born 20 March 1963) is a Slovak politician and diplomat who held the office of [Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Slovakia\)) of the [Slovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Republic) from 2009 to 2010 and again from 2012 to 2020, serving across three different governments led by [Robert Fico](4127226.md) and one government led by [Peter Pellegrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pellegrini).[^cite_note-1] In addition, Lajčák also served as [President of the United Nations General Assembly](780080.md) for the [72nd session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-second_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly) from 2017 until 2018.[^cite_note-2]

A key figure in the mediation of the post-conflict crises in the Western Balkans, Lajčák acted as Executive Assistant to the UN Secretary-General's Special Envoy for the Balkans from 1999 to 2001. He negotiated, organized and supervised the [referendum on the independence of Montenegro in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Montenegrin_independence_referendum) on behalf of the European Union.

From 2007 to 2009, Lajčák led the mission as [High Representative of the International Community](2386573.md) and [European Union Special Representative](2899127.md) in Bosnia and Herzegovina. During his tenure, Bosnia and Herzegovina signed the landmark Stabilization and Association Agreement with the European Union.[^cite_note-3]

From 2020 to 2025, Lajčák oversaw the Belgrade-Pristina Dialogue and other Western Balkan regional issues as the [EU Special Representative](2899127.md).[^cite_note-32021D0470-4] Since 2025, he acted as advisor to Slovak Prime Minister [Robert Fico](4127226.md) for foreign affairs and national security.

In late 2025 and early 2026, the release of the "[Epstein files](80415168.md)" by the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) triggered a major political scandal in Slovakia. Documents revealed extensive correspondence between Lajčák and convicted child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) from 2017 to 2019, including reports that Lajčák wanted to be introduced to "young girls", expressed a desire to participate in Epstein's private "games" and discussed strategic political interests with Epstein's network.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] While photographs confirmed their personal connection, Lajčák maintained that the interactions were strictly professional and routine for his diplomatic duties.[^cite_note-7]

On 31 January 2026, following a joint statement from opposition politicians calling for his departure, and mounting pressure from the media, Lajčák submitted his resignation, which Fico accepted.[^cite_note-8]

## Biography

### Early life and education

Lajčák attended primary school in [Stará Ľubovňa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star%C3%A1_%C4%BDubov%C5%88a). In 1977 his family moved to [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava), where he enrolled in grammar school on Bilíková Street. He finished the final year of his secondary education at grammar school in [Banská Štiavnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansk%C3%A1_%C5%A0tiavnica). Later he studied law at the [Comenius University in Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comenius_University_in_Bratislava) for a year before he obtained a master's degree in [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) from the [Moscow State Institute of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Institute_of_International_Relations) (MGIMO). As a student, he joined the [Communist Party of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Czechoslovakia). He also studied at the [George C. Marshall European Center for Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_C._Marshall_European_Center_for_Security_Studies) in [Garmisch-Partenkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmisch-Partenkirchen), [Germany](11867.md).[^cite_note-settimes-cocoon-9]

### Diplomatic career (1988–2005)

A member of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party), Lajčák joined the [Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) foreign ministry in 1988. Between 1991 and 1993 Lajčák was posted to the Czechoslovak and subsequently Slovak embassy in Moscow.

Upon his return to Slovakia in 1993 to the newly established Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Slovakia, he took an active part in forming the national Foreign Service. He became Director of the Cabinet of the Foreign Minister and later on Director of the Cabinet of the Prime Minister [Jozef Moravčík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozef_Morav%C4%8D%C3%ADk).[^cite_note-auto1-10]

In 1994, he was appointed Slovak Ambassador to Japan, becoming the youngest-ever Head of Diplomatic Mission of Slovakia. At the age of 31, he was also the youngest foreign ambassador in Japan. In 1998 after his posting to Japan he returned to the Foreign Ministry to become for the second time Director of Slovakia Foreign Minister's Cabinet.[^cite_note-auto1-10]

His international engagements started in 1999 when he served as the Executive Assistant to the United Nations Secretary–General ́s Special Envoy for the Balkans, [Eduard Kukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Kukan) (until 2001).[^cite_note-auto-11]

Between 2001 and 2005, Lajčák was based in [Belgrade](55904.md) as Slovakia's Ambassador to the [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) (later [Serbia and Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro)), with accreditation also to Albania and the [Republic of Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Macedonia).

Following his term in Belgrade, he was named Political Director at the Foreign Ministry of Slovakia (2005–2007).[^cite_note-auto-11]

### Supervisor of Montenegro's independence referendum (2005)

In 2005 the EU diplomacy chief [Javier Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Solana) called Lajčák to supervise the [2006 Montenegrin independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Montenegrin_independence_referendum), which was approved with a 55.5% of favourable votes.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### International High Representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina (2007–2009)

On 1 July 2007, Solana again chose Lajčák to succeed [Christian Schwarz-Schilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Schwarz-Schilling) as the double-hatted [High Representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina](2386573.md)/[EU Special Representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Special_Representative_for_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (OHR/EUSR).[^cite_note-14] With a mandate from the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council), he liaised regularly with the [United Nations](31769.md) and reported periodically to the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council). During his tenure, [BiH](3463.md) signed the [Stabilization and Association Agreement with the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilization_and_Association_Agreement), the most remarkable integration success of the country for many years to come. He also launched activities to reach out to the population including speaking tours and a website for dialogue with citizens.[^cite_note-auto2-15]

He was soon acclaimed as "person of the year" by both [Banja Luka](385109.md)-based [Nezavisne novine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nezavisne_novine)[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] and [Sarajevo](26786.md)-based [Dnevni Avaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnevni_Avaz)[^cite_note-18] dailies.

Lajčák acted in 2007–09 in line with a moderately strong role of the OHR (using [Bonn powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_powers) more than Schwarz-Schilling but less than [Paddy Ashdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Ashdown)); critics of the international supervision of Bosnia and Herzegovina, including [David Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chandler_\(scholar_of_international_relations\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), pointed to his "authoritarian stance" as responsible for creating a further crisis by trying to impose major institutional change and alter the Dayton peace agreement framework without domestic ownership or legitimacy.[^cite_note-19] Lajčák is deemed to have achieved results on the ground but at the price of endangering the credibility of EU conditionality by accepting merely cosmetic legal changes. He left [BiH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BiH) in January 2009 after being nominated to serve as [Minister of Foreign Affairs of Slovakia.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Slovakia\))

Lajčák did resort to the use of the Bonn Powers in the crisis related to the 2007 Law on the Council of Ministers, which caused a showdown with [Milorad Dodik](4218129.md)'s SNSD.[^cite_note-20] The law, aimed at revising decision-making procedures to make the BiH government less prone to blockages, triggered the resignation of the Bosnian prime minister [Nikola Špirić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_%C5%A0piri%C4%87) (SNSD) and withdrawal of [Bosnian Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Serbs) from state institutions. The OHR then published an "authentic interpretation" of the law, claiming that it did not intend to change the composition of the Council of Ministers.[^cite_note-21] Lajčák also removed [RS](347422.md) police officials deemed complicit in war crimes.[^cite_note-22] Upon instructions of Solana,[^cite_note-23] Lajčák contented himself of cosmetic changes to bring to an end the police reform saga,[^cite_note-24] leading to the signature of the [Stabilisation and Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilisation_and_Association_Agreement) between Bosnia and Herzegovina and the EU in June 2008, despite claims that the EU had lost his credibility by lowering the bar which had been set by [Paddy Ashdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Ashdown) in 2005.

### Minister of Foreign Affairs of Slovakia (2009–2010, 2012–2020)



Lajčák with [U.S. Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Secretary_of_State) [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in Washington, D.C., May 2009

From 26 January 2009 until July 2010, Lajčák served as [Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Slovakia\)) in [Robert Fico's First Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fico's_First_Cabinet).[^cite_note-25]

From December 2010 to April 2012 Lajčák served as managing director for Europe and Central Asia in the EU's [External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service).[^cite_note-26]

In April 2012 Lajčák was appointed again, as an independent, to the post of foreign minister and deputy prime minister in [Robert Fico's Second Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fico's_Second_Cabinet).[^cite_note-27] In addition, he became Chairman of the Government's Council for Human Rights, National Minorities and Gender Equality.[^cite_note-auto2-15]

After the 2014 Bosnian general election, he encouraged Dodik's SNSD party to enter the government coalition, despite having lost the Presidency seat, claiming that "new authorities must have legitimacy."[^cite_note-28]



Lajčák with [U.S. Deputy Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Deputy_Secretary_of_State) [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) in Washington, D.C., December 17, 2015

After the [annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), in May 2014, he visited Moscow and met with the Russian foreign minister [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) and deputy prime minister [Dmitry Rogozin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Rogozin). Rogozin and Lajčák were co-chairs of a joint Slovak-Russian cooperation body.[^cite_note-29]

In November 2015 Slovakia voted against [Kosovo](17391.md)'s membership in [UNESCO](21786641.md). Lajčák later explained that Slovakia wanted Belgrade and Pristina to interpret it as a message, that the international community expects parties to submit such proposals on the basis of mutual agreement and consent. "Our interest is to strengthen the dialogue. One of the reasons why we took a negative attitude is that the issue was not the subject of the dialogue and we are afraid it could worsen it," he added.[^cite_note-30]

In 2016 Lajčák called on the EU to abandon its "ideological" approach to Russia.[^cite_note-31]



Lajčák with U.S. Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) in Bratislava, February 2019

In October 2018, he threatened to freeze relations with Vietnam over the case of a Vietnamese businessman who was kidnapped by Vietnamese agents and smuggled back home through Slovakia.[^cite_note-32]

In November 2018, Lajčák lambasted as "anti-democratic" the proposed [Kosovo–Serbia land swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93Serbia_land_swap) and cautioned against the regional repercussions of such a proposal.[^cite_note-33]

In November 2018, Lajčák announced that he would contemplate his resignation if the parliament rejected the [Global Compact for Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Compact_for_Migration).[^cite_note-34] On 29 November 2018, after the parliament had voted to refuse the compact, the Foreign Affairs Minister decided to resign,[^cite_note-35] but later withdrew his resignation.[^cite_note-36]

Since 2019, Lajčák has been serving on the Transatlantic Task Force of the [German Marshall Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Marshall_Fund) and the Bundeskanzler-Helmut-Schmidt-Stiftung (BKHS), co-chaired by [Karen Donfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Donfried) and [Wolfgang Ischinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Ischinger).[^cite_note-37]

Between the EU-facilitated 5 August political agreement and the December breakthrough on a new SNSD-led government, on 27 Oct 2019, Lajčák invited both [Milorad Dodik](4218129.md) and [Dragan Čović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_%C4%8Covi%C4%87) to Bratislava for "international mediation" which paved the way for [BiH](3463.md) adopting its ANP with [NATO](21133.md).[^cite_note-38]

Lajčák announced in November 2019 that he would leave Slovak politics following the [2020 Slovak parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Slovak_parliamentary_election).

### EEAS Managing Director for Europe and Central Asia (2010–2012)

From 2010 to 2012, Lajčák helped shape the newly formed diplomatic service of the European Union, the [European External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service), as its managing director for Europe and Central Asia. In addition, he also served as the EU's Chief Negotiator for the [Ukraine–European Union Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93European_Union_Association_Agreement) and [Moldova–European Union Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova%E2%80%93European_Union_Association_Agreement), as well as the EU Representative for the [5+2 Talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5+2_format) on the [Transnistrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnistrian) Settlement Process.[^cite_note-39]

### Slovak Presidency of the Council of the EU (2016)



Miroslav Lajčák at the Bratislava Summit during Slovakia's EU presidency, September 2016

In June 2016, Slovakia took over the Presidency of the [EU Council](5866.md).[^cite_note-40] In his speech presenting the priorities for the Slovak Presidency Lajčák said "Today, we call the EU our home, the euro our currency and [Schengen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen) our area. The Presidency is the culmination of our integration journey. We are at the core of Europe. And we are grateful for that because we were given a lot. It's time to give back."[^cite_note-41]

In November 2016, following revelations by a whistleblower, [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) Slovakia accused Lajčák of dubious procurement contracts during the Slovak EU Council Presidency.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The accusation was never proven.

### Candidacy for UN Secretary-General (2016)

The candidacy of Lajčák for the position of [Secretary-General](162415.md) of the United Nations was officially launched following his formal nomination by the Government of Slovakia on May 25, 2016. Positioned as a representative of the [Eastern European Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_European_Group), Lajčák's campaign sought to fulfill the principle of regional rotation, as [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) remained the only regional bloc yet to hold the office. His platform emphasized a commitment to multilateralism, conflict prevention, and a comprehensive reform of the UN Secretariat to increase organizational efficiency.[^cite_note-44]

Throughout the 2016 selection process, Lajčák participated in public hearings and televised debates designed to improve transparency. After a series of informal straw polls by the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council), his bid reached a peak in the final round on October 5, 2016, finishing in second place behind [António Guterres](471806.md). Lajčák secured 7 "encourage" votes, 6 "discourage" votes, and 2 "no opinion" votes, marking the highest finish for any candidate from the Eastern European bloc during that cycle.[^cite_note-45]

While the Security Council ultimately moved to appoint Guterres, the visibility gained during the campaign led to Lajčák's successful election as President of the [72nd UN General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-second_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly) the following year.[^cite_note-46]

### President of the UN General Assembly (2017–2018)



Lajčák is seated on the elevated podium directly behind U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), serving as the President of the 72nd UN General Assembly, October 2017

Lajčák served as [President of the 72nd Session of the UN General Assembly](https://www.un.org/pga/72/) from 2017 to 2018 where he advocated for dialogue, strengthening multilateralism and the need to serve all people. He was the first president to publish his financial disclosure summary.[^cite_note-47]

### OSCE Chairperson-in-Office (2019)



Lajčák, as OSCE Chairperson-in-office and Minister of Foreign Affairs of Slovakia, with Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) in Kyiv, June 2019

In 2019, Lajčák was particularly active in East and [South-East Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Europe) as Chairperson-in-office of the [OSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSCE). Slovakia's OSCE Chairmanship focused on people, dialogue and stability.[^cite_note-48]

During his tenure, Lajčák visited 15 OSCE field presences[^cite_note-49] to highlight the OSCE's important work on the ground, and held high-level talks with interlocutors in the OSCE region, including Russian Foreign Minister [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) in February,[^cite_note-50] June,[^cite_note-51] and September 2019,[^cite_note-52] as well as [US Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Secretary_of_State) [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) in April 2019.[^cite_note-53]

Ahead of the 26th OSCE Ministerial Council, Lajčák shared his Bratislava Appeal, an informal initiative addressed to foreign ministers from across the OSCE area arguing for more support to the OSCE and multilateralism.[^cite_note-54]

### EU Special Representative (2020–2025)



EU Special Representative Lajčák and Greek Minister of Foreign Affairs [Nikos Dendias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikos_Dendias) participating in a videoconference on the European perspective of the Western Balkans, Athens, May 2021

On 2 April 2020, Miroslav Lajčák was appointed by the EU Council as the [EU Special Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Special_Representative) (EUSR) for the Belgrade-Pristina Dialogue and other Western Balkan regional issues. His mandate included the tasks of achieving a comprehensive normalization of relations between [Serbia](29265.md) and [Kosovo](17391.md), improving good neighborly relations, and contributing to the consistency of EU action in the region.[^cite_note-55] His term was extended several times to provide continuity during critical negotiations.[^cite_note-32021D0470-4]

In April 2024, German Member of the European Parliament [Reinhard Bütikofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_B%C3%BCtikofer) publicly criticized Lajčák, describing his performance in the role as a "disaster". These remarks followed reports that Lajčák would be transitioning from his mediation post to serve as the EU Ambassador to Switzerland and the Principality of Liechtenstein starting in September 2024. Bütikofer expressed hope that Lajčák's new appointment would not negatively impact diplomatic relations between the EU and Switzerland as his previous tenure had in the Balkans. Throughout his mandate, Lajčák faced various criticisms from Kosovo officials and political analysts who questioned his impartiality in the regional dialogue process.[^cite_note-56] His transition to the role of EU Ambassador to Switzerland and the Principality of Liechtenstein was ultimately cancelled due to "unforeseen developments". Consequently, the EU Council formally extended his mandate a final time until 31 January 2025.[^cite_note-57]

Following the completion of his service, Lajčák was succeeded on 1 February 2025 by Danish diplomat [Peter Sørensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_S%C3%B8rensen_\(diplomat\)).[^cite_note-58]

### Advisor for foreign affairs and national security (2025–2026)

After five years as the EU's chief negotiator in the Balkans, Lajčák returned to Slovak diplomacy, where he was appointed as an Advisor to Slovak Prime Minister [Robert Fico](4127226.md), focusing on security issues and the Western Balkans.[^cite_note-59]

## Epstein files



Miroslav Lajčák and Slovak Prime Minister [Robert Fico](4127226.md) at the Bratislava Summit during Slovakia's EU presidency, September 2016

In late 2025 and early 2026, the release of over three million pages of documents related to convicted child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md), known as the "[Epstein files](80415168.md)" by the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md), sparked a significant political controversy in Slovakia. The documents revealed, that Lajčák had extensive email communication with Epstein between 2017 and 2019. During the period of correspondence, Lajčák simultaneously served as [Minister of Foreign and European Affairs of Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_and_European_Affairs_\(Slovakia\)) in Fico's Third Cabinet and [President of the United Nations General Assembly](780080.md).[^cite_note-60]

### November 2025

In November 2025, it was disclosed that the correspondence featured a familiar tone, with Epstein referring to Lajčák as "Miro" and discussing his potential advancement to top roles in the UN or NATO. Notably, in a March 15, 2018, email to [Steve Bannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bannon), Epstein explicitly identified Lajčák as the person who would lead a "European project".[^cite_note-61]

While Lajčák admitted to social contact, he maintains the interactions were purely professional and routine for his diplomatic duties.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]



[Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) in 2013

Despite intense pressure from the opposition and coalition partner [Slovak National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_National_Party) to dismiss Lajčák, Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico has staunchly defended him.[^cite_note-64] Fico characterized Lajčák as an "excellent diplomat" and stated he would not "execute" a colleague based on social meetings without evidence of moral or legal failure.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

### December 2025

A photograph released in December 2025 confirmed their personal connection, showing Lajčák and Epstein together at the Slovak diplomatic residence in Vienna.[^cite_note-67]

The meeting took place in March 2019. While planning the gathering in Vienna, Lajčák was told that Epstein would be arriving with three female assistants and arranged for a minivan to pick them up. Epstein attempted to dismiss any potential concerns about their age by assuring Lajčák, "Don't worry, they all look at least 20".[^cite_note-68]

### January 2026

In January 2026, files released by the U.S. Department of Justice showed the correspondence between Lajčák and Epstein was frequent and covered a range of topics. In addition to global politics and international affairs, their exchanges included personal subjects, specifically mentions of women and girls, often framed in an informal or suggestive manner. Lajčák's name appears more than 300 times throughout the documentation.[^cite_note-69]

The records allegedly show Lajčák asking Jeffrey Epstein to introduce him to "young girls" and boasting that Epstein had not yet seen him "in action". Epstein responded by offering him "young girls and their sisters" and Lajčák explicitly requested invitations to participate in Epstein's private "games".[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]
