**Yuval Abraham** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): יובל אברהם) is an Israeli [investigative journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_journalism), film director, and Arabic–Hebrew translator. He rose to international prominence when he co-directed *[No Other Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Other_Land)* (2024), an [Oscar-winning documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature_Film#2020s) about the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) and [settler violence in the West Bank](47246246.md), and gave a pro-equality speech at the [74th Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival).

In 2024, he was awarded the [Emil Grunzweig Human Rights Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Grunzweig_Human_Rights_Award) for his "outstanding contribution" to the advancement of [human rights in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Israel).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

## Early life and education

Yuval Abraham was born to an Israeli middle-class family in the southern city of [Beersheba](312755.md).[^cite_note-2] He is of [Mizrahi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jewish) and [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish) ancestry; his [Jewish Yemenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Jews) grandfather was a fluent [Palestinian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arabic) speaker.[^cite_note-ce_feb_24-3] One of his grandmothers was born in an [Italian concentration camp in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_concentration_camps_in_Libya), and one of his grandfathers lost most of his family in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust).[^cite_note-4]

At 19, Abraham was enlisted and assigned to the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF)'s [Intelligence Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Intelligence_Corps), but he never assumed his role; after a week in training, he refused to serve for political and personal reasons and decided to leave the army. The process of leaving took a few weeks, during which he was assigned to be a [quartermaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartermaster) in the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md). After leaving the military, he did volunteer work with Israeli and Palestinian children at schools for two years.[^cite_note-5]

## Career

### Writing and translation

Learning Arabic and meeting Palestinians in the [West Bank](33209.md), including staying with families as their homes were demolished by the IDF, was how Abraham became an outspoken critic of the Israeli military's occupation of Palestinians.[^cite_note-tna_jul_22-6][^cite_note-variety_feb_24-7] He has worked in language education and taught Arabic.[^cite_note-8]

In 2019, Abraham contacted [London](17867.md)-based journalist Ahmed Alnaouq, who runs the Gaza writers' collective [We Are Not Numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Not_Numbers) (WANN), for an interview. He subsequently helped Alnaouq, who wanted the stories to reach a wider audience, connect with translators.[^cite_note-9] Together, Abraham and Alnaouq founded Across the Wall, a platform where Palestinians' stories are translated into Hebrew with the aim of humanising Palestinians and challenging mainstream Israeli narratives that demonise them.[^cite_note-tna_jul_22-6] The webpage reportedly received over a million visitors in 2021. The project was put on indefinite hiatus in November 2023 after 23 members of Alnaouq's family, including young children, were [killed by Israeli bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Gaza).[^cite_note-10]

As of 2024, was Abraham working as a journalist and investigative reporter for the independent media outlets *[+972 Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/+972_Magazine)* and *[Local Call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Call)*, having called them "the only places" he can "try to use my privilege to expose the mechanisms of oppression in our country, whether it's by documenting the demolition of a Palestinian family's home in Jerusalem or speaking to refugees in Jenin".[^cite_note-11] He also worked for Social TV, contributed to publications such as *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*,[^cite_note-12] *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation)*, and appeared on networks such as *[Democracy Now!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Now!)*[^cite_note-13] and [CNN](62028.md).[^cite_note-14]

In 2024, he was awarded the [Emil Grunzweig Human Rights Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Grunzweig_Human_Rights_Award) for his outstanding contribution to the advancement of [human rights in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Israel).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

### *No Other Land*

Main article: [No Other Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Other_Land)



Abraham and Basel Adra with their Berlinale award

Abraham co-wrote, co-directed, co-filmed, and featured in the documentary *[No Other Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Other_Land)* with [Basel Adra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Adra), [Hamdan Ballal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamdan_Ballal), and [Rachel Szor](79369018.md), about Adra's long-term attempts to resist [Israeli settler violence](47246246.md) and displacement from his home in [Masafer Yatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masafer_Yatta). The group had no experience in documentary filmmaking and initially approached the story as activists and journalists.[^cite_note-ce_feb_24-3] Working together for over five years, with Adra and Ballal having footage including home videos from Adra's family, the documentary depicts Adra befriending Abraham and juxtaposes Adra's life under [occupation](2781576.md) with Abraham's life of freedom.[^cite_note-variety_feb_24-7][^cite_note-15] *No Other Land* opened in February 2024 to critical acclaim at the [74th Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) (Berlinale), where it won two awards: The Panorama Audience Award for Best Documentary Film[^cite_note-16] and the Berlinale Documentary Film Award.[^cite_note-17]

In his acceptance speech, Abraham called for a [ceasefire in Gaza](74998519.md) and for the "situation of [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_and_apartheid)... \[and] inequality" between himself and Adra in the [occupied West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_West_Bank) to end.[^cite_note-18] This drew accusations of antisemitism toward the event from Israeli media and German politicians such as [Kai Wegner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Wegner).[^cite_note-euronews_feb27-19][^cite_note-independent_feb28-20] After receiving death threats and a right-wing mob searching for him at his family's house in [Israel](9282173.md), Abraham was forced to cancel his flight back home.[^cite_note-independent_feb28-20] Abraham made a viral statement on social media: "The appalling misuse of this word... to silence Israelis like me who support a ceasefire... empties the word antisemitism of meaning and thus endangers Jews all over the world". He found it "particularly outraging" coming from German politicians and also expressed concern for Adra's safety "under a military occupation surrounded by violent settlements".[^cite_note-21] The [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists) (CPJ) urged authorities to protect Abraham and his family.[^cite_note-22] In an interview on Israeli television, Abraham said he found it most difficult that his mother paid a price for his speech when a mob came to his house to search for him, causing her to leave, adding that he accepted the criticism of those who scolded him for not mentioning the [Israeli hostages held in Gaza](75025451.md) in his speech, saying "in retrospect, I would have mentioned the hostages", noting the speech was short and unplanned as they did not expect to win, and that calling for a ceasefire benefited the hostages too.[^cite_note-23]

### *NAZA*

Main article: [NAZA (film)](84051935.md)

Abraham co-wrote, co-directed, and featured in the 2026 feature documentary film *[NAZA](84051935.md)*, again with Rachel Szor. It was shot in [Tel Aviv](31453.md) during the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md), and reveals the [Israeli Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Government)'s role in the [mass killing of Palestinian civilians](75668427.md) during the conflict.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-TOI_i748-25][^cite_note-\:0-26] Produced by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* and British producer [James Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wilson_\(producer\))'s JW Films, the film was selected for the main competition of the [83rd Venice International Film Festival](83023289.md)[^cite_note-\:02-27] and was awarded the Special Jury Prize.[^cite_note-28]

On 14 September 2026, shortly after the Venice awards ceremony, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that Israeli culture minister, [Miki Zohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miki_Zohar), had used the social media platform [X](9988187.md) to declare that he would "act immediately to revoke the Israeli citizenship of these despicable creators \[Abraham and Szor] for treason against the state".[^cite_note-29] [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), the [Prime Minister of Israel](24898.md), subsequently announced that his government would seek to pass legislation to revoke the citizenship of Israelis who "defame" Israeli soldiers abroad, citing Abraham and Szor in the statement.[^cite_note-30]
