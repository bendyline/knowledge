The **Gulfstream G100**, formerly known as the **IAI Astra**, is an [Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries)-manufactured twin-engine [business jet](247003.md), that was produced for [Gulfstream Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_Aerospace). Deliveries began in 1986. The [United States Navy](20518076.md) employs the aircraft as the **C-38A Courier**. A later derivative known as the **G150** was launched in 2002. Gulfstream announced the final sale of the G150 in September 2016 and the last delivery by mid-2017.[^cite_note-20160928PR-2]

## Design and development



IAI Astra at the 1988 Paris Air Show

[Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries) (IAI) developed the Astra from its [Model 1124 Westwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Westwind) business jet. Work on an improved Westwind began in the late 1970s,[^cite_note-AI_DEC93p314-3] with the first prototype flight on 19 March 1984.[^cite_note-AI_DEC93p316-4] The first production Astra flew on 20 March 1985, with [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA) certification granted on 29 August 1985 and customer deliveries starting in 1986.[^cite_note-AI_DEC93p316-4]

The original 1125 Astra was replaced by the **Astra SP**, announced in 1989; 37 were built. The third variant, the *Astra SPX*, flew for the first time in August 1994. This variant was renamed G100 from September 2002 following Gulfstream's acquisition of [Galaxy Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_Aerospace), which held the Astra [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate), in May 2001.

In September 2002, Gulfstream announced the improved **G150**, based on the G100. This last variant features a wider (12 in) and longer fuselage (16 in aft of rear pressure bulkhead) updated [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) and an increase in [maximum takeoff weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_takeoff_weight) (MTOW) to 26,100 pounds (11,839 kg) compared to the G100's MTOW of 24,650 pounds (11,181 kg). It first flew on 3 May 2005 and was certified by the FAA in late 2005.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-TCDS-6] It has been certified for the steeper-than-normal approach path required to land at [London City Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_City_Airport).[^cite_note-awG150-7] IAI continued to manufacture G150s in Israel and the completed airframes were then flown to [Dallas](53838.md) in the U.S. for interior outfitting. In September 2016, Gulfstream announced that, owing to slow sales, production would be stopped, with delivery of the final aircraft due in mid-2017.[^cite_note-8]

The Astra was further developed in the 1990s; the wing was modified and mated to a completely new fuselage. This development became the IAI Galaxy (later the [Gulfstream G200](11251639.md)).[^cite_note-9]

By 2018, Gulfstream G150s from 2006–2008 were in the range of \$3.8 to \$4.8 million.[^cite_note-AINdec2018-10]

## Operational history



C-38A (G100) and [Boeing C-40 Clipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_C-40_Clipper) of the United States [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard)



A civil-registered G100 landing at [Palm Springs International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs_International_Airport), 2023

The G100 was ordered for the [United States Air Force](32090.md) in 1997 as the *C-38A Courier*. The C-38A was operated by the [201st Airlift Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/201st_Airlift_Squadron) at [Andrews Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrews_Air_Force_Base) in Maryland until 2015. The C-38A replaced the earlier [Learjet C-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_35/36). The C-38A differs from the standard Gulfstream G100, being fitted with various military avionics systems.[^cite_note-11]

The C-38A was also ordered for the [United States Navy](20518076.md), replacing [North American T-2 Buckeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_T-2_Buckeye) at [Naval Air Station Patuxent River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Patuxent_River) beginning in September 2015. Two aircraft remain in service as of 2023, both former [201st Airlift Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/201st_Airlift_Squadron) C-38's. The C-38 is tasked with acting as a [chase plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_plane), radar test target, and pilot proficiency aircraft for the test and evaluation squadron [VX-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VX-20).[^cite_note-paxriver-12]

In 2012, an IAI Astra operated by the [Eritrean Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Air_Force) as the Eritrean presidential aircraft was stolen by two pilots, both serving Eritrean Air Force officers. They flew the Astra to Saudi Arabia and requested political asylum after landing at [Jizan Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizan_Regional_Airport).[^cite_note-Eritrea-13]

## Notable accidents and incidents

- On March 10 2024, an IAI 1125 Astra SP registration N1125A crashed on approach to Ingalls Field Airport, [Hot Springs, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Virginia). It struck trees and hillside terrain short of the runway. The aircraft was owned and operated by SkyJet Elite and had 5 people on board at the time of the accident.
