***Yakuza*** ([Japanese](15606.md): ヤクザ; IPA: [\[jaꜜkɯ(d)za\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese); English: [/jəˈkuːzə, ˈjækuːzə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), also known as ***gokudō*** (極道; "the extreme path", IPA: [\[gokɯꜜdoː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)), or simply the **Japanese mafia**, are members of transnational [organized crime syndicates](22625.md) originating in [Japan](15573.md). The [Japanese police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_police) and media (by request of the police) call them ***bōryokudan*** (暴力団; "violent groups", IPA: [\[boːɾʲokɯꜜdaɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)), while the yakuza call themselves ***ninkyō dantai*** (任侠団体; "chivalrous organizations", IPA: [\[ɲiŋʲkʲoː dantai\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)). The English equivalent for the term *yakuza* is [gangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangster), meaning an individual involved in a [Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia)-like criminal organization, but is generally used in English-speaking countries to refer to the criminal organization as a whole.[^cite_note-3]

The yakuza are known for their strict codes of conduct, their organized [fiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) nature, and several unconventional ritual practices such as *[yubitsume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubitsume)*, the amputation of the left little finger.[^cite_note-4] Members are often portrayed with heavily tattooed bodies and wearing a *[fundoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundoshi)*, sometimes with a [kimono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimono) or, in more recent years, a Western-style "sharp" suit covering them.[^cite_note-5]

At their height, the yakuza maintained a large presence in the [Japanese media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Japan), and they also [operated internationally](6760775.md). In 1963, the number of yakuza members and quasi-members reached a peak of 184,100.[^cite_note-npa99-6] However, this number has drastically dropped, a decline attributed to changing market opportunities and [several legal and social developments](#Law_enforcement_and_indirect_enforcement) in Japan that discourage the growth of yakuza membership.[^cite_note-7] In 1991, there were 63,800 members and 27,200 quasi-members, but by 2025, only 9,400 members and 8,200 quasi-members.[^cite_note-npa-2] The yakuza are aging because young people no longer readily join, and the average age of members at the end of 2022 was 54 years. Of that, only 5% were in their 20s, while 13% in their 30s, 26% in their 40s, 31% in their 50s, 13% in their 60s, and 12% in their 70s or older. More than half of the members were at least 50 years or older.[^cite_note-8]

The yakuza still regularly engage in an array of criminal activities, and many Japanese citizens remain fearful of the threat these individuals pose to their safety.[^cite_note-Shikata2006-9] There remains no strict prohibition on yakuza membership in Japan today, although many pieces of legislation have been passed by the [Japanese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_government) aimed at impeding revenue and increasing liability for criminal activities.[^cite_note-Shikata2006-9]

## Etymology

The name *yakuza* gives status and originates from the traditional Japanese card game *[Oicho-Kabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oicho-Kabu)*, a game in which the goal is to draw three cards adding up to a score of 9. If the sum of the cards is 10 or more, the second digit is the score. So a sum of 13 is a score of 3, a sum of 14 is a score of 4, etc. A sum of 10 or 20 is a score of 0. If the three cards drawn are 8-9-3 (pronounced **ya-ku-za** when counting in Japanese), the sum is 20 and therefore the score is zero, making one of the worst possible hands that can be drawn.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] In [Japanese](15606.md), the word *yakuza* is commonly written in [katakana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katakana) (ヤクザ).

## Origins



A yakuza with a dragon tattoo is running to help his comrade, who is fighting against the police.



Yakuza boss [Shimizu Jirocho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimizu_Jirocho) (Chogoro Yamamoto)

Despite uncertainty about the single origin of yakuza organizations, most modern yakuza derive from two social classifications which emerged in the mid-[Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) (1603–1868): *[tekiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekiya)*, those who primarily peddled [illicit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illicit_trade), stolen or shoddy goods; and *[bakuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakuto)*, those who were involved in or participated in [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling_in_Japan).[^cite_note-12]

*Tekiya* (peddlers) ranked as one of the [lowest social groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_occupations) during the Edo period. As they began to form organizations of their own, they took over some administrative duties relating to commerce, such as stall allocation and protection of their commercial activities.[^cite_note-13] During [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) [festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_festivals), these peddlers opened stalls and some members were hired to act as security. Each peddler paid rent in exchange for a stall assignment and [protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket) during the fair.

The *tekiya* were a highly structured and hierarchical group with the *oyabun* (boss) at the top and *kobun* (gang members) at the bottom.[^cite_note-kyotojournal.org-14] This hierarchy resembles a structure similar to the family – in traditional [Japanese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Japan), the *oyabun* was often regarded as a surrogate father, and the *kobun* as surrogate children.[^cite_note-kyotojournal.org-14] During the Edo period, the government formally recognized the *tekiya*. At this time, within the *tekiya*, the *oyabun* were appointed as supervisors and granted near-[samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai) status, meaning they were allowed the dignity of a surname and two swords.[^cite_note-15]

*Bakuto* (gamblers) had a much lower social standing even than traders, as gambling was illegal. Many small gambling houses cropped up in abandoned temples or shrines at the edges of towns and villages all over Japan. Most of these gambling houses ran [loan-sharking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_shark) businesses for clients, and they usually maintained their own security personnel. Society at large regarded the gambling houses themselves, as well as the *bakuto*, with disdain. Much of the undesirable image of the yakuza originates from *bakuto*; this includes the name *yakuza* itself.

Because of the economic situation during the mid-Edo period and the predominance of the merchant class, developing yakuza groups were composed of misfits and delinquents who had joined or formed the groups to extort customers in local markets by selling fake or shoddy goods.

[Shimizu Jirocho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimizu_Jirocho) (1820–1893) is Japan's most famous yakuza and folk hero.[^cite_note-16] He was born Chogoro Yamamoto, but changed his name when he was adopted, a common Japanese practice.[^cite_note-jirocho-17] His life and exploits were featured in sixteen films between 1911 and 1940.

The roots of the yakuza survive today in [initiation ceremonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiation_ceremony), which incorporate tekiya or bakuto [rituals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rituals). Although the modern Yakuza has diversified, some gangs still identify with one group or the other; for example, a gang whose primary source of income is illegal gambling may refer to themselves as *bakuto*.

### Kyushu



[Kyushu](16914.md), a well-known source of many yakuza members

The island of [Kyushu](16914.md) (and particularly its northern prefecture [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Prefecture)) has a reputation for being a large source of yakuza members,[^cite_note-JapaneseJusticeJohnson2002-18] including many renowned bosses in the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi).[^cite_note-Baradel_2021_pp._74–91-19] Isokichi Yoshida (1867–1936) from the [Kitakyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitakyushu) area was considered by some scholars and political watchers as one of the first renowned modern yakuza.[^cite_note-Siniawer_2012_pp._623–641-20] Recently [Shinobu Tsukasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinobu_Tsukasa) and Kunio Inoue, the bosses of the two most powerful clans in the Yamaguchi-gumi, originate from Kyushu. [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Prefecture), the northernmost part of the island, has the largest number of [designated syndicates](#Designated_bōryokudan) among all of the prefectures.[^cite_note-21]

## Organization and activities

### Structure



A simplified organizational chart of the Yakuza. At the top of the image is *oyabun* (*kumichō*, 組長), the pseudo-father of the organization; to the lower right of *oyabun* are *oyabun'*&#x73; pseudo younger brothers *shatei* (舎弟), and directly below *oyabun* is *wakagashira* (若頭), oyabun's pseudo first son. Below *wakagashira* are *kumiin* (組員), pseudo younger brothers of *wakagashira* who are pseudo children of *oyabun*, and they are called *wakanaka* or *wakashū*. *Kumiin* can refer to any member of the organization, regardless of position.



Large yakuza organizations have a multi-layered structure, with executives and members running their own organizations.

During the formation of the yakuza, they adopted the traditional Japanese hierarchical structure of oyabun-kobun where *kobun* (子分; lit. foster child) owe their allegiance to the *oyabun* (親分; lit. foster parent). In a much later period, the code of *jingi* (仁義; justice and duty) was developed where loyalty and respect are a way of life. The oyabun-kobun relationship is formalized by ceremonial sharing of [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake) from a single cup. This ritual is not exclusive to the yakuza – it is also commonly performed in traditional Japanese [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) weddings, and may have been a part of sworn brotherhood relationships.[^cite_note-trutv.com-22]

The structure of yakuza organizations is characterized by a multi-layered hierarchical structure. The *kobun* (pseudo child) of an organization become *oyabun* (family head and pseudo parent) and run their own subsidiary organizations, and the *kobun* of the subsidiary organizations also become *oyabun* and run their own subsidiary organizations. A large yakuza organization such as the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi) is made up of five or six layered organizations.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In other words, the heads of subsidiary organizations are often executives of the parent organizations.[^cite_note-26]

The *oyabun* of the organization is generally called a *kumichō* (組長) or *kaichō* (会長, chairman), the person corresponding to pseudo younger brother of the *kumichō* is called a *shatei* (舎弟), and the person corresponding to pseudo child of the *kumichō* (*kobun*) is called a *wakanaka* (若中) or *wakashū* (若衆).[^cite_note-zak101015-27] *Shatei* (舎弟) means younger brother, and its status varies greatly depending on whose brother it is. From the perspective of the organization as a whole, *shatei* generally refers to the younger brother of an *oyabun*, and thus to a high-ranking person in the organization,[^cite_note-zak101015-27] but it is also possible for a lower-level member to refer to his pseudo younger brother as a *shatei*.[^cite_note-matsue8-28]

Although positions in yakuza organizations vary from clan to clan, the three most important yakuza positions are *kumichō*, *wakagashira* (若頭, second-in-command and pseudo eldest son of the *kumichō*), and *shateigashira* (舎弟頭, pseudo oldest younger brother of the *kumichō*). *Honbuchō* (本部長, general manager), *fuku-kumichō* (副組長, deputy *kumichō*), and *jimukyokuchō* (事務局長, secretary general), are also important positions.[^cite_note-bungei280522-29][^cite_note-shogaku10424-30] In general, the *shikkōbu* (執行部, executive office) of an organization is run by the *wakagashira*, *shateigashira*, and *honbuchō*. Since the *shatei* are the pseudo-younger brothers of the *kumichō*, they are generally older and have higher nominal positions but relatively little real authority.[^cite_note-31] In the Yamaguchi-gumi pecking order as of 2024, the order is *kumichō*, *wakagashira*, *shateigashira*, and *honbuchō*.[^cite_note-shogaku10424-30]

Yakuza groups are headed by an *oyabun* (*kumichō* or *kaichō*) who gives orders to his subordinates, the *kobun*. In this respect, the organization is a variation of the traditional Japanese [senpai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senpai)-[kōhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohai) (senior-junior) model. Members of yakuza cut their real family ties and transfer their loyalty to the *oyabun*. They refer to each other as family members—*oyaji* (親父, fathers), *ojiki* (叔父貴, uncles), and *kyōdai* (兄弟, elder and younger brothers). Among brothers, it is common to refer to a person equal to oneself as *kyōdai*, a younger brother as *kyōdai* or *shatei*, and an older brother as *aniki* (兄貴, older brother).[^cite_note-matsue8-28] The yakuza is populated almost entirely by men and the very few women who are acknowledged are the wives of bosses, who are referred to by the title *ane-san* (姐さん; older sister). When the 3rd Yamaguchi-gumi boss ([Kazuo Taoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuo_Taoka)) died in the early 1980s, his wife (Fumiko) took over as boss of Yamaguchi-gumi, albeit for a short time.

During the [World War II](32927.md) period in Japan, the more traditional tekiya/bakuto form of organization declined as the entire population was mobilised to participate in the war effort and society came under the control of the strict military government. However, after the war, the Yakuza adapted again.[^cite_note-32]

#### Rituals



An early example of [irezumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irezumi) tattoos, 1870s

[Yubitsume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubitsume), also referred to as *otoshimae*, or the cutting off of one's finger, is a form of penance or apology. Upon a first offence, the transgressor must cut off the tip of his left little finger and give the severed portion to his boss. Sometimes an underboss may do this in penance to the oyabun if he wants to spare a member of his own gang from further retaliation. This practice has started to wane amongst the younger members, due to it being an easy identifier for police.[^cite_note-cnn.com-33]

Its origin stems from the traditional way of holding a [Japanese sword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenjutsu). The bottom three fingers of each hand are used to grip the sword tightly, with the thumb and index fingers slightly loose. The removal of digits starting with the little finger and moving up the hand to the index finger progressively weakens a person's sword grip.

The idea is that a person with a weak sword grip then has to rely more on the group for protection—reducing individual action. In recent years, [prosthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosthetic) fingertips have been developed to disguise this distinctive appearance.[^cite_note-trutv.com-22]

Many yakuza have full-body tattoos (including their [genitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genitalia)). These tattoos, known as [irezumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irezumi) in Japan, are still often "[hand-poked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand-poked)", that is, the ink is inserted beneath the skin using non-electrical, hand-made, and handheld tools with needles of sharpened bamboo or steel. The procedure is expensive and painful, and can take years to complete.[^cite_note-34]

When yakuza play [Oicho-Kabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oicho-Kabu) cards with each other, they often remove their shirts or open them up and drape them around their waists. This enables them to display their full-body tattoos to each other. This is one of the few times that yakuza display their tattoos to others, as they normally keep them concealed in public with long-sleeved and high-necked shirts. When new members join, they are often required to remove their trousers as well and reveal any lower body tattoos.

### Number of members and quasi-members

The total number of yakuza members and quasi-members peaked at 184,100 in 1963, and then continued to decline due to police crackdowns.[^cite_note-npa99-6] The number of regular members decreased with the implementation of the Act on Prevention of Unjust Acts by Organized Crime Group Members ([暴力団員による不当な行為の防止等に関する法律](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9A%B4%E5%8A%9B%E5%9B%A3%E5%93%A1%E3%81%AB%E3%82%88%E3%82%8B%E4%B8%8D%E5%BD%93%E3%81%AA%E8%A1%8C%E7%82%BA%E3%81%AE%E9%98%B2%E6%AD%A2%E7%AD%89%E3%81%AB%E9%96%A2%E3%81%99%E3%82%8B%E6%B3%95%E5%BE%8B)) in 1992,[^cite_note-genmika-35] and the total number of members and quasi-members began to decline rapidly with the implementation of the [yakuza exclusion ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_exclusion_ordinances) in all 47 prefectures around 2010. Between 1990 and 2020, the total number of members and quasi-members decreased by 70 percent.[^cite_note-nhk300622-36]

The National Police Agency reported that Japanese yakuza organizations had 9,400 members and 8,200 quasi-members in 2025.[^cite_note-npa-2]

### &#x20;Designated yakuza (*Shitei Bōryokudan*)

A designated yakuza (指定暴力団, *Shitei Bōryokudan*)[^cite_note-37] is a "particularly harmful" yakuza group[^cite_note-38] registered by the [Prefectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_prefectures) [Public Safety Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Safety_Commission_\(Japan\)) under the Act on Prevention of Unjust Acts by Organized Crime Group Members (暴力団対策法, *Bōryokudan Taisaku Hō*) enacted in 1991.[^cite_note-39] Groups are designated as *Shitei Bōryokudan* (designated yakuza) if their members take advantage of the gang's influence to do business, are structured to have one leader, and have a large portion of their members hold criminal records.[^cite_note-openscholarship.wustl.edu-40] After the Act on Prevention of Unjust Acts by Organized Crime Group Members was enacted, many yakuza syndicates made efforts to restructure to appear more professional and legitimate.[^cite_note-openscholarship.wustl.edu-40]

As of 2025, under the Act on Prevention of Unjust Acts by Organized Crime Group Members, the Prefectural Public Safety Commissions have registered 25 syndicates as the designated yakuza groups. Three of these organizations have more than 1,000 regular members, eight have more than 100, and 14 have less than 100. [Fukuoka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Prefecture) has the largest number of designated yakuza groups among all of the prefectures, at 5; the [Kudo-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudo-kai), the [Taishu-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishu-kai), the [Fukuhaku-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuhaku-kai), the [Dojin-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dojin-kai), and the [Namikawa-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_Seido-kai).[^cite_note-npa-2]

In August 2021, the Fukuoka District Court sentenced [Satoru Nomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoru_Nomura), the fifth head of [Kudo-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudo-kai), to death for murder and attempted murder. This was the first death sentence handed down to a designated yakuza head. His death sentence was eventually overturned and reduced to life imprisonment in March 2024. Kudo-kai is the only one of the designated yakuza to be designated as an especially dangerous designated yakuza (特定危険指定暴力団, *Tokutei Kiken Shitei Bōryokudan*), a more dangerous type of yakuza.[^cite_note-41]

### Three largest syndicates and six major syndicates

As of 2025, the National Police Agency has designated [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi), [Kobe Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Yamaguchi-gumi), [Kizuna-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizuna-kai), [Ikeda-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeda-gumi?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([ja](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B1%A0%E7%94%B0%E7%B5%84)), [Sumiyoshi-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi-kai), and [Inagawa-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagawa-kai) as *Shuyō dantai* (主要団体, major organizations) among the designated yakuza. These six organizations have a total of 7,000 members and 5,500 quasi-members, for a total of 12,500 members, or 71.0 percent of the total 17,600 yakuza members and quasi-members in Japan.[^cite_note-npa-2]

Kobe Yamaguchi-gumi split off from Yamaguchi-gumi in August 2015, Kizuna-kai split off from Kobe Yamaguchi-gumi in April 2017, and Ikeda-gumi split off from Kobe Yamaguchi-gumi in July 2020. These Yamaguchi-gumi and the three organizations that split from them are fighting each other.[^cite_note-npa-2]

In recent years, the three major yakuza syndicates have formed a loose alliance, and in April 2023, [Kiyoshi Takayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyoshi_Takayama), the *wakagashira* (second-in-command) of the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi), [Shuji Ogawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuji_Ogawa?action=edit\&redlink=1), the *kaichō* (chairman) of the [Sumiyoshi-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi-kai), and [Kazuya Uchibori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuya_Uchibori?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([ja](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%86%85%E5%A0%80%E5%92%8C%E4%B9%9F)), the *kaichō* of the [Inagawa-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagawa-kai), held a social gathering.[^cite_note-42]

| [Sumiyoshi-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi-kai) (住吉会) | The Sumiyoshi-kai is the second-largest yakuza family, with an estimated 2,100 members and 1,100 quasi-members as of 2025.[^cite_note-npa-2] Sumiyoshi-kai is a confederation of smaller yakuza groups. Its current head (会長 kai-cho) is Shūji Ogawa. Structurally, Sumiyoshi-kai differs from its principal rival, the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi), in that it functions like a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation). The chain of command is more relaxed, and its leadership is distributed among several other members. |  |

## Current activities

### Japan

In the 2010s and 2020s, the yakuza's main source of funding is a fraud called *tokushu sagi* (特殊詐欺, special fraud), which mainly targets the elderly to cheat them out of large amounts of money. Their methods include calling the homes of elderly people to beg for money by pretending to be the elderly person's son, or visiting the homes of elderly people posing as employees of financial institutions to swindle them out of their money. In 2014, *tokushu sagi* accounted for 10.4 percent of all yakuza arrests, surpassing theft for the first time at 10.2 percent, and 34.6 percent of *tokushu sagi* perpetrators were yakuza and their associates. The total damage from *tokushu sagi* identified in 2019 exceeded 30 billion yen, and while yakuza accounted for just over 10 percent of the lowest level members of criminal groups, such as cash receivers who play the role of financial institution employees, yakuza accounted for nearly 40 percent of the main perpetrators leading fraud schemes.[^cite_note-nik120515-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

The yakuza and its affiliated gangs control drug trafficking in Japan, especially [methamphetamine](11038318.md).[^cite_note-47] While many yakuza syndicates, notably the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi), officially forbid their members from engaging in [drug trafficking](218449.md), some other yakuza syndicates, like the [Dojin-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dojin-kai), are heavily involved in it. The most common charge for yakuza and their associates arrested in 2014 was violation of the Stimulants Control Act (覚せい剤取締法), which prohibits the import, export, sale, transfer, possession, and use of methamphetamine, accounting for 26.5 percent of arrests.[^cite_note-nik120515-44][^cite_note-48]

Some yakuza groups are known to deal extensively in [human trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_trafficking_in_Japan).[^cite_note-49] In the [Philippines](23440.md), yakuza trick girls from impoverished villages into coming to Japan by promising them respectable jobs with good wages. Instead, they are forced into becoming sex workers and strippers.[^cite_note-50]



The [Kabukicho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabukicho) district of [Shinjuku](147734.md) has historically been known as a popular yakuza hangout in Tokyo.

Yakuza frequently engaged in a unique form of Japanese [extortion](162449.md) known as *[sōkaiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%8Dkaiya)*. In essence, this is a specialized form of [protection racket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket). Instead of harassing small businesses, the Yakuza harass a stockholders' meeting of a larger corporation. Yakuza operatives obtain the right to attend by making a small purchase of stock, and then at the meeting physically intimidate other stockholders. The number of *sōkaiya* has decreased over the years, and in 2024 there were only about 130 *sōkaiya*, of whom 20 worked in groups and 110 worked alone.[^cite_note-npa-7-51]

Yakuza also had ties to the Japanese real estate market and banking sector through *jiageya*. Jiageya specializes in inducing holders of small real estate to sell their property so that estate companies can carry out much larger development plans. The [Japanese bubble economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_bubble_economy) of the 1980s is often blamed on real estate speculation by banking subsidiaries. After the collapse of the property bubble, a manager of a major bank in [Nagoya](61720.md) was assassinated, prompting much speculation about the banking industry's indirect connection to the Japanese underworld.[^cite_note-52]



Yakuza often take part in local festivals such as [Sanja Matsuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanja_Matsuri), where they often ride the shrine through the streets proudly showing off their elaborate tattoos.

In 1989, Susumu Ishii, the [Oyabun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyabun) of the [Inagawa-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagawa-kai) (a well-known yakuza group) bought [US\$](18717338.md)255 million worth of [Tokyo Kyuko Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Kyuko_Electric_Railway)'s stock.[^cite_note-53] Japan's [Securities and Exchange Surveillance Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Surveillance_Commission) has knowledge of more than 50 listed companies with ties to organized crime, and in March 2008, the [Osaka Securities Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Securities_Exchange) decided to review all listed companies and expel those with yakuza ties.[^cite_note-adelstein-54]

Yakuza involvement in [Japanese politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_politics) functions similarly to that of a [lobbying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbyist), with them backing those who share in their opinions or beliefs. The Yakuza had a long, intertwined history with politics since the end of World War II, having leveraged on [black markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_markets) for economic gains and funded [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative) political parties such as the ruling [Liberal Democratic Party](164231.md) (LDP) for protection and favoritism. Their involvement ranged from controlling the vital [construction industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_industry) that was essential for post-war rebuilding to [intelligence work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_field) against communist and leftist influence in Japan with the [involvement of the American Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_activities_in_Japan). The most prominent intelligence asset was [Yoshio Kodama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshio_Kodama), who played a key intermediary between the Japanese government, the CIA and the Yakuza underworld.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

#### Yakuza's aid in earthquakes

In the wake of the [1995 Kobe earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hanshin_earthquake), the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi) yakuza group, who are based in the area, mobilized to provide disaster relief services (including the use of a helicopter). Media reports contrasted this rapid response with the much slower pace at which the Japanese government's official relief efforts took place.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

Following the [Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami) on 11 March 2011, the yakuza sent hundreds of trucks filled with food, water, blankets, and sanitary accessories to aid the people in the affected areas of the natural disaster.[^cite_note-adelstein20110318-60] [CNN México](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_en_Espa%C3%B1ol) said that although the yakuza operates through extortion and other violent methods, they "\[moved] swiftly and quietly to provide aid to those most in need."[^cite_note-cnnmexico-61]

### United States

The presence of individuals affiliated with the yakuza in the United States has increased tremendously since the 1960s, and although much of their activity is concentrated in [Hawaii](13270.md), they have made their presence known in other parts of the country, especially in [Los Angeles](18110.md) and the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area), as well as [Seattle](11388236.md), [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_metropolitan_area), [Arizona](21883824.md), [Virginia](32432.md), [Chicago](6886.md), and [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-crimelibrary-62][^cite_note-yakuza03-america-63] The yakuza are said to use Hawaii as a midway station between Japan and mainland America, smuggling [methamphetamine](11038318.md) into the country and smuggling firearms back to Japan. They easily fit into the local population, since many tourists from Japan and other Asian countries visit the islands on a regular basis, and there is a large population of residents who are of full or partial Japanese descent. They also work with local gangs, funneling Japanese tourists to gambling parlors and brothels.[^cite_note-crimelibrary-62]

In California, the yakuza have made alliances with local Korean gangs as well as [Chinese triads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_\(organized_crime\)) and Vietnamese gangs. The yakuza identified these gangs as useful partners due to the constant stream of Vietnamese cafe shoot-outs and home invasion burglaries throughout the 1980s and early 1990s. In New York City, they appear to collect finder's fees from Russian, Irish and Italian gang members and businessmen for guiding Japanese tourists to gambling establishments, both legal and illegal.[^cite_note-crimelibrary-62]

[Handguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handgun) manufactured in the US account for a large share (33%) of handguns seized in Japan, followed by handguns manufactured in [China](5405.md) (16%) and in the [Philippines](23440.md) (10%). In 1990, a [Smith & Wesson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_&_Wesson) [.38 caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.38_caliber) [revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolver) that cost \$275 in the US could sell for up to \$4,000 in Tokyo.

In 2001, the FBI's representative in Tokyo arranged for [Tadamasa Goto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadamasa_Goto), the head of the group [Goto-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goto-gumi), to receive a [liver transplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_transplant) at the [UCLA Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Medical_Center) in the United States, in return for information of Yamaguchi-gumi operations in the US. This was done without prior consultation of the [NPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Agency_\(Japan\)). The journalist who uncovered the deal received threats from Goto and was given police protection in the US and in Japan.[^cite_note-adelstein-54]

The [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) suspects that the yakuza were using various operations to launder money in the US as of 2008.[^cite_note-adelstein-54]

### Brazil

As the country with the biggest Japanese diaspora outside of Japan, [Brazil](3383.md) has had a significant yakuza presence approximately since the 1990s. A yakuza leader from the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi) faction, Hitoshi Tanabe, was arrested in the country after Japan expedited an arrest warrant for him in the accusation of trafficking cocaine, he was later extradited.[^cite_note-64]

In 2011, 15 people allegedly connected to the yakuza were arrested in [São Paulo](390875.md), were they also established an alliance with the Brazilian drug cartel [Primeiro Comando da Capital](4711820.md) (PCC).[^cite_note-65]

Also in 2011, [Interpol](14338129.md) agents arrested two Brazilian citizens of Japanese descent working for the yakuza, Cristian Ito and Marcelo Cristian Gomes Fukuda, accused of the murder of the merchant Yoshitaka Kawakami and attempted murder of his wife Naomi Kawakami in [Tokyo](30057.md) in August 2001.[^cite_note-66]

### Asia (outside Japan)

The yakuza have engaged in illegal activities in [Southeast Asia](28741.md) since the 1960s; they are working there to develop sex tourism and drug trafficking.[^cite_note-67] This is the area where they are still the most active today.[^cite_note-68]

In addition to their presence in Southeast Asian countries such as [Thailand](30128.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), and [Vietnam](202354.md), yakuza groups also operate in [South Korea](27019.md), [China](5405.md), [Taiwan](25734.md), and in the [Pacific Islands](341111.md) (mainly [Hawaii](13270.md)).[^cite_note-page104-69]

Yakuza groups also have a presence in [North Korea](21255.md), with alleged close ties to the regime.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] In 2009, yakuza Yoshiaki Sawada was released from a North Korean prison after spending five years there attempting to bribe a North Korean official and smuggle drugs.[^cite_note-72]

## Constituent members

Prospective yakuza come from all walks of life. The most romantic tales tell how yakuza accept sons who have been abandoned or exiled by their parents. Many yakuza start out in junior high school or high school as common street thugs or members of [bōsōzoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C5%8Ds%C5%8Dzoku) gangs. Perhaps because of its lower socio-economic status, numerous yakuza members come from burakumin and ethnic Korean backgrounds. Low-ranking youth may be referred to as *chinpira* or *chimpira*.[^cite_note-Dore2015-73][^cite_note-SJ2014-74]

According to statistics published annually by the Japanese government, the nationalities of yakuza members newly incarcerated in prisons in 2024 were as follows, 576 Japanese nationals, 8 South Korean nationals, and 1 American national.[^cite_note-75]

According to a 2006 speech by Mitsuhiro Suganuma, a former officer of the Public Security Intelligence Agency, around 60 percent of yakuza members come from burakumin, the descendants of a feudal outcast class and approximately 30 percent of yakuza are Japanese-born Koreans, and only 10 percent are from non-burakumin Japanese and Chinese ethnic groups.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

### Burakumin

The [burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin), a group that has historically been discriminated against within Japanese society dating back to the [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period) in the 11th century, have a disproportionate percentage of yakuza members. The burakumin are believed to be [descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineal_descendant) of various outcast communities in pre-modern Japan, especially the [feudal era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal_Japan_hierarchy). Those communities are mainly those people in occupations considered tainted because their association with death or [ritual impurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual_impurity), such as butchers, [executioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executioner), [undertakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undertaker), or [leather workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather_worker). Burakumin traditionally lived in their own secluded [hamlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)) and villages away from other groups.[^cite_note-78]

According to [David E. Kaplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Kaplan_\(author\)) and Alec Dubro, burakumin account for about 70% of the members of [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi), the largest yakuza syndicate in Japan.[^cite_note-79]

### Ethnic Koreans

While [ethnic Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_Japan) make up only 0.5% of the Japanese population, they are a prominent part of yakuza because they suffer discrimination in Japanese society along with the *[burakumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burakumin)*.[^cite_note-yakuza03-133-80][^cite_note-81] In the early 1990s, 18 of 90 top bosses of *[Inagawa-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagawa-kai)* were ethnic Koreans. The [Japanese National Police Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Agency_\(Japan\)) suggested Koreans composed 10% of the yakuza proper.[^cite_note-yakuza03-133-80] Some of the representatives of the [designated Bōryokudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C5%8Dryokudan#Designated_b%C5%8Dryokudan) are also Koreans.[^cite_note-82] The Korean significance had been an untouchable taboo in Japan and one of the reasons that the Japanese version of Kaplan and Dubro's *Yakuza* (1986) had not been published until 1991 with the deletion of Korean-related descriptions of the *Yamaguchi-gumi*.[^cite_note-83]

Japanese-born people of Korean ancestry who retain South Korean nationality are considered resident aliens and are embraced by the yakuza precisely because they fit the group's "outsider" image.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-cnn.com-33]

Notable yakuza of Korean ancestry include [Hisayuki Machii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisayuki_Machii), the founder of the [Tosei-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toa-kai), [Tokutaro Takayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokutaro_Takayama), the head of the 4th-generation [Aizukotetsu-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aizukotetsu-kai), Jiro Kiyota, the head of the 5th-generation [Inagawa-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagawa-kai), Shinichi Matsuyama, the head of the 5th-generation [Kyokuto-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyokuto-kai), and Hirofumi Hashimoto, the founder of the now-defunct Kyokushinrengo-kai.

## Law enforcement and indirect enforcement

### Operation Summit

Between 1964 and 1965, the Japanese police carried out mass arrests of yakuza leaders and executives in what they called the *Daiichiji chōjō sakusen* (第一次頂上作戦, First Operation Summit) in response to public demands for the yakuza to be banished from society. As a result, crime declined and the number of arrested yakuza fell from about 59,000 in 1964 to 38,000 in 1967. The number of yakuza organizations and members also declined, from 5,216 organizations and 184,091 members in 1963 to 3,500 organizations and 139,089 members in 1969.[^cite_note-moj89i-85] As a result, 1963, the year before the First Operation Summit was launched, was the peak of yakuza power.[^cite_note-npa99-6]

From around 1970, yakuza leaders and executives who had been imprisoned began to be released from prison, and yakuza organizations that had been disbanded during the First Operation Summit were revived and reorganized, leading the police to conduct the Second Operation Summit in 1970 and the Third Operation Summit in 1975. These series of police crackdowns led to a decline in the number of yakuza organizations and members, from 2,957 organizations with 123,044 members in 1972 to 2,517 organizations with 106,754 members in 1979. As a result, small yakuza organizations were forced to dissolve, and the total number of members decreased, but some members transferred to large yakuza organizations, so the number of members of large organizations actually increased during this period. The three major organizations, Yamaguchi-gumi, Sumiyoshi-kai, and Inagawa-kai, expanded during this period. During this period, Japan was in a recession following the [energy crisis of the 1970s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis), and it became difficult for the yakuza to acquire sufficient financial resources through traditional methods alone, so it was inevitable that they would consolidate into large yakuza organizations with diverse or legal sources of funding.[^cite_note-npa99-6][^cite_note-moj89ni-86]

### Anti-yakuza laws

The Act on Prevention of Unjust Acts by Organized Crime Group Members ([暴力団員による不当な行為の防止等に関する法律](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9A%B4%E5%8A%9B%E5%9B%A3%E5%93%A1%E3%81%AB%E3%82%88%E3%82%8B%E4%B8%8D%E5%BD%93%E3%81%AA%E8%A1%8C%E7%82%BA%E3%81%AE%E9%98%B2%E6%AD%A2%E7%AD%89%E3%81%AB%E9%96%A2%E3%81%99%E3%82%8B%E6%B3%95%E5%BE%8B)), passed in 1991 and enacted in 1992, was a landmark piece of legislation that cracked down on the yakuza. The law prohibited 27 acts by yakuza, including demanding hush money or donations, collecting debts and conducting land grabbing activities in an unjustified manner. The law also made it illegal to demand and collect so-called *mikajime-ryō* (みかじめ料, protection racket) from downtown restaurants and bars, which were the yakuza's main source of funding. Police could issue two cease-and-desist orders to offenders who demanded *mikajime-ryō*, and could arrest offenders who still refused to comply. Until then, the yakuza had charged bouncer fees to restaurants and bars in their territory, especially those open at night, and made various threats, such as ramming dump trucks into businesses that refused, and business owners, fearing reprisals, had paid *mikajime-ryō*, but the new law resulted in more businesses refusing *mikajime-ryō* and the yakuza's financial resources were lost. In 1991, the yakuza had 63,800 members, but by 1992, when the new law took effect, the number had dropped sharply to about 56,600, then to about 48,000 in 1994 and 43,100 in 2001.[^cite_note-genmika-35][^cite_note-ncrco-87]

Additional regulations can be found in a 2008 anti-yakuza amendment which allows prosecutors to place the blame on any yakuza-related crime on crime bosses. Specifically, the leader of the [Yamaguchi-gumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi-gumi) has since been incarcerated and forced to pay upwards of 85 million yen in damages of several crimes committed by his gangsters, leading to the yakuza's dismissal of around 2,000 members per year; albeit, some analysts claim that these dismissals are part of the yakuza's collective attempt to regain a better reputation amongst the populace. Regardless, the yakuza's culture, too, has shifted towards a more secretive and far less public approach to crime, as many of their traditions have been reduced or erased to avoid being identified as yakuza.[^cite_note-apjjf.org-88]

Beginning in 2009, led by agency chief [Takaharu Ando](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AE%89%E8%97%A4%E9%9A%86%E6%98%A5), Japanese police began to crack down on the gangs. Yamaguchi-gumi's number two and [Kodo-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodo-kai) chief [Kiyoshi Takayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyoshi_Takayama) was arrested in late 2010. In December 2010, police arrested Yamaguchi-gumi's alleged number three leader, [Tadashi Irie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadashi_Irie).[^cite_note-afp260111-89]

### Yakuza exclusion ordinances

In addition to the anti-yakuza laws, the [Yakuza exclusion ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_exclusion_ordinances) enacted by each of Japan's 47 prefectures between 2009 and 2011 also contributed significantly to the decline of the yakuza.[^cite_note-asahi031223-90][^cite_note-nhk300622-36] Ordinances were enacted in Osaka and Tokyo in 2010 and 2011 to try to combat yakuza influence by making it illegal for any business to do business with the yakuza.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] While the anti-yakuza laws prohibited the yakuza from making unreasonable demands on businesses and citizens, these ordinances prohibited businesses and citizens from offering benefits to the yakuza. This made it increasingly difficult for the yakuza to raise funds, as fewer businesses and citizens succumbed to the yakuza's threats and offered benefits to the yakuza, such as contracting work or paying money to the yakuza.[^cite_note-asahi031223-90][^cite_note-nhk300622-36] According to the media, encouraged by tougher anti-yakuza laws and yakuza exclusion ordinances, local governments and construction companies have begun to shun or ban yakuza activities or involvement in their communities or construction projects.[^cite_note-afp260111-89]

In addition, these ordinances have made it difficult for yakuza members to lead normal civilian lives. The ordinances also require businesses and citizens to refuse to rent meeting rooms or parking spaces to the yakuza, or to print business cards with the name of yakuza organizations on them. Companies can now also refuse to open bank accounts, sign mobile phone contracts, credit card contracts, lease real estate, or process various loans for people identified as yakuza under the anti-yakuza laws, making it more difficult for yakuza to live in society.[^cite_note-asahi031223-90][^cite_note-soc250124-93][^cite_note-tok030723-94] Even companies that provide lifelines have become tough on the yakuza, with [Osaka Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Gas) terminating contracts if a customer is discovered to be a yakuza. To prevent yakuza from nominally leaving the organization and signing contracts with companies, these ordinances allow companies to treat a person as a yakuza for five years even if he or she has nominally left the yakuza and become a civilian.[^cite_note-asahi031223-90][^cite_note-soc250124-93][^cite_note-tok030723-94]

Since 2011, regulations outlawing business with yakuza members, government-ordered audits of yakuza finances, and the enactment of [yakuza exclusion ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_exclusion_ordinances) have hastened a decline in yakuza membership. The number of yakuza members and quasi-members fell from 78,600 in 2010 to 25,900 in 2020.[^cite_note-asahi031223-90]

### Outside Japan

Yakuza organizations also face pressure from the US government; in 2011, a federal executive order required financial institutions to freeze yakuza assets, and as of 2013, the [U.S. Treasury Department](53667.md) had frozen about US\$55,000 of yakuza holdings, including two Japan-issued American Express cards.[^cite_note-95]

### Current situation

The number of yakuza members and quasi-members fell by about 70 percent in the 30 years between 1990, before the anti-yakuza law, and 2020, after the anti-yakuza laws and the yakuza exclusion ordinances took effect.[^cite_note-nhk300622-36]

With the addition of the employer liability clause in the 2008 amendment to the Anti-yakuza law, there have been a number of situations in which yakuza bosses have been held liable for crimes committed by members of the yakuza. For example, in a civil case, the [Tokyo High Court](4519211.md) held the head of [Sumiyoshi-kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi-kai) liable for a *[tokushu sagi](#Current_activities)* (特殊詐欺, special fraud) committed by members of Sumiyoshi-kai under the employer liability article of the Anti-yakuza law in 2021. As a result, Sumiyoshi-kai paid 652 million yen to the victims (approximately \$6 million US dollars at the time), 35 million yen more than the amount of damages.[^cite_note-96]

In criminal cases, [Nomura Satoru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoru_Nomura) became the first "designated yakuza" (指定暴力団, *Shitei Bōryokudan*) boss to be [sentenced to death](4967816.md) under the employer liability clause on 24 August 2021. Nomura was involved in one murder and assaults of three people. The presiding judge Adachi Ben of the Fukuoka District Court characterized the murders as extremely vicious attacks.[^cite_note-satoru-97][^cite_note-98] On 12 March 2024, the Fukuoka High Court overturned Nomura's death sentence and downgraded it to life imprisonment. The High Court found him not guilty of murder.[^cite_note-99]

On top of the already staggering anti-yakuza legislation, Japan's younger generation may be less inclined to gang-related activity, as modern society has made it easier, especially for young men, to gain even semi-legitimate jobs such as ownership in bars and massage parlors and pornography that can be more profitable than gang affiliation, all while protecting themselves by abiding by the strict anti-yakuza laws.[^cite_note-apjjf.org-88]

Citizens who take a stronger stance seem to also have taken action that does not lead to violent reactions from the yakuza. In Kyushu, although store owners initially were attacked by gang members, the region has reached stability after local business owners banned known yakuza and posted warnings against yakuza entering their premises.[^cite_note-100]

### Yakuza in society

The yakuza have had mixed relations with [Japanese society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_society). Despite their pariah status, some of their actions may be perceived to have positive effects on society. For example, they stop other criminal organizations from acting in their areas of operation.[^cite_note-101] They have been known to provide relief in times of disaster. These actions have at times painted yakuza in a fairly positive light within Japan. The yakuza also attract membership from traditionally scorned minority groups, such as the [Korean-Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_Japan).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] However, gang wars and the use of violence as a tool have caused their approval to fall with the general public.[^cite_note-104] According to Jake Adelstein, one study found that 1 in 10 adults under the age of 40 believed that the yakuza should be allowed to exist.[^cite_note-adelstein20110318-60]

### Film

Main article: [Yakuza film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_film)



Poster for the 1948 [Akira Kurosawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Kurosawa) film *[Drunken Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drunken_Angel)*, a *[yakuza eiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_eiga)*-genre film

The yakuza have been in media and culture in many different fashions. Creating its own [genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_film) of movies within Japan's film industry, the portrayal of the yakuza mainly manifests in one of two archetypes; they are portrayed as either honorable and respectable men or as criminals who use fear and violence as their means of operation.[^cite_note-105] Movies like *[Battles Without Honor and Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_Without_Honor_and_Humanity_\(film\))* and *[Dead or Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_or_Alive_\(1999_film\))* portray some of the members as violent criminals, with the focus being on the violence, while other movies focus more on the "business" side of the yakuza.

The 1992 film *[Minbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbo)*, a satirical view of yakuza activities, resulted in retaliation against the director, as real-life yakuza gangsters attacked the director [Juzo Itami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juzo_Itami) shortly after the release of the film.[^cite_note-106]

Yakuza films have also been popular in the Western market with films such as the 1975 film *[The Yakuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Yakuza)*, the 1989 films *[Black Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Rain_\(1989_American_film\))* and *[The Punisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Punisher_\(1989_film\))*, the 1995 film *[Johnny Mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Mnemonic_\(film\))*, the 2005 film *[Into the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Sun_\(2005_film\))*, 2013's *[The Wolverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wolverine_\(film\))*, 2018 film *[The Outsider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Outsider_\(2018_film\))*, and *[Snake Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_Eyes_\(2021_film\))* in 2021 as well as the 2025 Telugu feature film *[They Call Him OG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Call_Him_OG) and [Normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_\(2025_film\)).*

### Television

The yakuza feature prominently in the 2015 American dystopian series *[The Man in the High Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_in_the_High_Castle_\(TV_series\))*. They are also the basis for the 2019 BBC TV Series *[Giri/Haji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giri/Haji)*, which features a character whose life is put in danger after he comes under suspicion for a murder tied to the yakuza. The 2022 [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max) series [*Tokyo Vice*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Vice_\(TV_series\)) explores the dealings of the yakuza from the perspective of an American reporter [Jake Adelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Adelstein). The anime series *[Akiba Maid War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiba_Maid_War)* is a [dark comedic parody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy) in which [maid cafés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maid_caf%C3%A9s) and yakuza culture are synonymous with one another.

The video game series *[Like a Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_\(franchise\)),* formerly known as *Yakuza* outside of Japan, launched in 2005, portrays the actions of several different ranking members of the yakuza, as well as criminal associates such as dirty cops and loan sharks. The series addresses some of the same themes as the yakuza genre of film does, like violence, honor, politics of the syndicates, and the social status of the yakuza in Japan. The series has been successful, spawning sequels, spin-offs, [a live-action movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Dragon_\(film\)) and a web TV series.

*[Grand Theft Auto III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_III)* features a yakuza clan that assists the [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_\(Grand_Theft_Auto\)) in the second and third act after they cut their ties with the [Mafia](18293303.md). The yakuza derive most of their income from a casino, Kenji's, and are currently fighting to keep other gangs from peddling drugs in their territory while seeking to protect their activities from police interference. Towards the end of the third act, the player assassinates the leader of the clan, and the other members are later executed by Colombian gangsters. In *Grand Theft Auto III*'s prequel, *[Grand Theft Auto: Liberty City Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto:_Liberty_City_Stories)*, the yakuza play a major role in the storyline. In *[Grand Theft Auto: Vice City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto:_Vice_City)*, the yakuza are mentioned, presumably operating in Vice City.

*[Hitman 2: Silent Assassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman_2:_Silent_Assassin)* features a mission set in Japan that sees [Agent 47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_47) assassinating the son of a wealthy arms dealer during his dinner meeting with a yakuza boss at his private estate. A mission in the 2016 game, *[Hitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman_\(2016_video_game\))*, set at a secluded mountaintop hospital, features a notorious yakuza lawyer and fixer as one of two targets to be assassinated.

He must not reveal his membership in the yakuza, under penalty of being immediately excluded.

While Yashiro would like to take advantage of Dômeki's body, the latter is helpless.
