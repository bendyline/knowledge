The **2026 Washington wildfire season** is an ongoing series of [wildfires](56106.md) in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Washington](13015878.md).

## Background

Further information: [List of Washington wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Washington_wildfires)

See also: [Climate change in Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Washington)

The state legislature appropriated \$120 million for firefighting in 2026, twice the previous year's budget.[^cite_note-1]

As of May 2026, the [National Interagency Fire Center](5818754.md) predicted above average wildfire risk for [Eastern Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington) in June 2026; above average for all of Eastern Washington and most of [Western Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Washington) in July; and above average for the entire state in August. The risk assessments reflected a combination of early-season drying due to low snowpack, which led to drought conditions in the region; the [Washington State Department of Ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Department_of_Ecology) declared statewide drought emergency in early April.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Events

The fire season started on May 7, 2026, with the Libby Creek Fire in the [Okanogan–Wenatchee National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okanogan%E2%80%93Wenatchee_National_Forest). It was suspected to be the result of human activity in the forest,[^cite_note-KitsapSun20260508-4] and was contained in about a day.[^cite_note-5]

On May 27 and May 28, members of the [Washington National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_National_Guard) conducted wildfire training in anticipation of summer mobilizations.[^cite_note-6]

By June 12, the Department of Natural Resources had responded to over 200 wildfires.[^cite_note-7]

The first fatality of the year was due to the [Upriver Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upriver_Fire) just outside Spokane city limits.[^cite_note-Duggan-8]

Another fatality was reported in July due to the [Chelan Hills Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelan_Hills_Fire).[^cite_note-9]

On July 15 and July 16, much of Washington was subject to isolated [dry thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_thunderstorms), leading to the issuance of red flag warnings for a large portion of the state.[^cite_note-10] These dry thunderstorms, further aided by hot and windy conditions, would spark numerous large wildfires across Washington and Oregon.[^cite_note-F13(0716)-11] The [Kaiser Canyon Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Canyon_Fire) in particular would scorch over 50,000 acres in a matter of days, prompting numerous evacuations for the entire population of [Nespelem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nespelem,_Washington).[^cite_note-12]

On August 1, the [Storm Prediction Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Prediction_Center) would issue a rare [Extremely Critical Risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Storm_Prediction_Center_extremely_critical_days) in its Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook, encompassing the majority of Eastern Washington, due to dangerously high winds and low humidity.[^cite_note-13] This would lead to the issuance of the first ever [PDS Red Flag Warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particularly_Dangerous_Situation) in Washington State history.[^cite_note-14] The same day, Governor [Bob Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ferguson_\(governor\)) declared a statewide [fire emergency](298608.md) and outdoor burn ban through September 30.[^cite_note-15]

On August 5, air quality index (AQI) in [Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavenworth,_Washington) was recorded at more than seven times the "past traditional index maximum" of 500. Some transit services were shut down per [Washington Department of Labor and Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Department_of_Labor_and_Industries) safety rules when the AQI value exceeded 849. Most of the smoke was from the [Little Giant Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Giant_Fire) about 30 miles (48 km) away.[^cite_note-16]

On August 7, the governor declared a statewide [public health emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health_emergency_\(United_States\)) due to wildfire smoke.[^cite_note-17]
