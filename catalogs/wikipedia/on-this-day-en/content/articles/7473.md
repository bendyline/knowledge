**Chelsea Football Club** is a professional [association football](10568.md) club based in [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham), West London, England. They compete in the [Premier League](11250.md), the top tier of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), and rank among the [most successful clubs in English football history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_in_England_by_competitive_honours_won). Domestically, the club has secured six top flight league titles, eight [FA Cups](11237.md), five [League Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup), four [FA Community Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield), and two [Full Members' Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Members'_Cup). Internationally, their honours include two [UEFA Champions Leagues](44220.md), two [UEFA Europa Leagues](232175.md), two [UEFA Cup Winners' Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), two [UEFA Super Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup), two [FIFA Club World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup), and one [UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League).

The club was founded in 1905, taking its name from the adjacent area of [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea,_London), and plays its home matches at [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\)). Chelsea secured their first major trophy, the [First Division championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_English_football_champions), in [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954%E2%80%9355_Football_League), and won their first [Premier League](11250.md) title in the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Premier_League). Their first European honour came in the [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final) [Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), followed by a second victory in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final). They subsequently won the [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UEFA_Champions_League_final) in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UEFA_Champions_League_final) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Champions_League_final), and the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_UEFA_Europa_League_final) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Europa_League_final). After winning the [UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Conference_League_final), Chelsea became the first club to win [all four main UEFA competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records_and_statistics#List_of_teams_to_have_won_the_main_European_club_competitions) as well as all UEFA men's competitions. They also won the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final), the latter of which was the first iteration of the tournament with 32 teams.

Nicknamed *the Blues*, the team adopted their distinctive royal blue home strip early in the club's history, moving away from their original light blue shirts. Also associated with the club is their traditional anthem, "[Blue Is the Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Is_the_Colour_\(song\))". Chelsea contest the [West London derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_London_derby) against local rivals [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.), alongside fierce capital fixtures with [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.%E2%80%93Chelsea_F.C._rivalry) and [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C._rivalry). As one of the most successful clubs in the country, they also share a prominent and [long-standing rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Leeds_United_F.C._rivalry) with [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.).

## History

See also: [List of Chelsea F.C. seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chelsea_F.C._seasons)

### Founding and early years (1905–1952)

Main article: [History of Chelsea F.C. (1905–1952)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chelsea_F.C._\(1905%E2%80%931952\))



The first Chelsea team, September 1905

In 1904, English businessman [Gus Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Mears) acquired the [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\)) athletics stadium in [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham) with the aim of turning it into a football ground. An offer to lease it to nearby [Fulham F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) was turned down, so Mears opted to found his own club to use the stadium. As there was already a team named Fulham in the borough, the name of the adjacent borough of [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Chelsea) was chosen for the new club; names like *Kensington FC*, *Stamford Bridge FC* and *London FC* were considered.[^cite_note-1] Chelsea F.C. was founded on 10 March 1905 at The Rising Sun pub (now The Butcher's Hook),[^cite_note-teamhistory-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-capacity-4] opposite the present-day main entrance to the ground on [Fulham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_Road), and were elected to the Football League shortly afterwards.[^cite_note-5]

Chelsea won promotion to the First Division in their second season, and yo-yoed between the First and Second Divisions in its early years. The team reached the [1915 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1915_FA_Cup_final), where they lost to [Sheffield United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.) at Old Trafford, and finished third in the First Division in 1920, the club's best league campaign to that point.[^cite_note-6] Chelsea had a reputation for signing star players[^cite_note-glanville-7] and attracted large crowds. The club had the highest average attendance in English football in ten separate seasons[^cite_note-8] including [1907–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1907%E2%80%9308_in_English_football),[^cite_note-9] [1909–10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1909%E2%80%9310_in_English_football),[^cite_note-10] [1911–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911%E2%80%9312_in_English_football),[^cite_note-11] [1912–13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912%E2%80%9313_in_English_football),[^cite_note-12] [1913–14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913%E2%80%9314_in_English_football)[^cite_note-13] and [1919–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919%E2%80%9320_in_English_football).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Chelsea_InterW-15] They were FA Cup semi-finalists in [1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_1919-20) and [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_1931-32) and remained in the First Division throughout the 1930s, but success eluded the club in the inter-war years.[^cite_note-Chelsea_InterW-15][^cite_note-16]

### Modernisation and the first league championship (1952–1983)

Main article: [History of Chelsea F.C. (1952–1983)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chelsea_F.C._\(1952%E2%80%931983\))



Chart showing the progress of Chelsea's league finishes from 1906 to the present

Former [Arsenal](2174.md) and England centre-forward [Ted Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Drake) was appointed manager in 1952 and proceeded to modernise the club. He removed the club's [Chelsea pensioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_pensioner) crest, improved the youth set-up and training regime, rebuilt the side with shrewd signings from the lower divisions and amateur leagues, and led Chelsea to their first major trophy success – the League championship – in [1954–55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954%E2%80%9355_Chelsea_F.C._season). The following season saw UEFA create the [European Champions' Cup](44220.md), but after objections from [The Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League), Chelsea were persuaded to withdraw from the competition before it started.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Chelsea failed to build on this success, and spent the remainder of the 1950s in mid-table. Drake was dismissed in 1961 and replaced by player-coach [Tommy Docherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Docherty).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Docherty built a new team around the group of talented young players emerging from the club's youth set-up, and Chelsea challenged for honours throughout the 1960s, enduring several near-misses. They were on course for a treble of League, FA Cup and League Cup going into the final stages of the 1964–65 season, winning the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) but faltering late on in the other two.[^cite_note-21] In three seasons the side were beaten in three major semi-finals and were FA Cup runners-up. Under Docherty's successor, [Dave Sexton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sexton), Chelsea won the [FA Cup in 1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FA_Cup_final), beating [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.) 2–1 in a final replay.[^cite_note-Chelsea_Story4-22] The following year, Chelsea took their first European honour,[^cite_note-23] a [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) triumph, with another replayed win, this time over [Real Madrid](26413.md) in Athens.[^cite_note-Chelsea_Story4-22]

### Redevelopment and financial crisis (1983–2003)

Main article: [History of Chelsea F.C. (1983–2003)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chelsea_F.C._\(1983%E2%80%932003\))

The late 1970s through to the '80s was a turbulent period for Chelsea. An ambitious redevelopment of Stamford Bridge threatened the financial stability of the club,[^cite_note-24] star players were sold and the team were relegated. Further problems were caused by a notorious [hooligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooliganism) element among the support, which was to plague the club throughout the decade.[^cite_note-25] In 1982, at the nadir of their fortunes, Chelsea were acquired by [Ken Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Bates) from Mears' great-nephew [Brian Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Mears), for the nominal sum of £1. Bates bought a controlling stake in the club and floated Chelsea on the [AIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Investment_Market) stock exchange in March 1996[^cite_note-Football_Economy-26] although by now the Stamford Bridge freehold had been sold to property developers, meaning the club faced losing their home.[^cite_note-27] On the pitch, the team had fared little better, coming close to relegation to the [Third Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) for the first time, but in 1983 manager [John Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Neal_\(footballer,_born_1932\)) put together an impressive new team for minimal outlay. Chelsea won the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) title in 1983–84 and established themselves in the top division with two top-six finishes, before being relegated again in 1988. The club bounced back immediately by winning the Second Division championship in 1988–89.[^cite_note-28]

After a long-running legal battle, Bates reunited the stadium freehold with the club in 1992 by doing a deal with the banks of the property developers, who had been bankrupted by a market crash.[^cite_note-29] In the mid-1990s Chelsea fan and businessman [Matthew Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Harding) became a director and loaned the club £26 million to build the new North Stand and invest in new players.[^cite_note-The_battle_of_Stamford_Bridge-30] Chelsea's form in the new Premier League was unconvincing, although they did reach the [1994 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FA_Cup_final). The appointment of [Ruud Gullit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_Gullit) as player-manager in 1996 began an upturn in the team's fortunes. He added several top international players to the side and led the club to their first major honour since 1971, the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FA_Cup_final). Gullit was replaced by [Gianluca Vialli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluca_Vialli), whose reign saw Chelsea win the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Football_League_Cup_final), the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final) and the [UEFA Super Cup in 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_UEFA_Super_Cup), and the [FA Cup in 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_FA_Cup_final). They mounted a strong title challenge in 1998–99, finishing four points behind champions Manchester United, and made their first appearance in the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md). Vialli was sacked in favour of [Claudio Ranieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Ranieri), who guided Chelsea to the [2002 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FA_Cup_final) and Champions League qualification in 2002–03.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### Abramovich ownership (2003–2022)

Main article: [History of Chelsea F.C. (2003–2022)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chelsea_F.C._\(2003%E2%80%932022\))



Abramovich at [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\)) during a 4–0 victory over [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.), August 2008

With the club facing an apparent financial crisis,[^cite_note-33] Bates unexpectedly sold Chelsea F.C. in June 2003 for £60 million.[^cite_note-TelegraphBates-34] In so doing, he reportedly recognised a personal profit of £17 million on the club he had bought for £1 in 1982 (his stake had been diluted to just below 30% over the years). The club's new owner was [Russian oligarch](1209554.md) and billionaire [Roman Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich), who took on responsibility for the club's £80 million of debt, quickly paying some of it. Russian businessman [Sergei Pugachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Pugachev) alleged Chelsea was bought on the orders of [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), an allegation Abramovich has denied.[^cite_note-ny1-35] Bates mentioned that Abramovich was in talks to buy Manchester United and Tottenham Hotspur before he bought Chelsea in a deal sealed in a day.[^cite_note-36]



Chelsea players celebrate their first [UEFA Champions League title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UEFA_Champions_League_final) against [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) (2012).

Over £100 million was spent on new players, but Ranieri was unable to deliver any trophies,[^cite_note-37] and was replaced by [José Mourinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mourinho).[^cite_note-38] Under Mourinho, Chelsea became the fifth English team to win back-to-back league championships since the Second World War ([2004–05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Premier_League) and [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_FA_Premier_League)),[^cite_note-39] in addition to winning an FA Cup ([2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FA_Cup_final)) and two League Cups ([2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Football_League_Cup_final) and [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Football_League_Cup_final)). After a poor start to the 2007–08 season, Mourinho was replaced by [Avram Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avram_Grant),[^cite_note-40] who led the club to their first [UEFA Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_UEFA_Champions_League_final), which they lost on penalties to [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.). The club did not turn a profit in the first nine years of Abramovich's ownership, and made record losses of £140m in June 2005.[^cite_note-first_profit-41]

In 2009, under caretaker manager [Guus Hiddink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guus_Hiddink), Chelsea won another [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FA_Cup_final).[^cite_note-42] In [2009–10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_in_English_football), his successor [Carlo Ancelotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti) led them to their first [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Premier_League) and [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FA_Cup_final) [Double](88184.md), becoming the first English top-flight club to score 100 league goals in a season since [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962%E2%80%9363_in_English_football).[^cite_note-Wigan80-43] In 2012, [Roberto Di Matteo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Di_Matteo) led Chelsea to their seventh [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_FA_Cup_final),[^cite_note-44] and their first [UEFA Champions League title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UEFA_Champions_League_final), beating [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) 4–3 on penalties, the first London club to win the trophy.[^cite_note-45] The following year the club won the [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_UEFA_Europa_League_final),[^cite_note-46] making them the first club to hold two major European titles simultaneously and one of [five clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records#List_of_teams_to_have_won_the_three_main_European_club_competitions) to have won the three main UEFA trophies.[^cite_note-47] Mourinho returned as manager in 2013 and led Chelsea to [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Football_League_Cup_final) success in March 2015,[^cite_note-48] and the Premier League title two months later.[^cite_note-49] Mourinho was sacked after four months of the following season after a poor start.[^cite_note-50]

In November 2012, Chelsea announced a profit of £1.4 million for the year ending 30 June 2012, the first time the club had made a profit under Abramovich's ownership.[^cite_note-first_profit-41][^cite_note-Chelsea_FC_announces_annual_profit-51] This was followed by a loss in 2013 and then their highest ever profit of £18.4 million for the year to June 2014.[^cite_note-bbc.com-52] In 2018 Chelsea announced a record after-tax profit of £62 million.[^cite_note-Chelseafc.com-53]

In 2017, under new coach [Antonio Conte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Conte), Chelsea won their sixth English title and the following season won their eighth FA Cup.[^cite_note-54] In 2018 Conte was sacked after a fifth-place finish and replaced with [Maurizio Sarri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Sarri),[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] under whom Chelsea reached the [League Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_EFL_Cup), which they lost on penalties to [Manchester City](165813.md)[^cite_note-57] and won the [Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Europa_League_final) for a second time, beating [Arsenal](2174.md) 4–1 in the final. Sarri then left the club to become manager of [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC) and was replaced by former Chelsea player [Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard).[^cite_note-58]

In Lampard's [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Premier_League), he guided Chelsea to fourth place in the Premier League and reached the FA Cup [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_FA_Cup_final), losing 2–1 to Arsenal.[^cite_note-59] Lampard was dismissed in January 2021 and replaced with [Thomas Tuchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]



Chelsea players celebrating their first [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) title (2021) after beating Brazilian side [Palmeiras](187243.md) in the final

Under Tuchel, Chelsea reached the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FA_Cup_final), losing 1–0 to [Leicester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C.), and won their second UEFA Champions League title with a [1–0 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Champions_League_final) over Manchester City in Porto.[^cite_note-62] The club subsequently won the [2021 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Super_Cup) for the second time by defeating [Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarreal_CF) 6–5 in a penalty shootout, after it had ended 1–1 in Belfast after extra time,[^cite_note-63] and the [2021 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) (the first for the club) in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) after beating Brazilian side [Palmeiras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociedade_Esportiva_Palmeiras) 2–1.[^cite_note-64]

On 18 April 2021, Chelsea announced it would be joining a new [European Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Super_League), a league competition comprising the biggest European clubs.[^cite_note-65] After a backlash from supporters, the club announced their withdrawal days later.[^cite_note-66] The club opted against furloughing their non-matchday staff during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), with the decision reportedly coming from Abramovich himself. Chelsea, one of the first clubs to help the [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service), lent the club-owned Millennium Hotel for the NHS staff.[^cite_note-tsep1-67]

> "\[Chelsea] have been a success machine for the last 10–20 years. That doesn't just come with money, we've seen at Manchester United and Arsenal where they've put billions into the team and not had the success that Chelsea have had. Chelsea can feel comfortable that they'll have rich owners, but will they have football-smart owners? Because that's what Abramovich has been.

— [Gary Neville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Neville) on Abramovich's legacy.

[^cite_note-68]

Amidst financial sanctions leveled at Russian oligarchs by Western governments in response to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Abramovich stated on 26 February that he would hand over the [stewardship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewardship) of Chelsea to the trustees of the Chelsea Foundation.[^cite_note-69] The trustees did not immediately agree, due to legal concerns regarding the rules of the [Charity Commission for England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Commission_for_England_and_Wales).[^cite_note-70] A week later, Abramovich [wrote-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_relief) the £1.5 billion the club owed him, and put the club up for sale, pledging to donate net proceeds from it to the victims of the war in Ukraine.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

On 10 March 2022, the [British government](25318118.md) announced sanctions on Abramovich with Chelsea allowed to operate under a special license until 31 May.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] In the following weeks, reports emerged of Abramovich's involvement in brokering a peace deal between Ukraine and Russia and securing safe evacuation corridors in besieged Ukrainian cities.[^cite_note-abrpea1-75][^cite_note-abrpea2-76] An American government official revealed that the Ukrainian president, [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) had requested that the US government not levy sanctions against Abramovich, given his importance to war relief efforts.[^cite_note-ukrabr-77]

### BlueCo ownership (2022–present)



Chelsea players lifting the inaugural [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) title (2025) under the expanded format after beating French side [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) in the final

Main article: [History of Chelsea F.C. (2022–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chelsea_F.C._\(2022%E2%80%93present\))

On 7 May 2022, Chelsea confirmed that terms had been agreed for a new ownership group, led by [Todd Boehly](35675191.md), [Clearlake Capital](73127593.md), [Mark Walter](35264441.md) and [Hansjörg Wyss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansj%C3%B6rg_Wyss) to acquire the club. The group was later known as [BlueCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlueCo).[^cite_note-ChelseaSale-78][^cite_note-79] The UK government approved the £4.25bn takeover,[^cite_note-80] ending Abramovich's 19-year ownership of the club.[^cite_note-81] [Bruce Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Buck), who served as chairman since 2003, was replaced by Boehly,[^cite_note-82] while long-serving club director and *de facto* [sporting director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_director) [Marina Granovskaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Granovskaia) left,[^cite_note-83] as did [Petr Čech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_%C4%8Cech) from the role of technical and performance advisor.[^cite_note-84]

The club brought in [Graham Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Potter) from [Brighton & Hove Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_&_Hove_Albion_F.C.) to replace Tuchel on 8 September 2022. Chelsea won six of the first 11 games of the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Chelsea_F.C._season), but only five of the remaining 27. Potter was sacked on 2 April 2023 and eventually replaced by Frank Lampard as caretaker manager.[^cite_note-85] Under Lampard the club won only one of their last 11 matches resulting in a 9% win percentage. Lampard's win percentage was the worst for any Chelsea manager who managed three games or more.[^cite_note-86] Chelsea scored a record-low 38 goals across the entire season and finished in the bottom half of the table for the first time since 1995–96.[^cite_note-87]

[Mauricio Pochettino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Pochettino) was announced as Lampard's replacement in 2023.[^cite_note-88] He led Chelsea to a 6th-place finish, and a place in the [Conference League play-off round qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Conference_League_qualifying_phase_and_play-off_round). He also led Chelsea to the [2024 EFL Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_EFL_Cup_final), narrowly losing 1–0 to Liverpool.[^cite_note-89] After clashing with the sporting directors Laurence Stewart and Paul Winstanley over strategy and management of the young squad,[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-JO-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Pochettino agreed to leave the club at the end of the season.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-JS-95]

On 3 June 2024, [Enzo Maresca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Maresca) was announced as Pochettino's replacement.[^cite_note-96] He led Chelsea to win the Conference League after a 4–1 win against [Real Betis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Betis) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Conference_League_final) in [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw),[^cite_note-97] as they became the first team to win all of the European trophies except the European Champion Clubs' Cup, the predecessor of the UEFA Champions League.[^cite_note-98] On 13 July 2025, he also guided Chelsea to victory in the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), the first edition of the expanded competition, when Chelsea secured the trophy with a 3–0 win over [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League) winners [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-99] He left Chelsea on 1 January 2026 by mutual consent.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

On 6 January 2026, [Liam Rosenior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Rosenior) was appointed as the new Chelsea head coach on a six-and-a-half-year deal.[^cite_note-102] He was sacked on 22 April 2026, after losing five consecutive league games; [Calum McFarlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calum_McFarlane) was appointed as interim head coach until the end of the season.[^cite_note-103]

On 17 May 2026, [Xabi Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xabi_Alonso) was appointed as the new Chelsea manager on a four-year contract, starting from 1 July 2026.[^cite_note-104]

On 16 September 2026, Clearlake Capital acquired the ownership interest of Todd Boehly and Mark Walter and therefore took full control of the club. Todd Boehly stepped down as chairman after his four-year tenure.[^cite_note-ChelseaOwnership-105]

## Stadium

Main article: [Stamford Bridge (stadium)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\))



[Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\)), West Stand

Chelsea have only had one home ground, Stamford Bridge, where they have played since the team's foundation. The stadium was officially opened on 28 April 1877 and for the next 28 years it was used by the [London Athletic Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Athletic_Club) as an arena for athletics meetings. In 1904, the ground was acquired by businessman [Gus Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Mears) and his brother [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mears), who had purchased nearby land (formerly a large market garden) with the aim of staging football matches on the now 12.5 acre (51,000 m<sup>2</sup>) site.[^cite_note-stadium-106] Stamford Bridge was designed for the Mears family by the noted football architect [Archibald Leitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Leitch), who had designed [Ibrox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrox_Stadium), [Craven Cottage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craven_Cottage) and [Hampden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Park).[^cite_note-107] Most football clubs were founded first, and then sought grounds in which to play, but Chelsea were founded for Stamford Bridge.[^cite_note-108]

Starting with an open bowl-like design and one grandstand with seating, Stamford Bridge had an original capacity of around 100,000, making it the second biggest stadium in England after [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre).[^cite_note-stadium-106] The early 1930s saw the construction of a terrace on the southern part of the ground with a roof that covered around 20% of the stand. As the roof resembled that of a corrugated iron shed, the stand eventually became known as the "Shed End", although it is unknown who first coined this name. From the 1960s, it became known as the home of Chelsea's most loyal and vocal supporters.[^cite_note-stadium-106] In 1939, another small seated stand was added, the North Stand, which remained until its demolition in 1975.[^cite_note-stadium-106]

In the early 1970s, the club's owners announced a modernisation of Stamford Bridge with plans for a state-of-the-art 50,000 all-seater stadium.[^cite_note-stadium-106] Work began in 1972 but the project was beset with problems and ultimately only the East Stand was completed; the cost brought the club close to bankruptcy. The [freehold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fee_simple) was sold to property developers and the club were under threat of eviction from the stadium.[^cite_note-stadium-106] Following a long legal battle, it was not until the mid-1990s that Chelsea's future at Stamford Bridge was secured and renovation work resumed.[^cite_note-stadium-106] The north, west and southern parts of the ground were converted into all-seater stands and moved closer to the pitch, a process completed by 2001. The East Stand was retained from the 1970s development. In 1996, the north stand was renamed the [Matthew Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Harding) stand, after the club director and benefactor who was killed in a helicopter crash earlier that year.[^cite_note-109]



Chelsea *vs.* [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.) at Stamford Bridge on 23 September 1905; Chelsea won 1–0.

When Stamford Bridge was redeveloped in the [Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Bates) era, many additional features were added to the complex including two [Millennium & Copthorne hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_&_Copthorne_Hotels), apartments, bars, restaurants, the Chelsea Megastore, and an interactive visitor attraction called Chelsea World of Sport. The intention was that these facilities would provide extra revenue to support the football side of the business, but they were less successful than hoped, and before the [Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich) takeover in 2003, the debt taken on to finance them was a major burden on the club. Soon after the takeover, a decision was taken to drop the "Chelsea Village" brand and refocus on Chelsea as a football club. However, the stadium is sometimes still referred to as part of *"Chelsea Village"* or *"The Village"*.

The Stamford Bridge [freehold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freehold_\(law\)), the [pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football_pitch), the turnstiles and Chelsea's [naming rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights) are now owned by [Chelsea Pitch Owners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Pitch_Owners), a non-profit organisation in which fans are the shareholders. The CPO was created to ensure the stadium could never again be sold to developers. As a condition for using the Chelsea FC name, the club has to play its first team matches at Stamford Bridge, which means that if the club moves to a new stadium, they may have to change their name.[^cite_note-110] Chelsea's [training ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobham_Training_Centre) is located in [Cobham, Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobham,_Surrey). Chelsea moved to Cobham in 2004. Their previous training ground in [Harlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlington,_London) was taken over by [QPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C.) in 2005.[^cite_note-111] The new training facilities in Cobham were completed in 2007.[^cite_note-112]



Aerial view of present-day [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\))

Stamford Bridge hosted the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_final) from 1920 to 1922,[^cite_note-113] has held 10 [FA Cup Semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_Semi-finals) (most recently in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_FA_Cup)), ten [FA Charity Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield) matches (the last in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FA_Charity_Shield)), and three [England international](9904.md) matches, the last in 1932; it was the venue for an unofficial *Victory International* in 1946.[^cite_note-114] The [2013 UEFA Women's Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final) was played at Stamford Bridge as well.[^cite_note-115] The stadium has been used for a variety of other sports. In October 1905 it hosted a [rugby union](25405.md) match between the [All Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Original_All_Blacks) and Middlesex,[^cite_note-116] and in 1914 hosted a baseball match between the touring [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants_\(NL\)) and the [Chicago White Sox](5945.md).[^cite_note-117] It was the venue for a [boxing](4243.md) match between world [flyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyweight) champion [Jimmy Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wilde) and Joe Conn in 1918.[^cite_note-118] The running track was used for [dirt track racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirt_track_racing) between 1928 and 1932,[^cite_note-119] [greyhound racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_racing) from 1933 to 1968, and [Midget car racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midget_car_racing) in 1948.[^cite_note-120] In 1980, Stamford Bridge hosted the first international [floodlit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodlights_\(sport\)) cricket match in the UK, between [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_County_Cricket_Club) and the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_cricket_team).[^cite_note-121] It was the home stadium of the [London Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Monarchs) [American football](18951490.md) team for the [1997 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_London_Monarchs_season).[^cite_note-122]

The previous owner Abramovich and the club's then executive board determined that a larger stadium is necessary in order for Chelsea to stay competitive with rival clubs who have significantly larger stadia, such as [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Stadium) and [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford).[^cite_note-123] Owing to its location next to a main road and two railway lines, fans can only enter Stamford Bridge via the [Fulham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_Road), which places constraints on expansion due to [health and safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_safety_and_health) regulations.[^cite_note-124] The club have consistently affirmed their desire to keep Chelsea at their current home,[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] but have nonetheless been linked with a move to various nearby sites, including the [Earls Court Exhibition Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earls_Court_Exhibition_Centre), [Battersea Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battersea_Power_Station) and the [Chelsea Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Barracks).[^cite_note-127] In October 2011, a proposal from the club to buy back the freehold to the land on which Stamford Bridge sits was voted down by Chelsea Pitch Owners shareholders.[^cite_note-128] In May 2012, the club made a formal bid to purchase Battersea Power Station, with a view to developing the site into a new stadium,[^cite_note-129] but lost out to a Malaysian consortium.[^cite_note-130] The club subsequently announced plans to redevelop Stamford Bridge into a 60,000-seater stadium,[^cite_note-131] and in January 2017 these plans were approved by Hammersmith and Fulham council.[^cite_note-132] However, on 31 May 2018, the club released a statement saying that the new stadium project had been put on hold indefinitely, citing "the current unfavourable investment climate".[^cite_note-133]

In July 2022, it was reported that the club's new owner [Todd Boehly](35675191.md) had appointed American architect [Janet Marie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Marie_Smith) to oversee the renovation of the stadium.[^cite_note-134]

## Identity

### Crest

Chelsea has had four main [crests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crest_\(sports\)), which all underwent minor variations. The first, adopted when the club was founded, was the image of a [Chelsea Pensioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Pensioner), the army veterans who reside at the nearby [Royal Hospital Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Hospital_Chelsea). This contributed to the club's original "pensioner" nickname, and remained for the next half-century, though it never appeared on the shirts. When [Ted Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Drake) became Chelsea manager in 1952, he began to modernise the club. Believing the Chelsea pensioner crest to be old-fashioned, he insisted that it be replaced.[^cite_note-crest-135] A stop-gap badge which comprised the initials C.F.C. was adopted for a year. In 1953, the club crest was changed to an upright blue lion looking backwards and holding a [staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosier). It was based on elements in the [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of the [Metropolitan Borough of Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Chelsea)[^cite_note-136] with the "lion rampant regardant" taken from the arms of then club president [Viscount Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cadogan,_8th_Earl_Cadogan) and the staff from the [Abbots of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey), former Lords of the Manor of Chelsea. It featured three red roses, to represent England, and two footballs.[^cite_note-crest-135] This was the first Chelsea crest to appear on the shirts, in the early 1960s. In 1975, a [heraldic badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_badge) was granted by the [College of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Arms) to the [English Football League](14419588.md) for use by Chelsea. The badge took the form of the familiar lion and staff encircled by a blue ring but without lettering and without the red roses and red footballs ([blazoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blazon) as "*A lion rampant reguardant azure supporting with the forepaws a [crozier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crozier) or all within an annulet azure*").[^cite_note-137] In 1986, with [Ken Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Bates) owner of the club, Chelsea's crest was changed again as part of another attempt to modernise and because the old rampant lion badge could not be trademarked.[^cite_note-138] The new badge featured a more naturalistic non-heraldic lion, in white and not blue, standing over the C.F.C. initials. This lasted for the next 19 years, with some modifications such as the use of different colours, including red from 1987 to 1995, and yellow from 1995 until 1999, before the white returned.[^cite_note-HFK-139] With the new ownership of [Roman Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich), and the club's centenary approaching, combined with demands from fans for the popular 1950s badge to be restored, it was decided that the crest should be changed again in 2005. The new crest was officially adopted for the start of the [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_in_English_football) season and marked a return to the older design, used from 1953 to 1986, featuring a blue heraldic lion holding a staff. For the centenary season this was accompanied by the words '100 Years' and 'Centenary 2005–2006' on the top and bottom of the crest respectively.[^cite_note-new_crest-140]

### Colours















Chelsea's first home colours (1905 – c. 1912)

Chelsea have always worn blue shirts, although they originally used the paler [eton blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_blue), which was taken from the racing colours of then club president, [Earl Cadogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Cadogan), and was worn with white shorts and dark blue or black socks.[^cite_note-HFK-139] The light blue shirts were replaced by a royal blue version in around 1912.[^cite_note-141] In the 1960s Chelsea manager [Tommy Docherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Docherty) changed the kit again, switching to blue shorts (which have remained ever since) and white socks, believing it made the club's colours more modern and distinctive, since no other major side used that combination; this kit was first worn during the 1964–65 season.[^cite_note-142] Since then Chelsea have always worn white socks with their home kit apart from a short spell from 1985 to 1992, when blue socks were reintroduced.[^cite_note-143]

Chelsea's away colours are usually all yellow or all white with blue trim. More recently, the club have had a number of black or dark blue away kits which alternate every year.[^cite_note-change_kits-144] As with most teams, they have had some more unusual ones. At Docherty's behest, in the 1966 FA Cup semi-final they wore blue and black stripes, based on [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan)'s kit.[^cite_note-145] In the mid-1970s, the away strip was a red, white and green kit inspired by the [Hungarian national side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Team) of the 1950s.[^cite_note-146] Other away kits include an all jade strip worn from 1986 to 1989, red and white diamonds from 1990 to 1992, graphite and tangerine from 1994 to 1996, and luminous yellow from 2007 to 2008.[^cite_note-change_kits-144] The graphite and tangerine strip has appeared in lists of the worst football kits ever.[^cite_note-147]

### Songs and fan chants

The song "[Blue is the Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Is_the_Colour_\(song\))" was released as a single in the build-up to the [1972 League Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Football_League_Cup_final), with all members of Chelsea's first team squad singing; it reached number five in the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart).[^cite_note-148] The song has since been adopted by a number of other sports teams around the world, including the [Vancouver Whitecaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_\(1986%E2%80%932010\)) (as "White is the Colour")[^cite_note-149] and the [Saskatchewan Roughriders](254166.md) (as "Green is the Colour").[^cite_note-150]

Chelsea released the song "[No One Can Stop Us Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_One_Can_Stop_Us_Now)" in 1994 for reaching the [1994 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FA_Cup_final). It reached number 23 in the UK Singles Chart.[^cite_note-Roberts_2005_102-151] In the build-up to the [1997 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FA_Cup_final), the song "[Blue Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Day_\(Suggs_song\))", performed by [Suggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suggs_\(singer\)) and members of the Chelsea squad, reached number 22 in the UK chart.[^cite_note-152] In 2000, Chelsea released the song "[Blue Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Tomorrow)". It reached number 22 in the UK Singles Chart.[^cite_note-Roberts_2005_102-151]

At matches, Chelsea fans sing [chants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_chants) such as "[Carefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carefree_\(chant\))" (to the tune of "[Lord of the Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_of_the_Dance_\(hymn\))", whose lyrics were probably written by supporter [Mick Greenaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Greenaway)),[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] "Ten Men Went to Mow", "We All Follow the Chelsea" (to the tune of "[Land of Hope and Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Hope_and_Glory)"), "Zigga Zagga", and the celebratory "Celery". The latter is often accompanied by fans throwing celery at each other, although the vegetable was banned inside Stamford Bridge after an incident involving midfielder [Cesc Fàbregas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas) at the [2007 League Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Football_League_Cup_final).[^cite_note-155] Popular fan chants include, "Super Chelsea", "Super Frank" (dedicated to all-time leading goal scorer [Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard)), "We love you Chelsea" and "Come on Chelsea". There are situation-specific or team-specific chants meant to rile up opposition teams, managers or players.[^cite_note-156]

## Support



Chelsea fans at a match against [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.), on 11 March 2006

Chelsea is among the most widely supported football clubs in the world.[^cite_note-157] It has the sixth-highest average attendance in the history of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_England),[^cite_note-All-time_attendance-158] and regularly attract over 40,000 fans to Stamford Bridge; they were the ninth best-supported [Premier League](11250.md) team in the 2023–24 season, with an average gate of 39,700.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-capacity2024-5-160]

Chelsea's traditional fanbase comes from all over the [Greater London](45367389.md) area, including working-class parts such as [Hammersmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammersmith) and [Battersea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battersea), wealthier areas like Chelsea and [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington), and from the [home counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_counties). There are numerous official supporters clubs in the United Kingdom and all over the world.[^cite_note-161] Between 2007 and 2012, Chelsea were ranked fourth worldwide in annual replica kit sales, with an average of 910,000.[^cite_note-162] As of 2023, Chelsea has 118.9 million followers on social media, the fourth highest among football clubs.[^cite_note-163]

During the 1970s and 1980s, Chelsea supporters were associated with [football hooliganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)_hooliganism). The club's "[football firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_hooliganism)", originally known as the Chelsea Shed Boys, and subsequently as the [Chelsea Headhunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Headhunters), were nationally notorious for football violence, alongside hooligan firms from other clubs such as [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.)'s [Inter City Firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_City_Firm) and [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.)'s [Bushwackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_Bushwackers), before, during and after matches.[^cite_note-164] The increase of hooligan incidents in the 1980s led chairman Ken Bates to propose erecting an [electric fence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_fence) to deter them from invading the pitch, a proposal that the [Greater London Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_London_Council) rejected.[^cite_note-165]

Since the 1990s, there has been a marked decline in crowd trouble at matches, as a result of stricter policing, [CCTV](87179.md) in grounds and the advent of [all-seater stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-seater_stadium).[^cite_note-166] In 2007, the club launched the [Back to the Shed campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_the_Shed_campaign) to improve the atmosphere at home matches, with notable success. According to [Home Office](225695.md) statistics, 126 Chelsea fans were arrested for football-related offences during the [2009–10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_in_English_football) season, the third highest in the division, and 27 [banning orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banning_order) were issued, the fifth-highest in the division.[^cite_note-167]

Chelsea fans share friendly relations with Scottish club [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangers_F.C.) and Northern Irish club [Linfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linfield_F.C.) fans, which are collectively known as the "Blue Brothers".[^cite_note-168] In addition, since the mid-1970s, Chelsea fans have developed quite friendly relations with fans of the Italian club [Hellas Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellas_Verona_FC).[^cite_note-169]

### Rivalries

Main articles: [Arsenal F.C.–Chelsea F.C. rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.%E2%80%93Chelsea_F.C._rivalry), [Chelsea F.C.–Liverpool F.C. rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Liverpool_F.C._rivalry), [Chelsea F.C.–Tottenham Hotspur F.C. rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C._rivalry), [West London derbies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_London_derbies), and [Chelsea F.C.–Leeds United F.C. rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Leeds_United_F.C._rivalry)

Chelsea have long-standing rivalries with North London clubs [Arsenal](2174.md) and [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.).[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] A [strong rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Leeds_United_F.C._rivalry) with [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_A.F.C.) dates back to several heated and controversial matches in the 1960s and 1970s, particularly the [1970 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FA_Cup_final).[^cite_note-172] More recently a [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.%E2%80%93Liverpool_F.C._rivalry) with [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) has grown following repeated clashes in cup competitions.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] Fellow [West London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_London_derby) clubs [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.), [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) and [Queens Park Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C.) (QPR) are considered rivals, but less so in recent times, as matches have only taken place intermittently due to the teams often being in separate divisions.[^cite_note-175]

A 2004 survey by Planetfootball.com found that Chelsea fans consider their main rivalries to be with (in descending order): Arsenal, Tottenham Hotspur and [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.). In the same survey, fans of Arsenal, Fulham, Leeds United, QPR, Tottenham, and West Ham United named Chelsea as one of their three main rivals.[^cite_note-176] A 2012 survey, conducted among 1,200 supporters of the top four league divisions across the country, found that many clubs' main rivals had changed since 2003 and reported that Chelsea fans consider Tottenham to be their main rivals, above Arsenal and Manchester United. Additionally, fans of Arsenal, Brentford, Fulham, Liverpool, Manchester United, QPR, Tottenham and West Ham identified Chelsea as one of their top three rivals.[^cite_note-177]

## Records and statistics

Further information: [List of Chelsea F.C. records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chelsea_F.C._records_and_statistics)



[Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard) is Chelsea's all-time highest goalscorer.

Chelsea's highest appearance-maker is ex-captain [Ron Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Harris_\(English_footballer\)), who played in 795 competitive games for the club between 1961 and 1980.[^cite_note-stats-178] Five other players made more than 500 appearances for the club: [Peter Bonetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bonetti) (729; 1959–79), [John Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Terry) (717; 1998–2017), [Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard) (648; 2001–2014), [John Hollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hollins) (592; 1963–1975 and 1983–1984), and [César Azpilicueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Azpilicueta) (508; 2012–2023).[^cite_note-179] With 103 [caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_\(sport\)) (101 while at the club) for England, Lampard is Chelsea's most capped international player. Every starting player in Chelsea's 57 games of the 2013–14 season was a full international – a new club record.[^cite_note-180]

Lampard is Chelsea's all-time top goalscorer, having scored 211 goals in 648 games (2001–2014);[^cite_note-stats-178] he passed [Bobby Tambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Tambling)'s longstanding record of 202 in May 2013.[^cite_note-181] Eight other players have scored over 100 goals for Chelsea: [George Hilsdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hilsdon) (1906–1912), [George Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mills_\(footballer\)) (1929–1939), [Roy Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Bentley) (1948–1956), [Jimmy Greaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Greaves) (1957–1961), [Peter Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Osgood) (1964–1974 and 1978–1979), [Kerry Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Dixon) (1983–1992), [Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba) (2004–2012 and 2014–2015), and [Eden Hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Hazard) (2012–2019). Greaves holds the club record for the most goals scored in one season (43 in 1960–61).[^cite_note-182] While a Chelsea player, Greaves became the youngest ever player to score 100 goals in the English top-flight, at 20 years and 290 days.[^cite_note-183]

Chelsea's biggest winning scoreline in a competitive match is 13–0, achieved against [Jeunesse Hautcharage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeunesse_Hautcharage) in the [Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) in 1971.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] The club's biggest top-flight win was an 8–0 victory against [Wigan Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigan_Athletic_F.C.) in 2010, which was matched in 2012 against [Aston Villa](42173.md).[^cite_note-186] Chelsea's biggest loss was an 8–1 reverse against [Wolverhampton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.) in 1953.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] The club's 21–0 [aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregate_score) victory over Jeunesse Hautcharage in the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup in 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup) is a record in European competition.[^cite_note-189] Officially, Chelsea's highest home attendance is 82,905 for a [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) match against Arsenal on 12 October 1935. However, an estimated crowd of over 100,000 attended a [friendly match](977090.md) against Soviet team [Dynamo Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Moscow) on 13 November 1945.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]



In January 2011 Chelsea broke the British transfer record to sign [Fernando Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Torres) for £50 million; the record stood until 2014

From 20 March 2004 to 26 October 2008, Chelsea went a record 86 consecutive league matches at home without defeat, beating the previous record of 63 matches unbeaten set by Liverpool between 1978 and 1980.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] Chelsea hold the English record for the fewest goals conceded during a league season (15), the highest number of clean sheets overall in a Premier League season (25) (both set during the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Premier_League)),[^cite_note-records-194] and the most consecutive clean sheets from the start of a league season (6, set during the [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_FA_Premier_League)).[^cite_note-195] Chelsea is the only Premier League side to have won its opening nine league games of the season, doing so in 2005–06.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] From 2009 to 2013, Chelsea were unbeaten in a record 29 consecutive [FA Cup](11237.md) matches (excluding penalty shoot-outs).[^cite_note-198]

### Firsts

On 25 August 1928, Chelsea, along with Arsenal, became the first club to play with shirt numbers, in their match against [Swansea Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.).[^cite_note-199]

They were the first English side to travel by aeroplane to a domestic away match, when they visited [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) on 19 April 1957,[^cite_note-200] and the first First Division side to play a match on a Sunday, when they faced [Stoke City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C.) on 27 January 1974. On 26 December 1999, Chelsea became the first British side to field an entirely foreign [starting line-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_line-up) (no British or Irish players) in a Premier League match against [Southampton](81576.md).[^cite_note-201]

In May 2007, Chelsea were the first team to win the FA Cup at the new [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md), having been the last to win it at the old Wembley.[^cite_note-202] They were the first English club to be ranked No. 1 under [UEFA's five-year coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient) system in the 21st century.[^cite_note-203] They were the first Premier League team, and the first team in the English top flight since 1962–63, to score at least 100 goals in a single season, reaching the milestone during the 2009–10 season.[^cite_note-Wigan80-43] Chelsea is the only London club to have won the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md), triumphing in the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League).[^cite_note-Chelsea_win_breaks_London_duck-204][^cite_note-Chelsea_etch_new_name_on_trophy-205] Upon winning the [2012–13 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_UEFA_Europa_League), Chelsea became the first English club to win then-all three UEFA club trophies and the only club to hold the Champions League and the Europa League at the same time.[^cite_note-206]

In 2025, Chelsea became the first club to have [won all four UEFA main club competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records_and_statistics#List_of_teams_to_have_won_the_main_European_club_competitions); the [European Cup/UEFA Champions League](44220.md), the [European/UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), the [UEFA Cup/UEFA Europa League](232175.md), and the [UEFA Europa Conference League/UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League).[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208] They are also the first and as of 2025 only club to have won all three pre-1999 main UEFA club competitions except the European Champion Clubs' Cup more than once each, having won the Cup Winners' Cup in [1970–71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup) and [1997–98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), the Europa League in [2012–13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_UEFA_Europa_League) and [2018–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Europa_League), and the Champions League in [2011–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League) and [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Champions_League). Chelsea has also won the [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup) twice, in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_UEFA_Super_Cup) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Super_Cup), as well as the [UEFA Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Youth_League) (in [2014–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_UEFA_Youth_League) and [2015–16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_UEFA_Youth_League), the first and as of 2025 only club to have retained the title). Chelsea is also the only London club to have won the Champions League[^cite_note-209] and the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup).[^cite_note-210]

Chelsea have broken the record for the [highest transfer fee paid by a British club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progression_of_British_football_transfer_fee_record) three times. Their £30.8 million purchase of [Andriy Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Shevchenko) from [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) in June 2006 was a British record until surpassed by the £32.5 million paid by Manchester City for [Robinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinho) in September 2008.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212] The club's £50 million purchase of [Fernando Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Torres) from Liverpool in January 2011[^cite_note-213] held the record until [Ángel Di María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Di_Mar%C3%ADa) signed for Manchester United in August 2014 for £59.7 million.[^cite_note-214] The club's £71 million purchase of [Kepa Arrizabalaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepa_Arrizabalaga) in August 2018 remains a [world record fee paid for a goalkeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_association_football_transfers).[^cite_note-215]

In 2023, Chelsea broke the spending record in the winter transfer window with a £289 million spending spree on eight new signings, with the £106.8 million signing of [Enzo Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fernandez) from [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica) breaking the British transfer record.[^cite_note-216]

On 21 July 2026, Chelsea signed [Morgan Rogers](59876948.md) from [Aston Villa](42173.md) for £117 million, breaking the club's own record and record for an English footballer.[^cite_note-217]

## Ownership and finances

### Early years

Chelsea Football Club was founded by [Gus Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Mears) in 1905. After his death in 1912, his descendants continued to own the club until 1982, when [Ken Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Bates) bought the club from Mears' great-nephew [Brian Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Mears) for £1. Bates bought a controlling stake in the club and floated Chelsea on the [AIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Investment_Market) stock exchange in March 1996.[^cite_note-Football_Economy-26] In the mid-1990s Chelsea fan and businessman [Matthew Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Harding) became a director, and loaned the club £26 million to build the new North Stand and invest in new players.[^cite_note-The_battle_of_Stamford_Bridge-30]

### Abramovich era

In July 2003, [Roman Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich) purchased just over 50% of Chelsea Village plc's share capital, including Bates' 29.5% stake, for £30 million and over the following weeks bought out most of the remaining 12,000 shareholders at 35 pence per share, completing a £140 million takeover. Other shareholders at the time of the takeover included the [Matthew Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Harding) estate (21%), [BSkyB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSkyB) (9.9%) and various anonymous offshore trusts.[^cite_note-TelegraphBates-34]

At the time of the Abramovich takeover, the club had debts of around £100 million, which included a 10-year £75 million [Eurobond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobond_\(external_bond\)) taken out in 1997 by the Bates regime to buy the freehold of Stamford Bridge and finance the redevelopment of the stadium. The 9% interest on the loan cost the club around £7 million a year and according to [Bruce Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Buck), Chelsea were struggling to pay an instalment due in July 2003.[^cite_note-218] Abramovich paid off some of that debt immediately, but the outstanding £36 million on the Eurobond was not fully repaid until 2008.[^cite_note-219] Since then, the club had no external debt.[^cite_note-220]

Abramovich changed the ownership name to Chelsea FC plc, whose ultimate parent company was Fordstam Limited, which was controlled by him.[^cite_note-221] Chelsea were additionally funded by Abramovich via interest free [soft loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_loan) channelled through his holding company Fordstam Limited. The loans stood at £709 million in December 2009, when they were all converted to [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(finance\)) by Abramovich, leaving the club themselves debt free,[^cite_note-222] although the debt remained with Fordstam.[^cite_note-223]

Chelsea did not turn a profit in the first nine years of Abramovich's ownership, and made record losses of £140m in June 2005.[^cite_note-first_profit-41] In November 2012, Chelsea announced a profit of £1.4 million for the year ending 30 June 2012, the first time the club had made a profit under Abramovich's ownership.[^cite_note-first_profit-41][^cite_note-Chelsea_FC_announces_annual_profit-51] This was followed by a loss in 2013 and then their highest ever profit of £18.4 million for the year to June 2014.[^cite_note-bbc.com-52] In 2018, Chelsea announced a record after-tax profit of £62 million.[^cite_note-Chelseafc.com-53]

Chelsea has been described as a global brand; a 2012 report by Brand Finance ranked Chelsea fifth among football brands and valued the club's brand value at US\$398 million – an increase of 27% from the previous year, valuing it at US\$10 million more than the sixth best brand, London rivals Arsenal – and gave the brand a strength rating of AA (very strong).[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] In 2016, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine ranked Chelsea the seventh most valuable football club in the world, at £1.15 billion (\$1.66 billion).[^cite_note-Forbes_Valuation-226] As of 2016, Chelsea was ranked eighth in the [Deloitte Football Money League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte_Football_Money_League) with an annual commercial revenue of £322.59 million.[^cite_note-227]

### Clearlake era

On 26 February 2022, during the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), Abramovich handed over "stewardship and care" of Chelsea to the Chelsea Charitable Foundation.[^cite_note-228] Abramovich released an official statement on 2 March 2022 confirming that he was selling the club due to the ongoing situation in Ukraine.[^cite_note-229] Although the UK government froze Abramovich's assets in United Kingdom on 10 March due to his "close ties with Kremlin", it was made clear that the Chelsea club would be allowed to operate in terms of activities which are football-related.[^cite_note-230] On 12 March 2022, the Premier League disqualified Abramovich as a director of Chelsea.[^cite_note-231]

On 19 March 2022, there were five confirmed bids to acquire Chelsea; submitted to Raine Capital which was handling the sale of the club. Some of these were a consortium led by ex-Liverpool chairman Sir [Martin Broughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Broughton), a group of investors led by the Ricketts family (among them [Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Ricketts) and [Pete Ricketts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Ricketts)), Swiss and American businessmen [Hansjörg Wyss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansj%C3%B6rg_Wyss) and [Todd Boehly](35675191.md) as well as Aethel Partners headed by Portuguese Ricardo Santos Silva and British businessman [Nick Candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Candy), supported by former Chelsea striker [Gianluca Vialli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluca_Vialli).[^cite_note-232] On 7 May, the club finally confirmed that "terms have been agreed" for a new ownership group led by Todd Boehly and Clearlake Capital.[^cite_note-ChelseaSale-78] On 30 May, it was confirmed that the Boehly consortium had completed the purchase of the club.[^cite_note-233] The consortium includes Wyss and [Mark Walter](35264441.md). Walter and Boehly are also owners of the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md), the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and the [Los Angeles Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Sparks). The consortium was later known as [BlueCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlueCo). The transaction had received all necessary approvals from the governments of the United Kingdom and, the Premier League, and other authorities.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235] As of May 2022, Chelsea was ranked the eighth-most valuable club in the world according to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*,[^cite_note-236] and eighth according to Deloitte, with an annual commercial revenue of €493.1 million.[^cite_note-237]

As of 2025, Chelsea is the [tenth-most-valuable football club in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_most_valuable_football_clubs), worth \$3.25 billion, and earning \$592M in revenue.[^cite_note-forbes2024-238] As of 2024, it was the [tenth-highest-earning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte_Football_Money_League) football club in the world.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240]

In February 2026, Chelsea recorded an English record loss of £355 million for the 2024–25 season.[^cite_note-241]

On 16 September 2026, Chelsea announced that Clearlake Capital acquired the ownership interest of Todd Boehly. Hansjörg Wyss remained an important stakeholder and partner in the ownership group. As part of the transition, Clearlake also acquired Mark Walter’s ownership interest and therefore acquire full control of the Club. Todd Bohely stepped down as Chairman after his four-year tenure.[^cite_note-ChelseaOwnership-105]

### Sponsorship

Chelsea's kit has been manufactured by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) since July 2017. Previously, the kit was manufactured by [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas), which was originally contracted to supply the club's kit from 2006 to 2018. The partnership was extended in October 2010 in a deal worth £160 million over eight years.[^cite_note-242] This deal was again extended in June 2013 in a deal worth £300 million over another 10 years.[^cite_note-243] In May 2016, Adidas announced that by mutual agreement, the kit sponsorship would end six years early on 30 June 2017.[^cite_note-adidasshirt-244] Chelsea had to pay £40m in compensation to Adidas. In October 2016, Nike was announced as the new kit sponsor, in a deal worth £900m over 15 years, until 2032.[^cite_note-245] Previously, the kit was manufactured by [Umbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbro) (1975–81),[^cite_note-Umbro_LeCoq-246] [Le Coq Sportif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Coq_Sportif) (1981–86),[^cite_note-Umbro_LeCoq-246] The Chelsea Collection (1986–87),[^cite_note-247] Umbro (1995–2006),[^cite_note-248] and Adidas (2006–2017).[^cite_note-adidasshirt-244]

Chelsea's first shirt sponsor was [Gulf Air](377403.md), agreed during the 1983–84 season. The club was then sponsored by Grange Farms, [Bai Lin Tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Foster) and [Simod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simod) before a long-term deal was signed with [Commodore International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_International) in 1989; [Amiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiga), an offshoot of Commodore, appeared on the shirts. Chelsea was subsequently sponsored by [Coors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coors_Brewing_Company) beer (1994–97), [Autoglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoglass) (1997–2001), [Emirates](142715.md) (2001–05), [Samsung Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Mobile) (2005–08), [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Group) (2008–15)[^cite_note-yokohama-249][^cite_note-250] and [Yokohama Tyres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama_Rubber_Company) (2015–20). From July 2020, Chelsea's sponsor was [Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_\(telecommunications\));[^cite_note-251] however, it temporarily suspended its sponsorship in March 2022 in response to sanctions leveled by the UK government against owner [Roman Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich).[^cite_note-252] It restored its sponsorship after the change of ownership of the club.[^cite_note-253] The club's [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Chelsea_F.C._season) shirt sponsor was American sports technology firm [Infinite Athlete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Athlete), after starting the season without a primary shirt sponsor.[^cite_note-254] The deal with Infinite Athlete lasted only one season, and Chelsea started the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Chelsea_F.C._season) again without a primary shirt sponsor. The club signed an agreement with Emirati property development company [DAMAC Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAMAC_Properties) in April 2025 to become the team's shirt sponsor for the balance of the 2024–25 season.[^cite_note-255] Chelsea started the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Chelsea_F.C._season) without a primary shirt sponsor, the third consecutive season that had started without one for the club,[^cite_note-256] and remained without a front-of-shirt sponsor until February 2026, when the club and artificial intelligence company [IFS AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFS_AB) announced an agreement for the remainder of the season.[^cite_note-257] The club began the [2026–27 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_Chelsea_F.C._season) without a primary shirt sponsor for the fourth consecutive season,[^cite_note-258] although Chelsea announced on 28 August a partnership with [Circle Internet Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Internet_Group), which includes a front-of-shirt sponsorship.[^cite_note-259]

Following the introduction of sleeve sponsors in the Premier League, Chelsea had [Alliance Tyres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Tire_Company) as its first sleeve sponsor in the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Chelsea_F.C._season),[^cite_note-260] followed by [Hyundai Motor Company](42257.md) in [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Chelsea_F.C._season).[^cite_note-261] In [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Chelsea_F.C._season), Amber Group became the new sleeve sponsor, with the flagship digital asset platform WhaleFin appearing on the sleeves of both men's and women's teams.[^cite_note-262] WhaleFin was replaced by cryptocurrency exchange BingX for the 2023–24 season.[^cite_note-263] Ticketing platform Fever became the club's sleeve sponsor for the 2024–25 season, before that deal was ended mid-season and replaced with rival ticketing platform [Live Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation_Entertainment).[^cite_note-264] Live Nation was replaced by Vietnamese IT company [FPT Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FPT_Corporation) in November 2025.[^cite_note-265]

The club has a variety of other sponsors and official partners, which include [Cadbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadbury), [EA Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports), [FICO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FICO), [Hilton Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Worldwide), [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_\(company\)), [Levy Restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levy_Restaurants), [MSC Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSC_Cruises), [Oman Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_Air), [Parimatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parimatch), [Rexona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rexona), [Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singha), [The St. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_St._James_\(sports_complex\)), [Trivago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivago) and BingX.[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267]

## Popular culture



Chelsea parade through the streets of Fulham and Chelsea after winning their league and cup double, May 2010

In 1930, Chelsea featured in one of the earliest football films, *[The Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Game_\(1930_film\))*.[^cite_note-268] One-time Chelsea centre forward, [Jack Cock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Cock), who by then was playing for [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.), was the star of the film and several scenes were shot at [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\)), including on the pitch, the boardroom and the [dressing rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressing_room). It included guest appearances by then-Chelsea players [Andrew Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Nesbit_Wilson), [George Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mills_\(footballer\)), and [Sam Millington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Millington).[^cite_note-269] Owing to the notoriety of the [Chelsea Headhunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Headhunters), a [football firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_firm) associated with the club, Chelsea have featured in films about football [hooliganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooliganism), including 2004's *[The Football Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Factory_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-270] Chelsea appeared in the [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) film *[Jhoom Barabar Jhoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhoom_Barabar_Jhoom)*.[^cite_note-271] In April 2011, Montenegrin comedy series *Nijesmo mi od juče* made an episode in which Chelsea played against [Sutjeska Nikšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Sutjeska_Nik%C5%A1i%C4%87) for qualification of the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md).[^cite_note-272]

Up until the 1950s, the club had a long-running association with the [music halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_hall); their underachievement often provided material for comedians such as [George Robey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Robey).[^cite_note-273] It culminated in comedian Norman Long's release of a [comic song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelty_song) in 1933, ironically titled "On the Day That Chelsea Went and Won the Cup", the lyrics of which describe a series of bizarre and improbable occurrences on the hypothetical day when Chelsea finally won a trophy.[^cite_note-glanville-7] In [Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock)'s 1935 film *[The 39 Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_39_Steps_\(1935_film\))*, Mr Memory claims that Chelsea last won the Cup in 63 BC, "in the presence of the [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roman_emperors) [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero)".[^cite_note-274] Scenes in a 1980 episode of *[Minder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minder_\(TV_series\))* were filmed during a real match at Stamford Bridge between Chelsea and [Preston North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston_North_End_F.C.), with Terry McCann ([Dennis Waterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Waterman)) standing on the terraces.[^cite_note-275]

## Honours



[Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba) holding the Champions League trophy after Chelsea's victory in 2012

Main article: [List of Chelsea F.C. records and statistics § Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chelsea_F.C._records_and_statistics#Honours)

Upon winning the [2012–13 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_UEFA_Europa_League), Chelsea became the fourth club in history to have won the "[European Treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records_and_statistics#List_of_teams_to_have_won_the_main_European_club_competitions)" of [European Cup/UEFA Champions League](44220.md), [UEFA Cup/UEFA Europa League](232175.md), and [European Cup Winners' Cup/UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) after [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC), [Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Ajax) and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich). Chelsea is the first English club to have won all three major UEFA trophies of the past, and the first club overall in the present form.[^cite_note-279] After winning the [UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Conference_League_final), Chelsea became the first club to win [all four main UEFA competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records_and_statistics#List_of_teams_to_have_won_the_main_European_club_competitions).[^cite_note-280]

## Chelsea Women

Further information: [Chelsea F.C. Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C._Women)

Chelsea operate a [women's football](191466.md) team, [Chelsea Football Club Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_L.F.C.), formerly known as Chelsea Ladies. They have been affiliated to the men's team since 2004[^cite_note-282] and are part of the club's Community Development programme. They play their home games at [Kingsmeadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsmeadow,_Kingston_upon_Thames), formerly the home ground of the [EFL League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_Two) club [AFC Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Wimbledon). The club were promoted to the [Premier Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Women's_Premier_League_National_Division) for the first time in 2005 as [Southern Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Women's_Premier_League_Southern_Division) champions and won the Surrey County Cup nine times between 2003 and 2013.[^cite_note-283] In 2010, Chelsea Ladies were one of the eight founder members of the [FA Women's Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Women's_Super_League).[^cite_note-284] In 2015, Chelsea Ladies won the [FA Women's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_FA_Women's_Cup) for the first time, beating [Notts County Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notts_County_Ladies_F.C.) at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md),[^cite_note-285] and a month later clinched their first FA WSL title to complete a league and cup double.[^cite_note-286] In 2018, they won a second league and FA Cup double.[^cite_note-287] Two years later, in 2020, they repeated their double success by winning the third league title and the [FA Women's League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Women's_League_Cup) for the first time.[^cite_note-288][^cite_note-289] In the [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Chelsea_F.C._Women_season) season, Chelsea won a domestic treble by winning the league, FA Cup and League Cup.[^cite_note-290] They reached the final of the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) for the first time, losing to [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Femeni) 4–0.[^cite_note-291]

[John Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Terry), former captain of the Chelsea men's team, is the president of Chelsea Women.[^cite_note-292]
