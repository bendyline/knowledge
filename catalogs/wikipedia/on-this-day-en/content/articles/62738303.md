The second [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) of [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) was formed on 13 January 2020, following the latter's election as [prime minister of Spain](203556.md) by the [Congress of Deputies](352254.md) on 7 January and his swearing-in on 8 January, as a result of the [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](272596.md) (PSOE) emerging as the largest parliamentary force at the [November 2019 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2019_general_election).[^cite_note-2] It succeeded the [first Sánchez government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_government_of_Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez) and was the [government of Spain](1306113.md) from 13 January 2020 to 21 November 2023, a total of 1,408 days, or 3 years, 10 months and 8 days.

The cabinet comprised members of the PSOE (including its sister party, the [Socialists' Party of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialists'_Party_of_Catalonia), PSC) and [Unidas Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidas_Podemos)—with the involvement of [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_\(Spanish_political_party\)), [United Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Left_\(Spain\)) (IU), the [Communist Party of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Spain) (PCE) and [Catalonia in Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalunya_en_Com%C3%BA) (CatComú)—as well as [independents](2172378.md) proposed by both parties, to become the first nationwide [coalition government](6036.md) to be formed in Spain since the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] It has been alternatively dubbed as the "progressive coalition" ([Spanish](26825.md): *coalición progresista*), after the name of the political agreement signed by PSOE and Unidas Podemos.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] It was automatically dismissed on 24 July 2023 as a consequence of the [2023 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Spanish_general_election), but remained in acting capacity until the next government was sworn in.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

At 22 [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_government_departments), it was the second largest cabinet in Spain since the [country's transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy), only behind the [third Súarez government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_government_of_Adolfo_Su%C3%A1rez); the first time that a government includes four deputy prime ministers; and the third oldest government to be formed, with a median age of 54.2 upon its formation.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] After the July 2021 [cabinet reshuffle](845103.md), the median age of the government lowered to 50, and the proportion of women at ministerial posts increased to 63.6% (14 out of 22).[^cite_note-11]

## Cabinet changes

Sánchez's second government saw a number of cabinet changes during its tenure:

- On 30 December 2020, it was announced that [Salvador Illa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Illa) would be stepping down as [Minister of Health](33528060.md) in order to run as the [Socialists' Party of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialists'_Party_of_Catalonia) (PSC)'s leading candidate for [President of the Government of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Government_of_Catalonia) in the upcoming [2021 Catalan regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Catalan_regional_election).

  [^cite_note-17]

  &#x20;[Minister of Territorial Policy and Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Territorial_Policy) [Carolina Darias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Darias) was proposed to replace him in his post due to her knowledge and cooperation in Illa's ministry's management of the [COVID-19 pandemic](63217011.md), whereas outgoing PSC candidate [Miquel Iceta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miquel_Iceta) was commented as a likely pick for Darias's former ministry.

  [^cite_note-RTVE301220-18]

  [^cite_note-19]

- After the surprise announcement on 10 March 2021 by [Madrilenian president](608649.md) [Isabel Díaz Ayuso](59693424.md) to expel [Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\)) (Cs) from [her government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_government_of_Isabel_D%C3%ADaz_Ayuso) and call [a snap election in the Community of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Madrilenian_regional_election) for 4 May, as a result of a foiled attempt from both PSOE and Cs to bring down the [Murcian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_government_of_Fernando_L%C3%B3pez_Miras) of [Fernando López Miras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_L%C3%B3pez_Miras), the [second deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Spain) [Pablo Iglesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Iglesias_Turri%C3%B3n) announced that he would be stepping down in shortly from his cabinet posts in order to run as the [Unidas Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidas_Podemos) leading candidate in the regional election.

  [^cite_note-20]

  &#x20;Iglesias pointed to [Labour and Social Economy minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Labour_\(Spain\)) [Yolanda Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolanda_D%C3%ADaz) as his successor in the Unidas Podemos leadership, whereas State Secretary for the 2030 Agenda, [Ione Belarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ione_Belarra), would replace him at the helm of the [Ministry of Social Rights and 2030 Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Social_Rights_and_2030_Agenda).

  [^cite_note-21]

  [^cite_note-22]

  &#x20;Díaz would be appointed as new [third deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Spain) as a way to compensate for her strong labour portfolio, whereas [Nadia Calviño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Calvi%C3%B1o) was to ascend as new second deputy.

  [^cite_note-23]

- On 10 July 2021, Pedro Sánchez unveiled one of the largest cabinet reshuffles in Spanish democracy: seven ministers—[Carmen Calvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Calvo) ([First Deputy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Spain) and [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Presidency_\(Spain\))), [Arancha González Laya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arancha_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Laya) ([Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Spain\))), [Juan Carlos Campo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Campo) ([Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Spain\))), [José Luis Ábalos](31311432.md) ([Transport](19633684.md)), [Isabel Celaá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_Cela%C3%A1) ([Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Spain\))), [José Manuel Rodríguez Uribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Uribes) ([Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Spain\))) and [Pedro Duque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Duque) ([Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science_\(Spain\)))—were replaced by new officeholders, with further two ministries ([Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Spain\)) and [Territorial Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Territorial_Policy)) seeing changes in their structures, with civil service competences being transferred from the latter to the former, as well as the office of the [spokesperson of the Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokesperson_of_the_Government_of_Spain). After Calvo's dismissal from the cabinet, the third remaining deputy prime ministers ([Nadia Calviño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Calvi%C3%B1o), [Yolanda Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolanda_D%C3%ADaz) and [Teresa Ribera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_Ribera)) were promoted, with the post of the [fourth deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Spain) being disestablished.

  [^cite_note-24]

  &#x20;The changes were effective from 12 July, with the new ministers taking their posts on that day.

  [^cite_note-25]

- On 16 December 2021, [Manuel Castells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Castells) announced his will to resign as [Ministry of Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Universities) due to health concerns, being replaced in the post on 20 December by [Joan Subirats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Subirats), former councillor in the [City Council of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Council_of_Barcelona) under [mayoress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Barcelona) [Ada Colau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Colau).

  [^cite_note-26]

- On 14 and 17 November 2022, [Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Industry_\(Spain\)) and [Health](33528060.md) ministers [Reyes Maroto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reyes_Maroto) and [Carolina Darias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Darias) were announced as PSOE candidates for the mayorships of [Madrid](41188263.md) and [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria), respectively.

  [^cite_note-27]

  [^cite_note-28]

  &#x20;They were both replaced in their posts by [Héctor Gómez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_G%C3%B3mez_Hern%C3%A1ndez) and [José Miñones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mi%C3%B1ones) on 28 March 2023, one week ahead of the official call of the [28 May local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Spanish_local_elections).

  [^cite_note-29]

## Council of Ministers
