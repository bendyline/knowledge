The **Curaçao national football team** ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Curaçaos voetbalelftal*; [Papiamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papiamento_language): *selekshon di futbòl Kòrsou*) represents [Curaçao](65205.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). The team is governed by the [Federashon Futbòl Kòrsou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cura%C3%A7ao_Football_Federation).[^cite_note-2]

Following a constitutional change that allowed its predecessor, the [Colony of Curaçao and Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cura%C3%A7ao_and_Dependencies), to become a unified [constituent country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_state) consisting of several island territories as the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles) and its dissolution in 2010, Curaçao has played under a new constitutional status as a separate constituent country since 2011.[^cite_note-3]

Both [FIFA](11049.md) and [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) recognize the Curaçao national team as the direct successor of the dependant [Territory of Curaçao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Cura%C3%A7ao_national_football_team) (1921–1958) and the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles_national_football_team) (1958–2010).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In November 2025, they topped their qualifying group and qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), becoming the smallest nation by both population and area to qualify for the tournament.[^cite_note-7] They faced Germany in their debut match in 2026 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Curaçao became the first non-sovereign national team from the Americas to qualify for the FIFA World Cup, and the seventh overall, after England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland (which are constituent countries of the United Kingdom and not independent countries), the Netherlands (a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands), and the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team), a Dutch colony that competed in the [1938 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup).

## History

Further information: [Territory of Curaçao national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Cura%C3%A7ao_national_football_team) and [Netherlands Antilles national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles_national_football_team)

The first national football team to bear the name Curaçao was the [Territory of Curaçao national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Cura%C3%A7ao_national_football_team), which made its debut in 1924 in an away match against neighboring [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba_national_football_team), a match which the Territory of Curaçao won four to nil.

In December 1954, the territory of Curaçao became the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles), and following a constitutional change the Netherlands Antilles were designated a country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands, which included the islands of [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba), [Bonaire](108737.md), [Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_\(island\)), [Sint Eustatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint_Eustatius) and [Sint Maarten](9529005.md). The name of the Curaçao team changed to [Netherlands Antilles national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles_national_football_team), representing all six islands.

In 1986, Aruba became a country within the Kingdom in its own right, with its own [Aruba national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba_national_football_team) and subsequently Aruban players no longer represented the Netherlands Antilles.

On 10 October 2010, the Netherlands Antilles were dissolved, and Curaçao and [Sint Maarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint_Maarten_national_football_team) became countries in their own right, while Bonaire, Saba and Sint Eustatius became part of the Netherlands proper. Although not a [sovereign state](28791.md), Curaçao (the largest island territory in the Netherlands Antilles) appeared on the FIFA member list in March 2011, as successor of the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles_national_football_team).

As well as taking on the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles)' FIFA membership, [Curaçao](65205.md) was recognised as the direct successor of the former (similarly to how [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team) is regarded the direct successor of [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team), and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) for the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team)), and took on its historical records and FIFA ranking.

They played their first match as the newly formed Curaçao national team on 20 August 2011 against [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_football_team) at the [Estadio Panamericano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Panamericano,_San_Crist%C3%B3bal), with the match ending in a 1–0 loss for Curaçao.

During the [CONCACAF Qualification for the 2018 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)), Curaçao achieved a major feat when they defeated Cuba 1–1 with the [away goals rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule).

### CONCACAF Gold Cup debut

After a strong qualification campaign, Curaçao defeated host [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique_national_football_team) in the semi-finals of the [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Caribbean_Cup) edition of the [Caribbean Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Cup) with the score of 2–1. They met defending champions and six-time winners, [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team). Curaçao won its first ever Caribbean Cup by defeating Jamaica, again with the scoreline of 2–1, which saw Curaçao qualified to its first ever [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). Curaçao was then drawn in Group C in the [2017 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) alongside Jamaica, [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team) and [Mexico](808402.md) but lost all of its group stage matches.

In the next edition of the [2019 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), Curaçao was then drawn in Group C alongside El Salvador, Jamaica and [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team). On 21 June 2019, Curaçao got its first win in the CONCACAF Gold Cup when [Leandro Bacuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Bacuna) scored in a 1–0 win over Honduras. With a much needed win to qualify to the next round, [Juriën Gaari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juri%C3%ABn_Gaari) scored a stoppage time goal against Jamaica in the last group stage fixtures which saw Curaçao finishing as runners-up in the group stage, thus qualifying to the knockout stage. In the quarter-finals, Curaçao then bowed out from the tournament after losing to [United States](582488.md).

### FIFA World Cup debut

In November 2025, during the [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification](73169443.md#Group_B), Curaçao drew 0–0 against Jamaica, making Curaçao the smallest nation by both population and area (only five weeks after [Cape Verde](1205575.md) had broken the area record) ever to qualify for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in history.[^cite_note-10] On 23 February 2026, manager [Dick Advocaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Advocaat) would resign as Curaçao's manager due to personal reasons regarding the health of his daughter.[^cite_note-11] Subsequently, [Fred Rutten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Rutten) was appointed as head coach ahead of the World Cup.[^cite_note-12] On May 11, after his daughter's health improved, Advocaat returned to the national team. This was also due to disagreements between the national team's players and Advocaat's successor [Fred Rutten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Rutten) as well as pressure from the national team's main sponsor, Dutch airline company Corendon.[^cite_note-13] With his return, Advocaat will become the oldest manager in history to coach a team during the FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

Curaçao's first goal at a World Cup was scored by [Livano Comenencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livano_Comenencia) during their opener against [Germany](250204.md), which would eventually defeat the Blue Wave 7–1. In their second match, Curaçao earned their first-ever point at a World Cup in a 0–0 draw with [Ecuador](1016797.md); [Eloy Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eloy_Room) finished one save short of [Tim Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Howard)'s record for most saves in a World Cup match with 15, making him the first Curaçaoan player to be named Man of the Match in a World Cup contest.

## Competitive record

All competitive matches played from 1921 to 1958 were contested as the [Territory of Curaçao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Cura%C3%A7ao_national_football_team) (comprising all six islands of the Netherlands Antilles). From 1958 to 2010 all matches were contested as the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles_national_football_team), successor of the Territory of Curaçao, (still comprising six islands until 1986, when Aruba seceded). All competitive fixtures after 2010 were contested by Curaçao, which solely consists of the island nation itself. Under the newly formed governing body, Curaçao have so far only competed in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)) and [2022 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)), [2012 Caribbean Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Caribbean_Cup_qualification), the [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Caribbean_Cup) and [2017 Caribbean Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Caribbean_Cup), the [2017 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), [2019 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), and the [ABCS Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABCS_Tournament).
