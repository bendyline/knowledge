General elections were held in [Chile](5489.md) on 16 November 2025.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Voters went to the polls to elect the [38th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_Chile) [president of Chile](11885300.md), renew all 155 seats in the [Chamber of Deputies](716259.md), and fill 23 of the 50 seats in the [Senate](683411.md). [Republican Party](61114536.md) candidate [José Antonio Kast](55052190.md) defeated [Communist Party](1579860.md) member [Jeannette Jara](70303364.md) in a runoff election on 14 December.

In the first round, Jara, a member of the [Communist Party of Chile](1579860.md),[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-LAR-20251117-5] won a [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)) of the vote.[^cite_note-nbcnews-6][^cite_note-7] Kast, who has been described as [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative) or [hard-right](221220.md), placed second.[^cite_note-nbcnews-6][^cite_note-8] Combined, right-leaning candidates received approximately 70 percent of the first-round presidential vote.[^cite_note-9] [Johannes Kaiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kaiser_\(Chilean_politician\)) and [Evelyn Matthei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Matthei), both candidates on the right,[^cite_note-LAR-20251117-5] endorsed Kast for the runoff, while [Franco Parisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Parisi) declined to make an endorsement, calling on his voters to decide themselves who to vote for.[^cite_note-bbc1-10] In the runoff, Kast defeated Jara in a [landslide victory](41053306.md) with 58% of the vote, the second-highest percentage of the vote since Chile's transition to democracy. Kast received 7.2 million votes, which was the highest ever vote total in Chile's history. He won in all sixteen [regions of the country](299550.md).[^cite_note-tercera1-11]

In the parliamentary election, [Unidad por Chile](72497176.md), a coalition of the ruling leftist and center-left parties, emerged as the largest bloc of the Chamber of Deputies, while in the Senate the left-wing and right-wing blocs reached a tie with 25 senators each.[^cite_note-infodep-12][^cite_note-uchile-13]

This was the first general election since the reintroduction of [compulsory voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_voting) in 2022.[^cite_note-14]

## Background

Main article: [2021 Chilean general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_general_election)

The [2021 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_general_election) saw left-wing candidate [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md), a former student protest leader during the [2011–2013 student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932013_Chilean_student_protests), defeat conservative rival [José Antonio Kast](55052190.md) in a runoff. Boric's victory was attributed to widespread youth mobilization, dissatisfaction with the center-right administration of [Sebastián Piñera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Pi%C3%B1era), and economic strains following the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Chile).[^cite_note-15] His platform emphasized social equity, feminist reforms, and a "dignified life" for marginalized groups, pledging to dismantle Chile's "patriarchal inheritance".[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

By mid-2023, Boric's approval ratings had plummeted to 28%, with 66% disapproving of his administration, according to polls.[^cite_note-18] Analysts cited economic stagnation, legislative gridlock, and public safety concerns as primary factors. The right-wing [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_\(Chile\)), led by Kast, capitalized on this discontent, securing a dominant victory in the [May 2023 Constitutional Council elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chilean_Constitutional_Council_election), resulting in the failed [2023 Chilean constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chilean_constitutional_referendum). Kast declared the results a mandate for "a major change in government", positioning himself as a frontrunner for 2025.[^cite_note-19] Meanwhile, [Evelyn Matthei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Matthei) of the [Independent Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Democratic_Union) (UDI) also gained traction in opinion polls.[^cite_note-20]

## Electoral system

This was the first presidential and congressional election since a constitutional amendment was passed and promulgated on 27 December 2022, restoring [compulsory voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_voting) for all elections and plebiscites for the first time since 2012, except in primary elections.[^cite_note-21] Eligible citizens who did not vote faced a fine of approximately 33,000 [pesos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_peso) (around US\$35).[^cite_note-22]

### President

The President is elected using the [two-round system](25932.md); if no candidate receives over 50% of the vote to win outright in the first round, a second round will be held.[^cite_note-spo-23]

### National Congress

- Chamber of Deputies: The 155 members are elected from 28 multi-member constituencies (3–8 seats each) using [open-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-list_proportional_representation) with the [d'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method).

  [^cite_note-24]

- Senate: The 50 senators serve staggered eight-year terms. Half the chamber (25 seats) is renewed each general election, elected from 16 regional constituencies (2–5 seats each) under similar proportional rules.

  [^cite_note-25]

### Withdrawn

- [Félix González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Gatica) ([Green Ecologist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Ecologist_Party_\(Chile\))), deputy for District 20 in the [Biobío Region](730685.md), announced his presidential bid on 6 January 2025, saying, "we have decided to collect the signatures to register an ecologist candidacy, because environmental issues are absent from the public agenda." He emphasized urgent social and climate security, adding, "we have the obligation to raise pensions... and face fires, floods, and droughts."

  [^cite_note-61]

  &#x20;González ultimately did not register and did not appear on the November 2025 ballot.

  [^cite_note-Martínez-2025b-26]

- [Vlado Mirosevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlado_Mirosevic) ([Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Chile_\(2013\)), PL), deputy for District 1 in the [Arica and Parinacota Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arica_y_Parinacota_Region), was proclaimed his party's presidential candidate on 12 October 2024 and planned to run in the center-left primaries. But with the Liberal Party lacking national status, it needed 35,361 signatures to validate his candidacy; by 31 March, it had only 9,851 members. Mirosevic withdrew on 16 April 2025 and endorsed Carolina Tohá.

  [^cite_note-González-2025a-62]

  &#x20;He denied the signature shortfall was the reason, arguing that multiple candidates from the PS, PPD, and PL risked handing victory to the Communist Party, and called Tohá the most competitive option.

  [^cite_note-63]

- [Francesca Muñoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesca_Mu%C3%B1oz) ([Christian Social Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Party_\(Chile\)), PSC), deputy for District 20 in the Biobío Region, was proclaimed her party's presidential candidate on 29 April 2025, following the resignation of Senator [Rojo Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rojo_Edwards) and amid a new alliance with the Republican and National Libertarian parties.

  [^cite_note-64]

  &#x20;But on 30 June, she ended her bid and endorsed [José Antonio Kast](55052190.md), calling the moment one that "demands brave, generous, and responsible decisions," and urging her supporters to back his candidacy.

  [^cite_note-65]

- [Ximena Rincón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ximena_Rinc%C3%B3n) ([Demócratas)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_\(Chile\)), senator for the [Maule Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maule_Region), was proclaimed as the party's presidential candidate on 15 March 2025.

  [^cite_note-Emol-2025c-66]

  &#x20;The party indicated it would explore the possibility of joining a primary with other political forces, emphasizing a centrist path "far from the extremes" and aiming for broader parliamentary representation. Rincón framed her prospective campaign around dialogue and moderation, asserting that Chile could "once again be admired and respected around the world."

  [^cite_note-Emol-2025c-66]

  &#x20;However, she ultimately did not register her candidacy with Servel and therefore did not appear on the ballot for the November 2025 election.

  [^cite_note-Martínez-2025b-26]

- Marcelo Trivelli (independent, ex-DC), former intendant of the [Santiago Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Metropolitan_Region), ended his presidential bid on 31 July 2025 after failing to gather the 36,000 signatures required to run as an independent. He acknowledged the lack of both citizen support and backing from the Christian Democratic Party, which instead endorsed Jeannette Jara. Trivelli criticized the DC for "abandoning its historic convictions" and stated he would continue working on initiatives promoting social cohesion.

  [^cite_note-67]

- [Alberto Undurraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Undurraga) ([Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_\(Chile\)), DC), deputy for District 8 and party president, formally withdrew his presidential candidacy on 10 May 2025 during a National Council meeting, following mounting internal criticism and isolation from broader center-left alliances.

  [^cite_note-Fuentes-2025-68]

  [^cite_note-69]

He cited the need to avoid damaging the party and pledged to focus on parliamentary negotiations and promoting the DC's programmatic agenda.

  [^cite_note-Fuentes-2025-68]

- [Paulina Vodanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulina_Vodanovic) ([Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Chile), PS), senator for the Maule Region and PS president, withdrew her presidential candidacy on 28 April 2025, just two weeks after being unanimously proclaimed by the party's central committee.

  [^cite_note-70]

  &#x20;She cited the lack of support from other parties and the need to back a unified candidacy within the center-left, which consolidated around Carolina Tohá. Vodanovic stated that continuing her campaign would have required political conditions that did not materialize and emphasized the importance of unity against the right.

### Declined

- [Michelle Bachelet](986814.md) ([Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Chile)), former [president of Chile](11885300.md) (2006–2010, 2014–2018), announced on 5 March 2025 that she would not run for a third term, stating that "good politics demands renewal." Her decision ended months of speculation within the ruling coalition. In a statement from her foundation Horizonte Ciudadano, she said others in her sector were "valuable and capable", and pledged to support whoever is ultimately chosen to represent the center-left in the November election.

## Campaign

### First round

The campaign for the general election officially began on 17 September 2025 and with eight presidential candidates, the main contest was between Jeannette Jara and José Antonio Kast.[^cite_note-90]

A polarising election, the campaign's main concerns were rising gang violence and migration from [Venezuela](32374.md), rather than traditional economic or social issues.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

Among Kast's campaign pledges were cuts to public spending and the creation of a police force against illegal migration inspired by the [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md).[^cite_note-93] Analysts also expected the results could pave way for more market friendly initiatives as Kast was favored to win.[^cite_note-94]

In general terms, right-wing and far-right parties proposed fiscal austerity measures, promotion of private investment, reduction of the state and tough security policies such as militarization, mass deportations and police defense.[^cite_note-pais-95] Jara, for her part, advocated for greater state intervention, expansion of social benefits such as basic income, regulation of prices for essential services, strengthening gun control, and temporary biometric registration for immigrants.[^cite_note-pais-95]

### Second round

The campaign for the second round began on 17 November.[^cite_note-euronews2-96] Jeannette Jara started it with an event involving women in the [La Pintana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pintana) neighborhood of Santiago while José Antonio Kast started his campaign in the city of [Temuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temuco).[^cite_note-euronews2-96]

Insecurity was once again the key issue of the campaign, with Jara declaring that she would strengthen the police force, lift banking secrecy in order to track down money linked to drug trafficking, and reclaim neighborhoods from criminal gangs.[^cite_note-euronews2-96] For his part, Kast, speaking from Temuco, the capital of a region militarized since 2022 due to violence from criminal gangs, promised to deport migrants and erect a "border shield".[^cite_note-euronews2-96]

The main televised debate between the two candidates took place on the evening of 9 December and was organized by the National Television Association (Anatel), which, according to Anatel, was watched by over seven million people.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Throughout his campaign, Kast used [bulletproof glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletproof_glass) at his rallies, a fact that was criticized by Jara.[^cite_note-99]

## Results

### President

Jara emerged as the leading candidate in the first round with 27% of the vote, followed closely by José Antonio Kast with 24%.[^cite_note-euronews-139] However, no candidate obtained the required majority to win the presidency outright. As a result, the election proceeded to a runoff between Jeannette Jara and José Antonio Kast on 14 December 2025.[^cite_note-140] Third place went to Franco Parisi, who obtained 20% of the vote, a surprising result given he had been polling at under 10%.[^cite_note-bbc1-10] Kaiser received 14% of the vote, and fifth place went to Evelyn Matthei, who received 12% of the vote, a significant defeat as she was expected to come third and had led the polls for several months at one point in the campaign.[^cite_note-euronews-139][^cite_note-141] The other candidates received less than 5% of the votes.[^cite_note-bbc1-10]

In his third attempt to become president of Chile, Kast won the second round with 58% of the vote, marking the most significant shift to the right since the end of the [Pinochet dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_of_Chile).[^cite_note-142] He received the second-highest percentage of the vote since Chile's transition to democracy. Kast received 7.2 million votes, the highest ever vote total in Chile's history. He won all [regions](299550.md) of the country, with Jara only winning the overseas vote.[^cite_note-tercera1-11][^cite_note-143]

## Reactions and aftermath

### First round

Matthei was the first to make a statement after the results were announced, accepting defeat and pledging her support for Kast in the second round.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-debate-146] Kaiser also called for people to vote for Kast in the second round.[^cite_note-debate-146] President Boric congratulated the winners and called for "a debate with high standards."[^cite_note-debate-146]

### Second round

In keeping with tradition, outgoing President [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md) congratulated President-elect Kast on his victory during a televised phone call.[^cite_note-147] Kast thanked him for his congratulations and expressed his desire for a "very orderly and respectful" transition, also hoping to hear Boric's views on the country once he assumed the presidency on 11 March 2026.[^cite_note-148]

In his first speech as president-elect, Kast stated that "it wasn't one person who won here, Chile won," and when some members of the audience booed Jara's name, he demanded respect and silence, adding that "she took on a very difficult challenge and gave it her all in her own style until the end, and that, at least for me, is something I value."[^cite_note-149]

Jara personally congratulated Kast after his victory.[^cite_note-infobae-150] In her following public statement, Jara acknowledged defeat and expressed her support for the president-elect "in everything that is good for Chile."[^cite_note-infobae-150] Kast received a visit from Jara that night at campaign headquarters.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]



President Boric with president-elect José Antonio Kast in Palacio de la Moneda, 15 December 2025

Kast was congratulated by several international leaders, including Argentine President [Javier Milei](56599840.md), Paraguayan President [Santiago Peña](58414156.md), Ecuadorian President [Daniel Noboa](73980059.md), Bolivian President [Rodrigo Paz](69731047.md), Costa Rican President [Rodrigo Chaves](70007255.md), Brazilian President [Lula da Silva](99328.md), Mexican President [Claudia Sheinbaum](57813311.md) as well as U.S. Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md), former Colombian President [Iván Duque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iv%C3%A1n_Duque) and former Ecuadorian President [Guillermo Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Lasso).[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] He was also congratuled by Spanish politician and leader of the [Vox](41928488.md) party [Santiago Abascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Abascal), Spanish Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) and by [Alberto Núñez Feijóo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_Feij%C3%B3o).[^cite_note-155]

Colombian President [Gustavo Petro](13391962.md) criticized Kast's election harshly, stating "Fascism is advancing. I will never shake hands with a Nazi, nor with the son of a Nazi;[^cite_note-156] they are death incarnate. It's sad that [Pinochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Pinochet) had to impose himself by force, but it's even sadder now that people are choosing their own Pinochet."[^cite_note-157] Boric's outgoing government responded to Petro's statements, rejecting them and deciding to file a formal protest.[^cite_note-158]

The Mapuche Political Platform issued a statement stating that the electoral results "not only signify an electoral defeat for the country's progressive and indigenous forces but also a cultural defeat".[^cite_note-159]

The following day, on 15 December, President Boric and Kast held a meeting at the [Palacio de la Moneda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_la_Moneda) to prepare for the transfer of power.[^cite_note-160]

### Parliamentary election

After failing to reach the minimum legal threshold of votes, at least 13 political parties were expected to dissolve, including the longstanding [Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_of_Chile).[^cite_note-161] The order to do so was given by the [Servel](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servel) on 12 February 2026.[^cite_note-162]
