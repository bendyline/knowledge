The **Women's Candidates Tournament 2026** was an eight-player [chess tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_tournament) held to determine the challenger for the [Women's World Chess Championship 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Championship_2027). The tournament took place at the Cap St Georges Hotel and Resort in [Pegeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegeia), Cyprus, between 28 March and 16 April 2026. The event was held alongside the [Candidates Tournament 2026](75933820.md).[^cite_note-fide10nov2025-1][^cite_note-regulations-2]

It was a double [round-robin tournament](939742.md). [Vaishali Rameshbabu](48981821.md), the lowest-rated player in the field, won the tournament and earned the right to play the [Women's World Chess Championship 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Championship_2027) match against the reigning Women's World Chess Champion [Ju Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_Wenjun).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

### FIDE Women's Events 2024–25

In conjunction with the Open [Candidates Tournament 2026](75933820.md), the runner-up of [the previous championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Championship_2025) match no longer automatically qualified, unlike any previous [Women's Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Candidates_Tournament). Instead, the 2025 match was part of the FIDE Women's Events 2024–25, a new qualification path which was a circuit that included the 2024 and 2025 World Rapid and Blitz Championships, the Grand Prix series, the World Cup and the Grand Swiss.[^cite_note-8] A player's score was the sum of her highest scores in up to 5 qualifying events.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

## Organization

The tournament was an eight-player, double round-robin tournament, meaning there were 14 rounds with each player facing each of the others twice: once with the white pieces and once with the black pieces. The tournament winner will qualify to play [Ju Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_Wenjun) for the [World Championship in 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Chess_Championship_2026).

### Regulations

The time control was 90 minutes for the first 40 moves, then 30 minutes for the rest of the game, plus a 30-second increment per move starting from move 1. Players got 1 point for a win, ½ point for a draw and 0 points for a loss.

Tiebreaks for the first place would have been addressed as follows:[^cite_note-regulations-2]

- If two players were tied, they would play two [rapid chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_chess) games at 15 minutes plus 10 seconds per move. If a three- to six-way tie occurred, a single round-robin would be played. If seven or eight players are tied, a single round-robin would be played with a time limit of 10 minutes plus 5 seconds per move.

- If any players were tied for first after the rapid chess games, they would play two [blitz chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitz_chess) games at 3 minutes plus 2 seconds per move. In the case of more than two players being tied, a single round-robin would be played.

- If any players were still tied for first after these blitz chess games, the remaining players would play a knock-out blitz tournament at the same time control. In each mini-match of the proposed knock-out tournament, the first player to win a game would win the mini-match.

Ties for places other than first were broken by, in order: (1) [Sonneborn–Berger score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonneborn%E2%80%93Berger_score); (2) total number of wins; (3) head-to-head score among tied players; (4) drawing of lots.

### Prize money

The prize money was [€](9472.md)28,000 for first place, €17,000 for second place, and €8,600 for third place (with players on the same number of points sharing prize money, irrespective of tie-breaks), plus €2,200 per half-point for every player, for a minimum total prize pool of €300,000, according to the regulations.[^cite_note-regulations-2]

### Arbiters

The Chief Arbiter for the event was Takis Nikolopoulos (Greece) with Andrew Howie (Scotland) and [Ana Srebrnič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Srebrni%C4%8D) (Slovakia) both acting as Deputy Chief Arbiter and Fair Play Officer.[^cite_note-11]
