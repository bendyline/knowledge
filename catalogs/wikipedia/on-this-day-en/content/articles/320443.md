**East Java** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Jawa Timur*, [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language): ꦙꦮꦶꦮꦺꦠꦤ꧀, romanized: *Jawi Wétan*, [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_language): ꦗꦧꦠꦺꦩꦺꦴꦂ, romanized: *Jhâbâ Tèmor*) is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) of [Indonesia](14579.md) located in the easternmost third of [Java](69336.md) island. It has a land border only with the province of [Central Java](453584.md) to the west; the [Java Sea](467143.md) and the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) border its northern and southern coasts, respectively, while the narrow [Bali Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali_Strait) to the east separates Java from [Bali](4147.md) by around 2.29 kilometers (1.42 mi). Located in eastern [Java](69336.md), the province also includes the island of [Madura](183981.md) (which is connected to Java by the longest bridge in Indonesia, the [Suramadu Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suramadu_Bridge)), as well as the [Kangean islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangean_Islands) and other smaller island groups located further east (in the northern [Bali Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali_Sea)) and the [Masalembu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masalembu_Islands) archipelago to the north. Its capital is [Surabaya](212635.md), the [second largest city in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indonesian_cities_by_population), a major industrial center and also a major business center. [Banyuwangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyuwangi_\(town\)) is the largest regency in East Java and the largest on the island of Java.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

The province covers a land area of 48,036.84 square kilometers (18,547.13 sq mi), and according to the [2010 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indonesian_census), there were 37,476,757 people residing there,[^cite_note-Biro_Pusat_Statistik_2011-8] making it [Indonesia](14579.md)'s second-most-populous province; the [2020 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Indonesian_census) showed an increase to 40,665,696 people,[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-9] while the official estimate for mid 2025 was 42,089,271 (comprising 20,978,460 males and 21,110,830 females).[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] Almost a quarter of the population lives inside the [Greater Surabaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya_metropolitan_area) metropolitan area.[^cite_note-10] East Java is inhabited by many different ethnic groups, such as the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians). Most of the people in East Java adhere to [Islam](6037917.md), forming around 94% of the total population. Other religions are also practiced, such as [Christianity](5211.md), [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) which are mostly practiced by [Tionghoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians) people and immigrants from [Eastern Indonesia](59023273.md) and [North Sumatra](453595.md), and also [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) which are practiced by the [Tenggerese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenggerese_people) in the [Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park](8790147.md) and the [Balinese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people) inhabiting the easternmost part of the province bordering [Bali](4147.md) as well as the dominant minority [Indian Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians) in [Surabaya](212635.md) city.&#x20;

The [Indonesian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is the official language of the province as well as the [whole nation](14579.md), but [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) and [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_language) are most frequently used, especially the Surabaya dialect ([Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language): *Suroboyoan* or *Surabayaan* — the Javanese dialect of [Surabaya](212635.md)) used mainly in the capital [Surabaya](212635.md). [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is only used for inter-ethnic communication and official purposes.&#x20;

East Java offers different types of tourist attractions. There are a variety of natural attractions, including mountains, beaches, caves, and waterfalls. Almost every regency or city in East Java has its own unique tourist destinations, such as the [Ijen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijen) volcano in [Banyuwangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyuwangi_Regency), [Baluran National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluran_National_Park) in [Situbondo](6575314.md), and [Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park](8790147.md) in [Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang_Regency), [Pasuruan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasuruan_Regency), [Lumajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumajang_Regency), and [Probolinggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probolinggo_Regency).&#x20;

## History

### Prehistory

East Java has been inhabited by humans since prehistoric times. This was proven by the discovery of fossils remains of the *[Pithecanthropus mojokertensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojokerto_child)* in Kepuhlagen, [Mojokerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojokerto_Regency);[^cite_note-11] *[Pithecanthropus erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_Man)* on [Trinil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinil), [Ngawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngawi_Regency);[^cite_note-12] and *[Homo wajakensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wajak_crania)* in Wajak, [Tulungagung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulungagung_Regency).[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

### Hindu-Buddhist era

See also: [Hinduism in Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Java)



[Gadjah Mada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajah_Mada) is a warlord and *Mahapatih* (Prime Minister) who was very influential during the [Majapahit Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit).

The Dinoyo inscriptions found near the city of [Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang) are the oldest written sources in East Java, dating from 760 CE. It tells of many political and cultural events in the Kingdom of Kanjuruhan. The name Malang is thought to come from the name of a sacred building called Malangkuseswara. This name is contained in the [Mantyasih inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantyasih_inscription) written in 907 CE.&#x20;

In 1222, [Ken Arok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Arok) founded the [Kingdom of Singhasari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhasari), which he ruled until 1227. Before coming to power, Ken Arok seized power in Tumapel ([Kediri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kediri_\(city\))) from Tungul Ametung. Ken Arok's descendants became kings of [Singhasari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhasari) and [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) from the 13th until the 15th century.&#x20;

In 1227, [Anusapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anusapati) killed Ken Arok, and later became king of Singhasari. Anusapati's power only lasted 20 years, before he was killed by Tohjaya. Three years later, Tohjaya was killed in an uprising led by Jaya Wisnuwardhana, son of Anusapati. In 1268, Wisnuwardhana died, and he was succeeded by [Kertanegara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kertanagara) (1268–1292). In 1292 Kertanegara was defeated by a rebel named Jayakatwang, ending the Singhasari.&#x20;

In 1293, [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan), founder of the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), sent a large invasion fleet to Java with 20,000 to 30,000 soldiers, beginning the [Mongol invasion of Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Java).[^cite_note-14] This was a [punitive expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punitive_expedition) against King Kertanegara of Singhasari, who had refused to pay tribute to the Yuan and maimed one of its ministers. However, it ended with failure for the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols).&#x20;

In 1294, the [Kingdom of Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) was founded by [Raden Wijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raden_Wijaya). The Majapahit reached its peak during the reign of [Hayam Wuruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayam_Wuruk). He was accompanied by the *Mahapatih* [Gajah Mada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajah_Mada). Together they managed to unite the archipelago under the name Dwipantara. Majapahit developed to become one of the strongest empires in Southeast Asia.&#x20;

In 1357, the [Battle of Bubat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bubat) occurred, starting a war between the [Sunda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kingdom) and the Majapahit. The event stemmed from the desire of king Hayam Wuruk to take a [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people) princess. Dyah Pitaloka as queen. However, because of a misunderstanding about the procedure of marriage, leading to a battle in Bubat. Majapahit troops, under the command of Gajah Mada, captured and killed the present Members of the Royal Family of [Pajajaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakuan_Pajajaran). In 1389, Hayam Wuruk died, and was succeeded by [Wikramawardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikramavardhana). This resulted in the beginning of the decline of the Majapahit Empire. As the Majapahit Empire went into decline in the late 1300s, Islam moved to fill the vacuum.[^cite_note-15]&#x20;

### Islamic era

Main articles: [Spread of Islam in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam_in_Indonesia) and [Islam in East Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_East_Java)



The [Ampel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampel_Mosque) in [Surabaya](212635.md), the [oldest surviving mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_oldest_mosques) in [Java](69336.md) and second oldest in [Indonesia](14579.md), was built in 1421

The precise date when Islam entered Java remains unclear. This is due to the absence of a definite source regarding the arrival of Islam in Java. However, according to some experts, it is estimated that Islam entered Java around the 11th century, with the tomb of Fatimah Binti Maimun in the village of Leran in [Gresik Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresik_Regency) which dates from 475 AH (1085 AD). The tomb also shows that by the 11th century, the North coast of Java had been frequented by [Arab](2185.md) traders from the Middle East. In addition, several Islamic tombs were discovered in [Trowulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trowulan), located in what is now part of the [Mojokerto Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojokerto_Regency), near the site of the former Majapahit palace.[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

In the 15th century, a Chinese [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) voyager named [Ma Huan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Huan) visited East Java. He then wrote the book *[Yingya Shenglan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingya_Shenglan)*, which tells the story of the countries he visited over the course of the [Ming treasure voyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_treasure_voyages). He mentioned that at that time, there were three different peoples inhabiting Eastern Java: Arabs from the Middle East, the ancestor of the modern [Arab Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Indonesians); Chinese Muslims originating from modern-day [Guangdong](65109.md) province, and the native [Javanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people).[^cite_note-17]&#x20;

By the 16th century, the Majapahit Empire was defeated by Islamic kingdoms in Java, resulting in the exile of many Majapahit aristocrats to the neighboring island of [Bali](4147.md).[^cite_note-18] Those who remained in Java were forced to convert to Islam, while a small pocket of isolated people living in the [Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park](8790147.md) spread around [Pasuruan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasuruan_Regency), [Probolinggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probolinggo_Regency), [Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang_Regency), and [Lumajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumajang_Regency) [Regencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_\(Indonesia\)) remains adhered to [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) to this day. They are known as the [Tenggerese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenggerese_people). Their population of roughly 100,000 is centered in 30 villages in the isolated Tengger mountains.[^cite_note-19]&#x20;

When Islamic sultanates started ruling Java, cities in the northern coast started developing, becoming thriving ports. One of them was the town of [Tuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuban), which was a wealthy and important port with many Chinese settlers. Being a port of the Majapahit and the point of departure for the [Moluccas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands), it exported an abundant supply of foodstuffs and imported a rich variety of products from the Moluccas.[^cite_note-20] At the end of the 16th century, Islam had surpassed Hinduism and Buddhism as the dominant religion in Java. At first, the spread of Islam was very rapid and was accepted by ordinary people, until the [da'wah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawah) entered and it was carried out by the rulers of the island.&#x20;

### European colonization

The relationship between the Javanese and European colonial powers began in 1522, with the signing of a treaty between the Sunda Kingdom and the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca). After the failure of the treaty, the Portuguese presence was then limited to Malacca in the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and the [Maluku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands). An expedition under the leadership of the Dutch explorer [Cornelis de Houtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_de_Houtman) consisting of four ships in 1596 became the beginning of Dutch presence in the island.[^cite_note-21] At the end of the 18th century, the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire) had succeeded in expanding their influence on the Islamic sultanates in the interior of the island of Java.&#x20;

At the onset of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), the [British conquered Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Java_\(1811\)) in 1811. Java briefly became part of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), with Sir [Stamford Raffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Raffles) as its Governor-General. In 1814, Britain returned Java to the Netherlands as stipulated in the [Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1814).[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

### Japanese occupation and revolution



[Operation Transom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Transom), destroyed [Tanjung Perak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Perak) in 1944

During the [Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies), there was persistent resistance against the Japanese rule. In [Blitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitar), an uprising by *[Pembela Tanah Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenders_of_the_Homeland)* (PETA) led by [Supriyadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supriyadi), Moeradi, Halir Mangkudijoyo, and Soemarto occurred in early 1945, but it was crushed by the Japanese.&#x20;

Two weeks after the [proclamation of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence), [Surabaya](212635.md) established its own government under, [Raden Sudirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudirman). The formation of government in Surabaya caused disputes between the republican forces and Japanese troops, resulting in various skirmishes across the city. This was because when the Japanese surrendered, they were obliged to remain in power until the allied forces arrived. The arrival of allied forces in Surabaya created tensions with the newly established government of Indonesia, reaching peak on 10 November 1945 where a major [battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Surabaya) between the Surabayan residents led by [Sutomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutomo) and allied forces.&#x20;

The battle forced the governor, Suryo, on the advice of People's Security Army (TKR), to move the seat of the government to [Mojokerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojokerto). A week later, the government retreated again to a more secure location in Kediri. However, security situation Kediri worsen until finally, in February 1947, the East Java provincial government fled to Malang. While the administration was based in Malang, Governor [Soerjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ario_Soerjo) was replaced by R.P. Suroso, who was in turn replaced again by Dr. Moerdjani. On 21 July 1947, although still bound by the [Linggadjati Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linggadjati_Agreement), a ceasefire agreement in effect since 14 October 1946, the Dutch commenced a military action, [Operation Product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Product), which led to deteriorating security conditions in Malang. The East Java provincial government moved again to Blitar.&#x20;

This military action ended after the [Renville Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renville_Agreement). However, this agreement reduced the territory controlled by the East Java provincial government. The Netherlands then turned the areas under its control into new federal states, the [State of Madura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Madura) and the [State of East Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_East_Java). Amid the difficulties faced by the government of Indonesia, a left-wing opposition group, *Front Demokrasi Rakyat* (FDR, People's Democratic Front) launched [rebellion in Madiun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madiun_Affair) on 18 September 1948. However, eventually this revolt was defeated by the [Indonesian Army](1711324.md). On 19 December 1948, the Dutch launched [Operation Kraai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Kraai). Blitar, the seat of the East Java provincial government was attacked by the Dutch. Governor Moerdjani and his staff were forced to flee and joined the guerrillas on the slopes of [Mount Wilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Wilis). Operation Kraai ended after the [Roem–Van Roijen Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roem%E2%80%93Van_Roijen_Agreement) on 7 May 1949.&#x20;

Following the [Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference), at which the Netherlands agreed to transfer sovereignty to the [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia), the Dutch withdrew its troops from East Java. East Java changed its status from a province into a state. However, on 25 February 1950, this was dissolved and became part of the territory of the [Republic of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Indonesia_\(1949%E2%80%931950\)). The [State of Madura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Madura) also decided to join Indonesia.&#x20;

### Contemporary era

Along with rapid growth of urbanization in East Java, the governments could not satisfy the population's needs for affordable housing, which led to the building of [shanty towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanty_town) along the rivers and rail tracks. Today, the shanty towns still exist; although some have been transformed into "better" housing.[^cite_note-23]&#x20;

East Java has twice hosted the [Indonesian National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Week) ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Pekan Olahraga Nasional*) (PON), the 7th (1969) and 15th PONs (2000), and became the overall champions in 2000 and 2008.&#x20;

## Geography

East Java province borders the [Java Sea](467143.md) in the north, the [Strait of Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali_Strait) in the east, and the Indian Ocean in the south, as well as the [Central Java](453584.md) province in the west. The length of the east–west stretch of about 400 km. The width of the stretch of the north–south in the west about 200 km, but in the eastern part of narrower by about 60 km. [Madura](183981.md) is the largest island in East Java, separated from the mainland Java by the [Strait of Madura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madura_Strait). [Bawean Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawean) is located about 150 km north of Java. In the east of Madura there are clusters of islands, the easternmost are the [Kangean Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangean_Islands) and the northernmost are the [Masalembu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masalembu_Islands). Off the south coast of Java there are two small islands namely [Nusa Barong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusa_Barong) and [Sempu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sempu_\(island\)).&#x20;

### Geology

In physiographic of geology, East Java Province can be grouped into three zones: the southern zone (plato), the middle zone (volcanic), and the northern zone (folds). Lowlands and highlands in the middle (of Ngawi, Blitar, Malang, to Bondowoso) has a fairly fertile soil. In the northern part ( Bojonegoro, Tuban, Gresik, to Madura Island) lies the Kapur Utara mountains and the Kendeng mountains which are relatively barren.&#x20;

In the middle of the province stretch mountain ranges and volcanoes: On the border with Central Java is [Mount Lawu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lawu) (3,265 meters). Southeast from Madiun is [Mount Wilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Wilis) (2,169 meters), and Mount Liman (2,563 meters). In the middle of the corridor lies the Anjasmoro mountains with peaks [Mount Arjuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjuno-Welirang) (3,339 meters), Mount Welirang (3,156 meters), Mount Anjasmoro (2,277 meters), Mount Kawi (2,551 meters), and [Mount Kelud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelud) (1,731 meters); The mountains are located in most Kediri, Blitar, Malang, Pasuruan, Mojokerto and Jombang. The group has the peak of Mount Bromo Tengger (2,329 meters), and Mount Semeru (3,676 meters). [Mount Semeru](2407461.md), which is also called Mahameru is the highest mountain in the island of Java. In the easternmost part if the province, there are two groups of mountains: the Iyang mountains with the peak Mount Argopuro (3,088 meters), the Ijen mountains with the peak [Mount Raung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raung) (3344 meters) In the south there is a series of hills, that of the south coast of Pacitan, Trenggalek, Tulungagung, Blitar, Malang. the Kapur Selatan mountains is a continuation of a series of the Sewu mountains in Yogyakarta.&#x20;

### Water



[Light snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifications_of_snow) and [frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost) are common at East Java highlands over 2,000 [meters above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_mean_sea_level) in middle of year during late night until morning

Two of the most important rivers in East Java are the [Brantas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brantas_River) (290 kilometers), and the [Solo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_River) (548 kilometers). The Brantas River has headwaters on the slopes Mount Arjuno near Batu, and flows through most areas in East Java, like Malang, Blitar, Tulungagung, Kediri, Jombang and Mojokerto. In Mojokerto, the Brantas River split into two: [Kali Mas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mas_River), and Porong; both empty into the Madura Strait. The Solo River has headwaters on the slopes of Mount Lawu which lies on the border between East Java and Central Java, and flows through a portion of the eastern part of Central Java and East Java, which eventually empties into the sea at Gresik. The Brantas River and Bengawan Solo are managed by Perum Jasa Tirta I. On the slopes of Mount Lawu near the border with Central Java are Sarangan, a natural lake. The main dam in East Java, among others Ir. Sutami and Selorejo Dam, which is used for irrigation, fish farming and tourism.&#x20;

### Climate

East Java has a [tropical monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) and [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) climate at lower elevation and [subtropical at higher elevation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate#Subtropical_highland_variety_\(Cfb,_Cwb\)). Compared with the western part of Java Island, East Java in general has less rainfall. Average rainfall is 1,900 mm per year, with a rainy season during the 100 days. The average temperature ranges between 19–34 °C. Temperatures in the lower mountain areas, and even in areas Ranu Pani (slopes of Mount Semeru), temperatures can reach –4 °C, causing a frost and fall of light snow.[^cite_note-24]&#x20;

Akin to [Central Java](453584.md), the region's birth rates are not necessarily any lower than the rest of Java, however due to net population outflows, especially in times of calamity, not limited to volcanic eruptions or droughts, the region has varying rates of population growth that are generally lower than national average. Ethnic [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) dominate the Java mainland as well as the total population of the province overall, while ethnic [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people) (include [Bawean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawean_people)) inhabit Madura and the [Kangean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangean_people) and Masalembu archipelagos, though centuries of migrations have led the Java mainland to have a larger proportion of Madurese people than Madura itself. Minorities include distinct Javanese ethnicities such as the [Tengger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenggerese_people) people in the [Mount Bromo](429333.md) region, the [Samin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saminism_Movement) in Bojonegoro near the Central Java border, and the [Osing people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osing_people) in [Banyuwangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyuwangi_\(town\)). East Java also hosts a significant population of foreign ethnic groups, such as [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians), and [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Indonesians).&#x20;

### Ethnic and linguistic composition

2. [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people) (17.5%)

3. [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians) (0.66%)

4. Others (1.12%)

Besides the national language ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language)), the people of East Java predominantly use the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) in daily life. Javanese as spoken in the western part of East Java (*Kulonan*) is a similar dialect to the one spoken in [Central Java](453584.md), with its hierarchy of high, medium, and low registers. In the eastern part, such as Surabaya, Malang, and others, a more egalitarian version of Javanese is spoken, with less regard of hierarchy and a richer vocabulary for vulgarity. The dialect is notable for its roughness compared to other dialects spoken elsewhere in Java (especially the Mataraman dialect spoken around [Surakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta) and [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta), which is renowned for its smoothness) and contributes to the stereotype among Javanese people of East Javanese being "blunt" and "loud".[^cite_note-29] Variants of Javanese are also used by Osing and Tengger minorities, the former utilizing a [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_language)-influenced Javanese by virtue of its closeness with Bali island, and the latter speaking an archaic form of the language that retains many features now lost in other more-innovative Javanese dialects.[^cite_note-30] Other than Javanese, minority language includes [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_language), spoken by around 4 million ethnic Madurese people inhabiting the [Madura Islands](183981.md), as well as the [eastern salient of Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_salient_of_Java) area; though they live practically next door with the Javanese, the language is actually closer genetically to Balinese and [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_language).[^cite_note-31]&#x20;

### Religion

A long time ago, Hinduism and Buddhism dominated the island until [Islam](6037917.md) gradually [supplanted Hinduism in the 14th and 15th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam_in_Indonesia). The last nobles and people of the fallen [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) fled to [Bali](4147.md). Islam spread from northern cities in Java where many [Muslim](19541.md) traders from [Gujarat](53707.md), India stopped by. The eastern part of East Java, from Surabaya to Pasuruan, and the cities along the coast, and back to Banyuwangi to Jember, are known as the [eastern salient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_salient_of_Java), or "*Kawasan Tapal Kuda*" (the Horseshoe Region).&#x20;

[Surabaya](212635.md) is one of the industrial, transportational, commercial, and financial center of Indonesia.

East Java is known as the hub and center economy of [Central and Eastern Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Indonesia),[^cite_note-33] and has a high economic significance, which contributes over 15% to the [Gross Domestic Product](12594.md) of Indonesia.[^cite_note-34]&#x20;

### Industry

East Java has a number of large industries,[^cite_note-\:02-35] including the largest shipbuilding shipyard in Indonesia, [PT PAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAL_Indonesia) in [Surabaya](212635.md), military industrial by [Pindad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pindad) in [Southern Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang_Regency), largest railway industry in Southeast Asia, [Industri Kereta Api](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industri_Kereta_Api) (INKA) in [Madiun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madiun), PT Tjiwi Kimia, a paper mill company based in [Sidoarjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidoarjo_Regency), Kertas Leces based in [Probolinggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probolinggo)); cigarette factories ([Wismilak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismilak) in Surabaya, [Gudang Garam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudang_Garam) in [Kediri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kediri_\(city\)), [Sampoerna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampoerna) in Surabaya and [Bentoel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentoel_Group) in Malang). In Gresik there are [Semen Gresik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_\(company\)), and [Petrokimia Gresik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrokimia_Gresik). In [Tuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuban) there are the largest cement factories in Indonesia, namely Semen Indonesia (ex-Semen Gresik), and Semen Holcim and the Petrochemical Refinery Area. The government has established 12 industrial estate companies, including Surabaya Industrial Estate Rungkut (SIER) in Surabaya, Pasuruan Industrial Estate Rembang (PIER) in Pasuruan Regency, Madiun Industrial Estate Balerejo (MIEB) in Madiun, Ngoro Industrial Park (NIP) in the Mojokerto Regency, Jabon Industrial Zone in Sidoarjo Regency, and Lamongan Integrated Shorebase (LIS) in [Lamongan Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamongan_Regency). Small industrial centers are spread throughout the districts / cities, and some of them have penetrated exports; The leather craft industry in the form of bags and shoes at Tanggulangin, Sidoarjo is one of the very famous [small industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium_enterprises).[^cite_note-\:02-35]&#x20;

### Energy and power plant

The Cepu Block, one of the largest oil producers in Indonesia, is refined in [Bojonegoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojonegoro_Regency). The power plant in East Java is managed by PT Pembangkit Jawa Bali (PJB), which includes [hydroelectricity](381399.md) power plant (Ir. Sutami, Selorejo, Bening), [thermal power station](2549543.md) in Paiton, [Probolinggo Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probolinggo_Regency); which provides electricity to the Java-Bali system. Some regions develop [micro hydro power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_hydro), and [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy).[^cite_note-36]&#x20;

## Transportation

### Roads



The Highway of [Pandaan–Malang Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandaan%E2%80%93Malang_Toll_Road)

East Java is crossed by several national roads as primary arterial roads, including the northern coastal national road or locally known as [*pantura* road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Coast_Road_\(Java\)) ([Anyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyer)-[Jakarta](16275.md)-[Semarang](1134311.md)-[Surabaya](212635.md)-[Banyuwangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyuwangi_Regency)),[^cite_note-nas-37] and inland national roads (Jakarta-[Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung)-[Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta)-Surabaya). The expressway network in East Java covers the [Surabaya-Gempol Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya%E2%80%93Gempol_Toll_Road); [Gempol-Pandaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gempol%E2%80%93Pandaan_Toll_Road); [Pandaan-Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandaan-Malang_Toll_Road); [Gempol-Pasuruan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gempol%E2%80%93Pasuruan_Toll_Road); and [Pasuruan-Probolinggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasuruan%E2%80%93Probolinggo_Toll_Road) which connects the northern and middle East Java regions with the southern and [eastern salient (*tapal kuda*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_salient_of_Java), the [Surabaya-Gresik Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya%E2%80%93Gresik_Toll_Road) and [Krian-Manyar Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krian%E2%80%93Legundi%E2%80%93Bunder%E2%80%93Manyar_Toll_Road) that connects the middle and southern East Java to the north, and the [Surabaya-Mojokerto Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya%E2%80%93Mojokerto_Toll_Road); [Mojokerto-Kertosono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kertosono%E2%80%93Mojokerto_Toll_Road); and [Solo-Kertosono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo-Kertosono_Toll_Road) which connects [Central Java](453584.md). [Suramadu Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suramadu_Bridge) that crosses the Madura Strait connects Surabaya and [Madura Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madura_Island). In the near future, the construction of the [Probolinggo-Banyuwangi Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probolinggo-Banyuwangi_Toll_Road) will be realized, which is the final segment of the [Trans Java Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Java_Toll_Road), the Krian-Legundi-Bunder-Manyar Toll Road that connects the industrial estates in the city of Surabaya; [Sidoarjo Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidoarjo_Regency); and [Gresik Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresik_Regency), as well as plans for the Gresik-Tuban Toll Road that connects East Java with cities on the northern coast of Java.[^cite_note-38]&#x20;

### Railways



*KA Jenggala* train of [Greater Surabaya Commuter Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Surabaya_Commuter_Line) passing by Sidoarjo station. This train serves the easternmost rail route in East Java specially in [Greater Surabaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Surabaya)

The railway system in East Java has been built since the [colonial era of the Dutch East Indies](23476997.md). The railway line in East Java consists [Surabaya Commuterline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Surabaya_Commuter_Line) of the northern line ([Surabaya Pasar Turi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya_Pasar_Turi_railway_station)-[Semarang](1134311.md)-[Cirebon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirebon)-[Jakarta](16275.md)), the middle lane ([Surabaya Gubeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya_Gubeng_railway_station)-[Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta)-[Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung)-Jakarta), the southern ring railway line (Surabaya Gubeng-[Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang_railway_station)-[Blitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitar_railway_station)-Kertosono-Surabaya), and east line (Surabaya Gubeng-Probolinggo-[Jember](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jember_railway_station)-[Ketapang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketapang_railway_station_\(Banyuwangi\))). East Java also has a commuter train transportation system with [Bojonegoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojonegoro_railway_station)-Surabaya-[Sidoarjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidoarjo_railway_station), Surabaya-Sidoarjo-Porong, Surabaya-Lamongan, Surabaya-Mojokerto, Surabaya-Malang, and Jember-Banyuwangi routes.[^cite_note-39]&#x20;

At present, the Surabaya Gubeng-Kertosono-Madiun line is being developed into a [double track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-track_railway) and is planned to be completed by the end of 2018 or early 2019 and ready for use in mid-2019.[^cite_note-40]&#x20;

## Art and culture

### Art

East Java has a number of distinctive art. Ludruk is one of the East Javanese art is quite well known, namely the art of the stage that generally all the players are male. Unlike the Ketoprak which tells the life of the palace, ludruk tells the daily life of ordinary people, often spiced with humor and social criticism, and generally opened with Dance Remo, and *parikan*. Currently traditional ludruk groups can be found in the area of Surabaya, Mojokerto and Jombang; although its presence increasingly defeated by modernization.&#x20;



[Reog Ponorogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reog_Ponorogo) is the famous traditional dance in East Java.

[Reog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reog) from Ponorogo is a typical art that has been patented since 2001, and has now also become the icon of East Javanese art. Staging reog accompanied by horse braid (kuda lumping) are accompanied by elements of the occult.[^cite_note-44] Famous arts of East Java include puppet purwa East Javanese style, mask mastermind in Madura, and made. In the area Mataraman Middle Javanese arts such as Ketoprak, and shadow puppets are quite popular. Famous legend of East Java, among others [Damarwulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damarwulan), Angling Darma, and Sarip Tambak-Oso.&#x20;



[Gandrung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandrung), iconic dance in [Banyuwangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyuwangi_Regency).

Traditional dance in East Java can be generally grouped into Middle Javanese style, East Javanese style, Osing style and Madurese styles. Classical dances include [gandrung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandrung), gambyong dance, dance [srimpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srimpi), dance bondan, and wanderer. There is also a sort of lion dance culture in East Java. Art exists in two districts namely, Bondowoso, and Jember. Singo Wulung is a distinctive culture Bondowoso. While Jember have the tiger kadhuk. Both are art that is rarely encountered.&#x20;

### Culture



Islamic *[Santri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santri)* students celebrate *Santri* day in [Southern Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang_Regency)

Culture and customs of Javanese in the western part of East Java received a lot of influence from the Middle Javanese, so this area is known as Mataraman, indicating that the area was once the territory of the [Sultanate of Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Mataram). The area includes the former residency of Madiun (Madiun, Ngawi, Magetan, Ponorogo, Pacitan), ex-residency Kediri (Kediri, Tulungagung, Blitar, Trenggalek, Nganjuk), and part of Bojonegoro. As is the case in Central Java, wayang kulit, and Ketoprak quite popular in this region.&#x20;

East Java's west coast region is heavily influenced by the Islamic culture. This area covers an area of Tuban, Lamongan and Gresik. In the past the north coast of East Java is the entry area, and the center of the development of Islam. Five of the nine members of the walisongo are buried in this area.&#x20;

In the area of ex-residency of Surabaya (including Sidoarjo, Mojokerto and Jombang), and ex-residency Malang, had little cultural influence Mataraman, considering this region is an area arek (the term for offspring Kenarok), especially in the area of Malang that make this area difficult to be affected by Mataraman culture.&#x20;



[Karapan sapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karapan_sapi) is a [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people) traditional bull racing festival

Customs in horse hooves region heavily influenced by the culture of Madura, given the magnitude of Madura tribe population in the region like [Karapan sapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karapan_sapi) a bull racing festival by Madurese. Osing social mores of the culture is a blend of Java. While the Tenggerese tribe is heavily influenced by Hindu culture.[^cite_note-45]&#x20;

Villagers in East Java, as well as in Central Java, has ties based on friendship, and territorial. Various ceremonies were held, among others: tingkepan (ceremony gestational age of seven months for the first child), babaran (ceremony before the birth of the baby), sepasaran (ceremony after the baby was five days), Pitonan (ceremony after the baby was seven months old), circumcision, fiancé.&#x20;

East Java's population generally embraced monogamy. Prior to application, the men doing the show nako'ake (ask if the girl already had a husband), once that is done peningsetan (application). The wedding ceremony was preceded by a gathering or kepanggih. Communities on the west coast: Tuban, Lamongan, Gresik, even Bojonegoro has a habit of women's families applying common man (Ganjuran), different from the usual habits of other regions in Indonesia, where the men apply for women. And generally men will then be entered into the family of the woman.&#x20;

To pray for the deceased person, usually a family party did send donga on day 1, 3rd, 7th, 40th, 100th, 1 year and 3 years after death.&#x20;

## Tourism

[Malang metropolitan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Malang) is a famous tourist destination in Indonesia with the City of Batu as its center.[^cite_note-48]&#x20;

East Java has a number of interesting sights. One of the tourist icon is Mount Bromo in East Java, which is inhabited by the Tengger tribe, where the ceremony is held every year Kasada. In the mountainous region of Tengger also there is a waterfall that is Madakaripura which is the last hermitage Mahapatih Gajah Mada before serving in the kingdom of Majapahit. Madakaripura Waterfall has a height of about 200 meters, which makes it the highest waterfall on the island of Java and the second highest in Indonesia. East Java also has some other mountainous tourist area of which is Malang Raya mountainous area known as the natural mountain tourist area that includes Malang and Batu. Tretes and Trawas mountainous areas, are also known to have characteristics such as Puncak area in West Java province. Other natural attractions in East Java is the National Park (4 of 12 National Parks in Java) and Taman Safari Indonesia II in Prigen.&#x20;

East Java is also contained relics of history in the classical era. Trowulan sites in Mojokerto, once the center of the Majapahit Kingdom, there are dozens of temples and tombs of the kings of Majapahit. Other temples spread throughout much of East Java, including Penataran temple in Blitar.[^cite_note-49] In Madura, Sumenep is the center of the kingdom of Madura, where there are Kraton Sumenep, museums, and the tombs of the kings of Madura (Asta Tinggi Sumenep).&#x20;

East Java is known for its coastal scenery.[^cite_note-50] On the south coast there is the Prigi Beach, Pelang Beach and Pasir Putih Beach in Trenggalek, Popoh Beach in Tulungagung, Ngliyep Beach, and tourism areas such as the [Jatim Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawa_Timur_Park), [Museum Angkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Angkut), Batu Secret Zoo, Batu Night Spectacular, Eco Green Park in Batu, and the Watu Ulo Beach in Jember. East Java also has a beach that the waves is one of the best in the world, namely the Plengkung Beach located in Banyuwangi. In addition there Kenjeran Beach in Surabaya, and the White Sand Beach in Situbondo. Lake in East Java, among others Sarangan in Magetan, Ir. Sutami Dam in Malang, and Selorejo Dam in Blitar.&#x20;

In the coastal area of the north there are a number of tombs of the saints, which are places of pilgrimage for Muslims.[^cite_note-51] Five of the nine *walisongo* are buried in East Java: [Sunan Ampel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Ampel) in Surabaya, [Sunan Giri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Giri), and [Maulana Malik Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Malik_Ibrahim) in Gresik, [Sunan Drajat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Drajat) in Paciran (Lamongan), and [Sunan Bonang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Bonang) in Tuban. In the northern coastal region, there are a number of caves, including Maharani Cave in Lamongan and Tuban Akbar Cave and Cave Gong located in Pacitan. Other pilgrimage sites include the tomb of Indonesia's first president [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno), in [Blitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitar), as well as the tomb of the fourth Indonesian president [Abdurrahman Wahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdurrahman_Wahid), known as Gus Dur, in Jombang.&#x20;

Surabaya is the center of government, entertainment, financial, and business center of East Java, where there are Tugu Pahlawan, the Museum MPU Tantular, Surabaya Zoo, Submarine Monument, Ampel Region, and the Downtown Tunjungan area.

## Cuisine



[Rujak Cingur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rojak), traditional dish from East Java

Some typical East Javanese dishes include Bakso Malang, Rawon, and Tahu Campur Lamongan.[^cite_note-53] Surabaya is famous for Rojak Cingur, Semanggi, Lontong Balap, clam satay, mussels, and rice cakes.[^cite_note-54] Malang is popular for a variety of processed fruits (especially apples), [tempeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempeh) crisps, [Bakpao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakpao) telo, [Bakso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakso) Malang, rawon, and Cwie noodles.[^cite_note-55] Kediri is famous for tahu takwa, tahu pong, tungku fried rice and getuk pisang.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Madiun is known for pecel madiun, and as a producer of Brem.[^cite_note-58] The subdistrict of Babat, Lamongan is famous as a producer of [wingko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingko).[^cite_note-59] Bondowoso is also well known as a producer of [tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapai). Gresik famous is for nasi krawu, otak-otak bandeng, and bonggolan. Sidoarjo is famous for shrimp crackers, shrimp paste, and petis. Ngawi is famous[^cite_note-60] for tempeh chips, tahu tepo, and nasi lethok. Blitar has the typical food that is pecel. [Rambutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambutan) is also native to Blitar. Banyuwangi is famous[^cite_note-61] for sego tempong, salad soup, and pecel rawon. Jember have special food made of tape that is Suwar-suwir and tape proll that is very sweet. Corn is known as one of the staple food of the Madurese, while cassava is processed into gaplek, a staple food used to be a part of the population in Pacitan and Trenggalek. Tulungagung is famous for its lodho, goat satay and pati rice.[^cite_note-62] Ponorogo is famous for Satay Ponorogo, Tiwul Goreng and Dawet Jabung, Probolinggo is famous for Ketan Kratok, Olok Crab, Glepungan Rice and Kraksaan Soto.&#x20;

## Sports

See also: [List of East Java major league professional sports teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_East_Java_major_league_professional_sports_teams)



[CLS Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLS_Knights_Indonesia), most famous professional [basketball](3921.md) club in Indonesia.

East Java is the province with the highest number of [professional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_sports) [football](10568.md) clubs in Indonesia and also province with clubs in top division league.[^cite_note-63] By [2023–24 Liga 1 (Indonesia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Liga_1_\(Indonesia\)), [Indonesian First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_1_\(Indonesia\)) clubs from East Java are [Persik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persik_Kediri), [Madura United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madura_United_F.C.), [Arema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arema_F.C.), and [Persebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persebaya_Surabaya). Arema and Persebaya are football clubs from East Java who have become top teams in Indonesia by repeatedly winning championships and national competitions and often as [representatives of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Indonesia) in [AFC Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Champions_League) and [AFC Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Cup) between clubs in Asia.&#x20;

East Java once hosted the [Indonesian National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Week_\(Indonesia\)) ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Pekan Olahraga Nasional*), namely PON VII in 1969, and PON XV in 2000, and became the overall champion of PON in 2000, and 2008. Since 1996 the East Java Football Team always won medals gold was included in 2008, and was recorded as the fourth gold medal received consecutively.&#x20;

East Java has several professional basketball clubs, mainly in [Surabaya](212635.md). The famous basketball clubs namely [CLS Knights Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLS_Knights_Indonesia) and [Pacific Cesar Surabaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Caesar_Surabaya). East Java is also the venue for several bicycle racing events namely [Tour de East Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_East_Java) and [Tour de Ijen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_Ijen).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]&#x20;

Other sports such as volleyball in East Java also have superior clubs, namely [Surabaya Samator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surabaya_Samator) and [Gresik Petrokimia Pupuk Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresik_Petrokimia_Pupuk_Indonesia). Apart from that, in the futsal sport there are two well-known clubs, namely [Bintang Timur Surabaya Futsal Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintang_Timur_Surabaya_Futsal_Club) and [Unggul Futsal Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unggul_Futsal_Club). In badminton, there is the [Surya Naga Surabaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Surya_Naga_Surabaya\&action=edit\&redlink=1) club which also contributes a number of national badminton athletes.&#x20;
