**Lublin Voivodeship** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *województwo lubelskie* [\[vɔjɛˈvut͡stfɔ luˈbɛlskʲɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b9/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubelskie.wav/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubelskie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b9/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubelskie.wav/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-KaMan-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_lubelskie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland) ([province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province)) of [Poland](22936.md), located in the southeastern part of the country, with its capital being the city of [Lublin](74594.md).

The region is named after its largest city and regional capital, [Lublin](74594.md), and its territory is made of four historical lands: the western and central part of the voivodeship, with Lublin itself, belongs to [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland), the eastern part of Lublin Area belongs to [Cherven Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherven_Cities)/[Red Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia), and the northeast belongs to [Polesie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesie) and [Podlasie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlasie).[^cite_note-3] Lublin Voivodeship borders [Subcarpathian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcarpathian_Voivodeship) to the south, [Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awi%C4%99tokrzyskie_Voivodeship) to the south-west, [Masovian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masovian_Voivodeship) to the west and north, [Podlaskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaskie_Voivodeship) along a short boundary to the north, [Belarus](3457.md) ([Brest Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest_Region)) and [Ukraine](31750.md) ([Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Oblast) and [Volyn Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volyn_Oblast)) to the east. The region's population as of 2024 was 1,996,440. It covers an area of 25,155 square kilometres (9,712 sq mi).

## History



[Lublin](74594.md) in the 17th century

The [Polish historical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_historical_regions) that encompasses [Lublin](74594.md), and approximates Lublin Voivodeship as it was before the [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland), is known as *Lubelszczyzna*. Provinces centred on Lublin have existed throughout much of Poland's history; for details see the section below on [Previous Lublin Voivodeships](#Previous_Lublin_Voivodeships). Cities and towns of greatest historic importance are Lublin, [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m), [Kazimierz Dolny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Dolny) and [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87). Lublin hosted several sessions of the Polish Parliament, including the session which established the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) (see [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin)), and was the seat of the [Crown Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Tribunal) for the [Lesser Poland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Province,_Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland), the highest appeal court in the Kingdom of Poland, and [Parczew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parczew) also hosted one session of the Polish Parliament.[^cite_note-4] [Horodło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horod%C5%82o) was the place of signing of the Polish–Lithuanian [Union of Horodło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Horod%C5%82o), predating the Union of Lublin.

In the 17th century, there were sizeable [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_in_Poland) communities in Lublin and Zamość,[^cite_note-5] and also a smaller one in [Opole Lubelskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opole_Lubelskie).[^cite_note-6] Lublin and Zamość also hosted [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Poland) minorities.[^cite_note-7]

The industry of the region was greatly expanded as part of the [Central Industrial Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Industrial_Region_\(Poland\)) of Poland.



[Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87) in the 1930s

The region was, before World War II, one of the world's leading centres of [Judaism](15624.md). Before the middle of the 16th century, there were few Jews in the area, concentrated in [Lublin](74594.md), [Kazimierz Dolny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Dolny), and perhaps [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m); but the founding of new private towns led to a large movement of Jews into the region to develop trade and services. Since these new towns competed with the existing towns for business, there followed a low-intensity, long-lasting feeling of resentment, with failed attempts to limit the Jewish immigration. The Jews tended to settle mostly in the cities and towns, with only individual families setting up businesses in the rural regions; this urban/rural division became another factor feeding resentment of the newly arrived economic competitors. By the middle of the 18th century, Jews were a significant part of the population in [Kraśnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra%C5%9Bnik), [Lubartów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubart%C3%B3w) and [Łęczna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C4%99czna).

By the 20th century, Jews represented greater than 70% of the population in eleven towns and close to 100% of the population of [Łaszczów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81aszcz%C3%B3w) and [Izbica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izbica). From this region came both religious figures such as [Mordechai Josef Leiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordechai_Josef_Leiner?action=edit\&redlink=1) of Izbica, [Chaim Israel Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Israel_Morgenstern?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Puławy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu%C5%82awy), and [Motele Rokeach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motele_Rokeach?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Biłgoraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi%C5%82goraj), as well as famous secular author [Israel Joshua Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Joshua_Singer). Israel's brother, the Nobel prize winner [Isaac Bashevis Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Bashevis_Singer), was not born in Biłgoraj but lived part of his life in the city. The "Old Town" of the city of [Lublin](74594.md) contained a famous [yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva), Jewish hospital, synagogue, cemetery, and kahal, as well as the [Grodzka Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodzka_Gate?action=edit\&redlink=1) (known as the Jewish Gate).

Before the war, there were 300,000 [Jews](25955086.md) living in the region, which became the site of the [Majdanek concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek_concentration_camp), [Bełżec extermination camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be%C5%82%C5%BCec_extermination_camp) and [Sobibór extermination camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobib%C3%B3r_extermination_camp) as well as several [labour camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_camp) ([Trawniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawniki), [Poniatowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poniatowa), [Budzyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budzy%C5%84,_Opole_Lubelskie_County), [Puławy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu%C5%82awy), [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87), [Biała Podlaska](389586.md), and the Lublin work camps [Lipowa 7 camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipowa_7_camp), [Flugplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_airfield_camp), and [Sportplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportplatz?action=edit\&redlink=1)) which produced military supplies for the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) and [Luftwaffe](17885.md). This was once one of the biggest forced labour centres in [German-occupied Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-occupied_Europe), with approximately 45,000 Jewish prisoners. After the war, the few surviving Jews largely left the area; today there is some restoration of areas of Jewish historical interest, and a surge of tourism by Jews seeking to view their families' historical roots.

Polish people were also victims of German persecution and [crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation), including the *[AB-Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_AB-Aktion_in_Poland)*, *[Aktion T4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktion_T4)* and [Operation Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87) with deportations to [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps), forced labour, [kidnapping of children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_of_children_by_Nazi_Germany) and massacres in the region.[^cite_note-8] It witnessed the Polish [Zamość uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87_uprising) against German occupation. There were also [German prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) for Polish, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Military_Internees), Dutch, Belgian, [Senegalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_Tirailleurs) and Soviet prisoners of war in [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m), [Dęblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%99blin), [Biała Podlaska](389586.md), [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87), [Kaliłów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali%C5%82%C3%B3w) with several [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) subcamps, where many died from starvation, epidemics or were massacred.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Lublin Voivodeship was created on January 1, 1999, out of the former [Lublin](#Lublin_Voivodeship_1975–1998), [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m_Voivodeship), [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87_Voivodeship), [Biała Podlaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska_Voivodeship) and (partially) [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg_Voivodeship) and [Siedlce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siedlce_Voivodeship) Voivodeships, pursuant to [Polish local government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_local_government_reforms) adopted in 1998.

## Economy



[Bogdanka Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdanka_Coal_Mine)

The [Gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the province was 18.5 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 3.7% of Polish economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 14,400 euros or 48% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 54% of the EU average. Lublin Voivodship is the province with the lowest GDP per capita in Poland.[^cite_note-13]

The Lublin Coal Basin is located in the voivodeship, centered at the [Bogdanka Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdanka_Coal_Mine) in [Bogdanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdanka,_Lublin_Voivodeship) near [Łęczna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C4%99czna).

[Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m) contains the [Chełm Chalk Tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m_Chalk_Tunnels), a system of tunnels dug into the [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) under the city, a unique structure in Europe, whereas Lublin offers an underground tourist route in historic cellars under the Old Town Market Square.

There are also numerous World War II memorials scattered across the voivodeship, including Holocaust memorials and memorials at the sites of German massacres of Poles, including the largest massacres in [Rury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rury,_Lublin), [Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Castle), [Borów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bor%C3%B3w,_Kra%C5%9Bnik_County), [Aleksandrów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr%C3%B3w,_Bi%C5%82goraj_County), [Sochy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochy_massacre), [Smoligów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolig%C3%B3w), [Jamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamy,_Lublin_Voivodeship), [Kumowa Dolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m) and [Olszanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olszanka,_Gmina_Kra%C5%9Bniczyn). ## Cuisine

In addition to traditional nationwide [Polish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine), Lublin Voivodeship is known for its variety of regional and local [traditional foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_food) and drinks, which include especially various [honeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey), meat products (incl. various types of [kiełbasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kie%C5%82basa), [bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon) and [kaszanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaszanka)), beverages and various dishes and meals, officially protected by the [Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development_\(Poland\)).

There are local types of [pierogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierogi), [cakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cake), [pastries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastries) (incl. [pączki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%85czki) and [poppy seed rolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppy_seed_roll)) and [racuchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racuchy).[^cite_note-20] Local specialities include the [meringue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meringue) of [Ostrów Lubelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostr%C3%B3w_Lubelski), cake puffs of Lublin, and [chocolate pralines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate_praline) of Lublin.

Traditional beverages include [nalewki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalewka) from [Janów Lubelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan%C3%B3w_Lubelski), [Kraśnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra%C5%9Bnik), [Opole Lubelskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opole_Lubelskie) and [Gmina Końskowola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_Ko%C5%84skowola), [mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mead_in_Poland), [raspberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raspberry) vodka from Kraśnik[^cite_note-21] and [cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider) from [Mikołajówka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko%C5%82aj%C3%B3wka,_Lublin_Voivodeship). Traditional non-alcoholic drinks include various types of juices, tea, [syrups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrup), and [hot chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_chocolate) from Lublin.

## Previous Lublin Voivodeships

### Lublin Voivodeship 1474–1795

Further information: [Lublin Voivodeship (1474–1795)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Voivodeship_\(1474%E2%80%931795\))



Lublin Voivodeship within the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth)

Lublin Voivodeship ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Palatinatus Lublinensis*; [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Województwo Lubelskie*) was an administrative region of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) created in 1474 out of parts of [Sandomierz Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandomierz_Voivodeship) and lasting until the [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) in 1795. It was part of the *[prowincja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prowincja)* of [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland).

### Lublin Voivodeship 1816–1837

Lublin Voivodeship was one of the [voivodeships of Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_Congress_Poland). It was formed in 1816 from [Lublin Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Department), and in 1837 was transformed into [Lublin Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Governorate).

### Lublin Voivodeship 1919–1939

Further information: [Lublin Voivodeship (1919–1939)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Voivodeship_\(1919%E2%80%931939\))

&#x20;Lublin Voivodeship (*Województwo Lubelskie*) was one of the administrative regions of the interwar [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic). In early 1939 its area was 26,555 square kilometres (10,253 sq mi) and its population was 2,116,200.[^cite_note-24] According to the 1931 census, 85.1% of its population was Polish, 10.5% Jewish, and 3% Ukrainian.



Lublin Voivodeship 1975–1998

### Lublin Voivodeship 1945–1975

Lublin Voivodeship (*województwo lubelskie*) was an administrative region of Poland between 1945 and 1975. In 1975 it was transformed into [Chełm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82m_Voivodeship), [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamo%C5%9B%C4%87_Voivodeship), [Biała Podlaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska_Voivodeship), [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg_Voivodeship) and [Siedlce Voivodeships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siedlce_Voivodeship) and a smaller Lublin Voivodeship.

### Lublin Voivodeship 1975–1998

Lublin Voivodeship (*województwo lubelskie*) existed as one of Poland's 49 voivodeships from 1975 until 1998, when it was incorporated into the current (larger) Lublin Voivodeship.
