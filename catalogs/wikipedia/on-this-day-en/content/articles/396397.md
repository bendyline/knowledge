[Turkey](11125639.md) is a [presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) with a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). Major parties are defined as political parties that received more than 7% of the votes in the latest general election and/or represented in parliament. Minor parties are defined as political parties that have fulfilled the requirements of the [Supreme Election Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Election_Council_\(Turkey\)) (*Yüksek Seçim Kurulu* in Turkish, abbreviated as YSK) and don't have any representatives in the parliament. Forming a political party without prior permission is a [constitutional right](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Turkey_\(2017\)#Chapter_Four:_Political_Rights_and_Duties), but the [Interior Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Turkey\)) may delay registering a new party for years,[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] so the party cannot stand in elections.[^cite_note-3]

If the ID and serial number of a person's [Turkish identity card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_identity_card) is known, anyone can query their political party membership via the website of the General Prosecution Office of the [Supreme Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_\(Turkey\)) or mobile phone messages.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]
