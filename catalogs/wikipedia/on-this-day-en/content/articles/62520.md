**Southern California** (commonly shortened to **SoCal**) is a [geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic) and [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_area) [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_California) that generally comprises the southern portion of the U.S. state of [California](5407.md). Its densely populated coastal region includes [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) (the second-most populous [urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration) in the [United States](3434750.md))[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] and [San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County) (the second-most populous county in California). The region generally contains ten of California's 58 counties: [Los Angeles](79734.md), [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California), [Orange](45330.md), [Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_County,_California), [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County,_California), [Kern](80557.md), [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California), [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County,_California), [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County,_California), and [Imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_County,_California) counties.

Although geographically smaller than [Northern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_California) in land area, Southern California has a higher population, with 23.76 million residents as of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census). The sparsely populated [desert region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_of_California) of California occupies a significant portion of the area: the [Colorado Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Desert), along with the [Colorado River](180968.md), is located on Southern California's eastern border with [Arizona](21883824.md), and the [Mojave Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert) shares a border with [Nevada](21216.md) to the northeast. Southern California's southern border with [Baja California](199793.md) is part of the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border).

## Constituent metropolitan areas

Southern California encompasses eight metropolitan areas (MSAs), three of which together form the [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Combined_Statistical_Areas) (CSA) with over 18 million people, the second-biggest CSA after [the New York CSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area). These three MSAs are the [Los Angeles metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area) ([Los Angeles](79734.md) and [Orange](45330.md) counties, with 13.3 million people), the [Inland Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire) ([Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_County,_California) and [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County,_California) counties, including the [Coachella Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Valley) cities, with 4.3 million people), and the [Oxnard–Thousand Oaks–Ventura metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard%E2%80%93Thousand_Oaks%E2%80%93Ventura_metropolitan_area) (0.8 million people). In addition, Southern California contains the [San Diego metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_metropolitan_area) with 3.3 million people, [Bakersfield](80557.md) metro area with 0.9 million, and the [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County,_California), [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County), and [El Centro (Imperial County)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_County,_California) metropolitan areas.

The **Southern California** **[Megaregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaregions_of_the_United_States)** (or [megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis)) is larger still, extending northeast into [Las Vegas](47737.md), [Nevada](21216.md) and south across the [Mexican](3966054.md) border into [Tijuana](101739.md).[^cite_note-6]

## Significance



[San Diego Marina district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina,_San_Diego,_California)



Sunset in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice,_California), a neighborhood in Los Angeles

Within Southern California are two major cities, Los Angeles and San Diego, as well as three of the [country's largest metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_metropolitan_areas).[^cite_note-7] With a population of approximately 4 million, Los Angeles is the most populous city in California and the second most populous in the United States. South of Los Angeles and with a population of approximately 1.4 million is San Diego, the second most populous city in the state and the eighth most populous in the nation.



[Three Arch Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Arch_Bay) in [Laguna Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Beach)

The counties of [Los Angeles](79734.md), [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California), [Orange](45330.md), [Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_County,_California), and [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County,_California) are the five most populous in the state, and are among the top 15 most populous counties in the United States.[^cite_note-8]

The [motion picture](418334.md), [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_United_States) and [music industry](171080.md) are centered in the Los Angeles area in Southern California. [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), a district of Los Angeles, gives its name to the American motion picture industry, which is synonymous with the neighborhood name. Headquartered in Southern California are [The Walt Disney Company](37398.md) (which owns [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)), [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures), [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures), [MGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer), [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures), and [Warner Bros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros.). Universal, Warner Bros., and Sony also run major record companies.

Southern California is also home to a large surf and skateboard culture. Companies such as [Vans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vans), [Volcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcom), [Quiksilver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiksilver), [No Fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Fear), [Stüssy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%BCssy), and [Body Glove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_Glove) are all headquartered there. Skateboarder [Tony Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Hawk); surfers [Rob Machado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Machado), [Timmy Curran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timmy_Curran), [Bobby Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Martinez), [Pat O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_O'Connell_\(surfer\)), [Dane Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Reynolds), and [Chris Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Ward_\(surfer\)) live in Southern California. Some of the most famous surf locations are in Southern California as well, including [Trestles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trestles_\(surfing\)), [Rincon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rincon_\(surfspot\)), [The Wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedge_\(surfing\)), [Huntington Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach,_California), and [Malibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California). Some of the world's largest [action sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_sports) events, including the [X Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Games),[^cite_note-9] [Boost Mobile Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASP_World_Tour),[^cite_note-10] and the [U.S. Open of Surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_of_Surfing), are held in Southern California. The region is also important to the world of [yachting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yachting) with premier events including the annual [Transpacific Yacht Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transpacific_Yacht_Race), or *Transpac*, from Los Angeles to [Hawaii](13270.md). [San Diego Yacht Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Yacht_Club) hosted the three [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup) races from 1988 to 1995. The first modern-era [triathlon](31524.md) was held in San Diego's [Mission Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Bay,_San_Diego,_California) in 1974. Since then, Southern California, and San Diego in particular, have become a mecca for [triathlon](31524.md) and multi-sport racing, products, and culture.

Southern California has multiple sports franchises and networks, such as [Fox Sports Net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_Net).

Many of these locals and tourists frequent the [Southern California coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_\(California\)) for its beaches. Some of Southern California's most popular beaches are [Malibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California), [Laguna Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Beach), [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla), [Manhattan Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Beach,_California), and [Hermosa Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermosa_Beach). Southern California is also known for its mountain resort communities, such as [Big Bear Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bear_Lake,_California), [Lake Arrowhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Arrowhead,_California), and [Wrightwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrightwood), and their ski resorts, like [Bear Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Mountain_\(ski_area\)), [Snow Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_Summit), [Snow Valley Mountain Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_Valley_Mountain_Resort), and [Mountain High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_High). The inland desert city of [Palm Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs) is also popular.[^cite_note-11]

## Northern boundary



California counties below the latitude 35°45'N

Southern California is generally considered the area of California south of the latitude 35°45'N,[^cite_note-12] approximately one-third of the state, formed by the northern boundaries of [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County,_California), [Kern](80557.md), and [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County,_California) counties, which are not exactly a straight line.[^cite_note-13] Another definition for Southern California uses [Point Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Conception) and the [Tehachapi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehachapi_Mountains) as the northern geographical barriers, especially when defining California's [bioregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioregion).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In this definition, [Owens Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owens_Valley) and [Death Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Valley) are part of the Southern California desert system.[^cite_note-16] Because of the barrier formed by the Tehachapi and [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) ranges, cartographer [George Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wheeler_\(explorer\)) observed in 1876 that Northern California was better connected to Oregon and Nevada than it was to Southern California.[^cite_note-Wheeler1876-17]



The [Tehachapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehachapi_Mountains) and [Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) mountains formed a significant barrier to human travel before highways were built,

[^cite_note-Wheeler1876-17]

&#x20;and continue to affect weather, plants and animals.

Following the acquisition of the territory of California by the United States, several pro-slavery politicians attempted to arrange the division of Alta California at 36 degrees, 30 minutes, the [line of the Missouri Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Compromise_line). Instead, the passing of the [Compromise of 1850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1850) enabled California to be admitted to the Union as a [free state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_and_free_states), preventing the southern half of California from becoming its own separate [slave state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_state).[^cite_note-18]



The Pico Act of 1859 proposed to divide California to create the [Territory of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Colorado_\(California\)) in the south

Subsequently, southern Californians dissatisfied with inequitable taxes and land laws attempted several times in the 1850s to achieve a separate statehood or territorial status [separate from northern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_and_secession_in_California). The only successful proposal, the Pico Act of 1859, was passed by the [California State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Legislature) and signed by State Governor [John B. Weller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Weller).[^cite_note-19] It was approved overwhelmingly by nearly 75 percent of southern California voters to form the proposed [Territory of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Colorado_\(California\)). The Pico Act bisected California starting at a latitude of six standard parallels south of the [Mount Diablo meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Diablo_meridian), then ducking southward around part of the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_\(California\)) before cutting northeast to the California border.[^cite_note-20] Six standard parallels is 144 miles south of [Mount Diablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Diablo)—which corresponds to the current northern border of [San Luis Obispo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County), at the latitude 35°45'N. The proposal was sent to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), with a strong advocate in Senator [Milton Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Latham). However, the secession crisis following the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._presidential_election,_1860) of [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) in 1860 and the subsequent [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) led to the proposal never coming to a vote.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In 1900, the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* defined Southern California as including "the seven counties of Los Angeles, San Bernardino, Orange, Riverside, San Diego, Ventura, and Santa Barbara." This definition left out San Luis Obispo and Kern counties. (At this time, Imperial County was part of San Diego County, not becoming its own county until three years later, in 1903.)[^cite_note-23]

Southern California was the name of a [proposed new state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cal_3) which failed to get on the 2018 California ballot. The ballot measure proposed splitting the existing state into three parts.[^cite_note-24]

In December 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the state government led by Governor [Gavin Newsom](418947.md) divided the state into five regions for the purpose of issuing stay-at-home orders. The Southern California region consisted of the following counties: [Imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_County,_California), [Inyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inyo_County,_California), Los Angeles, [Mono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mono_County,_California), [Orange](45330.md), [Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_County,_California), [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County,_California), San Diego, [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County,_California), [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County,_California), and [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California). However, Kern County was grouped with other counties of the [San Joaquin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley), California's central agricultural valley.[^cite_note-25]

Southern California consists of a heavily developed urban environment, home to some of the largest [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urbanized_areas_in_California_\(by_population\)) in the state, along with the [Deserts of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_of_California) (part of which was even [proposed to become a new county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Desert_County,_California) due to cultural, economic and geographic differences relative to the rest of the more urban region)[^cite_note-proposal1998-37][^cite_note-goforit-38][^cite_note-Wilson-39] that have been left undeveloped. It is the third most populated [megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis) in the United States, after the [Great Lakes megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_megalopolis) and the [Northeast megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_megalopolis). Much of Southern California is famous for its large, spread-out, suburban communities and use of automobiles and highways. The dominant areas are Los Angeles, Orange County, San Diego, and [Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside,_California)–[San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino), each of which are the centers of their respective metropolitan areas, composed of numerous smaller cities and communities. The urban area is also host to an international [San Diego–Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego%E2%80%93Tijuana) metropolitan region, created by the urban area spilling over into [Baja California](199793.md).

The main barrier to urbanization along the [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_California) corridor is [Camp Pendleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Pendleton). The cities and communities along [Interstate 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_15_in_California) and [Interstate 215](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_215_\(California\)) are so interrelated that [Temecula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temecula) and [Murrieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrieta) have as much connection with the [San Diego metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_metropolitan_area) as they do with the [Inland Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire). To the east, the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) considers the San Bernardino and Riverside County areas, [Riverside-San Bernardino area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside-San_Bernardino_area) as a separate [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) from Los Angeles County. Newly developed [exurbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurbs) formed in the [Antelope Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope_Valley), north of Los Angeles, the [Victor Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Valley), and the [Coachella Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Valley) with the [Imperial Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Valley). Also, population growth was high in the [Bakersfield-Kern County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield,_California), [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_California), and [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo) areas.



The skyline of [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles) as seen at sunset in October 2006. Standing 1,018 ft (310 m) high, with 73 floors, the [U.S. Bank Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bank_Tower_\(Los_Angeles\)) was the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States)'s tallest building when it was built in 1989, until the neighboring [Wilshire Grand Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire_Grand_Center) surpassed it in 2017.

## Climate

See also: [Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_forests,_woodlands,_and_scrub)



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Southern California

Most of Southern California has a [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate)-like climate, with warm and dry summers, mild and wet winters, where cool weather and freezing temperatures are rare. Southern California contains other types of climates, including [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid), [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) and [mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_climate), with infrequent rain and many sunny days. Summers are hot or warm, and dry, while winters are mild, and rainfall is low to moderate depending on the area. Rain is infrequent, but is often heavy when it does occur, making [flash floods](244391.md) an aspect of living in Southern California. This climatic pattern was alluded to in the hit song "[It Never Rains in Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Never_Rains_in_Southern_California)". While snow is very rare in lower elevations, mountains above 5,000 feet (1,500 m) receive plentiful snowfall in the winter.

Since the first decade of the 21st century, [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_in_California) and [wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_California_wildfires) have increased in frequency as a result of [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

Southern California consists of one of the more varied collections of geologic, topographic, and natural ecosystem landscapes in a diversity outnumbering other major regions in the state and country. The region spans from Pacific Ocean [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands_of_California), [shorelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoreline), beaches, and [coastal plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plain), through the [Transverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_Ranges) and [Peninsular Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Ranges) with their peaks, and into the large and small interior valleys, to the vast [deserts of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_of_California).

#### Earthquakes

Each year, Southern California has about 10,000 earthquakes. Nearly all of them are too small to be felt. Only several hundred have been greater than [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) (M<sub>w</sub>) 3.0, and only about 15–20 have been greater than M<sub>w</sub> 4.0.[^cite_note-42] California as a whole enacted the [Alquist Priolo Special Studies Zone Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alquist_Priolo_Special_Studies_Zone_Act) in the wake of the [1971 San Fernando earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_San_Fernando_earthquake). The act prohibits new construction of residential buildings closer than 50 feet (15 m) from a surface rupturing active fault zone. In addition, the act improved safety by requiring new structures (both residential and commercial) to be seismically retrofitted. It also required existing infrastructure to comply.

Since 1972, numerous large magnitude earthquakes have struck Southern California with little widespread damage in part due to the act. However, exceptions can be noted for epicenters that lie directly on top of densely populated regions such as the M<sub>w</sub> 6.7 [1994 Northridge Earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_Earthquake) and, to a lesser extent, the smaller M<sub>w</sub> 5.5 [2008 Chino Hills earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Chino_Hills_earthquake). The Northridge earthquake occurred on a blind-thrust fault directly underneath the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley), which until the earthquake was previously undiscovered. [Seismic retrofitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_retrofit) of existing and new construction is aimed to prevent damage and save lives in the aftermath of a major quake, but it cannot guarantee that buildings will be unscathed if the epicenter is relatively close by.

The [1994 Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_earthquake) was particularly destructive, causing a substantial number of deaths, injuries, and structural collapses. The quake caused the most property damage of any earthquake in U.S. history at an estimated \$20 billion.[^cite_note-43]

Many Southern California [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) are able to produce a M<sub>w</sub> 6.7 earthquake or greater, such as the [San Andreas Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andreas_Fault), which can produce M<sub>w</sub> 8.0 or greater. The largest known earthquake in California was the [1857 Fort Tejon earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1857_Fort_Tejon_earthquake) that ruptured 200+ miles (320+ kilometers) of the San Andreas Fault from [Parkfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkfield,_California) to [Wrightwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrightwood,_California). With a recurrence interval of roughly 150 years, this part of the San Andreas fault is well within its window to produce another large earthquake. Along with the southern section of the San Andreas (in the [Palm Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs,_California) region, which has not ruptured in \~400 years), the entire Southern California portion of the San Andreas Fault is ready to produce a powerful earthquake in the near future.

Notable faults capable of large magnitude events include the [San Jacinto Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jacinto_Fault_Zone) (a splay of the San Andreas that runs directly under the I-10 & I-215 interchange), the [Newport–Inglewood-Rose Canyon Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport%E2%80%93Inglewood_Fault) (located adjacent to [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) and responsible for [Signal Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Hill,_California)), the [Elsinore Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsinore_Fault_Zone) (created [Lake Elsinore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Elsinore)), the [Garlock Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlock_Fault) (which marks boundary between of the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) and the [Mojave Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert)), and the [Hollywood fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_fault) (which is within feet of [Capitol Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records) and is roughly parallel to [Hollywood Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard)).

The [United States Geological Survey](23814944.md) (USGS) has released a California earthquake forecast,[^cite_note-44] which models earthquake occurrence in California.

Ethnic origins in Southern California



[Downtown San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Bernardino)

As of the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Southern California has a population of 23,762,904. Despite a reputation for high growth rates, Southern California's population has grown slower than the state average since the 2000s. This is due to California's growth becoming concentrated in the northern part of the state as result of a stronger, tech-oriented economy in the [Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) and an emerging [Greater Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Sacramento) region.

Southern California consists of one [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area), eight [Metropolitan Statistical Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Statistical_Area), one international [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), and multiple metropolitan divisions. The region is home to two extended metropolitan areas that exceed five million in population. These are the [Greater Los Angeles Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Area) at 17,786,419, and [San Diego–Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego%E2%80%93Tijuana) at 5,105,768.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Of these metropolitan areas, the [Los Angeles-Long Beach-Santa Ana metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles-Long_Beach-Santa_Ana_metropolitan_area), [Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside-San_Bernardino-Ontario_metropolitan_area), and [Oxnard-Thousand Oaks-Ventura metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard-Thousand_Oaks-Ventura_metropolitan_area) form Greater Los Angeles;[^cite_note-50] while the [El Centro metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Centro_metropolitan_area) and [San Diego-Carlsbad-San Marcos metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego-Carlsbad-San_Marcos_metropolitan_area) form the [Southern Border Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Border_Region_\(California\)).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] North of Greater Los Angeles are the [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County,_California), [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County,_California), and [Bakersfield](80557.md) metropolitan areas.

Los Angeles (with a population of approximately 3.9 million people) and San Diego (at nearly 1.4 million people) are the two largest cities in all of California, and are among the top eight largest cities in the United States.[^cite_note-53] In Southern California, there are also 14 cities with more than 200,000 residents and 48 cities over 100,000 residents. Many of Southern California's most developed cities lie along or in close proximity to the coast, with the exception of San Bernardino and Riverside.

## Economy

### Industries

Southern California is one of the largest economies in the United States. It is dominated by, and heavily dependent upon, the abundance of petroleum, as opposed to other regions where automobiles are not nearly as dominant, due to the vast majority of transport that runs on this fuel. Southern California is famous for tourism and the [entertainment industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_industry). Other industries include software, automotive, aerospace, finance, biomedical, ports and regional logistics. The region was a leader in the housing bubble from 2001 to 2007, and has been heavily impacted by the housing crash.

Since the 1920s, motion pictures, petroleum, and aircraft manufacturing have been major industries. In one of the richest agricultural regions in the U.S., cattle and citrus were major industries until farmlands were turned into suburbs. Although military spending cutbacks have had an impact, aerospace continues to be a major factor.[^cite_note-54]

### Major central business districts



[Taco Bell](37563.md) Headquarters in [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California)

Southern California is home to many major business districts. Central business districts (CBD) include [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), [Downtown Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Riverside), [Downtown San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Bernardino), [downtown San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego), and the [South Coast Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Metro). Within the Los Angeles Area are the major business districts of [Downtown Pasadena](92408.md), [Downtown Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California), [Downtown Santa Monica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California), [Downtown Glendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_California) and [Downtown Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Long_Beach). Los Angeles proper has many business districts, such as [Downtown LA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles) and those lining [Wilshire Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire_Boulevard), including [Mid-Wilshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Wilshire), the [Miracle Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_Mile,_Los_Angeles), [Downtown Beverly Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Beverly_Hills), and [Westwood](374055.md); others include [Century City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_City,_Los_Angeles) and [Warner Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Center,_Los_Angeles) in the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley). The area of Santa Monica and Venice (and perhaps some of Culver City) is informally referred to as "Silicon Beach" because of the concentration of financial and marketing technology-centric firms located in the region.

The [San Bernardino-Riverside Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino-Riverside_Area) maintains the business districts of [Downtown San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Bernardino), [Hospitality Business/Financial Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality_Lane_District,_San_Bernardino,_California), [University District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_District,_San_Bernardino) which are in the cities of San Bernardino and Riverside.

In Orange County, has highly developed suburban business centers (also known as [edge cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_cities)) including the [Anaheim–Santa Ana edge city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim%E2%80%93Santa_Ana_edge_city) along I-5; and another, the [South Coast Plaza–John Wayne Airport edge city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Plaza%E2%80%93John_Wayne_Airport_edge_city) that stretches from the [South Coast Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Metro) to the [Irvine Business Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Business_Complex); [Newport Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Center,_Newport_Beach,_California); and [Irvine Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Spectrum). [Downtown Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Santa_Ana) is an important government, arts and entertainment, and retail district.

[Downtown San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego) is the CBD of San Diego, though the city is filled with business districts. These include [Carmel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel_Valley,_San_Diego), [Del Mar Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Mar_Heights,_San_Diego), [Mission Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Valley), [Rancho Bernardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Bernardo), [Sorrento Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento_Mesa), and [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City,_San_Diego). Most of these districts are located in Northern San Diego and some within [North County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_County_\(San_Diego_area\)) regions.

Southern California is home to [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md), the second-busiest airport in the United States by passenger volume (see [World's busiest airports by passenger traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic)) and the third-busiest by international passenger volume (see [Busiest airports in the United States by international passenger traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busiest_airports_in_the_United_States_by_international_passenger_traffic)); [San Diego International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_International_Airport), the busiest single-runway airport in the United States; [Van Nuys Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Nuys_Airport), the world's busiest [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airport; major commercial airports at [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_International_Airport), [Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport), [Bakersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadows_Field), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA/Ontario_International_Airport), [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Burbank_Airport), and [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Airport); and numerous smaller commercial and general aviation airports.

Six of the seven lines of the [commuter rail](6286.md) system, [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)), run out of downtown Los Angeles, connecting Los Angeles, [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California), San Bernardino, Riverside, Orange, and San Diego counties with the other line connecting San Bernardino, Riverside, and Orange counties directly.

Southern California is also home to the [Port of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Los_Angeles), the country's busiest commercial port; the adjacent [Port of Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Long_Beach), the country's second busiest container port; and the [Port of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_San_Diego).
