[Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) defeated two-time defending champion [Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md)[^cite_note-1] in the final, 4–6, 6–4, 6–4, 6–4 to win the gentlemen's singles tennis title at the [2025 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] It was his first [Wimbledon](91195.md) title and fourth [major](197638.md#Tournaments) title overall.[^cite_note-carlos-4] With the win, Sinner ended Alcaraz's undefeated 5–0 record in major finals,[^cite_note-carlos-4] and became the first Italian to win a Wimbledon singles title.[^cite_note-5]

At 23 years, 331 days old, Sinner was the second-youngest man to reach the final at all four majors after [Jim Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Courier) at 22 years, 321 days old, a record that was later broken by Alcaraz at the [2026 Australian Open](81715568.md) at 22 years, 272 days old.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Sinner and Alcaraz joined [Roger Federer](262376.md) and [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) as the only men's pair to contest both the [French Open](147724.md) and Wimbledon finals in the same season.[^cite_note-8]

With his third round win, [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) recorded his 100th match win at Wimbledon and became the second man to record 100 or more wins at two majors (after Federer), in addition to the French Open.[^cite_note-9] Djokovic made his 19th career third round appearance and 14th semifinal appearance at Wimbledon, the most by any man in the Open Era.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] His defeat to Sinner in the semifinals marked the first time since [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) that Djokovic did not reach the final, ending a streak of six consecutive final appearances.[^cite_note-12] It also marked the first instance where none of the "[Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_career_statistics)" were present in a Wimbledon final since [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), ending their streak at 21-consecutive finals, of which 9 featured both finalists from the Big Four.[^cite_note-13]

13 out of the 32 seeds lost in the first round, the most to do so at Wimbledon in the Open Era,[^cite_note-14] and tied the record set for any major at the [2004 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-15]

The event marked the final professional appearance of former world No. 9 [Fabio Fognini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Fognini). He lost in the first round to Alcaraz.[^cite_note-16]
