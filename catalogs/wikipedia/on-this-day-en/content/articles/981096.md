# Flotilla

This article is about the naval term. For the 2010 video game, see [Flotilla (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flotilla_\(video_game\)).



A Spanish flotilla being engaged by the [Royal Navy](26061.md) in the [action of 5 October 1804](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_of_5_October_1804)

A **flotilla** (from [Spanish](26825.md), meaning a small *flota* ([fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_fleet)) of ships), or **naval flotilla**, is a [formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_formation) of small ships that may be part of a larger fleet.

## Composition

A flotilla is usually composed of a homogeneous group of the same [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_class) of warship, such as [frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigates), [destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyers), [torpedo boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boats), [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarines), [gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboats), or [minesweepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minesweeper_\(ship\)). Groups of larger warships are usually called [squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(naval\)), but similar units of non-capital ships may be called squadrons in some instances, and flotillas in others. Formations including more than one [capital ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_ship), e.g. [men-of-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-of-war), [battleships](4054.md), and [aircraft carriers](2219.md), typically alongside smaller ships and support craft, are typically called [fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_fleet), each portion led by a capital ship being a squadron or [task force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_force).&#x20;

A flotilla is usually commanded by a [rear admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_admiral), a [commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) or a [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(naval\)), depending on the importance of the command (a [vice admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral) would normally command a squadron). A flotilla is often divided into two or more [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(naval\)), each of which might be commanded by the most senior [commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander), nearly always a [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_\(navy\)) at the very least. A flotilla is often, but not necessarily, a permanent formation.

In modern navies, flotillas have tended to become administrative units containing several [squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(naval\)).[^cite_note-1] As warships have grown larger, the term squadron has gradually replaced the term flotilla for formations of destroyers, frigates and submarines in many navies.

A naval flotilla has no direct equivalent on land, but is, perhaps, the rough equivalent in tactical value of a [brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) or [regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment).

## Specific usage

### United States Coast Guard Auxiliary

In the [United States Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary), a flotilla is the basic organizational unit and consists of members at a local level where the majority of the work of the auxiliary is done. A flotilla is led by an elected flotilla commander assisted by an elected vice flotilla commander, who is in turn assisted by appointed flotilla staff officers.[^cite_note-2] A Coast Guard Auxiliary division consists of multiple flotillas and a district consists of multiple divisions. Auxiliary districts are organized along Coast Guard district lines and are administered by a Coast Guard officer (usually a [commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(United_States\)) or [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(United_States_O-6\))) who is called the "director of the auxiliary".[^cite_note-3]

### Russian and Soviet navies

In the [Imperial Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Navy), [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy), and [Russian Federation Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation_Navy), the word *flotilla* (флотилия, *flotilliya*) has tended to be used for "[brown-water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-water_navy)" naval units – those operating not on the oceans and real seas, but on inland seas or rivers. Among the former are the present-day [Caspian Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Flotilla), the early-20th-century [Satakundskaya Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satakundskaya_Flotilla), or the [Aral Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Flotilla?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the 1850s;[^cite_note-4] among the latter, the [Don Military Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Military_Flotilla) (which was created several times over more than 200 years), the [Dnieper Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Flotilla) (also extant in the 18th and 20th centuries), the Red [Volga Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Flotilla?action=edit\&redlink=1), which participated in the [Kazan Operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Operation) during the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), and the [Danube Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Flotilla_\(Soviet_Union\)). In the 18th century, the term also applied to the comparatively small fleets operating on those seas where Russia did not have much naval presence yet, e.g. the [Okhotsk Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhotsk_Flotilla?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### Non-military usage

The word flotilla has also been used at times to refer to a small fleet of vessels, commercial or otherwise.[^cite_note-5] There is also such a thing as a "flotilla holiday", which is a group of chartered yachts that set sail together on the same route. Also outside of a military context, the [Center for International Maritime Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_International_Maritime_Security), an open-membership Naval Strategy [think tank](37101.md) based in the [United States](3434750.md), maintains a similar use of the word Flotilla to that of the United States Coast Guard Auxiliary. In this context, Flotilla refers to a specialized sub-group of individuals within the broader organization, such as the Center's Warfighting Flotilla.[^cite_note-6]
