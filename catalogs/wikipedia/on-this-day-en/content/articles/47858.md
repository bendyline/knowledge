The **Arabian Peninsula**,[^cite_note-3] or simply **Arabia**, is a [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia). It accounts for the majority of the land situated on the [Arabian plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_plate).[^cite_note-arabia-4] With an area of 3,237,500 km<sup>2</sup> (1.25 million mi<sup>2</sup>), it is the world's largest peninsula—roughly comparable in size to [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Nine countries are located on the Arabian Peninsula: [Bahrain](18933277.md),[^cite_note-AP_1-1] [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Oman](22316.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), and [Yemen](350939.md), as well as the southern halves of [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Jordan](7515964.md).[^cite_note-10]

Geographically, the Arabian Peninsula is bounded by [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) and the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) to the north and northwest and surrounded by the [Indian Ocean](14580.md): the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), and the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) to the east and northeast; the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the southeast; and the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md) to the south, the [Strait of Mandeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Mandeb) to the southwest, and the [Red Sea](26191.md) to the west. Prior to the 7th century AD, [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) conceptions of "Arabia" were broader than the modern peninsula and extended into the [Southern Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Levant), including the [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), the [Negev](142273.md), and regions of modern [Jordan](7515964.md), as reflected in the Roman province of [Arabia Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea), whose capital was [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra). In this context, Sinai formed part of a wider regional classification rather than an isolated extension of the peninsula, despite being partly separated from it by the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba) and situated on the [African plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_plate).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Most of the peninsula is covered by the [Arabian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Desert), which is an extension of the [Sahara Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_Desert).

Between 56 and 23 million years ago, the peninsula was formed as a result of the [Red Sea Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_Rift) involving the African and Arabian plates. It was among the [first regions to be occupied by modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Arabia) following their [departure from Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans) during the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic). The historical record of the Arabian Peninsula is thought to have begun in the early 1st millennium BC, when writing systems were [introduced to the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing). For much of the [peninsula's ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Islamic_Arabia), it was sparsely populated by [Arab tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribes_of_Arabia) and home to a variety of [local and foreign religious practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_pre-Islamic_Arabia), while numerous empires and kingdoms exercised limited political authority in different areas. However, in the 7th century AD, the entire Arabian Peninsula was politically, and religiously united by [Prophet Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad).

While the [Greco-Roman world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) had known the Arabian Peninsula in three large regions—[Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea) for the north, [Deserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Deserta) for the centre, and [Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Felix) for the south—[medieval Muslim geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_and_cartography_in_the_medieval_Islamic_world) instead divided it into four main regions: the Central Plateau ([Najd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najd) and [Al-Yamama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yamama)), [South Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia) ([Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_\(region\)), [Hadhramaut](325752.md), and southwestern Oman), [Al-Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Arabia) (Eastern Arabia or [Al-Hassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahsa_Oasis)), and the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) ([Tihamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihamah) for the western coast).[^cite_note-13] Since the 20th century, it has been of critical significance in both Arab and global geopolitics due to the discovery and subsequent industrialization of vast reserves of [oil and natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_natural_gas_industry). Most of the peninsula's countries are [petrocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrocracy) and accordingly play a key role in the [petroleum politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_politics) defining the contemporary [Middle East](19323.md).

## Etymology

Main article: [Etymology of *Arab*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Arab)

In antiquity, the term "Arabia" encompassed a larger area than the current term "Arabian Peninsula" and included the [Arabian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Desert) and large parts of the [Syrian–Arabian desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Desert). During the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), the area was known as *Arabia* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἀραβία). The [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) named three regions "Arabia":&#x20;

- [Arabia Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea) ('Stony Arabia'

  [^cite_note-Arabia_Petraea-14]

  ): it consisted of the former [Nabataean Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabataean_Kingdom) in the southern Levant, the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) and north-western Arabian Peninsula. It was the only one that became a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province), with [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra) (in [Jordan](7515964.md)) as its capital.

- [Arabia Deserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Deserta) ('Desert Arabia'): signified the desert lands of Arabia. As a name for the region, it remained popular into the 19th and 20th centuries, and was used in [Charles M. Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_M._Doughty)'s *[Travels in Arabia Deserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travels_in_Arabia_Deserta)* (1888).

- [Arabia Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Felix) ('Fortunate Arabia'): was used by geographers to describe the [southern part of the Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia), mostly what is now [Yemen](350939.md), which enjoys more rainfall, is much greener than the rest of the peninsula and has long enjoyed much more productive fields.

One of the [nomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nome_\(Egypt\)) of [Ptolemaic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt) was named *Arabia*.[^cite_note-15]

Arabians used a north–south division of Arabia: *ash-Sham* vs. *al-Yaman*, or *Arabia Deserta* vs. *Arabia Felix*. Arabia Felix had originally been used for the whole peninsula, and at other times only for the southern region. Because its use became limited to the south, the whole peninsula was simply called Arabia. Arabia Deserta was the entire desert region extending north from Arabia Felix to Palmyra and the Euphrates, including all the area between Pelusium on the Nile and Babylon. This area was also called Arabia and not sharply distinguished from the peninsula.[^cite_note-16]

The Arabs and the Ottoman Empire considered the west of the Arabian Peninsula region where the Arabs lived 'the land of the Arabs'—bilad **al-'Arab** (Arabia), and its major divisions were the bilad **al-Sham** ([Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\))), bilad **al-Yaman** ([Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia)), and bilad **al-'Iraq** ([Iraq](7515928.md)).[^cite_note-california-17] The Ottomans used the term Arabistan in a broad sense for the region starting from [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia), where the Euphrates river makes its descent into Syria, through [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), and on through the remainder of the Sinai and Arabian peninsulas.[^cite_note-18]

The provinces of Arabia were: al-Tih, the Sinai Peninsula, Hejaz, Asir, Yemen, Hadramaut, Mahra and Shilu, Oman, Hasa, Bahrain, Dahna, Nufud, the Hammad, which included the deserts of Syria, Mesopotamia and Babylonia.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-nie-20]

## History

### Prehistoric Arabia

Main article: [Prehistoric Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Arabia)

[Prehistoric Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Arabia) is the period of the Arabian Peninsula before any written records are known, going back to when humans first began to settle in the region, until around 1000 BC, when systematic written documentation begins to appear in the archaeological record.[^cite_note-21] Stone tools from the [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic) era along with fossils of other animals discovered at Ti's al Ghadah, in north-western Saudi Arabia, might imply that [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominins) migrated through a "Green Arabia" between 300,000 and 500,000 years ago.[^cite_note-22]

Two-hundred-thousand-year-old stone tools were discovered at Shuaib Al-Adgham in the eastern [Al-Qassim Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qassim_Province), which would indicate that many prehistoric sites, located along a network of rivers, had once existed in the area.[^cite_note-23] [Acheulean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulean) tools found in Sadaqah, [Riyadh Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Region) reveal that hominids lived in the Arabian Peninsula around 188,000 years ago.[^cite_note-24] Human habitation in Arabia may have occurred as early as 130,000 years ago.[^cite_note-25] A fossilized *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)* finger bone found at Al Wusta in the [Nefud Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nefud_Desert) dates to approximately 90,000 years ago and is the oldest human fossil discovered outside of Africa and the Levant. This indicates human migrations from Africa to Arabia occurred around this time.[^cite_note-26]

The Arabian Peninsula may have been the homeland of a '[Basal Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_Eurasian)' population, which diverged from other Eurasians soon after the Out-of-Africa migration, and subsequently became isolated, until it started to mix with other populations in the Middle East around 25,000 years ago. These different Middle Eastern populations would later spread Basal Eurasian ancestry via the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution) to all of Western Eurasia.[^cite_note-27]

### Pre-Islamic Arabia

Further information: [Religion in pre-Islamic Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_pre-Islamic_Arabia)



Pre-Islamic Arabia in 1000 BC



Map of the Arabian Peninsula in 600 AD, showing the various [Arab tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribes_of_Arabia) and their areas of settlement. The [Lakhmids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhmid_kingdom) (yellow) formed an Arab monarchy as clients of the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), while the [Ghassanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghassanids) (red) formed an Arab monarchy as clients of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire).

[Archaeology](18951655.md) has revealed the existence of many civilizations in pre-Islamic Arabia (such as the [Thamud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamud)), especially in [South Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] [South Arabian civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabian_kingdoms_in_pre-Islamic_Arabia) include the [Kingdom of Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba), [Awsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awsan), [Ma'in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'in), and [Himyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himyar). From 106 AD to 630 AD, north-western Arabia was under the control of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), which renamed it [Arabia Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea).[^cite_note-30] Central Arabia was the location of the [Kingdom of Kinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kinda) in the 4th, 5th, and early 6th centuries, as well as the [Ma'add](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'add) tribes. Eastern Arabia was home to the [Dilmun civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilmun_civilization). The earliest known events in Arabian history are migrations from the peninsula into neighbouring areas.[^cite_note-31]

The Arabian Peninsula has long been accepted as the original *[Urheimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urheimat)* of the [Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages) by most scholars.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Rise of Islam

Main articles: [Early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) and [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age)



The [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) (661–750)

The seventh century saw the rise of Islam as the peninsula's dominant religion. The [Islamic prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_prophet) [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) was born in Mecca in about 570 and first began preaching in the city in 610, but [migrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijra_\(Islam\)) to [Medina](36636.md) in 622. From there he and his companions united the [tribes of Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribes_of_Arabia) under the banner of [Islam](6037917.md) and created the [first Islamic state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Islamic_state)—a single Arab Muslim religious polity in the Arabian Peninsula.

Under the subsequent [Rashidun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun) and [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad) [Caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate), rapid expansion of Arab power well beyond the Arabian Peninsula formed a vast Muslim Arab Empire with an area of influence that stretched from the north-west [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), across [Central Asia](6742.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), [North Africa](21714.md), southern [Italy](14532.md), and the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), to the [Pyrenees](24707.md).

Following [Muhammad's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad#Death) in 632, disagreement broke out over who would succeed him as leader of the Muslim community. [Umar ibn al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_ibn_al-Khattab), a prominent [companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahaba) of Muhammad, nominated [Abu Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr), who was Muhammad's intimate friend and collaborator. Others added their support and Abu Bakr was made the first [caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph). This choice was disputed by some of Muhammad's companions, who held that [Ali ibn Abi Talib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ibn_Abi_Talib), his cousin and son-in-law, had been designated his successor. Abu Bakr's immediate task was to avenge a recent defeat by [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) (or [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire)) forces, although he first had to put down a rebellion by Arab tribes in an episode known as the [Ridda wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridda_wars), or "Wars of Apostasy".[^cite_note-36]



The Middle East, c. 1190. [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin)'s empire and its vassals shown in red

On his death in 634, he was succeeded by [Umar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar) as caliph, followed by [Uthman ibn al-Affan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman_ibn_al-Affan) and [Ali ibn Abi Talib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ibn_Abi_Talib). The period of these first four caliphs is known as *al-khulafā' ar-rāshidūn*: the [Rashidun or "rightly guided" Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate). Under the Rashidun Caliphs, and, from 661, their [Umayyad successors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate), the Arabs rapidly expanded the territory under Muslim control outside of Arabia. In a matter of decades Muslim armies decisively defeated the [Byzantine army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and destroyed the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire), [conquering huge swathes of territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) from the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) to India. The political focus of the Muslim world then shifted to the newly conquered territories.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-L._Gardet-38]

Nevertheless, [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) and [Medina](36636.md) remained the most spiritually important places in the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world). The [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an) requires every able-bodied Muslim who can afford it, as one of the [five pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_pillars_of_Islam), to make a pilgrimage, or [Hajj](23487999.md), to Mecca during the [Islamic month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) of [Dhu al-Hijjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhu_al-Hijjah) at least once in his or her lifetime.[^cite_note-39] The [Masjid al-Haram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid_al-Haram) (the Grand Mosque) in Mecca is the location of the [Kaaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaaba), Islam's holiest site, and the [Masjid al-Nabawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid_al-Nabawi) (the Prophet's Mosque) in Medina is the location of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad)'s grave; as a result, from the 7th century, Mecca and Medina became the pilgrimage destinations for large numbers of Muslims from across the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world).[^cite_note-40]

### Middle Ages



[Portuguese colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in Arabia

Despite its spiritual importance, in political terms, Arabia soon became a peripheral region of the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world), in which the most important [medieval Islamic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) were based at various times in far-away cities such as [Damascus](8914.md), [Baghdad](4492.md), and [Cairo](6293.md). However, from the 10th century (and, in fact, until the 20th century) the [Hashemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemite) [Sharifs of Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharifs_of_Mecca) maintained a state in the most developed part of the region, the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz). Their domain originally comprised only the holy cities of Mecca and Medina but in the 13th century it was extended to include the rest of the Hejaz. Although, the Sharifs exercised at most times independent authority in the Hejaz, they were usually subject to the [suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty) of one of the major Islamic empires of the time. In the Middle Ages, these included the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasids) of Baghdad, and the [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids), [Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubids), and [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluks) of [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_Online:_History_of_Arabia-41]

### Modern history



The expansion of the [first Saudi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Diriyah) from 1744 to 1814



The Arabian Peninsula in 1914



Territorial evolution of the [Third Saudi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Saudi_State) (1902–1934)

The provincial Ottoman Army for Arabia (Arabistan Ordusu) was headquartered in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\)), which included Palestine, the Transjordan region in addition to Lebanon ([Mount Lebanon was, however, a semi-autonomous mutasarrifate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon_Mutasarrifate)). It was put in charge of Syria, Cilicia, Iraq, and the remainder of the Arabian Peninsula.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The Ottomans never had any control over central Arabia, also known as the [Najd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najd) region.

Historically, the [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) Bedouin tribes of the Arabian Peninsula engaged in frequent inter-tribal [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_warfare), conducting seasonal [raids](6873191.md) (known as *ghazu*) against rival clans as well as merchant and pilgrimage caravans over access to scarce pasturages and water resources.[^cite_note-44] One of the most significant such incidents occurred during the [1757 Hajj caravan raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1757_Hajj_caravan_raid).[^cite_note-45] Another prominent case was the long-standing rivalry between the [Shammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shammar) and [Anizah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anizah) tribal confederations across northern Arabia and the Syrian Desert.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-Pahlitzsch-47] During the late eighteenth century, these conflicts, together with the rise of the Wahhabi movement, contributed to the [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-modern_human_migration) of a major Shammar faction under the Al Jarba family across the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) into Upper Mesopotamia, where it later emerged as a dominant tribal force.[^cite_note-Pahlitzsch-47]

The emergence of what was to become the Saudi royal family, known as the [Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saud), began in [Najd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najd) in central Arabia in 1744, when [Muhammad bin Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Saud), founder of the dynasty, joined forces with the religious leader [Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Abd_al-Wahhab), founder of the [Wahhabi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabi_movement), a strict puritanical form of Sunni Islam.[^cite_note-48] The [Emirate of Diriyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Diriyah) established in the area around Riyadh rapidly expanded and briefly controlled most of the present-day territory of Saudi Arabia, [sacking Karbala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabi_sack_of_Karbala) in 1802, and [capturing Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_early_Islamic_heritage_sites_in_Saudi_Arabia) in 1803.[^cite_note-49]

The [Damascus Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus_Protocol) of 1914 provides an illustration of the regional relationships. Arabs living in one of the existing districts of the Arabian peninsula, the Emirate of [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz), asked for a British guarantee of independence. Their proposal included all Arab lands south of a line roughly corresponding to the northern frontiers of present-day Syria and Iraq. They envisioned a new Arab state, or confederation of states, adjoining the southern Arabian Peninsula. It would have comprised [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia)—[İskenderun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0skenderun) and [Mersin](1146241.md), Iraq with Kuwait, Syria, [Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon_Mutasarrifate), Jordan, and [Palestine](241405.md).[^cite_note-50]

In the modern era, the term bilad al-Yaman came to refer specifically to the south-western parts of the peninsula. Arab geographers started to refer to the whole peninsula as 'jazirat al-Arab', or the peninsula of the Arabs.[^cite_note-california-17]

#### Late Ottoman rule and the Hejaz Railway

The railway was started in 1900 at the behest of the Ottoman Sultan [Abdul Hamid II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_II) and was built largely by the [Turks](11125639.md), with [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) advice and support. A public subscription was opened throughout the Islamic world to fund the construction. The railway was to be a [waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf), an inalienable religious endowment or charitable trust.[^cite_note-51]

#### The Arab Revolt and the foundation of Saudi Arabia



Physical and political elements of Arabia in 1929



[Abdulaziz Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud), the founding father and first king of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)

The major developments of the early 20th century were the [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt) during World War I and the subsequent collapse and [partitioning of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioning_of_the_Ottoman_Empire). The Arab Revolt (1916–1918) was initiated by the [Sherif Hussein ibn Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherif_Hussein_ibn_Ali) with the aim of securing independence from the ruling [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and creating a single unified Arab state spanning from [Aleppo](159244.md) in Syria to [Aden](38050.md) in Yemen. During World War I, Sharif Hussein entered into an alliance with the United Kingdom and France against the Ottomans in June 1916.[^cite_note-52]



The Arabian Peninsula in 1923

These events were followed by the foundation of Saudi Arabia under King [Abdulaziz Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud_of_Saudi_Arabia). After the collapse of the [Emirate of Diriyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Diriyah), the House of Saud regrouped and in 1824 founded the [Second Saudi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Saudi_State), which would control most of Arabia for the next two-thirds of a century. Ibn Saud, after his family lost power in 1891, would establish the [Third Saudi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Saudi_State), capturing [Riyadh](51554.md) in 1902, and, successively subduing Al-Hasa, [Jabal Shammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Jabal_Shammar) and [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hejaz) between 1913 and 1926. The Saudis then absorbed the [Emirate of Asir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idrisid_Emirate_of_Asir), with their expansion only ending in 1934 after a [war with Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi%E2%80%93Yemeni_War_\(1934\)).

#### North Yemen Civil War

Main article: [North Yemen civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yemen_civil_war)

The North Yemen Civil War was fought in [North Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_Arab_Republic) between royalists of the [Mutawakkilite Kingdom of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutawakkilite_Kingdom_of_Yemen) and factions of the Yemen Arab Republic from 1962 to 1970. The war began with a [coup d'état](25914154.md) carried out by the republican leader, [Abdullah as-Sallal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_as-Sallal), which dethroned the newly crowned [Muhammad al-Badr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Badr) and declared Yemen a republic under his presidency. The Imam escaped to the Saudi Arabian border and rallied popular support.

The royalist side received support from Saudi Arabia, while the republicans were supported by Egypt and the Soviet Union. Both foreign irregular and conventional forces were also involved. The [Egyptian President](335341.md), [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser), supported the republicans with as many as 70,000 troops. Despite several military moves and peace conferences, the war sank into a stalemate. Egypt's commitment to the war is considered to have been detrimental to its performance in the [Six-Day War](29328.md) of June 1967, after which Nasser found it increasingly difficult to maintain his army's involvement and began to pull his forces out of Yemen.

By 1970, King [Faisal of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_of_Saudi_Arabia) recognized the republic and a truce was signed. Egyptian military historians refer to Egypt's role in the war in Yemen as analogous to the [United States](3434750.md)' role in the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-enein-53]

#### Gulf War

Main article: [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War)

In 1990, Iraq invaded Kuwait.[^cite_note-54] The [invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kuwait) by Iraqi forces led to the 1990–91 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). Egypt, Qatar, Syria, and Saudi Arabia joined a multinational [coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_the_Gulf_War) that opposed Iraq. Displays of support for Iraq by Jordan and Palestine resulted in strained relations between many of the Arab states. After the war, a so-called "Damascus Declaration" formalized an alliance for future joint Arab defensive actions between Egypt, Syria, and the GCC member states.[^cite_note-55]

#### 2014 Yemen civil war

Main article: [Yemeni civil war (2014–present)](46215853.md)

The [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) reached Yemen in January 2011.[^cite_note-56] People of Yemen took to the street demonstrating against three decades of rule by President [Ali Abdullah Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abdullah_Saleh).[^cite_note-57] The demonstration led to cracks in the ruling General People's Congress (GPC) and Saleh's Sanhani clan.[^cite_note-58] Saleh used tactics of concession and violence to save his presidency.[^cite_note-59] After numerous attempts, Saleh accepted the Gulf Cooperation Council's mediation. He eventually handed power to Vice President Hadi, who was sworn in as President of Yemen on 25 February 2012. Hadi launched a national dialogue to address new constitutional, political and social issues. The [Houthi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement), dissatisfied with the outcomes of the national dialogue, [launched an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_takeover_in_Yemen) and stormed the Yemeni capital Sanaa on 21 September 2014.[^cite_note-60] In response, Saudi Arabia launched a [military intervention in Yemen](46230181.md) in March 2015.[^cite_note-61] The civil war and subsequent military intervention and [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Yemen) caused a [famine in Yemen](51686838.md).[^cite_note-62]

## Geography

See also: [Geography of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Saudi_Arabia) and [Geography of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Yemen)



A map of the geographic regions of the Arabian Peninsula

The Arabian Peninsula is located in the continent of Asia and is bounded by (clockwise) the Persian Gulf to the north-east, the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) and the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) to the east, the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the south-east, the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md) and the [Guardafui Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardafui_Channel) to the south, and the [Bab-el-Mandeb](62138.md) strait to the south-west and the [Red Sea](26191.md) to the south-west and west.[^cite_note-arabia-4]

The northern portion of the peninsula transitions into the [Syrian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Desert) with no clear borderline, although the northern boundary of the peninsula is generally considered to be the northern borders of Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, as well as the southern regions of Iraq and Jordan.[^cite_note-arabia-4]

The most prominent feature of the peninsula is the [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert), but in the south-west, there are mountain ranges, which receive greater rainfall than the rest of the peninsula. [Harrat ash Shaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrat_ash_Shaam) is a large volcanic field that extends from north-western Arabia into Jordan and southern [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-63]

The rocks exposed vary systematically across Arabia, with the oldest rocks exposed in the [Arabian-Nubian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian-Nubian_Shield) near the Red Sea, overlain by earlier sediments that become younger towards the Persian Gulf. Perhaps the best-preserved [ophiolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiolite) on Earth, the [Samail Ophiolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samail_Ophiolite), lies exposed in the mountains of the UAE and northern Oman.

The peninsula consists of:

1. A central plateau, the [Najd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najd), with fertile valleys and pastures used for the grazing of [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep) and other livestock

Oases and marshy coast-land in [Eastern Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Arabia), the most important of which are those of the [Al Ain emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ain,_Emirate_of_Abu_Dhabi) ([Tawam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawam_\(region\)) region) and [Hofuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofuf)/[Al-Ahsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahsa_Oasis) (in modern-day Saudi Arabia), according to an author

[Rub' al Khali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub'_al_Khali) is part of the larger [Arabian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Desert)

Arabia has few lakes or permanent rivers. Most areas are drained by ephemeral watercourses called [wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi), which are dry except during the rainy season. Plentiful ancient [aquifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) exist beneath much of the peninsula, however, and where this water surfaces, [oases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oases) form (e.g. Al-Hasa and [Qatif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatif), two of the world's largest oases) and permit agriculture, especially [palm trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_trees), which allowed the peninsula to produce more [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) than any other region in the world.&#x20;

In general, the climate is extremely hot and [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert), although there are exceptions. Higher elevations are made temperate by their altitude, and the Arabian Sea coastline can receive cool, humid breezes in summer due to cold upwelling offshore. The peninsula has no thick forests. Desert-adapted wildlife is present throughout the region.

A plateau more than 2,500 feet (760 m) high extends across much of the Arabian Peninsula. The plateau slopes eastwards from the massive, rifted escarpment along the coast of the Red Sea, to the shallow waters of the Persian Gulf. The interior is characterized by *cuestas* and valleys, drained by a system of *wadis*. A crescent of sand and [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel) deserts lies to the east.

### Land and sea



Coconut palms line corniches of Al-Hafa, Oman.



Red Sea coral reefs



*Al-Shaggain* rock formation in [Burum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burum,_Yemen), a [tentative World Heritage Site in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Yemen#Tentative_list)

Most of the Arabian Peninsula is unsuited to agriculture, making irrigation and land reclamation projects essential. The narrow coastal plain and isolated oases, amounting to less than 1% of the land area, are used to cultivate grains, [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), and [tropical fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_fruit). Goat, sheep, and [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel) husbandry is widespread elsewhere throughout the rest of the Peninsula.

Some areas have a summer humid [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate), in particular the [Dhofar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhofar) and [Al Mahrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mahrah) areas of Oman and Yemen. These areas allow for large scale coconut plantations. Much of Yemen has a tropical [monsoon](57630.md) rain influenced mountain climate. The plains usually have either a tropical or subtropical arid [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) or arid [steppe climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_climate).

The [Red Sea](26191.md) to the west and the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md) to the south have rich marine life and extensive [coral reefs](87410.md), home to more than 310 [coral species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral), with roughly 270 being [hard coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_coral).[^cite_note-78] The [protozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protozoa) and [zooxanthellae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooxanthellae) living in [symbiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiosis) with Red Sea corals have an unusual hot weather adaptation to sudden rise and fall in sea water temperature. Hence, these coral reefs are not affected by coral bleaching caused by rise in temperatures, as [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) coral reefs are.[^cite_note-79]

The reefs are also unaffected by mass tourism and diving or other large scale human interference. The Persian gulf has suffered significant loss and degradation of coral reefs with the biggest ongoing threat believed to be coastal construction activity altering the marine environment.[^cite_note-80]

The fertile soils of Yemen have encouraged settlement of almost all of the land from sea level up to the mountains at 10,000 feet (3,000 m). In the higher elevations, elaborate terraces have been constructed to facilitate grain, fruit, coffee, ginger, and [khat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khat) cultivation. The Arabian Peninsula is known for its rich oil, i.e. [petroleum](23195.md) production due to its geographical location.[^cite_note-81]

According to NASA's [Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_Recovery_and_Climate_Experiment) (GRACE) satellite data (2003–2013) analysed in a [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine) (UCI)-led study published in [Water Resources Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Resources_Research) in June 2015, the most over-stressed aquifer system in the world is the [Arabian Aquifer System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Aquifer_System), upon which more than 60 million people depend for water.[^cite_note-NASA_GRACE-82] Twenty-one of the 37 largest aquifers "have exceeded sustainability tipping points and are being depleted" and thirteen of them are "considered significantly distressed".[^cite_note-NASA_GRACE-82]

## Political boundaries



The constituent countries of Arabia

The Peninsula's constituent countries are (clockwise from north to south) [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (UAE) in the east, [Oman](22316.md) in the south-east, [Yemen](350939.md) in the south, and Saudi Arabia at the center. The island country of [Bahrain](18933277.md) lies just off the east coast of the Peninsula, to the northwest of Qatar.[^cite_note-arabia-4] Due to Yemen's jurisdiction over the [Socotra Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate), the Peninsula's geopolitical outline faces the [Guardafui Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardafui_Channel) and the Somali Sea to the south.[^cite_note-83]

The six countries of Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE form the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) (GCC).[^cite_note-84]

The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia covers the largest part of the Peninsula. The Peninsula contains the world's largest oil reserves. Saudi Arabia and the UAE are economically the wealthiest in the region. Qatar, the only peninsular country in the Persian Gulf on the larger peninsula, is home to the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) television station [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) and its English-language subsidiary [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English). Kuwait, on the border with Iraq, is an important country strategically, forming one of the main staging grounds for [coalition forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_the_willing_\(Iraq_War\)) mounting the [United States](3434750.md)–led [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md).

Despite its historically sparse population, political Arabia stands out for its rapid population growth, driven by both significant inflows of migrant labor and persistently high birth rates. The population is characterized by its relative youth and a heavily skewed gender ratio favoring males. In several states, the number of South Asians surpasses that of the native population.[^cite_note-yam-88]

The Arabian Peninsula is known for having one of the most uneven adult sex ratios in the world, with females in some regions, especially the east, constituting only a quarter of people aged between 20 and 40.[^cite_note-89]
