The **2025 Africa Cup of Nations**[^cite_note-3] was the 35th edition of the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md), a biennial [football](10568.md) tournament contested by the [men's national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams#CAF_\(Africa\)) of the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF). It was hosted by Morocco — the second time the country had hosted the tournament, after [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_African_Cup_of_Nations) — following the stripping of hosting rights from Guinea due to inadequate preparations.[^cite_note-Withdraw-4][^cite_note-2025Host-5]

Due to a scheduling conflict with the expanded [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) in June and July, the Africa Cup of Nations was played between 21 December 2025 and 18 January 2026 — the first time it was held over the Christmas and New Year period.[^cite_note-6]

[Defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_final) [Ivory Coast](1039856.md) were eliminated in the quarter-finals by [Egypt](1022953.md).[^cite_note-7] The [final](81129500.md) between [Senegal](1017436.md) and hosts [Morocco](1024865.md) was marred by controversy after Senegal walked off the pitch for 17 minutes in protest over a disallowed goal and a [VAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) decision in stoppage time. Senegal returned to the pitch and won 1–0 after [extra time](633242.md#Association_football),[^cite_note-8] but on 17 March 2026 the CAF Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the final through their actions, awarding Morocco a 3–0 victory.[^cite_note-CAF_appeal-9] Senegal have indicated that they will appeal to the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport).[^cite_note-10] It was Morocco's second Africa Cup of Nations title, after their first in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_African_Cup_of_Nations).

## Host selection

CAF stripped Cameroon from hosting [the 2019 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) of the tournament on 30 November 2018 due to lack of speed of progress in preparations,[^cite_note-11] but accepted former CAF president [Ahmad Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Ahmad)'s request to stage [the next edition in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). Consequently, the original hosts of 2021, [Ivory Coast](57744.md), became hosts of [the 2023 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) with Guinea instead hosting the 2025 edition, which until then had no hosts.[^cite_note-12] The CAF President confirmed the timetable shift after a meeting with Ivorian president [Alassane Ouattara](1173624.md) in [Abidjan](21371767.md), Ivory Coast on 30 January 2019.[^cite_note-13] On 30 September 2022, current CAF president [Patrice Motsepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Motsepe) announced that [Guinea](12176.md) had been stripped as host for the 2025 edition due to inadequacy and speed of progress in hosting preparations.[^cite_note-Withdraw-4] Consequently, a new process was re-opened for a replacement host bidder.[^cite_note-Reopen-14][^cite_note-15] On 27 September 2023, the 2025 edition was awarded to [Morocco](19291.md)[^cite_note-2025Host-5] and the 2027 edition to [Kenya](188171.md), [Tanzania](30118.md), and [Uganda](31816.md).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Mascot

The tournament mascot, named Assad ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): أسد), was revealed on 8 December 2025. It was a [Barbary lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_lion), a reference to Morocco's national animal and nickname of the [national team of Morocco](1024865.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Match ball

On 10 November 2025, CAF and [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_\(brand\)) unveiled Itri ([Standard Moroccan Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Moroccan_Amazigh): ⵉⵜⵔⵉ, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'star') as the official tournament edition match ball. The ball, covered in [*zellij*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zellij) art in red and green, was influenced by an ancient mosaic tradition known for its complex geometric decoration. Produced by Puma using Orbital 6 technology, the design combined star-based forms, floral motifs and circular patterns.[^cite_note-20] A special edition was made for the final where the official match ball was recoloured in gold.

### Official song

The official song was "Africallez" (a [wordplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordplay) in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Africa allez!*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Let's go Africa!'), which stood as one of the tournament's emblems.[^cite_note-21] The song was performed by three artists: Beninese musician [Angélique Kidjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang%C3%A9lique_Kidjo), and Moroccan singers [Lartiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lartiste) and [Jaylann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaylann).

## Prize money

The winners received US\$10 million, an increase from US\$7 million for the 2023 event.[^cite_note-22]

The qualifiers were held between 20 March and 19 November 2024, starting with the [preliminary round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification_preliminary_round) (20–26 March 2024) and then the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification#Group_stage) (2 September–19 November 2024).[^cite_note-23] The preliminary round draw was held on 20 February 2024, 14\:00 [CAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa_Time) ([UTC+2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+02\:00)) at the CAF headquarters in [Cairo](6293.md), [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-draw_result-24] The eight involved national teams were seeded into two pots of four based on the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) from 15 February 2024,[^cite_note-25] [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_national_football_team) and [Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_national_football_team) were excluded from the qualifiers. The eight teams were split into four ties which were played in home-and-away [two-legged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie) format.[^cite_note-draw_result-24] The four winners ([Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_national_football_team), [Eswatini](1212508.md), [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia_national_football_team), and [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan_national_football_team)) advanced to the group stage to join the 44 teams which entered directly.[^cite_note-result-26] The group stage draw took place on 4 July 2024, 14\:30 [CAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa_Time) ([UTC+2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+02\:00)) in [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa. The 48 national teams involved were divided into twelve groups of four each, which consisted of the 44 teams that entered directly, in addition to the four winners of the preliminary round, and were seeded into four pots of twelve each based on the June 2024 [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Men's_World_Rankings).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Qualified teams

The following 24 teams qualified for this edition; all of them had previously participated in the tournament.[^cite_note-29] [Morocco](1024865.md), the host country, played in the qualifiers in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification_Group_B) despite qualifying automatically. [Ghana](1029620.md), four-time African champions, failed to qualify after finishing bottom of the [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification_Group_F), missing out on the Africa Cup of Nations for the first time since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] [Tunisia](1029953.md) qualified for the 22nd time and extended their record for consecutive participations, reaching the tournament for the 17th time in a row, having not been absent since [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_African_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-32] [Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_national_football_team), [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon_national_football_team), [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team) and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_national_football_team) made their return to the continental tournament after missing out in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-33] [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_national_football_team) and [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team) made their return after an almost five-year absence from the event. [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana_national_football_team) qualified for the second time after their first participation in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-34] [Cape Verde](1205575.md), [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia_national_football_team), [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team), [Guinea-Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau_national_football_team), [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania_national_football_team) and [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) also failed to qualify after appearing in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

All times are local, [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time) ([UTC+1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00)).

The CAF announced the tournament schedule on 31 January 2025.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

The top two teams of each group, along with the best four third-placed teams, advanced to the round of 16.

### Tiebreakers

Teams were ranked according to the [three points for a win](4960100.md) system (3 points for a win, 1 for a draw, 0 points for a loss), and if tied on points, the following tiebreaking criteria were applied, in the order given, to determine the rankings (Regulations Article 74):[^cite_note-regulations-41]

1. Points in head-to-head matches among tied teams;

2. [Goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference) in head-to-head matches among tied teams;

3. Goals scored in head-to-head matches among tied teams;

4. If more than two teams were tied, and after applying all head-to-head criteria above, if two teams were still tied, all head-to-head criteria above were applied exclusively to these two teams;

5. Goal difference in all group matches;

6. Goals scored in all group matches;

7. Drawing of lots.

### Discipline

A player was automatically suspended for the next match for the following offences:

- Receiving a red card (red card suspensions could be extended for serious offences)

- Receiving two yellow cards in two matches

After the end of the group matches, all cautions received were cancelled for the rest of the competition. Nevertheless, a player having collected two yellow cards sustained the one match suspension.[^cite_note-regulations-41]

## Final controversies

During the [final](81129500.md) of the tournament, contested between [Senegal](1017436.md) and [Morocco](1024865.md), the score remained level at 0–0 for most of regulation time. In the fifth minute of stoppage time, referee [Jean-Jacques Ndala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Ndala) whistled a foul during a buildup that led to Senegal scoring what seemed initially as a valid goal by [Ismaïla Sarr](51591292.md). [Abdoulaye Seck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdoulaye_Seck_\(footballer,_born_1992\)) had come into contact with [Achraf Hakimi](52004386.md) after which the latter fell to the ground. [VAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) could not be consulted since the "goal" was scored after the referee's whistle.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

A few minutes later, in the eighth minute of stoppage time, [Brahim Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahim_D%C3%ADaz) was held by Senegalese defender [El Hadji Malick Diouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hadji_Malick_Diouf), an action that was sanctioned as a penalty kick after VAR review.[^cite_note-104] Disagreeing with the referee’s decisions, Senegal head coach [Pape Thiaw](5728292.md) instructed his players to leave the pitch in protest. The match was suspended for approximately 15 minutes, after which the Senegalese players returned to the field. It was during the VAR review and while play was suspended that unrest broke out among fans in the stands behind the goal of Moroccan goalkeeper [Yassine Bounou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yassine_Bounou). Several Senegalese [hooligans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_hooliganism) attempted to storm the pitch while throwing chairs and other objects. Clashes erupted with stewards and security personnel, many being hit and kicked by the hooligans. Later, police in protective gear gathered in front of the affected stands and carried out charges. Police and stewards eventually formed a barrier separating those supporters from the rest of the crowd until after the match was over.

The match eventually resumed while tensions remained evident on the other side of the field of play. Brahim Díaz took the penalty, but his attempt was saved by Senegal goalkeeper [Édouard Mendy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Mendy), and regulation time ended with the score still tied at 0–0.[^cite_note-105]

At least one steward was seriously injured and had to be taken off on a stretcher, prompting concern and circulating social‑media claims that the steward might have died. However, Moroccan authorities, including the [General Directorate for National Security](38272761.md), denied that any steward or stadium worker had died as a result of the clashes, stating that no such fatality was recorded and that reports of a death were false.[^cite_note-106] In total, 18 Senegalese fans and one Algerian fan were arrested in connection with the disturbances. The defendants will stand trial at the Rabat First Criminal Court, facing multiple charges, including participating in violent acts during a sporting event, forcibly entering the pitch, damaging sporting facilities, assaulting law enforcement officers and public forces, throwing objects, causing harm and throwing liquids, causing damage.[^cite_note-107]

The day after the final, the [Royal Moroccan Football Federation](1905329.md) announced that it would file a complaint against Senegal with [FIFA](11049.md) and [CAF](715265.md), alleging that the events prior to the penalty kick "affected the normal development of the match and the performance of the players".[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] On the same day, CAF released a public statement condemning "the unacceptable behaviour of some players and officials" during the final. [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) reacted on these events condemning "the behaviour of some "supporters" as well as some Senegalese players and technical staff members".[^cite_note-110]

## Disciplinary ruling

On 17 March 2026, the CAF Appeal Board overturned the result of the final, in which Senegal had originally won on the field, and awarded a 3–0 victory to Morocco.[^cite_note-CAFstatement-111]

The ruling cited Article 84 of the Africa Cup of Nations regulations, which provides for a forfeit if a team leaves the field of play without the referee’s permission.[^cite_note-CAFstatement-111] As a result, Morocco were officially recognized as champions of the tournament.[^cite_note-112]

The decision drew criticism in Senegal and received significant media attention internationally. Several outlets highlighted the unusual nature of overturning the result of a completed final and questioned the interpretation of the regulations.[^cite_note-113] The Senegalese Football Federation indicated that it intended to appeal the decision to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS).[^cite_note-114]

CAS acknowledged receipt of an appeal by Senegal at the end of March.[^cite_note-115]  Senegal is still in possession of the trophy, as it has refused to hand it over to the Moroccan national team.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]
