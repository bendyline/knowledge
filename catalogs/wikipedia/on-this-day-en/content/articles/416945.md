***Orthohantavirus*** is a [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) of [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus) which includes all **hantaviruses** that cause disease in humans. Hantaviruses are naturally found primarily in [rodents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodent), like those in the family [Cricetidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricetidae). In general, each hantavirus is carried by one rodent species and each rodent that carries a hantavirus carries one hantavirus species. Hantaviruses in their [natural reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) usually cause an [asymptomatic](602790.md), persistent infection. In humans, however, hantaviruses cause two diseases: [hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantavirus_hemorrhagic_fever_with_renal_syndrome) (HFRS) and [hantavirus pulmonary syndrome](39903079.md) (HPS). HFRS is mainly caused by hantaviruses in Africa, Asia, and Europe, called [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) hantaviruses, and HPS is usually caused by hantaviruses in the Americas, called [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) hantaviruses.

Hantaviruses are transmitted mainly through [aerosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol) and droplets that contain rodent excretions, as well as through contaminated food, bites, and scratches. Environmental factors such as rainfall, temperature, and humidity influence transmission. HFRS is marked by kidney disease with kidney swelling, excess protein in urine, and blood in urine. The case fatality rate of HFRS varies from less than 1% to 15% depending on the virus. A mild form of HFRS called nephropathia epidemica is often caused by [Puumala virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puumala_virus) and [Dobrava-Belgrade virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrava-Belgrade_virus). For HPS, initial symptoms are flu-like, with fever, headache, and muscle pain, followed by sudden respiratory failure. HPS has a higher case fatality rate than HFRS, at 30–60%. For both HFRS and HPS, illness is the result of increased [vascular permeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_permeability), decreased [platelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platelet) count, and overreaction of the immune system.

The hantavirus genome consists of three [single-stranded negative-sense RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative-strand_RNA_virus) segments that encode one protein each: an [RNA-dependent RNA polymerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA-dependent_RNA_polymerase) (RdRp), a spike glycoprotein precursor, and the N protein. Segments are encased in N proteins to form ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes that each have a copy of RdRp attached. RNP complexes are surrounded by a [lipid envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope) that has [spike proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_protein) emanating from its surface. Replication begins when spikes attach to the surface of cells. After entering the cell, the envelope fuses with [endosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosome) and [lysosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome), which empties RNPs into the cytoplasm. RdRp then [transcribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_\(biology\)) the genome to produce [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA) (mRNA) for [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_\(biology\)) by host ribosomes to produce viral proteins and replicates the genome for progeny viruses. Old World hantaviruses assemble in the [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus) and obtain their envelope from it, before being transported to the cell membrane to leave the cell via [exocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocytosis). [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) hantaviruses assemble near the [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane) and obtain their envelope from it as they leave the cell by budding from its surface.

Hantaviruses were first discovered following the [Korean War](16772.md). During the war, HFRS was a common ailment in soldiers stationed near the [Hantan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantan_River). The first hantavirus was isolated in 1978 in South Korea and was named [Hantaan virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantaan_virus). It was shown to be responsible for the outbreak during the war. Within a few years, other hantaviruses that cause HFRS were discovered throughout Eurasia. In 1982, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) gave HFRS its name, and in 1987, hantaviruses were classified as a genus for the first time. In 1993, [an outbreak of HPS occurred in the Four Corners region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Four_Corners_hantavirus_outbreak) in the United States, which led to the discovery of pathogenic New World hantaviruses and the second disease caused by hantaviruses. Since then, hantaviruses have been found not just in rodents but also in [moles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_\(animal\)), [shrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrew), and [bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat).

## Disease

Main articles: [Hantavirus infection](21180942.md), [Hantavirus hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantavirus_hemorrhagic_fever_with_renal_syndrome), and [Hantavirus pulmonary syndrome](39903079.md)

&#x20;World distribution of select hantaviruses  HCPS  NE/HFRS  HFRS

World distribution of select hantaviruses

&#x20; [HCPS](39903079.md)  [NE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puumala_virus)/HFRS  [HFRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantavirus_hemorrhagic_fever_with_renal_syndrome)

Hantaviruses are sorted into Old World hantaviruses (OWHVs), which typically cause [hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantavirus_hemorrhagic_fever_with_renal_syndrome) (HFRS) in Africa, Asia, and Europe, and New World hantaviruses (NWHVs) which are associated with [hantavirus pulmonary syndrome](39903079.md) (HPS) in the Americas. The case fatality rate of HFRS ranges from less than 1% to 15%, while for HPS it is 30–60%.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-toledo-3][^cite_note-kim-4][^cite_note-jacob-5] The severity of symptoms of HFRS varies depending on the virus: [Hantaan virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantaan_virus) causes severe HFRS, [Seoul virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_virus) moderate HFRS, [Puumala virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puumala_virus) mild HFRS,[^cite_note-tariq-6] and [Dobrava-Belgrade virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrava-Belgrade_virus) infection varies from mild to severe depending on genotype.[^cite_note-klempa2013-7] The mild form of HFRS caused by Puumala virus and Dobrava-Belgrade virus is often called nephropathia epidemica (NE).[^cite_note-ricco-8][^cite_note-afzal-9] Repeated infections of hantaviruses have not been observed, so recovering from infection likely grants life-long immunity.[^cite_note-hansen-10][^cite_note-kruger2011-11]

HFRS is characterized by five phases: febrile, [hypotensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension), low urine production ([oliguria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliguria)), high urine production ([polyuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyuria)), and recovery. Symptoms usually occur 12–16 days after exposure to the virus.[^cite_note-zhang-12] Acute kidney disease occurs with kidney swelling, excess protein in urine ([proteinuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteinuria)), and blood in urine ([hematuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematuria)). Other symptoms include headache, lower back pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, bloody stool, the appearance of spots on the skin ([petechiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petechiae)), and hemorrhaging in the respiratory tract.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-sehgal-13] Renal failure leads to oliguria, and restoration of kidney health comes with polyuria.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-tariq-6] Recovery typically takes a few months.[^cite_note-lupusoru-14] In more mild cases, the different phases of HFRS may be hard to distinguish,[^cite_note-avsic-15] or some phases may be absent, while in more severe cases, the phases may overlap.[^cite_note-tariq-6]

HPS is mainly caused by two viruses: [Andes virus](6944726.md) and [Sin Nombre virus](769409.md). The disease has three phases: prodromal (early), cardiopulmonary, and recovery. Symptoms occur about 1–8 weeks after exposure to the virus. Early symptoms include fever, headache, muscle pain, [shortness of breath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortness_of_breath) (dyspnea), and low platelet count ([thrombocytopenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombocytopenia)). During the cardiopulmonary phase, there is elevated heart rate ([tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia)), irregular heartbeats ([arrhythmias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia)), and [cardiogenic shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiogenic_shock). Pulmonary capillary leakage can lead to [acute respiratory distress syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_respiratory_distress_syndrome), buildup of fluids in the lungs ([pulmonary edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_edema)), hypotension, and buildup of fluid in the chest cavity ([pleural effusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleural_effusion)). These symptoms can cause sudden death.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-jacob-5][^cite_note-koehler-16] After the cardiopulmonary phase is resolved, recovery typically takes 3 to 6 months,[^cite_note-koehler-16] with polyuria. While HFRS is associated with renal disease and HPS with cardiopulmonary disease, HFRS may sometimes include cardiopulmonary symptoms associated with HPS and HPS may sometimes include renal symptoms associated with HFRS.[^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-noack-17]

## Transmission

The [western deer mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_deer_mouse), the natural reservoir of Sin Nombre virus

Hantaviruses that cause illness in humans are mainly transmitted by rodents. In rodents, hantaviruses usually cause an asymptomatic, persistent infection. Infected animals can spread the virus to uninfected animals through aerosols or droplets from their feces, urine, saliva,[^cite_note-tariq-6] and blood,[^cite_note-douglas-18] through consumption of contaminated food, from virus particles shed from skin or fur,[^cite_note-dsouza-19] via grooming,[^cite_note-jacob-5] or through biting and scratching. Hantaviruses can also spread through the fecal-oral route and across the placenta during pregnancy [from mother to child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_transmission). They can survive for 10 days at room temperature,[^cite_note-chen-2] 15 days in a temperate environment,[^cite_note-ricco-8] and more than 18 days at 4 °C (39 °F), which aids in the transmission of the virus.[^cite_note-chen-2] Environmental conditions favorable to the reproduction and spread of rodents are known to increase disease transmission.[^cite_note-toledo-3] Living in a rural environment, in unhygienic settings, and interacting with environments shared with hosts are the biggest risk factors for infection, especially among people who are hikers,[^cite_note-tariq-6] farmers, and forestry workers,[^cite_note-ricco-8] as well as those in mining, the military,[^cite_note-dsouza-19][^cite_note-mustonen-20] and [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology).[^cite_note-koehler-16]

Human-to-human transmission of [Andes virus](6944726.md) is sometimes reported. Although a systematic review of research did not find sufficient evidence of such transmission,[^cite_note-toledo-3] many experts consider it to be possible between close contacts while noting that ANDV is not highly transmissible.[^cite_note-maes-21][^cite_note-tortosa-22] It can reportedly spread through human saliva, airborne droplets from coughing and sneezing, and possibly to newborns through breast milk or the placenta.[^cite_note-chen-2] There is also suspicion that Puumala virus can spread from person to person through blood and platelet transfusions.[^cite_note-meier1-23]

Hantaviruses that cause HFRS can be transmitted through the bites of [mites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mite) and [ticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tick).[^cite_note-tkachenko-24] Research has also shown that pigs can be infected with Hantaan virus without severe symptoms, and sows can transmit the virus to offspring through the placenta. Pig-to-human transmission may also be possible; one swine breeder was infected with hantavirus with no contact with rodents or mites. Hantaan virus and Puumala virus have been detected in cattle, deer, and rabbits, and antibodies to [Seoul virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_virus) have been detected in cats and dogs, but the role of these hosts for hantaviruses is unknown.[^cite_note-chen-2] Hantaviruses can also spread among rats kept as pets. For example, in an outbreak in North America in 2017, [Seoul virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_virus) infected 31 people through contact with pet rats.[^cite_note-chen-2] In addition to rodents, some hantaviruses are found in small insectivorous mammals, such as moles,[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-klempa-25] shrews, and bats.[^cite_note-afzal-9][^cite_note-koehler-16] Hantavirus [antigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigen), indicative of infection, has also been detected in a variety of bird species.[^cite_note-tkachenko-24] Infection in other animals can potentially facilitate the evolution of hantaviruses by [gene reassortment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_reassortment).[^cite_note-koehler-16]

Human built environments are important in hantavirus transmission. Deforestation and excess agriculture may destroy rodents' natural habitat.[^cite_note-koehler-16] The expansion of agricultural land is associated with a decline in predator populations, which enables hantavirus host species to use farm monocultures as nesting and foraging sites. Agricultural sites built in close proximity to rodents' natural habitats can facilitate the proliferation of rodents as they may be attracted to animal feed.[^cite_note-douglas-18][^cite_note-moirano-26] Sewers and stormwater drainage systems may be inhabited by rodents, especially in areas with poor [solid waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewerage). Maritime trade and travel have also been implicated in the spread of hantaviruses.[^cite_note-douglas-18] Research results are inconsistent on whether urban living increases or decreases hantavirus incidence.[^cite_note-moirano-26] [Seroprevalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seroprevalence), which shows past infection to hantavirus, is consistently higher in occupations and areas that have greater exposure to rodents.[^cite_note-tortosa-22] Poor living conditions on battlefields, in military camps, and in refugee camps expose soldiers and refugees to infection.[^cite_note-mustonen-20]

## Environment

Further information: [Effects of climate change on hantavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_hantavirus)

A diagram of El Niño's effects on the climates of different regions of the world

[El Niño](317311.md)'s effect on local climates

Rodent species that carry hantaviruses inhabit a diverse range of habitats, including desert-like biomes, equatorial and tropical forests, swamps, savannas, fields, and salt marshes.[^cite_note-douglas-18] The [seroprevalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seroprevalence) of hantaviruses in their host species has been observed to range from 5.9% to 38% in the Americas, and 3% to about 19% worldwide, depending on testing method and location.[^cite_note-dsouza-19][^cite_note-orico-27] In some places, such as South Korea, routine trapping of wild rodents is performed to surveil hantavirus circulation.[^cite_note-kim-4] High humidity can benefit rodent populations in warm climates, where it may positively impact plant growth and thus food availability.[^cite_note-douglas-18] Increased forest coverage is associated with increased hantavirus incidence, particularly in Europe.[^cite_note-moirano-26]

[Climate change](5042951.md) and environmental degradation increase contact areas between rodent hosts and humans, which increases potential exposure to hantaviruses. An example of this was the 1993 Four Corners outbreak in the United States, which was immediately preceded by elevated rainfall from the 1992–1993 [El Niño](317311.md) warming period. This caused a substantial growth in the food supply for rodents, which led to rapid growth in their population and facilitated greater spread of the hantavirus that caused that outbreak.[^cite_note-douglas-18][^cite_note-dsouza-19][^cite_note-tian-28]

Rainfall is consistently associated with hantavirus incidence in various patterns. Heavy rainfall is a risk factor for outbreaks in the following months,[^cite_note-hansen-10] but may negatively affect incidence by flooding rodent burrows and nests.[^cite_note-tian-28] In places that have wet and dry seasons, infections are more common in the wet season than in the dry season.[^cite_note-douglas-18] Low rainfall and drought are associated with decreased incidence since such conditions result in a smaller rodent population,[^cite_note-tian-28] but displacement of rodent populations via drought or flood can lead to an increase in rodent-human interactions and infections.[^cite_note-douglas-18] In Europe, however, no association between rainfall and disease incidence has been found.[^cite_note-tian-28]

Temperature has varying effects on hantavirus transmission. Higher temperatures create unfavorable environments for virus survival and decreases activity levels of [Neotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotropic) rodents, but it can cause rodents to seek shelter from heat in human settings and is beneficial for aerosol production.[^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-douglas-18] Lower temperature can prolong virus survival outside a host.[^cite_note-douglas-18] Higher average winter temperature is associated with reduced survival of bank voles, the natural reservoir of Puumala virus, but increased survival of striped field mice in China, the natural reservoirs of Hantaan virus.[^cite_note-tian-28] Extreme temperatures, whether hot or cold, are associated with lower disease incidence.[^cite_note-hansen-10]

## Genome and structure

A transmission electron micrograph of Sin Nombre virus, showing numerous virions next to a cell

A transmission electron micrograph of Sin Nombre virus

The genome of hantaviruses is segmented into three parts: the large (L), medium (M), and small (S) segments. Each part is a [single-stranded negative-sense RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative-strand_RNA_virus) strand and consists of 10,000–15,000 nucleotides in total.[^cite_note-jacob-5] The segments form into circles via non-covalent bonding of the ends of the genome.[^cite_note-ortho-29] The L segment is about 6.6 kilobases (kb) in length[^cite_note-dsouza-19] and encodes a viral [RNA-dependent RNA polymerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA-dependent_RNA_polymerase) (RdRp), which mediates [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_\(biology\)) and replication of viral RNA. The M segment, about 3.7 kb in length,[^cite_note-dsouza-19] encodes a glycoprotein precursor that is co-translated and cleaved into Gn and Gc. Gn and Gc bind to cell receptors, regulate immune responses, and induce protective antibodies. The S segment is around 2.1 kb in length[^cite_note-dsouza-19] and encodes the [nucleocapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleocapsid) protein N, which binds to and protects viral RNA. An [open reading frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_reading_frame) in the N gene on the S segment[^cite_note-bae-30] of some orthohantaviruses also encodes the non-structural protein NS that inhibits [interferon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interferon) production in host cells. The untranslated regions at the ends of the genome are highly conserved and participate in the replication and transcription of the genome.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-jacob-5][^cite_note-tariq-6]

Individual hantavirus particles (virions) are usually spherical, but may be oval, pleomorphic,[^cite_note-deng-31] or tubular.[^cite_note-jacob-5] The diameter of the virion is 70–350 nanometers (nm).[^cite_note-dsouza-19] The outer part of the virion is a [lipid envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope) that is about 5 nm thick. Embedded in the envelope are the surface spike glycoproteins Gn and Gc,[^cite_note-chen-2] which are arranged in a lattice pattern.[^cite_note-dsouza-19] Each surface spike is composed of a tetramer of Gn and Gc (four units each) that has four-fold rotational symmetry, and extends about 10 nm out from the envelope.[^cite_note-dsouza-19] Gn forms the stalk of the spike and Gc the head.[^cite_note-jacob-5] Inside the envelope are helical nucleocapsids made of many copies of the nucleocapsid protein N, which are attached to the virus's genome to form ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes. Each RNP complex has a copy of RdRp attached to it.[^cite_note-chen-2] Hantaviruses do not encode matrix proteins to assist with structuring the virion, so how surface proteins organize into a sphere with a symmetrical lattice is not yet known.[^cite_note-gcalvo-32]

## Life cycle

&#x20;Ten major steps of the hantavirus life cycle.

Ten major steps of the hantavirus life cycle.

Vascular [endothelial cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endothelial_cell) and [macrophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrophage) are the primary cells infected by hantaviruses.[^cite_note-afzal-9] [Podocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podocyte), tubular cells, [dendritic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendritic_cell), and [lymphocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphocyte) can also be infected.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-koehler-16] Attachment and entry into the host cell is mediated by the binding of the viral glycoprotein spikes to host cell receptors, particularly [β3 integrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%923_integrin). Decay acceleration factors, complement receptors, and, for New World hantaviruses, [protocadherin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocadherin)-1 have also been proposed to be involved in attachment.[^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-gcalvo-32] After attachment, hantaviruses rely on several ways to enter a cell, including micropinocytosis, [clathrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clathrin)-independent receptor-mediated [endocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis) and [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol)- or [caveolae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caveolae)-dependent endocytosis.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-jacob-5][^cite_note-koehler-16] Old World hantaviruses use clathrin-dependent endocytosis while New World hantaviruses use clathrin-independent endocytosis.[^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-meier1-23][^cite_note-lapointe-33]

After entering a cell, virions form vesicles that are transported to early [endosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosome), then late endosomes and lysosomal compartments. A decrease in pH then causes the viral envelope to fuse with the endosome or [lysosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome).[^cite_note-dsouza-19][^cite_note-meier1-23][^cite_note-lapointe-33] This fusion releases viral ribonucleoprotein complexes into the cell cytoplasm, which initiates transcription and replication by RdRp.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-dsouza-19] RdRp transcribes viral –ssRNA into complementary positive-sense strands, then [snatches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_snatching) 5′ ("five prime") ends of host [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA) (mRNA) to prepare mRNA for translation by host ribosomes to produce viral proteins.[^cite_note-jacob-5][^cite_note-dsouza-19] Complementary RNA strands are also used to produce copies of the genome, which are encapsulated by N proteins to form RNPs.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-dsouza-19]

During virion assembly, the glycoprotein precursor is cleaved in the endoplasmic reticulum into the Gn and Gc glycoproteins by host cell [signal peptidases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_peptidase).[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-jacob-5] Gn and Gc are modified by N-glycan chains, which stabilize the spike structure and assist in assembly in the Golgi apparatus for Old World hantaviruses[^cite_note-chen-2] or at the cell membrane for New World hantaviruses.[^cite_note-koehler-16] Old World hantaviruses obtain their viral envelope from the [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus) and are then transported to the cell membrane in vesicles to leave the cell via [exocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocytosis). On the other hand, New World hantavirus RNPs are transported to the cell membrane, where they bud from the surface of the cell to obtain their envelope and leave the cell.[^cite_note-koehler-16][^cite_note-dsouza-19][^cite_note-meier1-23]

The most common form of evolution for hantaviruses is [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation) through single nucleotide substitutions, insertions, and deletions.[^cite_note-chen-2] Hantaviruses are usually restricted to individual [natural reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) species and evolve alongside their hosts,[^cite_note-chen-2] but this one-species-one-hantavirus relationship is not true for all hantaviruses. The exact evolutionary history of hantaviruses is likely obscured by many instances of genome reassortment, host spillover, and host-switching.[^cite_note-kuhn-35] Within species, geography has affected the evolution of hantaviruses. For example, Hantaan virus and Seoul virus have both formed multiple lineages corresponding to their geographic distribution.[^cite_note-chen-2]

Because hantaviruses have segmented genomes, they are capable of [genetic recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_recombination) and [reassortment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reassortment) in which segments from different viruses can combine to form new viruses. This occurs often in nature and facilitates the adaptation of hantaviruses to multiple hosts and ecosystems. Recombination in OWHVs of the S and M segments is usually observed amongst viruses within species, but can occur between species. Reassortment in NWHVs of the S and M segments has been observed in rodents. Among Puumala viruses isolated from rodents in 2005–2009, 19.1% of them were identified as reassortments.[^cite_note-chen-2][^cite_note-kabwe-36] Diploid progeny are also possible, in which virions may possess two of the same segment from two parent viruses.[^cite_note-klempa-25]

## History

A grainy portrait photograph of Ho Wang Lee

Ho Wang Lee, 1972

Hantavirus hemorrhagic disease was likely first described in the *[Huangdi Neijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangdi_Neijing)*, an ancient Chinese medical text, in Imperial China during the [Warring States Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_Period) of 475–221 BCE.[^cite_note-kuhn-35] Hantaviruses have been suggested as a cause of "trench nephritis" in soldiers during the [US Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Civil_War) and in British soldiers in Flanders, Belgium[^cite_note-kuhn-35] during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). The disease was also mentioned in East Asia, where it was probably endemic, and was first described scientifically in [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) in 1913–1914. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) in 1942, an outbreak of disease with symptoms characteristic of hantavirus infection occurred in [Salla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salla), Eastern Lapland, Finland among German and Finnish soldiers. This outbreak was later reported in 1980 to be caused by a virus transmitted by bank voles and was named Puumala virus.[^cite_note-mustonen-20] Also during the war, around 10,000 Japanese soldiers stationed in [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria) developed HFRS.[^cite_note-tariq-6]

Around 3,200[^cite_note-mustonen-20] cases of HFRS occurred among United Nations soldiers stationed near the [Hantan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantan_River)[^cite_note-gcalvo-32] during the [Korean War](16772.md), where it was first identified in 1951[^cite_note-chen-2] and named "Korean hemorrhagic fever" and "epidemic hemorrhagic fever".[^cite_note-kuhn-35] After the war, in 1976 in South Korea, [Ho Wang Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Wang_Lee)[^cite_note-lupusoru-14] tested striped field mice and showed that antigens from their lungs were reactive to antibodies in sera from war survivors.[^cite_note-kuhn-35] In 1978, the virus was isolated for the first time, and in 1980, it was named [Hantaan virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantaan_virus) after the river.[^cite_note-sehgal-13] Retrospective analysis showed that Hantaan virus was responsible for the viral outbreak during the war.[^cite_note-mustonen-20] Other hantaviruses that caused HFRS were then discovered throughout Eurasia. The disease had a variety of names, so in 1982, the World Health Organization officially named it *hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome*.[^cite_note-tariq-6][^cite_note-kuhn-35] In 1985, this group of viruses were named "hantaviruses" after Hantaan virus,[^cite_note-deng-31] and in 1987, the genus *Hantavirus* was established to accommodate them in the then-family *Bunyaviridae*.[^cite_note-orthohistory-1] During the 1980s, Lee and his team developed the first [hantavirus vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantavirus_vaccine), Hantavax, to prevent HFRS. The first paper on the vaccine was published in 1988, and it was licensed by the Korean government in 1990.[^cite_note-42]

In 1993, [an outbreak of highly lethal acute respiratory distress syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Four_Corners_hantavirus_outbreak) occurred in the [Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners) region of the United States. This outbreak was determined to be caused by a hantavirus, now named [Sin Nombre virus](769409.md), and represented the first confirmed instance of pathogenic hantaviruses in the Americas as well as the discovery of a new type of disease caused by hantaviruses. The new disease was named *hantavirus pulmonary syndrome*. In subsequent years, numerous other hantaviruses were discovered in the Americas.[^cite_note-jacob-5][^cite_note-kuhn-35] HFRS, however, remains much more common than HPS—more than 100,000 cases of HFRS occur each year,[^cite_note-moirano-26] compared to only a few hundred cases of HPS annually.[^cite_note-engdahl-43]

Over time, hundreds of bunyaviruses were discovered but could not be accommodated within the genera of the *Bunyaviridae* family. To address this, in 2017 bunyaviruses were elevated to the rank of order, *Bunyavirales*, and hantaviruses, along with the other bunyavirus genera, were elevated to the rank of family. Hantaviruses, also called hantavirids, now also refer to members of the family *Hantaviridae*. The prior genus of *Hantavirus* was renamed *Orthohantavirus* to distinguish them from members of the family, and the genus's members are often called orthohantaviruses. In 2019, additional genera and subfamilies were created to classify non-rodent hantaviruses,[^cite_note-kuhn-35] and in 2023, [binomial nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_nomenclature) was adopted for hantaviruses.[^cite_note-chen-2]
