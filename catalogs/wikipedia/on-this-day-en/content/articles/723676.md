**Mair Eluned Morgan, Baroness Morgan of Ely** (born 16 February 1967), is a Welsh politician who served as [First Minister of Wales](602076.md) and Leader of [Welsh Labour](6732798.md) from 2024 to 2026.[^cite_note-3] Morgan was the first woman and the first member of the House of Lords to serve as First Minister.[^cite_note-\:4-4][^cite_note-\:5-5] Before becoming First Minister, she served as [Cabinet Secretary for Health and Social Care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_for_Health_and_Social_Care_\(Wales\)) from 2021 to 2024.[^cite_note-Health_Minister-1][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Morgan has been a member of the [House of Lords](13658.md) since 2011, a [member of the Senedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Senedd) (MS) from 2016 to 2026 and was a [member of the European Parliament](258909.md) (MEP) from 1994 to 2009.&#x20;

A [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) speaker,[^cite_note-8] Morgan served as [Minister for the Welsh Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government) from 2017 to 2021 as well as [Minister for Mental Health and Wellbeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Mental_Health_and_Wellbeing) from 2020 to 2021.

As leader of Welsh Labour and First Minister, Morgan has revived Welsh Labour's [clear red water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_red_water) strategy used by some of her predecessors, shifting Welsh Labour back to the [political left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_left) and distancing it from the centrist [UK Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Labour) leadership of [Keir Starmer](18681491.md), criticising some of the policies implemented by Starmer's government.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Having led the party into the [2026 Senedd elections](68245236.md), Morgan lost her seat contesting the [Ceredigion Penfro constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceredigion_Penfro), becoming the first leader of a government in the UK to be unseated while still in office.[^cite_note-13] She subsequently announced that she would stand down as leader of Welsh Labour, initiating the [2026 Welsh Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Welsh_Labour_leadership_election).[^cite_note-14]

## Personal life and education

Morgan was born and brought up in [Ely, Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Cardiff), the daughter of [Bob Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Morgan_\(priest\)) and Elaine Morgan.[^cite_note-15] She was educated at [Ysgol Gyfun Gymraeg Glantaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ysgol_Gyfun_Gymraeg_Glantaf), as well as, through a scholarship, the independent [United World College of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_World_College_of_the_Atlantic), and later gained a degree in European Studies from the [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:0-17]

Morgan is married to Rhys Jenkins, who is a GP and also a [non-stipendiary priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-supporting_minister).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Morgan's family hails from [St Davids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Davids) in [Pembrokeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembrokeshire).[^cite_note-20]

In March 2022, Morgan was banned from driving for six months following repeated speeding fines. The offence which took her over the 12-point limit was on a 30 mph road in Wrexham.[^cite_note-21]

She has been described as a "committed Christian".[^cite_note-22]

## Professional career and voluntary positions

Morgan formerly worked as a researcher for [S4C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S4C), Agenda TV and the [BBC](19344654.md).[^cite_note-23]

After leaving the European Parliament she worked as the Director of National Development for SSE in Wales ([SWALEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWALEC)) from 2009 to June 2013 where she was responsible for establishing the new [SWALEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWALEC) Smart Energy Centre in [Treforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treforest). She was appointed Chair of the Cardiff Business Partnership.

Morgan is a Fellow of [Trinity College Carmarthen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Carmarthen)[^cite_note-24] and is an Honorary-Distinguished Professor and Fellow of Cardiff University.[^cite_note-25] She served on the board of the [International Baccalaureate Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate_Organisation) for three years.[^cite_note-26] She was the Chair of the Cardiff Business Partnership.[^cite_note-27] She was a member of the External Advisory Board to the Wales Governance Centre.[^cite_note-28] She served on the Council of [Atlantic College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_College). She was Chair of [Live Music Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Music_Now) in Wales, a charity which sends talented young musicians to care homes and special schools and demonstrates the transformational impact of music, from 2012 to 2016.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-\:2-30]

## Political career

### Welsh Labour and Yes for Wales

Morgan served on the Welsh Labour Party Executive for ten years and was appointed to the Welsh Assembly Advisory Group, which was responsible for developing the standing orders of the Senedd. She was a founding member of the [Yes for Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_for_Wales) [cross-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisanship) group, which campaigned for the [Welsh Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Assembly) to be established.[^cite_note-31]

### European Parliament



Morgan as an [MEP](258909.md) at a meeting of the European Parliament in Strasbourg in 1995

In 1990, Morgan worked as a [stagiaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intern) in the European Parliament for the Socialist Group.

In 1994, Morgan was elected as a [Member of the European Parliament](258909.md) representing [Mid and West Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_and_West_Wales_\(European_Parliament_constituency\)). At the time she was the youngest MEP when she took up her seat, aged 27.[^cite_note-32] She continued as an MEP representing the [Wales](1837740.md) constituency, being elected at both the 1999 and 2004 elections, before standing down at the 2009 elections.[^cite_note-\:1-33]

Morgan served as the budget control spokesperson for the 180 strong [Socialist Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance_of_Socialists_and_Democrats). She was also the Labour Party's European spokesperson on Energy, Industry and Science.[^cite_note-34] She was responsible for drafting the European Parliament's response to the Energy Green Paper and also took the lead role in negotiating on behalf of the Parliament the revision of the Electricity Directive.

### House of Lords

On 19 November 2010 it was announced that Morgan had been granted a [life peerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peerage) and would sit on the Labour benches of the [House of Lords](13658.md),[^cite_note-35] and was gazetted on 27 January 2011 as **Baroness Morgan of Ely**, in the [City of Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Cardiff).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] From 2013–2016, Morgan served as the Shadow Minister for Wales in the House of Lords, and from 2014 to 2016 she served as Shadow Minister for Foreign Affairs and also as a whip.[^cite_note-\:2-30] She was responsible for leading for Labour in the House of Lords on the EU Referendum Bill and led for Labour on two Wales Bills.[^cite_note-\:3-38]

Morgan is currently on a [leave of absence](507071.md) from the House of Lords, and has been since 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-30] In 2018, Morgan stated that if she were to become First Minister, she would renounce her peerage.[^cite_note-39] In 2024, when Morgan became First Minister, she instead stated she wanted to 'pause' her peerage, and 'have that opportunity if necessary to think about what happens in the future.'[^cite_note-\:5-5][^cite_note-40]

### Senedd and Welsh Government

A video of Morgan taking the daily COVID-19 press conference in November 2020

In 2015 Morgan was selected as a candidate for the [2016 Welsh election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election) on the [Mid and West Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_and_West_Wales_\(Senedd_electoral_region\)) regional list.[^cite_note-41] On 5 May 2016 she was elected from the regional list as an [Assembly Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Senedd) in the [Senedd](238480.md).

In November 2017 she was appointed Minister for Welsh Language and Lifelong Learning.[^cite_note-42] She contested the [2018 Welsh Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Welsh_Labour_leadership_election), but was not successful, coming third. She was then appointed by First Minister [Mark Drakeford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Drakeford) as Minister for International Relations and the Welsh Language in December 2018 before being moved to [Minister for Mental Health, Wellbeing and the Welsh Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Mental_Health,_Wellbeing_and_the_Welsh_Language?action=edit\&redlink=1) in October 2020.

Morgan was re-elected at the [2021 Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Senedd_election),[^cite_note-43] and a week later was appointed [Minister for Health and Social Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_for_Health_and_Social_Care_\(Wales\)).[^cite_note-44] In March 2023, the [Welsh Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Conservatives) submitted a motion of no confidence in her, following mounting criticism of Morgan's handling of the [Betsi Cadwaladr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsi_Cadwaladr_University_Health_Board) crisis. The motion was defeated, with 26 votes in favour and 29 against.[^cite_note-45]

Morgan endorsed [Vaughan Gething](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughan_Gething) in the [February–March 2024 Welsh Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February%E2%80%93March_2024_Welsh_Labour_leadership_election).[^cite_note-46] She retained her role as [Cabinet Secretary for Health and Social Care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_for_Health_and_Social_Care_\(Wales\)) in the [Gething government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gething_government).[^cite_note-47]

#### Leader of Welsh Labour

In July 2024, Morgan announced that she would be standing in the [July 2024 Welsh Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2024_Welsh_Labour_leadership_election) on a [unity ticket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_ticket) with [Huw Irranca-Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huw_Irranca-Davies), who would serve as her [deputy First Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_First_Minister_of_Wales) if she was elected leader.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] On 24 July 2024, she was confirmed to be the only candidate that met the five–nomination threshold, and was therefore elected as leader of Welsh Labour.[^cite_note-50]

### First Minister of Wales (2024–2026)

See also: [Eluned Morgan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eluned_Morgan_government)



Morgan being [sworn in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sworn_in) as First Minister on 6 August 2024

First Minister Eluned Morgan meeting Prime Minister Keir Starmer in Cardiff

Morgan meeting Prime Minister Keir Starmer in Cardiff

On 6 August 2024, Morgan was officially sworn in as the First Minister of Wales, following her nomination by the [Senedd](238480.md), which was recalled from its summer recess for this purpose. As the leader of the largest party in the Senedd,[^cite_note-\:4-4] Morgan secured the position with 28 votes, marking the beginning of her tenure as Wales' first female First Minister.[^cite_note-51]

Upon entering government as the First Minister, one of the first issues to face Morgan was the prospect of the [2024 United Kingdom riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_riots) beginning in Wales. In a statement, Morgan said that she was "not complacent", further adding that Wales was a "society and a nation that should be welcoming people... we cannot let those people who are determined to be destructive within our communities to get a hold".[^cite_note-52]

Shortly after taking office, Morgan met with [Prime Minister](24150.md) [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) in Cardiff during his visit to Wales.[^cite_note-53] The meeting focused on "resetting the relationship" between the [UK](25318118.md) and [Welsh governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government). Key topics included [NHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_Wales) funding, energy independence, and the future of [Tata Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel_Europe) jobs in Wales. They also discussed the development of renewable energy through the Welsh Government's initiative, [Trydan Gwyrdd Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trydan_Gwyrdd_Cymru), aimed at producing clean energy and creating skilled jobs in Wales.[^cite_note-54]

On 2 October 2024, she was sworn into the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)).[^cite_note-55]

#### 2026 election

In the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md), she was a candidate in the [Ceredigion Penfro constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceredigion_Penfro).[^cite_note-56] She was also the lead candidate on the party list.[^cite_note-57] Eluned Morgan was alleged to have intervened in the controversial Labour selection in Cardiff, after would-be candidate Owain Williams was initially ruled out on an administrative issue.[^cite_note-58] On 23 April, she admitted that she was at risk of [losing her seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseating) in a "knife edge election".[^cite_note-59]

On 8 May 2026, Morgan lost her seat in the Senedd and she subsequently resigned as leader of [Welsh Labour](6732798.md) with immediate effect,[^cite_note-60] which triggered the [2026 Welsh Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Welsh_Labour_leadership_election).
