***Axios*** (styled **ΛXIOS** in the logo) is an American news website based in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia). It was founded in 2016 and launched the following year by former *[Politico](9172097.md)* journalists [Jim VandeHei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_VandeHei), [Mike Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Allen_\(journalist\)), and Roy Schwartz. The site's name is based on the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἄξιος (*áxios*, lit. 'worthy').[^cite_note-VF1-2]

*Axios* articles are often brief to facilitate quick reading; most are shorter than 300 words and use bullet points. In addition to news articles, *Axios* produces daily and weekly industry-specific [newsletters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsletter) (including Allen's *Axios AM*, a successor to his newsletter *[Politico Playbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politico_Playbook)* for *Politico*).[^cite_note-VF1-2]

In September 2022, [Cox Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_Enterprises) completed its acquisition of *Axios* for \$525 million.[^cite_note-arlnow-3][^cite_note-4]

## History

VandeHei said he wanted *Axios* to be a "mix between *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* and [Twitter](9988187.md)", which attracted criticism.[^cite_note-5] The company initially covered a mix of business, politics, technology, health care, and media. VandeHei said *Axios* would focus on the "collision between tech and areas such as bureaucracy, healthcare, energy, and the transportation infrastructure".[^cite_note-VF1-2] At its launch, Nicholas Johnston, a former managing editor at [Bloomberg L.P.](432006.md), was named its editor-in-chief.[^cite_note-\:4-6]

In 2016, *Axios* secured \$10 million in a round of financing led by Lerer Hippeau Ventures. Backers include media-partner [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News), [Laurene Powell Jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurene_Powell_Jobs)'s [Emerson Collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson_Collective), [Greycroft Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greycroft_Partners), and David and Katherine Bradley, owners of [Atlantic Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Media).[^cite_note-VF1-2] The company had raised \$30 million as of November 2017.[^cite_note-\:3-7][^cite_note-8] It planned to focus on "business, technology, politics, and media trends".[^cite_note-VF1-2] *Axios* generates revenue through short-form [native advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_advertising) and sponsored newsletters.[^cite_note-9] It earned more than \$10 million in revenue in its first seven months.[^cite_note-\:3-7]

In January 2017, *Axios* hired as an [executive vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_vice_president) [Evan Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Ryan), the [Assistant Secretary of State for Educational and Cultural Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Secretary_of_State_for_Educational_and_Cultural_Affairs) and a former staffer for Vice President [Joe Biden](145422.md).[^cite_note-\:2-10] *Axios* had 6 million unique visitors in September 2017, according to [Comscore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comscore). As of November 2017, *Axios* reported it had 200,000 subscribers to 11 newsletters, with an average [open rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_rate) of 52 percent. The same month, it said it would use a new \$20 million investment to expand data analysis, product development, fund audience growth, and increase staff to 150, up from 89.[^cite_note-\:3-7]

In March and April 2019, *[HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)* and *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\))* reported that *Axios* had paid a firm to improve its reputation by lobbying for changes to the [Wikipedia](5043734.md) articles on *Axios* and [Jonathan Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Swan).[^cite_note-HuffPost-11][^cite_note-Wired-12]

In July 2020, under the [first presidency of Donald Trump](52231341.md), *Axios* received \$4.8 million in federal loans from the [Paycheck Protection Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paycheck_Protection_Program) for salary replacement during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). It later returned the money, with VandeHei explaining that the loans had become "politically polarizing".[^cite_note-13] In September 2020, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that *Axios* was on track to be profitable in 2020 "despite the economic turmoil stemming from the coronavirus that led to broad layoffs and pay cuts at many media outlets".[^cite_note-14]

In May 2021, *The Wall Street Journal* reported that merger discussions between *Axios* and *[The Athletic](54936152.md)* had ended, with *The Athletic* opting to pursue a deal with *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Company)*.[^cite_note-15]

On August 8, 2022, *Axios* announced that it had been sold to [Cox Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_Enterprises) for \$525 million. As part of the transaction, *Axios* spun out [Axios HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axios_HQ) as a separate software company.[^cite_note-NYTimesSold-16][^cite_note-\:0-17] According to the deal, Cox owns 70% of the company, while *Axios* employees and its founders retain ownership of the remaining 30%.[^cite_note-wapo-cox-1] The acquisition was completed the following month.[^cite_note-arlnow-3]

Since 2021, *Axios* has launched a network of local newsletters across the United States.[^cite_note-18] The company has also focused on growing its events business.[^cite_note-19]

In March 2023, *Axios* fired Ben Montgomery, a [Pulitzer Prize](24230.md) finalist, after he described as "propaganda" a [Florida Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Department_of_Education) press release about an event hosted by Governor [Ron DeSantis](36729527.md) "exposing the [diversity, equity and inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity,_equity_and_inclusion) scam in higher education".[^cite_note-20]

In August 2024, *Axios* laid off 50 employees, which amounted to 10 percent of its total staff.[^cite_note-21]

## Content

*Axios* content is designed for digital platforms, such as [Facebook](7529378.md) and [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat), as well as its own website.[^cite_note-VF1-2] Its articles are typically less than 300 words long, as most *Axios* content is geared for quick, concise reads.[^cite_note-22] In addition to its website, *Axios* content is distributed via newsletters covering politics, technology, healthcare, and other subjects.[^cite_note-\:1-23] Among the newsletters is a daily report by co-founder Mike Allen, who formerly wrote *Politico*'s *Playbook* newsletter.[^cite_note-VF1-2] Some *Axios* newsletters are free, while others are paid. The company sells a subscription service called *Axios Pro* that bundles several paid newsletters, starting at \$599 per year.[^cite_note-\:1-23]

*Axios* reporters have made television appearances on NBC News and [MSNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC) under an agreement with NBC, secured in 2017.[^cite_note-\:4-6] Its [NBC Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Universal) partnership has featured Allen on MSNBC's show *[Morning Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Joe)*.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

In 2021, the documentary series *Axios* on [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) won the News and Documentary [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards) for Outstanding Interview for the interview with President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) conducted by [Jonathan Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Swan).[^cite_note-26]

In May 2026, a report by *[Press Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Gazette)* revealed that *Axios* had cut content output by 22% in the first quarter of the year, while traffic increased 30%, signaling a change in editorial approach.[^cite_note-27]
