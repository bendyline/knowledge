**Latvia**,[^cite_note-15] officially the **Republic of Latvia**,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] is a country in the [Baltic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_region) of [northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe). It is one of the three [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), along with [Estonia](28222445.md) to the north and [Lithuania](17675.md) to the south. It borders [Russia](25391.md) to the east and [Belarus](3457.md) to the southeast and shares a [maritime border](29613061.md) with [Sweden](5058739.md) to the west. Latvia covers an area of 64,573 km<sup>2</sup> (24,932 sq mi),[^cite_note-18] with a population of 1.83 million.[^cite_note-19] The country has a [temperate seasonal climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate).[^cite_note-20] Its capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Latvia) is [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga). [Latvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvians), who are the [titular nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_nation) and comprise 65.5% of the country's population, belong to the [ethnolinguistic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnolinguistic_group) of the [Balts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts) and speak [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language). [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Latvia) are the most prominent minority in the country, at almost a quarter of the population; 37.7% of the population speak [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) as their native tongue.[^cite_note-eng.lsm.lv-21]

After centuries of [Teutonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order), [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Livonia), [Polish-Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflanty_Voivodeship), and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) rule, the independent Republic of Latvia was established on 18 November 1918 after breaking away from the Russian Empire in the aftermath of [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-Germanis-4] The country became increasingly autocratic after the [coup in 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Latvian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) established the dictatorship of [Kārlis Ulmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81rlis_Ulmanis).[^cite_note-22] Latvia's *de facto* independence was interrupted at the outset of [World War II](32927.md), beginning with [forcible incorporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Latvia_in_1940) into the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), followed by the [invasion and occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Latvia_during_World_War_II) by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in 1941 and the [re-occupation by the Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_re-occupation_of_Latvia_in_1944) in 1944, which formed the [Latvian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) for the next 45 years. As a result of extensive [immigration during the Soviet occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Latvia), ethnic Russians became the most prominent minority in the country. The peaceful [Singing Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singing_Revolution) started in 1987 among the Baltic Soviet republics and ended with the restoration of both *de facto* and [official independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_continuity_of_the_Baltic_states) on 21 August 1991.[^cite_note-23] Latvia has since been a democratic [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic).

Latvia is a [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) with a [high-income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_high-income_economy), [advanced economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Latvia) ranking 39th in the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index). It is a member of the [European Union](9317.md), [Eurozone](184391.md), [NATO](21133.md), the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), the [United Nations](31769.md), the [Council of the Baltic Sea States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Baltic_Sea_States), the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), the [Nordic-Baltic Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic-Baltic_Eight), the [Nordic Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Investment_Bank), the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development), the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md), and the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization).

## Etymology

The name *Latvija* is derived from the name of the ancient [Latgalians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgalians), one of four [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) [Baltic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts) (along with [Curonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curonians), [Selonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selonians) and [Semigallians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigallians)), which formed the ethnic core of modern [Latvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvians) together with the [Finnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Finnic_peoples) [Livonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonians).[^cite_note-24] [Henry of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_of_Latvia) coined the [latinisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinisation_of_names) of the country's name, "Lettigallia" and "Lethia", both derived from the Latgalians. The terms inspired the variations on the country's name in [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) from "Letonia" and in several [Germanic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) from "Lettland".[^cite_note-25]

## History

Main article: [History of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Latvia)



Baltic tribes before the coming of the [Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Order) (c. 1200 AD). The East Balts are shown in brown hues while the West Balts are shown in green. The boundaries are approximate.

Around 3000 BC, the Proto-Baltic ancestors of the Latvian people settled on the eastern coast of the [Baltic Sea](3335.md).[^cite_note-26] The [Balts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts) established trade routes to Rome and [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium), trading local [amber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber) for precious metals.[^cite_note-27] By 900 AD, four distinct Baltic tribes inhabited Latvia: [Curonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curonians), [Latgalians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgalians), [Selonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selonians), [Semigallians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigallians) (in Latvian: *kurši*, *latgaļi*, *sēļi* and *zemgaļi*), as well as the Finnic tribe of [Livonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_people) (*lībieši*) speaking a Finnic language.[^cite_note-28]

In the 12th century in the territory of Latvia, there were lands with their rulers: [Vanema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanema?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Ventava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventava_\(land\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Bandava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandava), [Piemare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piemare), [Duvzare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duvzare?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Selen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selen_\(land\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Koknese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Koknese), [Jersika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersika), [Tālava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81lava) and [Atzele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atzele?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-29]

### Medieval period

Although the local people had contact with the outside world for centuries, they became more fully integrated into the European socio-political system in the 12th century.[^cite_note-30] The first missionaries, sent by the pope, sailed up the [Daugava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugava) River in the late 12th century, seeking converts.[^cite_note-Lonely-31] The local people, however, did not convert to Christianity as readily as the church had hoped.[^cite_note-Lonely-31]



[Turaida Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turaida_Castle) near [Sigulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigulda), built in 1214 under [Albert of Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_of_Riga)

At the beginning of the 13th century, Germans ruled large parts of what is currently Latvia.[^cite_note-Lonely-31] The influx of German crusaders especially increased in the second half of the 13th century following the [decline and fall of the Crusader States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades#Crusades_and_the_Holy_Land,_1095%E2%80%931291) in the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-32] Together with southern Estonia, these conquered areas formed the [crusader state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states) that became known as [Terra Mariana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Mariana) ([Medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) for "Land of [Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_\(mother_of_Jesus\))") or Livonia.[^cite_note-33] In 1282, [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), and later the cities of [Cēsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%93sis), [Limbaži](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limba%C5%BEi), [Koknese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koknese) and [Valmiera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiera), became part of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League).[^cite_note-Lonely-31] Riga became an important point of east–west trading[^cite_note-Lonely-31] and formed close cultural links with [western Europe](33800.md).[^cite_note-34] The first German settlers were knights from northern Germany and citizens of northern German towns who brought their [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) language to the region, which shaped many loanwords in the Latvian language.[^cite_note-35]

### Reformation period and Lithuanian, Polish, and Swedish rule

Main articles: [Swedish Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Livonia), [Duchy of Courland and Semigallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Courland_and_Semigallia), [Duchy of Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Livonia), and [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth)



The [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) at its largest extent; modern-day boundaries are also shown.



The [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) (1560–1815)

After the [Livonian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_War) (1558–1583), Livonia (northern Latvia and southern Estonia) fell under the hegemony of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).[^cite_note-Lonely-31] The lands were ceded to the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) and formed into the [Duchy of Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Livonia) (*Ducatus Livoniae Ultradunensis*). [Gotthard Kettler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Kettler), the last master of the [Order of Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_Order), formed the [Duchy of Courland and Semigallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Courland_and_Semigallia).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-Matulevicius-37] Though the duchy was a vassal state to the Lithuanian Grand Duchy and later of Poland–Lithuania, it retained a considerable degree of autonomy and experienced a golden age in the 16th century.[^cite_note-Matulevicius-37] [Latgalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgalia), the easternmost region of Latvia, became a part of the [Inflanty Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflanty_Voivodeship) of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.[^cite_note-38]

In the 17th and early 18th centuries, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire), and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) struggled for supremacy in the eastern Baltic. After the [Polish–Swedish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Swedish_War_\(1600%E2%80%931611\)), northern Livonia (including Vidzeme) came under Swedish rule. Riga became the capital of [Swedish Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Livonia) and the largest city in the entire Swedish Empire.[^cite_note-39] Fighting continued sporadically between Sweden and Poland until the [Truce of Altmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truce_of_Altmark) in 1629.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] In Latvia, the Swedish period is generally remembered as positive; serfdom was eased, a network of schools was established for the peasantry, and the power of the regional [barons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Germans) was diminished.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Several important cultural changes occurred during this time. Under Swedish and largely German rule, western Latvia adopted [Lutheranism](23371382.md) as its main religion.[^cite_note-44] The ancient tribes of the Couronians, Semigallians, Selonians, Livs, and northern Latgallians assimilated to form the Latvian people, speaking one Latvian language.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] An actual Latvian state had not been established, so the borders and definitions of who fell within that group are largely subjective. Meanwhile, largely isolated from the rest of Latvia, southern Latgallians adopted [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) under Polish/[Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) influence. The native dialect remained distinct, although it acquired many Polish and Russian loanwords.[^cite_note-47]

### Livonia and Courland in the Russian Empire

During the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) (1700–1721), up to 40 percent of Latvians died from famine and plague.[^cite_note-48] Half the residents of Riga were killed by [plague in 1710–1711](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War_plague_outbreak).[^cite_note-49] The [capitulation of Estonia and Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitulation_of_Estonia_and_Livonia) in 1710 and the [Treaty of Nystad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nystad), ending the Great Northern War in 1721, gave Vidzeme to Russia (it became part of the [Riga Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_of_Livonia)). The Latgale region remained part of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth as Inflanty Voivodeship until 1772, when it was incorporated into Russia. The [Duchy of Courland and Semigallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Courland_and_Semigallia), a vassal state of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, was annexed by Russia in 1795 in the [Third Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland), bringing all of what is now Latvia into the Russian Empire. All three Baltic provinces preserved local laws, German as the local [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) and their own parliament, the [Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag).

The emancipation of the serfs took place in Courland in 1817 and in Vidzeme in 1819.[^cite_note-50] In practice, however, the emancipation was actually advantageous to the landowners and nobility, as it dispossessed peasants of their land without compensation, forcing them to return to work at the estates "of their own free will".[^cite_note-51] During these two centuries Latvia experienced economic and construction boom – ports were expanded (Riga became the largest port in the Russian Empire), railways built; new factories, banks, and a university were established; many residential, public (theatres and museums), and school buildings were erected; new parks formed; and so on. Riga's boulevards and some streets outside the Old Town date from this period. [Numeracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeracy) was also higher in the Livonian and Courlandian parts of the Russian Empire, which may have been influenced by the Protestant religion of the inhabitants.[^cite_note-52]



Latvians national rally in [Dundaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundaga) in 1905

During the 19th century, the social structure changed dramatically.[^cite_note-LNA-53] A class of independent farmers established itself after reforms allowed the peasants to repurchase their land, but many landless peasants remained. Many Latvians left for the cities and sought education and industrial jobs.[^cite_note-LNA-53] There also developed a growing urban [proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletariat) and an increasingly influential Latvian [bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie).[^cite_note-LNA-53] The [Young Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Latvians) ([Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *Jaunlatvieši*) movement laid the groundwork for nationalism from the middle of the century, many of its leaders looking to the [Slavophiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavophilia) for support against the prevailing German-dominated social order.[^cite_note-YoungLatvians-54][^cite_note-55] The rise in use of the Latvian language in literature and society became known as the [First National Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Awakening).[^cite_note-YoungLatvians-54] [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) began in Latgale after the Polish led the [January Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising) in 1863: this spread to the rest of what is now Latvia by the 1880s. The Young Latvians were largely eclipsed by the [New Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Current), a broad leftist social and political movement, in the 1890s.[^cite_note-56] Popular discontent exploded in the [1905 Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1905), which took a nationalist character in the Baltic provinces.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

### Declaration of independence and interwar period



[Jānis Čakste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C4%81nis_%C4%8Cakste) (1859–1927), the first [president of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia)

[World War I](4764461.md) devastated the territory[^cite_note-60] and other western parts of the Russian Empire. Demands for [self-determination](29269.md) were initially confined to [autonomy](191145.md), until a power vacuum was created by the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) in 1917, followed by the [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk) between Russia and Germany in March 1918, then the Allied [armistice with Germany on 11 November 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_11_November_1918). The [People's Council of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Council_of_Latvia) proclaimed independence, and [Kārlis Ulmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81rlis_Ulmanis) was entrusted to set up a government and he took the position of prime minister.[^cite_note-61] The general representative of Germany [August Winnig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Winnig) formally handed over political power to the Latvian provisional government on 26 November, and he became its first prime minister.[^cite_note-62]

The [war of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_War_of_Independence) that followed was part of a general chaotic period of civil and new border wars in eastern Europe. By the spring of 1919, there were actually three governments: the provisional government headed by Ulmanis, supported by the [Tautas padome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautas_padome) and the [Inter-Allied Commission of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Inter-Allied_Commission_of_Control); the [Latvian Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Socialist_Soviet_Republic) led by [Pēteris Stučka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%93teris_Stu%C4%8Dka), supported by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army); and the provisional government headed by [Andrievs Niedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrievs_Niedra), supported by [Baltic-German forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freikorps_in_the_Baltic) composed of the *[Baltische Landeswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltische_Landeswehr)* ("Baltic Defence Force") and the [Freikorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freikorps) formation *[Eiserne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiserne_Division)* ("Iron Division"). Estonian and Latvian forces defeated the Germans at the [Battle of Wenden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_C%C4%93sis_\(1919\)) in June 1919,[^cite_note-63] and a massive attack by a predominantly German force—the [West Russian Volunteer Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Russian_Volunteer_Army)—under [Pavel Bermondt-Avalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Bermondt-Avalov) was repelled in November. Eastern Latvia was cleared of Red Army forces by Latvian and Polish troops in early 1920 (from the Polish perspective the [Battle of Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Daugavpils) was a part of the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Soviet_War)).

A freely elected [constituent assembly](266437.md) convened on 1 May 1920 and adopted a liberal constitution, the *[Satversme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Latvia)*, in February 1922.[^cite_note-64] On 15 May 1934, Ulmanis staged [a bloodless coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Latvian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), establishing a nationalist dictatorship that lasted until 1940.[^cite_note-Country_profile-65] After 1934, Ulmanis established [government corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_corporation) to buy up private firms with the aim of "Latvianising" the economy.[^cite_note-66] The constitution was partly suspended by Ulmanis after his coup in 1934 but reaffirmed in 1990. Since then, it has been amended and is still in effect in Latvia today. With most of Latvia's industrial base evacuated to the interior of Russia in 1915, radical land reform was the central political question for the young state. In 1897, 61.2% of the rural population had been landless; by 1936, that percentage had been reduced to 18%.[^cite_note-67]

### Occupations, 1940–1990

Main articles: [Soviet occupation of Latvia in 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Latvia_in_1940), [German occupation of Latvia during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Latvia_during_World_War_II), [Occupation of Latvia by Soviet Union 1944–1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Latvia_by_Soviet_Union_1944%E2%80%931945), and [Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)

See also: [The Holocaust in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Latvia), [Latvian partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_partisans), and [Latvian anti-Nazi resistance movement 1941–45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_anti-Nazi_resistance_movement_1941%E2%80%9345)



[Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) troops enter [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) (1940)

Early in the morning of 24 August 1939, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Nazi Germany](21212.md) signed a 10-year non-aggression pact, called the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact).[^cite_note-68] The pact contained a secret protocol, revealed only after Germany's defeat in 1945, according to which the states of Northern and Eastern Europe were divided into German and Soviet "[spheres of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_of_influence)".[^cite_note-mrtext-69] In the north, Latvia, Finland and Estonia were assigned to the Soviet sphere.[^cite_note-mrtext-69]

After the conclusion of the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact, most of the [Baltic Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Germans) left Latvia by agreement between Ulmanis's government and Nazi Germany under the [Heim ins Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heim_ins_Reich) programme.[^cite_note-Latvia_in_WWII-70] Most of those who remained left for Germany in summer 1940, when a second resettlement scheme was agreed.[^cite_note-71] The racially approved being resettled mainly in Poland, being given land and businesses in exchange for the money they had received from the sale of their previous assets.[^cite_note-PBBG-72]<sup>: 46 </sup>

On 5 October 1939, Latvia was forced to accept a "mutual assistance" pact with the Soviet Union, granting the Soviets the right to station between 25,000 and 30,000 troops on Latvian territory.[^cite_note-73] State administrators were murdered and replaced by Soviet cadres.[^cite_note-wettig20-74] Elections were held with single pro-Soviet candidates listed for many positions. The resulting people's assembly immediately requested admission into the USSR, which the Soviet Union granted.[^cite_note-wettig20-74] Latvia, then a puppet government, was headed by [Augusts Kirhenšteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusts_Kirhen%C5%A1teins).[^cite_note-75] The Soviet Union incorporated Latvia on 5 August 1940, as the *[Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).*



German soldiers enter Riga, July 1941.

The Soviets dealt harshly with their [perceived opponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_of_the_people#Soviet_Union) – prior to [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), in less than a year, at least 34,250 Latvians were [deported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_deportation) or killed.[^cite_note-76] Most were deported to Siberia where deaths were estimated at 40 percent.[^cite_note-PBBG-72]<sup>: 48 </sup>

On 22 June 1941, German troops attacked Soviet forces in Operation Barbarossa.[^cite_note-77] There were some spontaneous uprisings by Latvians against the Red Army which helped the Germans. By 29 June [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) was reached and with Soviet troops killed, captured or retreating, Latvia was left under the control of German forces by early July.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-PBBG-72]<sup>: 78–96 </sup>

Under German occupation, Latvia was administered as part of *[Reichskommissariat Ostland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ostland)*.[^cite_note-79] Latvian paramilitary and [Auxiliary Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arajs_Kommando) units established by the occupation authority participated in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) and other atrocities.[^cite_note-Country_profile-65] 30,000 Jews were shot in Latvia in the autumn of 1941.[^cite_note-PBBG-72]<sup>: 127 </sup> Another 30,000 Jews from the Riga ghetto were killed in the Rumbula Forest in November and December 1941, to reduce overpopulation in the ghetto and make room for more Jews being brought in from Germany and the West.[^cite_note-PBBG-72]<sup>: 128 </sup> There was a pause in fighting, apart from partisan activity, until after the [siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leningrad) ended in January 1944, and the Soviet troops advanced, entering Latvia in July and eventually capturing Riga on 13 October 1944.[^cite_note-PBBG-72]<sup>: 271 </sup>



[Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) soldiers in front of the [Freedom Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Monument) in Riga in 1944

The Soviets reoccupied the country in 1944–1945, and further [deportations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Priboi) followed as the country was [collectivised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivization_in_the_Soviet_Union) and [Sovietised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovietisation).[^cite_note-Country_profile-65]

In the post-war period, Latvia was made to adopt Soviet farming methods. Rural areas were forced into [collectivization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivization_in_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-80] An extensive program to impose [bilingualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingualism) was initiated in Latvia, limiting the use of Latvian language in official uses in favor of using Russian as the main language. All of the minority schools (Jewish, Polish, Belarusian, Estonian, Lithuanian) were closed down leaving only two media of instructions in the schools: Latvian and Russian.[^cite_note-81] An influx of new colonists, including laborers, administrators, military personnel and their dependents from Russia and other Soviet republics started. By 1959 about 400,000 Russian settlers arrived and the ethnic Latvian population had fallen to 62%.[^cite_note-82]

Since Latvia had maintained a well-developed infrastructure and educated specialists, Moscow decided to base some of the Soviet Union's most advanced manufacturing in Latvia. New industry was created in Latvia, including a major [machinery factory RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_Autobus_Factory) in [Jelgava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelgava), electrotechnical factories in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), chemical factories in [Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugavpils), [Valmiera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiera) and [Olaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaine)—and some food and oil processing plants.[^cite_note-83] Latvia manufactured trains, ships, minibuses, mopeds, telephones, radios and hi-fi systems, electrical and diesel engines, textiles, furniture, clothing, bags and luggage, shoes, musical instruments, home appliances, watches, tools and equipment, aviation and agricultural equipment and long list of other goods. Latvia had its own film industry and musical records factory (LPs). However, there were not enough people to operate the newly built factories. To maintain and expand industrial production, skilled workers were migrating from all over the Soviet Union, decreasing the proportion of ethnic Latvians in the republic.[^cite_note-84] The population of Latvia reached its peak in 1990 at just under 2.7 million people.

In the second half of the 1980s, Soviet leader [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev) started to introduce political and economic reforms in the Soviet Union that were called [glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost) and [perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika). In the summer of 1987, the first large demonstrations were held in Riga at the [Freedom Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Monument)—a symbol of independence. In the summer of 1988, a national movement, coalescing in the [Popular Front of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_of_Latvia), was opposed by the [Interfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Front_of_the_Working_People_of_Latvia). The Latvian SSR, along with the other [Baltic Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) was allowed greater autonomy, and in 1988, the old pre-war [Flag of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Latvia) flew again, replacing the Soviet Latvian flag as the official flag in 1990.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] In 1989, the [Supreme Soviet of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_USSR) adopted a resolution on the *[Occupation of the Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Baltic_states)*, in which it declared the occupation "not in accordance with law", and not the "will of the Soviet people". Pro-independence Popular Front of Latvia candidates gained a two-thirds majority in the [Supreme Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_of_the_Republic_of_Latvia) in the [March 1990 democratic elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Latvian_Supreme_Soviet_election).

### 1990–present

Further information: [Singing Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singing_Revolution), [Baltic Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Way), and [On the Restoration of Independence of the Republic of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Restoration_of_Independence_of_the_Republic_of_Latvia)



Barricade in Riga to prevent the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Army) from reaching the [Latvian Parliament](499765.md) in July 1991

On 4 May 1990, the Supreme Council adopted the [Declaration on the Restoration of Independence of the Republic of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_on_the_Restoration_of_Independence_of_the_Republic_of_Latvia), and the Latvian SSR was renamed Republic of Latvia.[^cite_note-Imagining-87]

However, the central power in Moscow continued to regard Latvia as a Soviet republic in 1990 and 1991. In January 1991, Soviet political and military forces unsuccessfully tried to overthrow the Republic of Latvia authorities by occupying the central publishing house in Riga and establishing a Committee of National Salvation to usurp governmental functions. During the transitional period, Moscow maintained many central Soviet state authorities in Latvia.[^cite_note-Imagining-87]

[The Popular Front of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_of_Latvia) advocated that all permanent residents be eligible for Latvian citizenship, however, universal citizenship for all permanent residents was not adopted. Instead, citizenship was granted to persons who had been citizens of Latvia on the day of loss of independence in 1940 as well as their descendants. As a consequence, the majority of ethnic non-Latvians did not receive Latvian citizenship since neither they nor their parents had ever been citizens of Latvia, becoming [non-citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-citizens_\(Latvia\)) or citizens of other former Soviet republics. By 2011, more than half of non-citizens had taken [naturalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization) exams and received Latvian citizenship, but in 2015 there were still 290,660 non-citizens in Latvia, which represented 14.1% of the population. They have [no citizenship of any country](789786.md), and cannot participate in the parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-88] Children born to non-nationals or stateless persons after the re-establishment of independence on 21 August 1991 are automatically entitled to citizenship.[^cite_note-89]

The Republic of Latvia declared the end of the transitional period and restored full independence on 21 August 1991, in the aftermath of the failed [Soviet coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt).[^cite_note-FINemb-5] Latvia resumed diplomatic relations with Western states, including Sweden.[^cite_note-90] The [Saeima](499765.md), Latvia's parliament, was again elected in 1993. Russia ended its military presence by completing its troop withdrawal in 1994 and shutting down the [Skrunda-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skrunda-1) radar station in 1998.

The major goals of Latvia in the 1990s, to join [NATO](21133.md) and the [European Union](9317.md), were achieved in 2004. The [NATO Summit 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Summit_2006) was held in Riga.[^cite_note-91] [Vaira Vīķe-Freiberga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaira_V%C4%AB%C4%B7e-Freiberga) was [President of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia) from 1999 until 2007. She was the first female head of state in the former Soviet bloc state and was active in Latvia joining both NATO and the European Union in 2004.[^cite_note-92] Latvia signed the [Schengen agreement](179592.md) on 16 April 2003 and started its implementation on 21 December 2007.[^cite_note-93]



The ceremonial meeting of the [Saeima](499765.md) in 2018 in honor of the 100th anniversary of the proclamation of the Republic of Latvia at the [Latvian National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Theatre), where the country was [founded on 18 November 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Latvia\))

Approximately 72% of Latvian citizens are Latvian, while 20% are Russian.[^cite_note-94] The government denationalized private property confiscated by the Soviets, returning it or compensating the owners for it, and [privatized](24661.md) most state-owned industries, reintroducing the [prewar currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_lats). Latvia is one of the fastest growing economies in the European Union,[^cite_note-95] despite its difficult re-orientation towards Western Europe and economic liberalization. In November 2013, [the roof collapsed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolit%C5%ABde_shopping_centre_roof_collapse) at a shopping center in Riga, causing Latvia's worst post-independence disaster with the deaths of 54 rush hour shoppers and rescue personnel.[^cite_note-96]

In late 2018 the National Archives of Latvia released a full alphabetical index of some 10,000 people recruited as agents or informants by the Soviet KGB. The publication took place after two decades of public debate, as well as the passage of a special law. As a result, the names, code names, birthplaces, and other data on active and former KGB agents (as of 1991, when Latvia regained independence) were revealed.[^cite_note-97]

In May 2023, the parliament elected [Edgars Rinkēvičs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgars_Rink%C4%93vi%C4%8Ds) as new [President of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia), making him the European Union's first openly gay head of state.[^cite_note-98] After years of debates, Latvia ratified the EU *Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence Against Women and Domestic Violence*, otherwise known as the [Istanbul Convention](31752276.md) in November 2023.[^cite_note-99]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Latvia)

See also: [Baltic Sea](3335.md), [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), and [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe)



Latvia lies in Northern Europe, on the eastern shores of the [Baltic Sea](3335.md).

Latvia lies in Northern Europe, on the eastern shores of the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) and northwestern part of the [East European Craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_European_craton) (EEC), between latitudes [55°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/55th_parallel_north) and [58° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/58th_parallel_north) (a small area is north of 58°), and longitudes [21°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_meridian_east) and [29° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/29th_meridian_east) (a small area is west of 21°). Latvia has a total area of 64,559 km<sup>2</sup> (24,926 sq mi) of which 62,157 km<sup>2</sup> (23,999 sq mi) land, 18,159 km<sup>2</sup> (7,011 sq mi) agricultural land,[^cite_note-Agriculture_–_Key_Indicators-100] 34,964 km<sup>2</sup> (13,500 sq mi) forest land[^cite_note-Forestry_–_Key_Indicators-101] and 2,402 km<sup>2</sup> (927 sq mi) inland water.[^cite_note-Geographical_Data_–_Key_Indicators-102]

The total length of Latvia's boundary is 1,866 km (1,159 mi). The total length of its land boundary is 1,368 km (850 mi), of which 343 km (213 mi) is shared with [Estonia](28222445.md) to the north, 276 km (171 mi) with the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) to the east, 161 km (100 mi) with [Belarus](3457.md) to the southeast and 588 km (365 mi) with [Lithuania](17675.md) to the south. The total length of its [maritime boundary](29613061.md) is 498 km (309 mi), which is shared with Estonia, Sweden and Lithuania. Extension from north to south is 210 km (130 mi) and from west to east 450 km (280 mi).[^cite_note-Geographical_Data_–_Key_Indicators-102]

Most of Latvia's territory is less than 100 m (330 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). Its largest lake, [Lubāns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lub%C4%81ns), has an area of 80.7 km<sup>2</sup> (31.2 sq mi), its deepest lake, [Drīdzis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr%C4%ABdzis), is 65.1 m (214 ft) deep. The longest river on Latvian territory is the [Gauja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauja), at 452 km (281 mi) in length. The longest river flowing through Latvian territory is the [Daugava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugava_River), which has a total length of 1,005 km (624 mi), of which 352 km (219 mi) is on Latvian territory. Latvia's highest point is [Gaiziņkalns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaizi%C5%86kalns), 311.6 m (1,022 ft). The length of Latvia's [Baltic](3335.md) coastline is 494 km (307 mi). An inlet of the Baltic Sea, the shallow [Gulf of Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Riga) is situated in the northwest of the country.[^cite_note-Latvia_in_brief-103]

### Climate



&#x20; Humid continental climate warm summer subtype



&#x20; Oceanic climate

Latvia has a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate) climate that has been described in various sources as either [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental) ([Köppen *Dfb*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate#Dfb/Dwb/Dsb:_Warm_summer_subtype)) or [oceanic/maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (Köppen *Cfb*).[^cite_note-liepu-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

Coastal regions, especially the western coast of the [Courland Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courland_Peninsula), possess a more maritime climate with cooler summers and milder winters, while eastern parts exhibit a more continental climate with warmer summers and harsher winters.[^cite_note-liepu-104] Nevertheless, the temperature variations are little as the territory of Latvia is relatively small.[^cite_note-LatviaClimate-107] Moreover, Latvia's terrain is particularly flat (no more than 350 meters high), thus the Latvian climate is not differentiated by altitude.[^cite_note-LatviaClimate-107]

Latvia has four pronounced seasons of near-equal length. Winter starts in mid-December and lasts until mid-March. Winters have average temperatures of −6 °C (21 °F) and are characterized by stable snow cover, bright sunshine, and short days. Severe spells of winter weather with cold winds, extreme temperatures of around −30 °C (−22 °F) and heavy snowfalls are common. Summer starts in June and lasts until August. Summers are usually warm and sunny, with cool evenings and nights. Summers have average temperatures of around 19 °C (66 °F), with extremes of 35 °C (95 °F). Spring and autumn bring fairly mild weather.[^cite_note-108]

### Environment



Latvia has the fifth highest proportion of land covered by forests in the European Union.

Most of the country is composed of fertile lowland plains and moderate hills. In a typical Latvian landscape, a mosaic of vast forests alternates with fields, farmsteads, and pastures. Arable land is spotted with birch groves and wooded clusters, which afford a habitat for numerous plants and animals. Latvia has hundreds of kilometres of undeveloped seashore—lined by pine forests, dunes, and continuous white sand beaches.[^cite_note-Latvia_in_brief-103][^cite_note-Nature_and_Environment_in_Latvia-111]

Latvia has the fifth highest proportion of land covered by forests in the European Union, after Sweden, Finland, Estonia and Slovenia.[^cite_note-112] Forests account for 3,497,000 ha (8,640,000 acres) or 56% of the total land area.[^cite_note-Forestry_–_Key_Indicators-101]

Latvia has over 12,500 rivers, which stretch for 38,000 km (24,000 mi). Major rivers include the [Daugava River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugava_River), [Lielupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lielupe), [Gauja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauja), [Venta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venta_\(river\)), and [Salaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaca), the largest spawning ground for [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon) in the eastern Baltic states. There are 2,256 lakes that are bigger than 1 ha (2.5 acres), with a collective area of 1,000 km<sup>2</sup> (390 sq mi). [Mires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mire) occupy 9.9% of Latvia's territory. Of these, 42% are raised bogs; 49% are fens; and 9% are transitional mires. 70% percent of the mires are untouched by civilization, and they are a refuge for many rare species of plants and animals.[^cite_note-Nature_and_Environment_in_Latvia-111]

Agricultural areas account for 1,815,900 ha (4,487,000 acres) or 29% of the total land area.[^cite_note-Agriculture_–_Key_Indicators-100] With the dismantling of collective farms, the area devoted to farming decreased dramatically – now farms are predominantly small. Approximately 200 farms, occupying 2,750 ha (6,800 acres), are engaged in ecologically pure farming (using no artificial fertilizers or pesticides).[^cite_note-Nature_and_Environment_in_Latvia-111]

Latvia's [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park) are [Gauja National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauja_National_Park) in [Vidzeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidzeme) (since 1973),[^cite_note-113] [Ķemeri National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B6emeri_National_Park) in [Zemgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemgale) (1997), [Slītere National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sl%C4%ABtere_National_Park) in [Kurzeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courland) (1999), and [Rāzna National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%81zna_National_Park) in [Latgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgale) (2007).[^cite_note-114]

Latvia has a long tradition of conservation. The first laws and regulations were promulgated in the 16th and 17th centuries.[^cite_note-Nature_and_Environment_in_Latvia-111] There are 706 specially state-level protected natural areas in Latvia: four national parks, one biosphere reserve, 42 nature parks, nine areas of protected landscapes, 260 nature reserves, four strict nature reserves, 355 nature monuments, seven protected marine areas and 24 microreserves.[^cite_note-115] Nationally protected areas account for 12,790 km<sup>2</sup> (4,940 sq mi) or around 20% of Latvia's total land area.[^cite_note-Geographical_Data_–_Key_Indicators-102] Latvia's Red Book (Endangered Species List of Latvia), which was established in 1977, contains 112 plant species and 119 animal species. Latvia has ratified the international Washington, Bern, and Ramsare conventions.[^cite_note-Nature_and_Environment_in_Latvia-111]

The 2012 [Environmental Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Index) ranks Latvia second, after Switzerland, based on the environmental performance of the country's policies.[^cite_note-116]

Access to [biocapacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biocapacity) in Latvia is much higher than world average. In 2016, Latvia had 8.5 global hectares[^cite_note-footprintdata-117] of biocapacity per person within its territory, much more than the world average of 1.6 global hectares per person.[^cite_note-118] In 2016 Latvia used 6.4 global hectares of biocapacity per person - their [ecological footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_footprint) of consumption. This means they use less biocapacity than Latvia contains. As a result, Latvia is running a biocapacity reserve.[^cite_note-footprintdata-117]

### Biodiversity



The [white wagtail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_wagtail) is the national bird of Latvia.

[^cite_note-119]

Approximately 30,000 species of flora and fauna have been registered in Latvia.[^cite_note-120] Larger mammalian wildlife in Latvia include [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer), [wild boar](52255.md), [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose), [lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_lynx), [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_bear), [fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_fox), [beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_beaver) and [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_wolf).[^cite_note-121] [Non-marine molluscs of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_non-marine_molluscs_of_Latvia) include 170 species.[^cite_note-122]

Species that are endangered in other European countries but common in Latvia include: black stork (*[Ciconia nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciconia_nigra)*), corncrake (*[Crex crex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crex_crex)*), lesser spotted eagle (*[Aquila pomarina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquila_pomarina)*), white-backed woodpecker (*[Picoides leucotos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picoides_leucotos)*), Eurasian crane (*[Grus grus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grus_grus)*), Eurasian beaver (*[Castor fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castor_fiber)*), Eurasian otter (*[Lutra lutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutra_lutra)*), European wolf (*[Canis lupus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canis_lupus)*) and European lynx (*[Felis lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_lynx)*).[^cite_note-Nature_and_Environment_in_Latvia-111]

[Phytogeographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytogeography), Latvia is shared between the Central European and Northern European provinces of the [Circumboreal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumboreal_Region) within the [Boreal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_Kingdom). According to the [WWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature), the territory of Latvia belongs to the [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion) of [Sarmatic mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatic_mixed_forests). 56 percent[^cite_note-Forestry_–_Key_Indicators-101] of Latvia's territory is covered by forests, mostly [Scots pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_pine), [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch), and [Norway spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_spruce).[^cite_note-123] It had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 2.09/10, ranking it 159th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-124]

Several species of flora and fauna are considered national symbols. [Oak](39696.md) (*[Quercus robur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)*, [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *ozols*), and [linden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilia) (*[Tilia cordata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilia_cordata)*, [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *liepa*) are Latvia's national trees and the [daisy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leucanthemum_vulgare) (*Leucanthemum vulgare*, [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *pīpene*) its national flower. The white wagtail (*[Motacilla alba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_wagtail)*, [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *baltā cielava*) is Latvia's national bird. Its national insect is the [two-spot ladybird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalia_bipunctata) (*Adalia bipunctata*, [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *divpunktu mārīte*). [Amber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber), fossilized tree resin, is one of Latvia's most important cultural symbols. In ancient times, amber found along the Baltic Sea coast was sought by Vikings as well as traders from Egypt, Greece and the Roman Empire. This led to the development of the [Amber Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Road).[^cite_note-125]

Several nature reserves protect unspoiled landscapes with a variety of large animals. At [Pape Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pape_Nature_Reserve), where [European bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_bison), wild horses, and [recreated aurochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heck_cattle) have been reintroduced, there is now an almost complete [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) [megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megafauna) also including moose, deer, and wolf.[^cite_note-126]

### Government

Main articles: [Parliament of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Latvia) and [Cabinet of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Latvia)



The [building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Livonian_Noble_Corporation) of the *Saeima*, the parliament of Latvia, in Riga

A [unicameral](204304.md) parliament, called the *[Saeima](499765.md),* is the [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of the Republic of Latvia.[^cite_note-129] The Saeima is also responsible for adopting the [state's budgets](4360909.md), approving the state's accounts, appointing and exercising control of the Government, and taking part in international co-operation. [Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(proposed_law\)) may be initiated by the Government or by [members of parliament](98066.md).

Latvia is a [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) with [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage). Membership of the Saeima is based on [proportional representation](23298.md) of political parties, with a 5% electoral threshold. Latvia elects 100 members to the Saeima. Parliamentary elections are held at least every four years, but it is within the powers of the prime minister to ask the president to call for an election before the term has elapsed. On a [vote of no confidence](208101.md), the Saeima may force a single minister or an entire government to resign.[^cite_note-131]

The Government of Latvia operates as a [cabinet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)), where executive authority is exercised by the prime minister and other [cabinet ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_minister), who head ministries. As the executive branch, the Cabinet is responsible for proposing bills and a budget, executing the laws, and guiding the foreign and internal policies of Latvia. The position of prime minister belongs to the person most likely to command the [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) of a majority in the Saeima; this is often the current leader of the largest [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) or, more effectively, through a [coalition of parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance). A single party generally does not have sufficient political power in terms of the number of seats to form a cabinet on its own; Saeima has often been ruled by [coalition governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_governments), themselves usually [minority governments](271434.md) dependent on non-government parties.

The [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Latvia) is elected by the *Saeima* in a separate election, also held every four years. The president appoints a prime minister who, together with his cabinet, forms the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) of the government, which has to receive a confidence vote by the *Saeima*. This system also existed before [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-132] The most senior civil servants are the thirteen [Secretaries of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State).[^cite_note-133]

Following the October [2022 Latvian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Latvian_parliamentary_election), Prime Minister [Krišjānis Kariņš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kri%C5%A1j%C4%81nis_Kari%C5%86%C5%A1) formed the [Second Kariņš cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Kari%C5%86%C5%A1_cabinet) in December 2022, a coalition of [New Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Unity), [National Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Alliance_\(Latvia\)), and [United List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_List_\(Latvia\)).[^cite_note-134] On 14 August 2023, Kariņš resigned, citing National Alliance's opposition to expanding the coalition to include [The Progressives](57853722.md) and the [Union of Greens and Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Greens_and_Farmers).[^cite_note-135] The [Siliņa cabinet](74828536.md), comprising New Unity, Union of Greens and Farmers, and The Progressives, was sworn in on 15 September 2023.[^cite_note-136]

According to [International IDEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Democracy_and_Electoral_Assistance)'s Global State of Democracy (GSoD) Indices and Democracy Tracker, Latvia performs in the high range on overall democratic measures, with particular weaknesses in inclusive suffrage, electoral participation, and civic engagement.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

Latvia is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state), currently divided into 43 local government units consisting of 36 municipalities ([Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *novadi*) and 7 state cities ([Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *valstspilsētas*) with their own city council and administration: [Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugavpils), [Jelgava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelgava), [Jūrmala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%ABrmala), [Liepāja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liep%C4%81ja), [Rēzekne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%93zekne), [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), and [Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventspils). There are four [historical and cultural regions in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_regions_of_Latvia) – [Courland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courland), [Latgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgale), [Vidzeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidzeme), [Zemgale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemgale), which are recognised in the [Constitution of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Latvia). [Selonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selonia), a part of Zemgale, is sometimes considered culturally distinct region, but it is not part of any formal division. The borders of historical and cultural regions usually are not explicitly defined and in several sources may vary. In formal divisions, Riga region, which includes the capital and parts of other regions that have a strong relationship with the capital, is also often included in regional divisions; e.g., there are five [planning regions of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_regions_of_Latvia) ([Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language): *plānošanas reģioni*), which were created in 2009 to promote balanced development of all regions. Under this division Riga region includes large parts of what traditionally is considered Vidzeme, Courland, and Zemgale. [Statistical regions of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_regions_of_Latvia), established in accordance with the EU [Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenclature_of_Territorial_Units_for_Statistics), duplicate this division.[^cite_note-140]

The largest city in Latvia is Riga, the second largest city is Daugavpils and the third largest city is Liepaja.

### Political culture

In 2010 parliamentary [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Latvian_parliamentary_election) ruling centre-right coalition won 63 out of 100 parliamentary seats. Left-wing opposition [Harmony Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony_Centre) supported by Latvia's Russian-speaking minority got 29 seats.[^cite_note-141] In November 2013, Latvian Prime Minister [Valdis Dombrovskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Dombrovskis), in office since 2009, resigned after at least 54 people were killed and dozens injured in the [collapse at a supermarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolit%C5%ABde_shopping_centre_roof_collapse) in Riga.[^cite_note-142]

In 2014 parliamentary [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Latvian_parliamentary_election) was won again by the ruling centre-right coalition formed by the [Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_\(Latvia\)) Party, the [National Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Alliance_\(Latvia\)) and the [Union of Greens and Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Greens_and_Farmers). They got 61 seats and Harmony got 24.[^cite_note-143] In December 2015, country's first female prime minister, in office since January 2014, [Laimdota Straujuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laimdota_Straujuma) resigned.[^cite_note-144] In February 2016, a coalition of Union of Greens and Farmers, The Unity and National Alliance was formed by new Prime Minister [Maris Kucinskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maris_Kucinskis).[^cite_note-145]

In 2018 parliamentary [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Latvian_parliamentary_election) pro-Russian Harmony was again the biggest party securing 23 out of 100 seats, the second and third were the new populist parties [KPV LV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPV_LV) and [New Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Conservative_Party_\(Latvia\)). Ruling coalition, comprising the Union of Greens and Farmers, the National Alliance and the Unity party, lost.[^cite_note-146] In January 2019, Latvia got a government led by new Prime Minister [Krisjanis Karins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krisjanis_Karins) of the centre-right [New Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Unity_\(Latvian_political_party\)). Karins' coalition was formed by five of the seven parties in parliament, excluding only the pro-Russia Harmony party and the Union of Greens and Farmers.[^cite_note-147] On 15 September 2023, [Evika Siliņa](74610811.md) became the new prime minister of Latvia, following resignation of former Prime Minister Krišjānis Kariņš previous month. Siliņa's government is a three-party coalition between her own [New Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Unity) (JV) party, the Greens and Farmers Union (ZZS), and the social-democratic Progressives (PRO) with total 52 of 100 seats in the parliament.[^cite_note-148]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Latvia)



The building of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Riga

Latvia is a member of the United Nations, [European Union](9317.md), [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), [NATO](21133.md), [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development), [OSCE](22796.md), [IMF](15251.md), and [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization). It is also a member of the [Council of the Baltic Sea States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Baltic_Sea_States) and [Nordic Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Investment_Bank). It was a member of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) (1921–1946). Latvia is part of the [Schengen Area](16565139.md)[^cite_note-149] and joined the [Eurozone](184391.md) on 1 January 2014.[^cite_note-150]

Latvia has established diplomatic relations with 158 countries. It has 44 diplomatic and consular missions and maintains 34 embassies and 9 permanent representations abroad. There are 37 foreign embassies and 11 international organisations in Latvia's capital [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga). Latvia hosts one European Union institution, the [Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_of_European_Regulators_of_Electronic_Communications) (BEREC).[^cite_note-151]

Latvia's foreign policy priorities include co-operation in the Baltic Sea region, European integration, active involvement in international organisations, contribution to European and transatlantic security and defence structures, participation in international civilian and military peacekeeping operations, and development co-operation, particularly the strengthening of stability and democracy in the EU's [Eastern Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Partnership) countries.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]



Foreign ministers of the Nordic and Baltic countries in Helsinki, 2011

Since the early 1990s, Latvia has been involved in active trilateral [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) co-operation with its neighbours [Estonia](28222445.md) and [Lithuania](17675.md), and Nordic-Baltic co-operation with the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries). Latvia is a member of the interparliamentary [Baltic Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Assembly), the intergovernmental [Baltic Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Council_of_Ministers) and the [Council of the Baltic Sea States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Baltic_Sea_States).[^cite_note-155] [Nordic-Baltic Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic-Baltic_Eight) (NB-8) is the joint co-operation of the governments of Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, Norway, and Sweden.[^cite_note-Co-operation_of_Baltic_and_Nordic_States-156] Nordic-Baltic Six (NB-6), comprising Nordic-Baltic countries that are European Union member states, is a framework for meetings on EU-related issues. Interparliamentary co-operation between the Baltic Assembly and [Nordic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council) was signed in 1992 and since 2006 annual meetings are held as well as regular meetings on other levels.[^cite_note-Co-operation_of_Baltic_and_Nordic_States-156] Joint Nordic-Baltic co-operation initiatives include the education programme NordPlus[^cite_note-157] and mobility programmes for public administration,[^cite_note-158] business and industry[^cite_note-159] and culture.[^cite_note-160] The [Nordic Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council_of_Ministers) has an office in Riga.[^cite_note-161]

Latvia participates in the [Northern Dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dimension) and [Baltic Sea Region Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea_Region_Programme), European Union initiatives to foster cross-border co-operation in the Baltic Sea region and Northern Europe. The secretariat of the Northern Dimension Partnership on Culture (NDPC) will be located in Riga.[^cite_note-162] In 2013 Riga hosted the annual [Northern Future Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Future_Forum), a two-day informal meeting of the prime ministers of the Nordic-Baltic countries and the UK.[^cite_note-163] The Enhanced Partnership in Northern Europe or *e-Pine* is the [U.S. Department of State](31975.md) diplomatic framework for co-operation with the Nordic-Baltic countries.[^cite_note-164]

Latvia hosted the [2006 NATO Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Riga_summit) and since then the annual Riga Conference has become a leading foreign and security policy forum in Northern Europe.[^cite_note-165] Latvia held the [Presidency of the Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union) in the first half of 2015.[^cite_note-166]

Since February 2022 Latvia's relations with Russia have deteriorated to the extent that Latvia withdrew its ambassador from Russia and expelled Russia's ambassador to Latvia in January 2023[^cite_note-167] and banned Russians from entering Latvia.

The [National Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Armed_Forces) (Latvian: *Nacionālie bruņotie spēki (NAF)*) of Latvia consists of the [Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Land_Forces), [Naval Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Naval_Forces), [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Air_Force), [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Guard), [Special Tasks Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Special_Tasks_Unit), [Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Military_Police), [NAF staff Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Armed_Forces_Staff_Battalion), Training and Doctrine Command, and Logistics Command. Latvia's defence concept is based upon the Swedish-Finnish model of a rapid response force composed of a mobilisation base and a small group of career professionals. From 1 January 2007, Latvia switched to a professional fully contract-based army.[^cite_note-168]

Latvia participates in international peacekeeping and security operations. Latvian armed forces have contributed to [NATO](21133.md) and EU military operations in Bosnia and Herzegovina (1996–2009), Albania (1999), Kosovo (2000–2009), Macedonia (2003), Iraq (2005–2006), Afghanistan (since 2003), Somalia (since 2011) and Mali (since 2013).[^cite_note-Participation_in_International_operations-169][^cite_note-Previous_operations-170][^cite_note-171] Latvia also took part in the US-led [Multi-National Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Force_%E2%80%93_Iraq) operation in Iraq (2003–2008)[^cite_note-172] and [OSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSCE) missions in Georgia, Kosovo and Macedonia.[^cite_note-173] Latvian armed forces contributed to a UK-led [Battlegroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Battlegroup) in 2013 and the [Nordic Battlegroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Battle_Group) in 2015 under the [Common Security and Defence Policy](1074631.md) (CSDP) of the European Union.[^cite_note-174] Latvia acts as the lead nation in the coordination of the [Northern Distribution Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)#Northern_Distribution_Network) for transportation of non-lethal [ISAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISAF) cargo by air and rail to Afghanistan.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] It is part of the Nordic Transition Support Unit (NTSU), which renders joint force contributions in support of Afghan security structures ahead of the withdrawal of Nordic and Baltic ISAF forces in 2014.[^cite_note-178] Since 1996 more than 3600 military personnel have participated in international operations,[^cite_note-Previous_operations-170] of whom 7 soldiers perished.[^cite_note-179] Per capita, Latvia is one of the largest contributors to international military operations.[^cite_note-180]

Latvian civilian experts have contributed to EU civilian missions: border assistance mission to Moldova and Ukraine (2005–2009), rule of law missions in Iraq (2006 and 2007) and Kosovo (since 2008), police mission in Afghanistan (since 2007) and monitoring mission in Georgia (since 2008).[^cite_note-Participation_in_International_operations-169]

Since March 2004, when the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) joined NATO, fighter jets of NATO members have been deployed on a rotational basis for the [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) mission at [Šiauliai Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai_International_Airport) in Lithuania to guard the Baltic airspace. Latvia participates in several [NATO Centres of Excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Centres_of_Excellence): Civil-Military Co-operation in the Netherlands, [Cooperative Cyber Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_Cyber_Defence_Centre_of_Excellence) in Estonia and Energy Security in Lithuania. It plans to establish the NATO Strategic Communications Centre of Excellence in Riga.[^cite_note-181]

Latvia co-operates with Estonia and Lithuania in several trilateral Baltic defence co-operation initiatives:

- Baltic Battalion *(BALTBAT)* – infantry battalion for participation in international peace support operations, headquartered near [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), Latvia;

- [Baltic Naval Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Naval_Squadron) *(BALTRON)* – naval force with mine countermeasures capabilities, headquartered near [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), Estonia;

- [Baltic Air Surveillance Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Surveillance_Network) *(BALTNET)* – air surveillance information system, headquartered near [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas), Lithuania;

- Joint military educational institutions: [Baltic Defence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Defence_College) in [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu), Estonia, Baltic Diving Training Centre in [Liepāja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liep%C4%81ja), Latvia and Baltic Naval Communications Training Centre in [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), Estonia.

  [^cite_note-182]

Future co-operation will include sharing of national infrastructures for training purposes and specialisation of training areas *(BALTTRAIN)* and collective formation of battalion-sized contingents for use in the NATO rapid-response force.[^cite_note-183] In January 2011, the Baltic states were invited to join [Nordic Defence Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Defence_Cooperation), the defence framework of the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries).[^cite_note-184] In November 2012, the three countries agreed to create a joint military staff in 2013.[^cite_note-185]

On 21 April 2022, Latvian Saeima passed amendments developed by the Ministry of Defence for the legislative draft Amendments to the Law on Financing of National Defence, which provide for gradual increase in the defence budget to 2.5% of the country's GDP over the course of the next three year.[^cite_note-186]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Latvia)



Latvia is part of \
&#x20; +   [European single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_single_market)&#x20;

&#x20; [Eurozone](184391.md)

&#x20;[Schengen Area](16565139.md) (not shown).



Real GPD per capita development of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania

Latvia is a member of the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (1999) and the [European Union](9317.md) (2004). On 1 January 2014, the [euro](9472.md) became the country's currency, superseding the [Lats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_lats). According to statistics in late 2013, 45% of the population supported the introduction of the euro, while 52% opposed it.[^cite_note-203] Following the introduction of the Euro, Eurobarometer surveys in January 2014 showed support for the euro to be around 53%, close to the European average.[^cite_note-204]

Since the year 2000, Latvia has had one of the highest (GDP) growth rates in Europe.[^cite_note-205] However, the chiefly consumption-driven growth in Latvia resulted in the collapse of Latvian GDP in late 2008 and early 2009, exacerbated by the global economic crisis, shortage of credit and huge money resources used for the bailout of [Parex Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parex_Bank).[^cite_note-206] The Latvian economy fell 18% in the first three months of 2009, the biggest fall in the European Union.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

The economic crisis of 2009 proved earlier assumptions that the fast-growing economy was heading for implosion of the [economic bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_bubble), because it was driven mainly by growth of domestic [consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_\(economics\)), financed by a serious increase of private [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt), as well as a negative foreign [trade balance](4810.md). The prices of real estate, which rose 150% from 2004 to 2006, was a significant contributor to the economic bubble.[^cite_note-209]

[Privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) in Latvia is almost complete. Virtually all of the previously state-owned small and medium companies have been privatised, leaving only a small number of politically sensitive large state companies. The private sector accounted for 70% of the country's GDP in 2006.[^cite_note-210]

Foreign investment in Latvia is still modest compared with the levels in north-central Europe. A law expanding the scope for selling land, including to foreigners, was passed in 1997. Representing 10.2% of Latvia's total foreign direct investment, American companies invested \$127 million in 1999. In the same year, the United States of America exported \$58.2 million of goods and services to Latvia and imported \$87.9 million. Eager to join Western economic institutions like the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development), and the [European Union](9317.md), Latvia signed a Europe Agreement with the EU in 1995—with a 4-year transition period. Latvia and the United States have signed treaties on investment, trade, and intellectual property protection and avoidance of double taxation.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

In 2010, Latvia launched a Residence by Investment program (Golden Visa) in order to attract foreign investors and make local economy benefit from it. This program allows investors to get a Latvian residence permit by investing at least €250,000 in property or in an enterprise with at least 50 employees and an annual turnover of at least €10M.

Economic contraction and recovery (2008–12)

Main article: [2008–2010 Latvian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932010_Latvian_financial_crisis)



An [airBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic) Boeing 757−200WL takes off at [Riga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_International_Airport) (RIX).

The Latvian economy entered a phase of fiscal contraction during the second half of 2008 after an extended period of credit-based speculation and unrealistic appreciation in real estate values. The national account deficit for 2007, for example, represented more than 22% of the GDP for the year while inflation was running at 10%.[^cite_note-cia-213]

Latvia's unemployment rate rose sharply in this period from a low of 5.4% in November 2007 to over 22%.[^cite_note-214] In April 2010 Latvia had the highest unemployment rate in the EU, at 22.5%, ahead of Spain, which had 19.7%.[^cite_note-215]

[Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman), the Nobel Laureate in economics for 2008, wrote in his [New York Times](30680.md) Op-Ed column on 15 December 2008:

> The most acute problems are on Europe's periphery, where many smaller economies are experiencing crises strongly reminiscent of past crises in Latin America and Asia: Latvia is the new Argentina[^cite_note-216]

However, by 2010, commentators[^cite_note-economist-217][^cite_note-218] noted signs of stabilisation in the Latvian economy. Rating agency [Standard & Poor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_&_Poor's) raised its outlook on Latvia's debt from negative to stable.[^cite_note-economist-217] Latvia's current account, which had been in deficit by 27% in late 2006 was in surplus in February 2010.[^cite_note-economist-217] Kenneth Orchard, senior analyst at [Moody's Investors Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's_Investors_Service) argued that:

> The strengthening regional economy is supporting Latvian production and exports, while the sharp swing in the current account balance suggests that the country's 'internal devaluation' is working.[^cite_note-219]

The [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) concluded the First Post-Program Monitoring Discussions with the Republic of Latvia in July 2012 announcing that Latvia's economy has been recovering strongly since 2010, following the deep downturn in 2008–09. Real GDP growth of 5.5 percent in 2011 was underpinned by export growth and a recovery in domestic demand. The growth momentum has continued into 2012 and 2013 despite deteriorating external conditions, and the economy is expected to expand by 4.1 percent in 2014. The unemployment rate has receded from its peak of more than 20 percent in 2010 to around 9.3 percent in 2014.[^cite_note-220]

Economic recovery

GDP at current prices rose from €23.7 billion in 2014 to €30.5 billion in 2019. The employment rate rose in the same period from 59.1% to 65% with unemployment falling from 10.8% to 6.5%.[^cite_note-221]

### Infrastructure

#### Transport

Main article: [Transport in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Latvia)



The [Port of Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_port_of_Ventspils) is one of the busiest ports in the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states).

The transport sector is around 14% of GDP. Transit between Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan as well as other Asian countries and the West used to be large.[^cite_note-wpt-222]

The four biggest ports of Latvia are located in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), [Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventspils), [Liepāja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liep%C4%81ja) and [Skulte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saulkrasti). Most transit traffic uses these and half the cargo is crude oil and oil products.[^cite_note-wpt-222] [Free port of Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_port_of_Ventspils) is one of the busiest ports in the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states). Apart from road and railway connections, Ventspils is also linked to oil extraction fields and prior to 2022, transportation routes of Russian Federation via system of two pipelines from [Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polotsk), Belarus.

[Riga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_International_Airport) is the busiest airport in the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) with 7.8 million passengers in 2019. It has direct flight to over 80 destinations in 30 countries. The only other airport handling regular commercial flights is [Liepāja International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liep%C4%81ja_International_Airport). [airBaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirBaltic) is the Latvian flag carrier airline and a low-cost carrier with hubs in all three [Baltic States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_States), but main base in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), Latvia.[^cite_note-223]

[Latvian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Railways)'s main network consists of 1,860 km of which 1,826 km is 1,520 mm [Russian gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_gauge) railway of which 251 km are electrified, making it the longest railway network in the [Baltic States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_States). Latvia's railway network is currently incompatible with European [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) lines.[^cite_note-224] However, [Rail Baltica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_Baltica) railway, linking Helsinki-Tallinn-Riga-Kaunas-Warsaw is under construction and is set to be completed in 2026.[^cite_note-225]

National road network in Latvia totals 1675 km of main roads, 5473 km of regional roads and 13 064 km of local roads. Municipal roads in Latvia totals 30 439 km of roads and 8039 km of streets.[^cite_note-226] The best known roads are [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Baltica) ([European route E67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E67)), connecting [Warsaw](32908.md) and [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), as well as [European route E22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E22), connecting [Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventspils) and [Terehova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terehova,_Latvia). In 2017 there were a total of 803,546 licensed vehicles in Latvia.[^cite_note-227]

Main article: [Energy in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Latvia)

Latvia has three large hydroelectric power stations in [Pļaviņu HES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%BCavi%C5%86u_HES) (908 MW), [Rīgas HES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%ABgas_HES) (402 MW) and [Ķeguma HES-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B6eguma_HES-2) (248 MW).[^cite_note-228] In recent years a couple of dozen of wind farms as well as biogas or biomass power stations of different scale have been built in Latvia.[^cite_note-229] In 2022, the Latvian Prime Minister announced about the planned investments of 1 billion euros in the new [wind farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm) and the completed project will expectedly provide additional 800 MW of capacity.[^cite_note-230]

### Ethnic groups

In 2025, [Latvians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_people) formed about 65.5% of the population, while [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_the_Baltic_states) were 24.1%, [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) 2.9%, [Ukrainians](145144.md) 2.9%, [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) 1.9%, [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians) 1.1%, and other ethnic groups 1.6%.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

In some cities, including [Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugavpils) and [Rēzekne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%93zekne), ethnic Latvians constitute a minority of the total population. Despite a steadily increasing proportion of ethnic Latvians for more than a decade, ethnic Latvians also still make up slightly less than a half of the population of the capital city of Latvia – [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga).

The share of ethnic Latvians declined from 77% (1,467,035) in 1935 to 52% (1,387,757) in 1989.[^cite_note-236] In the context of a decreasing overall population, there were fewer Latvians in 2011 than in 1989, but their share of the population was larger – 1,285,136 (62.1% of the population).[^cite_note-237]

The majority of Latvia's population are Latvians, who are an ethnic Baltic people. The country also has a significant Russian minority, as well as smaller populations of Ukrainians, Belarusians, and other Slavic peoples. These ethnic groups are all descended from peoples who settled in Latvia during the centuries of Russian and Soviet rule.

Latvia's ethnic diversity is a result of a number of factors, including a long history of foreign rule, its location on the Baltic Sea trade route, and its proximity to other Slavic countries. The Russian Empire conquered Latvia in the 18th century and ruled the country for over 200 years. During this time, the Russian authorities encouraged the settlement of Russian colonists in Latvia. After the collapse of the Russian Empire in 1918, Latvia became an independent country. However, the country was occupied by the Soviet Union in 1940 and remained under Soviet rule until 1991. The Soviets expelled some groups and resettled others in Latvia, especially Russians. After 1991 many of the expellees returned to Latvia.[^cite_note-238]

### Language

Further information: [Language policy in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_policy_in_Latvia)

The sole official language of Latvia is [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language), which belongs to the [Baltic language sub-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_languages) of the [Balto-Slavic branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balto-Slavic_languages) of the [Indo-European language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages). Another notable language of Latvia is the nearly extinct [Livonian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_language) of the [Finnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnic_languages) branch of the [Uralic language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralic_languages), which enjoys protection by law; [Latgalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latgalian_language) – as a dialect of Latvian is also protected by Latvian law but as a historical variation of the Latvian language. [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), which was widely spoken during the Soviet period, is still the most widely used minority language by far (in 2023, 37.7% spoke it as their mother tongue and 34.6% spoke it at home, including people who were not ethnically Russian).[^cite_note-eng.lsm.lv-21] Besides Latvian, schools also include English, German, French, Russian and other languages in their curricula. English is also widely accepted in Latvia in business and tourism. As of 2014 there were 109 schools for minorities that used Russian as the language of instruction (27% of all students) for 40% of subjects (the remaining 60% of subjects were taught in Latvian).

From 2019, instruction in the [Russian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_Latvia) was gradually discontinued in private colleges and [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university) in Latvia, as well as for general instruction in Latvian public [high schools](554992.md),[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240] except for subjects related to culture and history of the Russian minority, such as Russian language and literature classes.[^cite_note-241] All schools, including preschools, still using the Russian language in 2023 needed to transition to using Latvian as the sole language of instruction within three years.[^cite_note-242] As of September 2025, this transition was completed.[^cite_note-243]

On 18 February 2012, Latvia held a [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Latvian_constitutional_referendum) on whether to adopt Russian as a second official language.[^cite_note-244] According to the Central Election Commission, 74.8% voted against, 24.9% voted for and the voter turnout was 71.1%.[^cite_note-245]

In the context of [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](70149799.md), Latvian government has updated the residency requirements for Russian nationals due to national security reasons. The amendments will require those citizens of Russia who have denounced their Latvian citizenship or non-citizen status to pass the A2 level Latvian language test in order to renew their residence permits.[^cite_note-246] Those who did not manage to pass the test in 2023 were given a two-year extension, however, in 2025 841 Russian nationals who had not fulfilled the language requirement were issued letters ordering them to leave Latvia.[^cite_note-247]

In the [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) Poll 2010, 38% of Latvian citizens responded that "they believe there is a God", while 48% answered that "they believe there is some sort of spirit or life force" and 11% stated that "they do not believe there is any sort of spirit, God, or life force".

Lutheranism was more prominent before the Soviet occupation, when it was adhered to by about 60% of the population, a reflection of the country's strong historical links with the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries), and to the influence of the [Hansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) in particular and Germany in general. Since then, Lutheranism has declined to a slightly greater extent than Catholicism in all three [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states). The Evangelical Lutheran Church, with an estimated 600,000 members in 1956, was affected most adversely. An internal document of 18 March 1987, near the end of communist rule, spoke of an active membership that had shrunk to only 25,000 in Latvia, but the faith has since experienced a revival.[^cite_note-249]

The country's Orthodox Christians belong to the [Latvian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Orthodox_Church), a semi-autonomous body within the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church). In 2011, there were 416 religious [Jews in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Latvia) and 319 [Muslims in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Latvia).[^cite_note-Tieslietu_Ministrija-248] As of 2004, there were more than 600 Latvian [neopagans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neopaganism), *[Dievturi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dievtur%C4%ABba)* (The Godskeepers), whose religion is based on [Latvian mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_mythology).[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] About 21% of the total population is not affiliated with a specific religion.[^cite_note-Tieslietu_Ministrija-248]

Latvia has been seeking for a number of years to separate the Latvian Orthodox Church from Moscow, stating that longstanding ties to Russia pose "national security concerns".[^cite_note-252] This was achieved in September 2022 with a law removing all influence or power over the Orthodox Church from non-Latvians, which would include the patriarch of Moscow.[^cite_note-253]

### Education and science

Main article: [Education in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Latvia)

See also: [List of universities in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Latvia)



University of Latvia

The [University of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Latvia) and [Riga Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_Technical_University) are two major universities in the country, both successors to Riga Polytechnical Institute, and located in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga).[^cite_note-254] Other important universities, which were established on the base of State University of Latvia, include the [Latvia University of Life Sciences and Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_University_of_Life_Sciences_and_Technologies) (established in 1939 on the basis of the Faculty of Agriculture) and [Riga Stradiņš University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_Stradi%C5%86%C5%A1_University) (established in 1950 on the basis of the Faculty of Medicine). Both nowadays cover a variety of different fields. The [University of Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Daugavpils) is another significant centre of education.

Latvia closed 131 schools between 2006 and 2010, which is a 12.9% decline, and in the same period enrolment in educational institutions has fallen by over 54,000 people, a 10.3% decline.[^cite_note-255]

Latvian policy in science and technology has set out the long-term goal of transitioning from labor-consuming economy to knowledge-based economy.[^cite_note-256] By 2020 the government aims to spend 1.5% of GDP on research and development, with half of the investments coming from the private sector. Latvia plans to base the development of its scientific potential on existing scientific traditions, particularly in organic chemistry, medical chemistry, genetic engineering, physics, materials science and information technologies.[^cite_note-257] The greatest number of patents, both nationwide and abroad, are in medical chemistry.[^cite_note-258] Latvia was ranked 41st in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Latvia)

The Latvian healthcare system is a [universal programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care), largely funded through government taxation.[^cite_note-261] It is among the lowest-ranked healthcare systems in Europe, due to excessive waiting times for treatment, insufficient access to the latest medicines, and other factors.[^cite_note-262] There were 59 hospitals in Latvia in 2009, down from 94 in 2007 and 121 in 2006.[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Latvia)



The [historic Centre of Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Riga) was declared a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 1997.

Traditional Latvian [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore), especially the dance of the [folk songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music), dates back well over a thousand years. More than 1.2 million texts and 30,000 melodies of folk songs have been identified.[^cite_note-266]

In the 19th century, Latvian nationalist movements emerged. They promoted Latvian culture and encouraged Latvians to take part in cultural activities. The 19th century and beginning of the 20th century is often regarded by Latvians as a classical era of Latvian culture. Posters show the influence of other European cultures, for example, works of artists such as the Baltic-German artist [Bernhard Borchert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Borchert) and the French [Raoul Dufy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Dufy).[^cite_note-267] With the onset of World War II, many Latvian artists and other members of the cultural elite fled the country yet continued to produce their work, largely for a Latvian émigré audience.[^cite_note-268]



Participants of the Latvian Song and Dance Festival in 2018

The [Latvian Song and Dance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Song_and_Dance_Festival) is an important event in [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvians) culture and social life. It has been held since 1873, normally every five years. Approximately 30,000 performers altogether participate in the event.[^cite_note-269] Folk songs and classical choir songs are sung, with emphasis on [a cappella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_cappella) singing, though modern popular songs have recently been incorporated into the repertoire as well.[^cite_note-270]

After incorporation into the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), Latvian artists and writers were forced to follow the [socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism) style of art. During the Soviet era, music became increasingly popular, with the most popular being songs from the 1980s. At this time, songs often made fun of the characteristics of Soviet life and were concerned about preserving Latvian identity. This aroused popular protests against the USSR and also gave rise to an increasing popularity of poetry. Since independence, theatre, [scenography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenography), [choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir) music, and classical music have become the most notable branches of Latvian culture.[^cite_note-271]

During July 2014, [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) hosted the eighth [World Choir Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Choir_Games) as it played host to over 27,000 choristers representing over 450 choirs and over 70 countries. The festival is the biggest of its kind in the world and is held every two years in a different host city.[^cite_note-272]

Starting in 2019 Latvia hosts the inaugural [Riga Jurmala Music Festival](https://riga-jurmala.com/) [Archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20210702105201/https://riga-jurmala.com/) 2 July 2021 at the [Wayback Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayback_Machine), a new festival in which world-famous orchestras and conductors perform across four weekends during the summer. The festival takes place at the [Latvian National Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Opera), the Great Guild, and the Great and Small Halls of the Dzintari Concert Hall. This year features the [Bavarian Radio Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Radio_Symphony_Orchestra), the [Israel Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Philharmonic_Orchestra), the [London Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Symphony_Orchestra) and the Russian National Orchestra.[^cite_note-273]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Latvian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_cuisine)

Latvian cuisine typically consists of agricultural products, with meat featuring in most main meal dishes. Fish is commonly consumed due to Latvia's location on the Baltic Sea. Latvian cuisine has been influenced by neighbouring countries. Common ingredients in Latvian recipes are found locally, such as potatoes, wheat, barley, cabbage, onions, eggs, and pork. Latvian food is generally quite fatty and uses few spices.[^cite_note-274]

Grey peas with speck are generally considered as staple foods of Latvians. [Sorrel soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrel_soup) (*skābeņu zupa*) is also consumed by Latvians.[^cite_note-275] Rye bread is considered the national [staple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_food).[^cite_note-276]

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Latvia)



[Arena Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Riga) during the 2006 IIHF World Championship

Ice hockey is usually considered the most popular sport in Latvia. Latvia has had many famous hockey stars like [Helmuts Balderis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmuts_Balderis), [Artūrs Irbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art%C5%ABrs_Irbe), [Kārlis Skrastiņš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81rlis_Skrasti%C5%86%C5%A1) and [Sandis Ozoliņš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandis_Ozoli%C5%86%C5%A1) and more recently [Zemgus Girgensons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemgus_Girgensons), whom the Latvian people have strongly supported in international and NHL play, expressed through the dedication of using the NHL's All Star Voting to bring Zemgus to number one in voting.[^cite_note-277] [Dinamo Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinamo_Riga) is the country's strongest hockey club, playing in the [Latvian Hockey Higher League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Hockey_Higher_League). The national tournament is the Latvian Hockey Higher League, held since 1931. The [2006 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_IIHF_World_Championship) was held in Riga.



Kristaps Porziņģis

The second most popular sport is basketball. Latvia has a long basketball tradition, as the [Latvian national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_men's_national_basketball_team) won the first ever [EuroBasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket) in [1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1935) and silver medals in [1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1939), after losing the final to [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_men's_national_basketball_team) by one point. Latvia has had many European basketball stars like [Jānis Krūmiņš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C4%81nis_Kr%C5%ABmi%C5%86%C5%A1), [Maigonis Valdmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maigonis_Valdmanis), [Valdis Muižnieks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Mui%C5%BEnieks), [Valdis Valters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Valters), [Igors Miglinieks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igors_Miglinieks), as well as the first Latvian [NBA](22093.md) player [Gundars Vētra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundars_V%C4%93tra). [Andris Biedriņš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andris_Biedri%C5%86%C5%A1) is one of the most well-known Latvian basketball players, who played in the NBA for the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) and the [Utah Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Jazz). Current NBA players include [Kristaps Porziņģis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristaps_Porzi%C5%86%C4%A3is), who plays for the [Boston Celtics](43376.md), [Dāvis Bertāns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%81vis_Bert%C4%81ns), who plays for the [Oklahoma City Thunder](18256220.md), and [Rodions Kurucs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodions_Kurucs), who last played for the [Milwaukee Bucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks). Former Latvian basketball club [Rīgas ASK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%ABgas_ASK) won the [Euroleague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroleague_Basketball) tournament three times in a row before becoming defunct. Currently, [VEF Rīga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BK_VEF_R%C4%ABga), which competes in [EuroCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocup_Basketball), is the strongest professional basketball club in Latvia. [BK Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BK_Ventspils), which participates in [EuroChallenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroChallenge), is the second strongest basketball club in Latvia, previously winning [LBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvijas_Basketbola_l%C4%ABga) eight times and [BBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Basketball_League) in 2013. Latvia was one of the [EuroBasket 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2015) hosts and will be one of the hosts once again in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2025).

Other popular sports include [football](10568.md), [floorball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floorball), tennis, volleyball, cycling, [bobsleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobsleigh) and [skeleton](159776.md). The [Latvian national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_national_football_team)'s only major [FIFA](11049.md) tournament participation has been the [2004 UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004).[^cite_note-278]

[Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_at_the_Olympics) has participated successfully in both [Winter](34069.md) and [Summer Olympics](27566.md). The most successful Olympic athlete in the history of independent Latvia has been [Māris Štrombergs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ris_%C5%A0trombergs), who became a two-time Olympic champion in 2008 and 2012 at Men's BMX.[^cite_note-279]

In Boxing, [Mairis Briedis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mairis_Briedis) is the first and only Latvian to date, to win a boxing world title, having held the WBC cruiserweight title from 2017 to 2018, the WBO cruiserweight title in 2019, and the IBF / The Ring magazine cruiserweight titles in 2020.

In 2017, Latvian tennis player [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko) won the [2017 French Open Women's singles title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_Singles), being the first unseeded player to do so in the open era.

In futsal, Latvia will host the [UEFA Futsal Euro 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Futsal_Euro_2026) alongside Lithuania, their national team will make their debut as co-host.
