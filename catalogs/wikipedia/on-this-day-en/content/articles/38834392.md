**ARA *San Juan* (S-42)** was a [TR-1700-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TR-1700-class_submarine) [diesel-electric submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel-electric_submarine) in service with the [Submarine Force of the Argentine Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Submarine_Force) from 1985 to 2017. It was built in [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), entering service on 19 November 1985, and underwent a [mid-life update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-life_update) from 2008 to 2013.

On 15 November 2017, [*San Juan* went missing](56000283.md) with 44 crewmen during a routine patrol in the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic) off [Patagonia](65433.md). She was believed to have suffered an electrical malfunction, and a multi-nation search operation was mounted. Within hours of *San Juan*'s last transmission, an acoustic anomaly consistent with an [implosion](383833.md) was detected in the vicinity of the vessel's last known location. When the submarine was not located for a week, the crew were presumed dead by the Argentine government.[^cite_note-ToI1-2] On 30 November, the search and rescue operation was abandoned.

The [Argentine Navy](490409.md) reported on 16 November 2018 that the wreck of *San Juan* had been found at a depth of 907 metres (2,976 ft), 460 kilometres (290 mi) southeast of [Comodoro Rivadavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comodoro_Rivadavia).[^cite_note-Ocean_Infinity-3] The submarine's imploded wreckage was strewn over an area of 8,000 square metres (86,000 ft<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-BBC1-4][^cite_note-NYT1-5]

## Design

Main article: [TR-1700-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TR-1700-class_submarine)

Built by [Thyssen Nordseewerke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordseewerke),[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] *San Juan* was [laid down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keel_laying) on 18 March 1982 and [launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_ship_launching) on 20 June 1983. It had a single-[hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_hull) design, with a lightweight [bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_\(watercraft\)) and [stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern) and a watertight [superstructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstructure) in the central part. Its [sister vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_ship), [ARA *Santa Cruz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARA_Santa_Cruz_\(S-41\)), is the only other one of its type, though the program originally sought to produce a larger number of submarines.[^cite_note-Castro-8]

## Name

The submarine's name derives from the province of [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Province,_Argentina); the names of all Argentine submarines begin with the letter *S*. Past ships with the same name are a [destroyer](8779.md) (1911), a [surveyor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_vessel) (1929), and a [torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat) (1937).[^cite_note-9]

The prefix ARA is the acronym of the Argentine Navy in Spanish (Spanish: *Armada de la República Argentina*).[^cite_note-ButtressHeaney2012-10][^cite_note-Donaghy2014-11]

## Operational history

The submarine entered service on 18 November 1985.[^cite_note-Snorkel-12]

In 1994, during the FleetEx 2/94 "George Washington" exercise with the [United States Navy](20518076.md), *San Juan* avoided detection by United States anti-submarine forces for the entire duration of the [war game](1225714.md), penetrating the destroyer defense and "sinking" the command ship [USS *Mount Whitney*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Mount_Whitney).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The submarine took part in other exercises including [Gringo-Gaucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gringo-Gaucho) and [UNITAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNITAS).[^cite_note-Snorkel-12]

The vessel underwent a [mid-life update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-life_update) between 2008 and 2013, taking longer than expected due to budget constraints.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Defensa-16] The upgrade cost around 100 million [pesos](407877.md) (US\$12.4 million) and comprised more than 500,000 work hours during which the submarine was cut in half and had its four [MTU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Friedrichshafen) engines and batteries replaced.[^cite_note-17] Shortly before the refit, the submarine had been equipped with a portable navigation radar, communication equipment, and a [Garmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmin) GPSMAP 5012 [plotter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plotter_\(instrument\)).[^cite_note-18] The updates were carried out at the Argentine Industrial Naval Complex's (CINAR) [Tandanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandanor) and Storni shipyards, in the southern sector of [Buenos Aires port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Buenos_Aires).[^cite_note-Castro-8] Later, *San Juan* was tasked with carrying out surveillance exercises in the [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) around [Puerto Madryn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Madryn), particularly in the role of combating [illegal fishing](6346579.md).[^cite_note-InfoBae-19]

On 17 November 2017, it was announced that *San Juan* had not been heard from since 15 November when the vessel was 430 kilometres (270 mi) from the coast, off [San Jorge Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jorge_Gulf) on its way to [Mar del Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_del_Plata) from [Ushuaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushuaia) following a military exercise, and that a [search and rescue](172599.md) operation had been launched in the same area.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] There were 44 sailors on board the missing submarine,[^cite_note-melgarhanna-25] including Argentina's first female submarine officer, [Eliana Krawczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliana_Krawczyk).[^cite_note-auto-26] The submarine carried oxygen for no more than seven days when submerged.[^cite_note-air-27][^cite_note-auto-26]

### Search

The search and rescue operation was carried out under the auspices of the [International Submarine Escape and Rescue Liaison Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Submarine_Escape_and_Rescue_Liaison_Office), an organisation of over 40 countries set up in 2003[^cite_note-28] following the [*Kursk* submarine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster).[^cite_note-29] The search area was 482,507 square kilometres (186,297 sq mi) in size[^cite_note-30] and weather conditions throughout the search and rescue period changed, making the task far more difficult on days with large waves and high winds.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Search efforts continued several days, without success.

The director-general of the Vienna-based UN International Atomic Energy Agency IAEA [Rafael Grossi](62195943.md), an Argentinian national, proposed the use of the international [hydrophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophone) network, owned by the [Preparatory Commission for the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_Commission_for_the_Comprehensive_Nuclear-Test-Ban_Treaty_Organization), also based in Vienna, to seek information about the vessel's fate. He convinced [Lassina Zerbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassina_Zerbo), CTBTO executive director, to search the organization's records for anomalies. On 23 November the Argentine Navy said an event consistent with an implosion had been detected[^cite_note-BBCdashes-33] at [46°07′S 59°41′W  /  46.12°S 59.69°W  / -46.12; -59.69](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=ARA_San_Juan_\(S-42\)\&params=46.12_S_59.69_W_)[^cite_note-34] by the [CTBTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTBTO) listening posts on [Ascension Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Island) (HA10) and [Crozet Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crozet_Islands) (HA04), and records matched with the day the submarine stopped communicating.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-BBCdashes-33][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

By 24 November, the search and rescue operation involved more than 30 aircraft and ships from Argentina, the United Kingdom, Brazil, the United States, Chile, and other countries. More than 4,000 personnel from 13 countries assisted in the search, scouring an area the size of Spain.[^cite_note-38]

On 27 November, a press release revealed that according to the submarine's last report from 15 November, *San Juan*'s [snorkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_snorkel) had leaked water into the forward storage batteries the day before, which ignited a fire. After extinguishing the fire, the crew disconnected the forward batteries. The submarine continued onward, powered by the [aft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aft) batteries.[^cite_note-39]

On 30 November, 15 days after *San Juan* went missing, the Navy declared that the rescue phase of the operation was over, and the search for the submarine on the seabed would continue.[^cite_note-finalizo-1] The loss of 44 crewmen constituted the largest loss of life aboard a submarine since the [Chinese submarine *361*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_submarine_361) malfunctioned in April 2003,[^cite_note-40] until being surpassed by the loss of 53 crewmen aboard the Indonesian submarine [KRI *Nanggala*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRI_Nanggala_\(402\)) in April 2021.[^cite_note-41] A criminal investigation was launched into the disappearance.[^cite_note-BBC1-4]

### Wreck discovery

On 16 November 2018, the remains of ARA *San Juan* were found at a depth of 907 metres (2,976 ft), at [45°56′59″S 59°46′22″W  /  45.94972°S 59.77278°W  / -45.94972; -59.77278](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=ARA_San_Juan_\(S-42\)\&params=45_56_59_S_59_46_22_W_)[^cite_note-wreck-20] (20 km or 12 mi [NNW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Points_of_the_compass) from the seismic anomaly previously reported by the [CTBTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_Commission_for_the_Comprehensive_Nuclear-Test-Ban_Treaty_Organization)) and nearly 270 nautical miles (500 km; 310 mi) from [Comodoro Rivadavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comodoro_Rivadavia), by a remote submersible operated by the Norwegian ship [*Seabed Constructor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed_Constructor) of the company [Ocean Infinity](59089286.md), a private maritime company hired by the Argentine government.[^cite_note-Ocean_Infinity-3][^cite_note-42]

It had been considered that the probability of locating the wreck in the area where it was eventually found was 90%, but previous searches failed to find it due to insufficient technology and presence of numerous [submarine canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_canyon).[^cite_note-43] A "hydro-acoustic anomaly" consistent with an [implosion](383833.md) had been detected 30 nautical miles (56 km; 35 mi) north of the submarine's last known position at 10\:31 [ART](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Argentina) (13\:31 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)) on 15 November 2017. Photographs showed the shattered remains of the submarine broken up on the seabed. Ocean Infinity will receive a reward of US\$7.5 million for finding the missing vessel.[^cite_note-BBC1-4][^cite_note-NYT1-5] Argentine Navy spokesman Captain Jorge Balbi presented close-up photos of the wreck in a press briefing. The pictures show the imploded pressure hull, with the bow section, sail and propellers scattered in a 100-metre (330 ft) diameter area of 8,000 square metres (86,000 ft<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

## Commemoration

On 24 October 2021 a memorial commemorating the 44 crew members lost was unveiled in front of the [Mar del Plata Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_del_Plata_Naval_Base).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]
