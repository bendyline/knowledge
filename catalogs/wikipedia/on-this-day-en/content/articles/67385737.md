# Discharge of radioactive water of the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant



Water is stored in three types of facilities though there are occasional leaks.

[^cite_note-Shozugawa_2020-1]

&#x20;Two varieties of above-ground water tanks are seen at the back, and the workers are working in an underground storage pool.

[^cite_note-storage_types-2]

Radioactive water from the [Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant](11986472.md) in Japan began being discharged into the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) on 11 March 2011, following the [Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_disaster) triggered by the [Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami). Three of the plant's reactors experienced meltdowns, leaving behind [melted fuel debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corium_\(nuclear_reactor\)). Water was introduced to prevent the meltdowns from progressing further. When cooling water, groundwater, and rain came into contact with the melted fuel debris, they became contaminated with [radioactive nuclides](37245.md), such as [iodine-131](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine-131), [caesium-134](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-134), [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137), and [strontium-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strontium-90).[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-IAEAFAQs-4]

Over 500,000 tonnes of untreated wastewater (including 10,000 tonnes released to free up storage space) escaped into the ocean shortly after the accident. In addition, persistent leakage into groundwater was not admitted by the plant operator until 2013. The radioactivity from these sources exceeded legal limits.[^cite_note-national_geographic_20130809-5][^cite_note-ReferenceB-6]

Since then, contaminated water has been pumped into storage units and gradually treated using the Advanced Liquid Processing System (ALPS) to eliminate most [radionuclides](37245.md),[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-Tokyo_Shimbun_20210413a-7] except notably [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium) with a [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life) of 12.32 years, which is chemically bonded to the water.[^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-Guardian_20201016-9] In 2021, the [Japanese cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suga_Cabinet) approved the release of ALPS-treated water containing tritium.[^cite_note-science2021-10][^cite_note-11] Because it is still radioactive immediately after treatment, the solution will be diluted by sea water to a lower concentration before being discharged.[^cite_note-koreaherald-12]

A review report by the [International Atomic Energy Agency](14984.md) (IAEA) shows that the plan of discharging diluted ALPS-treated water into the sea is consistent with relevant international safety standards. It also emphasizes that the release of the treated water is a national decision by the Government of Japan and its report is neither a recommendation nor an endorsement of the decision.[^cite_note-13]

On 24 August 2023, the power plant started releasing the treated portion of its wastewater into the Pacific Ocean. At the time, its storage units held over a million tonnes of wastewater in total. Because new wastewater is constantly being formed and even treated water must be discharged slowly by diluting it with more sea water, the entire process could take more than 30 years.[^cite_note-14] The decision to release this water into the ocean has faced concerns and criticism from other countries and international organisations.

As of the fourth round of discharge in March 2024, no elevated tritium levels have been detected in nearby waters.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Initial atmospheric release



[Caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137) concentration in the air, 19 March 2011

Radioactive materials were dispersed into the atmosphere immediately after the disaster and account for most of all such materials leaked into the environment. 80% of the initial atmospheric release eventually deposited over rivers and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), according to a [UNSCEAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNSCEAR) report in 2020.[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.104-105-17] Specifically, "the total releases to the atmosphere of [iodine-131](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine-131) and [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137) ranged generally between about 100 to about 500 P[Bq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bq) \[petabecquerel, 10<sup>15</sup> Bq] and 6 to 20 PBq, respectively. The ranges correspond to about 2% to 8% of the total inventory of iodine-131 and about 1% to 3% of the total inventory of caesium-137 in the three operating units (Units 1–3)".[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.104-105-17]

### Deposition on river

The indirect deposition to rivers come from the earlier direct discharge to the atmosphere. "Continuing indirect releases of about 5 to 10 T[Bq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bq) \[terabecquerel, 10<sup>12</sup> Bq] of [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137) per year via rivers draining catchment areas", according to the [UNSCEAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNSCEAR) report in 2020.[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.104-105-17]

## Discharge to ocean, untreated water (2011)



Sea water sampling supervised by IAEA staff (left).

On 5 April 2011, the operator of the nuclear plant, [Tokyo Electric Power Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Electric_Power_Company) (TEPCO), discharged 11,500 tons of untreated water into the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) in order to free up storage space for water that is even more radioactive. The untreated water was the least [radioactively contaminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_contamination) among the stored water, but still 100 times the legal limit.[^cite_note-reuters.com-18][^cite_note-asahi.com-19] TEPCO estimated that a total of 520,000 tons of untreated radioactive water had escaped into the ocean before it could place silt fences to contain further spills.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-6]

The [UNSCEAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNSCEAR) report in 2020 determined "direct releases in the first three months amounting to about 10 to 20 P[Bq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bq) \[petabecquerel, 10<sup>15</sup> Bq] of [iodine-131](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine-131) and about 3 to 6 PBq of [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137)".[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.104-105-17] About 82 percent having flowed into the sea before 8 April 2011.[^cite_note-IRSN-Oct-2011-20]

## Discharge to soil and groundwater by leakage



[Abukuma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abukuma_River) was banned from fishing for 10 years for radioactivity reasons. It reopened in April 2021.

[^cite_note-21]

Scientists suspected that radioactive elements continued to leak into the ocean. High levels of caesium-134 were found in local fish, despite the isotope's comparatively shorter half-life. Meanwhile, radiation levels in the nearby sea water did not fall as expected.[^cite_note-national_geographic_20130809-5] After repeated denials,[^cite_note-bloomberg1-22] the operator of the nuclear plant, [Tokyo Electric Power Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Electric_Power_Company) (TEPCO), finally admitted on 22 July 2013 that leaks to groundwater had been happening.[^cite_note-national_geographic_20130809-5][^cite_note-huffingtonpost1-23] Some groundwater samples contained 310 Bq/L of cesium-134 and 650 Bq/L of cesium-137, exceeding WHO's maximum guideline of 10 Bq/L for drinking water.[^cite_note-national_geographic_20130809-5]

It was later determined that some of the leaks came from the storage tanks for wastewater.[^cite_note-Shozugawa_2020-1] Since then, TEPCO has had a record of being dishonest on its figures and has lost the public trust.[^cite_note-CCNE_Japan_20210411-24][^cite_note-Yuto_Chiba-25] For instance, in 2014, TEPCO blamed its own measuring method and revised the [strontium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strontium) in a groundwater well in July 2013 from 900,000 Bq/L to 5,000,000 Bq/L, which is 160,000 times the standard for discharge.[^cite_note-26]

While soil naturally absorbs the caesium in groundwater, strontium and [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium) can flow through more freely.[^cite_note-Scientific_American_20130813-27] At one time, nearly 400 tonnes of radioactive water was being formed every day (150,000 tonnes per year). TEPCO has since tried to stem or divert the inflow of groundwater to the damaged reactor sites and prevent contaminated water from escaping into the ocean.[^cite_note-national_geographic_20130809-5]

The [UNSCEAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNSCEAR) report in 2020 concluded "Direct release of about 60 T[Bq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bq) \[terabecquerel, 10<sup>12</sup> Bq] of [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137) in [ground water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_water) draining from the site up to October 2015, when measures were taken to reduce these releases, and about 0.5 TBq per year thereafter".[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.104-105-17]

In February 2024, a leak at the power plant was detected by a contractor and eventually repaired by TEPCO. The company estimated that 5.5 tonnes of water, which potentially contained 22 billion becquerels of radioactive materials such as caesium and strontium, had escaped from an air vent, pooled outside and seeped into the surrounding soil, but did not leave the plant compound. It said this was caused by 10 out of 16 valves being left open when they should have been closed for flushing.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-independent2024-29]

## Discharge to ocean, treated water

### Advanced Liquid Processing System (2013–)

"Metric ton" and "cubic meter" are used interchangeably here.



One of the three types of water storage facilities at the power plant.

[^cite_note-storage_types-2]

To prevent the reactor [meltdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_meltdown) from worsening, a continuous supply of new water is necessary to cool the [melted fuel debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corium_\(nuclear_reactor\)). As of 2013, 400 metric tonnes of water was becoming [radioactively contaminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_contamination) each day. The contaminated water is pumped out and combined into the reactor-cooling loop, which includes strontium–cesium removal (KURION, SURRY) and [reverse osmosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_osmosis) desalination processes.[^cite_note-wnn-20130926-30][^cite_note-ALPS-loop-31]

In October 2012, TEPCO introduced the "Advanced Liquid Processing System" (ALPS, [Japanese](15606.md): 多核種除去設備), which is designed to remove [radionuclides](37245.md) other than [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium) (which is chemically bonded to the water) and [carbon-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-14).[^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] ALPS works by first pre-processing the water by iron [coprecipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprecipitation) (removes [alpha-emitting nuclides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alpha-emitting_nuclides) and organics) and carbonate coprecipitation (removes [alkali earth metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali_earth_metal) including [strontium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strontium) elements). The water is then passed through 16 absorbent columns to remove nuclides.[^cite_note-iaea.cr-34]<sup>: §1.1 </sup>[^cite_note-ALPS-loop-31]

Wastewater is pumped to ALPS along with the concentrated saltwater from desalination.[^cite_note-wnn-20130926-30][^cite_note-ALPS-loop-31] As some tritium still remains, even treated water would require dilution to meet drinkable standards.[^cite_note-Guardian_20201016-9][^cite_note-35] Although carbon-14 is not removed, the content in pre-treatment water is low enough to meet drinkable standards without dilution.[^cite_note-Policy-36]

Japan's [Nuclear Regulation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Regulation_Authority) (NRA) approved the design of ALPS in March 2013. ALPS is to be run in three independent units and will be able to purify 250 tons of water per day.[^cite_note-Tokyo_Shimbun_20210413a-7][^cite_note-37] Unit "A" started operation in April. In June, unit A was found to be leaking water and shut down. In July, the cause was narrowed down to chloride and [hypochlorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypochlorite) corrosion of water tanks; TEPCO responded by adding a rubber layer into the tanks. By August, all systems were shut down awaiting repair. One unit was expected to come online by September, with full recovery planned by the end of 2013.[^cite_note-38]

By September 2018, TEPCO reports that 20% of the water had been treated to the required level.[^cite_note-39]

By 2020, the daily buildup of contaminated water was reduced to 170 metric tonnes thanks to groundwater isolation installations.[^cite_note-Guardian_20201016-9] TEPCO reports that 72% of the water in its tanks, some from early trials of ALPS, needed to be repurified.[^cite_note-METI_Q\&A-40] The portion of ready-to-discharge water raised to 34% by 2021,[^cite_note-41] and to 35% by 2023.[^cite_note-42]

Some scientists expressed reservations due to potential [bioaccumulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioaccumulation) of ruthenium, cobalt, strontium, and plutonium, which sometimes slip through the ALPS process and were present in 71% of the tanks.[^cite_note-science2021-10]

#### Japanese approval and monitoring (2021-)



Prime Minister [Yoshihide Suga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshihide_Suga) holding a bottle of treated radioactive water and was affirmed "after [diluting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration) it would be drinkable". [Fukushima plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_power_plant), 2020.

[^cite_note-Asahi_Shimbun_20201103-43]

[^cite_note-44]

Since the 2011 [Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_disaster), the nuclear plant has accumulated 1.25 million [tonnes](31185.md) of waste water, stored in 1,061 tanks on the land of the nuclear plant, as of March 2021.[^cite_note-Japan_Times_20210413-45] It was projected that the site would run out of land for water tanks by 2022.[^cite_note-Japan_Times_20210413-45] It has been suggested the government could have solved the problem by allocating more land surrounding the power plant for water tanks, since the surrounding area had been designated as unsuitable for humans. Regardless, the government was reluctant to act.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] *[Mainichi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainichi_Shimbun)* criticized the government for showing "no sincerity" in "unilaterally push\[ing] through with the logic that there will no longer be enough storage space".[^cite_note-Mainichi_20210414-48]

On 13 April 2021, the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suga_Cabinet) of Prime Minister [Yoshihide Suga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshihide_Suga) unanimously approved that TEPCO dump the stored water to the Pacific Ocean over a course of 30 years. The Cabinet asserted the dumped water will be treated and [diluted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration) to [drinkable standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water).[^cite_note-BBC_20210413-49][^cite_note-Bloomberg_20210412-50] The idea of dumping had been floated by Japanese experts and officials as early as June 2016.[^cite_note-Tokyo_Shimbun_20210413a-7] A [kawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawaii) mascot for the dump, Tritium-kun, was even created in hopes of improving public opinion on the issue; it was quickly scrapped after backlash.[^cite_note-51]

In April 2023, Japan's NRA announced a *Comprehensive Radiation Monitoring Plan*, in which the concentration of radionuclides in food (land and sea), soil, water, and air will be continually monitored across Japan. NRA also set up a system to monitor the radionuclide concentration in ALPS-processed water in order to verify TEPCO's readings.[^cite_note-iaea.cr-34]<sup>: §3.5.2 </sup>[^cite_note-52]

#### International testing

An IAEA task force was dispatched to Japan in 2021, and they released their first report in February 2022.[^cite_note-53] Among other findings, TEPCO has demonstrated to IAEA that their pump setup thoroughly mixes waters in tanks.[^cite_note-iaea.cr-34]<sup>: §3.3.2 </sup>

In May 2023, 3 IAEA laboratories and 4 national laboratories participated in an interlaboratory comparison to verify TEPCO's testing of ALPS-treated water.[^cite_note-iaea.cr-34]<sup>: §4.1 </sup> Out of the 30 radionuclides TEPCO regularly tests for, 12 were found to be above detection limits. 52 out of 53 results were found to agree with the combined result; the only problematic result was of I-129, where [Korea Institute of Nuclear Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Institute_of_Nuclear_Safety) reported a value too low compared to the weighted average. TEPCO's methology was found to be fit for purpose: although it is less sensitive for actinides than some participating labs, the detection limits were far enough from regulatory limits, and the alpha-emission screening test appears accurate enough. TEPCO's testing method for Am-141 may require additional review.[^cite_note-54] The same sample was tested by Japan's NRA with no disagreements found.[^cite_note-iaea.cr-34]<sup>: §3.5.2 </sup>

The tritium that is not filtered out has a radioactivity of 148,900 Bq/L, compared to 620,000 Bq/L before treatment. TEPCO intends to dilute it down to 1,500 Bq/L or less before release.[^cite_note-koreaherald-12]

### Discharge into the Pacific Ocean (2023–)



[Prime Minister Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) visiting the Fukushima plant in August 2023; Kishida's government continued with the planned water discharge.

On 22 August 2023, Japan announced that it would start releasing treated radioactive water from the tsunami-hit Fukushima nuclear plant into the Pacific Ocean in 48 hours, despite opposition from its neighbours.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Japan says the water is safe after the use of Advanced Liquid Processing System (ALPS), which removes nearly all traces of radiation from the wastewater, with tritium being the primary exception to this. As a result, Japan has committed to diluting the water in order to bring levels of tritium below the regulatory standards set by the International Atomic Energy Agency. The International Atomic Energy Agency has stated that the plan meets safety standards, but critics contend that more studies need to be done and the release should be halted.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] On 24 August, Japan began the discharge of treated waste water into the Pacific Ocean, sparking protests in the region and China to expand its ban to all aquatic imports from Japan.[^cite_note-59] Over 1 million tonnes of treated wastewater will be released by Japan over the next thirty years as per the plan.[^cite_note-BBC20230824-60]

On August 25, TEPCO reported that the amount of tritium in seawater around Fukushima has remained below the detection limit of 10 Bq/L.[^cite_note-61] The Japanese Fishery Agency reported that fish caught 4 km away from the discharge pipe contained no detectable amounts of tritium.[^cite_note-62]

On March 15, 2024, the discharge was suspended temporarily after the Fukushima coastal region experienced another 5.8-magnitude earthquake. No abnormalities were detected with the wastewater treatment.[^cite_note-63]

On January 22, 2025, the Global Times, affiliated with the Chinese Communist Party's official newspaper, reported that Chinese experts had completed their own analysis of seawater samples. According to the report, no abnormalities were found in the concentrations of radioactive materials such as cesium and strontium.[^cite_note-64]

On July 7, 2025, the China Atomic Energy Authority announced that an analysis by Chinese research institutions of seawater and marine life collected in February near TEPCO's Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant confirmed no abnormalities in the concentrations of radioactive materials, including tritium.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

### Reactions

#### Official nuclear science panels

- The Japanese expert panel "ALPS subcommittee", chaired by nuclear scientist Ichiro Yamamoto, released a report in January 2020 which calculated that discharging all the water to the sea in one year would cause a radiation dose of 0.81 [microsieverts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsievert) to the locals, therefore it is negligible as compared to the Japanese' [natural radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_radiation) of 2,100 microsieverts per year.

## Environmental effects

See also: [Radiation effects from the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_effects_from_the_Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_disaster)

### Initial discharge

A large amount of caesium entered the sea from the initial atmospheric release (see above). By 2013, the concentrations of caesium-137 in the Fukushima coastal waters were around the level before the accident. However, concentrations in coastal sediments declined more slowly than in coastal waters, and the amount of caesium-137 stored in sediments most likely exceeded that in the [water column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_column) by 2020. The sediments may provide a long-term source of caesium-137 in the seawater.[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.34-126] According to Buesseler, the release of [strontium-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strontium-90) could be more problematic because, unlike some of the other isotopes, it gets into a person's bones.[^cite_note-national_geographic_20130809-5]

Data on [marine foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life) indicates their radioactive concentrations are falling toward initial levels. 41% of samples caught off the Fukushima coast in 2011 had caesium-137 concentrations above the legal limit (100 becquerels per kilogram), and this had declined to 0.05% in 2015.[^cite_note-UNSCEAR_2020,_p.34-126] [United States Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) stated in 2021 that "FDA has no evidence that radionuclides from the Fukushima incident are present in the U.S. food supply at levels that are unsafe".[^cite_note-127] Yet, presenting the science alone has not helped customers to regain their trust on eating Fukushima fishery products.[^cite_note-Yuto_Chiba-25]

### 2023 discharge

The most prevalent radionuclide in the wastewater is tritium. A total of 780 terabecquerels (TBq) will be released into the ocean at a rate of 22 TBq per year.[^cite_note-Policy-36] Tritium is routinely released into the ocean from operating nuclear power plants, sometimes in much greater quantities. For comparison, the [La Hague nuclear processing site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Hague_site) in France released 11,400 TBq of tritium in the year of 2018. In addition, about 60,000 TBq of tritium is produced naturally in the atmosphere each year by cosmic rays.[^cite_note-Policy-36][^cite_note-theconversation-128][^cite_note-wired-129]

Other radionuclides present in the wastewater, like [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137), are not normally released by nuclear power plants. However, the concentrations in the treated water is minuscule relative to regulation limits.[^cite_note-wired-129]

"There is consensus among scientists that the impact on health is minuscule, still, it can't be said the risk is zero, which is what causes controversy", Michiaki Kai, a Japanese nuclear expert, told [AFP](471981.md).[^cite_note-130] David Bailey, a physicist whose lab measures radioactivity, said that with tritium at diluted concentrations, "there is no issue with marine species, unless we see a severe decline in fish population".[^cite_note-BBC20230826-123]

Ferenc Dalnoki-Veress, a scientist-in-residence at the [Middlebury Institute of International Studies at Monterey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlebury_Institute_of_International_Studies_at_Monterey), said regarding dilution that bringing in living creatures makes the situation more complex.[^cite_note-middlebury-125] Robert Richmond, a biologist from the [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi), told the BBC that the inadequate radiological and ecological assessment raises the concern that Japan would be unable to detect what enters the environment and "get the genie back in the bottle".[^cite_note-BBC20230826-123] Dalnoki-Veress, Richmond, and three other panelists consulting for the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum) wrote that dilution may fail to account for bioaccumulation and exposure pathways that involve organically-bound tritium (OBT).[^cite_note-insidewater-122]

According to a 2023 nationwide public opinion poll by the *[Yomiuri Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Shimbun)*, 57% of respondents approved of the government beginning the ocean release of treated water from the plant, exceeding the 32% who disapproved.[^cite_note-131]
