**County Kildare** ([Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *Contae Chill Dara*) is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Ireland) in [Ireland](14560.md). It is in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Ireland) of [Leinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster) and is part of the [Eastern and Midland Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_and_Midland_Region). It is named after the town of [Kildare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare). [Kildare County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_County_Council) is the [local authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland) for the county, which had a population of 246,977 at the 2022 census.[^cite_note-cso2022-2]

## Geography and subdivisions

Kildare is the 24th-largest of [Ireland's 32 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Ireland) in area and the seventh-largest in terms of population. It is the eighth largest of Leinster's twelve counties in size, and the second largest in terms of population. It is bordered by the counties of [Carlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Carlow), [Laois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Laois), [Meath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Meath), [Offaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Offaly), [South Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dublin) and [Wicklow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Wicklow). As an inland county, Kildare is generally a lowland region. The county's highest points are the [foothills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothills) of the [Wicklow Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicklow_Mountains) bordering to the east. The highest point in Kildare is [Cupidstown Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupidstown_Hill) on the border with South Dublin, with the better-known [Hill of Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_of_Allen) in central Kildare.

The county has three major rivers running through it: the [Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Barrow), the [Liffey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Liffey) and the [Boyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Boyne). The [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_\(Ireland\)) crosses the county from [Lyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyons_Hill) on the east to [Rathangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rathangan,_County_Kildare) and [Monasterevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasterevin) on the west. A southern branch joins the Barrow navigation at [Athy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athy). The [Royal Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canal) stretches across the north of the county along the border with Meath. Pollardstown Fen is the largest remaining [calcareous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcareous) fen in Ireland, covering an area of 220 hectares and is recognised as an internationally important fen ecosystem with unique and endangered plant communities, and was declared a National Nature Reserve in 1986.

The [Bog of Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog_of_Allen) is a large bog measuring 958 km<sup>2</sup> (370 sq mi) that extends across County Kildare, [County Meath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Meath), [County Offaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Offaly), [County Laois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Laois), and [County Westmeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Westmeath). Kildare has 243 km<sup>2</sup> (94 sq mi) of bog (almost 14% of Kildare's land area) mostly located in the south-west and north-west, a majority of this being [Raised Bog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog). It is a habitat for over 185 plant and animal species.

There are 8,472 hectares (20,930 acres) of forested land in Kildare, accounting for roughly 5% of the county's total land area. 4,056 hectares (10,020 acres) of this is coniferous, while there is 2,963 hectares (7,320 acres) of broadleaf and the remaining area are unclassified species. [Coillte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coillte_Teoranta) and [Dúchas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BAchas) currently own 47% of the forestry. Coillte runs Donadea Forest Park which is in North-Central Kildare. The forest covers 259 hectares (640 acres) of mixed woodland (60% [broadleaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad-leaved_tree), 40% [conifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer)) and is the largest forest park in Kildare.

## History

Main article: [History of County Kildare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_County_Kildare)

Kildare was [shired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire) in 1297[^cite_note-5] and assumed its present borders in 1832, following amendments to remove a number of enclaves and exclaves.

The county was the home of the powerful [Fitzgerald family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FitzGerald_dynasty). Parts of the county were also part of [the Pale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pale_\(Ireland\)) area around [Dublin](8504.md).

### Local government

[Kildare County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_County_Council) is the [local authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland) for the county. The council has 40 members, elected in the [local electoral areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_electoral_area) of: Athy (5 seats), [Celbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celbridge) (4 seats), [Leixlip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leixlip) (3 seats), Clane (5 seats), [Maynooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynooth) (5 Seats), [Kildare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare) (5 seats), [Newbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbridge,_County_Kildare) (6 Seats) and [Naas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naas) (7 Seats). These form the municipal districts of Athy, Celbridge–Leixlip, Clane–Maynooth, Kildare–Newbridge, and Naas.[^cite_note-6] The current council was elected in May 2024.

Kildare County Council nominates three councillors to the [Eastern and Midland Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_and_Midland_Regional_Assembly), who are part of the [Mid-East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-East_Region,_Ireland) strategic planning area committee.[^cite_note-si573-7]

### Former districts

Kildare was formerly divided into the [rural districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_district_\(Ireland\)) of Athy No. 1, Celbridge No. 1, Edenderry No. 2, and Naas No. 1, and the [urban districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_and_rural_districts_\(Ireland\)) of Athy and Naas.[^cite_note-8] The rural districts were abolished in 1925.[^cite_note-9] Newbridge or Droichead Nua, within the former rural district of Naas No. 1, had [town commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_commissioners), and Leixlip was given that status too in 1988.[^cite_note-10] The urban districts of Athy and Naas and the town commissioners of Newbridge and Leixlip became [town councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_council_\(Ireland\)) in 2002.[^cite_note-11] All town councils in Ireland were abolished in 2014.[^cite_note-12]

### National elections

For elections to [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann), there are two constituencies in the county: [Kildare North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_North_\(D%C3%A1il_constituency\)) (5 seats) and [Kildare South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_South_\(D%C3%A1il_constituency\)) (4 seats).[^cite_note-13] At the [2024 Irish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election), Kildare North returned Aidan Farrelly (SD), Réada Cronin (SF), Joe Neville (FG), Naoise Ó Cearúil (FF), and James Lawless (FF),[^cite_note-14] while Kildare South returned Sean Ó Fearghaíl (FF) (returned automatically as outgoing Ceann Comhairle), Mark Wall (Labour), Martin Heydon (FG), and Shónagh Ní Raghallaigh (SF).[^cite_note-15]

For elections to the [European Parliament](9581.md), it is part of the [Midlands–North-West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands%E2%80%93North-West) constituency (5 [MEPs](258909.md)).

## Demographics

The county's population effectively doubled, from approximately 104,000 to 210,000, between 1981 and 2011.[^cite_note-16] The northeastern region of Kildare had the highest average per-capita income in Ireland outside [County Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Dublin) in 2003. East Kildare's population has increased rapidly, for example, the population of the Naas suburb of [Sallins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sallins) increased sixfold (from 854 to 5,849) in the 20 years between the 1996 and 2016 census.[^cite_note-17]

As of 2022 the population of the county was 246,977. Ethnically, the 2016 census recorded County Kildare as 84% white Irish, 9% other white ethnicities, 2% black, 2% Asian, 1% of other ethnicities, and 2% not stated. For religion, the census recorded a population that was 80% Catholic, 9% of other stated religions, 10% with no religion and 2% not stated.[^cite_note-cso2016-3]

## Transport

### Road

County Kildare houses the hub of Ireland's network of major roads.

The [N4 (M4)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N4_road_\(Ireland\)) from Dublin to [Sligo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sligo) travels along the north of the county [by-passing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bypass_\(road\)) the towns of Leixlip, Maynooth and Kilcock.

The [M7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M7_motorway_\(Ireland\)) from Dublin to [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick) runs through the county and bypasses the towns of Naas, Newbridge, Kildare and Monasterevin. This road is colloquially referred to as the "Naas [Dual carriageway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway)" because when it was originally up-graded in 1964 the road from Dublin to Naas was a double-lane carriageway, one of the first of its kind in Ireland.

The [M9](18461865.md) is another [motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorways_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland) that commences at [Kilcullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilcullen) and ends at [Waterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford). It is motorway standard for its entire length.

### Rail

The county is also served by the trains connecting with Dublin, southern Leinster, Munster and Connacht, with daily connections to [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_\(city\)), Waterford, Limerick, and [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway). The principal [Irish Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rail) InterCity train station in the county is Kildare, however, Newbridge, Sallins and [Hazelhatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazelhatch) are also served by South Western Commuter services, while Maynooth, in northern County Kildare, is served by Western Commuter and Sligo InterCity services.

### Waterway



River Barrow and [White's Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White's_Castle), [Athy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athy)

Kildare is the centre of Ireland's [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_\(Ireland\)) network built in the late 18th century.[^cite_note-20] This connects Kildare with Waterford, Dublin, Limerick and Athlone. The [Royal Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canal) runs west from Dublin and parts of it form the boundary with [County Meath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Meath).

## Irish language

There are 4,491 Irish speakers in County Kildare; 2,451 attending the seven [Gaelscoils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelscoil) (Irish language primary schools) and one [Gaelcholáiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelchol%C3%A1iste) (Irish language secondary school).[^cite_note-21] According to the Irish Census 2006, 2,040 people in the county identify themselves as being daily Irish speakers outside the education system.

## Education



[Newbridge College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbridge_College)

- Two third-level educational institutions – [St. Patrick's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_College,_Maynooth) founded by [King George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_III) in 1795 to educate Ireland's Catholics and [Maynooth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynooth_University) founded in 1997 – are located in [Maynooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynooth). They share campus space and many facilities. The two institutions were formally separated in 1997. Maynooth University is the only university in the Republic of Ireland not situated in a city.

- [Clongowes Wood College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clongowes_Wood_College) is a private secondary boarding school for boys, located near Clane. Founded by the [Society of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) (The Jesuits) in 1814, it is one of Ireland's oldest Catholic schools.

- [Newbridge College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbridge_College) is a co-educational fee-paying secondary school. The [Dominican Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) founded Newbridge College in 1852 as a boarding school for boys.

- Leinster Senior College is a small private fee-paying secondary school geared solely towards the Leaving Certificate.

- The town of Clane is home to another educational institute, Clane College, a provider of further education to the wider Kildare community.

- [Naas C.B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naas_C.B.S.), St. Mary's College Naas, Piper's Hill College and [Gaelcholáiste Chill Dara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelchol%C3%A1iste_Chill_Dara) are the main secondary schools in [Naas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naas).

## Sport

### GAA



Horses near Pollardstown Fen

The nickname for the [Kildare GAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_GAA) team is the *Lilywhites*, as a result of its early jerseys being made from the bags of the Lilywhite Bakery. The all-white jerseys they wear are in reference to this.

In 1928, Kildare became the first team to win the [Sam Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Maguire) trophy for the [All-Ireland Senior Football Championship](885732.md), defeating Cavan 2–6 to 2–5.[^cite_note-22] However, since then Kildare has reached the All-Ireland Football Championship Final on four occasions, the last being in 1998, but has failed on all four attempts.

County Kildare is also known as the *Shortgrass County* which is a reference to how short the grass is on the commons of [the Curragh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curragh).

### Golf

The [Michael Smurfit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Smurfit) owned [K Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Club), situated on the River Liffey near [Straffan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straffan) played host to the 2006 [Ryder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryder_Cup).[^cite_note-23]

[Carton House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carton_House) Golf Club is located in Maynooth. The [Golfing Union of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golfing_Union_of_Ireland), the longest established golf union in the world,[^cite_note-24] have their national headquarters on the estate. This facility also comprises the GUI National Academy, an 8.9-hectare (22-acre; 89,000-square-metre) teaching facility for up-and-coming golfers, as well as being a facility available to all golfers in Ireland.[^cite_note-25]

Other prominent courses are located at Knockanally and [Clane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clane).

### Horse racing

Kildare is famous worldwide for its horse racing.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] [The Curragh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curragh_Racecourse) [horse-racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse-racing) course is the home to all five Irish Classic Flat races. Also located in County Kildare are two other courses, [Punchestown Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punchestown_Racecourse), home of the National Hunt Festival of Ireland, and [Naas Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naas_Racecourse), which runs both National Hunt and Flat meetings and is used by top racehorse trainers as a test for horses preparing for the Cheltenham festival.

The county is famous for the quality of horses bred in the many stud farms to which it is home, including the [Irish National Stud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_National_Stud) and many other top studs such as Gilltown, Moyglare and [Kildangan Stud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildangan_Stud), and race horse training establishments, such as the Osborne Stables.

### Motorsports

Kildare is the home to [Mondello Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondello_Park), Ireland's only international motorsport venue. The venue was established in 1968,[^cite_note-28] and was purchased by [Martin Birrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Birrane) in 1986. Biranne oversaw major redevelopment of the venue with the incorporation of 3.5 km (2.2 mi) of race track, which was opened in 1998.[^cite_note-29] The Circuit also has 3 km (1.9 mi) of extreme off-road driving trails and a 2-hectare (5-acre) off-road activities centre and the Museum of Motorsport. Mondello Park was awarded the [FIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA) International race track status in 2001.[^cite_note-30] It is host to National and International Race events, Motor Shows, Car & Bike Track days, Training Schools and Corporate Events.

### Soccer

Soccer ([association football](10568.md)) in County Kildare was represented at national level by [Kildare County F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_County_F.C.), which competed in the League of Ireland from 2002 until its dissolution in 2009.[^cite_note-31] Following a 17-year absence of League of Ireland football in the county, [Newbridge Town F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbridge_Town_F.C.) was announced in 2026 as a new entrant to the [FAI National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAI_National_League), restoring senior national league football to Kildare.[^cite_note-32]
