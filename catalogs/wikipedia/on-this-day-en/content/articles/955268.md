There have been twenty-five [heads of government](37570.md) of the [Central African Republic](5478.md) and the [Central African Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Empire). The office of prime minister, the head of government, was created when [Ubangi-Shari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubangi-Shari), a [French colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire), became an [autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_entity) territory of [France](5843419.md) (as the Central African Republic) in December 1958. It was originally the highest post of the Central African Republic, though France did maintain a governor in the territory. After the Central African Republic declared its independence on 13 August 1960, [David Dacko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dacko) held both the prime minister and newly created [president of the Central African Republic](378644.md) posts briefly before eliminating the prime minister position and placing all [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) in the office of the President.

President [Jean-Bédel Bokassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-B%C3%A9del_Bokassa) restored the office of prime minister to assist him in governing the country in 1975, shortly before he declared himself [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Central_Africa). He selected as prime minister [Elisabeth Domitien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Domitien), who become [Africa's first female head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_first_women_holders_of_political_offices_in_Africa). After Domitien was removed from office, Bokassa named [Ange-Félix Patassé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ange-F%C3%A9lix_Patass%C3%A9) to become his next prime minister. Patassé continued serving as prime minister after Bokassa declared the establishment of the Central African Empire in December 1976. [Henri Maïdou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Ma%C3%AFdou) succeeded Patassé and continued serving as prime minister after Bokassa was overthrown from power. During the following two years of Dacko's presidency, three more politicians served as prime minister. The post was abolished when Dacko was overthrown from the presidency by [André Kolingba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Kolingba) on 1 September 1981. The position, as it exists today, was recreated in 1991, when President Kolingba was forced to relinquish some of the executive power. The president has the authority to name the prime minister and can remove them from office at any time. The prime minister is the head of the government; within days of being appointed, they must select individuals for their [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)), who they will work with to coordinate the government.

The current prime minister of the Central African Republic is [Félix Moloua](63445994.md), since 7 February 2022.[^cite_note-1]

## Footnotes

- [**<sup>A</sup>**](#ref_Note1A)  Goumba had served as President of the Government Council since 26 July 1958.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalck2005198-2]

  &#x20;When the Central African Republic became a territorial autonomy, he served as the acting leader the government from 1 December 1958 to 8 December 1958.

- [**<sup>B</sup>**](#ref_Note1B)  Boganda was killed in a mysterious plane crash on 29 March 1959, while en route to Bangui.

  [^cite_note-19]

  &#x20;The exact cause of the crash was not determined,

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalck200527-20]

  &#x20;but sabotage was widely suspected.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETitley199716-21]

  &#x20;Experts found a trace of explosives in the plane's wreckage, but revelation of this detail was withheld. Although those responsible for the crash were never identified, people have suspected the [French secret service](633707.md), and even Boganda's wife, of being involved.

- [**<sup>C</sup>**](#ref_Note1C)  Dacko removed the prime minister position and consolidated power in the presidency.

- [**<sup>D</sup>**](#ref_Note1D)  [President for Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_for_Life) [Jean-Bédel Bokassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-B%C3%A9del_Bokassa) established a new government on 2 January 1975 and reintroduced the position of prime minister. He appointed Domitien as president of MESAN and Prime Minister of the Central African Republic.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETitley199783-22]

- [**<sup>E</sup>**](#ref_Note1E)  Domitien was removed from office because she publicly expressed her disapproval of Bokassa's plans to establish a monarchy in the Central African Republic.

  [^cite_note-23]

  &#x20;Bokassa then had her placed under [house arrest](247643.md).

  [^cite_note-24]

- [**<sup>F</sup>**](#ref_Note1F)  On 4 December 1976, Bokassa instituted a new constitution and declared the republic a [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy), the [Central African Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Empire).

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalck2005xxxiv–xxxv-25]

- [**<sup>G</sup>**](#ref_Note1G)  President Dacko appointed Maïdou as vice president on 27 September 1979.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalck2005200-9]

- [**<sup>H</sup>**](#ref_Note1H)  Prime Minister Ayandho was dismissed from office on 22 August 1980 by Dacko, who saw him as a political threat, and placed under house arrest.

  [^cite_note-Lentzp154-7]

- [**<sup>I</sup>**](#ref_Note1I)  Dacko created the [Central African Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Democratic_Union) in February 1980 as the country's only political party.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalck2005xxxvii-26]

- [**<sup>J</sup>**](#ref_Note1J)  Malendoma was removed as prime minister and replaced by Lakoué.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalck2005xlvii-27]

- [**<sup>K</sup>**](#ref_Note1K)  In April 1995, Mandaba resigned as prime minister, preempting a threatened [vote of no-confidence](208101.md) from his own party, following accusations of incompetence and corruption.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurison2003200-28]

- [**<sup>L</sup>**](#ref_Note1L)  Gbezera-Bria was named prime minister on 30 January 1997 to replace Ngoupande, who had been accused of siding with disgruntled soldiers, who had sparked a mutiny on 15 November 1996 to demand higher wages. Ngoupande also didn't strongly support President Patassé's decision to call in French troops to suppress the soldier uprising.

  [^cite_note-29]

- [**<sup>M</sup>**](#ref_Note1M)  President Patassé fired Dologuélé on 1 April 2001 and replaced him with Ziguélé, a senior diplomat who had served as ambassador to [Benin](3459.md) for the last two years. Patassé did not provide an explanation for his decision, but political observers state that the nonpartisan Dologuélé had become widely unpopular with the ruling MLPC party.

  [^cite_note-30]

- [**<sup>N</sup>**](#ref_Note1N)  Ziguélé left office when [François Bozizé](213625.md) seized power on 15 March 2003.

- [**<sup>O</sup>**](#ref_Note1O)  On 11 December 2003, Goumba was dismissed as prime minister and was appointed as vice president.

  [^cite_note-31]

- [**<sup>P</sup>**](#ref_Note1P)  Gaombalet resigned as prime minister on 11 June 2005 after being elected as Speaker of the National Assembly on 7 June.

  [^cite_note-32]

- [**<sup>Q</sup>**](#ref_Note1Q)  In mid-January 2008, members of the [National Assembly](3604671.md) filed a [censure motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censure_\(motion\)) against the Doté government, in response to countrywide civil service strike initiated by [trade unions](17626.md) to protest the government's failure to pay [arrears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrears) to government employees.

  [^cite_note-33]

  &#x20;On 18 January, Doté announced his resignation as prime minister.

  [^cite_note-34]

- [**<sup>R</sup>**](#ref_Note1R)  Tiangaye resigned with President [Michel Djotodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Djotodia) in [N'Djamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Djamena), [Chad](5488.md) on 10 January 2014.
