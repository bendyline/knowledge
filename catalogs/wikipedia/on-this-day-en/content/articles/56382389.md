# Donald Trump and golf



Trump playing golf with Japanese prime minister [Shinzo Abe](2634434.md) in 2019

&#x20;Throughout his career as a businessman and politician, [Donald Trump](4848272.md) has had a relationship with the sport of [golf](19568112.md). As a real estate developer,[^cite_note-GolfDigest-1] Trump began acquiring and constructing [golf courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_courses) in 1999. By 2016, he owned 17 golf courses worldwide through his [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company), [the Trump Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trump_Organization).[^cite_note-stanley20161123-2][^cite_note-GolfDigest-1]

Courses owned by Trump have been selected to host various [PGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Golfers'_Association_of_America) and [LPGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LPGA) events, including the 2022 [PGA Championship](224616.md),[^cite_note-USAToday-3] although the PGA terminated this in the aftermath of the [January 6 United States Capitol attack](66307490.md) in 2021.[^cite_note-4] A spokesman for the Trump Organization said that "This is a breach of a binding contract and they have no right to terminate the agreement."[^cite_note-5]

Following his election, Trump broke precedent with recent presidents and chose not to [divest](981983.md) from his business holdings, including his golf courses. Although this was not illegal, ethics lawyers and journalists identified potential [conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest). At least three lawsuits (*[D.C. and Maryland v. Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.C._and_Maryland_v._Trump)*, *[Blumenthal v. Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumenthal_v._Trump)* and *[CREW v. Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CREW_v._Trump)*) were filed claiming that foreign payments at Trump golf courses and hotels violate the [Emoluments Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Emoluments_Clause) of the [U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). The three lawsuits were dismissed as moot, dismissed for lack of standing, and allowed, respectively.

Trump, according to [Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus), "loves the game of golf more than he loves money". According to *[Golf Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_Digest)*, his [handicap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicap_\(golf\)) is as low as 2.8,[^cite_note-stanley20161123-2] a figure that sportswriter [Rick Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Reilly) dismisses at length in his book *[Commander in Cheat: How Golf Explains Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Cheat:_How_Golf_Explains_Trump)*.[^cite_note-Reilly_book-6]

Trump began playing golf while attending [Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University).[^cite_note-GolfDigest-1] In the introduction to his 2005 book *The Best Golf Advice I Ever Received*, Trump wrote, "for me and millions of people—men, women, young and old around the world—golf is more than a game. It is a passion".[^cite_note-Trump2005-7]

Trump's love of golf has also affected American diplomacy. [Prime Minister of Japan](24833.md) [Shinzo Abe](2634434.md) played golf with him five times, helping the two leaders to become personally close. After Trump's victory in the [2024 United States presidential election](59568546.md), [President of South Korea](98630.md) [Yoon Suk Yeol](62877952.md) reportedly began playing golf again to similarly benefit his country when Trump was again president.[^cite_note-nbc20241111-8]



Donald Trump golfing in 2006

In 1999, Trump opened his first golf course: the [Trump International Golf Club, West Palm Beach](53109904.md) in Florida. Land for the US\$45 million course was acquired through a lawsuit against [Palm Beach County, Florida](73647.md), after Trump's purchase of the [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) resort.[^cite_note-Golf2007-9] By 2007, Trump owned four courses around the US.[^cite_note-Golf2007-9] After the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), Trump began purchasing existing golf courses and re-designing them.[^cite_note-USAToday-3]

Golf courses owned by Trump hosted the [LPGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LPGA) Tour finale from 2001 to 2008, as well as the 2009 [US Junior Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Junior_Amateur_Golf_Championship) and [US Junior Girls Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Girls'_Junior_Golf_Championship).[^cite_note-Golf-10] In 2014, the [Professional Golfers' Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Golfers'_Association_of_America) announced a multi-year partnership with the Trump Organization. The PGA of America selected Trump golf courses to host the 2017 [Senior PGA Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_PGA_Championship) and the 2022 [PGA Championship](224616.md).[^cite_note-PGA-11]

In June 2015, Trump announced his [candidacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_presidential_campaign,_2016) in the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election) with a controversial speech which led to companies such as [Macy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy's) and [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) cutting ties with the businessman.[^cite_note-CBS-12] While speaking on [illegal immigration](5044573.md), Trump falsely claimed that Mexico is "sending people that have lots of problems... they're bringing drugs, they're bringing crime. They're rapists. And some, I assume, are good people,"[^cite_note-13] drawing criticism from immigration and [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) [advocacy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy_group).[^cite_note-CBS-12] The LPGA, PGA of America, [PGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour), and [United States Golf Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Golf_Association) issued a joint statement, saying that while the organizations "do not usually comment on presidential politics, Mr. Trump's comments are inconsistent with our strong commitment to an inclusive and welcoming environment in the game of golf."[^cite_note-GolfDigest2015-14]

The PGA of America also decided to relocate the 2015 [PGA Grand Slam of Golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Grand_Slam_of_Golf)—an exhibition match which had been scheduled to take place at [Trump National Golf Club, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_National_Golf_Club_\(Los_Angeles\)).[^cite_note-NYTimes-15] In 2018, [PGA Tour Latinoamérica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour_Latinoam%C3%A9rica) held its [Shell Tour Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_PGA_Tour_Latinoam%C3%A9rica) at [Trump National Doral Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_National_Doral_Miami)'s Golden Palm course after plans were announced to demolish the [Melreese Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melreese_Country_Club?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Miami, which had held the event, for a football stadium.[^cite_note-16]

In July 2024, an hour-long YouTube video was released of Trump playing 18 holes with [Bryson DeChambeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryson_DeChambeau).[^cite_note-17]

### Cheating allegations

Interviewed under oath in 2022 before a grand jury, Senator [Lindsey Graham](363279.md) was asked if Trump cheats at golf, to which Graham replied: "Some people say you may outdrive him, but you’re not going to outdrive his caddy. It is what it is."[^cite_note-Mazza_1/16/2026-18]

Trump's cheating at golf has been the subject of much commentary. Sportswriter [Rick Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Reilly)'s book *[Commander in Cheat: How Golf Explains Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Cheat:_How_Golf_Explains_Trump)* details Trump's cheating at golf.[^cite_note-Reilly_book-6]

Rick Reilly examined a lie Trump frequently tells: "Trump's boast about winning 18 club championships is a lie that's so over-the-top Crazytown it loses all credibility among golfers the second it's out of his mouth." Reilly cites [Gary Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Player): "When Trump told Gary Player he'd won 18 championships, Player scoffed. 'I told him that if anyone beats him, he kicks them out. So, he had to win.'" He describes how Trump's name is only on the wall of golf clubs he owns, and in one case the club wasn't even open at the time he claimed to have won the championship there: "Trump International in West Palm Beach, Florida, has a plaque on the wall that lists all the men who've won the men's club championship. Trump appears three times: 1999, 2001, and 2009. But hold on. The course wasn't even *open* in 1999."[^cite_note-Reilly_4/2/2019-19]

Tom D'Angelo has examined Trump's long history of cheating at golf. One time, [Mike Tirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tirico) "hit a 3-wood about 230 yards onto the green on one hole. When he arrived the ball was in a bunker about 50 feet from the pin. 'Tough break,' Trump said. Tirico later was told by Trump's caddie that his shot was about 10 feet from the hole and Trump threw it into the bunker. 'I watched him do it,' the caddie said." ### Puerto Rico

In 2007, the Trump Organization took over the management and licensed Trump's name to the 4-year old, 36-hole oceanfront golf course at Coco Beach, Puerto Rico. It hosted the 2008 PGA Puerto Rico Open, but the club kept losing money and in 2015 filed for bankruptcy protection.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-CNBC-26]

### Dubai

According to the Trump Organization, it is neither the owner nor the developer of the Trump International Golf Club.[^cite_note-wages-27][^cite_note-28] The financial disclosures Trump filed with the Federal Election Commission in 2016 show that the Trump Organization manages the two golf courses in Dubai. The second Trump-branded golf course, the Trump World Golf Club, was designed by [Tiger Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Woods) and developed and built by [DAMAC Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAMAC_Properties), a company founded by [Hussain Sajwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Sajwani). It was scheduled to be opened in 2017, the year in which a report revealed that [migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_workers_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates) working on the project were not being paid on time.[^cite_note-wages-27] In February 2021, it was announced that the inauguration was delayed at least until 2022, as the work on the project was paused. Some officials working on the construction project were told that the delay was because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), but developers in the Emirates were reportedly struggling to finish such developments even before the global health crisis.[^cite_note-29]

## Ferry Point, New York

From 2015 to 2023, the Trump Organization operated the Trump Links at Ferry Point, New York, a public golf course built and owned by New York City, under a 20-year contract awarded in 2013 by the administration of then-[Mayor Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-harwell-31][^cite_note-kranish-32] Under the agreement, the city paid the course's utility and water bills while collecting no income for the first four years. In the first year of operation, ending in March 2016, the company had \$8 million in gross receipts, and the city paid \$1 million in water and sewage bills.[^cite_note-harwell-31] In the second year of operation, gross receipts dropped 9.5%.[^cite_note-33] For the operating year that ended March 2019, the Trump Organization reported a loss of \$122,000; it now faces contractual fees of at least \$300,000 per operating year from the city.[^cite_note-34]

On January 13, 2021, New York City mayor [Bill de Blasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio) announced that the city government would be terminating all contracts with the Trump Organization effective November 14, 2021, for "directly incit\[ing] a deadly insurrection at the U.S. Capitol."[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-sues-36][^cite_note-37] The city of New York also stated that the Trump Organization had defaulted in its contractual obligations because it had failed to attract a major tournament. In June 2021, the Trump Organization sued the city for wrongful termination of the contract.[^cite_note-sues-36] The court allowed the Trump Organization to continue operating the golf course while the case was pending.[^cite_note-38] In April 2022, the judge ruled that "the city had not given a valid legal reason for ending the contract."[^cite_note-39]

In September 2023, the Trump Organization sold the operating rights to gaming and entertainment company [Bally's Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally's_Corporation) for \$60 million.[^cite_note-40] The course was renamed "Bally Links" in January 2024.[^cite_note-41]

## Coats of arms



The coat of arms granted to Davies in 1939



The coat of arms granted to "The Trump International Golf Club Scotland Ltd" in 2012

Trump has used a number of logos in the style of coats of arms for his businesses.

[Joseph E. Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_E._Davies), third husband of [Marjorie Merriweather Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Merriweather_Post) and a former U.S. ambassador of [Welsh](69894.md) origins, was granted a coat of arms, bearing the [motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motto) *Integritas*, by [British heraldic authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Arms#Kings_of_Arms_for_England,_Wales_and_Northern_Ireland) in 1939. After Donald Trump purchased [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md), the Florida estate built by Merriweather Post, in 1985, the Trump Organization started using Davies's coat of arms at [Trump golf courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_National_Golf_Club_\(disambiguation\)) and estates across the country.[^cite_note-42] It was also registered with the U.S. patent and trademark office.[^cite_note-43]

In 2008, Trump attempted to establish the American logo at his new [Trump International Golf Links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_International_Golf_Links,_Scotland) in [Balmedie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmedie), [Scotland](26994.md), but was warned by the [Lord Lyon King of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Lyon_King_of_Arms), the highest authority for [Scottish heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_heraldry), that an act of the Scottish Parliament from 1672 disallows people using unregistered arms. In January 2012, shortly after the inauguration of the golf course, Trump unveiled the new coat of arms that had been granted to *The Trump International Golf Course Scotland Ltd* by the [Court of the Lord Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Lord_Lyon), Scotland's heraldic authority, in 2012.[^cite_note-deadline-44][^cite_note-45]

From 2014, Trump used the same logo for [the Trump International Golf Links, Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_International_Golf_Links_and_Hotel_Ireland), the golf resort built from his acquisition of [Doonbeg](18340192.md) Golf Club.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

## First presidency



Most of Donald Trump's 2017 revenue was derived from his golf and real estate ventures. Revenue from such ventures increased somewhat in his second term that started in 2025, but was substantially smaller than his new [cryptocurrency](36662188.md) ventures.

[^cite_note-NYTimes_20260723-48]

Following his election in 2016, Trump announced that he would not [divest](981983.md) his business holdings, as other recent presidents had. Instead, Trump kept his ownership stake in the Trump Organization and appointed his sons [Donald Trump Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_Jr.) and [Eric Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Trump) to manage the business.[^cite_note-USAToday2017-49] In an unusual rebuke from the [Office of Government Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Government_Ethics), director [Walter Shaub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Shaub) called Trump's actions "wholly inadequate" and "meaningless from a [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) perspective."[^cite_note-WaPo-50] In an interview with *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, Trump explained: "As far as the, you know, potential conflict of interests, though, I mean I know that from the standpoint, the law is totally on my side, meaning, the president can't have a conflict of interest."[^cite_note-NYTimes2016-51]



Trump and [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinz%C5%8D_Abe)

Just days after his [inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump), [a lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CREW_v._Trump) was filed in federal court seeking to block the president from receiving payments from foreign government entities at his businesses. The lawsuit alleged that these payments constitute a violation of the [Foreign Emoluments Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_of_Nobility_Clause) of the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution).[^cite_note-NYTimes2017-52] In February 2017, Trump invited [Prime Minister of Japan](24833.md) [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinz%C5%8D_Abe) to play at the [Trump International Golf Club](53109904.md) in Florida and stay at his [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) resort. Legal and ethical concerns were raised by organizations such as the [Sunlight Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunlight_Foundation) over foreign payments Trump may receive from the visit. Trump has vowed to donate any such payments to the [Treasury Department](53667.md), although the specifics of this arrangement remain unclear.[^cite_note-NBCNews-53] In June 2017, the [attorneys general of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Maryland) and [the District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_for_the_District_of_Columbia) filed [a separate lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.C._and_Maryland_v._Trump), claiming Trump was "flagrantly violating" the Emoluments Clause.[^cite_note-WaPo6_2017-54]

A 2016 investigation by *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)* found that [lobbyists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbyist) and [corporate executives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_management) had been purchasing memberships at Trump golf courses to gain favor or contact with the president. Membership fees at Trump courses can exceed US\$100,000, leading to ethical concerns over a sitting president accepting money from people lobbying the government.[^cite_note-USAToday7_2017-55]

While campaigning to be president, Trump declared in August 2016: "I'm going to be working for you. I'm not going to have time to play golf".[^cite_note-TrumpContradictTrump-56] As president, the amount of time he spent golfing generated controversy. Despite having frequently criticized his predecessor [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) for having played golf too much as president, Trump golfed 11 times during his first eight weeks in office, when Obama did not golf at all in his first eight weeks. *Golf Digest* concluded that Obama played 333 rounds of golf over his two terms,[^cite_note-57] which *Golf Digest* describes as "...a fairly remarkable amount of golf while in office".[^cite_note-GolfDigest2017-58] Trump visited a Trump Organization property on 428 (nearly one in three) of the 1,461 days of his first presidency and is estimated to have played 261 rounds of golf, one every 5.6 days.[^cite_note-59]

According to [CNN](62028.md), Trump visited Trump-owned golf courses 92 times between becoming president in January 2017 and January 3, 2018, although the White House did not disclose whether he played golf during a visit to a golf course.[^cite_note-CNN-60] The White House on some occasions denied that Trump played golf during his visits even after photos published on social media showed him doing so.[^cite_note-TrumpContradictTrump-56] In November 2018, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* found that the average number of days between golf rounds was around 5 days for Trump, and around 12–13 days for Obama.[^cite_note-61]

Journalists and ethics experts have alleged that these frequent visits are a means of boosting publicity at the courses to sell more memberships.[^cite_note-MiamiHerald-62][^cite_note-Newsweek-63][^cite_note-BBC-64] White House press secretary [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Huckabee_Sanders) defended Trump's golfing, saying that his time on the course was spent "developing deeper and better relationships with members of [Congress](31756.md) in which those relationships have helped push forward the president's agenda." CNN reported in January 2018 that Trump was known to have played golf with members of Congress only seven times.[^cite_note-CNN-60]

Vice President [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence) stayed at the Trump International Golf Links and Hotel Ireland in 2019 while meeting with Irish officials in [Dublin](8504.md). Pence was originally going to end his trip in [Doonbeg](18340192.md), where he has familial ties, but Trump suggested that he stay at the Trump property instead, which required daily flights of more than one hour each way.[^cite_note-65]

## 2024 presidential campaign

At the [presidential debate between Trump and Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Joe_Biden%E2%80%93Donald_Trump_presidential_debate) on June 27, 2024, the candidates had a brief open-mic exchange about their golfing abilities.[^cite_note-66]

On September 15, 2024, during a tour of his course in West Palm Beach, Trump's security detail spotted an armed man, [Ryan Wesley Routh](77880898.md), behind a fence. The incident is considered to have been the [second attempt to assassinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_International_Golf_Club_shooting) Trump in 2024. Security opened fire on Routh, and he fled in a vehicle; he was later captured.[^cite_note-67]

## Second presidency

Trump began his second term on January 20, 2025. He visited his golf clubs 88 times in 2025, amounting to roughly 25 percent of the days during the first year of his second term.[^cite_note-\:0-68]

By July, his expenses for taxpayer-funded golf in his second term had reached \$52 million.[^cite_note-69] By the end of October, a month into the [government shutdown](81225965.md), the total had exceeded \$60 million,[^cite_note-70] and by Thanksgiving, it exceeded \$70 million.[^cite_note-71] By the end of March 2026, it exceeded \$100 million.[^cite_note-72] Those numbers are from HuffPost analyses.

The Trump Golf Tracker estimated higher numbers, with taxpayer costs over \$110 million by the end of 2025.[^cite_note-\:0-68]

On December 31, 2025, five years into a 50-year lease, the National Links Trust said the [Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior) had terminated its lease agreement for three public golf courses in Washington, DC.[^cite_note-73]

On January 2, 2026, it was reported that Trump planned to renovate the Courses at Andrews, known as the "president’s golf course", at [Joint Base Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Base_Andrews) in Maryland, about 15 miles from the White House. There was no prior government record of him having played the course, though he had visited the base.[^cite_note-74]

Trump's public schedule said he planned to golf in Bedminster, New Jersey on May 23, 2026, the day his son, Donald Trump, Jr., intended to marry [Bettina Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettina_Anderson) in the Bahamas. In advance of the wedding, President Trump canceled the golf trip and said he would instead stay in Washington that day.[^cite_note-75]
