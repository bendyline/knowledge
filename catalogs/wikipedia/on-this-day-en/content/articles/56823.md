**Abuja** ([/əˈbuːdʒə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is the capital city of [Nigeria](21383.md),[^cite_note-4] strategically situated at the geographic midpoint of the country within the [Federal Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Capital_Territory_\(Nigeria\)) (FCT). As the seat of the [Federal Government of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Government_of_Nigeria), it hosts key national institutions, landmarks, and buildings spread across its over 50 districts. It replaced [Lagos](85232.md) (the most populous city in Nigeria) as the capital on 12 December 1991.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbba2012\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_March_2026\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (March_2026)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-6]

Abuja's [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography) is defined by [Aso Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aso_Rock), a 400-metre (1,300 ft) [monolith](318257.md) left by [water erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_erosion). The [Presidential Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Presidential_Complex), [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_National_Assembly),[^cite_note-7] [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Supreme_Court), and much of the city extends to the south of the rock. [Zuma Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuma_Rock), a 725-metre (2,379 ft) monolith, lies just north of the city on the [expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) to [Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaduna_\(city\)).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

At the 2006 [census](6889.md), the city of Abuja had a population of 776,298[^cite_note-plac-11] and 179,674 households,[^cite_note-12] making it one of the [ten most populous cities in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_cities_by_population) (eighth place in 2006). According to the United Nations, Abuja grew by 139.7% between 2000 and 2010, making it the fastest-growing city in the world.[^cite_note-euromonitor2010-13] As of 2015, the city is experiencing an annual growth of at least 35%, retaining its position as the fastest-growing city on the African continent and one of the fastest-growing in the world.[^cite_note-abujafacts5-14][^cite_note-15]

Major religious sites include the [Nigerian National Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_National_Mosque) and the [Nigerian National Christian Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_National_Christian_Centre). The city is served by the [Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nnamdi_Azikiwe_International_Airport).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Abuja is known for being one of the few purpose-built capital cities in Africa, as well as being one of the wealthiest.[^cite_note-bbc-19]

Abuja is Nigeria's [administrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_\(government\)) and political capital. It is also a key capital on the African continent due to Nigeria's [geopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geo-political) influence in regional affairs.[^cite_note-amlsnconference-20] Abuja is also a conference centre and hosts various meetings annually, such as the 2003 [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) Heads of Government meeting and the 2014 World Economic Forum (Africa) meetings.[^cite_note-amlsnconference-20][^cite_note-21] Abuja joined the [UNESCO](21786641.md) Global Network of Learning Cities in 2016.[^cite_note-UNESCO_UIL-GNLC-22]

## History



[National Assembly](1898990.md) Building with Mace, Abuja, Nigeria



Millennium Park

Abuja was in the early 20th century the name of the nearby town now called [Suleja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleja).[^cite_note-fcda-about-23]

The indigenous inhabitants of Abuja are the Abawa (Ganagana), [Basa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basa_languages), [Gwandara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwandara), [Gbagyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gbagyi) (Gwari) having the majority population in the region. Other groups in the area include Egburra, [Nupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nupe_people) and [Koro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koro_people?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-24] The [Gwandara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwandara) speaking people in Federal Capital Territory are mostly found in AMAC and [Bwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwari) Area Council.[^cite_note-25] Some of their villages/settlements are Wuse, Asokoro now relocated to Sabon Wuse, Aso in Niger and other neighbouring states. Others are [Karshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karshi_Abuja), [Kurudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurudu,_Abuja?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nyanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyanya_\(Abuja\)), [Idu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idu,_Abuja), [Gwagwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwagwa?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Jiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiwa,_Abuja?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sheretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheretti?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Karmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmo,_Abuja?action=edit\&redlink=1), Takunshara, [Burum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burum,_Abuja?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Dutse Alhaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutse_Alhaji?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Zuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuba,_Nigeria), [Kuje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuje), [Kwoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwoi), [Karonmagaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karonmagaji?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Ija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ija?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kanwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanwu?action=edit\&redlink=1), [and Sherre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherre?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-26]

In light of the ethnic and religious [divisions of Nigeria](21386.md), plans had been devised since Nigeria's independence to have its capital in a place deemed neutral to all major ethnic parties, and also near all the regions of Nigeria. The location was eventually designated in the centre of the country in the early 1970s as it signified neutrality and national unity.[^cite_note-27]

Another impetus for Abuja came because of [Lagos](85232.md)' population boom that made that city overcrowded and conditions squalid.[^cite_note-28] As Lagos was already undergoing rapid economic development, the Nigerian government felt the need to expand the economy towards the inner part of the country, and hence decided to move its capital to Abuja.[^cite_note-29] The logic used was similar to the way Brazil planned its capital, [Brasília](4752.md).

The Federal Military Government of Nigeria promulgated Decree No. 6 on 4 February 1976, which initiated the removal of the Federal Capital from Lagos to Abuja.[^cite_note-Moore_1984_Political_History-30][^cite_note-naij-why-31] The initial work for Abuja's planning and implementation was carried out by the Military Government of Generals [Murtala Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Mohammed) and [Olusegun Obasanjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olusegun_Obasanjo). However, the foundation of Abuja was under the Administration of Shehu Shagari in 1979.[^cite_note-Moore_1984_Political_History-30] Construction started in 1979 but, due to economic and political instability, the initial stages of the city were not complete until the late 1980s.[^cite_note-32]

The move of Nigeria's Capital to Abuja was controversial, and the biggest opposition to it was led by [Obafemi Awolowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obafemi_Awolowo).[^cite_note-Moore_1984_Political_History-30] Awolowo, as a politician and a representative of the Yoruba people, defended their claims against the move of the Capital from Lagos. During the hotly-contested campaign for the presidency, he vowed to hire the [Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Company) to convert the new site (Abuja) into an amusement park if he was elected.[^cite_note-Moore_1984_Political_History-30] However, after his election as president, Alhaji [Shehu Shagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehu_Shagari) laid the first foundation for Abuja's infrastructure and pushed for its early completion. His first journey outside of Lagos after his election was to Abuja, where he visited contractors and workers on site to urge a speedy completion of the project. On his return, he confided in Alhaji [Abubakar Koko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Koko) his disappointment with the slow progress of work.[^cite_note-Moore_1984_Political_History-30] Shehu Shagari rescheduled the planned relocation to Abuja from 1986 to 1992 which later proved difficult.[^cite_note-Moore_1984_Political_History-30]

Abuja is a [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community) built mainly in the 1980s based on a [master plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_planning) by International Planning Associates (IPA),[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-amlsnconference-20][^cite_note-34] a consortium of three [Wallace, Roberts, McHarg & Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace,_Roberts,_McHarg_&_Todd?action=edit\&redlink=1) (WRMT – a group of architects) as the lead, Archi systems International (a subsidiary of the Howard Hughes Corporation), and Planning Research Corporation. The International Planning Associates (IPA) was commissioned in June 1977, by the Federal Capital Development Authority (FCDA)[^cite_note-35] to produce the Abuja Master Plan and its regional grid.[^cite_note-36] According to the terms of reference, the master planning process was to include a review of relevant data, selection of a capital city site, preparation of regional and city plans and the accompanying design and development standards manual.[^cite_note-37] After winning the bid, and completing the project, the firms were disbanded.

The final report was submitted to the pioneer Executive Secretary of FCDA, Alhaji [Abubakar Koko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Koko), on 15 February 1979. In the preface to the master plan, the following declaration was made by IPA:

> "The master plan for Abuja the new Capital City of Nigeria represents the culmination of 18 months' work by the Federal Capital Development Authority (FCDA) board, and several advisory panel consultants. Without the unstinting efforts of these experts, the momentum now exhibited at the beginning of the actual implementation of the new capital could not have been achieved. The plan itself represents a milestone in the process of building the new capital city. It is a necessary element in the monumental effort about to be undertaken by the Nigerian people."

The master plan for Abuja defined the general structure and major design elements of the city that are now visible.&#x20;

More detailed design of the central areas of the capital, particularly its monumental core and the Central Business [District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District), was accomplished by Japanese architect [Kenzo Tange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenzo_Tange)[^cite_note-Tange-38][^cite_note-bbc-19][^cite_note-39] with his team of city planners at Kenzo Tange and Urtec company.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbba2012\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_March_2026\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (March_2026)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-6]

Most countries relocated their [embassies](8970.md) to Abuja, and many maintain their former embassies as [consulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consul_\(representative\)) in [Lagos](85232.md), the commercial capital of Nigeria.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Abuja is the headquarters of the [Economic Community of West African States](36848.md) (ECOWAS) and the regional headquarters of [OPEC](166346.md). Abuja and the FCT have experienced huge population growth; it has been reported that some areas around Abuja have been growing at 20% to 30% per year.[^cite_note-WBC-42] Squatter settlements and towns have spread rapidly in and outside the city limits.[^cite_note-bbc-19][^cite_note-fig-43] Tens of thousands of people have been [evicted](538394.md) since former FCT minister [Nasir Ahmad el-Rufai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_Ahmad_el-Rufai) started a demolition campaign in 2003.[^cite_note-NYT-44][^cite_note-45]

## Administration

The [Abuja Municipal Area Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuja_Municipal_Area_Council) (AMAC) is the [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_Nigeria) authority responsible for administering the city of Abuja. It operates under the oversight of the [Federal Capital Territory Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Capital_Territory_Administration) (FCTA), which governs the entire [Federal Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Capital_Territory_\(Nigeria\)) (FCT). The AMAC's chairman is responsible for leading the council's activities and representing the interests of the municipality's residents.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The Minister of the FCT, [Nyesom Wike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyesom_Wike),[^cite_note-49] heads the FCTA while AMAC's chairman is Christopher Zaka Maikalangu.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

For ease and co-ordination of developmental efforts, the city was divided into 'Phases' by its planners, with the city's development taking a concentric form with Phase 1, which consists of the city's inner districts-Central Area, [Maitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitama), [Asokoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asokoro), [Wuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuse), Wuse II, [Garki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garki,_Abuja), [Garki II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garki_II?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Guzape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guzape?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Guzape II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guzape_II?action=edit\&redlink=1)-at its core spreading out from the foot of Aso Rock, while Phase 5, consisting of the newly created Kyami District covering the vicinity of the [Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nnamdi_Azikiwe_International_Airport)[^cite_note-52] and the permanent campus of the [University of Abuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Abuja), over 40 kilometres west of Aso Rock. Each Phase is separated from the other by an expressway (some still under construction); for example, Phases 1 and 2 are separated from each other by the [Nnamdi Azikiwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nnamdi_Azikiwe) expressway, while the entirety of the city proper (Phases 1–5) is enclosed by the Murtala Muhammed (Outer Northern-ONEX and Outer Southern-OSEX) expressways as well as the Federal A2 highway which traverses the Federal Capital Territory on its way to Kaduna (north-bound) and Lokoja (south-bound). Thus, there is an integration of the city's road network with the Federal highway network, providing access to the immediate outlying countryside and the surrounding states of the country i.e. Niger State to the west, Kaduna State to the north, Nasarawa State to the east and Kogi State to the south.[^cite_note-53]

Abuja is organised into phases, which are further divided into districts.[^cite_note-54]

#### Phase 1

##### Asokoro District

Main article: [Asokoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asokoro)

[Asokoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asokoro), the [doyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doyen) of the districts, houses all of the state's lodges/guesthouses. The [ECOWAS](36848.md)[^cite_note-55] secretariat is a focal point of interest. Asokoro is to the east of the Garki District and south of the Central District. It is one of the most exclusive districts of Abuja and houses virtually all of the federal cabinet ministers as well as most of the diplomatic community in the city; in addition, the Presidential Palace (commonly referred to as Aso Rock) is in Asokoro District. By virtue of this fact, [Asokoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asokoro) is the most secure area of the city.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

##### Central Business District

Abuja's [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District,_Abuja), also called Central Area, is a strip of land stretching from [Aso Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aso_Rock) in the east to the National Stadium and the Old City gate in the West. It is like the city's spinal cord, dividing it into the northern sector with Maitama and Wuse, and the southern sector with Garki and Asokoro. While each district has its own demarcated commercial and residential sectors, the Central District is the city's principal Business Zone, where practically all parastatals and [multinational corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporations) have their offices. An attractive area in the Central District is the Three Arms Zone, so-called because it houses the administrative offices of the executive, legislative, and judicial arms of the federal government. A few of the other sites worth seeing in the area are the federal secretariats alongside [Shehu Shagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehu_Shagari) Way, Aso Hill, the Abuja Plant Nursery, [Eagle Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Square,_Abuja) (which has important historic significance, as it was in this grounds that the present democratic dispensation had its origin on 29 May 1999) and in which all subsequent Presidential Inauguration ceremonies have taken place. The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier is situated across Shehu Shagari Way, facing Eagle Square. This section is usually closed to traffic during the annual Armed Forces Remembrance Day ceremonies, that is observed on 15 January. The [National Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuja_National_Mosque) and [National Christian Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Christian_Centre) are opposite each other on either side of Independence Avenue.[^cite_note-58] A well-known government office is the Ministry of Defense, colloquially nicknamed "Ship House".[^cite_note-59] Also located here is the yet-to-be-completed National Square, Millennium Tower and [Nigeria](21383.md) Cultural Centre multi-functional complex.

##### Garki District

The [Garki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garki_District,_Abuja) is the area in the southwest corner of the city, having the Central District to the north and the Asokoro District to the east. The district is subdivided into units called "areas". Garki uses a distinctive naming convention of "area" to refer to parts of Garki. These are designated as Areas 1 to 11. Garki II is used to differentiate the area from Garki Area 2. Visitors may find this system confusing.[^cite_note-60]

Garki is presently the principal business and administrative district of Abuja. Numerous buildings of interest are in this area. Some of them include the General Post Office, Abuja International Conference Centre along the busy Herbert Macaulay Way, Nicon Luxury Hotel (formally known as Abuja Sofitel Hotel and Le Meridian), Agura Hotel and Old Federal Secretariat Complex Buildings (Area 1).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] A new five-star hotel, Hawthorn Suites Abuja, is in Garki.

Area 2 is mainly used for residential purposes, although a zoological garden, as well as a small shopping centre, are to be found here as well. Several banks and other commercial offices are located along Moshood Abiola Way in Area 7. The headquarters of the [Nigerian Armed Forces](21391.md) – Army, Airforce and Navy – is located on [Muhammadu Buhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadu_Buhari) Way in the Garki District.

The tallest building in this district is the Radio House located in the Area 11 sector, which houses the Federal Ministry of Information and Communications, the [Federal Radio Corporation of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Radio_Corporation_of_Nigeria) (FRCN) and [Voice of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_Nigeria) (VON). The [Nigerian Television Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Television_Authority) (NTA) stations and corporate headquarters are also in Garki. The Federal Capital Development Authority (FCDA) which oversees and runs the Administration of the Federal Capital Territory has its offices in Garki.

The Office of the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory is in Area 11.[^cite_note-63] This is the location of the Federal Capital Development Authority and other administrative buildings. A popular sub-neighbourhood here is found in the vicinity of Gimbiya Street because it has the unique characteristic of being a purely administrative zone on weekdays while transforming purely into an entertainment zone on weeknights and weekends.[^cite_note-64]



Jabi lake mall

Other places of note include the [Cyprian Ekwensi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian_Ekwensi) Arts & Culture Centre and The Nigerian Police Mobile Force CID (Criminal Investigation Department) headquarters in Area 10. The Abuja Municipal Area Council, which is the local government administration has its headquarters in Area 10. The new [United States Embassy](6654039.md) is in the Diplomatic Zone which adjoins Garki.

##### Maitama District

Main article: [Maitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitama)



Millennium Park water fountain at night

Maitama District is to the north of the city, with the [Wuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuse) and Central Districts lying to its southwest and southeast, respectively. This area is home to the top-bracket sections of society and business and has the reputation of being very exclusive and very expensive. Interesting buildings include the Transcorp Hilton Abuja,[^cite_note-65] [Nigerian Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Communications_Commission) Headquarters (NCC), [National Universities Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Universities_Commission) (NUC), Soil Conservation Complex, and [Independent National Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_National_Electoral_Commission_\(Nigeria\)) (INEC).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The [British High Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_High_Commission) is located along [Aguiyi Ironsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguiyi_Ironsi) Way, in Maitama.[^cite_note-68]

Also, the Maitama District Hospital is another notable building in Maitama. Maitama District is home to many of the European and Asian embassies.

###### Maitama II

Maitama II District Cadastral Zone A10 is a new district created by the FCT administration of [Muhammed Bello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammed_Bello) in 2018. Maitama 2 as it is unofficially called was created from Mpape Hills, a suburb bordering the Bwari and Maitama Districts of the FCT. Maitama II has a high net worth of individuals especially legislators as allottees, which makes it a destination for real estate investors who desire to secure the future today.

It is not very clear what the government plans for the district as not much has been said by government agents, but cadastral zoning A indicates the government has good plans for Maitama 2 to correct some errors in Maitama District.



[Millennium Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Park_\(Abuja\)) in Maitama District

It was learned that plots of land were revoked from allottees of Mpape district to create the new Maitama II District, although a compensation plan is underway to relocate the revoked allottees to another district the government intends to create. Fortunately, some developers of the Mpape district who have building plan approval and have started building maintain their plots and allocations and are covered in the Maitama II District layout. It is not clear though if a fresh allocation bearing Maitama II District has been issued to this set of allottees.

Notable landmarks of the Maitama 2 district are Jubilation Avenue, Mount Pleasant Haven and Hilltop Estate.

The district infrastructure is in top gear as the Federal Government had in the 2018 and 2019 budgets allocated about N1.3b for the engineering design of the district. Villages around the district are living in fear of demolition at any time with the information reaching them that government will commence the infrastructure project of Maitama II District.[^cite_note-69]

##### Wuse District

Wuse District is the northwestern part of the city, with the Maitama District to its north and the Central District to its south. The District is numbered Zones 1–6. The Wuse Market is Abuja's principal market. The second most important post office in the city is here. This district houses the Sheraton Hotel and Towers (Zone 4), Grand Ibro International Hotel, the [Federal Road Safety Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Road_Safety_Corps_\(Nigeria\)) Headquarters (Zone 3), [Nigerian Customs Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_Customs_Service) Headquarters, Federal Civil Service Commission (Zone 3), [National Agency for Food and Drugs Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Agency_for_Food_and_Drug_Administration_and_Control) (NAFDAC) (Zone 7), Wuse General Hospital, and the [Nigerian Tourism Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Tourism_Development_Corporation). Just as Garki District has Garki II, Wuse has Wuse II. This is distinct from Wuse Zone 2. Like other inner Abuja districts such as Asokoro or Garki, there are very few large housing estates. Instead, office buildings, mansions and apartment blocks are prominent features of the area.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

### Climate

Abuja under [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) features a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Aw*). The FCT experiences three weather conditions annually. This includes a warm, humid [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_season) and a blistering [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season).[^cite_note-80] In between the two, there is a brief interlude of [harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan) occasioned by the northeast trade wind, with the main feature of [dust haze](998156.md) and cloudless skies.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

The [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) begins from April and ends in October,[^cite_note-84] when daytime temperatures reach 28 °C (82.4 °F) to 30 °C (86.0 °F) and nighttime lows hover around 22 °C (71.6 °F) to 23 °C (73.4 °F).[^cite_note-\:0-85] In the dry season, daytime temperatures can soar as high as 40 °C (104.0 °F) and overnight temperatures can dip to 20 °C (68.0 °F). Even the coolest nights can be followed by daytime temperatures well above 30 °C (86.0 °F).[^cite_note-\:0-85] The medium altitude and undulating terrain of the FCT act as a moderating influence on the weather of the territory. The city's inland location causes the [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) to be much larger than coastal cities with similar climates such as [Lagos](85232.md).

Rainfall in the FCT reflects the territory's location on the windward side of the [Jos Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos_Plateau) and the zone of rising air masses with the city receiving frequent rainfall during the rainy season from April to October every year.[^cite_note-86]

The effects of [climate change](5042951.md) have been observed in Abuja, according to a study conducted by the Nigerian Meteorological Agency and Atmospheric Science Group of [Imo State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imo_State_University).[^cite_note-\:3-87] Between 1993 and 2013, the average maximum temperature trended downward, but the average minimum and mean temperatures trended upwards.[^cite_note-\:3-87] There was a downward trend for rainfall in Abuja, based on data collected between 1986 and 2016.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The drought occurrence probability for Abuja increased by 15.4% between 1975 and 2014.[^cite_note-90]

### Vegetation

The FCT falls within the [Guinean forest-savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest-savanna_mosaic) zone of the West African sub-region.[^cite_note-93] Patches of [rain forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_forest), however, occur in the Gwagwa plains, especially in the rugged terrain to the southeastern parts of the territory, where a landscape of gullies and rough terrain is found. ## Demographics

At the 2006 census, the city of Abuja had a population of 776,298,[^cite_note-plac-11] making it than the [eighth most populous city in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_cities_by_population). United Nations figures showed that Abuja grew by 139.7% between 2000 and 2010, making it the fastest-growing city in the world.[^cite_note-euromonitor2010-13] As of 2015, the city is experiencing an annual growth of at least 35%, retaining its position as the fastest-growing city on the African continent and one of the fastest-growing in the world.[^cite_note-abujafacts5-14]

### Conurbation

Abuja has witnessed a huge influx of people into the city; the growth has led to the emergence of satellite towns, such as [Karu Urban Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karu_Urban_Area), [Suleja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleja), Gwagwalada, Lugbe, [Kuje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuje) and smaller settlements towards which the planned city is sprawling. The urban agglomeration centred upon Abuja had a population estimated at 3,770,000 in 2022.[^cite_note-Demographia-2] The city has a large and growing immigrant community consisting mainly of nationals from the ECOWAS sub-region. The city has been undergoing a rapid pace of physical development over the last fifteen years.

## Economy and infrastructure

### Real estate

Real estate is a major driver of the Abuja economy - with the rise of numerous real estate companies in Nigeria, such as Hall7 Projects,[^cite_note-94] Cosgrove, Ecogroverealty amongst many others. This correlates with the considerable growth and investment the city has seen as it has developed. The sources of this investment have been both foreign and local. The real estate sector continues to have a positive impact on the city, as it is a major avenue for employment.[^cite_note-95]

### Annual trade fair

The [Abuja International Trade Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuja_International_Trade_Fair?action=edit\&redlink=1) (AITF) has been held annually since 2005, under the auspices of the Abuja Chamber of Commerce and Industry (ACCI).[^cite_note-96] ACCI has also sought to link businesses to enhance trade with [East Africa](260987.md).[^cite_note-97]

### Postal system

Abuja is served by the [Nigerian Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Postal_Service) (NIPOST) which maintains postal codes, street names and zones.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

[Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nnamdi_Azikiwe_International_Airport) is the main airport serving Abuja and the surrounding capital region.[^cite_note-i082-100] It was named after Nigeria's first president, [Nnamdi Azikiwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nnamdi_Azikiwe). #### Highways

Abuja is also linked to [Nasarawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasarawa_State), [Plateau](1723458.md), [Benue](1556364.md) and Northeast Nigeria by the A234 Federal Highway, which starts from the city as the [Goodluck Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Jonathan) expressway, some portions of which are still under construction.
