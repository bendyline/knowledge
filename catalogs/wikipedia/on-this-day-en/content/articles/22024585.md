# Mining in Venezuela

&#x20;[Venezuela](32374.md) is a major producer and exporter of minerals, notably [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [gold](12240.md), [iron ore](145352.md), and [oil](4207510.md), and the state controls most of the country's vast mineral reserves.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In 2003 estimated reserves of bauxite totaled 5.2 million tons.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

The third largest producer of coal in Latin America, after [Colombia](5222.md) and [Brazil](3383.md), Venezuela produced 5.8 million short tons (1 short ton=2,000 pounds) in 2002, as compared with 9.3 million short tons in 2000, and exported most of it to other countries in the region, the eastern United States, and Europe.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Known reserves for coal total 10.2 billion tons, of which approximately 528 million short tons are recoverable [bituminous coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituminous_coal).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

The main coalfields are located in the western [Zulia State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulia_State), on the border with Colombia.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Other known reserves include natural [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen) (42 billion tons).[^cite_note-\:0-1] Exploitable gold reserves, located mostly in the southeast, total an estimated 10,000 tons.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In 2003 production totaled 20 million grams (or 20 tons), including 6 million grams attributed to unofficial mining activities, marking a sharp increase from 1999, when only 5.9 million grams were produced.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In 2003 Venezuela's estimated reserves of iron ore totaled 14.6 million tons.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Proven reserves total 4.1 billion tons, of which 1.7 billion tons are high-grade.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Production has been increasing and totaled a record 19.2 million tons in 2003, two-thirds of which were exported.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Iron-ore reserves are concentrated in the southeast.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Oil

With 77,800,000,000 barrels (1.237×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of proven [oil](4207510.md) reserves in 2004, Venezuela has the largest proven oil reserves in South America and the sixth largest in the world—more than [Canada](5042916.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), and the [United States](3434750.md) combined.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Venezuela's 2002 production of 2,800,000 barrels per day (450,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) (bbl/d) of crude was a drop of 8.3 percent over 2001 and the country's lowest production figure since 1994.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In 2003, a year in which production was halted for a couple of months by a [general strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_general_strike_of_2002%E2%80%932003) and further disrupted by the firing of nearly half of the state oil company's work force, Venezuela's total oil production was an estimated 2,600,000 bbl/d (410,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-\:0-1] In 2004 oil production totaled about 3,000,000 bbl/d (480,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d), according to the government's estimate.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Prior to President Chávez's December 1998 election, Venezuela regularly exceeded its [Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries) (OPEC)-agreed oil production targets.[^cite_note-\:0-1] [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez) maintained a policy of strict adherence to OPEC quotas and played a leading role in shifting OPEC from a volume-oriented strategy to one of controlling prices.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Gold

Venezuela has experienced an oil dependency for an extended part of their history, but in the recent years, their focus has shifted over to gold.[^cite_note-2] As a part of the 2016 settlement of a dispute over withdrawal of a gold concession to [Gold Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Reserve_\(company\)), a Canadian mining company, the government entered into a joint venture to exploit the [Brisas mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisas_mine) and Las Cristinas goldmines.[^cite_note-nsnbc022616-3]
