The **United States District Court for the District of Maryland** (in [case citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_citation), **D. Md.**) is the [federal district court](70235.md) whose jurisdiction is the state of [Maryland](18858.md). Appeals from the District of Maryland are taken to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Fourth_Circuit) (except for [patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent) claims and claims against the U.S. government under the [Tucker Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Act), which are appealed to the [Federal Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Federal_Circuit)).

Notable past judges of this district include [William Paca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Paca), a signer of the [United States Declaration of Independence](31874.md). The [United States Attorney for the District of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_for_the_District_of_Maryland) represents the United States in civil and criminal litigation in the court. As of June 25, 2025, the U.S. attorney is [Kelly O. Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_O._Hayes).[^cite_note-1]

## Organization of the court



View of U.S. District Court for Maryland, Northern Division, in Baltimore, Maryland.



View of U.S. District Court for Maryland, Southern Division, in Greenbelt, Maryland.

Under [28 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_28_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/100), Maryland consists of a single federal judicial district with two statutory divisions.&#x20;

### Northern Division

The Northern Division includes [Allegany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegany_County,_Maryland), [Anne Arundel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Arundel_County,_Maryland), [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_County,_Maryland), [Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_County,_Maryland), [Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroll_County,_Maryland), [Cecil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_County,_Maryland), [Dorchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester_County,_Maryland), [Frederick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_County,_Maryland), [Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_County,_Maryland), [Harford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harford_County,_Maryland), [Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_County,_Maryland), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_County,_Maryland), [Queen Anne's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne's_County,_Maryland), [Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_County,_Maryland), [Talbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talbot_County,_Maryland), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Maryland), [Wicomico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicomico_County,_Maryland), [Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_County,_Maryland) counties and the City of [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), is located in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), while the statute also provides for the court to sit in [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland,_Maryland) and [Denton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denton,_Maryland). The Court also maintains a location in [Salisbury, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_Maryland) that is only staffed on days when court is in session.[^cite_note-2]

### Southern Division

The Southern Division includes [Calvert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvert_County,_Maryland), [Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_County,_Maryland), [Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Maryland), [Prince George's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George's_County,_Maryland), and [St. Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_County,_Maryland) counties and sits in [Greenbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenbelt,_Maryland).

## Chief judges

Chief judges have administrative responsibilities with respect to their district court. Unlike the Supreme Court, where one justice is specifically nominated to be chief, the office of chief judge rotates among the district court judges. To be chief, a judge must have been in active service on the court for at least one year, be under the age of 65, and have not previously served as chief judge.&#x20;

A vacancy is filled by the judge highest in seniority among the group of qualified judges. The chief judge serves for a term of seven years, or until age 70, whichever occurs first. The age restrictions are waived if no members of the court would otherwise be qualified for the position.

When the office was created in 1948, the chief judge was the longest-serving judge who had not elected to retire, on what has since 1958 been known as [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status), or declined to serve as chief judge. After August 6, 1959, judges could not become or remain chief after turning 70 years old. The current rules have been in operation since October 1, 1982.
