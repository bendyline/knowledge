The **Royal Challengers Bengaluru**, also known as **RCB**, formerly the **Royal Challengers Bangalore**, are a professional [Twenty20 cricket](770690.md) team based in [Bengaluru](44275267.md), Karnataka, that competes in the [Indian Premier League](13260340.md) (IPL).

They won their first title in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League) and their second title in [2026](79331486.md).[^cite_note-\:1-4] The team finished as the runners-up on three occasions in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Indian_Premier_League), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_Premier_League), and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Indian_Premier_League), and have also qualified for the playoffs in eleven of the nineteen seasons.

As of 2026, the team is captained by [Rajat Patidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajat_Patidar) and coached by [Andy Flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Flower). The franchise has competed in the [Champions League Twenty20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champions_League_Twenty20), finishing as runners-up in the [2011 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Champions_League_Twenty20). As of 2024, RCB's [brand value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_value) was estimated at \$117 million, making it one of the most valuable IPL brands.[^cite_note-5]

Established in 2008, the franchise was purchased by [United Spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Spirits) and later acquired by a consortium comprising the [Aditya Birla Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aditya_Birla_Group), [The Times Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_Group) and [Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_Inc.) in 2026.[^cite_note-6] The team was acquired for a record \$1.78 billion, representing the highest valuation for a franchise in IPL history.

## History

### 2008–2010: Initial seasons

Main articles: [Royal Challengers Bangalore in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2008), [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2009), and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2010)

In September 2007, the [Board of Control for Cricket in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Control_for_Cricket_in_India) announced the establishment of the [Indian Premier League](13260340.md) (IPL) a [Twenty20](770690.md) competition set to begin in 2008.[^cite_note-7] On 24 January 2008, an auction was held in Mumbai for the league's teams, which represented eight different cities in India, including Bengaluru. The Bangalore franchise was acquired by [Vijay Mallya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Mallya) for US\$111.6 million, making it the second-highest bid, slightly less than [Reliance Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Industries)' US\$111.9 million bid for the [Mumbai Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Indians).[^cite_note-8]



[Rahul Dravid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahul_Dravid) was the team's icon player in 2008.

Ahead of the [2008 player auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2008_Indian_Premier_League_auctions_and_personnel_signings), the IPL designated [Rahul Dravid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahul_Dravid) as the [icon player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon_player#Indian_Premier_League) for the Bangalore franchise. This designation ensured that Dravid would be paid 15% more than the highest bid player at the auction.[^cite_note-9] The franchise acquired several prominent Indian and international players, including [Jacques Kallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Kallis), [Anil Kumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Kumble), [Zaheer Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaheer_Khan), [Mark Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Boucher), [Dale Steyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Steyn), and [Cameron White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_White).[^cite_note-10] In the second round of the auction, they also signed [Ross Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Taylor), [Misbah-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misbah-ul-Haq), and India [under-19 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Under-19_Cricket_World_Cup)-winning captain [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli).[^cite_note-11] The franchise named Dravid as the captain of the team and appointed [Venkatesh Prasad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venkatesh_Prasad) as the head coach.[^cite_note-12] The team struggled in the [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Indian_Premier_League), winning only four out of their 14 matches and finishing seventh in the eight-team table.[^cite_note-13] Dravid was the sole player to score more than 300 runs in the tournament, and the team even had to bench their most expensive foreign player, Kallis, for several matches due to his poor form.[^cite_note-mruns-14][^cite_note-benc-15] Midway through the season, the string of failures led to the sacking of CEO [Charu Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charu_Sharma), who was replaced by [Brijesh Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brijesh_Patel). Mallya publicly criticised Dravid and Sharma for their selection of players at the auction, stating that his "biggest mistake was to abstain from the selection of the team."[^cite_note-sack-16] Eventually, the chief cricketing officer of the franchise, [Martin Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Crowe), resigned and Prasad was replaced by [Ray Jennings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Jennings) as the head coach.[^cite_note-17]

At the [2009 player auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2009_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes), the franchise signed [Kevin Pietersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Pietersen) for a record US\$1.55 million, making him the most expensive player alongside [Andrew Flintoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Flintoff) ([Chennai Super Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Super_Kings)).[^cite_note-18] They also traded [Zaheer Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaheer_Khan) for [Robin Uthappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Uthappa) with the [Mumbai Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Indians) and brought in local batsman [Manish Pandey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manish_Pandey).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Due to [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Indian_general_election) in India, the tournament was held in [South Africa](17416221.md).[^cite_note-21] Pietersen was named captain, but after a string of initial losses, Anil Kumble took over the captaincy when Pietersen left for national duty.[^cite_note-22] RCB's performance improved under Kumble, winning six of their last eight matches to finish third in the league table.[^cite_note-23] They defeated Chennai Super Kings in the semi-final but fell short by six runs in the final against [Deccan Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Chargers).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]



[Ross Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Taylor) was one of the top performers for RCB in 2009 and 2010.

In [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indian_Premier_League), the Royal Challengers, under [Kumble's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Kumble) captaincy, secured seven wins from 14 matches, accumulating 14 points. Tied with three other teams for a playoff spot, their superior net run rate qualified them for the semi-finals.[^cite_note-26] In the semi-final, they were defeated by the table-toppers, the Mumbai Indians, by 35 runs.[^cite_note-27] However, the Royal Challengers secured a convincing nine-wicket win over the defending champions, the Deccan Chargers, in the third-place playoff, thus qualifying for the [2010 Champions League Twenty20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Champions_League_Twenty20).[^cite_note-28] Kumble retired at the conclusion of the Champions League, having led the team to the semi-finals of both the IPL and the CLT20 that year.[^cite_note-29]

### 2011–2012: IPL and CLT20 finals

Main articles: [Royal Challengers Bangalore in 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2011) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2012)

On 8 January 2011, the IPL Governing Council held the [auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2011_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes) for the fourth season of the league. Franchises had the option of retaining a maximum of four players for a sum of US\$4.5 million.[^cite_note-30] However, RCB retained only Virat Kohli, leaving their other players to re-enter the auction pool. During the auction, Bangalore made significant acquisitions, including [Tillakaratne Dilshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillakaratne_Dilshan), [Zaheer Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaheer_Khan), [AB de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_de_Villiers), [Daniel Vettori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Vettori), [Saurabh Tiwary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurabh_Tiwary), [Dirk Nannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Nannes), and [Cheteshwar Pujara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheteshwar_Pujara).[^cite_note-31] Daniel Vettori was named captain for the season.[^cite_note-32] RCB began their campaign with a win over the newly formed [Kochi Tuskers Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi_Tuskers_Kerala).[^cite_note-33] However, they faced three consecutive defeats against [Mumbai Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Indians), [Deccan Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Chargers), and [Chennai Super Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Super_Kings).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Dirk Nannes was replaced by [Chris Gayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Gayle) because of an injury.[^cite_note-37] Gayle's inclusion led RCB to a seven-match winning streak.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In their final league match, RCB defeated defending champions Chennai Super Kings, securing the top position in the points table.[^cite_note-40] They lost the final, again facing the Super Kings, by 58 runs. Chris Gayle was named Man of the Tournament.[^cite_note-41] Royal Challengers Bangalore reached the [2011 Champions League Twenty20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Champions_League_Twenty20) semi-finals after finishing runner-up in the IPL. They won their final group match against [Southern Redbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Redbacks) on the last ball and beat [New South Wales Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales_cricket_team) in the semis.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] They lost the final to Mumbai Indians.[^cite_note-44]

Before the [2012 auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2012_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes), RCB retained Chris Gayle and secured [Andrew McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_McDonald_\(cricketer\)) from [Delhi Daredevils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Daredevils).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] In the auction, RCB acquired [Vinay Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinay_Kumar) and [Muttiah Muralitharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muttiah_Muralitharan).[^cite_note-47] RCB began the [2012 IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Indian_Premier_League) season without Chris Gayle, who was recovering from an injury.[^cite_note-48] AB de Villiers and Muttiah Muralitharan contributed to a winning start against Delhi Daredevils.[^cite_note-49] RCB won eight out of their sixteen games but failed to qualify for the playoffs.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Gayle was the highest run-scorer of the tournament with 733 runs, including a century.[^cite_note-52]

### 2016–2019: Runners-up and bottom-table finishes

Main articles: [Royal Challengers Bangalore in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2016), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2017), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2018), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore_in_2019)

Amid financial scandals involving owner and chairman [Vijay Mallya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Mallya), Amrit Thomas became the chairman of the Royal Challengers.[^cite_note-75] In the 2016 season, RCB introduced different jerseys for home and away matches.[^cite_note-76] During the [player auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2016_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes), they acquired [Shane Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Watson), [Kane Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Richardson), [Stuart Binny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Binny), [Travis Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Head), and [Samuel Badree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Badree), among others.[^cite_note-77] Additionally, [KL Rahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KL_Rahul) and [Parvez Rasool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvez_Rasool) joined RCB from [Sunrisers Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrisers_Hyderabad).[^cite_note-78] RCB won their first match of the season against Sunrisers, with [AB de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_de_Villiers) and Virat Kohli scoring 82 and 75 runs, respectively.[^cite_note-79] However, they struggled in the next six matches, securing only one win against [Rising Pune Supergiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_Pune_Supergiant), which made it challenging to qualify for the playoffs. However, RCB qualified for the playoffs, losing only one match in their next seven games.[^cite_note-80] They set a record by defeating the [Gujarat Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Lions) with a winning margin of 144 runs, the highest in IPL history.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-83] RCB finished the group stage in second place on the points table with 16 points.[^cite_note-84] In the playoffs, they faced Gujarat Lions in Qualifier 1 at their home ground, [M. Chinnaswamy Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Chinnaswamy_Stadium), and won by 4 wickets, advancing to their third final in nine seasons.[^cite_note-85] In the final against Sunrisers Hyderabad in Bangalore, RCB lost by 8 runs, ending the season as runners-up for the third time in IPL history.[^cite_note-86] Yuzvendra Chahal and Shane Watson were the second and third highest wicket-takers of the tournament, while Virat Kohli won the [Orange Cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Cap) for scoring the most runs in the tournament, amassing 973 runs – the highest ever in IPL history.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

In the [2017 player auctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2017_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes), the Royal Challengers acquired [Tymal Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tymal_Mills), [Aniket Choudhary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aniket_Choudhary), [Pawan Negi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawan_Negi), [Praveen Dubey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praveen_Dubey), and [Billy Stanlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Stanlake).[^cite_note-90] [Mitchell Starc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Starc) parted ways with the team to rehabilitate ahead of the [Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_ICC_Champions_Trophy), prompting the management to replace him with Tymal Mills.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] The season for RCB was impacted by injuries, with skipper Virat Kohli and AB de Villiers missing the initial matches. This led to Shane Watson stepping in as the interim captain. Batsmen [KL Rahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KL_Rahul) and Sarfaraz Khan were also ruled out for the season due to prolonged injuries.[^cite_note-93] RCB finished at the bottom of the table, losing ten out of their fourteen matches.[^cite_note-94] In a match against the [Kolkata Knight Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Knight_Riders), RCB was all out for just 49 runs, marking the lowest score ever in an IPL match.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] Virat Kohli was the leading run-scorer for the team with 308 runs in the tournament, while Pawan Negi took the most wickets, claiming 16.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Ahead of the 2018 IPL, RCB retained Virat Kohli, AB de Villiers, and Sarfaraz Khan.[^cite_note-99] During the [auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2018_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes), they acquired notable players such as [Brendon McCullum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendon_McCullum), [Chris Woakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Woakes), [Colin de Grandhomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_de_Grandhomme), [Moeen Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeen_Ali), [Quinton de Kock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinton_de_Kock), [Umesh Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umesh_Yadav), and Yuzvendra Chahal, among others.[^cite_note-100] In January 2018, [Gary Kirsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Kirsten) was appointed as the batting coach, and [Ashish Nehra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashish_Nehra) took on the role of bowling coach.[^cite_note-101] In the group stage, RCB finished sixth in the points table, failing to qualify for the playoffs.[^cite_note-102] Virat Kohli was the team's leading run-scorer, while Umesh Yadav took the most wickets.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

In August 2018, Gary Kirsten was appointed as the head coach of the Royal Challengers Bangalore, replacing Daniel Vettori.[^cite_note-105] During the [2019 IPL auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2019_Indian_Premier_League_personnel_changes), the team acquired [Shivam Dube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivam_Dube), [Shimron Hetmyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimron_Hetmyer), [Heinrich Klaasen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Klaasen), and [Devdutt Padikkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devdutt_Padikkal), along with other key players.[^cite_note-106] During the season, [Dale Steyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Steyn) joined the team, replacing the injured [Nathan Coulter-Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Coulter-Nile).[^cite_note-107] Steyn played only two games before being ruled out of the tournament due to a shoulder injury.[^cite_note-108] In the group stage, RCB played 14 games, winning five, and finished at the bottom of the table.[^cite_note-109] Virat Kohli was once again the highest run-scorer for the team, while [Yuzvendra Chahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzvendra_Chahal) was the highest wicket-taker.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

### 2020–2023: Regain in form

### 2024–present: Maiden and back-to-back IPL Titles

Main articles: [Royal Challengers Bengaluru in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_in_2024) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_season)

In the 2024 season, the Royal Challengers struggled in the first half, losing 7 of their 8 games. But they made a massive turnaround and managed to qualify for the playoffs by winning their next six matches.[^cite_note-142] In the eliminator against [Rajasthan Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan_Royals), RCB lost the match by four wickets.[^cite_note-143] [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli) won the Orange Cap, scoring 741 runs, while [Yash Dayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yash_Dayal) became the team's highest wicket-taker with 15 wickets.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145]

On 3 June 2025, at the [Narendra Modi Stadium](80201506.md) in Ahmedabad, Royal Challengers Bengaluru (RCB) won their first-ever Indian Premier League (IPL) title by defeating [Punjab Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Kings) by six runs. Batting first, RCB scored 190 for 9 in their 20 overs, with [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli) top-scoring with 43 runs from 35 balls. In reply, Punjab Kings managed 184 for 7, with [Shashank Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashank_Singh) remaining not out on 61 runs.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

RCB's bowlers played a crucial role in defending the total. [Krunal Pandya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krunal_Pandya) took 2 wickets for 17 runs, and [Bhuvneshwar Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhuvneshwar_Kumar) claimed 2 for 38, helping RCB control the game in the final overs.[^cite_note-148]

This win ended an 18-year wait for the franchise, which had previously finished as runners-up in 2009, 2011, and 2016. The title was a major milestone for Virat Kohli, who had been with the team since its founding in 2008, and was widely celebrated by fans and cricket enthusiasts across the country.[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:2-149]

RCB entered the [2026 season](79331486.md) as the defending champions and retained majority of their title winning squad from the last year in the mini auction.

They won 9 league matches and topped the league table for the second time in their history. They went onto beat Gujarat Titans in both the Qualifier 1 and [Final](80074642.md) to retain their title. In doing so, they become only the third team to win back-to-back IPL trophies.

## Ownership history

### Inception and Diageo era (2008–2026)

The franchise was established in 2008 when [Vijay Mallya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Mallya), through [United Breweries Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Breweries_Group), purchased the Bangalore-based team for \$111.6 million (approx. ₹446 crore). At the time, it was the second-most expensive franchise in the inaugural [Indian Premier League](13260340.md) season, trailing only the [Mumbai Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Indians).[^cite_note-IPL2008-150]

The first major ownership transition occurred between 2013 and 2016. In July 2013, British multinational [Diageo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diageo) acquired a controlling interest in [United Spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Spirits) Limited (USL).[^cite_note-DiageoUSL-151] Following Mallya's resignation as Chairman of USL in February 2016, Diageo assumed full operational and management control.[^cite_note-MallyaExit-152] In 2023, the franchise expanded by acquiring the [RCB Women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_\(WPL\)) in the [WPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Premier_League_\(cricket\)) for ₹901 crore (\$110 million).[^cite_note-WPLBid-153]

### Consortium acquisition and record valuation (2026–present)

In March 2026, United Spirits finalised the sale of 100% equity in Royal Challengers Sports Private Limited to a four-member consortium for approximately \$1.78 billion (₹16,660 crore).[^cite_note-MSN2026-154]

The transaction was a comprehensive acquisition that included:[^cite_note-WPLBid-153]

- The Royal Challengers Bengaluru men's team.

- The [Royal Challengers Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bengaluru_\(WPL\)) women's team.

- All associated brand intellectual property, the RCB Bar & Cafe hospitality ventures, and the RCB Unbox intellectual properties.

Upon completion, this deal was recognised as the highest valuation ever paid for a cricket franchise, surpassing the previous record of \$1.63 billion set earlier in 2026 by the sale of the [Rajasthan Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan_Royals) to a group led by Kal Somani and Rob Walton.[^cite_note-KhelNow2026-155]

The new ownership consists of:

- **[Aditya Birla Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aditya_Birla_Group)**: Led by chairman Aryaman Vikram Birla, owned by the [Birla family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birla_family).

- **[The Times Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_Group)**: Owned by the [Sahu Jain family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahu_Jain_family), via Bennett, Coleman & Co.

- **Bolt Ventures**: Owned by [David Blitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Blitzer), co-founder of Harris Blitzer Sports & Entertainment.

- **[Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_Inc.)**: An investment firm represented by Viral Patel, CEO of BXPE.

The deal received formal ratification from the [Board of Control for Cricket in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Control_for_Cricket_in_India) (BCCI) on 25 March 2026.[^cite_note-BCCIAproval-156]

## Team identity

### Naming

[Vijay Mallya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Mallya) wanted to associate one of his top-selling liquor brands, either *[McDowell's No.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDowell's_No.1)* or *[Royal Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challenge)* with the team.[^cite_note-mcd-157]

### Logo

The logo initially consisted of the *RC* emblem in yellow on a circular red base. The *RC* crown emblem with the roaring lion placed on the top of the logo was derived from the original *[Royal Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challenge)* logo. No significant changes took place in the design of the logo except for the replacement of colour yellow with gold from 2009. This logo also had a dotted white circle around the *RC* emblem. The team also uses an alternate logo for the *Game for Green* matches where the green plants surround the logo and the text *Game for Green* is placed below the logo. The logo was redesigned in 2016 with the inclusion of black as a secondary colour. The lion in the crest was enlarged and the shield removed.[^cite_note-158]

In 2020, a new logo was unveiled featuring a bigger lion and the crown returning from the previous logo. The RC emblem was omitted for this crest.[^cite_note-159] In 2024, the logo changed again after Royal Challengers Bangalore was renamed to Royal Challengers Bengaluru.

## Rivalries

### Kolkata Knight Riders

The rivalry between [Kolkata Knight Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Knight_Riders) and Royal Challengers Bangalore is one of the oldest in the IPL. The inaugural match of IPL was played between both the teams in which KKR won by 140 runs due to a 158[\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_out) off just 73 balls by [Brendon McCullum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendon_McCullum).[^cite_note-163]

In the [IPL 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Indian_Premier_League), RCB won in both encounters against Knight Riders. During the second time they faced each other, Ross Taylor played a notable innings, scoring 81[\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_out) runs off 33 balls, contributing significantly to RCB's victory by six wickets.[^cite_note-164]

In the [2012 edition of IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Indian_Premier_League), KKR was at the lower half of the IPL table and needed to win the crucial match against RCB. KKR won the toss and chose to bat first. Then KKR Skipper [Gautam Gambhir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautam_Gambhir) led from the front with 93 (51). In reply, RCB lost wickets at regular intervals, as only [Chris Gayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Gayle) managed to put up a fight with a score of 86 (58).[^cite_note-165] The next time they met, Gambhir again was the thorn in RCB's team as he top scored for KKR at a tough pitch, taking KKR to a competitive total of 165. RCB in reply made 129, as [Lakshmipathy Balaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmipathy_Balaji) ripped through their line-up with a 4/18 in 4 overs.[^cite_note-166]

In the 2015 IPL edition, RCB and KKR took part in a match reduced due to rain. It was reduced to a 10-over match. RCB won the toss and elected to field. For KKR, [Andre Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Russell) was the top scorer as he scored 45 off just 17 balls. He took them to a score of 111/4 in just 10 overs. [Mitchell Starc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Starc) took one wicket for 15 runs in 2 overs. In reply, RCB were at 0–48 at 3.4 overs before [Brad Hogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Hogg) got Chris Gayle out. After that, RCB stuttered and started to collapse as they were reduced to 3–81 in 7.2 overs. When [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli) got out to Andre Russell, the match looked to be over for RCB. However, [Mandeep Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandeep_Singh) scored 45 off just 18 balls, hitting 3 sixes and 4 fours.[^cite_note-\:0-167]

In the 2017 IPL edition, Kolkata Knight Riders and Royal Challengers Bangalore again faced each other twice. In the first match between them, RCB got KKR out for a score of 131 after KKR had made a strong start of 0–48 in 3.3 overs. However, KKR dismissed RCB for 49, the lowest team score in the history of IPL. [Nathan Coulter Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Coulter-Nile), [Colin de Grandhomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_de_Grandhomme) and [Chris Woakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Woakes) got three wickets each.[^cite_note-\:0-167] In the next match, [Sunil Narine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Narine) scored what was then the fastest fifty in IPL (50 off 15 balls – which is now the second-fastest). KKR made [the highest score made in powerplay in any IPL match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_Premier_League_records_and_statistics), and easily chased down the target offered by RCB.[^cite_note-\:0-167]

The [2019 IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_Premier_League) saw Virat Kohli scoring 84 off 49 and [AB de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_de_Villiers) scoring 63 off 32, taking RCB to a total of 205/3. KKR had a strong start, scoring 28/0 in 1.3 overs before losing wickets at regular intervals and having their run rate reduced. They were 139/4 in 15.5 overs. However, [Dinesh Karthik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinesh_Karthik) and Andre Russell brought back the chase under control. Karthik got out after scoring 19 off 15, leaving KKR at 153/5 in 17 overs. Andre Russell, however, took KKR over the line as he scored 48 off 13, hitting [Mohammed Siraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Siraj) for 23 runs in one over.[^cite_note-\:0-167]

In the next match, RCB struck back as Virat Kohli made his 5th IPL century, scoring 100 runs in 58 balls only. [Moeen Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeen_Ali) scored 66 runs in only 28 balls as RCB scored 213 runs. For KKR, [Nitish Rana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitish_Rana) scored 85 off 46 and Andre Russell scored 65 off 25, taking the game down to the wire. However, RCB won the match by 10 runs, with Virat Kohli being Man of the Match.[^cite_note-168]

### Chennai Super Kings

RCB's rivalry with [Chennai Super Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Super_Kings) is dubbed the "Southern Derby."[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] The Super Kings beat the Royal Challengers in the final of the [2011 IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_Premier_League), the only meeting between the two teams at an IPL final.[^cite_note-Final-171]

CSK held an upper hand over RCB in the subsequent seasons from 2012 to 2023, winning 14 out of the 20 encounters, including in 2015, when CSK defeated RCB thrice in a single season.[^cite_note-172]

The [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_Premier_League) and [2025 seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League) saw the tide turn in RCB's favour. In 2024, RCB knocked CSK out of the IPL by winning their last game of the season, which was also considered a virtual quarter final as the winner of the game would have made it to the qualifiers. RCB required a hefty win to surpass CSK's [net run rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_run_rate) and qualify for the playoffs in the 4th spot. It came down to wire at the Chinnaswamy where RCB were required to defend 35 runs in the last over to win, but more importantly not give away more than 16 runs to ensure qualification. [Yash Dayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yash_Dayal)'s last over saw RCB give away just 7 runs to win the match and qualify.[^cite_note-173]

In the 2025, for the first time, saw RCB win all of their games against CSK in a season while also win at [Chepauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._A._Chidambaram_Stadium), CSK's home, after 17 years. The last game at Chinnaswamy saw a repeat of the event of the previous season's game. Dayal defended 16 runs in the last over once moe to ensure RCB's win.[^cite_note-174]

RCB's win streak against CSK continued in [2026](79331486.md). In their latest clash, batting first, RCB scored 250/3, fuelled by [Tim David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_David)'s explosive knock of 70 off 25. That was the highest total of the season at the time, and their highest total against CSK. In the end, they won by 43 runs.[^cite_note-175]

As of April 2026, in the 36 matches played between the Royal Challengers & Super Kings, the Super Kings have won 21 to the Royal Challengers' 14, with one match ending in a draw.[^cite_note-176]

### Deccan Chargers and Sunrisers Hyderabad

Another notable rivalry involving RCB is with the Hyderabad franchises, first with the now defunct [Deccan Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Chargers) and with the [Sunrisers Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrisers_Hyderabad). The Deccan Chargers won six out of the 11 clashes between the two and the Sunrisers currently lead with 14 games to 12.[^cite_note-177] The Sunrisers have also played a key role in derailing the Royal Challengers' campaigns. In the 2009 and 2016 seasons, they were beaten in the finals by Chargers and Sunrisers respectively. In the 2020 season, Sunrisers knocked RCB out of the tournament in the eliminator. In the 2021 season, Sunrisers, who did not qualify for the playoffs, were able to beat RCB, causing them to finish in 3rd place and forcing them to play the eliminator, which they lost to KKR.[^cite_note-178] Their 2022 IPL campaign was also deailed by SRH, whom they lost to by 9 wickets after scoring 68 in their first encounter, putting them under pressure because of their negative run rate through their otherwise strong campaign.[^cite_note-179]

In the latest chapter of the rivalry between the two in IPL 2024, like the Kolkata Knight Riders, Sunrisers Hyderabad were the first to break the 263-run record set by RCB, which seemed insurmountable at the time as a result of Chris Gayle's 175. In a more humiliating turn, SRH again broke RCB's record against RCB themselves on their home ground, scoring 287 runs, thanks to a 39-ball century by [Travis Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Head) and a 30-ball 67 by [Heinrich Klassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Klassen). Although there was a valiant effort by RCB, spearheaded by [Dinesh Karthik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinesh_Karthik)'s 83 off 35 and captain [Faf du Plessis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faf_du_Plessis)'s 62 off 28, RCB still lost by 25 runs.[^cite_note-180] SRH would break RCB's 263 record for the third time and score 266 against the Delhi Capitals after setting an all-time T20 record by scoring 125 runs inside the power-play.

## Fan support

The Royal Challengers has a large and passionate [fan base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_base) in India and especially in the city of [Bengaluru](44275267.md). Their fans are known to be loyal and vocal in their support.[^cite_note-185] They turn up in large numbers for RCB's home matches, turning the stadium into what is called a sea of red.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] They are well known for their chants of "R-C-B, R-C-B" and "[Ee Sala Cup Namde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ee_Sala_Cup_Namde)" (transl. This time the cup is ours).[^cite_note-188] The stadium organisers also provide the home team fans with cheer kits, RCB flags and noisemakers.[^cite_note-189] Royal Challengers Bengaluru is also the most followed cricket franchise on [Instagram](31591547.md) with more than 21 million followers.[^cite_note-Rathod-190]

During the [2014 IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_IPL), the Royal Challengers became the first team to provide free [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) connectivity to fans at their home ground. 50 access points were set up using [fibre-optic cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre-optic_cable) to provide the connectivity to fans on match days at the Chinnaswamy.[^cite_note-191]

### Ee sala cup namde

**Ee sala cup namde** (Kannada: ಈ ಸಲ ಕಪ್ ನಮ್ದೇ; transl. This year, the cup will be ours) is a popular [Kannada-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) sports chant. It became widely used by fans of the RCB as a rallying cry for their hopes of winning the [Indian Premier League (IPL)](13260340.md). The phrase is often paired with its triumphant variant, **Ee Sala Cup Namdu** (transl. This year, the cup is ours), particularly after their maiden [IPL title in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League).[^cite_note-FPJ-192][^cite_note-HT-193]

The chant first gained traction around 2016–17 on Kannada social media and meme platforms, where fans used it humorously and aspirationally in anticipation of RCB finally winning an IPL title. Its use intensified as RCB reached finals and crucial knockout matches, becoming a ubiquitous refrain in [Bengaluru home stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Chinnaswamy_Stadium) and fan gatherings. The slogan's meaning is deeply rooted in Kannada identity, with the word-for-word translation being "this time the cup will be ours".[^cite_note-FPJ-192][^cite_note-HT-193] After RCB's historic victory over [Punjab Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Punjab_Kings_season) in the [IPL 2025 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League_final), the chant proudly evolved to "Ee Sala Cup Namdu", emphasizing possession rather than hope.[^cite_note-FPJ-192]

In popular culture, the chant transcended cricket. In 2018, lyricist [Pradyumna Narahalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pradyumna_Narahalli) wrote a fan anthem titled "Ee Sala Cup Namde", performed by [Sangeetha Rajeev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangeetha_Rajeev), which captured the collective sentiment of RCB supporters.[^cite_note-194] Multiple Kannada YouTube channels and local news outlets featured the chant and its celebratory use after RCB's 2025 triumph.[^cite_note-DH-195][^cite_note-ET-196]
