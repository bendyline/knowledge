A **senate** is a [deliberative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliberative_assembly), often the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) or [chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_chamber) of a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature). The name comes from the [ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) [Roman Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate) (Latin: *Senatus*), so-called as an assembly of the senior (Latin: *senex* meaning "the elder" or "old man") and therefore considered wiser and more experienced members of the [society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society) or [ruling class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruling_class). However the Roman Senate was not the ancestor or predecessor of modern parliamentarism in any sense, because the Roman senate was not a de jure [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative) body.[^cite_note-1]

Many countries have an assembly named a *senate*, composed of *senators* who may be [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election), appointed, have [inherited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance) the title, or gained membership by other methods, depending on the country. Modern senates typically serve to provide a chamber of "sober second thought" to consider [legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation) passed by a [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house), whose members are usually elected. Most senates have asymmetrical duties and powers compared with their respective lower house meaning they have special duties, for example to fill important political positions or to pass special laws. Conversely many senates have limited powers in changing or stopping bills under consideration and efforts to stall or veto a bill may be bypassed by the lower house or another branch of government.[^cite_note-2]

## Overview

The modern word *senate* is derived from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word *senātus* (senate), which comes from *senex*, 'elder man'.[^cite_note-3] A member or legislator of a senate is called **senator**. The Latin word *senator* was adopted into English with [no change in spelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_orthography). Its meaning is derived from a very ancient form of social organization, in which advisory or decision-making powers are reserved for the eldest men. For the same reason, the word *senate* is correctly used when referring to any powerful authority characteristically composed by the eldest members of a community, as a deliberative body of a faculty in an institution of higher learning is often called a senate. This form of adaptation was used to show the power of those in body and for the decision-making process to be thorough, which could take a long period of time. The original senate was the [Roman Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate), which lasted until at least CE 603,[^cite_note-4] although various efforts to revive it were made in Medieval Rome. In the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire), the [Byzantine Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_senate) continued until the [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade), circa 1202–1204. The female form **senatrix** also existed.&#x20;



The [Senate of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_United_States) in session

Modern democratic states with [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) [parliamentary systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) are sometimes equipped with a senate, often distinguished from an ordinary parallel [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house), known variously as the "[House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives)", "[House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons)", "[Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies)", "[National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly)", "[Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly)", or "[House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly)", by electoral rules. This may include minimum age required for voters and candidates, one house employing a proportional voting system and the other being elected on a [majoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majoritarianism) or [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting_system) basis, and an electoral basis or *collegium*. Typically, the senate is referred to as the upper house and has a smaller membership than the lower house. In some [federal states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_state) senates also exist at the subnational level. In the [United States](3434750.md), most states and territories have senates, with the exception of [Nebraska](21647.md), [Guam](11974.md), and the [U.S. Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Virgin_Islands) (whose legislatures are unicameral bodies called the "Legislature" but whose members refer to themselves as "senators") and the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia) (whose unicameral legislature is called the Council). There is also the [US Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Senate) at the federal level. Similarly in [Argentina](18951905.md), in addition to the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Argentina) at federal level, eight of the country's [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina), [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province), [Catamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catamarca_Province), [Corrientes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrientes), [Entre Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entre_R%C3%ADos_Province), [Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendoza_Province), [Salta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salta_Province), [San Luis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Province) (since 1987) and [Santa Fe](520560.md), have bicameral legislatures with a Senate. [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_Province,_Argentina) and [Tucumán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucum%C3%A1n_Province) changed to [unicameral](204304.md) systems in 2001 and 2003 respectively.

In [Australia](4689264.md) and [Canada](5042916.md), only the upper house of the federal parliament is known as the Senate. All [Australian states](4689096.md) other than [Queensland](59051.md) have an upper house known as a [Legislative council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_council). Several Canadian provinces also once had a Legislative Council, but these have all been abolished, the last being [Quebec](7954867.md)'s [Legislative council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Council_of_Quebec) in 1968.

In [Germany](11867.md), the last senate of a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) parliament, the [Bavarian Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Senate), was abolished in 2000.[^cite_note-5]

Senate membership can be determined either through elections or appointments. For example, elections are held every three years for half the membership of the [Senate of the Philippines](299521.md), the term of a senator being six years.[^cite_note-6] In contrast, members of the [Canadian Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada) are appointed by the [Governor General](38152.md) upon the recommendation of the [Prime Minister of Canada](24135.md), holding the office until they resign, are removed, or retire at the mandatory age of 75.

## Alternative meanings

The terms *senate* and *senator*, however, do not necessarily refer to a second chamber of a legislature:

- The [Senate of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Finland) was, until 1918, the executive branch and the supreme court.

- The [Senate of Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Latvia) ([lv](https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvijas%20Sen%C4%81ts)) fulfilled a similar judicial function during the interbellum (1918–1940).

- In German politics:&#x20;

  - In the *[Bundesländer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany)* (Federated States) of Germany which form a [City State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_State) (in German: *Stadtstaat*), i.e. [Berlin](3354.md) ([Senate of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Berlin)), [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\)) ([Senate of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Bremen)) and [Hamburg](13467.md) ([Senate of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Hamburg)), the senates (*[Senat](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Senat)* in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language)) are the executive branch, with senators (*Senator*) being the holders of ministerial portfolios.

    [\[Note 1\]](#cite_note-7)

  - In a number of cities which were former members of the *[Hanse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanse)* (a medieval confederacy of port cities mainly at the shores of the Baltic Sea and the North Sea), such as [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald), [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck), [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock), [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), or [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar), the city government is also called a Senate. However, in [Bavaria](3764.md), the Senate was a second legislative chamber until its abolition in 1999.

- In German jurisprudence:&#x20;

  - The term Senat (senate) in higher [courts of appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_appeal) refers to the "[bench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_\(metonymy\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)" in its broader [metonymy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymy) meaning, describing members of the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) collectively (usually five [judges](44719.md)), often occupied with a particular [subject-matter jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subject-matter_jurisdiction). However, the judges are not called "senators". The German term *Strafsenat* (literally "Penal Senate") in a German court translates to *Bench of [penal-law jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_jurisdiction)* and *Zivilsenat* (literally "Civil Senate") to *Bench of [private-law jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law)*. The [Federal Constitutional Court of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Court_of_Germany) consists of two senates of eight judges each. In its case the division is mostly of an organizational nature, as a matter of dividing the work load; both senates handle the same kind of constitutional cases. At some points in the past, one senate was considered more conservative and the other more liberal, but that is not the case as of 2011.

- In [Scotland](26994.md), judges of the [High Court of Justiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justiciary) are called [Senators of the College of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senators_of_the_College_of_Justice).

- In some, mostly federal countries with a unicameral legislature, some of the legislators are elected differently from the others and are called senators. In federal countries, such senators represent the territories, while the other members represent the people at large (this device is used to allow a federal representation without having to establish a bicameral legislature); this is the case with [St. Kitts and Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Kitts_and_Nevis), [Comoros](5403.md) and [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia). In other, non-federal countries, the use of the term *senator* marks some other difference between such members and the rest of the legislators (such as the method of selection); this is the case with [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica)'s [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_of_Dominica) and the [Saint Vincent](27228.md) [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines). Until [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jersey_general_election), this was also the case in the [States of Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Jersey).

  [^cite_note-8]

- In [Wales](69894.md), there is a translation of the word Senatus into the [Welsh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) (equivalent to 'Senate' in English) as the word "Senedd". The word was used first to refer specifically to what is now referred to as the [Senedd building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd_building), but the name later became a [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymy) for the [devolved unicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolved_parliament) it hosts, the "[National Assembly for Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_for_Wales)", which in [May 2020 adopted the name "Senedd Cymru" or "the Welsh Parliament"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd_and_Elections_\(Wales\)_Act_2020) with the term "[Senedd](238480.md)" becoming the common short name for the institution in both languages of Welsh and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language). There is no direct translation of the word "Parliament" in Welsh, with Senedd (being a cognate of Senate) meaning both "Senate" and "Parliament".

- An [academic senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_senate) is a governing body of some universities.

- In [Greece](12108.md) during the early stages of the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence), various local legislative and executive bodies were established by the Greek rebels. Two of them were styled "senate": the [Peloponnesian Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_Senate) and the [Senate of Western Continental Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Western_Continental_Greece).
