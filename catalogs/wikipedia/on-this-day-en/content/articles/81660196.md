# 2026 FIFA World Cup Group F

&#x20; Group F of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) took place from June 14 to 25, 2026.[^cite_note-match_schedule-1] The group consisted of the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), [Japan](993546.md), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team), and [Tunisia](1029953.md).

The Netherlands topped the group, winning two games and drawing the other. Japan finished as runners-up, winning one game and drawing the other two. Both teams advanced to the [round of 32](81667005.md#Round_of_32).

Sweden finished in third place, and their performance allowed them to advance as one of the [eight best third-place teams](36581929.md#Ranking_of_third-place_teams).[^cite_note-regulations-2]

However, the three teams performed poorly in the round of 32 and were eliminated from the World Cup. Tunisia were eliminated after losing their first two group games, and would go on to lose their third.

### Sweden vs Tunisia

The teams had met on four previous occasions, always in friendlies. Tunisia won the most recent meeting in 2003, by 1–0.[^cite_note-13]

The fixture marked Sweden's return to a major tournament after [five years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_knockout_stage#Sweden_vs_Ukraine), having failed to qualify for both the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) and [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). Additionally, Sweden's five goals were the second-most the team has ever scored in a single World Cup match, behind only their 8–0 victory over [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_football_team) in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-14]

After the match, Tunisia manager [Sabri Lamouchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabri_Lamouchi) was relieved from his managing duties. It was the first time that a manager was sacked during the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) since the [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) edition when Tunisia’s then manager [Henryk Kasperczak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Kasperczak) and his side lost its first two group stage matches 2–0 to [England](9904.md) and 1–0 to [Colombia](680175.md) respectively.[^cite_note-15] Additionally, [Carlos Alberto Parreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Parreira), who was managing [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), and [Cha Bum-kun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha_Bum-kun), who was managing [South Korea](1018627.md), both in the same tournament, were also terminated after their first two matches as well.

### Netherlands vs Sweden

The teams had met on 25 previous occasions, including a scoreless draw during the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_3) at the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), and most recently in a 2–0 win for the Netherlands in a [World Cup Qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_A) match in 2017.[^cite_note-18]

[Brian Brobbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Brobbey) scored in the fifth and seventeenth minute, the fourth-fastest brace in World Cup history.[^cite_note-19] The fixture was Sweden's worst defeat at the World Cup since [being thrashed 7–1 by Brazil in 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup_final_round#Brazil_vs_Sweden), which also remains Brazil's biggest win in a tournament match. The win set a new record for the longest unbeaten run in World Cup history, with fourteen matches (spanning from the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) and excluding 2018, which they failed to qualify for), surpassing the previous record by Brazil.[^cite_note-20]

### Tunisia vs Japan

The teams have previously met six times, including Japan's [2–0 group stage victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_H#Tunisia_vs_Japan) at the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) (which they co-hosted with South Korea). Their latest meeting took place in the 2023 Kirin Challenge Cup, which Japan also won by 2–0.[^cite_note-23]

This fixture was the 1,000th World Cup match, with the previous milestone (900th) having been reached in the [2018 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Moments before kickoff, a special pre-game celebration ceremony was held at the stadium with [Princess Takamado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisako,_Princess_Takamado) (Honorary Patron of the [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association)) in attendance.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

With Tunisia losing their match, they became the third team to be eliminated from the tournament, following [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti_national_football_team) and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team), after losing two consecutive matches. Additionally, Tunisia became the sole African team to be eliminated from the group stage.&#x20;

As for Japan, they became the first [Asian](715249.md) team to score multiple goals in a single FIFA World Cup match, surpassing their own previous scoring record set during a [3–1 victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#Denmark_vs_Japan) over [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) in [2010](168079.md).

### Japan vs Sweden

The teams had met five times, with Japan's only win from the notorious match in the [1936 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) in the first round. Their most recent meeting, a friendly in 2002, ended in a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-31]

In the 56th minute, Japan took the lead when [Daizen Maeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daizen_Maeda) scored with a low shot after running onto a pass from [Ritsu Dōan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritsu_D%C5%8Dan) in the penalty area. [Anthony Elanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Elanga) equalised in the 62nd minute when he curled into the net from out on the right edge of the penalty area, low into the left corner of the net. The draw was enough for Japan to finish in second place in the group.[^cite_note-32]

### Tunisia vs Netherlands



Pre-match ceremony between Tunisia and the Netherlands

The teams had previously faced each other thrice, most recently in a 2009 friendly match that ended in a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-35]

With Tunisia already eliminated, they were off to a very shaky start. [Ellyes Skhiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellyes_Skhiri) committed a costly own goal in the third minute, which allowed the formidable Dutch team to stretch the lead. [Brian Brobbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Brobbey) would follow with a shot four minutes later. On the second half, Tunisia's sole goal was made by [Hazem Mastouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazem_Mastouri) on the 53rd minute, while it was [Jan Paul van Hecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Paul_van_Hecke) who sealed the Netherlands' top spot in the group stage.

Tunisia suffered its worst tournament performance to date, finishing without a single point despite winning matches in its previous two appearances in [2018](13327177.md) and [2022](17742072.md). This stood in stark contrast to [its qualifying campaign](60000294.md#Group_H), where it became the first African team to reach the World Cup without conceding a single goal.

## Discipline

The team conduct ("fair play") score would have been used as a tiebreaker if the head-to-head and overall records of teams were tied. It would also be used as a tiebreaker for the [third-place ranking](36581929.md#Ranking_of_third-place_teams) between groups if the overall records of teams were tied. The score was calculated based on yellow and red cards received by players and team officials in all group matches as follows:[^cite_note-regulations-2]

- yellow card: −1 point;

- indirect red card (second yellow card): −3 points;

- direct red card: −4 points;

- yellow card and direct red card: −5 points;

Only one of the above deductions could be applied to a player or team official in a single match.
