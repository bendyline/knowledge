**Kermanshah province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان كرمانشاه)[^cite_note-9] is one of the 31 [provinces of Iran](448160.md), bordering Iraq. Its capital is the city of [Kermanshah](1489244.md).[^cite_note-Kermanshah_Province_Structure-10] It forms part of the area unofficially known as [Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md).

According to a 2014 segmentation by the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Iran\)), it is the center of [Region 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran),[^cite_note-hamshahrionline.ir-11] with the region's central secretariat located in Kermanshah.&#x20;

A majority of people in the province are [Shia](26961.md), and there are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) and [Yarsani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism) minority groups.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:0-15]

## History



At the top panel [Khosrow II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrow_II) is believed to be standing here in this relief at [Taq-e Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taq-e_Bostan). On his left is [Ahura Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahura_Mazda), on his right is [Anahita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahita), and below him is a mounted Persian knight.

The province has a rich Paleolithic heritage. Many caves with [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) remains have been surveyed or excavated there. some of these cave sites are located in Bisetun and north of Kermanshah. The first known physical remains of [Neanderthal man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) in Iran was discovered in [Bisitun Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisitun_Cave). [Do-Ashkaft Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do-Ashkaft_Cave), Kobeh, [Warwasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwasi), and Mar Tarik[^cite_note-16] are some of the [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic) sites in the region. [Kermanshah](1489244.md) also has many [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) sites, of which the most well-known are [Ganj Dareh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganj_Dareh), [Sarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarab,_East_Azerbaijan), and Asiab. At [Ganj Dareh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganj_Dareh), the earliest evidence for goat [domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication) have been documented.

In May 2009, based on a research conducted by the University of [Hamedan](43610555.md) and [UCL](52029.md), the head of Archeology Research Center of Iran's Cultural Heritage and Tourism Organization announced that the oldest prehistoric village in the [Middle East](19323.md) dating back to 9800 B.C., was discovered in [Sahneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahneh_County), located in west of Kermanshah.[^cite_note-ISNA-17][^cite_note-خبرگزاري_دانشجويان_ايران-18]

The monuments found in Kermanshah show two glorious periods, the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) eras. The mythical ruler of the [Pishdadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pishdadian_dynasty) is described as founding the city while [Tahmores Divband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahmores_Divband?action=edit\&redlink=1) built it. An alternative narrative is that the construction was by [Bahram IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_IV) of the Sassanid dynasty during the 4th century CE. Kermanshah reached a peak during the reign of [Hormizd IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormizd_IV) and [Khosrau I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrau_I) of Sassanids, before being demoted to a secondary royal residence.

The city suffered major damage during the [Arab invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) but recovered in the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid) period to make great progress. Concurrent with the [Afghan](737.md) attack and the fall of [Isfahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan_\(city\)), Kermanshah was almost completely destroyed by the [Ottoman](22278.md) invasion.

During the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) the province suffered heavy fighting. Most towns and cities were badly damaged and some like [Sar-e Pol-e Zahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sar-e_Pol-e_Zahab) and [Qhasr-e-Shirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qhasr-e-Shirin) were almost completely destroyed.

The November [2017 Iran–Iraq earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_earthquake) killed more than 600 people.

On 28 December 2017, Kermanshah became one of several Iranian provinces to break out into protests. [The Supreme Leader of Iran](385653.md) has blamed western interference. Some female organizations such as The National Council of Resistance of Iran ([NCRI](1181356.md)) have taken partial credit for the organization of these protests.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Population

At the time of the 2006 national census, the province's population was 1,842,457 in 444,185 households.[^cite_note-2006_Kermanshah_Province-21]

By the time of the following census in 2011, there were 1,945,227 people in 530,911 households.[^cite_note-2011_Kermanshah_Province-22]

The 2016 census measured the population of the province as 1,952,434 in 576,861 households.[^cite_note-2016_Kermanshah_Province-2]

## Geography

Kermanshah province is located in western Iran. It shares borders with the provinces of [Kurdistan](448112.md), [Hamadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadan_province), [Lorestan](433072.md), and [Ilam](448068.md) within Iran.[^cite_note-23] It also shares borders with the Iraqi governorates of [Halabja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halabja_Governorate), [Sulaymaniyah](752619.md) and [Diyala](377806.md).[^cite_note-24]

Major cities and towns besides the capital include [Eslamabad-e Gharb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eslamabad-e_Gharb), [Gilan-e Gharb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilan-e_Gharb), [Harsin](9590172.md), [Javanrud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanrud), [Kangavar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangavar), [Paveh](1070269.md), [Qasr-e Shirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasr-e_Shirin), [Ravansar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravansar), [Sahneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahneh), [Sarpol-e Zahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarpol-e_Zahab), and [Sonqor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonqor).

### Climate

As it is situated between two cold and warm regions, Kermanshah Province enjoys a moderate and mountainous climate. It rains most in winter and is moderately warm in summer. The annual rainfall is 500 mm. The average temperature in the hottest months is above 22 °C.

### City of Kermanshah

The province's capital is [Kermanshah](1489244.md), located in the middle of the western part of Iran. The population of the city is 946,651 as of 2016.[^cite_note-2016_Kermanshah_Province-2] It is built on the slopes of Mt. [Sefid Kooh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefid_Kooh) and extended toward south during last two decades. The built-up areas run alongside [Sarab River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarab_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Sarab Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarab_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1). The city's elevation averages about 1,350 meters above sea level.

The distance between Kermanshah and [Tehran](57654.md) is 525 km. It is the trade center of rich agricultural region that produces grain, rice, vegetable, fruits, and oilseeds, and there are many industrial centers, oil and sugar refineries, and cement, textile and flour factories, etc. The airport ([Shahid Ashrafi Esfahani Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Ashrafi_Esfahani_Airport)) is located in north east of the city, and the distance from Tehran is 413 km by air.

## Economy

### International and border trade

Kermanshah province has 371 km of common border with Iraq. There are five official border markets in the province. Three of these neighbor the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) of Iraq and the other two the Iraqi central government (Khosravi, Sumar). Based on figures from 9 November 2021, with a share of 5.7 to 6 percent Kermanshah province is among the first five exporters of non-oil products of Iran. Around 30 to 35 percent of theses [exports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exports) were from the province's own products. The highest share of exports belong to [Parviz border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasr-e_Shirin) which neighbors Iraqi Kurdistan followed by [Khosravi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosravi,_Kermanshah) and [Sumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumar,_Iran).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-financialtribune.com-26]

Exports from Kermanshah province hit \$2.7 billion in the fiscal year 2021-22 (ending 20 March), registering a 15% growth compared with the previous year, according to a local official.

The province's share in Iran's total exports during the period stood at 10% in terms of tonnage and 12% in value.

Kermanshah's share in total exports to Iraq stood at 53% and share in non-oil goods reached 43%.

Non-oil exports from Kermanshah mainly include tomato and tomato paste, air conditioners, cheese, industrial oil and their derivatives, biscuit, different types of melon, watermelon, tile and ceramic, plastic bags, marble, yogurt, milk, cream, cucumber, eggplant, apple and melamine dishes.[^cite_note-financialtribune.com-26]

### Local products

Kermanshah lends its name to a type of [Persian carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_carpet) named after the region. It also has famous sweets made of rice, locally known as [Nan-e berenji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan-e_berenji).[^cite_note-27] The other famous Kermanshahi good is a special kind of oil, locally known as [Rüne Dân](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCne_D%C3%A2n?action=edit\&redlink=1) and globally in Iran known as [Roghan Kermanshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roghan_Kermanshahi?action=edit\&redlink=1). The [Giveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giveh) of Kermanshah known as *Klash* is the highest quality Giveh.

## Historical attractions

Various attractions exist that date from the pre-Islamic era, such as the [Kohneh Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohneh_Bridge), to contemporary parks and museums. Some of the more popular sites are:

### [Bisotun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisotun)



[Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great)'s Behistun inscription

[Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great)'s inscription at Bisotun, which dates to 522 BCE, lies some 1300 meters high in the mountains, and counts as one of the most famous sites in Near Eastern [archeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeology). The site is a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md),

[^cite_note-28]

&#x20;and has been attracting visitors for centuries. The Behistun inscription is to [Old Persian cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian) what the [Rosetta Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone) is to [Egyptian hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyph): the trilingual inscription (in Old Persian, [Elamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elamite) and [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language)) was crucial in the decipherment of the script. The relief above the inscription depicts Darius facing nine rebels who objected to his crowning. At the king's feet lies [Gaumata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaumata). The location of this important historical document is not coincidental: Gaumata, a usurper who is depicted as lying at Darius' feet, was a Medean and in Achaemenid times Behistun lay on the Medea-Parsa highway.



Hellenistic-era depiction of [Bahram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verethragna) as Hercules.

Behistun is also notable for three reliefs at the foot of the hill that date from the Parthian era. Among them is a Hellenistic-era depiction of the divinity [Bahram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verethragna) as the Greek hero Hercules, who reclines with a goblet in his hand, a club at his feet and a lion-skin beneath him. Because it lies on the route of an ancient highway, this life-size rock sculpture may reflect Bahram's status as patron divinity of travelers.

### [Taq-e Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taq-e_Bostan)

The rock reliefs at Taq-e Bostan lie 6 kilometres (4 mi) northeast of Kermanshah, where a spring gushes from a mountain cliff and empties into a large reflecting pool. One of the more impressive reliefs, inside the largest grotto (*ivan*), is the oversized depiction of Sassanid king [Khosrau II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrau_II) (591–628 CE), who appears mounted on his favorite charger, Shabdiz. Both the horse and the rider are arrayed in full battle armor.

There are two hunting scenes on complementary sides of the *ivan*: one depicts an imperial boar hunt and the other depicting the king stalking deer. Elephants flush out the boar from a marshy lake for the king who stands poised with bow and arrow in hand while he is serenaded by female musicians following in other boats. These royal hunting scenes are narrative murals in stone are count among the most vivid of all Iranian rock reliefs.

The Taq-e Bostan reliefs are not limited to the Sassanid era. An upper relief depicts the 19th century [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) king Fath-Ali shah holding court.

### The [Kangavar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangavar) archaeological complex

Kangavar is the site of the archaeological remains of a vast Hellenic-style edifice on a raised platform. The visible remains at the site date to early Sassanid times,

[^cite_note-EI_Kangavar-29]

&#x20;but the platform of the complex may be several centuries older. By the time excavation began in 1968, the complex had been preemptorily associated with a comment by [Isidore of Charax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidore_of_Charax) who referred to a temple of [Anahita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahita) at Concobar (the Greek name of Kangavar, which was then in [Lower Medea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes)). Despite archaeological findings to the contrary,

[^cite_note-EI_Kangavar-29]

&#x20;the association with the divinity of fertility, healing, and wisdom has made the site a popular tourist attraction. The vast edifice was built of enormous blocks of dressed stone with an imposing entrance of opposed staircases that may have been inspired by the Apadana in [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis).

## Notable people



Mirza Ahmad Khan Motazed-Dowleh Vaziri

One of the renowned scientists and writers of this region is [Al-Dinawari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Dinawari) who was born at *Dinawar* north-east of [Kermanshah](1489244.md). He lived in the 9th century and has written many books in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany) and history.

Notable people born in Kermansha include British author, [Nobel Prize](21201.md) winner, [Doris Lessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Lessing), whose father, a [British Army](4887.md) [officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_officer), was stationed there at the time of her birth. [Mirza Ahmad Khan Motazed-Dowleh Vaziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Ahmad_Khan_Motazed-Dowleh_Vaziri?action=edit\&redlink=1) created the first [printing office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_office) and founded the first private school of Kermanshahan. [Guity Novin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guity_Novin) a painter and a graphic designer who has founded the [Transpressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transpressionism) movement was born in Kermanshah. The famous Sufi Scholar, [Abdul Qader Gailani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Qadir_Gilani), was born in the region.
