The **Russian Far East** is a region in [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia). It is the easternmost part of [Russia](25391.md) and the [Asian continent](689.md), and is coextensive with the [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District), which encompasses the area between [Lake Baikal](56865.md) and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). The area's largest city is [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk), followed by [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok). The region shares international land borders with [Mongolia](19271.md), [China](5405.md), and [North Korea](21255.md) to its south, as well as [maritime boundaries](29613061.md) with [Japan](15573.md) to its southeast, and with the [United States](3434750.md) along the [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait) to its northeast.

Although foreign sources often consider the Russian Far East to be a part of [Siberia](29639.md), it has been historically categorized separately from Siberia in Russian regional and cultural schemes (and previously during the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union) when it was called the **Soviet Far East**).[^cite_note-1]

## Terminology

In Russia, the region is usually referred to as simply the *Far East* (Russian: Дальний Восток, romanized: *Dal'niy Vostok*, IPA: [\[ˈdalʲnʲɪj vɐˈstok\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)). What is known in English as the [Far East](11672.md) is usually referred to as the [*Asia-Pacific Region*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific) (Азиатско-тихоокеанский регион, *Aziatsko-tiho-okeanskiy region*, abbreviated АТР (ATR)), or *[East Asia](19605700.md)* (Восточная Азия, *Vostochnaya Aziya*), depending on the context.

## History

### Russian expansion

Further information: [Russian conquest of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia), [Outer Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Manchuria), and [Transcathay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcathay)



[Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) in the early 1900s

[Russians](59510.md) reached the Pacific coast in 1647 with the establishment of [Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhotsk), and the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) consolidated its control over the Russian Far East in the 19th century, after the [annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_Annexation) of part of Chinese [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria) (1858–1860). [Primorskaya Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorskaya_Oblast) was established as a separate [administrative division of the Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_the_Russian_Empire) in 1856, with its administrative center at [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk).

### Administrative history

Several entities with the name "Far East" existed in the first half of the 20th century, all with rather different boundaries:

- 1918–1922: the [Ukrainian Far Eastern Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Far_Eastern_Movement?action=edit\&redlink=1), which encompassed [Green Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Ukraine);

- 1920–1922: the [Far Eastern Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Republic), which included [Transbaikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transbaikal_Oblast), [Amur](87627.md), [Primorskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorskaya_Oblast), and [Kamchatka Oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Oblast) and northern [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin);

- 1922–1926: [Far-Eastern Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-Eastern_Oblast?action=edit\&redlink=1), which included the [Amur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_Governorate?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Transbaikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transbaikal_Governorate?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Kamchatka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Governorate) [Governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_\(Russia\)) and others;

- 1926–1938: [Far-Eastern Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-Eastern_Krai), which included the present-day [Primorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai) and [Khabarovsk Krais](87626.md).

Until 2000 the Russian Far East lacked officially defined boundaries. A single term "Siberia and the Far East" (Сибирь и Дальний Восток) often referred to Russia's regions east of the [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_mountains) without drawing a clear distinction between "Siberia" and "the Far East".

In 2000 Russia's [federal subjects](701438.md) were grouped into larger [federal districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_districts_of_Russia), one of which, the [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District), comprised [Amur Oblast](87627.md), the [Chukotka Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukotka_Autonomous_Okrug), the [Jewish Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Autonomous_Oblast), [Kamchatka Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Oblast) with the [Koryak Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryak_Autonomous_Okrug), [Khabarovsk Krai](87626.md), [Magadan Oblast](480997.md), [Primorsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai), the [Sakha (Yakutia) Republic](57905.md), and [Sakhalin Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin_Oblast). In November 2018 [Zabaykalsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabaykalsky_Krai) and the [Republic of Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Buryatia) were added; they had previously formed part of the [Siberian Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Federal_District).[^cite_note-2] Since 2000, Russians have increasingly used the term "Far East" to refer to the federal district, though the term is often also used more loosely.

Defined by the boundaries of the federal district, the Far East has an area of 6.2 million square kilometres (2,400,000 sq mi)—over one-third of Russia's total area.

### Russo-Japanese War

Further information: [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War)

Russia in the early 1900s persistently sought a warm-water port on the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) for the [Imperial Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Navy) as well as to facilitate maritime trade. The recently established Pacific seaport of [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) (founded in 1860) was operational only during the summer season, but [Port Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCshunkou) (leased by Russia from China from 1896 onward) in Manchuria could operate all year. After the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War) (1894–1895) and the failure of the 1903 negotiations between [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) and the [Tsar Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_Nicholas_II)'s government, Japan chose war to protect its domination of [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Empire) and adjacent territories. Russia, meanwhile, saw war as a means of distracting its populace from government repression and of rallying patriotism in the aftermath of several general strikes. Japan issued a declaration of war on 8 February 1904. Three hours before Japan's declaration of war was received by the Russian government, the Imperial Japanese Navy attacked the Russian [1st Pacific Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_\(Russia\)) at Port Arthur. Eight days later Russia declared war on Japan.

The war ended in September 1905 with a Japanese victory following the fall of Port Arthur and the failed Russian invasion of Japan through the Korean Peninsula and [Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China); also, Japan had threatened to invade [Primorsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai) via Korea. The warring parties signed the [Treaty of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Portsmouth) on 5 September 1905, and both Japan and Russia agreed to evacuate Manchuria and to return its sovereignty to China, but Japan was allowed to lease the [Liaodong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaodong_Peninsula) (containing Port Arthur and [Talien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talien), aka [Kwantung Leased Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Leased_Territory)), and the [Russian rail system in southern Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Manchuria_Railway) with its access to strategic resources. Japan also received the [southern half of the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karafuto_Prefecture) of [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin) from Russia. In 1907 Japan forced Russia to confiscate land from Korean settlers (who formed the majority of Primorsky Krai's population) due to a fear of an invasion of Korea and of the ousting of Japanese troops by Korean guerrillas.

### Soviet era

Between 1937 and 1939, the Soviet Union under [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) deported [over 200,000 Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-saram) to [Uzbekistan](31853.md) and [Kazakhstan](16642.md), fearing that the Koreans might act as spies for Japan. Many Koreans died on the way in cattle trains due to starvation, illness, or freezing conditions. Soviet authorities purged and executed many community leaders; [Koryo-saram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-saram) were not allowed to travel outside of Central Asia for the next 15 years. Koreans were also not allowed to use the Korean language and its use began to become lost with the involvement of the [Koryo-mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-mar) dialect and the use of Russian.

Development of numerous remote locations in the Soviet Far East relied on [Gulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag) [labour camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_camp) during Stalin's rule, especially in the region's northern half. After the death of Stalin in 1953 the large-scale use of [forced labour](541048.md) waned and was superseded by volunteer employees attracted by relatively high wages.

#### Soviet–Japanese conflicts

Main article: [Soviet–Japanese border conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_border_conflicts)

During the [Japanese invasion of Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria) in 1931, the Soviets occupied [Bolshoy Ussuriysky Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshoy_Ussuriysky_Island), [Yinlong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yinlong_Island), and several adjacent islets to separate the city of [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk) from the territory controlled by a possibly hostile power.[^cite_note-3]

Indeed, Japan turned its military attention to Soviet territories. Conflicts between the Japanese and the Soviets frequently happened on the border of Manchuria between 1938 and 1945. The first confrontation occurred in Primorsky Krai, the [Battle of Lake Khasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lake_Khasan) (July–August 1938) involved an attempted military incursion of Japanese-controlled [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo) into territory claimed by the Soviet Union. This incursion was founded in the beliefs of the Japanese side that the Soviet Union had misinterpreted the demarcation of the boundary based on the 1860 [Treaty of Peking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Peking) between Imperial Russia and [Manchu China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_China). Primorsky Krai was always threatened by a Japanese invasion despite the fact that most of the remaining clashes occurred in Manchukuo.

The clashes ended shortly before and after the conclusion of [World War II](32927.md) (see [Soviet–Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_War)) when a war-weakened Japan found its territories of Manchukuo, [Mengjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengjiang), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), and [South Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sakhalin) [invaded by Soviet and Mongolian troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Manchuria) (August 1945).

#### World War II

Main articles: [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) and [Soviet–Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_War)

Both the Soviet Union and Japan regarded the Primorsky Krai as a strategic location in World War II, and clashes over the territory were common. The Soviets and the other [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) considered it a key location for the planned [invasion of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Downfall) through Korea; Japan viewed it as a key location to begin a [mass invasion of Eastern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokushin-ron). The Primorsky Krai served as the Soviet Union's Pacific headquarters in the war to plan an invasion for allied troops of Korea in order to reach Japan.

After the Soviet invasion, the USSR returned Manchukuo and Mengjiang to China; [Korea became liberated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Korea). The Soviet Union also occupied and annexed Japan's [Kuril Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuril_Islands) and southern Sakhalin. The planned Soviet invasion of Japan proper never happened.

#### Cold War

During the [Korean War](16772.md), Primorsky Krai became the site of extreme security concern for the Soviet Union.

[Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) was the site of the [Strategic Arms Limitation Talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Arms_Limitation_Talks) in 1974. At the time, the Soviet Union and the United States decided quantitative limits on various nuclear weapons systems and banned the construction of new land-based [ICBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) launchers. Vladivostok and other cities in Primorsky Krai soon became [closed cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_cities) because of the bases of the [Soviet Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Pacific_Fleet).

Incursions of [American](3434750.md) [reconnaissance aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft) from [Alaska](624.md) sometimes happened. Concerns of the Soviet military caused the infamous [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007) incident in 1983.

## Demographics

### Population



Students in [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) celebrating [St. Tatyana's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatiana_Day), or Russian Students Day (2009)



Graph depicting population change in the Russian Far East

According to the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census), the [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District) had a population of 7.98 million. Most of it is concentrated in the southern parts. Given the vast territory of the Russian Far East, 7.98 million people translates to slightly more than one person per square kilometer, making the Russian Far East one of the most sparsely populated areas in the world. The population of the Russian Far East has been rapidly declining since the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) (even more than for Russia in general), dropping by 14% in the last fifteen years. The Russian government had been discussing a range of re-population programs to avoid the forecast drop to 4.5 million people by 2015, hoping to attract in particular the remaining Russian population of the [near abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_abroad) but eventually agreeing on a program to resettle Ukrainian illegal immigrants.

Ethnic [Russians](59510.md) and [Ukrainians](145144.md) make up the majority of the population.

## Transportation



Transportation on the [Lena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River) (2004)

The region was not connected with the rest of Russia via domestic highways until the [M58 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M58_highway_\(Russia\)) was completed in 2010.

Uniquely for Russia, most cars have [right-hand drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-hand_drive) (73% of cars in the region),[^cite_note-rhdcars-5] though they are still driven on the right-hand side of the road.

Railways are better developed. The [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md) and [Baikal–Amur Mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal%E2%80%93Amur_Mainline) (since 1984) provide a connection with Siberia (and the rest of the country). The [Amur–Yakutsk Mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur%E2%80%93Yakutsk_Mainline) is aimed to link the city of [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk) to the Russian railway network. Passenger trains connect to [Nizhny Bestyakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Bestyakh) as of 2013.

As in nearby Siberia, for many remote localities, aviation is the main mode of transportation to/from civilisation, but the infrastructure is often lacking due to the harsh climate associated with the region. In 2018, Russia announced a \$235 million plan designed to upgrade existing airports within the far east.[^cite_note-6] [Kharbarovsk Novy Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk_Novy_Airport) is one of the largest airports serving the far east, connecting much of mainland Russia to the area, as well as certain regions of east Asia.

Maritime transport is important for delivering supplies to localities near the Pacific and [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean) coasts, and for shipping exports, especially oil, gas and ores.
