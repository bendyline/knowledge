***Nagatitan*** (lit. '[Nāga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81ga) giant') is an [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct) genus of [euhelopodid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhelopodid) sauropod dinosaur known from the [Early Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Cretaceous) ([Aptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptian)–[Albian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albian) ages) [Khok Kruat Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khok_Kruat_Formation) of Thailand. The genus contains a [single species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotypic_taxon), ***Nagatitan chaiyaphumensis***, known from a partial skeleton. *Nagatitan* is a large sauropod, with an estimated body length of 27 metres (89 ft) and mass of approximately 27 tonnes (30 short tons), making it the largest dinosaur known from Southeast Asia.

## Discovery and naming



Location of the Ban Pha Nang Sua locality (orange star) in Thailand

The *Nagatitan* fossil material was discovered in outcrops of the [Khok Kruat Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khok_Kruat_Formation) in [Chaiyaphum province](239573.md), Thailand by Thanom Luangnan on the bank of a pond in 2016.[^cite_note-Press-1] The specimen is housed in the [Sirindhorn Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirindhorn_Museum), where it is permanently [accessioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_number_\(cultural_property\)) as specimens SM2025-1-546 to SM2025-1-556. The specimen consists of several vertebrae, parts of the pelvis, and parts of the forelimb and hindlimb.[^cite_note-Nagatitan-2]

In 2026, Thitiwoot Sethapanichsakul and colleagues [described](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_description) *Nagatitan chaiyaphumensis* as a new genus and species of sauropod dinosaur based on these fossil remains, establishing SM2025-1-546–556 as the [holotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holotype) specimen. The [generic name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus), *Nagatitan*, combines a reference to the mythical serpetine [Nāga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81ga) in Asian cultures, with the word "titan" (lit. 'giant'), a common suffix for sauropod names, referencing the [pre-Olympian gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titans) of [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology). The [specific name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_name_\(zoology\)), *chaiyaphumensis*, references the discovery of the species in Chaiyaphum, Thailand.[^cite_note-Nagatitan-2]

## Description



Size compared to a human

To estimate the body mass and overall body length of *Nagatitan*, Sethapanichsakul et al. (2026) used calculations derived from measurements of the bones. The body mass was calculated using the minimum shaft circumference of the [humerus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humerus) (58.4 cm (23.0 in)) and [femur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femur) (68.2 cm (26.9 in)), yielding a mass of 26,582 kilograms (58,603 lb). The overall body length was calculated at about 27.2 metres (89 ft).[^cite_note-Nagatitan-2]

## Classification

To test the affinities and relationships of *Nagatitan*, Sethapanichsakul and colleagues (2026) included it in an expanded version of the [phylogenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic) matrix of Díez Díaz et al. (2025).[^cite_note-DiezDiaz2025-3] Using both equal character weighting and (b) extended [implied weighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implied_weighting), *Nagatitan* was recovered within the [somphospondylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somphospondylan) [titanosauriform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauriform) family [Euhelopodidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euhelopodidae). Using equal weighting, it was placed as the [sister taxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_taxon) to *[Europatitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europatitan)*—a European taxon not generally regarded as a member of this otherwise Asian clade—at the base of Euhelopodidae. Using implied weighting, *Europatitan* shifted outside of this clade, while *Nagatitan* was placed in a more euhelopodid [derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_\(phylogenetics\)) position, diverging after *[Tangvayosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangvayosaurus)* but before *[Phuwiangosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuwiangosaurus)*. The results of the latter analysis are displayed in the [cladogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladogram) below:[^cite_note-Nagatitan-2]

## Paleobiology



Paleoenvironmental reconstruction of the Khok Kraut Formation (Khok Pha Suam locality)

*Nagatitan* is known from the Khok Kraut Formation, which dates to the [Aptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptian) to [Albian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albian) ages of the end of the [Early Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Cretaceous). Many other dinosaurs have been described from this formation, including indeterminate (unnamed) [titanosauriforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosauriform), the [ornithopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithopod) *[Ratchasimasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchasimasaurus)*, *[Siamodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamodon)*, and *[Sirindhorna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirindhorna)*, the early [ceratopsian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceratopsian) *[Psittacosaurus sattayaraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psittacosaurus_sattayaraki)*, the [carcharodontosaurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcharodontosauria) *[Siamraptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamraptor)*, and indeterminate [spinosaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurid) bones. Non-dinosaurian taxa from this region include the [crocodyliform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodyliform) *[Khoratosuchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoratosuchus)*, in addition to remains of a [pterodactyloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterodactyloid) pterosaur, [adocid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adocid) and [carettochelyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carettochelyid) turtles, and [halecomorph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halecomorph), [ginglymodian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginglymodian), and [hybodontiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybodontiform) fishes. Outcrops of the [Grès Supérieurs Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A8s_Sup%C3%A9rieurs_Formation) in Laos are considered to be equivalent to those of the Khok Kruat Formation, and have yielded dinosaurs including the titanosauriform *[Tangvayosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangvayosaurus)*, the ornithopod *[Mandschurosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandschurosaurus)*, and the spinosaurid *[Ichthyovenator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyovenator)*.[^cite_note-Manitkoon2022-4]
