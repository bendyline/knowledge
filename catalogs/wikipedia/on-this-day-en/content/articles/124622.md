**Pittsburg** is the largest [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_town) by area in [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), located in [Coös County, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co%C3%B6s_County,_New_Hampshire), United States. The population was 800 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-Census_2020-3] It is the northernmost town in [New Hampshire](21134.md). [U.S. Route 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_3#New_Hampshire) is the only major highway in the town, although the northern terminus of [New Hampshire Route 145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_Route_145) also lies within Pittsburg.

Pittsburg is part of the [Berlin, New Hampshire micropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin,_New_Hampshire_micropolitan_area).

## History



Park in Pittsburg

Pittsburg derives its name from [William Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham), [Prime Minister of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Great_Britain).[^cite_note-whence-4] Prior to its incorporation in 1840, the area was settled c. 1810 and known as the Territory of Indian Stream. It had the unique distinction of having been its own [microstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate) briefly during the 1830s, called the [Republic of Indian Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Indian_Stream), due to an ambiguous boundary between the United States and Canada.[^cite_note-whence-4]

The [Indian Stream Schoolhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Stream_Schoolhouse) on Tabor Road, which dates to 1897, is listed in both the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) and the [New Hampshire State Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_State_Register_of_Historic_Places); the town hall on Main Street, which dates to 1883, is listed in the State Register.[^cite_note-5]

## Geography

Pittsburg shares an international border with [Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%C3%A9bec) province, [Canada](5042916.md), to its west and north, and borders the states of [Maine](19977.md) (to the east) and [Vermont](32578.md) (a very small portion to the southwest). Directly to the south is [Clarksville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksville,_New_Hampshire). Pittsburg is the only New Hampshire municipality to border Canada, the only one that borders both Maine and Vermont, the only one to share a land border with Vermont, and the only town entirely north of the [45th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_parallel_north). Pittsburg contains the only part of New Hampshire west of the [Connecticut River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_River), as that river defines the Vermont state line from Clarksville southward. The [Pittsburg–Chartierville Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg%E2%80%93Chartierville_Border_Crossing), New Hampshire's only Canadian border crossing, is located in the town, at the northern terminus of [U.S. Route 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_3). The western edge of Pittsburg is defined by [Halls Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halls_Stream), being the "northwesternmost headwaters of the Connecticut River", which defined (ambiguously) the border in the [Treaty of Paris of 1783](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)).

Contained within the boundaries of Pittsburg are the [Connecticut Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Lakes), which form the beginning of the Connecticut River. Early maps (e.g. 1854) also show several grants that were incorporated into the eastern edge of Pittsburg, including *Carlisle No. 1*, *Webster/Carlisle No. 2* and *Hubbards No. 3*, all north of [Atkinson and Gilmanton Academy Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atkinson_and_Gilmanton_Academy_Grant,_New_Hampshire).

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the town has a total area of 291.3 square miles (754.5 km<sup>2</sup>), the largest in New Hampshire and New England of any municipality incorporated as a town. A total of 281.3 square miles (728.6 km<sup>2</sup>) of Pittsburg are land and 10.0 square miles (25.8 km<sup>2</sup>) are water, the latter comprising 3.43% of the town.[^cite_note-CenPopGazetteer2021-1]

The highest point in Pittsburg is the summit of Stub Hill, at 3,627 feet (1,106 m). [Mount Magalloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Magalloway), elevation 3,383 feet (1,031 m), is a prominent summit reachable by hiking trail.

## Climate

According to the [Köppen Climate Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_Climate_Classification) system, First Connecticut Lake has a [warm-summer humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_humid_continental_climate), abbreviated "Dfb" on climate maps. The hottest temperature recorded at First Connecticut Lake was 93 °F (33.9 °C) on July 8, 1921, and July 19, 1953, while the coldest temperature recorded was −45 °F (−42.8 °C) on February 1, 1920.[^cite_note-NOWData-6]

As of the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-GR2-9] of 2000, there were 867 people, 386 households, and 264 families residing in the town. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 3.1 people per square mile (1.2/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 1,281 housing units at an average density of 4.5 per square mile (1.8/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the town was 98.27% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.12% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.58% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.23% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 0.81% from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race were 0.46% of the population.



Pittsburg School

There were 386 households, out of which 22.3% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 59.1% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) living together, 4.4% had a female householder with no husband present, and 31.6% were non-families. 26.7% of all households were made up of individuals, and 14.8% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.24 and the average family size was 2.67.

In the town, the population was spread out, with 18.9% under the age of 18, 5.4% from 18 to 24, 23.8% from 25 to 44, 32.9% from 45 to 64, and 19.0% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 46 years. For every 100 females, there were 105.9 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 105.6 males.

The median income for a household in the town was \$38,516, and the median income for a family was \$42,500. Males had a median income of \$31,250 versus \$25,455 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the town was \$17,703. About 5.0% of families and 7.4% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 7.8% of those under age 18 and 11.5% of those age 65 or over.

In 2014 the largest ancestry groups reported in Pittsburg were [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) (30.9%), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American) or [French Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadian) (21.0%), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) (9.8%), and "American" (9.2%).[^cite_note-10]

## Tourism



Looking south across Back Lake, August 2019

Pittsburg is a destination for [snowmobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmobile) and [ATV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-terrain_vehicle) enthusiasts year round. In the winter months thousands of people visit Pittsburg to take advantage of the state-funded and club-supported and maintained trails that can bring snowmobilers from elsewhere in the state of New Hampshire to Pittsburg, with access to the states of [Maine](19977.md), Vermont and the border crossing to [Canada](5042916.md). Some of the same trails are used in the summer for ATVs.

Another focus in the area for tourists is [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) and [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting). Pittsburg is home to the four [Connecticut Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Lakes), [Back Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Lake_\(New_Hampshire\)), part of [Lake Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Francis_\(Murphy_Dam\)), and numerous smaller water bodies. The lakes are annually stocked with fish and allow for fishing weekends. The area is known for [fly fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly_fishing). Two sections of the Connecticut River are fly fishing only, including the "Trophy Stretch". The Connecticut Lakes Region has attracted anglers and hunters since the early 1900s. There are maps on the local snowmobile club's website that show trails in Pittsburg as well as roads and access points to the lakes and rivers.[^cite_note-11]

[Lake Francis State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Francis_State_Park) allows for lakeside camping, fishing, and canoeing on the lake or the Connecticut River. [Deer Mountain Campground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_Mountain_Campground) enables camping and picnicking on the Connecticut River.

Pittsburg fell within the path of totality during the [solar eclipse of April 8, 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_April_8,_2024).[^cite_note-12]
