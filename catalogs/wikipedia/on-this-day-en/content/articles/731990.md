**Atlatlahucan** [/ˌætləˈtlaʊkæn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) (from the [Nahuatl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl) word [\[at͡ɬaˈt͡ɬaʍkan\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Nahuatl)) is a city in the Mexican state of [Morelos](195827.md). The name means *Place of red or brown water*, and today the water is stored in a type of cistern called a *jagüey*. To the north is the [State of Mexico](222243.md), south is [Cuautla](732078.md), east is [Yecapixtla](732223.md), and west are [Tlayacapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlayacapan) and [Yautepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yautepec._Morelos?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4] It stands at [18°56′N 98°54′W  /  18.933°N 98.900°W  / 18.933; -98.900](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Atlatlahucan\&params=18_56_N_98_54_W_),  at a mean height of 1,656 metres (5,433 ft.) above sea level. The municipality covers 71 km<sup>2</sup> (27.4 square miles).

The city serves as the municipal seat for the surrounding municipality of the same name. The municipality reported 22,079 inhabitants in the 2015 census.[^cite_note-inegi-5] The population of the municipality of Atlatlahucan was 25,232 and the city of Atlatlahucan was 9,018 in 2020.[^cite_note-censo2020-2]

## History

The [Xochimilcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xochimilco) extended their conquest to [Totolapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totolapan) and Atlatlahucan in 1436. [Moctezuma Ilhuicamina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moctezuma_Ilhuicamina) expanded his conquests in the Morelos valley include to Atlatlahucan, which was forced to pay tribute to the [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) sovereign.

After the Spanish conquest, the town ignored its annexation to the *Marquisate of the Valley of Oaxaca*. In 1533, Augustinian friars *Jerónimo de San Esteban* and *Jorge de Ávila* arrived. They built a parish church that was secularized in 1548. A monastery was founded in 1570, although the actual date may be a bit later because the town was still subject to Totolapan at that time.[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4]

Atlatlahucan was an assistant municipality of [Tlayacapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlayacapan), but on December 25, 1932, it became a municipality in its own right. *Ignacio Bello* was chosen as the first municipal president.[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4]

On August 24, 1964, the town was the site of an explosion that killed 37 people and injured 40 others, after a stockpile of fireworks went off prematurely while stored next to two tanks of butane.[^cite_note-6]

*Calixto Urbano Lagunas* of [Juntos Haremos Historia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juntos_Haremos_Historia) (Together we will make history coalition) was elected *Presidente Municipal* (municipal president) in the election of July 1, 2018.[^cite_note-7]

The state of Morelos reported 209 cases and 28 deaths due to the [COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Mexico), as of April 27, 2020; five cases were reported in Atlatahucan. Schools and many businesses were closed from mid March until June 1.[^cite_note-8] The state office of [DIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_System_for_Integral_Family_Development) sent food and water to vulnerable groups of people in eight municipalities including Atlatlahucan on May 26.[^cite_note-9] On June 2, Atlatlahucan reported twelve confirmed cases and two deaths; the reopening of the state was pushed back until at least June 13.[^cite_note-10] Atlatahucan reported 33 cases, 25 recuperations, and five deaths as of August 31.[^cite_note-11] Sixty-one cases were reported on December 27, 2020.[^cite_note-12]

## Tourist Attractions

### Ex-Convento de San Mateo Apóstol

The monastery of San Mateo Atlatahucan was founded by Fray [Jorge de Avila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_de_Avila?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the order of the [Augustinians](144980.md) (1570?), who also founded the temples and of Tlayacapan, Ocuituco, Yecapixtla, and Totolapan. All these monasteries are part of the [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasteries_on_the_slopes_of_Popocat%C3%A9petl).&#x20;

The complex is rectangular. There is an open chapel in the northwest corner built with three arches. The façade of the church has an undecorated arched door; above that is a rectangular coral window set in a niche surrounded by small pilasters. Above that, there is a classic clock dating from the latter part of the 19th century. To the rear of the church, there is a bell tower which appears to have been built later than the church, although it is in the same style.

The cloister is simple and austere, without luxury or decorations, in sharp contrast to the public areas of the church, tower, open chapel, and *capillas posas* used for unbaptized Indigenous.[^cite_note-13]

The feast of St. [Matthew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_the_Apostle) is September 21.

### Other attractions

Thirteen neighborhoods were built with their respective chapels of *Los Reyes*, *La Asunción, Santa Ana, San Sebastian, San Andrés, Santa Barbara, San Lucas, Santo Tomás, San Marcos, Santiago, San Antonio, San Juan Evangelista*, and *San Miguel*, of which only four still stand: *Capilla de los Reyes* (the Three Kings) and *Capilla de la Asunción* (the Assumption) in Atlatlhucan; *San Juan Bautista* in Texcalpan (feast day, June 24); *San Miguel Arcángel* in Tlatetelco (feast day, September 29).[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4]

Major festivals are *Carnaval* on the Fourth Sunday of Lent[^cite_note-Gob-14] and the *Festivity of Christ Appeared*. According to Catholic tradition, Jesus made twelve appearances between his Resurrection and Ascension.[^cite_note-15] This is celebrated in Atlatlalhucan in *La Cuevita* (the Little Cave).[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4] A brass band usually accompanies the *Dance of the [Chinelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinelos)* during the festivals.[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4]

## Geography

The municipality covers 71 km2 (27.4 square miles). The municipality of Atlatlahucan is based on a series of projections forming ravines that separate the hills of the municipality. The north part of the municipality and parts of the southeast are hilly, covering 7.4% of the surface area. Northern valleys cover 67%, and southern plains cover 25%.[^cite_note-enciclopedia-4]

Of the total surface, 3,000 hectares (7,400 acres) are used for agriculture and 3,990 hectares (9,860 acres) for forests. There are 1,846 ejido hectares (4,562 acres) and 3,990 communal hectares.

### Climate

The climate is considered temperate-cold, with an average annual temperature of 20°C (68°F). There is 1,005 mm<sup>3</sup> (35.6 inches) of annual precipitation.

### Hydrography

*Water resources* include the ravine of *El Salto* or Totolapan (waterfall), which joins the *Arroyo del Bosque* (stream of the woods) in [Oaxtepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxtepec), and the ravine that flows into the [Yautepec River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yautepec_River).

### Flora and fauna

The flora consists mainly of pine-oak forest and pine forest. The fauna are white-tailed deer, raccoon, skunks, squirrels, volcanic mice, puma or mountain lion, Moctezuma quail, mountain hen, bellotera pigeon, blue magpie, goldfinch, Florida mulatto, red spring, rattlesnake, rat viper, frogs, and lizards.[^cite_note-Gob-14]
