**Frankfort** is the [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) of the U.S. state of [Kentucky](16846.md). It is a [home rule-class city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kentucky_cities) and the [seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Franklin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_County,_Kentucky).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-GR62-6] As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 28,602, making it the [13th-largest city in Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Kentucky) and 4th-smallest U.S. state capital by population.[^cite_note-QF2020-7] Located along the [Kentucky River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_River) in the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South), Frankfort is the principal city of the [Frankfort, Kentucky micropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort,_Kentucky_micropolitan_area), which includes all of Franklin and [Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_County,_Kentucky) counties.

Before Frankfort was founded, the site was a [ford](30432825.md) across the Kentucky River, along one of the great [buffalo trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_trails) used as highways in [colonial America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_history_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-ky.gov2-8] English explorers first visited the area in the 1750s. The site evidently received its name after an incident in 1780, when pioneer Stephen Frank was killed in a skirmish with [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States); the crossing was named "**Frank's Ford**" in his memory.[^cite_note-ky.gov2-8] In 1786, the [Virginia legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_General_Assembly) designated 100 acres (40 hectares) as the town of Frankfort and, after Kentucky became a state in 1792, it was chosen as capital.[^cite_note-ky.gov2-8][^cite_note-\:32-9]

The city is located in the inner [Bluegrass region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_region) of Kentucky.[^cite_note-\:02-10] The Kentucky River flows through the city, making a turn as it passes through the center of town; the Downtown and South Frankfort districts are opposite one another on each side of the river. The suburban areas on either side of the river valley are known as East and West Frankfort. Frankfort has four distinct seasons; winter is normally cool with some snowfall, while summers are hot and humid.[^cite_note-NCDC2-11][^cite_note-extremes2-12]

Because of the city's location on the Kentucky River, it has flooded many times, with the two highest recorded floods occurring in [1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River_flood_of_1937#Kentucky) and 1978.[^cite_note-\:02-10] The North Frankfort levee, finished in 1969, and the South Frankfort floodwall, built in the 1990s, were constructed for flood protection.[^cite_note-\:02-10] Five bridges cross the river in downtown Frankfort, including the [St. Clair Street bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singing_Bridge) and Capitol Avenue bridge.[^cite_note-\:02-10] Notable locations include the [Kentucky State Capitol building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_State_Capitol), the Capital City Museum,[^cite_note-\:4-13] and [Fort Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hill_\(Frankfort,_Kentucky\)), a [promontory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promontory) with a view of downtown.

As of 2016, the city's largest industry was [public administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration) with 28% of the workforce.[^cite_note-\:13-14] [Manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_States) totaled over 12% of the workforce.[^cite_note-\:13-14] Frankfort is adjacent to [Interstate 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_64), and [Interstate 75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_75) is nearby; general aviation access is via the [Capital City Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_City_Airport_\(Kentucky\)), and commercial air travel is available through [Blue Grass Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Grass_Airport) in [Lexington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky).[^cite_note-\:13-14]

## History

### 18th and 19th centuries

The town of Frankfort likely received its name from an event that took place in the 1780s. Native Americans attacked a group of early European colonists from [Bryan Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky), who were on their way to make salt at Mann's Lick in Jefferson County. Pioneer Stephen Frank was killed at the Kentucky River and the settlers thereafter called the crossing "Frank's Ford". This name was later [elided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elision) to Frankfort.[^cite_note-ky.gov-15]

In 1786, [James Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wilkinson) purchased a 260-acre (110-hectare) tract of land on the north side of the Kentucky River, which developed as downtown Frankfort. He was an early promoter of Frankfort as the state capital. Wilkinson felt Frankfort would be a center of transportation using the Kentucky River to ship farm produce to the Ohio River and then to the Mississippi and on to New Orleans.

After Kentucky became [the 15th state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states) in 1792, five commissioners from various counties were appointed, on June 20, 1792, to choose a location for the capital. They were John Allen and John Edwards (both from [Bourbon County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_County,_Kentucky)), Henry Lee (from [Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_County,_Kentucky)), Thomas Kennedy (from [Madison](95567.md)), and [Robert Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Todd_\(pioneer\)) (from [Fayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayette_County,_Kentucky)). A number of communities competed for this honor, but Frankfort won. According to early histories, the offer of Andrew Holmes' log house as capitol for seven years, a number of town lots, £50 worth of locks and hinges, 10 boxes of glass, 1,500 pounds (680 kg) of nails, and \$3,000 in gold helped the decision go to Frankfort.[^cite_note-\:3-16]



Downtown Frankfort is seen in the foreground, while South Frankfort lies across the river in the background. Fort Hill is in the lower left-hand corner (1871).

Frankfort had a United States [post office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_office) by 1794, with Daniel Weisiger as postmaster. On October 1, 1794, Weisiger sent the first quarterly account to Washington.[^cite_note-ren-17]

[John Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_\(Kentucky_politician,_born_1757\)), a Virginia lawyer and statesman, built a home now called [Liberty Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Hall_\(Kentucky\)) in Frankfort in 1796. Before Kentucky statehood, he represented Virginia in the [Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress) (1777−78) and the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) (1789−91). While in Congress, he introduced the bill granting statehood to Kentucky. [After statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kentucky#1792_%E2%80%93_Admission_to_the_Union), he was elected by the state legislature as one of the state's [U.S. Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_senators_from_Kentucky).[^cite_note-18]

In 1796, the [Kentucky General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_General_Assembly) appropriated funds to provide a house to accommodate the governor; it was completed two years later. The [Old Governor's Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Governor's_Mansion_\(Frankfort,_Kentucky\)) is claimed to be the oldest official executive residence still in use in the United States. In 1829, [Gideon Shryock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_Shryock) designed the [Old Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_State_Capitol_\(Kentucky\)), Kentucky's third, in [Greek Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Revival) style. It served Kentucky as its capitol from 1830 to 1910. The separate settlement known as **South Frankfort** was annexed by the city on January 3, 1850.[^cite_note-SOS-19]

The *[Argus of Western America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argus_of_Western_America)* was published in Frankfort from 1808 until 1830.[^cite_note-20]

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the Union Army built fortifications overlooking Frankfort on what is now called [Fort Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hill_\(Frankfort,_Kentucky\)). The [Confederate Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_Army) also occupied Frankfort for a short time, starting on September 3, 1862, the only such time that Confederate forces took control of a Union capitol.[^cite_note-ky.gov-15]

The [Clinton Street High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Street_High_School), a segregated public school for African American students in Frankfort operated from either 1882 or 1884 until 1928.[^cite_note-\:0-21][^cite_note-\:1-22]

On February 3, 1900, [William Goebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Goebel) was assassinated in Frankfort while walking to the [capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_State_Capitol) on the way to the Kentucky Legislature. Former Secretary of State [Caleb Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Powers) and several others were later found guilty of a conspiracy to murder Goebel; however, all were later pardoned.[^cite_note-23]

### 20th century



View of Frankfort in 1934, Kentucky State Capitol in the foreground

The [Mayo–Underwood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo%E2%80%93Underwood_School), the successor to the Clinton Street High School, was a public school for African American students in Frankfort and operated from 1929 until 1964.[^cite_note-\:0-21][^cite_note-\:1-22] The school was torn down as part of an urban renewal plan,[^cite_note-24] and to make way for the Capital Plaza.

The Capital Plaza comprised the [Capital Plaza Office Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Plaza_Office_Tower_\(Frankfort,_Kentucky\)), the tallest building in the city, the Capital Plaza Hotel (formerly the Holiday Inn, Frankfort), and the Fountain Place Shoppes. The Capital Plaza Office Tower opened in 1972 and became a visual landmark for the center of the city. By the early 2000s, maintenance of the concrete structures had been neglected and the plaza had fallen into disrepair, with sections of the plaza closed to pedestrian activity out of concerns for safety. In 2018 the Capital Plaza was demolished[^cite_note-25] and a new state office building was constructed[^cite_note-26] to take its place. The new building was named The Mayo-Underwood State Office Building[^cite_note-27] to honor the namesake school that was razed for the sake of the original Capital Plaza.

Frankfort grew considerably with state government in the 1960s. A modern addition to the State Office Building was completed in 1967. The original building was completed in the 1930s on the location of the former Kentucky State Penitentiary. Some of the stone from the old prison was used for the walls surrounding the office building.[^cite_note-28]

### 21st century

Although there was some rapid economic and population growth in the 1960s, both tapered off in the 1980s and have remained fairly stable since that time.[^cite_note-29]

In August 2008, state government officials recommended demolition of the Capital Plaza Office Tower and redevelopment of the area over a period of years. Ten years later, the demolition of the office tower was completed on Sunday, March 11, 2018,[^cite_note-demolition-30] and was televised by [WKYT-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WKYT-TV) on [WKYT-DT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WKYT-TV), as well as streamed live on [Facebook](7529378.md). Demolition of the nearby convention center, which opened in 1972 and has hosted sporting events, concerts, and other local events, was completed in spring 2018.[^cite_note-demolition-30] State officials replaced the outdated office tower with a smaller building called the Mayo–Underwood Building (2019),[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] in order to create a more pedestrian-oriented scale at the complex, to encourage street activity.[^cite_note-33]

Frankfort is home to three [distilleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distilleries) including the [Buffalo Trace Distillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Trace_Distillery) ([Kentucky Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_whisky_brands#Kentucky_bourbon)), Castle & Key Distillery (spirits), and Three Boys Farm Distillery (bourbon and whiskey).[^cite_note-34]

In 2018, thousands of teachers protested in the city in response to [Senate Bill 151](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Kentucky_Senate_Bill_151) having been passed on March 29, 2018.[^cite_note-35] The bill was shortly overturned on December 13, 2018, by the [Kentucky Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Supreme_Court) as unconstitutional, which prevented the bill from going into effect on January 1, 2019.

## Geography



Astronaut photography of Frankfort, Kentucky, taken from the International Space Station (ISS)

Frankfort is located in the (inner) [Bluegrass region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_region) of Central Kentucky. The city is bisected by the [Kentucky River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_River), which makes an s-turn as it passes through the center of town. The river valley widens at this point, which creates four distinct parts of town. The valley within the city limits contains Downtown and South Frankfort districts, which lie opposite one another on the river. A small neighborhood with its own distinct identity, Bellepoint, is located on the west bank of the river to the north of Benson Creek, opposite the river from the "downtown" district. The suburban areas on either side of the valley are respectively referred to as the "West Side" and "East Side" (or "West Frankfort" and "East Frankfort").

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 14.6 square miles (37.8 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 14.3 square miles (37.0 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 0.3 square miles (0.78 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.

Frankfort does not have a commercial airport and travelers fly into [Blue Grass Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Grass_Airport) in [Lexington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky), the closest; [Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati/Northern_Kentucky_International_Airport) near [Covington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covington,_Kentucky) or [Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport](264229.md) in [Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky). [Capital City Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_City_Airport_\(Kentucky\)) serves general and military aviation.

### Climate

Frankfort has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) with four distinct seasons. Winter is generally cool with some snowfall. Spring and fall are both mild and relatively warm, with ample precipitation and thunderstorm activity. Summers are hot and humid.

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Frankfort had a population of 28,602 and 12,698 households. The median age was 37.9 years; 21.1% of residents were under 18 and 16.7% were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 91.9 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 88.5 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-43][^cite_note-Census2020PL-44]

99.9% of residents lived in urban areas, while 0.1% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-45]

There were 12,698 households in Frankfort, of which 27.1% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 31.8% were married-couple households, 22.8% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 36.9% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 39.1% of all households were made up of individuals and 14.3% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-43]

There were 14,221 housing units, of which 10.7% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 2.2% and the rental vacancy rate was 8.5%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-43]

### Income

The median income for a household in the city was \$50,211, and the median income for a family was \$43,949. Full-time male workers had a median income of \$37,445 versus \$34,613 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) was \$29,288. About 19.8% of families and 16.3% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 38.7% of those under age 18 and 7.5% of those age 65 or over.

### Other demographics

Frankfort is the focal point of a [micropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_micropolitan_area) consisting of Frankfort and [Franklin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_County,_Kentucky) as well as adjacent [Lawrenceburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceburg,_Kentucky) and [Anderson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_County,_Kentucky). The city is also classified in a [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) with Lexington and [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Kentucky) to the east.

Frankfort's municipal population makes it the [fourth least populous capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States#State_capitals) in the United States.[^cite_note-\:2-46]

## Education

[Kentucky State University](1912348.md) is located within the Frankfort city limits. KSU (also known as KYSU) is a public [historically black university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_university) and an 1890 [land-grant institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university).[^cite_note-educ1-54]

Two public [school districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) serve the city,[^cite_note-Censusschoolmap-55] with three public high schools within the city limits.[^cite_note-educ1-54]

Frankfort Independent School District serves the downtown neighborhoods including Downtown, South Frankfort, Bellepoint and Tanglewood. FIS operates The Early Learning Academy (a [preschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool)), Second Street School (primary and middle grades), [Frankfort High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort_High_School_\(Kentucky\)), and Panther Transition Academy (a non-traditional [high school](554992.md) program).[^cite_note-56]

Franklin County Public Schools serves the rest of the city and county, including seven [elementary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_school) (Bridgeport, Collins Lane, Early Learning Village, Elkhorn, Hearn, Peaks Mill, Westridge), two [middle schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school#United_States) (Bondurant, Elkhorn), and two high schools ([Franklin County High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_County_High_School_\(Kentucky\)) and [Western Hills High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hills_High_School_\(Frankfort,_Kentucky\))).[^cite_note-57]

There are several [private schools](612857.md) in the area, including Capital Day School, Frankfort Christian Academy, and Good Shepherd Catholic School.

Frankfort has a [lending library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_library), [Paul Sawyier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sawyier) Public Library, named in 1965 after the watercolor artist Paul Sawyier whose many paintings document the history of the area.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

## Transportation

[Frankfort Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort_Transit) provides deviated fixed-route and demand-response transit service throughout the city.

[U.S. Route 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_60) and [U.S. Route 460](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_460) pass east–west through Frankfort. [U.S. Route 127](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_127) and [U.S. Route 421](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_421) pass north–south through Frankfort. [Interstate 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_64_in_Kentucky) passes to the south of the city.

[Capital City Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_City_Airport_\(Kentucky\)), a public use airport, is one mile (1.5 kilometers) southwest of the central business district of Frankfort. The nearest airport with commercial flights is [Blue Grass Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Grass_Airport), 22 miles (35 kilometers) southeast of Frankfort.

Frankfort Union Station was a medium scale hub [passenger train station](26161590.md) for north-central Kentucky. It served the [Chesapeake & Ohio Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_&_Ohio_Railway), the [Frankfort and Cincinnati Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort_and_Cincinnati_Railroad) and the [Louisville and Nashville Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville_and_Nashville_Railroad).[^cite_note-61] Until the mid-1950s, Union Station served [Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky)-[Ashland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland,_Kentucky) sections of the C\&O's *[Sportsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsman_\(train\)).* Until 1971, the C\&O's *[George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_\(train\))* stopped in Frankfort.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]
