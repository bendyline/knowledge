**Lansana Kouyaté** (born 15 July 1950)[^cite_note-1] is a [Guinean](12176.md) politician and diplomat who served as [Prime Minister of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_government_of_Guinea) from 2007 to 2008. Previously he was Executive Secretary of the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS) from 1997 to 2002.

## Background and earlier career

Kouyaté was born in [Koba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koba,_Guinea), [Guinea](12176.md), then a [French](5843419.md) colony. He studied administration at the [University of Conakry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Conakry) before joining the [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service). In 1976, he was appointed as Director of Labour, then the following year, moved to become Director of Trade, Prices and Statistics, where he had responsibility for state-owned companies. <https://web.archive.org/web/20070928105618/http://62.210.150.98/lusaka/newslatf.asp?code=eng004651&dte=04%2F06%2F01>

In 1982, Kouyaté worked on a [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) development project, then moved to the [diplomatic](82433.md) service, joining Guinea's delegation in [Côte d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire). In 1985, he returned to the Foreign Ministry in [Conakry](56638.md) as head of African and [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) affairs. Two years later, he became Guinea's ambassador to [Egypt](8087628.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Sudan](27421.md), [Syria](7515849.md) and [Turkey](11125639.md). In 1992, he became Guinea's Permanent Representative at the [United Nations](31769.md), where he became Vice President of the [United Nations Economic and Social Council](31958.md). <https://web.archive.org/web/20070928105618/http://62.210.150.98/lusaka/newslatf.asp?code=eng004651&dte=04%2F06%2F01>

In 1993, he was appointed as the Deputy Special Representative of the Secretary-General in [Somalia](27358.md) for the [UNOSOM II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOSOM_II) mission, then in February 1994 became the Acting Representative. <https://www.un.org/Depts/DPKO/Missions/unosom2b.htm> In June 1994, he became the Assistant Secretary-General in the UN Department of Political Affairs, <https://www.un.org/ecosocdev/geninfo/afrec/vol11no3/watch.htm> one of his first missions being a tour around ECOWAS member states to discuss the situation in [Liberia](17791.md). He continued his involvement in discussions to build regional support for a resolution of the [First Liberian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Liberian_Civil_War). <https://www.un.org/Depts/dpko/dpko/co_mission/unomilFT.htm> He left this job in September 1997 to become the Executive Secretary of the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS), a post he held until February 2002.

During his time at ECOWAS, Kouyaté was awarded the [Legion d'Honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_d'Honneur) (Commander), the [African Star of Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Star_of_Liberia?action=edit\&redlink=1) and was made a Commander of the [Mono Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mono_Order?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Togo](30138.md). <https://web.archive.org/web/20070928105618/http://62.210.150.98/lusaka/newslatf.asp?code=eng004651&dte=04%2F06%2F01>

## Prime minister

As a result of a [general strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Guinean_general_strike) in early 2007, Kouyaté was nominated for the post of [Prime Minister of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Guinea) on 26 February 2007. He was selected by [President](392253.md) [Lansana Conté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansana_Cont%C3%A9) from a list provided by trade union leaders.[^cite_note-2] On 1 March, he was sworn in as Prime Minister at a ceremony in [Conakry](56638.md); Conté was not present.[^cite_note-3] His government was named on 28 March, composed of 19 ministers and three secretaries of state; it contained none of the members of the old government.[^cite_note-4]

On 5 December 2007, a decree restructuring ministries increased the powers of the Secretary-General of the Presidency at the expense of those of the Prime Minister, and on 3 January 2008 Conté dismissed and replaced [Justin Morel Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Morel_Junior?action=edit\&redlink=1), the Minister of Communication and Government Spokesman, without consulting Kouyaté. On 4 January, Kouyaté demanded that Morel be restored to his position and said that he would not sit at the Council of Ministers with Morel's replacement. Labor unions announced plans to begin a new "unlimited general strike" on 10 January, demanding that Conté's agreement with the unions be properly implemented and that Morel be restored.[^cite_note-5] Kouyaté's government chose to attempt to resolve the situation through dialogue with Conté in hopes of maintaining peace.[^cite_note-6] On 9 January, the unions withdrew their call for a strike.[^cite_note-7]

Tensions between Kouyaté and Conté increased over the decision by Kouyaté's government to repatriate Chantal Cole, Conté's advisor in charge of communications at the presidential palace, to France; in addition, they disagreed over Kouyaté's decision to allow Libyans to manage luxury hotels.[^cite_note-8]

## Dismissal and subsequent events

In a decree read on state television on 20 May 2008, Kouyaté was dismissed by Conté and replaced by [Ahmed Tidiane Souaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Tidiane_Souar%C3%A9).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Fires-11] This was considered surprising; it had been generally believed that Kouyaté would not be dismissed prior to the planned [December 2008 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Guinean_parliamentary_election?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-12] Following the announcement, protests were reported in Conakry and [Kouroussa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouroussa), although the cities were reportedly calm again by 21 May;[^cite_note-Fires-11] protests were also reported in [Kankan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankan) on 20 and 21 May.[^cite_note-13]

Kouyaté was widely considered a disappointment in his role as Prime Minister, and his unpopularity meant that his dismissal was not greeted with major unrest of the kind that led to his appointment a year earlier; in particular, his time in office was associated with rising [food prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_prices), deepening the country's economic problems. He was also accused of having presidential ambitions. Because he had not consulted with the opposition when forming his government and had not invited opposition politicians to participate in it, the opposition welcomed his dismissal and urged Souaré to avoid his mistakes. The Secretary-General of the [Union of Democratic Forces of Guinea](28077599.md) (UFDG), Amadou Oury Bah, described Kouyaté as "a danger to the democratic process".[^cite_note-14] One union leader, Rabiatou Serah Diallo, said that until the composition of Souaré's government was announced, she had nothing to say.[^cite_note-Fires-11] Souaré was sworn in on 23 May; Kouyaté was not present at the ceremony,[^cite_note-Friday-15][^cite_note-16] as he was unable to leave his home due to a crowd of supporters outside of it.[^cite_note-Friday-15]

Many soldiers, dissatisfied over their failure to receive wage arrears, were unhappy with Kouyaté's dismissal, feeling that it left them with no one to whom they could address their grievances. On 26 May 2008, unrest broke out among the soldiers as they demanded their wage arrears.[^cite_note-17]
