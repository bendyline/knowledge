**Israel–Uganda relations** refers to the [current and historical relationship](429154.md) between [Israel](9282173.md) and [Uganda](31816.md). Neither country has a resident ambassador. Uganda has a non-resident ambassador in [Cairo](6293.md).

## History

In 1903, the [British Uganda Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Uganda_Programme) proposed Uganda as a [Homeland for the Jewish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people). The British plan was promoted to [Theodor Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl) by Colonial Secretary [Joseph Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Chamberlain) as a potential refuge for Jews fleeing antisemitic violence in Eastern Europe, particularly following the 1903 anti-Jewish [Kishinev pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishinev_pogrom). Opposition from both [Zionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist) and white settlers in East Africa ultimately led to the withdrawal of the offer in 1905.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

### 1967–70



Prime Minister [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol) welcomed in Uganda by [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin), 1966

Under [Milton Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Obote), whose first term in office ran from 1966 to 1971, Uganda initially helped Israel support the [Anyanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyanya) rebels in [Southern Sudan](32350676.md) during their [long war with the north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sudanese_Civil_War). After the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md), however, Obote wanted to make peace with the [Khartoum](17320.md) government and cut off support for the rebels.&#x20;

Israeli diplomat [Uri Lubrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Lubrani) served as Israeli ambassador to Uganda from1964 until 1967.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] While Obote grew increasingly hostile to Israel, his army commander General [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin), shared an ethnic connection with the people of Southern Sudan and a personal affinity for Israel, and bypassed Obote. Lubrani developed a close relationship with Idi Amin when the two of them survived a military aircraft crash together in Uganda, and Amin publicly declared Lubrani to be his "[blood brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_brother)."[^cite_note-5]

### 1971

When Idi Amin overthrew Obote in 1971, he resumed backing the rebels and continued the military relationship with Israel.[^cite_note-6]

Amin visited Israel in 1971 and was toasted by Israeli Defence Minister [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan), celebrating their military cooperation.[^cite_note-7] The Israelis supplied and trained much of the [Uganda Army (1962–1971) Ugandan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Army_\(1962%E2%80%931971\)_Ugandan_Army?action=edit\&redlink=1) and undertook several construction projects in the country, including upgrading the [Entebbe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe_International_Airport).[^cite_note-8]

### 1972–92

But in February 1972, Amin suddenly decided to visit [Libya](17633.md) (while traveling on an Israeli jet) and meet with leader Colonel [Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddafi), where he was bribed with a massive cash and financial aid offer from Libya.[^cite_note-9] After the visit, Amin became vocally anti-Israel.

By March 1972, Amin had ordered all Israelis expelled from Uganda. One report says Amin became enraged over Israel's refusal to supply Uganda with jets for a war with neighboring [Tanzania](30118.md).[^cite_note-TallJstor-10] Amin became an outspoken critic of Israel.[^cite_note-11] By the end of the month, all 470 Israelis had gone, including some who had driven all their valuable construction equipment across the border into Kenya.[^cite_note-12]

### 1993–present

Following the 1993 [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) between Israel and the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLO), on July 29, 1994, Uganda and Israel officially restored diplomatic relations.[President Museveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Museveni) began making state visits to Jerusalem (in 2003 and 2011).Private Israeli security firms, agricultural experts, and technology companies slowly returned to the country.The Ugandan military began sending officers to Israel for elite counter-terrorism and intelligence training.[^cite_note-alj-13] In July 2016 when [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) visited Uganda, it was the first visit by a sitting Israeli Prime Minister to sub-Saharan Africa in decades.[^cite_note-alj-13]

In 2016, an Israeli company, the [Haifa](55606.md)-based Israeli firm Tzamir Architects and City Planning, was chosen by the Ugandan government to make a national master plan for the overhaul and development of Uganda, on the basis of the Israeli [National Outline Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Outline_Plan) system, namely National Outline Plan 35.[^cite_note-ynetnews-uganda-14] The master planning initiative advanced from a blueprint into official state policy, Uganda’s first-ever National Physical Development Plan (NPDP) 2018–2040, focusing on balancing urban growth corridors with protected agricultural blocks and natural reserves.[^cite_note-15]

## Agricultural cooperation

In a joint Israeli–Ugandan project, a professor from the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem)'s Faculty of Agriculture conducted a survey of [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria) with a Ugandan colleague from [Makerere University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere_University). They found that [Nile perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_perch), introduced by the British sixty years ago, have decimated native fish populations, leading to malnutrition in the lakeside communities.[^cite_note-Azoulay-16] She helped to set up artificial fish ponds to raise [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp), which had disappeared from the local diet. The [United States Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) sponsored the digging of the ponds and sent villagers to [Kibbutz](106200.md) [HaMa'apil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMa'apil) in [Emek Hefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emek_Hefer) to learn spawning techniques. Graduates of the training program established carp farms.[^cite_note-Azoulay-16]

## Diplomatic visits



[Miriam Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Eshkol) dancing with Idi Amin, 1966

In 1966, Prime Minister [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol) made a state visit to Uganda.

Ugandan President [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) has visited Israel twice since coming to power in 1986; in 2003, he met with Prime Minister [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) and Foreign Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) to primarily discuss arms deals between the two countries. In 2011, Museveni again visited Israel. His visit was facilitated by former Pensioner Affairs Minister and head of the [Mossad](27619007.md) turned international businessman [Rafi Eitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafi_Eitan), who had several investments in Uganda including a cattle farm. Eitan and Museveni are close friends. In February 2020, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu visited Uganda.[^cite_note-Ravid-17] In July 2016, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had also visited Uganda.[^cite_note-18]

## Entebbe hijacking



Memorial plaque at Entebbe International Airport

[Operation Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Entebbe) was a [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) hostage-rescue mission carried out by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) at [Entebbe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe_International_Airport) in [Uganda](31816.md) on the night of 3 July and early morning of 4 July 1976.[^cite_note-HOSTAGES_FREED_AS_ISRAELIS_RAID_UGANDA_AIRPORT-19] In the wake of the [hijacking](2075.md) of [Air France](14998012.md) Flight 139 and the hijackers' threats to kill the [hostages](146794.md) if their prisoner release demands were not met, a plan was drawn up to airlift the hostages to safety.[^cite_note-Mossad_took_photos-20] These plans took into account the likelihood of armed resistance from Ugandan military troops.[^cite_note-Back_to_Entebbe-21] Originally [codenamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_name) *Operation Thunderbolt* by the IDF, the operation was retroactively renamed *Operation Yonatan* in memory of the [Sayeret Matkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayeret_Matkal) commander [Lieutenant Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Colonel) [Yonatan "Yoni" Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonatan_Netanyahu), who was killed by a Ugandan sniper.[^cite_note-The_Knesset_at_Sixty-22] Three hostages, seven hijackers, and 45 Ugandan soldiers were killed. In addition to the death of Netanyahu, five other Israeli [commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commando) were wounded.[^cite_note-Hostage_Rescue_at_the_Raid_on_Entebbe-23] A fourth hostage was [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Dora_Bloch) by Ugandan army officers at a nearby hospital.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-24]

## Jewish community in Uganda



Abayudaya prayer in [Putti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putti,_Uganda) village

The [Abayudaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abayudaya) are a Jewish community of [Baganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baganda) people in eastern [Uganda](31816.md) who live in several villages near the town of [Mbale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbale). They are devout in their religious practices, keeping [Shabbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shomer_Shabbat) and keeping [kosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashrut). The community is composed of [converts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Judaism) and the descendants of converts. The majority of the Abayudaya are recognized by the Reform and Conservative sects of Judaism. However, the villagers of [Putti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putti,_Uganda) are seeking an Orthodox conversion and observe [Orthodox Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism).[^cite_note-tjoa-25] Their population is estimated at approximately 1,100, having once been as large as 3,000 (prior to the persecutions of the [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin) regime); like their neighbors, they are subsistence farmers. Most Abayudaya are of [Bagwere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagwere) origin, except for those from [Namutumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namutumba) who are [Basoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basoga). They speak [Luganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luganda_language), [Lusoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusoga_language) or [Lugwere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugwere), although some have learned [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) as well.

In November 2017 [Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad) opened a permanent Jewish center in Uganda, becoming the 100th country to host a Chabad center.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]
