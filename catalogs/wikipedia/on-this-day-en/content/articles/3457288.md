The **Kan-Etsu Expressway** (関越自動車道, *Kan'etsu Jidōsha-dō*) is a national [expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressways_of_Japan) in Japan. It is owned and managed by [East Nippon Expressway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Nippon_Expressway_Company).

## Naming

Kan-Etsu (関越) is the *[kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji)* acronym of [Kantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) (**関**東) and the old [Echigo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echigo_Province) (**越**後国) comprising modern-day [Niigata Prefecture](181660.md).

Officially, the Kan-Etsu consists of two routes. Both begin in [Tokyo](30057.md) and end in Niigata Prefecture. The Kan-Etsu Expressway Niigata Route consists of the entire Kan-Etsu Expressway from Nerima to Nagaoka, as well as the [Hokuriku Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuriku_Expressway) beyond Nagaoka to its terminus in the city of [Niigata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niigata,_Niigata). The Kan-Etsu Expressway Jōetsu Route is concurrent with the Niigata Route until Fujioka Junction, where it branches off as the [Jōshin-etsu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dshin-etsu_Expressway) and traverses [Nagano Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to its terminus in [Jōetsu, Niigata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Detsu,_Niigata).[^cite_note-3]

## Route description



Kan-Etsu Expressway in [Minamiuonuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minamiuonuma,_Niigata)



[Kan-Etsu Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan-Etsu_Tunnel)

The expressway begins in [Nerima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerima,_Tokyo) Ward in the north of [Tokyo](30057.md); the Kan-etsu is the only national expressway linking Tokyo that does not have a direct connection with the urban [Shuto Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuto_Expressway) network. A junction with the [Tokyo Gaikan Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Gaikan_Expressway) near the origin links the Kan-etsu with other expressways serving northern parts of the Tokyo urban area. From here the expressway follows a roughly northwesterly course to its terminus in Niigata Prefecture, passing through central areas of [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture) and [Gunma Prefecture](212632.md). In Gunma the Kan-etsu Expressway provides access to Nagano Prefecture by way of the Jōshin-etsu Expressway at Fujioka Junction, and the completion of the [Kita-Kantō Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kita-Kant%C5%8D_Expressway) in 2011[^cite_note-4] facilitated access to [Tochigi Prefecture](180960.md) and [Ibaraki Prefecture](181029.md). The mountainous area separating Gunma and Niigata Prefectures is traversed by the [Kan-Etsu Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan-Etsu_Tunnel), the second longest road tunnel in Japan.[^cite_note-5] The expressway then passes through southern Niigata Prefecture before terminating at a junction with the [Hokuriku Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuriku_Expressway) in [Nagaoka](6793151.md). The route is six lanes from [Ōizumi Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cizumi_Junction) to Shibukawa-Ikaho Interchange, and all other sections are four lanes. The expressway parallels [National Route 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_17) and the [Jōetsu Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Detsu_Shinkansen) of [East Japan Railway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Japan_Railway_Company) for most of its length.[^cite_note-6]

## History

The first section opened in 1971 and the entire route was opened to traffic in 1985.[^cite_note-7]

On the night of 16 December 2020, about 1,000 vehicles were trapped on the expressway after a trailer blocked it off due to it becoming stuck in the snow. In response, the company that manages the route supplied drivers who were stuck on the road until the next morning.[^cite_note-8] The [Japan Ground Self-Defense Force](2978818.md) was also deployed to the expressway to aid in the distribution of supplies to the stranded drivers.[^cite_note-9]

In December 2025, a crash involving 50 vehicles in snowy conditions killed 2 people.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]
