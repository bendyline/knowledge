**Benguet** ([/bɛŋˈɡɛt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), officially the **Province of Benguet** ([Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language): *Probinsya ne Benguet*_;_ [Kankanaey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_language): *Probinsyan di Benguet*; [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language): *Luyag/Probinsia na Benguet*; [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Probinsia ti Benguet*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lalawigan ng Benguet*), is a landlocked [province](165100.md) of the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the southern tip of the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region) in the island of [Luzon](69375.md). Its [capital](181337.md) is [La Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trinidad,_Benguet).

The [highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland) province is known as the *Salad Bowl of the Philippines* due to its huge production of upland vegetables.[^cite_note-NSCBGov-1][^cite_note-ItsMoreFun-2][^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6]

Situated within the interior of Benguet is the [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Classification) of [Baguio](162480.md), which is administered independent from the province and also its largest city and regional center of Cordillera Administrative Region.

## Etymology

Hispanicized rendering of ***benget***, [Kankanaey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_language) word for "edge." This was the original name of the settlement at the edge of a swamp formed by the [Balili River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balili_River) flooding the flat valley floor.[^cite_note-7]

## History

### Early history

The [mountainous area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland) now covered by Benguet is generally presumed to have been settled from at least the 14th century by tribes coming from the surrounding lowlands, lured by the abundance of natural resources such as gold, hides, and wax. Two of these groups, the [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi) and the [Kankanaey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_people), are dominant ethnolinguistic groups of the area. In the pre-conquest period, these tribes enjoyed flourishing trade with lowland groups immediately to their west and south, such as the [Pangasinans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people). Governor Juan "Oraa" Cariño (1913–1918), one of Benguet’s governors, was the first Filipino governor.

The Kankanaey occupied the northern highlands of the province, while the Ibaloy occupied the southern portion, while all Igorots practiced [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) and [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_worship). Rituals were proscribed by the priests, *mambunong*. The economy of the region was based on [rice terraces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_terrace), [root crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_crop) [swidden farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swidden_farming), livestock raising, hunting, foraging, plus the mining and trading of gold. These Igorot gold mines were located in Suyoc, Tabio, Acupan, and Antamok. [Gold panning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_panning) took place in [placer deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placer_deposit) along the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River), the [Bued River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bued_River), the Suyoc River, and the [Amburayan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amburayan_River). Gold was also mined from [lode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lode) [veins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vein_\(geology\)) within [andesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite) and [diorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diorite). Gold in Mankayan was associated with copper. Gold mining resulted in social stratification, with the upper class consisting of the mine owners, the *baknang*, followed by the gold workers, or *abiteg*, and then the *bagaen*, or [slave class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattel_slavery). These slaves were made up of war captives, and their children. Gold mines were inheritable kinship property, while original ownership was bestowed on those individuals discovering the gold location, and then developing the property.[^cite_note-PhilStudies-8][^cite_note-Scott-9]<sup>: 183 </sup>

### Spanish colonial era

At the beginning of the [Spanish Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521-1898\)), colonisers heard of the rich gold mines in the mountains and attempted to colonize the highlands, but failed. In 1572, Spanish *conquistador* [Juan de Salcedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Salcedo) led a small expedition into the southern part of Benguet, but the natives forced it to retreat.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6] The first major expedition into the mountains occurred in 1620, when Spanish explorers went into the [La Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trinidad,_Benguet) Valley, followed by a second expedition in 1623, and a third in 1624. This was the last attempt to occupy the Baguio gold mines by the Spanish until the Galvey expeditions (1829-1839).[^cite_note-PhilStudies-8]<sup>: 473–475 </sup>

In the 1800s, Spanish colonizers made more serious attempts such as expeditions under Col. Guillermo Galvey[^cite_note-10]<sup>: 280 </sup> and succeeded in establishing a presence in the La Trinidad Valley, named after Galvey's wife.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6]

This area later became a district of the new province of [*La Montañosa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province) (or *La Montaña*)[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-MtProv-11][^cite_note-NSCB-12] in 1846. Eight years later, in 1854, Benguet became a separate *comandancia politico-militar*. Parts of the present province were established as component territories of other *comandancias* such as Lepanto and [Amburayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amburayan).[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6]

### American colonial era

When the [Americans took control of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898-1946\)), they established local civil governments in many parts of the country. American civilian government was established in Benguet on November 23, 1900, through *Act No. 48*, with [Canadian](19851291.md) journalist *H.P. Whitmarsh* appointed as the province's first governor.[^cite_note-DILG-CAR-13]

Upon the American colonial government enacting *Act No. 1876* on August 18, 1908, which created [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province) from areas of the old *La Montañosa*, Benguet (along with [Amburayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amburayan), [Apayao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apayao), Bontoc, [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao), [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_Province), and Lepanto) became sub-provinces of this new province.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6][^cite_note-PhilLaw-16] A year later in 1909, the township of [Baguio](162480.md) was abolished upon its conversion into a chartered city. In 1920, portions of the sub-provinces of Amburayan and Lepanto were incorporated into Benguet.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6]

Mining companies started operating in the province in the 1930s. This brought jobs, and many lowlanders migrated to Benguet, especially in towns surrounding the gold mines, such as [Itogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itogon,_Benguet), [Mankayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankayan,_Benguet) and [Tuba](2137478.md).[^cite_note-DILG-CAR-Tuba-17]

### Japanese occupation

See also: [Battle of Baguio (1945)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baguio_\(1945\))

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War), Igorot guerrillas and the combined Filipino and American forces fought [Japanese soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_Army) during the final days of the war in 1945.

As part of the [Japanese defence of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_\(1944%E2%80%931945\)), General [Tomoyuki Yamashita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoyuki_Yamashita) situated his military headquarters in Baguio, from where he organised the Japanese resistance on [Lúzon](69375.md) from December 1944 to April 1945, before he relocated to the town of [Bambang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambang,_Nueva_Vizcaya).[^cite_note-18]

### Contemporary history

On June 18, 1966, the huge [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province) was split into four provinces with the enactment of *[Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Acts_of_the_Philippines) No. 4695*. The four provinces were Benguet, [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province), [Kalinga-Apayao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga-Apayao) and [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao).[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6][^cite_note-ChanRobles-19][^cite_note-Benguet_Gov-20]

On June 22, 1973, as part of the *Integrated Organization Plan* of President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), Benguet was placed under the jurisdiction of the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region).[^cite_note-ChanRobles-PD224-21]

When the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region) was established by President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) through *[Executive Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(Philippines\)) 220* on July 15, 1987, Benguet was made one of its provinces.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6][^cite_note-CountrySTAT-22][^cite_note-DeptAgri-23]

## Geology



Dalupirip schist in [Itogon, Benguet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itogon,_Benguet)



Sandstone beds comprising the Zigzag formation as seen along the Bued River, Camp 6, Baguio City

The [Baguio Mining District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Baguio) consists of [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous) [ophiolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiolite) [basement rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basement_rock). These are overlain by [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) [back arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_arc) [tholeiitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tholeiitic_magma_series) Pugo [Metavolcanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metavolcanic_rock). These grade into the Dalupirip [Schist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schist), which is separated from the [Early Oligocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Oligocene)-[Early Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Miocene) [sedimentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary) Zigzag [Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(geology\)) by an [unconformity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconformity). The Zigzag is composed of deep [depositional environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depositional_environment) [interbedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbedded) green [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), red [siltstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siltstone), [oligomicitc conglomerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(geology\)), and minor [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) units. The late [Early Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Miocene)-early [Middle Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Miocene) [reefal](240844.md) Kennon Limestone overlays the Zigzag. The [Late Oligocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Oligocene)-[Early Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Miocene) Agno [Batholith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batholith) [intrudes](1106531.md) the Pugo and Zigzag. The Middle-Late Miocene Klondyke Formation was deposited during Central Cordillera [geologic uplift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_uplift), consisting of [coarse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_size) [clastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clastic_rock), polymictic conglomerates, sandstones, and vitric (glassy) [tuffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff), with minor shales and siltstones. Gold mineralization occurred with [dioritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diorite) and [gabbroic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabbro) intrusions, and [basaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) to [andesitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite) lavas, including the Monglo [adakite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adakite). The [Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliocene) Baguio Formation consists of conglomerates interbedded with lava flows and [pyroclastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_rock). [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) gold-copper mineralization occurred with [calc-alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calc-alkaline) and dioritic and [dacitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacite) adakitic intrusions. This mineralization characterizes the Philex Mine.[^cite_note-Polve-24]

The main gold [base metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_metal) [mineralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralization_\(geology\)) zone is approximately 8 km wide, trends north south for tens of kilometers, and is centrally located at 16° 20' to 16° 26' N latitude and 120° 37' 30" to 120° 43' 30" E longitude. The western boundary of this zone is defined by a volcanic belt, just west of Baguio, while the eastern boundary is defined by an intrusive belt, just east of the Itogon and Antamok mines. The Agno quartz diorite batholith is further east near [Binga Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binga_Dam). Key mines, starting from the south, include the Philex ([Cu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper)), Acupan ([Au](12240.md)), Itogon (Au), Black Mt. (Cu), Benguet Exploration (Cu), Atok (Au), Antamok (Cu and Au), and Baguio Gold (Au), where Baguio Gold and Antamok are east of Baguio. Continuing northwards, key mines include the King Solomon (Cu), St. Nino (Cu), Boneng (Cu), Lobo (Cu), Gambang (Cu and Au), and Lepanto (Cu and Au). By 1979, underground mining had reached Level 1850 in the Antamok Mine.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-people-26][^cite_note-Scott-9]

## Geography



Highland landscape at [Bokod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokod,_Benguet)

Benguet covers an area of 2,769.08 square kilometres (1,069.15 sq mi) occupying the southwestern tip of the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region). If Baguio is included for geographical purposes, the total area of Benguet is 2,826.59 square kilometres (1,091.35 sq mi).[^cite_note-nscb-27]

The province is bordered on the northeast by [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province) and [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao), on the southeast by [Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Vizcaya), on the south by [Pangasinan](1691800.md), on the west by [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union), and on the northwest by [Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Sur).

Situated within the [Cordillera mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central,_Luzon), Benguet is dominantly mountainous. [Mount Pulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pulag), the highest in [Luzon](69375.md) is located within [Kabayan](2137467.md). The mountains form the headwaters of several [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River), the major ones which include the [Agno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River), [Amburayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amburayan_River), [Bued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bued_River), Bakun, [Balili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balili_River) and the Asin. Some of these run through [river valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_valley) or [gorges](78478.md).

Several natural lakes, small in size, are found within the [hinterlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterlands). The largest are the "Four Lakes" in Kabayan; Lake Bulalacao, Lake Detepngepos, Lake Incolos and Lake Tabeyo.[^cite_note-PIA-KabayanLakes-28]

The province is the location of several conservation areas, the largest of which are the [Upper Agno River Basin Resource Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Agno_River_Basin_Resource_Reserve), Mount Pulag National Park, Mount Data National Park and [Lower Agno Watershed Forest Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Agno_Watershed_Forest_Reserve).

### Climate



Foggy forest in Benguet

Most of Benguet falls under the [subtropical highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) climate ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwb*) zone, experiencing annual average highs of 25.3 °C (77.5 °F) in April and lows of 13.3 °C (55.9 °F) in January.[^cite_note-Storm246-BenguetPH-29]

The province suffers from crop damage resulting from seasonal [frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost) during the cold months of December to March, especially in high-altitude towns such as Atok, Buguias, Mankayan and Kibungan.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-Inquirer,_Frost-31] In February 2007, Benguet suffered crop damage due to temperatures reaching as low as 7 °C (45 °F).[^cite_note-ManilaTimes-32]

### Administrative divisions

Benguet comprises 13 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_municipality), all encompassed by [double legislative districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_district_of_Benguet).[^cite_note-nscb-27]

The [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Classification) of [Baguio](162480.md), although administratively independent from Benguet, is situated in the interior of the province, surrounded by the municipalities of [La Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trinidad,_Benguet), [Itogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itogon,_Benguet), [Sablan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sablan,_Benguet) and [Tuba](2137478.md). The city used to be part of the province but became independent when the city's charter was enacted in 1909.

The population of Benguet in the 2024 census was 473,190 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–CAR-35] making it the most populous province in the region. It had a density of 170 inhabitants per square kilometre or 440 inhabitants per square mile.&#x20;

In the May 2000 census, Benguet had a total population of 330,129. ### Languages

Benguet residents generally speak their own languages in addition to [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), which are used for trade and commerce. The [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) tribe speak [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language), which is similar to [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language), while the [Kankanaey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_people) have their [own eponymous language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankana-ey_language), which is related to the [Bontoc language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bontoc_language).

The [SIL Ethnologue database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue) classifies the languages under the *[South-Central Cordilleran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South%E2%80%93Central_Cordilleran_languages)* branch. [Nabaloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language) (named in the database as *Ibaloi*) is part of the *Southern Cordilleran* branch which also includes [Pangasinense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language). The [Kankanaey language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_language) is under the *Central Cordilleran* branch, which also includes [Bontoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bontoc_language) and [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao_language).[^cite_note-EthnologueCom-Austronesian-44]

Benguet is a major producer of highland vegetables in the country.

[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) are the major industries in Benguet. Its cool climate and high altitude has made it an ideal place for producing highland vegetables. Benguet is often called the *Salad Bowl of the Philippines*.[^cite_note-ItsMoreFun-2][^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6][^cite_note-NCCA-41] The La Trinidad Vegetable Trading Post in the capital town of La Trinidad serves as the hub of vegetable trading in the province, attracting farmers, merchants and traders from the local community and the Philippines' different provinces.[^cite_note-BAR-59] Major crops produced which include [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [Baguio beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_bean), [peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pea), [strawberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry), [cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage), [broccoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broccoli), [cauliflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauliflower), [lettuce](57079.md), [sayote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayote) and [carrots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrot) are shipped throughout the country.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6][^cite_note-CNNPhil-HighlandVegetables-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-SunStar-Vegetables-62]

[Horticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horticulture) and [floriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floriculture) are practiced in the province.[^cite_note-ManilaBulletin-63] The province supplies flowers to the different municipalities, including Baguio, as well as to [Metro Manila](72340.md), including other parts of Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-InterAksyon-65][^cite_note-Sun.Star-66] [Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose) are produced, particularly in Barangay Bahong in La Trinidad, earning the barangay the title *Rose Capital of the Philippines*.[^cite_note-SunStar-BahongFlowers-67] Apisang (scientific name: *[Pittosporum resiniferum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittosporum_resiniferum)*), a plant [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) to the Philippines (as well as Malaysia), is grown in the municipalities of Kapangan and Kibungan as a potential alternative source of fuel and energy, rivaling the [jatropha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatropha) [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) plant.[^cite_note-PhilStar-68][^cite_note-Inquirer-69]

Other agricultural-related activities are [monggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mung_bean) processing, [fruit preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_preserves), [peanut brittle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_brittle) manufacturing, [broom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broom) making and [basket weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basket_weaving).[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6][^cite_note-ChoosePhil-70]

Mining is a major industry in Benguet, which is one of the country's leading [gold](12240.md) producers.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-6] The [Benguet Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguet_Corporation), the first and oldest mining company in the Philippines, has extracted gold, [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite) in [Itogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itogon,_Benguet) since 1903.[^cite_note-BengCorp-71][^cite_note-72]

Other mineral deposits are [silver](27119.md), copper, [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite), and [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone). [Silver](27119.md) smithing is a large industry in Benguet, and many entrepreneurs sell silver works at lower prices in [Baguio](162480.md) compared to [Manila](184334.md). In 2006, revenues from mining reached 4 billion pesos from just two (Lepanto Consolidated Mining Corporation and Philex Mines) of many mining firms operating in the province. The province's mining vigor has never translated into better quality of life of the Benguet people, simply because a bulk of the mining firm's taxes are not paid directly to the province.[^cite_note-Inquirer-Itogon-73] The two mining corporations, like many others around the country, have principal offices in the City of Makati, a set-up that makes Makati the prime mining tax beneficiary.

The location of Baguio within Benguet draws many tourists from the lowlands. Often, people who go to Baguio also explore the province, especially the strawberry fields in La Trinidad.

## Education

Higher education in the province is centered in the capital town of [La Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trinidad,_Benguet),[^cite_note-JosephGoshenLand-74] which houses the *Main Campus* of the only university in Benguet, the [Benguet State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguet_State_University) (BSU). The university has two [satellite campuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_campus) in two municipalities; the *Buguias Campus* at [Loo, Buguias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buguias,_Benguet), and the *Bokod Campus* at [Ambangeg, Bokod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokod,_Benguet).[^cite_note-75]

Other educational institutions which include Advocates Academic College, BVS Colleges, Cordillera Career Development College, King's College of the Philippines, [Philippine College of Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_College_of_Ministry), [Philippine Nazarene College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Nazarene_College), as well as the [Cordillera Regional Science High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Regional_Science_High_School), the Benguet National High School (with several annexes) and the Benguet [SPED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_education) Center are also situated within La Trinidad.

The independent chartered city of Baguio hosts several higher education institutions, which include most notably the [University of the Philippines Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Baguio), [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(Philippines\)), [University of the Cordilleras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Cordilleras) (formerly *Baguio Colleges Foundation*), [University of Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Baguio), Pines City Colleges, [Baguio Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Central_University), and Baguio School of Business and Technology, attracting students from across the Philippines.

## Tourism



Mount Pulag [sea of clouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_clouds)

The province is a major tourist destination in the country,[^cite_note-NSCBGov-1] with most of its tourist spots centered on culture and nature.[^cite_note-SunStar3-76] [Baguio](162480.md)'s location within the province provides a boost to the tourism industry of the province.[^cite_note-SunStar-77] Notable interesting places in the province include [Mount Pulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pulag) along [Kabayan](2137467.md), [Kennon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennon_Road) in [Tuba](2137478.md), [Ambuklao Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambuklao_Dam) in [Bokod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokod,_Benguet), [Binga Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binga_Dam) in [Itogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itogon,_Benguet), strawberry and flower farms in La Trinidad, and the Palina and Naguey rice terraces in [Atok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atok,_Benguet).

Tuba and [Tublay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tublay,_Benguet) hot springs are usually flocked by local tourists from the neighboring provinces. Vegetable terraces can be seen along the [Halsema Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halsema_Highway), especially during the growing season. Kabayan is known for its centuries-old [mummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabayan_Mummies), while [Buguias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buguias,_Benguet) is visited for its hot springs and the Apo Anno.
