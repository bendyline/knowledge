The **Nepali Army** (**NA**),**[^cite_note-1]** formerly known as the **Royal Nepali Army**,**[^cite_note-2]** is the [land warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare) branch of the [Nepali Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Armed_Forces). It traces its origins to 1559 with the establishment of the [Gorkha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_kingdom), where it was initially formed as the **Gorkhali Army**,**[^cite_note-3]** a term historically associated with the [Gurkhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha).

It was known as the Royal Nepali Army (RNA) following the [Unification of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Nepal), when the Gorkha Kingdom expanded its territory to include the whole country, by conquering and annexing the other states in the region, resulting in the establishment of a single [united Hindu monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nepal) over all of [Nepal](171166.md). It was officially renamed simply to the *Nepali Army* on 28 May 2008, upon the abolition of the 240-year-old [Nepalese monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_monarchy), and of the 449-year-old rule of the [Shah dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_dynasty), shortly after the end of the [Maoist insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Civil_War).

The Nepali Army has participated in various conflicts throughout its history, going as far back as the Nepali unification campaign launched by [Prithvi Narayan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithvi_Narayan_Shah) of the Gorkha Kingdom. It has engaged in an extensive number of battles within [South Asia](21566765.md), and continues to take part in global conflicts as part of [United Nations peacekeeping](15652027.md) coalitions. The Nepali Army is headquartered in [Kathmandu](17168.md) and the incumbent [Chief of Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Nepal\)) is General [Ashok Raj Sigdel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashok_Raj_Sigdel).

## Nepalese Armed Forces



Roundel of Nepal



A rescued old woman being assisted by the Nepali Armed Forces personnel while alighting from a Mi-17 helicopter

The **Nepali Armed Forces**, also known as **Nepalese Armed Forces**, are the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) forces of [Nepal](171166.md). Composed primarily of the ground-based Nepali Army, the Nepalese Armed Forces also operates the smaller [Nepalese Army Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Army_Air_Service) designed to support army operations and provide close light combat support.

### Army pilot training school

The Nepal Army Air Service has operated a flying and helicopter pilot training school since 2004 within the 11 Brigade. It is the only helicopter pilot training school in [Nepal](171166.md) (there is a fixed-wing pilot training school in [Bharatpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatpur,_Nepal) by a private pilot training school). This school produces both army air service and civilian pilots. The school provides the [Mil Mi-17](1493374.md), and [Eurocopter Ecureuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Ecureuil) helicopter flight training.

## History

- Nepali soldiers; drawing by Gustave Le Bon, 1885

  Nepali soldiers; drawing by [Gustave Le Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Le_Bon), 1885

The [Nepal unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Nepal) campaign was a turning point in the history of the Nepali army. Since unification was not possible without a strong army, the management of the armed forces had to be exceptional. Apart from the standard [Malla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malla_dynasty_\(Nepal\))-era temples in [Kathmandu](17168.md), the army organized itself in [Gorkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_Municipality). After the [Gorkhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) troops captured [Nuwakot](2919311.md), the hilly northern part of Kathmandu (Kantipur) in 1744, the Gorkhali armed forces came to be known as the Royal Nepali Army.

Their performance impressed their enemies so much that the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) started recruiting Nepali troops into their forces. British troops called the new soldiers "Gurkhas". The [Nepal–Sikh war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal%E2%80%93Sikh_war) began shortly after, in 1809 and the [Anglo-Nepalese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Nepalese_War) in 1814. It became even more involved in the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) until independence, remaining an independent entity (since it was a protected state of the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) until 1947, though The Crown had officially recognized its independence in 1923). In 1946, the Royal Nepali Army troops were led by [Commanding General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_General) [Baber Shamsher Jang Bahadur Rana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baber_Shumsher_Jung_Bahadur_Rana) at the Victory Parade in [London](17867.md).

Prior to 2006, the Royal Nepali Army was under the control of the [King of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Nepal). Following the [2006 Democracy Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Democracy_Movement) (Nepali: लोकतन्त्र आन्दोलन, romanized: *Loktantra Āndolan*) on 18 May, a bill was passed by the Nepali parliament curtailing royal power, which included renaming the army to simply the Nepali Army.[^cite_note-4] In 2004, Nepal spent \$99.2 million on its military (1.5% of its GDP). Between 2002 and 2006, the RNA was involved in the [Nepali Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_Civil_War). They were also used to quell pro-democracy protesters during the 2006 democracy movement.

## Organization

The Nepali Army has 96,500 infantry army and air service members protecting the sovereignty of [Nepal](171166.md). In August 2018, [The Himalayan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Himalayan_Times) estimated total army forces to be around 96,000[^cite_note-thehimalayantimes.com-5] while [The Kathmandu Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kathmandu_Post) estimated it to be 92,000.[^cite_note-6]

### Supreme Command

&#x20;Article 144 of Interim Constitution of Nepal states that the President of Nepal is the Supreme Commander-in-Chief of Nepal Army. Currently as the [President of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Nepal) [Ram Chandra Poudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Chandra_Poudel) who assumed office on 13 March 2023,[^cite_note-nepalnews.com-7] is the current supreme commander of [Nepal Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Army).

Until 2006, the King of Nepal (monarchy abolished) was in control of all military forces in the country. The National Army was renamed from Royal Nepalese Army to Nepalese Army after the recent national change from a monarchy to its suspension, following 9 point proclamation[^cite_note-8]on 4th Jestha 2063 B.S., following 2nd People movement(also known as Jana Andolan II, in Local terminology, in Nepal)[^cite_note-9]

### National Defence Council

Nepal's Interim Constitution's Article 145 has envisioned National Defence Council which includes Prime Minister, Defence Minister, Home Minister and other three minister appointed by Prime Minister which recommends to the Council of Ministers on mobilization, operation and use of the Nepal Army. Upon Council of Ministers recommendation, President authorizes mobilization, operation and use of Nepal Army.

Before the Interim Constitution replaced Constitution of Kingdom of Nepal in 1990, the 1990 constitution had a provision for the defense council. This Council used to have three members: the Prime Minister, the Defence Minister and the Chief of the Army Staff. In accordance with the Constitution, the King (as Supreme Commander) used to "operate and use" the "Royal Nepal Army on the recommendation" of this council.

### Chiefs of the Nepali Army

For list of Army Chiefs, see [Chief of the Nepalese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Nepalese_Army).

During the [Shah monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_dynasty), leadership of the Nepali Army was mainly concentrated among a small number of [aristocratic families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy) within the broader [Chhetri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhetri) community of the late [Gorkha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_Kingdom) and early [Kingdom of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nepal), such as the [Pande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pande_family), [Kunwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunwar_family), [Basnyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basnyat_family), and [Thapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thapa_dynasty) clans, before the rise of the [Rana regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_dynasty).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdhikari2015154-10] During the Rana regime, the Ranas made the position [hereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity) within their family.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdhikari2015154-10]

The first chief of the Gorkhali and later Nepali army was King [Prithvi Narayan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithvi_Narayan_Shah), who organized and commanded the army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdhikari2015153-11] The first non-royal army chief was [Kalu Pande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalu_Pande), a [Mulkaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulkaji), who played a significant role in the [Unification of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Nepal) campaigns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdhikari2015153-11]

[Bhimsen Thapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimsen_Thapa), who served as the second [Mukhtiyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhtiyar) of Nepal from 1806 to 1837, became the first person to formally hold the title of [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) as head of the Nepali army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdhikari2015155-12] King [Rajendra Bikram Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Bikram_Shah) appointed him to the position in recognition of his long service to the nation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPradhan2012149-13] However, on 14 June 1837, the King assumed direct command of the battalions previously under various [courtiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtier), and became the Commander-in-Chief.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcharya2012215-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENepal2007105-15]

Following the incarceration of the [Thapa family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thapa_dynasty) in 1837, a new government was formed with [Ranga Nath Poudyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranga_Nath_Poudyal) as the Mukhtiyar, and [Dalbhanjan Pande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalbhanjan_Pande) and [Rana Jang Pande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_Jang_Pande) as joint heads of military administrations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENepal2007106-16] Rana Jang Pande was removed from the position three months later in October 1837.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcharya2012160-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOldfield1880311-18][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENepal2007109-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPradhan2012164-20]

During Bhimsen Thapa’s tenure as Mukhtiyar, the title of Commander-in-Chief was introduced for the first time to formally designate the head of the army. In 1979, [Singha Bahadur Basnyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singha_Bahadur_Basnyat) changed the title to [Chief of the Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Army_Staff_\(Nepal\)) (CoAS). Since 1950, it has been a tradition for the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) to confer the honorary rank of [General of the Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Army_Staff_\(India\)) upon the chief of the Nepali Army.[^cite_note-21]

#### Battle against Mir Qasim

The fortress of Makawanpur has a historical and military significance for the Nepali. It was here that the Nepali defeated superior forces of Mir Qasim in 1763 and seized 500 guns and two cannons. Later on, these weapons were used by Nepali troops and four companies were established regular, namely, Srinath, Kalibox, Barda Bahadur (Bardabahini) and Sabuj. (Purano) Gorakh Company was established a few months later. It was the first rank and file system beginning a proper organizational history for the Royal Nepali Army. The battle against Mir Qasim troops was the first battle of the Royal Nepali Army against a foreign power.

Sardar Nandu Shah was the fortress Commander of Makawanpur with 400 troops, some guns and home-made traditional weapons like Dhanu, Khukuri, Talwar, Ghuyatro etc. They devised different hit-and-run strategies to surprise the enemy. A spoiling attack base was set up on the Taplakhar mountain ridge for night operations.

Mir Qasim's renowned warrior, Gurgin Khan was the commander on the other side with approximately 2,500 troops with cannons, guns, ammunition and a very good logistics back up. Their attack base was at the bottom of the Makawanpur Gadhi hill. They had planned a night attack. When the enemy's heavy forces marched in December 1762 and arrived at Harnamadi in January 1763, they found all the local houses already evacuated and the area short of food provisions. Makawanpur Gadhi was on top of a mountain, about nine kilometers uphill from the Harnamadi area. Although the Nepali had physically occupied all the fortresses en route, the enemy was able to initially push them back to the Makawanpur Gadhi area.

About 300 enemy launched a strong attack on 20 January 1763 putting the Nepali still more on the defensive. But they were totally surprised when they were resting in Taplakhar, as Kaji [Vamsharaj Pande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vamsharaj_Pande) led a downhill attack on them Kaju Naharsigh Basnyat led an uphill attack from below them and Nandu Shah led a frontal attack. The smooth coordination among the three, leading their, by now battle-hardened, troops in the dark of the night, led the bewildered enemy to scatter. About 1700 of them died and 30 Nepali soldiers were lost in that battle. The Nepali captured 500 rifles and two cannons with other military equipment. More importantly, the battle led to the beginning of a proper organization of the Royal Nepali Army.[^cite_note-22]

#### Disarmament of the Khampas

In 1974, The Royal Nepali Army (RNA) was mobilized to [disarm the Tibetan Khampas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament_of_the_Khampas?action=edit\&redlink=1) who had been using Nepali soil to engage guerilla war against the Chinese forces. The Khampas had secretly created their base in Mustang (north-west Nepal) and were operating from there against China. The RNA, under immense diplomatic pressure from China and the international community, moved nine infantry units towards the Khampa post in Mustang and gave them an ultimatum to either disarm themselves and surrender, or face consequences. The terms and conditions of their surrender was that they would be given Nepali citizenship, land, and some money. The Khampa commander Wang Di agreed to surrender but eventually fled the camp. He was later killed in Doti, far-western Nepal by RNA forces while trying to loot a Nepal Police post. This was first time that the RNA was mobilized in such a large number domestically.

### International operations



A Nepali UN peacekeeper



Women peacekeepers from [MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO)'s Nepali Battalion in [Djugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djugu) in 2021



A member of the Nepali Quick Reactionary Force (QRF) stands ready with a variant of the [IMI Galil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMI_Galil).

Nepal Army's long association with UN Peace Support Operations began with the deployment of five Military Observers in the Middle East, Lebanon (UNOGIL/ [United Nations Observation Group in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Observation_Group_in_Lebanon)) in 1958. And the first Nepali contingent, Purano Gorakh battalion was deployed in Egypt in 1974. Nepal's participation in the UN peacekeeping operations spans a period of 50 years covering 42 Nepal army-involved UN Missions, the latest being UNSMIL in Libya, in which over 60,652 Nepali soldiers have served in support of UN peacekeeping endeavors. The Nepal Army has contributed Force Commanders, military contingents, military observers, and staff officers. Nepali troops have taken part in some of the most difficult operations, and have suffered casualties in the service of the UN. To date, the number of those lost on duty with the UN is 74, while 77 were seriously wounded.

Its most significant contribution has been of ensuring peace and stability in Africa. It has demonstrated its capacity of sustaining large troop commitments over prolonged periods. Presently, Nepal is ranked as the second largest troop-contributing country (TCC) to the UN.

The Nepali Army has contributed more than 100,000 [peacekeepers](84617.md) to a variety of [United Nations](31769.md)-sponsored peacekeeping missions such as:

- [United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon](787766.md) (UNIFIL),

- [UNOSOMII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_Somalia_II) the [United Nations Protection Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Protection_Force) (UNPROFOR), UN Operational Mission Somalia II,

- [MINUSTAH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNMIH) the [United Nations Mission in Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_Haiti),

- [UNAMSIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAMSIL) – an 800-man battalion was sent to serve in the peacekeeping mission in [Sierra Leone](27308.md),

- [UNMIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNMIS) – The Nepali Army sent a protection company of 200 personnel for the United Nations Mission In Sudan. The Redeployment Coordination Headquarters (RCHQ) at [Kassala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassala) was also manned by the Nepali contingent. The RCHQ was intended to monitor withdrawals from the eastern sectors of the UNMIS area under the Sudan [Comprehensive Peace Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Peace_Accord).

### US-Nepal military relations



Mahabir Ranger with a U.S. soldier

See also: [Nepal–United States relations](15993658.md)

The US-Nepali military relationship focuses on support for democratic institutions, [civilian control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military), and the professional military ethic. Both countries have had extensive contact over the years. Nepali Army units have served with distinction alongside American forces in places such as Haiti, Iraq, and Somalia.

US-Nepali military engagement continues today through the [International Military Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Education_and_Training) (IMET) program, Enhanced International Peacekeeping Capabilities (EIPC) program, Global Peace Operations Initiative (GPOI), and various conferences and seminars. The US military sends many Nepali Army officers to America to attend military schools, such as the [Command and General Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_General_Staff_College) and the [US Army War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_War_College). The IMET budget for FY2001 was \$220,000.

The EIPC program is an inter-agency program between the [US Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Defense) and [US Department of State](31975.md) to increase the pool of international peacekeepers and to promote interoperability. Nepal received about \$1.9 million in EIPC funding.

The US [Commander in Chief, Pacific Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_in_Chief,_Pacific_Command) (CINCPAC) coordinates military engagement with Nepal through the [Office of Defense Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Defense_Cooperation) (ODC). The ODC Nepal is located in the American Embassy in [Kathmandu](17168.md).

### India-Nepal military relations

India has agreed to resume military aid to Nepal. The aid was in the pipeline before India imposed an embargo in February 2005 following the seizure of power by the then King Gyanendra.Since 1950, the Chief of the Army Staff of Nepal is also awarded the honorary Chief of the Army Staff of India and vice -versa in recognition of the mutual harmonious relationship between the two armies of different countries.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### China-Nepal military relations

In 2009, [People's Republic of China](5405.md) pledged military aid worth Rs100 million to Nepal.[^cite_note-28]

## Units

The first four army units—Shreenath, Kali Buksh, Barda Bahadur, and Sabuj companies—were founded in August 1762 by King [Prithvi Narayan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithvi_Narayan_Shah). These units were composed primarily of members from the dominant clans of the [Gorkha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_kingdom), such as [Gurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurung), [Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magars), [Chhetri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhetri), [Bahun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahun), and [Thakuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thakuri), well before the [Unification of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Nepal). ## Female participation in Nepali Army

The unofficial participation of women in Nepal Army was first during the [Anglo-Nepalese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Nepalese_War) on [Battle of Nalapani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nalapani). Battle of Nalapani was the first battle of the Anglo-Nepalese War of 1814–1816, fought between the forces of the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) and Nepal, then ruled by the [Gorkha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_Kingdom). Nepalese women were heavily involved in this battle supporting the male [Gurkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) warriors. With no firearms in hand, Nepalese women fought British troops with stones and wood.

According to the Nepal Army YouTube channel programme Nepali Senama Mahila Sahabhagita (documentary) - Episode 405, the official participation of women in the Nepal Army started in 1961 in the post of Nurse. Now the regular forces also include a significant number of female soldiers holding key appointments and commands.[^cite_note-30] The timeline of official women's participation in the Nepal Army is as follow:

- 1961 - Nurses

- 1965 - Parachute folding women team

- 1969 - Medical doctors

- 1998 - Lawyers

- 2004 - Engineering

- 2011 - Aviation

As of 2019 there were 3758 female Junior Commission, Non-Commission Officers and other ranks in general service and 907 in technical service. There were also 183 female officers in the general service and 214 female technical officers.[^cite_note-31]

## Equipment

The majority of equipment used by the army is imported from other countries. India is the army's largest supplier of arms and ammunition as well as other logistical equipment, which are often furnished under generous military grants.[^cite_note-Survey-32] Germany, the United States, Belgium, Israel, and South Korea have also either supplied or offered arms to the Nepali Army.[^cite_note-Small1-33]

The army's first standard rifle was the Belgian [FN FAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_FAL), which it adopted in 1960.[^cite_note-Small1-33] Nepali FALs were later complemented by [unlicensed, Indian-manufactured variants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishapore_1A1) of the same weapon, as well its British counterpart, the [L1A1 Self-Loading Rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L1A1_Self-Loading_Rifle).[^cite_note-Small1-33] Beginning in 2002 these were officially supplemented in army service by the American [M-16 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-16_rifle), which took the FAL's place as the army's standard service rifle.[^cite_note-Small1-33] Nevertheless, the FAL and its respective variants remain the single most prolific weapon in Nepali army service, with thousands of second-hand examples being supplied by India as late as 2005.[^cite_note-Survey-32]

### Shape of the emblem

The emblem of the Nepali Army is centered on a bold red [Shatkona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatkona), formed by two interlocking triangles, one pointing upward and the other downward, representing the eternal union of [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) and [Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakti), indicating total awareness, protection, and presence in all dimensions. The upward triangle symbolizes *Shiva*: pure consciousness, discipline, transcendence, and the element of fire, while the downward triangle represents *Shakti*: creation, nature, manifestation, and the element of water. Together, they express the harmony and balance of opposing yet complementary forces.



[Shatkona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatkona)

### Red colour

The [Shatkona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatkona) is rendered in vivid red colour, symbolizing power ([Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakti)), sacrifice, and the blood shed in defense of the nation. In [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), red also represents [divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity) [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy), reinforcing the presence of *Shakti* and the dynamic force that sustains and protects.

### Central symbol

At the center of the [Shatkona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatkona) is a golden depiction of [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva)'s attributes: the [Trishul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishula) (weapon) and the [Damaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaru) (musical instrument).

- The **Trishul** stands vertically as the central axis, symbolizing willpower ([iccha-shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iccha-shakti)), action ([kriyā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriy%C4%81)), and knowledge ([jñāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B1%C4%81na)), as well as control over creation, preservation, and destruction. Its upright form conveys authority, discipline, and command.

  

  Shiva with his [Trishul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishula)

- The **Damaru** is placed horizontally across the *Trishul*, representing the primordial sound, the very first cosmic vibration that existed at the beginning of the universe. It signifies [rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm), timing, and coordination, essential to both cosmic order and military structure.



A [Damaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaru)

- The [golden colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_\(color\)) of these elements contrasts with the red background, emphasizing [divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity), purity, and supreme authority.

### Overall symbolism

The emblem as a whole represents a disciplined force grounded in balance and higher principles. The [Shatkona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatkona) reflects order and completeness, while the central symbols of [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) represent controlled power guided by [wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom) and purpose.

Symbolically, the Nepali Army can be compared to Shiva in his cosmic form as [Nataraja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nataraja). Just as Shiva performs the [Tandav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandava), the dance that destroys ignorance and restores cosmic balance, the Nepali army represents disciplined and decisive force in action. The [Trishul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishula) reflects precision and authority, while the [Damaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaru) symbolizes coordination and unity of action. Like Shiva's cosmic dance, which restores harmony rather than chaos, the Nepali army's role is ultimately protective: to maintain stability, defend the nation, and ensure continuity.

## Gurkhas

Nepal is also notable for the [Gurkhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkhas). Significant sections of the [British Army](4887.md) and [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) are recruited from Nepal. This arrangement comes from the days of the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company)'s rule of [India](14533.md) when Company troops tried to invade Nepal and were beaten back. Both sides were impressed with the other, and Gurkhas were recruited into the company's forces. The Gurkhas remained loyal during the [Indian Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mutiny) of 1857 and were kept on in the Indian Army thereafter. Upon Indian independence in 1947, some units went to British service and some to Indian service, with a [Britain-India-Nepal Tripartite Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain-India-Nepal_Tripartite_Agreement) signed between the three nations. The Gurkhas are feared troops, and their signature weapon is the [khukuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukri).
