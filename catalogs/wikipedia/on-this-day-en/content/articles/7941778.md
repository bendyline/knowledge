**Colombians** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Colombianos*) are people identified with the country of [Colombia](5222.md). This connection may be residential, legal, historical or cultural. For most Colombians, several (or all) of these connections exist and are collectively the source of their being *Colombian*.

Colombia is considered to be one of the most [multiethnic societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicultural) in the world, home to people of various ethnic, religious and national origins. Many Colombians have varying degrees of [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people), [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora), [Arab](2185.md), [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_ethnicity) and [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora) ancestry.[^cite_note-29]

The majority of the Colombian population is [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo_Colombians) and [Castizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castizo), being the mixed descendants of [Indigenous peoples](4053513.md), [Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_people), and [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_people), especially [Iberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula).[^cite_note-\:03-30] Following the initial period of [Spanish conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest) and immigration, different waves of immigration and settlement of non-Indigenous peoples took place over the course of nearly six centuries and continue today. Elements of Native American and more recent immigrant customs, languages and religions have combined to form the culture of Colombia and thus a modern Colombian identity.[^cite_note-\:0-31]

### European Colombians

Main article: [White Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Colombians)

"British Colombian" redirects here. For something from British Columbia, see [British Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbian).

Most of Colombia's population descends from European immigration in the mid 16th to late 20th centuries. The greatest waves of European immigration to Colombia can generally be divided into three time periods: the 1820s-1850s, which brought hundreds of immigrants mainly from [Spain](26667.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Germany](11867.md) (including [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews)); the 1880s to 1910s, which brought many immigrants from [France](5843419.md), [Portugal](23033.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Astro-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [Denmark](76972.md), [Croatia](5573.md), and [Switzerland](26748.md); and the 1920s-1960s, the last great wave of European immigration to Colombia, which brought many [British](19097669.md) (including [Irish](775859.md)) immigrants, as well as other European groups such as the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_\(people\)), [Russian](59510.md), [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), and other Eastern European immigrants who primarily settled in Colombia's great urban centers. These immigrants came to Colombia attracted by the country's growing population and business opportunities. In addition to these waves of immigration, a great number of [Jews](25955086.md) fled to Colombia during and after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), seeking to escape violence in Europe. Immigrants went mostly to the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_region_of_Colombia) and [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_natural_region) regions.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] There are smaller numbers of [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Empire), [Swiss](26748.md), [Austrians](26964606.md), [Danish](76972.md), [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgians), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Colombians), [Hungarian](17106358.md), [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians_in_South_America), [Lithuanian](17675.md), [Ukrainian](145144.md), [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs), [Greek](42056.md) and [Croatian](5573.md) communities that immigrated during the Second World War and the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Mestizo Colombians

Main article: [Mestizo Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo_Colombians)

Estimates of the [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo_Colombians) population, people of mixed European (mostly [Spanish](963152.md)), [Indigenous](4053513.md), and [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_people) ancestry in Colombia vary as the national census does not include Mestizo as an ethnic option. According to the 2018 census, the population of people who did not identify with any ethnic group, being mostly White or Mestizo, made up 87% of the Colombian population, while an estimated 40% of Colombians were Mestizo or mixed race.[^cite_note-41] According to a genetic study published in *[PLOS Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_Genetics)*, people who autoidentified as "Mestizo" in [Colombia](5222.md) show a tri-hybrid ancestry profile characterized by approximately **62% European**, **28% Indigenous American**, and **6% African** ancestry.[^cite_note-PLOSGenetics2014-42]

### Indigenous Colombians

Main article: [Indigenous peoples in Colombia](4053513.md)



The [Wayuu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayuu_people) represent the largest indigenous group in Colombia.

Originally, Colombia's territory was inhabited entirely by [Indigenous](4053513.md) groups. Colombia's indigenous cultures evolved from three main groups—the [Quimbaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quimbaya), who inhabited the western slopes of the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\)); the [Chibcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibcha_terrane); and the [Kalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalina_people). The [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca_people) culture, a subset of the larger Chibcha ethnic group, were famous for their use of gold and responsible for the legend of [El Dorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dorado). Today, Indigenous people comprise roughly around 10% of the population in Colombia.[^cite_note-dane.gov.co-43][^cite_note-Raza/Etnia_a_la_que_pertenece-32] More than fifty different indigenous ethnic groups inhabit Colombia. Most of them speak languages belonging to the [Chibchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibchan_languages) and [Cariban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariban_languages) language families.

Historically, there are 567 reserves (*resguardos*) established for Indigenous peoples which are inhabited by more than 800,000 people. The [1991 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991) established that their native languages are official in their territories, and most of them have bilingual education systems, teaching both native languages and Spanish. Some of the largest indigenous groups in Colombia are the [Wayuu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayuu_people),[^cite_note-EPM-44] [Zenú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen%C3%BA), [Pastos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capul%C3%AD_culture), [Embera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ember%C3%A1_people), and [Páez](2478404.md). The [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) with the biggest indigenous populations are [Cauca](549662.md), [La Guajira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guajira_Department), [Nariño](549702.md), [Córdoba](549670.md) and [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre_Department).[^cite_note-dane.gov.co-43]

### Afro-Colombians

Main article: [Afro-Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Colombians)



[Afro-Colombian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Colombian) children

Also known as "Afro", or "Afro-colombianos" (in Spanish). According to the 2018 census, they are 5.34% of country population,[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] while genetic studies have obtained between 6.6%,[^cite_note-Jazlyn_A_2018-47] 9.2,[^cite_note-48] and 11%[^cite_note-\:03-30] of [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_People) DNA in the Colombian population. Also the percentage and numbers of Afro-Colombians can vary depending on the region, being the majority population in the [Pacific Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific/Choc%C3%B3_natural_region), frequently found in the [Caribbean Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_region_of_Colombia) but a minority in the [Andean Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_natural_region), [Orinoquia Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoqu%C3%ADa_natural_region) and [Amazon Region](9299263.md).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Colombia has the fourth-largest [African diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora) on the planet after the [Brazil](3383.md), the [United States](3434750.md), and [Haiti](13373.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

### Asian Colombians

Main article: [Asian Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Colombians)

[Asian Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Colombians) ([Spanish](26825.md): *colombianos asiáticos*; Latin American Spanish: [\[/kolombjanos/ /aˈsjatikos/\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) are [Asian](689.md) immigrants in [Colombia](5222.md) and their descendants. The majority of Asian Colombians are of [Chinese](372983.md) and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) descent,[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] with a smaller portion being of [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans) descent. There are also notable [South Asian](21566765.md), [Southeast Asian](28741.md), [Arab](2185.md), and [Middle Eastern](19323.md) descending populations.[^cite_note-Asian_Colombians_\:0-56][^cite_note-Asian_Colombians_\:1-57]

A study by [Latinobarómetro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinobar%C3%B3metro) in 2023 estimated that 0.4% of Colombians are Asian, this would equate to around 200,000 of Colombia's population of approximately 50 million people.[^cite_note-Asian_Colombians_2023est-58]



The Colombian singer [Shakira](6479315.md) is of [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people), Spanish and [Italian](385155.md) descent.

#### Arab Colombians

Main article: [Arab Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Colombians)

Most of whom were described as "Syrian-Lebanese" migrants established themselves in the [Caribbean Region of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Region_of_Colombia), in cities and towns near the basin of the [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River) such as [Maicao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maicao), [Riohacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riohacha), [Santa Marta](547214.md), [Lorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Lorica), [Fundación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundaci%C3%B3n), [Aracataca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aracataca), [Ayapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayapel), [Calamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calamar,_Bol%C3%ADvar), [Ciénaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ci%C3%A9naga,_Magdalena), [Cereté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceret%C3%A9), [Montería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monter%C3%ADa), [Valledupar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valledupar), [Sincelejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sincelejo) and [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla). The population later expanded to other areas, and by 1945, there were Arab Middle Easterners moving inland to areas such as [Ocaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oca%C3%B1a,_Norte_de_Santander), [Cúcuta](623811.md), [Barrancabermeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrancabermeja), [Ibagué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibagu%C3%A9), [Girardot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girardot,_Cundinamarca), [Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda,_Tolima), [Tunja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunja), [Villavicencio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villavicencio), [Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pereira,_Colombia), [Soatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soat%C3%A1), [Neiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiva,_Colombia), [Buga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buga,_Valle_del_Cauca), [Chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaparral,_Tolima) and [Chinácota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin%C3%A1cota).[^cite_note-Arab_Colombians_Posada-59]

The five major cities where the [Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) [Middle Eastern](19323.md) population was present were [Santa Marta](547214.md), [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla), [Cartagena](63927.md), [Bogotá](211271.md) and [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali). Most arrived as members of the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) and [Eastern Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic) churches, but the majority became [Roman Catholic](606848.md). Estimates of the number of immigrants entering the country in 1945 vary from 40,000 to 50,000; most of these immigrants were Christian and a minority were Muslim.[^cite_note-Arab_Colombians_Posada-59]

Many Arabs adapted their names and [surnames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname) to the [Spanish language](26825.md) as a way to adapt more quickly in the communities where they arrived. For example, people of Arab origin took surnames such as [Guerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerra) (originally [Harb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harb_\(surname\))), [Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom%C3%ADnguez) (Ñeca), [Durán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duran_\(surname\)) (Doura), Lara (Larach), and Cristo (Salibe).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

### Jewish Colombians

Main article: [History of the Jews in Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Colombia)

There are about 8,000 [Colombians of Jewish origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Colombia) who practice [Judaism](15624.md), most of them live in [Bogotá](211271.md). Colombia's Jewish community includes Sephardi Jews from countries such as Syria and Turkey also immigrated to the country and run their independent religious organizations. The Confederación de Comunidades Judías de Colombia coordinates Jews and institutions that practice the religion.

## Immigrant groups

Main article: [Immigration to Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Colombia)

Because of its strategic location, Colombia has received several immigration waves during its history. Most of these immigrants have settled in the Caribbean Coast; [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla) (the largest city in the Colombian Caribbean Coast) and other Caribbean cities have the largest population of Lebanese, German, British, French, Italian, Irish and [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) descendants. There are also important communities of [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_people) and [Chinese](372983.md) descendants in the Andean Region and Caribbean Coast especially in [Medellin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn), [Bogota](211271.md), [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali), [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla) and [Cartagena](63927.md). Most immigrants are Venezuelans, they are evenly distributed throughout the country.[^cite_note-63]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Colombia)

The educational experience of many Colombian children begins with attendance at a preschool academy until age five (*Educación preescolar*). Basic education (*Educación básica*) is compulsory by law.[^cite_note-66] It has two stages: Primary basic education (*Educación básica primaria*) which goes from first to fifth grade – children from six to ten years old, and Secondary basic education (*Educación básica secundaria*), which goes from sixth to ninth grade. Basic education is followed by Middle vocational education (*Educación media vocacional*) that comprises the tenth and eleventh grades. It may have different vocational training modalities or specialties (academic, technical, business, and so on.) according to the curriculum adopted by each school.

After the successful completion of all the basic and middle education years, a high-school diploma is awarded. The high-school graduate is known as a *bachiller*, because secondary basic school and middle education are traditionally considered together as a unit called *bachillerato* (sixth to eleventh grade). Students in their final year of middle education take the [ICFES test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICFES_Examination) (now renamed Saber 11) in order to gain access to higher education (*Educación superior*). This higher education includes undergraduate professional studies, technical, technological and intermediate professional education, and post-graduate studies.

*Bachilleres* (high-school graduates) may enter into a professional undergraduate career program offered by a university; these programs last up to five years (or less for technical, technological and intermediate professional education, and post-graduate studies), even as much to six to seven years for some careers, such as medicine. In Colombia, there is not an institution such as college; students go directly into a career program at a university or any other educational institution to obtain a professional, technical or technological title. Once graduated from the university, people are granted a (professional, technical or technological) diploma and licensed (if required) to practice the career they have chosen. For some professional career programs, students are required to take the Saber-Pro test, in their final year of undergraduate academic education.[^cite_note-sistema_educativo-67]

Public spending on education as a proportion of gross domestic product in 2012 was 4.4%. This represented 15.8% of total government expenditure. In 2012, the primary and secondary gross enrolment ratios stood at 106.9% and 92.8% respectively. School-life expectancy was 13.2 years. A total of 93.6% of the population aged 15 and older were recorded as literate, including 98.2% of those aged 15–24.[^cite_note-UNESCO-68]

## Religion

Main articles: [Religion in Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Colombia) and [Freedom of religion in Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion_in_Colombia)

The [National Administrative Department of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Administrative_Department_of_Statistics) (DANE) does not collect religious statistics, and accurate reports are difficult to obtain. However, based on various studies and a survey, about 90% of the population adheres to [Christianity](5211.md), the majority of which (70.9%) are [Roman Catholic](606848.md), while a significant minority (16.7%) adhere to [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) (primarily [Evangelicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism)). Some 4.7% of the population is [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist) or [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic), while 3.5% claim to believe in God but do not follow a specific religion. 1.8% of Colombians adhere to [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) and [Adventism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventism) and less than 1% adhere to other religions, such as [Islam](6037917.md), [Judaism](15624.md), [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Mormonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Hare Krishna movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare_Krishna_movement), [Rastafari movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari_movement), [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md), and spiritual studies. The remaining people either did not respond or replied that they did not know. In addition to the above statistics, 35.9% of Colombians reported that they did not practice their faith actively.[^cite_note-Religion-69][^cite_note-Religion2-70][^cite_note-Religion_in_Latin_America-71] 1,519,562 people in Colombia, or around 3% of the population reported following an [Indigenous religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism).

While Colombia remains a mostly Roman Catholic country by [baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) numbers, the 1991 Colombian constitution guarantees freedom and equality of religion.[^cite_note-72]
