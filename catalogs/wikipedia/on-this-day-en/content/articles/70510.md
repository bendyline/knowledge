**Telenor ASA** (Norwegian pronunciation: [\[ˈtêːlənuːr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Norwegian) or [\[tɛləˈnuːr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Norwegian))[^cite_note-2] is a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians) majority [state-owned](204768.md) multinational [telecommunications](33094374.md) company headquartered in [Fornebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fornebu), [Bærum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A6rum), west of [Oslo](22309.md). It is one of the [world's largest mobile telecommunications companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mobile_network_operators) with operations worldwide, mainly focused in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) and Asia. It has extensive broadband and TV distribution operations in four [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) and a 10-year-old research and business line for [machine-to-machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_to_machine) technology. Telenor owns networks in eight countries.[^cite_note-Global_presence-3]&#x20;

Telenor is listed on the [Oslo Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Stock_Exchange) and had a market capitalization in November 2015 of 225 billion [kr](64149.md), making it the third largest company listed on the Oslo Stock Exchange after [DNB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNB_ASA) and [Equinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinor) (previously known as Statoil).[^cite_note-Shares_overview_Oslo_Børs-4]&#x20;

### Telegraph

Telenor was established in 1855 as a state-operated [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly) provider of [telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph) services and was named **Telegrafverket**. The initial planning for a telegraph service in Norway was launched within the [Royal Norwegian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Navy) in 1848. By 1852, the plans were made public and the [Parliament of Norway](172897.md) decided to plan for the construction of a telegraph system throughout the country. Televerket began by connecting [Christiania](22309.md) (now Oslo) to [Sweden](5058739.md) (Norway was at that time in a [union with Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden-Norway)) as well as Christiania and [Drammen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen_\(town\)). By 1857, telegraph services had reached [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_\(city\)) on the west coast via [Sørlandet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8rlandet) on the south coast, and by 1871, it had reached [Kirkenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkenes) on the far north coast. Cable connections were connected to [Denmark](76972.md) in 1867 and to [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) in 1869. The telegraph was important for merchant marines who could now use the electric telegraph to instantly communicate between different locations, and get an advantage from better [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics).[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

### Telephone



Old logo

The first telephone service in Norway was established in 1878 between [Arendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arendal_\(town\)) and [Tvedestrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvedestrand_\(town\)), while the first international telephone service between Christiania and [Stockholm](26741.md) was offered in 1893. Automation of the telephone system was started in 1920 and completed in 1985. In 1946, the first [Telex service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telex) was offered. In 1976, [satellite](27683.md) telephone connections to the Norwegian merchant navy, at the time the largest in the world, and to the [oil platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_platform) in the [North Sea](21179.md) were established. This was the start of [Inmarsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inmarsat) Satellite Communication which was the foundation to [digitalising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_data) the telephone network between 1980–1985.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

Televerket opened its first [manual mobile telephone system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_radio_telephone) in 1966. This was replaced with the automatic [NMT system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Mobile_Telephone) in 1981 and upgraded to the enhanced NMT-900 service in 1986. Norway was the first country to get an automatic mobile telephone system. The [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_data) [GSM system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_System_for_Mobile_Communications) was established in 1993. The [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G)/[UMTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_System_for_Mobile_Communications) system began full operation in 2004.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

Telenor and Huawei conducted a successful test of 5G with 70 Gbit/s speeds in a lab environment.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

The [Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_\(web_browser\)) [web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser) was created in 1994 by [Jon Stephenson von Tetzchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Stephenson_von_Tetzchner) and [Geir Ivarsøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geir_Ivars%C3%B8y) during their tenure at Telenor. [Opera Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Software) was established in 1995, where they went on to continue the development of their browser.[^cite_note-9]&#x20;

### Deregulation and internationalisation

The corporation changed its name to **Televerket** in 1969. In 1994, the company, then known as Norwegian Telecom, was established as a [public corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-owned_corporation). The telecom sector in Norway was deregulated in stages between 1994 and 1998. An attempt to merge Telenor with its counterpart in [Sweden](5058739.md), [Telia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telia_Company), failed in 1999 while both still were owned by their respective governments. On 4 December 2000, the company was partially [privatised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatised) and listed on the Oslo Stock Exchange and [NASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ).[^cite_note-10] This privatisation gave the company 15.6 billion kr in new [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(economics\)), with the [Government of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Norway) owning 77.7 percent of the company. As of 2014, the Norwegian government holds 53.97 percent of the Telenor shares directly and another 4.66 percent through the [Government Pension Fund Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Pension_Fund_of_Norway).[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

In the second half of the 1990s, Telenor began mobile operations in other countries: [Russia](25391.md) (1994), [Bangladesh](3454.md), [Greece](12108.md), [Ireland](147575.md), [Germany](11867.md) and [Austria](26964606.md) (1997), [Ukraine](31750.md) (1998), [Malaysia](3607937.md) (1999), [Denmark](76972.md) and [Thailand](30128.md) (2000), [Hungary](13275.md) (2002), [Montenegro](20760.md) (2004), [Pakistan](23235.md) (2004), [Slovakia](26830.md), the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Serbia](29265.md) (2006), [Myanmar](19457.md) (2014). Operations in Greece, Ireland and Germany were sold between 1999-2000 and profits were re-invested into emerging markets. In October 2005, Telenor acquired [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone) Sweden, changing its name to Telenor in April 2006.&#x20;

[Grameenphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameenphone) was Telenor's first venture in the Asian telecom market and is now the largest mobile operator in [Bangladesh](3454.md). Telenor holds a 55.8 percent stake of the company. Grameenphone started trading its shares on the stock exchanges in [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Stock_Exchange) and [Chittagong](380451.md) on 16 November 2009. The success of Grammeenphone lead to an increased focus on Asia, with successful entries into [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Thailand](30128.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Myanmar](19457.md). Telenor also entered the [Indian](14533.md) market, but had to withdraw in 2017 sustaining substantial losses.&#x20;

In March 2018, Telenor sold its businesses in Southeast Europe ([Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenor_\(Bulgaria\)), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenor_Hungary), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenor_Montenegro) and [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenor_Serbia)) to the [PPF Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPF_\(company\)), totalling [€](9472.md)2.8 billion in order to focus on the Nordic and Asian market.[^cite_note-TelenorSale-12]&#x20;

In 2019, Telenor bought [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_Oyj), the third largest mobile operator in [Finland](10577.md).[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

On 21 June 2021, [Axiata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiata), Telenor and [Digi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digi_Telecommunications) agreed to a potential merger of [Celcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celcom) and Digi to create a stronger telco in Malaysia, which was approved by both Celcom and Digi shareholders on 18 November 2022. This comes after advanced discussions two months earlier.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The merged company became CelcomDigi. On completion, Axiata and Telenor held equal ownership of 33.1 percent each in the newly merged company. The merger was completed on 30 November 2022 and the company began its operations the next day.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]&#x20;

On 22 November 2021, Telenor and [Charoen Pokphand Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charoen_Pokphand) officially announced they have agreed to explore a [US\$](18717338.md)8.6 billion merger plan between Thailand's second and third largest telecom operators by subscribers, [True Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Corporation) (TRUE) and [Total Access Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Access_Communication) (DTAC). The proposed merger was subject to regulatory approvals.[^cite_note-23] The merger was acknowledged by the regulator NBTC at a meeting on 20 October 2022.[^cite_note-24] The newly merged company was founded on 1 March 2023, retaining the TRUE name and was listed on the [Stock Exchange of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_Exchange_of_Thailand) under the ticker **TRUE** on 3 March 2023.[^cite_note-25]&#x20;

## Operations

Telenor offers a full range of telecommunication services in the Nordic countries, including mobile and fixed telephony, internet access and cable TV access and content. Telenor still remains the largest telco in Norway despite competition from [Telia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeliaSonera) and others.&#x20;

The group is prominent in the Scandinavian broadband and TV markets, both with regard to the number of subscribers and to the extent of coverage. The TV distribution arm is called [Allente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allente). Telenor has operations in three Asian countries.&#x20;

Telenor has controlling interests in mobile operators in Norway, Denmark, Finland, Sweden and Bangladesh. It has minority interests in mobile operators in Malaysia and Thailand.&#x20;

### Telenor Amp

In 2022, Telenor established Telenor Amp, a business area managing companies adjacent to its core telecom operations. Amp follows a "develop-or-divest" strategy, focusing on areas such as IoT and security services. Its portfolio includes Telenor Connexion and since 2024, the cybersecurity subsidiary, Telenor Cyberdefence.[^cite_note-28]&#x20;

### Research

Telenor Research is Telenor's corporate unit for [Research & Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_Development). The unit conducts research and delivers research-based advice on topics such as market, technology, data analytics, innovation and organization. Telenor Research provides research-based analysis and strategic recommendations to the Telenor Group, as well as to the individual business units across Telenor's markets. Telenor Research's mission is to create business value for Telenor through applied research.&#x20;

### Internet of Things (IoT) and Machine-to-machine

Telenor started exploring the potential of [M2M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine-to-Machine) in 2000, when Telenor R\&D established a project aimed toward technology, services and business models. This was further spurred when Telenor acquired the Swedish mobile company Europolitan, which contained parts of Vodafone's research in the area. As a result, two separate companies were established: Telenor Connexion in [Stockholm, Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm,_Sweden)[^cite_note-29] (aiming higher up in the M2M value chain) and [Telenor Objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenor_Objects) (aiming further down in the value chain). The initiative has resulted in a substantial market share of Europe's fast-growing M2M market and is being used by [Nissan](21240.md) in [Europe](9239.md) to connect its customers' [electric cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_cars).[^cite_note-30] As of 2017, Telenor Objects is no longer active.[^cite_note-31]&#x20;

In 2021, Telenor unified its [IoT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_Things) operations under the brand Telenor IoT, combining Telenor Connexion with other units into a single global team of around 200 specialists.[^cite_note-32] Analysts have included Telenor in Gartner's Magic Quadrant for Managed IoT Connectivity Services and Transforma Insights' CSP IoT Peer Benchmarking 2024.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] In 2025, trade publications reported that Telenor IoT had surpassed deploying 25 million SIMs and through a partnership with Verizon Business enabled customers to access local U.S. networks via [eSIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESIM).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]&#x20;

### Broadcast

Telenor operates the national terrestrial broadcast network in [Norway](21241.md) through its subsidiary [Norkring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norkring).&#x20;

Telenor owns half of [Allente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allente), the result of a merger between Telenor's subsidiary [Canal Digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_Digital) and [Viasat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasat_\(Nordic_television_service\)) which was completed on 13 April 2021.&#x20;

Canal Digital was previously a leading TV content distributor in the Nordic region with about 2.7 million customers in 2011.[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

## Former operations

Telenor has sold a number of divisions after its privatisation, including Bravida,[^cite_note-38] the former installation division and [Findexa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findexa), now part of [Eniro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eniro) that is responsible for [telephone directories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_directory). The browser vendor [Opera Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Software) originated in Telenor's R\&D department.[^cite_note-39] Telenor formerly provided a range of services related to satellite communication, including [voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice), [television](29831.md) and [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) before its Telenor Satellite Services division was purchased by [Vizada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizada) in 2007.&#x20;

[Telenor Kystradio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenor_Kystradio) was responsible for the infrastructure of maritime radio communication in Norway which also included five staffed coast radio stations whose primary purpose is to monitor the maritime radio traffic (over e.g. [VHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_VHF_radio) and [MF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediumwave) bands) and to assist marine vessels in distress.. These tasks were returned to the state on 1 January 2026.&#x20;

Telenor also owned the [Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_\(satellite\)) family of satellites until selling them to *Space Norway*, part of the [Norwegian Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Space_Agency), in 2023.&#x20;

On 28 August 2025, it was announced that anti-[coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Myanmar_coup_d%27etat) activists in [Justice for Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_for_Myanmar) accused Telenor of providing surveillance equipment and customer data to the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) regime in a complaint to the [Norwegian Police](9679918.md) filed jointly with [ICJ Norway](14918.md). Telenor's data was also allegedly used by the Tatmadaw to track down [88 Generation Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/88_Generation_Students) member, [Ko Jimmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Jimmy) on 13 February 2021.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]&#x20;

The [prime minister of Norway](2926932.md) and two cabinet members have been questioned in writing by the Parliament's [Standing Committee on Scrutiny and Constitutional Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_on_Scrutiny_and_Constitutional_Affairs) and have been asked to answer by 6 November 2025. Furthermore, on 23 October 2025, the media stated the [Nobel Prize](21201.md) winner [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md) was one of the victims of the data violations.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;
