# Election interference



An 1839 speech by [William H. Roane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Roane) of Virginia regarding a bill on election interference by federal officials, readable pdf

**Election interference** generally refers to efforts to change the outcome of an election, especially by illegitimate means. Kinds of election interference may include:

- [Electoral fraud](382165.md), illegal interference with the process of an election&#x20;

  - [Vote buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_buying), when a political party or candidate distributes money to a voter with the expectation that they will vote for them

  - [Voter impersonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_impersonation), when an eligible voter votes more than once or a non-eligible voter votes under the name of an eligible one

- [Foreign electoral intervention](52846360.md), attempts by governments to influence elections in another country:

  [^cite_note-1]

  - [Russian interference in the 2016 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2016_United_States_elections)

  - [Chinese government interference in the 2019 and 2021 Canadian federal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_government_interference_in_the_2019_and_2021_Canadian_federal_elections)

  - [Russian interference in the 2020 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2020_United_States_elections)

  - [Russian interference in the 2024 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2024_United_States_elections)

  - American interference in 81 elections in at least 19 different countries between 1946-2000.

    [^cite_note-2]

    [^cite_note-3]

    [^cite_note-4]

- Illegal [electioneering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_campaign), such as campaigning or wearing political apparel too close to a polling place

  [^cite_note-5]

  [^cite_note-6]

- Recruiting candidates with identical or similar names to an existing one, intended to confuse voters

  [^cite_note-7]

- Where prohibited, the use of public funds to persuade voters to vote in a certain way
