# Gunboat

For the boxer, see [Gunboat Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_Smith). For the video game, see [Gunboat (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_\(video_game\)).

Not to be confused with [Gunship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunship).

"Canonnière" redirects here. For the French frigate, see [French frigate Minerve (1794)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Minerve_\(1794\)).



A [*Bramble*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble-class_gunboat_\(1886\)) gunboat, built for the [Royal Navy](26061.md) in 1886

A **gunboat** is a naval [watercraft](301664.md) designed for the express purpose of carrying one or more guns to [bombard coastal targets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_bombardment), as opposed to those military craft designed for [naval warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare), or for [ferrying troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troopship) or [supplies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_ship).

## History

### Pre-steam era

See also: [Fuerzas sutiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerzas_sutiles)

In the [Age of Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Sail), a gunboat was usually a small undecked vessel carrying a single [smoothbore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoothbore) [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) in the [bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_\(watercraft\)), or just two or three such cannons. A gunboat could carry one or two [masts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_\(sailing\)) or be oar-powered only, but the single-masted version of about 15 m (49 ft) length was most typical. Some types of gunboats carried two cannon, or else mounted a number of [swivel guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swivel_gun) on the railings.

The small gunboat had advantages: if it only carried a single cannon, the boat could manoeuvre in shallow or restricted areas – such as rivers or lakes – where larger ships could sail only with difficulty. The gun that such boats carried could be quite heavy; a 32-pounder for instance. As such boats were cheap and quick to build, naval forces favoured swarm tactics: while a single hit from a [frigate's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_frigate) [broadside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadside_\(naval\)) would destroy a gunboat, a frigate facing a large [squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(naval\)) of gunboats could suffer serious damage before it could manage to sink them all. For example: during the 1808 [Battle of Alvøen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alv%C3%B8en) of the [Gunboat War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_War), five Dano-Norwegian gunboats disabled the British frigate [HMS *Tartar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Tartar_\(1801\)). Gunboats used in the [Battle of Valcour Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Valcour_Island) (1776) on [Lake Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Champlain) during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) were mostly built on the spot, attesting to the speed of their construction.



A model of a type of decked "gun [yawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yawl)" designed by [Fredrik Henrik af Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrik_Henrik_af_Chapman) and used by the Swedish [archipelago fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago_fleet)

Spanish admiral [Antonio Barceló](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Barcel%C3%B3), experienced in the usage of small vessels in the conflicts against [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates), unveiled in 1781 a kind of small armored gunboat equipped with a heavy, long-range artillery piece. This originated the Spanish [Royal Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy)'s doctrine of *[fuerzas sutiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerzas_sutiles)*, which emphasized the usage of ships equipped with significant firepower but small enough to be difficult to hit back. His gunboats were first employed during the [Great Siege of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siege_of_Gibraltar), obtaining great success in the otherwise failed siege, after which they were adopted by the Royal Armada.[^cite_note-1]

All navies of the sailing era kept a number of gunboats on hand. Gunboats saw extensive use in the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) during the late 18th century as they were well-suited for the extensive coastal [skerries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skerry) and [archipelagoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) of Sweden, Finland and Russia. The rivalry between Sweden and Russia, in particular, led to an intense expansion of gunboat fleets and the development of new gunboat types. The two countries clashed during the [Russo-Swedish war of 1788–1790](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Swedish_War_\(1788%E2%80%931790\)), a conflict that culminated in the massive [Battle of Svensksund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Svensksund) in 1790, in which over 30,000 men and hundreds of gunboats, [galleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley) and other oared craft took part. The majority of these were vessels developed from the 1770s and onwards by the naval architect [Fredrik Henrik af Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrik_Henrik_af_Chapman) for the Swedish [archipelago fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago_fleet). The designs, copied and refined by the rival Danish and Russian navies, spread to the Mediterranean and to the Black Sea.[^cite_note-2]

Two variants occurred most commonly:

- a larger 20 m (66 ft) "gun sloop" (from the Swedish *kanonslup*) with two 24-pounder cannon, one in the [stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern) and one in the bow

- a smaller 15 m (49 ft) "gun yawl" (*kanonjolle*) with a single 24-pounder cannon

Many of the Baltic navies kept gunboats in service well into the second half of the 19th century.[^cite_note-3] British ships engaged larger 22 m (72 ft) Russian gunboats off [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku) in southeast Finland in 1854 during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War). The Russian vessels had the distinction of being the last oared vessels of war in history to fire their guns in anger.[^cite_note-4]

Gunboats played a key role in [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte)'s plan for the [invasion of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon's_invasion_of_England) in 1804. Denmark-Norway used them heavily in the Gunboat War. Between 1803 and 1812 the [United States Navy](20518076.md) had a policy of basing its navy on coastal gunboats, experimenting with a variety of designs. President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) (in office: 1801–1809) and his [Democratic-Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party) opposed a strong navy, regarding gunboats as adequate to defend the United States' major harbors. They performed well when properly supported with larger ships during battle, but were of little use on their own against the British blockade during the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812).[^cite_note-5]

### Steam era



The screw gunboat [USS *Alliance*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Alliance_\(1875\)), circa 1880

With the introduction of steam power in the early 19th century, the [Royal Navy](26061.md) and other navies built considerable numbers of small vessels propelled by side paddles and later by [screws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller). Initially, these vessels retained full sailing rigs and used steam engines for auxiliary propulsion.

The British Royal Navy deployed two wooden paddle-gunboats in the Lower [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) and [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River) during the [Rebellions of 1837](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellions_of_1837) in [Upper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada) and [Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada). The United States Navy deployed an iron-hulled paddle gunboat, [USS *Michigan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Michigan_\(1843\)), to the Great Lakes in 1844.

[*Von der Tann*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Tann_\(gunboat\)) became the first propeller-driven gunboat in the world. [Conradi shipyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conradi_shipyards?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel) built the steam-powered 120 [long tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_ton) (120 [t](31185.md)) gunboat in 1849 for the small navy of [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md). Initially called *"Gunboat No. 1"*, *Von der Tann* was the most modern ship in the navy. She participated successfully in the [First Schleswig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Schleswig_War) of 1848–1851.

Britain built a large number of wooden screw-gunboats during the 1850s, some of which participated in the Crimean War (1853–1856), [Second Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War) (1856–1860) and [Indian Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mutiny) (1857–1859). The requirement for gunboats in the Crimean War was formulated in 1854 to allow the Royal Navy to bombard shore facilities in the Baltic.[^cite_note-sag19-6] The first ships the Royal Navy built that met this requirement were the [*Arrow*-class gunvessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow-class_gunvessel).[^cite_note-sag19-6] Then in mid-1854 the Royal Navy ordered six [*Gleaner*-class gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleaner-class_gunboat) followed later in the year by an order for 20 [*Dapper*-class gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapper-class_gunboat).[^cite_note-sag19-6] In May 1855 the Royal Navy deployed six *Dapper*-class gunboats in the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), where they repeatedly raided and destroyed stores around its coast.[^cite_note-7] In June 1855 the Royal Navy reentered the Baltic with a total of 18 gunboats as part of a larger fleet.[^cite_note-sag28-8] The gunboats attacked various coastal facilities, operating alongside larger British warships from which they drew supplies such as coal.[^cite_note-sag28-8]



[Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) ironclad river gunboats assault the [Confederates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) at [Fort Donelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Donelson) in February 1862, during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War)

Gunboats experienced a revival during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) (1861–1865). [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) and [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) forces quickly converted existing passenger-carrying boats into armed [sidewheel steamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_steamer). Later, some purpose-built boats, such as [USS *Miami*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Miami_\(1861\)), joined the fray. They frequently mounted 12 or more guns, sometimes of rather large caliber, and usually carried some armor. At the same time, Britain's gunboats from the Crimean War period were starting to wear out, so a new series of classes was ordered. Construction shifted from a purely wooden hull to an iron–teak composite.[^cite_note-9]



[SMS *Panther*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Panther_\(1901\)), a [gunboat diplomat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_diplomacy) in the [Agadir Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadir_Crisis) of 1911

In the later 19th century and early 20th century, "gunboat" was the common name for smaller armed vessels. These could be classified, from the smallest to the largest, into [river gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_gunboat), [river monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_monitor), coastal-defense gunboats (such as [SMS *Panther*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Panther_\(1901\))), and full-fledged [monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_\(warship\)) for coastal bombardments. In the 1870s and 1880s, Britain took to building so-called ["flat-iron" (or Rendel) gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat-iron_gunboat) for coastal defence.[^cite_note-10] When there would be few opportunities to re-coal, vessels carrying a full sailing rig continued in use as gunboats; [HMS *Gannet*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Gannet_\(1878\)), a [sloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop) preserved at Chatham Historic Dockyard in the United Kingdom, exemplifies this type of gunboat.

In the United States Navy, these boats had the [hull classification symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_classification_symbol) "PG", which led to their being referred to as "patrol gunboats". They usually displaced under 2,000 long tons (2,000 t), were about 200 ft (61 m) long, 10–15 ft (3.0–4.6 m) [draught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_\(hull\)) and sometimes much less, and mounted several guns of calibers up to 5–6 in (130–150 mm). An important characteristic of these was the ability to operate in rivers, enabling them to reach inland targets in a way not otherwise possible before the development of [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft). In this period the naval powers used gunboats for [police actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_action) in colonies or in weaker countries, for example in [China](5405.md) (see e.g. [Yangtze Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_Patrol)). This category of gunboat inspired the term "[gunboat diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_diplomacy)". With the addition of torpedoes, they became "[torpedo gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_gunboat)", designated by the hull classification symbol "PTG" (Patrol Torpedo Gunboat).

In Britain, [Admiral Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fisher,_1st_Baron_Fisher)'s reforms in the 1900s saw the disposal of much of the gunboat fleet.[^cite_note-11] A handful remained in service in various roles at the start of World War I in 1914.[^cite_note-12] The last in active service were two of the second [*Bramble* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble-class_gunboat_\(1898\)) which survived until 1926, carrying out river patrols in west Africa.[^cite_note-13]



[Insect-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect-class_gunboat) HMS *Ladybird* (with larger-calibre guns installed in 1939)

In the circumstances of World War I (1914–1918), however, the Royal Navy re-equipped with small 625-long-ton (635 t), shallow-draught gunboats (12 ships of the [Insect class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect-class_gunboat)) with sufficient speed to operate in fast-flowing rivers and with relatively heavy armament. During the war and in the post-war period, these were deployed in Romania on the [Danube](21209639.md), in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) on the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) and [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris), in northern [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) on the [Northern Dvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dvina), and in China on the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze). In China, during anarchic and war conditions, they continued to protect British interests until World War II; other western Powers acted similarly.

More and larger gunboats were built in the late 1930s for the Far East. Some sailed there; others were transported in sections and reassembled at [Shanghai](27643.md).

### World War II

#### United Kingdom

Most British gunboats were based initially in East Asia. When war with Japan broke out, many of these vessels withdrew to the Indian Ocean. Others were given to the [Republic of China Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_Navy) (such as [HMS *Sandpiper*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sandpiper?action=edit\&redlink=1), which was renamed *Ying Hao*) and some were captured by the Japanese.

Some were later redeployed to the [Mediterranean theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_and_Middle_East_theatre_of_World_War_II) and supported land operations during the [North African campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_campaign), as well as in parts of [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe).

#### United States

In late 1941, the US Navy's [Yangtze Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_Patrol) boats based in China were withdrawn to the Philippines or scuttled. Following the [US defeat in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_\(1941%E2%80%931942\)), most of the remaining craft were [scuttled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuttling). However, [USS *Asheville*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Asheville_\(PG-21\)) survived until being sunk in action during the [Battle of Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Java) in 1942.

#### Soviet Union

##### Riverine armoured motor gunboats



The ex–Soviet Type 1124BKA armoured motor gunboat *BK-162 Yeysky Patriot* is now a memorial ship in [Yeysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeysk), 2017



The ex–Soviet Type 1125BKA armoured motor gunboat *BK-124 Geroy Sovietskogo Soyuza Golubets* is now a memorial ship in [Primorsko-Akhtarsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsko-Akhtarsk), 2024

During the 1930s, the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy) began developing riverine armoured motor gunboats ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): бронекатер, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *bronekater*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'armoured motorboat'; abbr. БКА, *BKA*) or "riverine tanks": vessels displacing 29 to 42 tonnes, on which the [turrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_turret) of [tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank) were mounted.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Gardiner_1980-15]

Two classes, numbering 210 vessels, saw service between 1935 and 1945:

Type 1124BKA armoured motor gunboat

The *Type 1124BKA*, Soviet designation [Project 1124](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1124BKA_armoured_motor_gunboat), was a series of 42-tonne vessels. Their standard armament was initially two [T-28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-28_\(medium_tank\)) (later [T-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-34)) tank turrets, each mounting a 76.2 mm (3 in) gun and a coaxial 7.62 mm [DT machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DT_machine_gun), as well as up to four 7.62 mm or 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine guns – in some cases the rear turret was replaced with a [Katyusha rocket launcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rocket_launcher). Propulsion: two 800-brake-horsepower petrol engines (in general); two shafts. Armour: [CT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conning_tower) and a [belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_armor) = 1/2 in (13 mm); machine gun turret faces = 2 in (51 mm).

[^cite_note-Gardiner_1980-15]

Type 1125BKA armoured motor gunboat

The *Type 1125BKA*, Soviet designation [Project 1125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1125BKA_armoured_motor_gunboat), was a series of 29-tonne vessels. Armament: one T-28 (later T-34) tank turret with a 76.2 mm gun and a coaxial 7.62 mm DT machine gun, as well as up to four 7.62 mm or 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine guns. Propulsion: one 720-brake-horsepower petrol engine (in general); one shaft. Armour: machine gun turret faces = 2 in (51 mm); light plating on CT and sides.

[^cite_note-Gardiner_1980-15]

*S-40* (Type 1125BKA version with two [Kharkiv model V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_model_V-2) diesel engines): one T-34 tank turret with a 76.2 mm gun and a coaxial 7.62 mm DT machine gun, as well as up to four 7.62 mm or 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine guns. A total of 7 S-40-subclass boats were built.

[^cite_note-Berezhnoy_1988-16]

With crews of 10 to 17 personnel, *riverine tanks* displaced 29 to 42 tonnes (29 to 41 long tons; 32 to 46 short tons), had armour 4–14 mm (0.16–0.55 in) thick, and were 22.6 to 25 m (74 ft 2 in to 82 ft 0 in) long. They saw significant action in the Baltic and [Black](3386.md) Seas between 1941 and 1945.

##### Seagoing armoured motor gunboats

Another type of (seagoing) armoured motor gunboat was developed during World War II. The design for this was drawn up shortly before the outbreak of war as an enlarged version of the Type 1124BKA. The construction of the prototype was begun in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad) in 1941 and this was completed in 1943. Further vessels were built in 1944. Intended for use in the shallow waters of the Baltic Sea, they were in the event employed for a wide range of duties.[^cite_note-Gardiner_1980-15]

MBK-type armoured motor gunboat

*MBK* (Russian: МБК, an initialism for морской бронекатер, *morskoy bronekater*, 'seagoing armoured motorboat'), Soviet designation Project 161, was a series of 150-tonne (150-long-ton; 170-short-ton) vessels. Armament: two T-34 tank turrets each with a 76.2 mm gun and a coaxial 7.62 mm DT machine gun (later two [T-34-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-34-85) tank turrets each with a 85 mm gun and a coaxial 7.62 mm DT machine gun), as well as two [45 mm 21-K anti-aircraft guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45_mm_anti-aircraft_gun_\(21-K\)), one [37 mm 70-K anti-aircraft gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_mm_automatic_air_defense_gun_M1939_\(61-K\)#Naval_version), and up to three 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine guns or two twin 12.7 mm machine guns instead of 45 mm 21-K guns. Propulsion: two 500-brake-horsepower diesel engines (in general); two shafts. Armour: CT and turrets = 1+1/4 to 2 in (32 to 51 mm).

[^cite_note-Gardiner_1980-15]

With crews of 38 to 42 personnel, MBK-type armoured motor gunboats measured 36.0 m × 5.4 m × 1.5 m (118 ft 1 in × 17 ft 9 in × 4 ft 11 in).[^cite_note-Gardiner_1980-15] A total of 20 boats of this type were built.[^cite_note-Berezhnoy_1988-16]

### Vietnam War



*Point Gammon* gets a camouflage coat of dark grey paint at [Da Nang](425140.md), October 1965, for its conversion from Coast Guard use to Vietnam War river patrol duties

US riverine gunboats in the [Vietnam War](32611.md), included [Patrol Boats River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_Boat,_River) (PBR), constructed of [fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberglass); [Patrol Craft Fast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_Craft_Fast) (PCF), commonly known as [Swift Boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_Boats), built of aluminum; and [Assault Support Patrol Boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Support_Patrol_Boat) (ASPB) built of steel. [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard) 82-foot (25 m) [Point-class cutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-class_cutter) supplemented these US Navy vessels. The ASPBs were commonly referred to as "Alpha" boats and primarily carried out mine-sweeping duties along the waterways, due to their all-steel construction. The ASPBs were the only US Navy riverine craft specifically designed and built for the Vietnam War.[^cite_note-17] All of these boats were assigned to the US Navy's "[Brownwater Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownwater_Navy)".[^cite_note-18]
