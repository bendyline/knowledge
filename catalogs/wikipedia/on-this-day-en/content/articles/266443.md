# Metalworking

For metal assembly, see [Metal fabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_fabrication).



A fireman turning a bar of metal on a [lathe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathe) on [the USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman) in 2004

**Metalworking** is the process of shaping and reshaping [metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal) in order to create useful objects, parts, assemblies, and large scale structures. As a term, it covers a wide and diverse range of processes, skills, and tools for producing objects on every scale: from huge [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship), buildings, and [bridges](3397.md), down to precise [engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine) parts and delicate [jewellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewellery).

The historical roots of metalworking predate recorded history; its use spans cultures, civilizations and millennia. It has evolved from shaping soft, [native metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_metal) like [gold](12240.md) with simple hand tools, through the [smelting](28716.md) of ores and hot [forging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forging) of harder metals like [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), up to and including highly technical modern processes such as [machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machining) and [welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welding). It has been used as an industry, a driver of trade, individual hobbies, and in the creation of art;[^cite_note-1] it can be regarded as both a science and a craft.

Modern metalworking processes, though diverse and specialized, can be categorized into one of three broad areas known as forming, cutting, or joining processes. Modern metalworking workshops, typically known as [machine shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_shop), hold a wide variety of specialized or general-use [machine tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tool) capable of creating highly precise, useful products. Many simpler metalworking techniques, such as [blacksmithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacksmithing), are no longer economically competitive on a large scale in developed countries; some of them are still in use in less developed countries, for artisanal or hobby work, or for historical reenactment.

## Prehistory

The oldest archaeological evidence of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) and working was the discovery of a copper [pendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendant) in northern [Iraq](7515928.md) from 8,700 BCE.[^cite_note-2] The earliest substantiated and dated evidence of metalworking in the [Americas](29833.md) was the processing of copper in [Wisconsin](33127.md), near [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan). Copper was hammered until it became brittle, then heated so it could be worked further. In America, this technology is dated to about 4000–5000 BCE.[^cite_note-3] The oldest [gold](12240.md) artifacts in the world come from the Bulgarian [Varna Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_Necropolis) and date from 4450 BCE.

Not all metal required fire to obtain it or work it. [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov) speculated that gold was the "first metal".[^cite_note-4] His reasoning being, that, by its [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), it is found in nature as nuggets of pure gold. In other words, gold, as rare as it is, is sometimes found in nature as a [native metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_metal). Some metals can also be found in [meteors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor). Almost all other metals are found in [ores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore), a mineral-bearing [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_\(geology\)), that require heat or some other process to liberate the metal. Another feature of gold is that it is workable as it is found, meaning that no technology beyond a stone [hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammer) and [anvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anvil) is needed to work the metal. This is a result of gold's properties of [malleability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ductility) and [ductility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ductility). The earliest [tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool) were stone, [bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone), [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood), and [sinew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinew), all of which sufficed to work gold.

At some unknown time, the process of liberating metals from rock by heat became known, and rocks rich in copper, [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin), and [lead](17747.md) came into demand. These ores were mined wherever they were recognized. Remnants of such ancient mines have been found all over [Southwestern Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Asia).[^cite_note-5] Metalworking was being carried out by the [South Asian](21566765.md) inhabitants of [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh) between 7000 and 3300 BCE.[^cite_note-Possehl-6] The end of the beginning of metalworking occurs sometime around 6000 BCE when copper [smelting](28716.md) became common in Southwestern Asia.

Ancient civilisations knew of seven metals. Here they are arranged in order of their [oxidation potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_potential) (in [volts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt)):

- [Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) +0.44 V,

- [Tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) +0.14 V

- [Lead](17747.md) +0.13 V

- [Copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) −0.34 V

- [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(element\)) −0.79 V

- [Silver](27119.md) −0.80 V

- [Gold](12240.md) −1.50 V.

The oxidation potential is important because it is one indicator of how tightly bound to the ore the metal is likely to be. As can be seen, iron is significantly higher than the other six metals while gold is dramatically lower than the six above it. Gold's low oxidation is one of the main reasons that gold is found in nuggets. These nuggets are relatively pure gold and are workable as they are found.

Copper ore, being relatively abundant, and tin ore became the next important substances in the story of metalworking. Using heat to smelt copper from ore, a great deal of copper was produced. It was used for both [jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewelry) and simple tools. However, copper by itself was too soft for tools requiring edges and stiffness. At some point tin was added into the molten copper and [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) was developed thereby. Bronze is an [alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) of copper and tin. Bronze was an important advance because it had the edge-durability and stiffness that pure copper lacked. Until the advent of iron, bronze was the most advanced metal for tools and [weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon) in common use (see [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) for more detail).

Outside Southwestern Asia, these same advances and materials were being discovered and used around the world. People in [China](5405.md) and [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) began using bronze with little time being devoted to copper. [Japanese](15573.md) began the use of bronze and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) almost simultaneously. In the Americas it was different. Although the peoples of the Americas knew of metals, it was not until the [European colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonisation_of_the_Americas) that metalworking for tools and weapons became common. Jewelry and [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art) were the principal uses of metals in the Americas prior to European influence.

About 2700 BCE, production of bronze was common in locales where the necessary materials could be assembled for smelting, heating, and working the metal. Iron was beginning to be smelted and began its emergence as an important metal for tools and weapons. The period that followed became known as the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age).

By the historical periods of the [Pharaohs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaohs) in [Egypt](8087628.md), the [Vedic kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period) in [India](14533.md), the [Tribes of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribes_of_Israel), and the [Maya civilization](18449273.md) in [North America](21139.md), among other ancient populations, humans had begun to attach value to [precious metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precious_metal) over and above any available functional metal tools and weapons. Gold or silver trinkets became [luxury goods](858665.md) in [ antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_world).[^cite_note-7] In some cases rules for ownership, distribution, and [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade) were created, enforced, and agreed upon by various peoples. By the above periods metalworkers were very skilled at creating objects of adornment, religious artifacts, and [trade instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_instrument?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [precious metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precious_metals) (non-ferrous), as well as weaponry (usually of [ ferrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) [metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metals) and/or [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloys)). Sophisticated techniques were practiced by artisans, [blacksmiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacksmith), [atharvavedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atharvaveda) practitioners, [alchemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy), and other categories of metalworkers around the globe. For example, the [granulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granulation_\(jewellery\)) technique occurs in numerous ancient cultures before the historic record shows people traveled to far regions to share this process. [Metalsmiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalsmith) today still use this and many other ancient techniques.

Over time, metal objects became more common and ever more complex. The need to further acquire and work metals grew in importance. Skills related to [ extracting metal ores from the earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) began to evolve, and metalsmiths became more knowledgeable. Metalsmiths became important members of society.[^cite_note-8] Fates and economies of entire civilizations were greatly affected by the availability of metals and metalsmiths. The metalworker depends on the extraction of precious metals to make [jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewelry), build more efficient [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), and for [industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector) and technological applications from [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) to [shipping containers](22849124.md) to [rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway), and [air transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_transport). Without metals, goods and services would cease to move around the globe on the scale we know today.

## General processes



A [combination square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combination_square) used for transferring designs



A caliper is used to precisely measure a short length.

Metalworking generally is divided into three categories: *forming*, *cutting*, and *joining*. Most metal cutting is done by high speed steel tools or carbide tools.[^cite_note-9] Each of these categories contains various processes.

Prior to most operations, the metal must be marked out and/or measured, depending on the desired finished product.

*[Marking out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marking_out)* (also known as layout) is the process of transferring a [design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design) or [pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern) to a [workpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workpiece) and is the first step in the handcraft of metalworking. It is performed in many industries or hobbies, although in industry, the repetition eliminates the need to mark out every individual piece. In the metal trades area, marking out consists of transferring the engineer's [plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_drawing) to the workpiece in preparation for the next step, machining or manufacture.

*[Calipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliper)* are hand tools designed to precisely measure the distance between two points. Most calipers have two sets of flat, parallel edges used for inner or outer diameter measurements. These calipers can be accurate to within one-thousandth of an inch (25.4 μm). Different types of calipers have different mechanisms for displaying the distance measured. Where larger objects need to be measured with less precision, a [tape measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tape_measure) is often used.

## Cutting processes



A CNC [plasma cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cutting) machine

Main article: [Cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting)

*Cutting* is a collection of processes wherein material is brought to a specified geometry by removing excess material using various kinds of tooling to leave a finished part that meets specifications. The net result of cutting is two products, the waste or excess material, and the finished part. In woodworking, the waste would be sawdust and excess wood. In cutting metals the waste is chips or [swarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarf) and excess metal.

Cutting processes fall into one of three major categories:

- Chip producing processes most commonly known as [machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machining)

- Burning, a set of processes wherein the metal is cut by oxidizing a kerf to separate pieces of metal

- Miscellaneous specialty process, not falling easily into either of the above categories

[Drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drilling) a hole in a metal part is the most common example of a chip producing process. Using an [oxy-fuel cutting torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxy-fuel_welding_and_cutting) to separate a plate of steel into smaller pieces is an example of burning. Chemical milling is an example of a specialty process that removes excess material by the use of etching chemicals and masking chemicals.

There are many technologies available to cut metal, including:&#x20;

- Manual technologies: [saw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw), [chisel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisel), [shear or snips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_snips)

- Machine technologies: [turning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning), [milling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milling_\(machining\)), [drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drilling), [grinding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinding_machine), [sawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw)

- Welding/burning technologies: burning by [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cutting), [oxy-fuel burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxy-fuel_welding_and_cutting), and [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cutting)

- Erosion technologies: by [water jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_jet_cutter), [electric discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark_erosion), or [abrasive flow machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrasive_flow_machining).

- Chemical technologies: [Photochemical machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemical_machining)

[Cutting fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_fluid) or [coolant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolant) is used where there is significant friction and heat at the cutting interface between a cutter such as a drill or an end mill and the workpiece. Coolant is generally introduced by a spray across the face of the tool and workpiece to decrease friction and temperature at the cutting tool/workpiece interface to prevent excessive tool wear. In practice there are many methods of delivering coolant.

Main article: [Milling (machining)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milling_\(machining\))

**Milling** is the complex shaping of metal or other materials by removing material to form the final shape. It is generally done on a [milling machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milling_machine), a power-driven machine that in its basic form consists of a [milling cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milling_cutter) that rotates about the spindle axis (like a [drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drill)), and a [worktable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worktable) that can move in multiple directions (usually two dimensions \[x and y axis] relative to the workpiece). The spindle usually moves in the z axis. It is possible to raise the table (where the workpiece rests). Milling machines may be operated manually or under [computer numerical control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_numerical_control) (CNC), and can perform a vast number of complex operations, such as slot cutting, [planing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planer_\(metalworking\)), [drilling and threading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drilling_and_threading), [rabbeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbet), [routing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_router), etc. Two common types of mills are the horizontal mill and vertical mill.

The pieces produced are usually complex 3D objects that are converted into x, y, and z coordinates that are then fed into the [CNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNC) machine and allow it to complete the tasks required. The milling machine can produce most parts in 3D, but some require the objects to be rotated around the x, y, or z coordinate axis (depending on the need). Tolerances come in a variety of standards, depending on the locale. In countries still using the imperial system, this is usually in the thousandths of an inch (unit known as *thou*), depending on the specific machine. In many other European countries, standards following the ISO are used instead.

In order to keep both the bit and material cool, a high temperature coolant is used. In most cases the coolant is sprayed from a hose directly onto the bit and material. This coolant can either be machine or user controlled, depending on the machine.

Materials that can be milled range from [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum) to stainless steel and almost everything in between. Each material requires a different speed on the milling tool and varies in the amount of material that can be removed in one pass of the tool. Harder materials are usually milled at slower speeds with small amounts of material removed. Softer materials vary, but usually are milled with a high bit speed.

The use of a milling machine adds costs that are factored into the manufacturing process. Each time the machine is used coolant is also used, which must be periodically added in order to prevent breaking bits. A milling bit must also be changed as needed in order to prevent damage to the material. Time is the biggest factor for costs. Complex parts can require hours to complete, while very simple parts take only minutes. This in turn varies the production time as well, as each part will require different amounts of time.

Safety is key with these machines. The bits are traveling at high speeds and removing pieces of usually scalding hot metal. The advantage of having a CNC milling machine is that it protects the machine operator.

### Turning



A lathe cutting material from a workpiece

Main article: [Turning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning)

Turning is a metal cutting process for producing a cylindrical surface with a single point tool. The workpiece is rotated on a spindle and the cutting tool is fed into it radially, axially or both. Producing surfaces perpendicular to the workpiece axis is called facing. Producing surfaces using both radial and axial feeds is called profiling.[^cite_note-americanmachinist-16]

A *lathe* is a machine tool which spins a block or cylinder of material so that when [abrasive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrasive), cutting, or [deformation tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knurling) are applied to the workpiece, it can be shaped to produce an object which has [rotational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_symmetry) about an [axis of rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_rotation). Examples of objects that can be produced on a lathe include [candlestick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlestick) holders, [crankshafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crankshaft), [camshafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camshaft), and [bearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bearing_\(mechanical\)) mounts.

Lathes have four main components: the bed, the headstock, the carriage, and the tailstock. The bed is a precise & very strong base which all of the other components rest upon for alignment. The headstock's [spindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindle_\(tool\)) secures the workpiece with a [chuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_\(engineering\)), whose jaws (usually three or four) are tightened around the piece. The spindle rotates at high speed, providing the energy to cut the material. While historically lathes were powered by [belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_\(mechanical\)) from a [line shaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_shaft), modern examples uses electric motors. The workpiece extends out of the spindle along the axis of rotation above the flat bed. The carriage is a platform that can be moved, precisely and independently parallel and perpendicular to the axis of rotation. A hardened [cutting tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_bit) is held at the desired height (usually the middle of the workpiece) by the toolpost. The carriage is then moved around the rotating workpiece, and the cutting tool gradually removes material from the workpiece. The tailstock can be slid along the axis of rotation and then locked in place as necessary. It may hold centers to further secure the workpiece, or cutting tools driven into the end of the workpiece.

Other operations that can be performed with a single point tool on a lathe are:[^cite_note-americanmachinist-16]

Chamfering: Cutting an angle on the corner of a cylinder.\
&#x20;Parting: The tool is fed radially into the workpiece to cut off the end of a part.\
&#x20;[Threading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threading_\(manufacturing\)): A tool is fed along and across the outside or inside surface of rotating parts to produce external or internal [threads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_thread).\
&#x20;[Boring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring_\(manufacturing\)): A single-point tool is fed linearly and parallel to the axis of rotation to create a round hole.\
&#x20;[Drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drilling): Feeding the drill into the workpiece axially.\
&#x20;[Knurling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knurling): Uses a tool to produce a rough surface texture on the work piece. Frequently used to allow grip by hand on a metal part.

Modern computer numerical control (CNC) lathes and (CNC) machining centres can do secondary operations like milling by using driven tools. When driven tools are used the work piece stops rotating and the driven tool executes the machining operation with a rotating cutting tool. The CNC machines use x, y, and z coordinates in order to control the turning tools and produce the product. Most modern day CNC lathes are able to produce most turned objects in 3D.

Nearly all types of metal can be turned, although more time & specialist cutting tools are needed for [harder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardness) workpieces.

### Threading



Three different types and sizes of taps

Main article: [Threading (manufacturing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threading_\(manufacturing\))

There are many threading processes including: cutting threads with a [tap or die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tap_and_die), thread milling, single-point thread cutting, thread rolling, cold root rolling and forming, and thread grinding. A *tap* is used to cut a female thread on the inside surface of a pre-drilled hole, while a *die* cuts a male thread on a preformed cylindrical rod.

### Grinding



A surface grinder

Main article: [Grinding (abrasive cutting)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinding_\(abrasive_cutting\))

*Grinding* uses an abrasive process to remove material from the workpiece. A **grinding machine** is a machine tool used for producing very fine finishes, making very light cuts, or high precision forms using an [abrasive wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinding_wheel) as the cutting device. This wheel can be made up of various sizes and types of stones, [diamonds](8082.md) or [inorganic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic) materials.

The simplest grinder is a bench grinder or a hand-held angle grinder, for deburring parts or cutting metal with a zip-disc.

Grinders have increased in size and complexity with advances in time and technology. From the old days of a manual toolroom grinder sharpening endmills for a production shop, to today's 30000 RPM CNC auto-loading manufacturing cell producing jet turbines, grinding processes vary greatly.

Grinders need to be very rigid machines to produce the required finish. Some grinders are even used to produce glass scales for positioning CNC machine axis. The common rule is the machines used to produce scales be 10 times more accurate than the machines the parts are produced for.

In the past grinders were used for finishing operations only because of limitations of tooling. Modern grinding wheel materials and the use of industrial diamonds or other man-made coatings (cubic boron nitride) on wheel forms have allowed grinders to achieve excellent results in production environments instead of being relegated to the back of the shop.

Modern technology has advanced grinding operations to include CNC controls, high material removal rates with high precision, lending itself well to aerospace applications and high volume production runs of precision components.

### Filing

Main article: [Filing (metalworking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filing_\(metalworking\))



A file is an abrasive surface like this one that allows machinists to remove small, imprecise amounts of metal.

*Filing* is combination of grinding and saw tooth cutting using a [file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File_\(tool\)). Prior to the development of modern machining equipment it provided a relatively accurate means for the production of small parts, especially those with flat surfaces. The skilled use of a file allowed a [machinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinist) to work to fine tolerances and was the hallmark of the craft. Today filing is rarely used as a production technique in industry, though it remains as a common method of [deburring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deburr).

### Other

[Broaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broaching_\(metalworking\)) is a machining operation used to cut [keyways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyway_\(engineering\)) into shafts. [Electron beam machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_beam_machining) (EBM) is a machining process where high-velocity electrons are directed toward a work piece, creating heat and vaporizing the material. [Ultrasonic machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasonic_machining) uses [ultrasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound) vibrations to machine very hard or brittle materials.

## Joining processes



Mig welding

### Welding

Main article: [Welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welding)

*Welding* is a [fabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrication_\(metal\)) process that joins materials, usually metals or [thermoplastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoplastic), by causing [coalescence](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/coalesce). This is often done by [melting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting) the workpieces and adding a filler material to form a pool of molten material that cools to become a strong joint, but sometimes pressure is used in conjunction with [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat), or by itself, to produce the weld.[^cite_note-17]

Many different energy sources can be used for welding, including a gas [flame](11145.md), an [electric arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_arc), a laser, an [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) beam, [friction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friction), and [ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound). While often an industrial process, welding can be done in many different environments, including open air, [underwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_welding) and in [space](177602.md). Regardless of location, however, welding remains dangerous, and precautions must be taken to avoid burns, [electric shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_shock), poisonous fumes, and overexposure to [ultraviolet light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_light).

### Brazing

Main article: [Brazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazing)

*Brazing* is a joining process in which a filler metal is melted and drawn into a [capillary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_action) formed by the assembly of two or more work pieces. The filler metal reacts metallurgically with the workpieces and solidifies in the capillary, forming a strong joint. Unlike welding, the work piece is not melted. Brazing is similar to soldering, but occurs at temperatures in excess of 450 °C (842 °F). Brazing has the advantage of producing less thermal stresses than welding, and brazed assemblies tend to be more ductile than weldments because alloying elements can not segregate and precipitate.

Brazing techniques include, flame brazing, resistance brazing, furnace brazing, diffusion brazing, inductive brazing and vacuum brazing.

### Soldering

Main article: [Soldering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldering)



Soldering a printed circuit board

*Soldering* is a joining process that occurs at temperatures below 450 °C (842 °F). It is similar to brazing in the way that a filler is melted and drawn into a capillary to form a joint, although at a lower temperature. Because of this lower temperature and different alloys used as fillers, the metallurgical reaction between filler and work piece is minimal, resulting in a weaker joint.

### Riveting

Main article: [Rivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivet)

*Riveting* is one of the most ancient metalwork joining processes.[^cite_note-18] Its use declined markedly during the second half of the 20th century,[^cite_note-19] but it still retains important uses in industry and construction, and in artisan crafts such as [jewellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewellery), [medieval armouring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_armour) and [metal couture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_couture) in the early 21st century. The earlier use of rivets is being superseded by improvements in [welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welding) and component [fabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrication_\(metal\)) techniques.

A [rivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivet) is essentially a two-headed and unthreaded [bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw) which holds two other pieces of metal together. Holes are [drilled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drill) or [punched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_\(tool\)) through the two pieces of metal to be joined. The holes being aligned, a rivet is passed through the holes and permanent heads are formed onto the ends of the rivet utilizing hammers and forming dies (by either [cold working](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_working) or [hot working](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_working)). Rivets are commonly purchased with one head already formed.

When it is necessary to remove rivets, one of the rivet's heads is sheared off with a [cold chisel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisel). The rivet is then driven out with a [hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammer) and [punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_\(tool\)).

### Mechanical fixings

This includes [screws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw), as well as [bolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolt_\(fastener\)). This is often used as it requires relatively little specialist equipment, and are therefore often used in [flat-pack furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready-to-assemble_furniture). It can also be used when a metal is joined to another material (such as [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood)) or a particular metal does not weld well (such as [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium)). This can be done to directly join metals, or with an intermediate material such as [nylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon). While often weaker than other methods such as welding or brazing, the metal can easily be removed and therefore reused or recycled. It can also be done in conjunction with an epoxy or glue, reverting its ecological benefits.

## Associated processes

While these processes are not primary metalworking processes, they are often performed before or after metalworking processes.

### Heat treatment

Main article: [Heat treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_treatment)

Metals can be heat treated to alter the properties of strength, ductility, toughness, hardness or resistance to corrosion. Common heat treatment processes include [annealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annealing_\(metallurgy\)), [precipitation hardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_hardening), [quenching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quenching), and [tempering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempering_\(metallurgy\)):

- annealing softens the metal by allowing recovery of cold work and grain growth.

- quenching can be used to harden alloy steels, or in precipitation hardenable alloys, to trap dissolved solute atoms in solution.

- tempering will cause the dissolved alloying elements to precipitate, or in the case of quenched steels, improve impact strength and ductile properties.

Often, mechanical and thermal treatments are combined in what is known as thermo-mechanical treatments for better properties and more efficient processing of materials. These processes are common to high alloy special steels, super alloys and titanium alloys.

### Plating

Main article: [Plating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plating)

[Electroplating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroplating) is a common surface-treatment technique. It involves bonding a thin layer of another metal such as [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium) or [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) to the surface of the product by hydrolysis. It is used to reduce corrosion, create abrasion resistance and improve the product's aesthetic appearance. Plating can even change the properties of the original part including conductivity, heat dissipation or structural integrity. There are four main electroplating methods to ensure proper coating and cost effectiveness per product: mass plating, rack plating, continuous plating and line plating.[^cite_note-20]

### Thermal spraying

Main article: [Thermal spraying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_spraying)

Thermal spraying techniques are another popular finishing option, and often have better high temperature properties than electroplated coatings due to the thicker coating. The four main thermal spray processes include electric wire arc spray, flame (oxy acetylene combustion) spray, plasma spray and high velocity oxy fuel (HVOF) spray.[^cite_note-21]
