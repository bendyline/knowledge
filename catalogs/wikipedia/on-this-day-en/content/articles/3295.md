**Bonn** (German pronunciation: [\[bɔn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/De-Bonn.ogg/De-Bonn.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/De-Bonn.ogg/De-Bonn.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), officially the **Federal City of Bonn** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Bundesstadt Bonn*), is a [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_city) in the German state of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), located on the banks of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine). With a population exceeding 320,000,[^cite_note-pop2025-4] it lies about 24 km (15 mi) south-southeast of [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), in the southernmost part of the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr) region. Bonn served as the capital of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) from 1949 until 1990 and was the seat of government for [reunified Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) until 1999, when the government relocated to [Berlin](3354.md). The city holds historical significance as the birthplace of Germany's current constitution, the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany).

Founded in the 1st century BC as a settlement of the [Ubii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubii) and later part of the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) province [Germania Inferior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_Inferior), Bonn is among Germany's oldest cities. It was the capital city of the [Electorate of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Cologne) from 1597 to 1794 and served as the residence of the Archbishops and Prince-electors of [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Cologne). The period during which Bonn was the capital of West Germany is often referred to by historians as the *[Bonn Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Republic)*.[^cite_note-5]

Following the German reunification, a political compromise known as the [Berlin-Bonn Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Bonn_Act) ensured that the German federal government retained a significant presence in Bonn. As of 2019, approximately one-third of all ministerial jobs remain in the city.[^cite_note-6] Bonn is considered an unofficial secondary capital of Germany and is the location of the secondary seats of the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany), the [chancellor](20890626.md), and the [Bundesrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesrat_of_Germany). Bonn is also the location of the primary seats of six federal ministries and twenty federal authorities. The city's title as Federal City (German: *Bundesstadt*) underscores its political importance.[^cite_note-7]

The global headquarters of [Deutsche Post DHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Post_DHL) and [Deutsche Telekom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Telekom), both [DAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAX)-listed corporations, are in Bonn. The city is home to the [Rheinische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) university, and a total of 20 [United Nations](31769.md) institutions, the highest number in all of Germany.[^cite_note-8] These institutions include the headquarters for Secretariat of the [UN Framework Convention Climate Change](31898.md) (UNFCCC), the Secretariat of the [UN Convention to Combat Desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_to_Combat_Desertification) (UNCCD), and the [UN Volunteers programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Volunteers).[^cite_note-9] Birthplace of composer [Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven), a center of [Rhenish carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenish_Carnival), and its geography by the [Middle Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Rhine) make it an important tourist destination. In Bonn the [Bönnsch Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6nnsch_Platt), a dialect of the Ripuarian language is spoken by all generations, especially during carnival.

## Geography



View over central Bonn as seen from the Stadthaus, including the [Siebengebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebengebirge), a hill range on the east bank of the [Middle Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Rhine)

### Topography

Situated in the southernmost part of the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr) region, Germany's largest metropolitan area with over 11 million inhabitants, Bonn lies within the [German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), on the border with [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md). Spanning an area of more 141.2 km<sup>2</sup> (55 sq mi) on both sides of the river [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), almost three-quarters of the city lies on the river's left bank.

To the south and to the west, Bonn borders the [Eifel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eifel) region which encompasses the [Rhineland Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland_Nature_Park). To the north, Bonn borders the [Cologne Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Lowland). Natural borders are constituted by the river [Sieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sieg_\(river\)) to the north-east and by the [Siebengebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebengebirge) (also known as the Seven Hills) to the east. The largest extension of the city in north–south dimensions is 15 km (9 mi) and 12.5 km (8 mi) in west–east dimensions. The city borders have a total length of 61 km (38 mi). The geographical centre of Bonn is the Bundeskanzlerplatz *(Chancellor Square)* in Bonn-Gronau.

### Administration

The [German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) is divided into five [governmental districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regierungsbezirk) (German: *Regierungsbezirk*), and Bonn is part of the [governmental district of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regierungsbezirk_K%C3%B6ln) (German: *Regierungsbezirk Köln*). Within this governmental district, the city of Bonn is an [urban district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Germany) in its own right. The urban district of Bonn is then again divided into four administrative municipal districts (German: *Stadtbezirk*). These are Bonn, Bonn-Bad Godesberg, Bonn-Beuel and Bonn-Hardtberg. In 1969, the independent towns of [Bad Godesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Godesberg) and Beuel as well as several villages were incorporated into Bonn, resulting in a city more than twice as large as before.

### Climate

See also: [Rhine-Ruhr § Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr#Climate)

Bonn has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*).[^cite_note-sklima-11] In the south of the Cologne lowland in the Rhine valley, Bonn is in one of Germany's warmest regions.

The Bonn weather station has recorded the following extreme values:[^cite_note-sklima-11]

- Its highest temperature was 40.9 °C (105.6 °F) on [25 July 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_heatwaves#Germany).

- Its lowest temperature was −23.0 °C (−9.4 °F) on 27 January 1942.

- Its greatest annual precipitation was 956.7 mm (37.67 in) in 2007.

- Its least annual precipitation was 381.5 mm (15.02 in) in 1959.

- The longest annual sunshine was 2013.9 hours in 2018.

- The shortest annual sunshine was 1240.7 hours in 1981.

## History



Gravestone of the Roman legionary [Quintus Petilius Secundus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Petilius_Secundus), stationed in Bonn

### Chronology

In 1989, Bonn celebrated its 2,000th anniversary. The city was commemorating the construction of the first fortified [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) [camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_military_camp#Legionary_fortresses) on the Rhine in 12 BCE, after the Roman governor [Agrippa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Vipsanius_Agrippa) had already settled the [Ubii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubii) there in 38 BCE. However, people had lived in the area of today's city much earlier. Evidence of this includes the 14,000-year-old [double burial at Oberkassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkassel,_Bonn#Bonn-Oberkassel_double_burial) as well as a trench and wooden palisades found on the [Venusberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venusberg_\(Bonn\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), dating back to around 4080 BCE.

In the years before the birth of Christ, Roman presence in Bonna was modest, but this changed after the Roman defeat in the [Battle of the Teutoburg Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Teutoburg_Forest) in 9 CE. In the following decades, a [legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_legion) was stationed there, which built the [Legionary Fortress Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legionary_Fortress_Bonn?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the northern part of present-day Bonn. Around the camp, and to the south along what is now Adenauerallee, traders and craftsmen settled in a [vicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicus_Bonnensis?action=edit\&redlink=1).



Bonn 1646 – Copper engraving by [Matthäus Merian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matth%C3%A4us_Merian) the Elder

With the [end of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Roman_Empire), Bonn declined during Late Antiquity and the Early Middle Ages. During the [Viking raids in the Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_raids_in_the_Rhineland), Bonn was burned twice in 882, and in 883, the recently rebuilt town was again attacked, burned, and looted by the Normans.

In the [Frankish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankish_Empire), and finally in the 9th and 10th centuries, a religious center developed around the [Bonn Minster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Minster) (the Villa Basilika), and a market settlement emerged in the area of today's market square. The year 1243 is considered the year in which Bonn was granted full city rights.[^cite_note-13]

The outcome of the [Battle of Worringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Worringen) in 1288 was of great importance for the further development of the city. The [Cologne prince-electors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Cologne) made Bonn—along with [Brühl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%BChl_\(Rhineland\)) and [Poppelsdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppelsdorf)—one of their residences, and eventually their [residence city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_city?action=edit\&redlink=1). The magnificent palaces built by the prince-electors in the 17th and 18th centuries gave the city its [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) splendor.

This era ended with the occupation by [French troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_First_Coalition) on October 8, 1794. This was followed by nearly two decades of occupation by the troops of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte). Taxes in the form of food, clothing, and accommodations, as well as the loss of the electoral state administration, led to poverty among the population and a decline in the number of inhabitants by around 20%.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-14] The French introduced a civil code ([Code civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_civil)) and a municipal constitution in Bonn. Even under [French occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Rhine), medium and large industrial companies, particularly in the textile sector, were established in Bonn. The French also pursued a thorough [secularization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularization): properties of the ecclesiastical electorate, especially the electoral buildings, were taken into state ownership.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-14] The areas on the right bank of the Rhine that are now part of Bonn (Vilich) came into the possession of the [Prince of Nassau-Usingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau-Usingen); Oberkassel belonged to the [Duchy of Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Berg), a French [satellite state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_state). By the [Treaty of Lunéville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lun%C3%A9ville) of February 9, 1801, the Rhine near Bonn was designated as the French eastern border. Bonn became the seat of a sub-prefecture in the newly formed [Rhin-et-Moselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhin-et-Moselle).



Market square with the [Old Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Hall_\(Bonn\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (right), [photochrom print](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochrom_print?action=edit\&redlink=1) around 1900

After the defeats of the French army in the [Russian campaign of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_campaign_of_1812) and the [Battle of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leipzig), the French evacuated Bonn in January 1814.

Following the decisions of the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), Bonn became part of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) in 1815. In the following decades, the city was shaped by the newly founded [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinische_Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universit%C3%A4t_Bonn), established by the Prussian government on October 18, 1818. The founder and namesake was [King Frederick William III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William_III_of_Prussia) of Prussia. A university had existed in Bonn at the end of the 18th century but was closed during the French occupation in 1794. The Prussian foundation was not a continuation of that earlier institution, but part of a program that also included the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau#Royal_University_of_Breslau_/_Silesian_Frederick_William_University). The term Rheinische in the name of the Bonn university was meant to mark it as a "sister" institution to the Berlin and Breslau universities. Over the next 100 years, Bonn became the preferred place of study for the [Hohenzollern princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern). It was nicknamed the "Princes' University," as both the then-Prussian Crown Prince [Frederick William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III,_German_Emperor), his son [Wilhelm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_II,_German_Emperor), and Wilhelm's four sons studied there.[^cite_note-15] Other sons of noble families also favored studying at this university in the 19th century. Before the founding of the Bonn university, Cologne had been its main rival. The "enlightened tradition" of Bonn, compared to the "holy Cologne," likely made it more suitable for a confessionally neutral university. Practical reasons also favored Bonn: the old electoral palace and the Poppelsdorf Palace were already available as suitable buildings.

From 1815 onward, professors, students, civil servants, and officers arrived in Bonn, including many Protestants from the Prussian provinces, which was unusual for the predominantly Catholic Rhineland. Prussia also made Bonn a garrison town. As a result, Bonn became popular as a retirement location for military officers. Tourism also grew after German unification in 1871, fueled by the [Romanticism on the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism_on_the_Rhine?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the time.



The government quarter in Bonn: roof of the [World Conference Center Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Conference_Center_Bonn?action=edit\&redlink=1) (front right), [Old Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeshaus_\(Bonn\)#Old_Parliament_House) and [Schürmann-Bau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%BCrmann-Bau) (center), [Langer Eugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langer_Eugen) (left), and [Post Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Tower) – with the [Siebengebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebengebirge) mountains in the background (2015)

After the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), the city was initially occupied by [Canadian](5042916.md), then [British](31717.md), and finally (until 1926) by [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) troops.

More than 1,000 Bonn residents, mostly of [Jewish](15624.md) descent, were murdered during the [Nazi era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_era) ([Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust)). About 8,000 people were forced to leave their hometown, were arrested, or imprisoned in [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camps). When [American troops](32087.md) entered Bonn on March 9, 1945, ending [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) for the city, 30% of the buildings were destroyed. Of these, 70% were slightly to severely damaged, and 30% were completely destroyed residential buildings. More than 4,000 Bonn residents had died in bombings. On May 28, 1945, Bonn became part of the [British occupation zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_zone).

After the Second World War, the city experienced rapid reconstruction and expansion, especially after the decision to make Bonn the provisional capital of the new Federal Republic of Germany instead of [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) on November 29, 1949[^cite_note-16] (see [Capital of Germany#The capital debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_Germany#The_capital_debate)). As a result of the [law implementing the Bundestag resolution of June 20, 1991, to complete German unification (Berlin/Bonn Act)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin/Bonn_Act)—which involved the relocation of the parliament, parts of the government, many diplomatic missions, lobbyists, and the privatization of the [German Federal Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bundespost)—the city underwent another transformation around the turn of the millennium. The remaining ministries, newly established federal agencies, headquarters of major German companies, [international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization_\(law\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), and institutions of science and research administration are now the drivers of this structural change, which has so far been considered successful and continues to this day.[^cite_note-17]

On October 30, 2014, under the patronage and active participation of Chancellor Angela Merkel, the [Unity Tree Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Trees?action=edit\&redlink=1) for German Unity was planted.[^cite_note-Einheitsbäume1-18][^cite_note-Einheitsbäume2-19][^cite_note-Einheitsbäume3-20]

### Municipal mergers

The city of Bonn was enlarged several times through [municipal mergers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_mergers?action=edit\&redlink=1). Around 1900, Bonn grew significantly. As a result, on June 1, 1904, the towns of [Poppelsdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppelsdorf), [Endenich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endenich), [Kessenich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kessenich_\(Bonn\)), and [Dottendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dottendorf?action=edit\&redlink=1)—which had already merged physically with Bonn—were incorporated.



Mergers on today's territory of Bonn&#x20;

&#x20; since 1899: Bad Godesberg

&#x20; since 1904: Bonn

&#x20; since 1952: Beuel

Through the [law on the municipal reorganization of the Bonn area ("Bonn Act")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1) of August 1, 1969, the city's population roughly doubled, and the [Sieg District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sieg_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) was merged with the [Bonn District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_District?action=edit\&redlink=1) to form the Rhein-Sieg District. The formerly independent cities of [Bad Godesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Godesberg) and [Beuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beuel) and the municipality of [Duisdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duisdorf?action=edit\&redlink=1) became independent boroughs of Bonn.

The borough of Beuel, on the right bank of the Rhine, was also assigned the villages of [Holzlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzlar), [Hoholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoholz?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Oberkassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkassel_\(Bonn\)) administrative area, which had previously belonged to the Sieg District. Bonn itself was expanded with the villages of [Ippendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ippendorf?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Röttgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6ttgen_\(Bonn\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Ückesdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cckesdorf?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Lessenich/Meßdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lessenich/Me%C3%9Fdorf?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Buschdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buschdorf_\(Bonn\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) from the former Bonn District, while [Lengsdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lengsdorf?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Duisdorf, along with some new housing developments, formed the borough of Hardtberg.[^cite_note-21]

The city of Bad Godesberg had already incorporated several villages earlier. As early as 1899, [Plittersdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plittersdorf_\(Bonn\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Rüngsdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCngsdorf?action=edit\&redlink=1) had joined Godesberg, and in 1904, [Friesdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesdorf_\(Bonn\)) was added, effectively merging Bad Godesberg with Bonn. In 1915, Bad Godesberg expanded southwest out of the valley, leading to the incorporation of [Muffendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muffendorf?action=edit\&redlink=1). On July 1, 1935, [Lannesdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lannesdorf?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Mehlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehlem?action=edit\&redlink=1) became districts of Bad Godesberg.

## Culture

Beethoven's birthplace is located in Bonngasse near the market place. Next to the market place is the Old City Hall, built in 1737 in [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) style, under the rule of [Clemens August of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemens_August_of_Bavaria). It is used for receptions of guests of the city, and as an office for the mayor. Nearby is the *[Kurfürstliches Schloss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Palace,_Bonn)*, built as a residence for the prince-elector and now the main building of the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn).&#x20;



Erected in the 11th and 13th century, the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) [Minster of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Minster) is one of Germany's oldest churches.

The *Poppelsdorfer Allee* is an avenue flanked by [chestnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut) trees which had the first [horsecar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsecar) of the city. It connects the *Kurfürstliches Schloss* with the *[Poppelsdorfer Schloss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppelsdorfer_Schloss)*, a palace that was built as a resort for the prince-electors in the first half of the 18th century, and whose grounds are now a [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) (the [Botanischer Garten Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanischer_Garten_Bonn)). This axis is interrupted by a railway line and [Bonn Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Hauptbahnhof), a building erected in 1883/84.

The [Beethoven Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beethoven_Monument_\(Bonn\)) stands on the Münsterplatz, which is flanked by the [Bonn Minster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Minster), one of Germany's oldest churches.

The three highest structures in the city are the [WDR](808025.md) [radio mast](2044496.md) in Bonn-Venusberg (180 m or 590 ft), the headquarters of the [Deutsche Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Post) called *[Post Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Tower)* (162.5 m or 533 ft) and the *[Langer Eugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langer_Eugen)* (114.7 m or 376 ft), originally offices for Members of the [Bundestag](3768.md), but today home of the [United Nations](31769.md) campus at Bonn.

### Museums



The [Bundeskunsthalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunst-_und_Ausstellungshalle_der_Bundesrepublik_Deutschland) focuses on the cultural heritage outside of Germany or Europe, at the crossroads of culture, the arts, and science.

Just as Bonn's other four major museums, the *[Haus der Geschichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haus_der_Geschichte)* or Museum of the History of the Federal Republic of Germany, is located on the so-called *Museumsmeile* ("Museum Mile")*.* The Haus der Geschichte is one of the foremost German museums of contemporary German history, with branches in [Berlin](3354.md) and [Leipzig](17955.md). In its permanent exhibition, the Haus der Geschichte presents German history from 1945 until the present, also shedding light on Bonn's own role as former capital of West Germany. Numerous temporary exhibitions emphasize different features, such as Nazism or important personalities in German history.[^cite_note-24]

The *[Kunstmuseum Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstmuseum_Bonn)* or Bonn Museum of Modern Art is an art museum founded in 1947. The Kunstmuseum exhibits both temporary exhibitions and its permanent collection. The latter is focused on Rhenish [Expressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionism) and post-war German art.[^cite_note-25] German artists on display include [Georg Baselitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Baselitz), [Joseph Beuys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Beuys), [Hanne Darboven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanne_Darboven), [Anselm Kiefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anselm_Kiefer), [Blinky Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinky_Palermo) and [Wolf Vostell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Vostell). The museum owns one of the largest collections of artwork by Expressionist painter [August Macke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Macke). His work is also on display in the [August-Macke-Haus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August-Macke-Haus), located in Macke's former home where he lived from 1911 to 1914.



The [Museum Koenig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Koenig) is Bonn's [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history) museum.

The [*Bundeskunsthalle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunst-_und_Ausstellungshalle_der_Bundesrepublik_Deutschland) (full name: Kunst- und Ausstellungshalle der Bundesrepublik Deutschland or Art and Exhibition Hall of the Federal Republic of Germany), focuses on the crossroads of culture, arts, and science. To date, it attracted more than 17 million visitors.[^cite_note-26] One of its main objectives is to show the [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage) outside of Germany or Europe.[^cite_note-27] Next to its changing exhibitions, the Bundeskunsthalle regularly hosts concerts, discussion panels, congresses, and lectures.

The *[Museum Koenig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Koenig)* is Bonn's natural history museum. Affiliated with the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), it is also a [zoological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology) research institution housing the *Leibniz-Institut für Biodiversität der Tiere*. Politically interesting, it is on the premises of the Museum Koenig where the [Parlamentarischer Rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parlamentarischer_Rat) first met.[^cite_note-28]

The *[Deutsches Museum Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum_Bonn)*, affiliated with one of the world's foremost science museums, the [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum) in Munich, is an interactive science museum focusing on post-war German scientists, engineers, and inventions.[^cite_note-29]

Other museums include the [Beethoven House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beethoven_House), birthplace of [Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven),[^cite_note-30] the [Rheinisches Landesmuseum Bonn](23264785.md) (Rhinish Regional Museum Bonn), the [Bonn Women's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Women's_Museum), the [Rheinisches Malermuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinisches_Malermuseum) and the [Arithmeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmeum).

### Nature



[Drachenburg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schloss_Drachenburg) in the [Siebengebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebengebirge) south of Bonn

There are several parks, leisure and protected areas in and around Bonn. The *[Rheinaue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinaue?action=edit\&redlink=1)* is Bonn's most important leisure park, with its role being comparable to what [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) is for New York City. It lies on the banks of the Rhine and is the city's biggest park intra muros. The Rhine promenade and the *Alter Zoll* (Old Toll Station) are in direct neighbourhood of the city centre and are popular amongst both residents and visitors. The *[Arboretum Park Härle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum_Park_H%C3%A4rle)* is an [arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum) with specimens dating to back to 1870. The *[Botanischer Garten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_Garden,_Bonn)* (Botanical Garden) is affiliated with the university. The natural reserve of *[Kottenforst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kottenforst)* is a large area of protected woods on the hills west of the city centre. It is about 40 square kilometres (15 square miles) in area and part of the [Rhineland Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland_Nature_Park) (1,045 km<sup>2</sup> or 403 sq mi).

In the very south of the city, on the border with [Wachtberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wachtberg) and [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md), there is an extinct volcano, the [Rodderberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodderberg), featuring a popular area for hikes. Also south of the city, there is the [Siebengebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebengebirge) which is part of the lower half of the [Middle Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Rhine) region. The nearby upper half of the [Middle Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Rhine) from [Bingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingen_am_Rhein) to [Koblenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koblenz) is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Europe) with more than 40 castles and fortresses from the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and important German vineyards.

### Air traffic



The [international airport of Cologne and Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Bonn_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association_airport_code): CGN) is Germany's [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Germany).

Named after [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer), the first post-war Chancellor of West Germany, [Cologne Bonn Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Bonn_Airport) is situated 15 kilometres (9.3 miles) north-east from the city centre of Bonn. With around 10.3 million passengers passing through it in 2015, it is the [seventh-largest passenger airport in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Germany) and the third-largest in terms of cargo operations. By traffic units, which combines cargo and passengers, the airport is in fifth position in Germany.[^cite_note-31] As of March 2015, Cologne Bonn Airport had services to 115 passenger destinations in 35 countries.[^cite_note-32] The airport is one of Germany's few 24-hour airports, and is a hub for [Eurowings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurowings) and cargo operators [FedEx Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx_Express) and [UPS Airlines](9704675.md).

The federal motorway (*Autobahn*) [A59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_59) connects the airport with the city. Long distance and regional trains to and from the airport stop at [Cologne/Bonn Airport station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne/Bonn_Airport_station). Another major airport within a one-hour drive by car is [Düsseldorf International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport).

### Rail and bus system



The underground [Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Stadtbahn) station at [Bonn Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Hauptbahnhof), Bonn's busiest railway station

Bonn's central railway station, [Bonn Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Hauptbahnhof) is the city's main public transportation hub. It lies just outside the old town and near the central university buildings. It is served by regional ([S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_S-Bahn) and [Regionalbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalbahn)) and long-distance ([IC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_\(Deutsche_Bahn\)) and [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity-Express)) trains. Daily, more than 67,000 people travel via Bonn Hauptbahnhof. In late 2016, around 80 long distance and more than 165 regional trains departed to or from Bonn every day.[^cite_note-general041116-33][^cite_note-ga.de-34][^cite_note-db041116-35] Another long-distance station, ([Siegburg/Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegburg/Bonn_station)), is located in the nearby town of [Siegburg](628297.md) and serves as Bonn's station on the [high-speed rail line between Cologne and Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ln%E2%80%93Frankfurt_high-speed_rail_line), offering faster connections to Southern Germany. It can be reached by Stadtbahn line 66 (approx. 25 minutes from central Bonn).

Bonn has a [Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtbahn) light rail and a tram system. The [Bonn Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Stadtbahn) has 4 regular lines that connect the main north–south axis (centre to [Bad Godesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Godesberg)) and quarters east of the Rhine ([Beuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beuel) and [Oberkassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkassel,_Bonn)), as well as many nearby towns like [Brühl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%BChl_\(Rhineland\)), [Wesseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesseling), [Sankt Augustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Augustin), [Siegburg](628297.md), [Königswinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigswinter), and [Bad Honnef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Honnef). All lines serve the Central Station and two lines continue to Cologne, where they connect to the [Cologne Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Stadtbahn). The [Bonn tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Bonn) system consists of two lines that connect closer quarters in the south, north and east of Bonn to the Central Station. While the Stadtbahn mostly has its own right-of-way, the tram often operates on general road lanes. A few sections of track are used by both systems. These urban rail lines are supplemented by a bus system of roughly 30 regular lines, especially since some parts of the city like [Hardtberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtberg) and most of [Bad Godesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Godesberg) completely lack a Stadtbahn/Tram connection. Several lines offer night services, especially during the weekends. Bonn is part of the [Verkehrsverbund Rhein-Sieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund_Rhein-Sieg) (*Rhine-Sieg Transport Association*) which is the public transport association covering the area of the [Cologne/Bonn Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne/Bonn_Region).

### Road network



Road network adjacent to Bonn

Four [Autobahns](1764276.md) run through or are adjacent to Bonn: the [A59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_59) (right bank of the Rhine, connecting Bonn with [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) and [Duisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duisburg)), the [A555](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_555) (left bank of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), connecting Bonn with Cologne), the [A562](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_562) (connecting the right with the left bank of the Rhine south of Bonn), and the [A565](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_565) (connecting the A59 and the A555 with the [A61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_61) to the southwest). Three Bundesstraßen, which have a general 100 kilometres per hour (62 miles per hour) [speed limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limit) in contrast to the Autobahn, connect Bonn to its immediate surroundings (Bundesstraßen B9, B42 and B56).

With Bonn being divided into two parts by the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), three bridges are crucial for inner-city road traffic: the Konrad-Adenauer-Brücke (A562) in the South, the Friedrich-Ebert-Brücke (A565) in the North, and the Kennedybrücke (B56) in the centre. In addition, regular ferries operate between Bonn-Mehlem and [Königswinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigswinter), Bonn-[Bad Godesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Godesberg) and Königswinter-[Niederdollendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederdollendorf), and Bonn-Graurheindorf and [Niederkassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederkassel)-Mondorf.

### Port

Located in the northern sub-district of Graurheindorf, the [inland harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_harbor) of Bonn is used for container traffic as well as oversea transport. The annual turnover amounts to around 500,000 t (490,000 long tons; 550,000 short tons). Regular passenger transport occurs to Cologne and Düsseldorf.

## Sports

Bonn is home of the [Telekom Baskets Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telekom_Baskets_Bonn), the only basketball club in Germany that owns its arena, the [Telekom Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telekom_Dome).[^cite_note-43]

The city also has a semi-professional football team [Bonner SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonner_SC) which was formed in 1965 through the merger of *Bonner FV* and *Tura Bonn*.

The successful [German Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_in_Germany) team [Bonn Capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Capitals) are also found in the city of Bonn.

The headquarters of the [International Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Paralympic_Committee) has been located in Bonn since 1999.

## International relations

See also: [List of twin towns and sister cities in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_twin_towns_and_sister_cities_in_Germany)

Since 1983, the City of Bonn has established friendship relations with the City of [Tel Aviv](31453.md), Israel, and since 1988 Bonn, in former times the residence of the Princes Electors of Cologne, and [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam), Germany, the formerly most important residential city of the Prussian rulers, have established a city-to-city partnership.

Central Bonn is surrounded by a number of traditional towns and villages which were independent up to several decades ago. As many of those communities had already established their own contacts and partnerships before the regional and local reorganisation in 1969, the Federal City of Bonn now has a dense network of city district partnerships with European partner towns.

The city district of Bonn is a partner of the English university city of [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), England, UK (since 1947), of [Budafok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budafok), District XXII of [Budapest](36787.md), [Hungary](13275.md) (since 1991) and of [Opole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opole), [Poland](22936.md) (officially since 1997; contacts were established 1954).

The district of Bad Godesberg has established partnerships with [Saint-Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Cloud) in France, [Frascati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frascati) in Italy, [Windsor and Maidenhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Borough_of_Windsor_and_Maidenhead) in England, UK and [Kortrijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kortrijk) in Belgium; a friendship agreement has been signed with the town of [Yalova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalova), Turkey.

The district of Beuel on the right bank of the Rhine and the city district of Hardtberg foster partnerships with towns in France: [Mirecourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirecourt) and [Villemomble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villemomble).

Moreover, the city of Bonn has developed a concept of international co-operation and maintains sustainability oriented project partnerships in addition to traditional city twinning, among others with [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) in Belarus, [Ulaanbaatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulaanbaatar) in Mongolia, [Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara) in Uzbekistan, [Chengdu](170375.md) in China and [La Paz](42821.md) in Bolivia.
