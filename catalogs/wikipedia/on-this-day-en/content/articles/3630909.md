[Norway](21241.md) first participated at the [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md) in 1900, and has sent athletes to compete in every Summer Games since then, except for the [1980 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics) in [Moscow](19004.md) when they participated in the [American-led boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-led_boycott_of_the_1980_Summer_Olympics) and, as previously thought, the [1904 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics) in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri), [United States](3434750.md). However, at the beginning of the 21st century, it was discovered that wrestlers [Charles Ericksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ericksen) and [Bernhoff Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhoff_Hansen), who were Norwegian expatriates in America whose medals at the 1904 Summer Olympics were previously attributed to United States, still held Norwegian citizenship at the time of the games. They won the gold medals in the wrestling [welterweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_freestyle_welterweight) and [heavyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_freestyle_heavyweight) events respectively.[^cite_note-post2024_wrest_welter-1][^cite_note-post2024_wrest_heavy-2]

Norwegian athletes have won a total of 169 medals at the Summer Olympics, with [sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_\(sport\)) and [shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_sports) as the top medal-producing sports. At the [Winter Olympic Games](34069.md), Norway has won 447 total medals including 166 gold medals, both considerably more than any other nation in Winter Olympic history. More than half of these medals have come from [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing_\(sport\)) and [speed skating](28028.md) (it used to be half the disciplines). Norway is one of only three nations (along with [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_at_the_Olympics) and [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_at_the_Olympics)) to have won more medals at the Winter Games than at the Summer Games.

The [National Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) for Norway was created and recognized in 1900.

## Disputed medals at the 1904 Summer Olympics

See also: [1904 Summer Olympics medal table § Changes in medal standings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics_medal_table#Changes_in_medal_standings)

The International Olympic Committee originally considered [Norwegian](21241.md)-American wrestlers [Charles Ericksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ericksen) and [Bernhoff Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhoff_Hansen) to have competed for the United States (both were Norwegian immigrants to the US); each won a gold medal. In 2012, Norwegian historians however found documentation showing that Ericksen did not receive American citizenship until March 22, 1905, and that Hansen, who was registered as an "alien" (foreigner) as late as 1925, probably never received American citizenship. The historians have therefore petitioned to have the athletes registered as Norwegians.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] In May 2013, it was reported that the [Norwegian Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Olympic_Committee) had filed a formal application for changing the nationality of the wrestlers in IOC's medal database.[^cite_note-5] In July 2021, the IOC modified data in the official database, recognizing Ericksen and Hansen as having competed for Norway.[^cite_note-pre2024_wrest_welter-6][^cite_note-pre2024_wrest_heavy-7]

Since 2024, the IOC has considered the National Olympic Committee (NOC) affiliation of the club or team for which an athlete competed to be the decisive factor for the first three Olympic Games (1896–1904), attributing participation and medals accordingly. In the case of Ericksen and Hansen, both medals are attributed to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics),[^cite_note-post2024_wrest_welter-1][^cite_note-post2024_wrest_heavy-2] as both competed for the American club Brooklyn Norwegier Turnverein.[^cite_note-8]

## Medals in art competitions

Main article: [List of Olympic medalists in art competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Olympic_medalists_in_art_competitions)

In addition to its accomplishments in sport, Norway has also earned recognition in Olympic [art competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_competitions_at_the_Summer_Olympics)—one of the three [non-sports events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_sports#Feats_and_artistic_events) once included in the Olympic Games. The country won its sole art competition medal, a silver, at the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_competitions_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-olympics_nor-11] These events were part of the official Olympic program in seven Summer Games, from 1912 to 1948. In 1952, the International Olympic Committee (IOC) formally discontinued all non-sport events (including art competitions), as well as awards for feats (such as [alpinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpinism_at_the_Olympic_Games) and [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics)). These were subsequently removed from official national medal counts.[^cite_note-e048-14][^cite_note-art-12]
