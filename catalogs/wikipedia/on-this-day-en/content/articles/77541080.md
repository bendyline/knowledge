[Presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election) are scheduled to be held in the [United States](3434750.md) on November 7, 2028, to elect the [president](24113.md) and [vice president](32759.md) to a four-year term, with the winners scheduled to be inaugurated on January 20, 2029.&#x20;

Incumbent Republican president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) is ineligible to run for a third term due to the term limits established by the [22nd Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) to the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). As a result, this is the first election since [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election) where Trump is not the Republican nominee.

## Background

Main articles: [2024 United States presidential election](59568546.md) and [Second presidency of Donald Trump](78298108.md)



[Donald Trump](4848272.md), the incumbent president, whose non-consecutive second term will expire at noon on January 20, 2029

The Republican Party returned to power in the United States in January 2025 with a [government trifecta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_trifecta)—controlling the Senate, House of Representatives, and the presidency—following the 2024 elections. Donald Trump, who was elected president [in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election) before losing a re-election bid [in 2020](48410011.md) to [Joe Biden](145422.md), defeated Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris), who immediately began [her campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris_2024_presidential_campaign) following [Biden's withdrawal from his re-election bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_Joe_Biden_from_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election). Trump's victory was attributed to [a post-pandemic surge in inflation](69605317.md#United_States),[^cite_note-4] and [a migrant crisis at the US–Mexico border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border_crisis),[^cite_note-8] amidst the global [anti-incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-incumbency) backlash.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] In March 2024, the [Supreme Court](31737.md) unanimously ruled in *[Trump v. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._Anderson)* that Trump could seek office after challenges to his ballot eligibility were raised by several state attorneys general.[^cite_note-12]

[JD Vance](53396477.md), a U.S. senator from [Ohio](22199.md), was selected as Trump's [running mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_mate)[^cite_note-13] and subsequently elected vice president.&#x20;

In addition, Republicans secured control of the Senate, flipping four seats and earning a three-seat majority, while also retaining a House majority, though the party's majority narrowed to three seats after losing two seats.[^cite_note-14]

## Electoral system

Main article: [United States Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College)

See also: [United States presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_primary) and [Primary elections in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_elections_in_the_United_States)

The president and vice president of the United States are elected through the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College), determined by the number of senators and representatives with an additional three representatives for [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) A majority of 270 votes is needed to win the election. 48 states use a [winner-take-all system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winner-take-all_system), in which states award all of their electors to the winner of the popular vote. In [Maine](19977.md) and [Nebraska](21647.md), two votes are allocated to the winner of the popular vote, while each of the individual congressional districts have one vote. Electoral votes are cast by state electors in December and are counted by [Congress](31756.md) on January 6.[^cite_note-15] Presidential candidates are selected in a [presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_primary), conducted through [primary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_elections_in_the_United_States) run by state governments or [caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus#Caucuses_to_select_election_candidates) run by state parties which bind convention delegates to candidates.[^cite_note-16] A [brokered convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokered_convention) occurs when a candidate does not receive a majority of votes on the first round of voting,[^cite_note-17] or when a candidate withdraws.[^cite_note-18]

[Election Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Day_\(United_States\)) in the United States is held on the first Tuesday that falls after the first Monday in November.[^cite_note-19] The 2028 presidential election will occur on November 7, 2028.

### Eligibility

The [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) limits occupancy of the presidency to individuals who are at least thirty-five, who have been a [resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_\(domicile\)#United_States) in the United States for at least fourteen years, and who are a [natural-born citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural-born-citizen_clause_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-20] Section three of the [Fourteenth Amendment](31666.md) prevents current and former federal, state, and military officials from holding office—including the presidency—if they have "engaged in insurrection or rebellion" against the United States.[^cite_note-21] A convicted felon may serve as president.[^cite_note-22] Incumbent president Trump, along with former presidents [Bill Clinton](3356.md), [George W. Bush](3414021.md), and [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama), are ineligible to be elected to a third term, as the [Twenty-second Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) prohibits any person from being elected president more than twice.[^cite_note-23]

#### Trump's suggestions on running for a third term

Main article: [Donald Trump third term proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_third_term_proposal)

See also: [Republican Party efforts to disrupt voting after the 2024 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_efforts_to_disrupt_voting_after_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election)



Trump meeting Democratic congressional leaders in September 2025, with Trump 2028 hats displayed

Since beginning his [second term](78298108.md) in January 2025, Trump has floated the possibility of seeking a third term. On October 27, 2025, when asked about a third term by a reporter on [Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_One), Trump said that he would "love to do it". Trump did rule out a run for vice president, stating that it "wouldn't be right". A hypothetical third term would give Trump four additional years as president after 2028, going through the 2032 United States presidential election, and ending on January 20, 2033;[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] however, the possibility of a third term is [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) under the [Twenty-second Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution), barring all former and current presidents from seeking more than two terms in office. If Trump intends to have the amendment repealed, he would have to either require approval from two-thirds of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, or he would need a constitutional convention called by two-thirds of state legislatures, a process viewed by experts as extremely unlikely.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### Electoral map

Main article: [Red states and blue states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states)



This map shows how partisan states are by the [Cook Partisan Voting Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Partisan_Voting_Index). Swing states are the states in lighter colors.

Most US states are usually not highly competitive in presidential elections, often voting consistently for the same party due to longstanding demographic and ideological differences. In the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College), this results in major-party candidates primarily focusing their campaigns on [swing states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_states), which can swing between parties from election to election. These states are critical for a presidential candidate's path to victory. For 2028, the expected swing states likely include the [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt) states of [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [Wisconsin](33127.md), and [Michigan](18859.md), the [Sun Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Belt) states of [Arizona](21883824.md), [Georgia](48830.md), [Nevada](21216.md), and [North Carolina](21650.md) and the [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) state of [New Hampshire](21134.md), with all except for New Hampshire having narrowly voted for Trump in 2024 and all except for North Carolina having narrowly voted for [Joe Biden](145422.md) in 2020.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Other possible swing states include traditional Democratic strongholds that shifted toward Trump and were only narrowly won by Harris in 2024: [New Jersey](21648.md)[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] and [Minnesota](19590.md);[^cite_note-33] though 2025 polls show a reversal of pro-Trump trends.[^cite_note-34]

States formerly considered swing states, such as [Florida](18933066.md), [Iowa](26810748.md), and [Ohio](22199.md), voted for Trump in all three of his previous election bids, as well as for Republican candidates in other state and federal elections, with increasing margins in each election and are now seen as solidly red states. Similarly, [Maine's 2nd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine's_2nd_congressional_district), which awards 1 electoral vote, has not voted Democratic in any statewide race since [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election_in_Maine) and is now considered a solid red district.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Former red or swing states that, since 2008, have consistently voted Democratic include [Colorado](5399.md), [New Mexico](21649.md), and [Virginia](32432.md).[^cite_note-39] Due to its recent record of voting Democratic even during Republican national victories, [Nebraska's 2nd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_2nd_congressional_district) has been called "the Blue Dot."[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]
