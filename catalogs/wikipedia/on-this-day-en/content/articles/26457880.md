**Air India** is the [flag carrier](465816.md) of India, headquartered in [Gurugram, Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurugram,_Haryana). Its primary hub is located at [Indira Gandhi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport) in [Delhi](37756.md), with secondary hubs at [Kempegowda International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempegowda_International_Airport) and [Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport). The airline is owned by [Air India Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Limited), which is owned by the [Tata Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group) (74.9%) and [Singapore Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Airlines) (25.1%). As of November 2025, the airline serves 87 domestic and international destinations, operates a variety of [Airbus](26220236.md) and [Boeing](18933266.md) aircraft, and is the second-largest airline in India by passenger volume, after [IndiGo](2085799.md). Air India became the 27th member of [Star Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Alliance) on 11 July 2014.

Founded in 1932 as Tata Airlines by [J. R. D. Tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._D._Tata), the airline was [nationalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalised) by the Government of India in 1953 and renamed to *Air India* following [World War II](32927.md). On 21 February 1960, it took delivery of its first [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) named *Gauri Shankar* and became the first Asian airline to induct a jet aircraft in its fleet. In 2000 to 2001, attempts were made to privatise Air India, and from 2006 onwards it suffered losses following its merger with [Indian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Airlines). Another privatisation attempt was launched in 2017, culminating in the return of ownership of the airline and associated properties to the Tata Group after 69 years in 2022.[^cite_note-4]

Air India also operates flights to domestic and Asian destinations through its subsidiary [Air India Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Express). Air India operates a mix of [narrow body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_body_aircraft) such as the [Airbus A320 family](164933.md) used for most domestic and short-haul international routes and [wide body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_body_aircraft) such as the [Airbus A350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350), [Boeing 777](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777), and [Boeing 787](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787) aircraft for long haul international routes. Air India's mascot is the *[Maharajah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja)* ('great king'), and the erstwhile logo consisted of a flying swan with the [wheel of Konark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konark_Sun_Temple) inside it, before being replaced by a new logo inspired by the airline's [Jharokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharokha) window pattern in 2023.

## History

### Early years (1932–1945)



Tata Air Service

Air India had its origin in [Tata Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group), founded by [J. R. D. Tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._D._Tata), an Indian aviator and business tycoon.[^cite_note-5] In April 1932, Tata won a contract to carry mail for [Imperial Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Airways) and the aviation department of Tata Sons was formed with two single-engine [de Havilland Puss Moths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Puss_Moth). On 15 October 1932, Tata flew a Puss Moth carrying [air mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mail) from [Karachi](17123.md) to [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juhu_aerodrome) (currently [Mumbai](19189.md)) and the aircraft continued to [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_International_Airport) (currently [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai)) piloted by [Nevill Vintcent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevill_Vintcent), a former [Royal Air Force](25679.md) pilot and friend of Tata.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-history-7] The airline fleet consisted of a Puss Moth aircraft and a [Leopard Moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Leopard_Moth).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-isbn8120813227-9] Initial service included weekly airmail service between Karachi and Madras via [Ahmedabad](206337.md) and Bombay. In its first year of operation, the airline flew 260,000 kilometres (160,000 mi), carrying 155 passengers and 9.72 tonnes (10.71 tons) of mail and made a profit of [₹](490783.md)60,000 (US\$620).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Later, the airline launched a domestic flight from Bombay to [Trivandrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram_International_Airport) (now [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram)) with a six-seater [Miles Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Merlin).[^cite_note-12] In 1938, it was re-christened as *Tata Air Services* and later as *Tata Airlines*. [Delhi](37756.md) and [Colombo](56636.md) were added to the destinations in 1938.[^cite_note-history-7] During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the airline helped the Royal Air Force with troop movements, shipping of supplies, rescue of refugees and maintenance of planes.[^cite_note-history-7]

### Post-Independence (1946–2000)



Air India became the first Asian carrier to induct a jet aircraft with the [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707)–420&#x20;



Air India Boeing 747-200B. The airline operated 11 Boeing 747-200B aircraft in total.

After [World War II](32927.md), regular commercial service was restored in India and Tata Airlines became a [public limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_limited_company) on 29 July 1946 under the name *Air India*.[^cite_note-Britannica-2] After the [Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_India) in 1947, 49% of the airline was acquired by the [Government of India](553883.md) in 1948.[^cite_note-13] On 8 June 1948, a [Lockheed Constellation L-749A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Constellation) named *Malabar Princess* (registered *VT-CQP*) took off from [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_International_Airport) bound for [London Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow) marking the airline's first international flight.[^cite_note-history-7] In 1953, the Government of India passed the Air Corporations Act and purchased a majority stake in the carrier from [Tata Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Sons), though its founder, J. R. D. Tata, would continue as chairman until 1977.[^cite_note-14] The company was renamed as Air India International Limited and the domestic services were transferred to [Indian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Airlines) as a part of restructuring.[^cite_note-15] From 1948 to 1950, the airline introduced services to [Nairobi](21482.md) in Africa and to major European destinations Rome, Paris and [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf).[^cite_note-Timeline-16] The airline took delivery of its first Lockheed Constellation L-1049 named *Rani of Jhansi* (registered *VT-DGL*) and inaugurated services to [Bangkok](56615.md), Hong Kong, Tokyo and Singapore.[^cite_note-Timeline-16]

On 21 February 1960, Air India International inducted its first [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707)–420 named *Gauri Shankar* (registered *VT-DJJ*), thereby becoming the first Asian airline to induct a jet aircraft in its fleet.[^cite_note-Jet-17][^cite_note-18] The airline inaugurated services to [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFK_International_Airport) on 14 May 1960.[^cite_note-Timeline-16] On 8 June 1962, the airline's name was officially truncated to *Air India*[^cite_note-Britannica-2] and on 11 June 1962, Air India became the world's first all-jet airline.

In 1971, the airline took delivery of its first [Boeing 747-200B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-200B) named *Emperor Ashoka* (registered *VT-EBD*)[^cite_note-nytimes.com-19] and introduced a new *Palace in the Sky* livery and branding. The airline operated 11 Boeing 747-200 in total. In 1986, Air India took delivery of its first [Airbus A310-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310) and in 1988, the airline took delivery of Boeing 747-300M.[^cite_note-Timeline-16] In 1993, Air India took delivery of a [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) named *Konark* (registered *VT-ESM*) and operated the first non-stop flight between New York and Delhi.[^cite_note-Jumbo-20]

### Financial trouble, merger with Indian and later (2000–2022)



Air India [Airbus A310-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310-300). It sold three A300s in March 2009 due to debt.



In January 2013, Air India cleared some of its debt by selling and leasing back the newly acquired [Boeing 787 Dreamliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner). Also, the airline posted its first positive [EBITDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBITDA) after almost six years.

In 2000–01, attempts were made to re-privatise Air India.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] In 2000, Air India introduced services to Shanghai, China. On 23 May 2001, the [Ministry of Civil Aviation](10257026.md) charged Michael Mascarenhas, the then-managing director, with corruption. According to the ministry reports, the airline lost approximately ₹570 million (US\$5.9 million) because of extra commissions that Mascarenhas sanctioned, and he was later suspended from the airline.[^cite_note-25] In May 2004, Air India launched a wholly owned [low cost subsidiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_cost_airline) called [Air-India Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-India_Express) connecting cities in India with the Middle East and Southeast Asia. Until 2007, Air India mainly operated on international long-haul routes while Indian Airlines operated on domestic and international short-haul routes. In 2007, Air India and Indian Airlines were merged under [Air India Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Limited)[^cite_note-26] and the airline took delivery of its first [Boeing 777](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777) aircraft.[^cite_note-Timeline-16] The airline was invited to be a part of the Star Alliance in 2007.[^cite_note-27]

Around 2006, both Air India and [Indian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Airlines) showed signs of financial crisis as combined losses were ₹7.7 billion (US\$80 million). After the merger of Indian Airlines with Air India in 2007, the losses went up to ₹72 billion (US\$750 million) by March 2009.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] In July 2009, [State Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Bank_of_India) was appointed to prepare a road map for the recovery of the airline.[^cite_note-30] The carrier sold three [Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300) and one [Boeing 747-300M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) in March 2009 for \$18.75 million to finance the debt.[^cite_note-31] By March 2011, Air India had accumulated a debt of ₹426 billion (US\$4.4 billion) and an operating loss of ₹220 billion (US\$2.3 billion), and was seeking ₹429 billion (US\$4.5 billion) from the government.[^cite_note-ref1-32][^cite_note-33] A report by the [Comptroller and Auditor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptroller_and_Auditor_General_of_India) blamed the decision to buy 111 new planes and the ill-timed merger with Indian Airlines for the poor financial situation.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] In August 2011, the invitation to join Star Alliance was suspended as a result of its failure to meet the minimum standards for the membership.[^cite_note-on_hold-36][^cite_note-37] The government pumped ₹32 billion (US\$330 million) into Air India in March 2012.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]



Air India [B777-300ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777)

On 1 March 2009, Air India made [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) its international hub for onward connections to the United States from India. However, the airline shut down the Frankfurt hub on 30 October 2010 because of high operating costs.[^cite_note-40] In 2010, financially less lucrative routes were terminated and the airline planned to open a new hub for its international flights at [Dubai](154742.md).[^cite_note-businessworld1-41] In 2012, a study commissioned by the Corporate Affairs Ministry recommended that Air India should be partly privatised.[^cite_note-42] In May 2012, the carrier invited offers from banks to raise \$800 million via [external commercial borrowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_commercial_borrowing) and [bridge financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_financing).[^cite_note-43] In May 2012, the airline was fined \$80,000 by the US Transportation Department for failing to post customer service and tarmac delay contingency plans on its website and adequately inform passengers about its optional fees.[^cite_note-44]



Air India [A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A320neo)

In 2013, the then-Civil Aviation Minister [Ajit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajit_Singh_\(politician,_born_1939\)) stated privatisation was the key to the airline's survival.[^cite_note-45] However, the opposition led by the [BJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BJP) and the [CPI(M)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPI\(M\)) slammed the government.[^cite_note-46] In 2013, the Indian government planned to delay equity infusion of ₹300 billion (US\$3.1 billion) that was slated to be infused into the airline slowly over eight years.[^cite_note-47] In January 2013, Air India cleared a part of its pending dues through funds raised by selling and leasing back the newly acquired [Boeing 787 Dreamliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] In March 2013, the airline posted its first positive [EBITDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBITDA) after almost six years and 20% growth in its operating revenue since the previous financial year.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Air India Limited split its engineering and cargo businesses into two separate subsidiaries, Air India Engineering Services Limited (AIESL) and Air India Transport Services Limited (AITSL) in 2013.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In December 2013, the airline appointed veteran pilot SPS Puri as its head of operations. The appointment was criticised by the Air India pilots union as Puri allegedly has multiple violations to his name.[^cite_note-54]



Air India New [B787-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner)

Air India became the 27th member of Star Alliance on 11 July 2014.[^cite_note-Star_Alliance-55][^cite_note-\:0-56] In August 2015, it signed an agreement with [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank) and [State Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Bank_of_India) to raise \$300 million in external commercial borrowing to meet working capital requirements.[^cite_note-\:1-57] For [FY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_year) 2014–15, its revenue, operating loss and net loss were ₹198 billion (US\$2.1 billion), ₹2.171 billion (US\$23 million) and ₹5.41 billion (US\$56 million) compared FY 2011–12, which were ₹147 billion (US\$1.5 billion), ₹5.138 billion (US\$53 million) and ₹7.55 billion (US\$78 million).[^cite_note-ndtv.com-58] As of February 2016, Air India was the third largest carrier in India, after [IndiGo](2085799.md) and [Jet Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Airways) with a market share of 15.4%.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

On 21 May 2021, it was reported that Air India was subjected to a cyberattack where the personal details of about 4.5 million customers around the world were compromised, including passport, credit card details, birthdates, name and ticket information.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-\:02-64][^cite_note-65] Air India's data processor, [SITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SITA_\(business_services_company\)), reported the [data breach](13307577.md) to Air India in around February 2021.[^cite_note-\:02-64][^cite_note-66] The data breach involved all information which was registered in the SITA data processor between 26 August 2011 and 20 February 2021,[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] and the cyberattackers gained access to the systems for a period of 22 days.[^cite_note-70] Air India told passengers that there was no conclusive evidence on whether any misuse of the personal data had been reported[^cite_note-71] and urged passengers to immediately change their passwords.[^cite_note-72]

### Post privatisation (2022–present)

On 28 June 2017, the [Government of India](553883.md) approved the [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_company) of Air India and set up a committee to decide the details.[^cite_note-73] In March 2018, the Government issued an Expression of Interest (EOI) to sell a 76% stake in Air India, along with [low-cost airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_airline) [Air India Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Express), and a 50% stake in AISATS, a ground handling joint venture with [Singapore Airport Terminal Services (SATS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SATS_\(company\)). According to the EOI, the new owner would have to take on a debt of ₹333.92 billion (US\$3.5 billion) and a bid would have to be submitted by mid-May as the Government wanted to complete the selling process by the end of 2018.[^cite_note-74] However, no private firms showed any interest to buy a share in the debt-laden airline.[^cite_note-75]

Having failed to sell off a majority stake in the airline, the Government decided to completely exit the airline and invited fresh EOIs on 27 January 2020. In order to attract bidders this time, the government reduced the debt burden on Air India by moving nearly ₹300 billion (US\$3.1 billion) of the company's debts and liabilities to a [Special Purpose Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Purpose_Vehicle) (SPV).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] In view of the prevailing situation arising out of [COVID-19](63030231.md), the last date for submission of interest was extended multiple times and the Government eventually received EOIs from seven parties by December 2020.[^cite_note-78] Five of these parties were disqualified and the Government invited financial bids for Air India from the two qualified parties in September 2021.[^cite_note-79]

In September 2021, [Spice Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_Jet)'s [Ajay Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajay_Singh_\(entrepreneur\))-led consortium and Tata Sons submitted their financial bids for Air India.[^cite_note-80] On 8 October 2021, Air India along with its low cost carrier [Air India Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Express) and fifty per cent of AISATS, a ground handling company, were sold for ₹180 billion (US\$1.9 billion) to [Tata Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] On 27 January 2022, the airline was officially handed over to Tata Group.[^cite_note-83] On 14 February 2022, after its re-privatisation, the airline appointed [İlker Aycı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0lker_Ayc%C4%B1), former chairman of [Turkish Airlines](167925.md) as its new CEO and managing director to take charge on or before 1 April 2022 which did not materialise.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] In March 2022, [Natarajan Chandrasekaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natarajan_Chandrasekaran), the chairman of Tata Sons was appointed as the chairman of the airline and in May 2022, [Campbell Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_Wilson) was announced as the [CEO](52234.md) and [MD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managing_Director).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

After the airline's acquisition, Tata Group began discussions on bringing the other airlines, including [Vistara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistara) and [AirAsia India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirAsia_India), where it held a stake, under a unified umbrella. In November 2022, Air India acquired [AirAsia](285974.md)'s stake in AirAsia India, renamed it AIX Connect and announced plans to merge it with Air India Express.[^cite_note-88] On 29 November 2022, Air India announced the merger with Vistara by March 2024 with [Singapore Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Airlines) which owns a 49% stake in Vistara getting a 25% stake in the newly formed airline.[^cite_note-89] Vistara brand would be discontinued post the merger and the brand will operate under the Air India name.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] The [National Company Law Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Company_Law_Tribunal) approved the merger between Air India and Vistara on 6 June 2024, followed by Singapore Airlines' FDI approval by the Indian government on 30 August 2024.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] On 12 November 2024, the merger between Vistara and Air India was completed.[^cite_note-97]

On 15 September 2022, the Tata Group announced Vihaan, a five-year transformation goal aimed at restructuring and transforming Air India in phases.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] As part of the plan, Air India announced flights to additional international destinations.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] The route expansion was supported by improving air-worthiness of existing fleet and leasing aircraft from other airlines.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] On 14 February 2023, Air India announced an order for 470 aircraft consisting of 250 from [Airbus](26220236.md) and 220 from [Boeing](18933266.md) at a cost of US\$70 billion, which set the record for the world's largest aircraft order at the time.[^cite_note-Rec-106] On 10 August 2023, the airline announced a rebranding exercise named "Vista" with a new livery and logo.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] On 1 January 2024, Air India announced that it will begin operating its first Airbus A350 aircraft on domestic routes from 22 January 2024 before expanding to international destinations.[^cite_note-109]

In July 2024, Air India was reported to be "consolidating its cargo operations" and plans to start a separate entity or a subsidiary like [Air India Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Cargo) to handle cargo operations with dedicated [freighters](2375692.md). There has been a 30% increase in the cargo revenues of the Air India Group in FY22-24. The cargo volume is expected to increase to 2.5 million tonnes by 2027. Air India is also developing a "mother software" to handle cargo data and revenue management. The airline is streamlining its cargo operations for optimisation of cargo handling. The airline has initiated a programme to "onboard, engage and expand with regional, national and global customers".[^cite_note-\:3-110]

In September 2024, Air India announced a \$400 million refurbishment programme to modernise 67 of its older aircraft. The programme will commence with the overhaul of 27 narrow-body [Airbus A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo_family) planes, followed by 40 wide-body Boeing aircraft.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

In October 2025, Air India was reported to be in advanced talks to acquire up to 300 additional aircraft from Airbus and Boeing, including 80–100 widebody models such as the Airbus A350 or Boeing 777X, as part of its fleet renewal and international expansion.[^cite_note-113] In November 2025, Air India discovered and repositioned a mothballed Boeing 737-200 that had been abandoned at Kolkata's airport, which had been left outside records for thirteen years after service with the [India Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Post). The plane, that Air India recorded as formally retired in 2012, was shipped to the firm's office in Bengaluru, where it will be used to train maintenance engineers and flight crew.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

In August 2026, the Board of Directors appointed former [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) Group chief [Tewolde Gebremariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tewolde_Gebremariam) as Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director succeeding Campbell Wilson.[^cite_note-116]

## Corporate affairs and identity

Air India Limited is headquartered in [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon), [Haryana](14189.md),[^cite_note-130] in Sector 16. Its head office is in 180,000 square feet (17,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of leased space on the ground floor and floors 1–6 in Block 5 in the Vatika One-on-One complex, and the lease began on 29 September 2023.[^cite_note-131] Air India Express, since 2024, occupies space in the same complex.[^cite_note-132]

### Mascot

Air India's mascot is *the [Maharajah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja)* (*[high king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_king)*).[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-Maharaja-145] It was created by Bobby Kooka, the then-commercial director of Air India, and Umesh Rao, an artist with J. Walter Thompson Limited in 1946.[^cite_note-146] Kooka stated that, "We call him a Maharajah for want of a better description. But his blood isn't blue. He may look like royalty, but he isn't royal".[^cite_note-147] Air India adopted the Maharajah as its mascot in 1946. It was used in promoting it although initially designed only for the airline's memo-pads.[^cite_note-148] The Maharajah was given a makeover in 2015 and the brand is represented by a younger version.[^cite_note-149] In 2023, the Maharajah was given a makeover and was revealed that the mascot would only be used in certain aspects of the airline like the premium lounges and crockery.[^cite_note-150]

### Logo and livery

Air India's colour scheme is red and white. The aircraft was painted white with red palace-style carvings on the outside of the windows and the airline's name written in red. The name is written in Hindi on the port side fuselage and in English on the port side tail. On the starboard side, the name is written in English on the fuselage and in Hindi on the tail.[^cite_note-151] The window scheme was designed in line with the slogan *Your Palace in the Sky*.[^cite_note-Timeline-16] The aircraft was earlier named after Indian kings and landmarks. In 1989, to supplement its *Flying Palace* livery, Air India introduced a new livery that included a metallic gold spinning wheel on a deep red-coloured tail and a Boeing 747, *Rajendra Chola*, was the first aircraft to be painted in the new colours.[^cite_note-Timeline-16]



Airbus A310 part of the now defunct Air India Cargo.

The first logo of Air India was a [centaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centaur), a stylised version of [Sagittarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_\(constellation\)) shooting an arrow in a circle representing the [wheel of Konark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konark_temple). The logo chosen by founder J. R. D. Tata was introduced in 1948 and represented the airline until 2007.[^cite_note-152] On 22 May 2007, Air India and Indian Airlines unveiled their new livery consisting of a Flying Swan with the wheel of Konark placed inside it. The flying swan was morphed from the centaur logo, and the chakra was derived from Indian's erstwhile logo.[^cite_note-153] On 15 May 2007, Air India refreshed its livery, making the Rajasthani arches along the windows slightly smaller, extending a stylised line from the tail of the aircraft to the nose and painting the underbelly red. The new logo features on the tail and the engine covers with red and orange lines running parallel to each other from the front door to the rear door.[^cite_note-Maharaja-145]

After the airline's acquisition by the Tata Group, the airline revealed its brand new logo and livery on 10 August 2023.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] The new livery features a palette of deep red, aubergine, and gold with [chakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakra) pattern and the new logo is inspired by the airline's [Jharokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharokha) window pattern.[^cite_note-156] In December 2023, the airline unveiled new uniforms for its crew and rolled out its new identity with an [Airbus A350-900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350-900), the first aircraft to be re-branded.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

### Art collection

Air India maintained a collection of Indian art from 1956 comprising works of important Indian artists and photographers, sculptures, wood carvings, glass paintings, rare textiles etc.[^cite_note-Art-160] The artworks were often sent to be hung in Air India [booking offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_ticket_office) around the world and used in promotional material.[^cite_note-Art-160] In 1967, the company commissioned ashtrays from [Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD) and gifted an elephant calf as payment.[^cite_note-161] In 2010, a plan to establish a museum from the collection was stalled and the artworks reside in a building in Nariman Point, Mumbai.[^cite_note-Art-160] Air India organised the first ever exhibition of these art at the [National Gallery of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Modern_Art) in Delhi, titled *Air India Salutes Indian Masters* in 2013.[^cite_note-162] Following a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) agreed between the airline and the [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(India\)), the art collection was transferred to the [National Gallery of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Modern_Art,_Mumbai) (NGMA) in Mumbai in January 2023.[^cite_note-163] The art was put up for exhibition titled *Maharaja's Treasure: Select Works of Art from the Famed Air India Collection*.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

### Fleet information

In 1932, Air India started operations with a De Havilland Puss Moth. On 21 February 1960, Air India International inducted its first [Boeing 707-420C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) named *Gauri Shankar* (registered *VT-DJJ*), thereby becoming the first Asian airline to induct a jet aircraft in its fleet.[^cite_note-Jet-17] In 1971, the airline took delivery of its first [Boeing 747-237B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-237B) named *Emperor Ashoka* (registered *VT-EBD*)[^cite_note-nytimes.com-19] In 1986, Air India took delivery of its first [Airbus A310-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A310).[^cite_note-Timeline-16] On 4 August 1993, Air India took the delivery of its first [Boeing 747-437](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) named *Konark* (registered *VT-ESM*)[^cite_note-Jumbo-20] In 1989, Indian Airlines introduced the Airbus A320-200 aircraft, which Air India now uses to operate both domestic and international short haul flights.[^cite_note-Timeline-16] In 2005, Indian Airlines introduced the smaller A319, which is now used mainly on domestic and regional routes.[^cite_note-Timeline-16] After the merger in 2007, Air India inducted the A321 to operate mainly on international short-haul routes and leased Airbus A330s to operate on medium-long-haul international routes. The airline's first Boeing 777-200LR aircraft was delivered on 26 July 2007, which was named *Andhra Pradesh*. Air India received its first Boeing 777-300ER aircraft on 9 October 2007 and the aircraft was named as *Bihar*. Air India received its first Boeing 787 dreamliner aircraft on 6 September 2012 and commenced flights on 19 September 2012.[^cite_note-186]

[Air India One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_One) is the [call sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_sign) of any Air India aircraft carrying the prime minister, [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) or the [vice-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_India).[^cite_note-ie2010-187][^cite_note-topnews-188][^cite_note-zee-189] Though the call-sign of Air India is used, these flights are operated on customised Boeing 777-300ER aircraft owned by [Indian Air Force](403355.md) and maintained by Air India on a special contract.[^cite_note-190]

### Fleet restructuring

As a part of the financial restructuring, Air India sold five of its eight Boeing 777-200LR aircraft to [Etihad Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Airways) in December 2013. According to the airline, plans for introducing ultra-long flights with service to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle%E2%80%93Tacoma_International_Airport), [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_International_Airport) and [Los Angeles](18131.md) were cancelled due to factors like high fuel prices and weak demand.[^cite_note-191] In April 2014, the airline decided to sell its remaining three Boeing 777-200LRs as well, citing higher operating costs.[^cite_note-192] On 24 April 2014, Air India issued a tender for leasing 14 [Airbus A320](164933.md) aircraft for up to six years, to strengthen its domestic network.[^cite_note-193] On 11 April 2024, Air India sold four of its last remaining Boeing 747-400 jumbo jets US company AerSale.[^cite_note-194] Air India is also planning to establish a dedicated subsidiary for cargo operations with dedicated [freighters](2375692.md).[^cite_note-\:3-110]

### Cabin

The [Boeing 777-300ERs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777) that have operated with Air India since before its privatisation have a three-class configuration with first, business, and economy class.[^cite_note-199] These include the carrier's older, 2-3-2 business class cabins and 3-3-3 economy class cabins. Air India also operates ex-[Etihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad) 777-300ERs which feature a different product compared to their legacy 777-300ERs, albeit also in a three-class configuration(First Class, Business Class, and Economy).[^cite_note-200]

Air India's [Boeing 787-8 Dreamliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787-8_Dreamliner) have a similar product to its older 777s but do not have first class, opting for a two-class configuration with a 2-2-2 business class and economy class. These aircraft are gradually being retrofitted with new interiors in a three-class configuration(Business class, Premium Economy, and Economy).[^cite_note-201]

Air India's [A350-900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350) aircraft have a three-class configuration with business, premium economy, and economy class.[^cite_note-202]

Within its [A319/320/321ceo fleet](164933.md), which primarily serves domestic and regional international services, only business and economy class are offered,[^cite_note-\:4-203] while its [A320/321neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo_family) aircraft have either a three-class configuration with business, premium economy, and economy class, or an all-economy configuration.[^cite_note-\:4-203][^cite_note-204]

In November 2024, the merger between Air India and Vistara was completed, and former Vistara aircraft started operating under Air India. The [787-9 Dreamliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) which Air India inherited from this merger are configured in a three-class layout.

Air India's took delivery of its first factory-fit [787-9 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) in January 2026, which is configured in a three-class layout(Business Class, Premium Economy, and Economy).[^cite_note-205]

### In-flight entertainment

Air India's widebody fleet is equipped with seatback on-demand in-flight entertainment systems on which passengers can choose from available content. This varies from the [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group) i5000 on the 777-300ER, the Thales i8000 on the 787-8s, to the [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic_Avionics_Corporation) eX3 on the A350-900 and 787-9.[^cite_note-206] The legacy 777 and 787 product are being replaced with Thales' Avant Up system.[^cite_note-207] Thales will also line-fit further deliveries of the A350-900 and the 787-9 with the same system.[^cite_note-208] Air India does not offer seatback entertainment on its narrowbody fleet, with the exception of the A321LR, which uses Panasonic's eX1 system.[^cite_note-209] On its other narrowbodies, and as an interim solution on its legacy widebody fleet, Air India uses the Bluebox streaming IFE service.[^cite_note-210] *Namaste.ai* is the current in-flight magazine published in English by Air India.[^cite_note-211] It replaced *Shubh Yatra* (meaning *Happy Journey*), which was a bilingual in-flight magazine published in English and Hindi.[^cite_note-212]

### On-Board Wi-fi

On 1 January 2025, Air India announced that it rolled out free onboard WiFi on all flights operated by the carrier's A350, Boeing 787-9 and select A321neo aircraft.[^cite_note-213] Other planes would get Wi-Fi connectivity after they go through a refurbishment. The carrier became the first airline in the nation to offer such complimentary service within India.[^cite_note-214] In April 2026, Air India selected Hughes Network Systems to transform its in-flight connectivity. The airline is also the global launch customer for a common connectivity platform that will be deployed across its widebody fleet, including the Airbus A350-1000, Boeing 787-8, and Boeing 777-300ER. For the A350 fleet, Air India will utilise the Airbus HBCplus programme, while the Boeing aircraft will be retrofitted with RAVE Aerospace Ka-band terminals.[^cite_note-215]

### Frequent flyer programme

*Flying Returns* was Air India's [frequent-flyer programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequent-flyer_programme). It was India's first frequent flyer programme and was shared by Air India and its subsidiaries. A member could earn mileage points and redeem them during future travel. On higher fares, passengers would earn bonus miles and clock mileage points.[^cite_note-216]

Following the merger of Air India and Vistara, Flying Returns and Club Vistara were combined and rebranded as Maharaja Club.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

## Missions



25th Anniversary of Air India's International Services postage stamp

As the flag carrier, Air India is often involved in the evacuation of civilians during wars. The airline entered the [Guinness Book of World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_Book_of_World_Records) for the most people evacuated by a civil airliner.[^cite_note-Guinness-220] Over 111,000 people were evacuated from [Amman](45007.md) to [Mumbai](19189.md), a distance of 4,117 kilometres (2,558 mi), by operating 488 flights from 13 August to 11 October 1990  – lasting 59 days. The operation was carried out during [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War) to evacuate [Indian expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_person_of_Indian_origin) from [Kuwait](7515890.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-Guinness-220][^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223] [The event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_airlift_of_Indians_from_Kuwait) was later featured in the film *[Airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlift_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-224]

In February–March 2011, Air India evacuated more than 15,000 Indian nationals during the [Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2011\)).[^cite_note-Evac-225] In March–April 2015, the airline was involved in *Operation Raahat* during the [Yemeni civil war](46215853.md).[^cite_note-Evac-225] In August 2021, Air India evacuated 669 people under *Operation Devi Shakti* from war-torn [Afghanistan](737.md) during the [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive).[^cite_note-226] In February 2022, Air India evacuated Indian citizens from [Russia](25391.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md) as a part of *Operation Ganga* during the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-227] In October 2023, the airline evacuated people from [Israel](9282173.md) during the [Gaza war](74998519.md) as a part of *Operation Ajay*.[^cite_note-228]

## Accidents and incidents

See also: [Air India Express § Accidents and incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Express#Accidents_and_incidents)

As of November 2025, Air India has been involved in eleven fatal crashes and twenty ground fatalities, two of which were caused by acts of terrorism.

### Fatal

- On 27 December 1947, a [Douglas C-48C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-48) (registered VT-AUG) carrying nineteen passengers and four crew en route from Karachi to Bombay, [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Korangi_Creek_crash) at [Korangi Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korangi_Creek) due to loss of control following instrument failure, killing all on board. This was the airline's first fatal accident. The aircraft had been notorious for electrical problems and had an unusual number of instrument replacements.

  [^cite_note-236]

- On 3 November 1950, [Air India Flight 245](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_245), a [Lockheed L-749 Constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Constellation) (registered VT-CQP, *Malabar Princess*) carrying forty passengers and eight crew on a flight from Bombay to London via Cairo and Geneva, crashed on [Mont Blanc](44768.md) in France, killing all 48 on board.

  [^cite_note-237]

  [^cite_note-238]

- On 13 December 1950, a [Douglas C-47B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47B) (registered VT-CFK) carrying 17 passengers and four crew from Bombay to [Coimbatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbatore_airport), crashed into high ground near [Kotagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotagiri) due to a navigational error, killing all on board.

  [^cite_note-239]

- On 15 September 1951, a [Douglas C-47A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47_Skytrain) (registered VT-CCA) lost control and crashed on takeoff from [HAL Bangalore Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_Bangalore_Airport) with the autopilot turned on, killing a crew member; all 23 passengers survived.

  [^cite_note-240]

- On 9 May 1953, a Douglas C-47A (registered VT-AUD) crashed shortly after takeoff from [Palam Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palam_Airport) following a loss of control due to pilot error, killing all thirteen passengers and five crew on board.

  [^cite_note-241]

- On 11 April 1955, *[Air India Flight 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_300)*, (Kashmir Princess) [Lockheed L-749A Constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Constellation) (registered VT-DEP) flying from [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Tak_Airport) to [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_airport), crashed while attempting a [water landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_landing) post a mid-air bomb explosion in the right main landing gear bay, killing all eleven passengers and five out of eight crew.

  [^cite_note-242]

  [^cite_note-243]



The Air India Memorial in Toronto, Ontario, Canada dedicated to the victims of [Air India Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182)

- On 24 January 1966, [Air India Flight 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_101) *[Kanchenjunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanchenjunga)*, a [Boeing 707-420](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707-420) (registered VT-DMN) carrying 117 people (106 passengers and 11 crew) crashed on [Mont Blanc](44768.md), France killing all 117 on board including Indian scientist [Homi J. Bhabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homi_J._Bhabha).

  [^cite_note-244]

  &#x20;The cause of crash was controlled flight into the terrain, which was attributed to a navigational miscalculation by the pilots while descending towards [Geneva Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Airport).

- On 1 January 1978, [Air India Flight 855](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_855) *Emperor Ashoka,* a [Boeing 747-237B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-237B) (registered VT-EBD) crashed into the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) post taking off from Bombay, killing all 190 passengers and 23 crew on board.

  [^cite_note-nytimes.com-19]

  &#x20;The cause of the crash was crew disorientation while dealing with a malfunctioning AI in the dark as the flight took off during the night time.

- On 21 June 1982, [Air India Flight 403](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_403) *Gouri Shankar*, a [Boeing 707-420C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) (registered VT-DJJ) carrying 99 passengers and 12 crew from [Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Abdul_Aziz_Shah_Airport) to Bombay via Madras crashed while landing at Sahar airport during a rainstorm. The fuselage broke apart, and seventeen people, including two crew members were killed. The cause of the crash was undershooting the runway due to flight crew error and miscalculation.

  [^cite_note-245]

  [^cite_note-246]

- On 23 June 1985, [Air India Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182), a [Boeing 747-237B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-237B) (registered VT-EFO nicknamed Kanishka), was blown up in mid-air by a suitcase-bomb planted by [Babbar Khalsa terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babbar_Khalsa) on the first leg of its [Montreal](7954681.md)-[London](17867.md)-Delhi-Bombay flight. The aircraft exploded off the coast of [County Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork), Ireland in the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), killing all 307 passengers and 22 crew on board.

  [^cite_note-247]

- On 17 December 2015, an Air India technician was killed in a [freak accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_accident) at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport in Mumbai after being sucked into the engine of an Airbus A319 during [pushback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushback_\(aviation\)) when the co-pilot mistook a signal and started the engine.

  [^cite_note-248]

- On 12 June 2025, [Air India Flight 171](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_171), a [Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787-8_Dreamliner) (registered VT-ANB) flying from [Ahmedabad Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Airport) to [Gatwick Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport), crashed into the hostel building of [B. J. Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._J._Medical_College,_Ahmedabad) shortly after takeoff. All but one of the 242 people on board were killed, as well as 19 people on the ground.

  [^cite_note-249]

  &#x20;A preliminary report found that two [fuel control switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_control_unit) were moved from their RUN to CUTOFF positions, causing both engines to immediately start losing thrust seconds after take-off.

  [^cite_note-250]

### Non-fatal

- On 19 July 1959, *Rani of Aera*, a [Lockheed L-1049G Super Constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Constellation) (registered VT-DIN) carrying 46 people (39 passengers and seven crew) crashed on approach to Santacruz airport, Bombay in poor visibility conditions due to rain. There were no fatalities but the aircraft suffered damage beyond repair and was written off.

  [^cite_note-251]

- On 23 January 1971, during a training flight for practice at [Santacruz Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport) in Bombay(now Mumbai), an Air India [Boeing 707-437](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707-437) (registered VT-DJI), went off the runway to the right while performing a 3-engined takeoff, and no. 3 and 4 engines struck a mound 9 feet high located 188 feet from the edge of the runway and were torn off. The wing structure broke off progressively inward and an intense fire broke out. There were no casualties among the 5 crew members, but the 11 year old Boeing 707 was damaged beyond repair and written off.

- On 29 August 1978, Air India Flight 123, a [Boeing 747-237B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-237B) (registered VT-EBO), flying from [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport) to [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) and carrying 377 passengers and crew, aborted take-off at 150 knots due to No. 3 engine failure. While the crew hit the brakes and deployed thrust reversers, the plane veered off the runway and entered soft ground, resulting in left-hand wing landing gear collapse and substantial damage, as No.3 and 4 reversers were not effective. The No. 3 engine failed due to ingestion of tire pieces. The plane sustained substantial damage but was repaired and put back into service.

- On 28 January 1983, Air India Flight 306 *Emperor Kanishka*, a [Boeing 747-200B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-200B) (registered VT-EFO), collided with an Indian Airlines Airbus A300 after landing at Palam Airport, Delhi, with the plane suffering substantial damage. The plane was repaired and put back into service, but it was lost to the bombing of [Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182).

  [^cite_note-252]

- On 7 May 1990, Air India Flight 132 *Emperor Vikramaditya*, a [Boeing 747-200B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-200B) (registered VT-EBO) flying on the London-Delhi-Bombay route carrying 215 people (195 passengers and 20 crew) caught fire on touch down at Delhi airport due to a failure of an engine pylon-to-wing attachment. There were no fatalities, but the aircraft was damaged beyond repair and written off.

  [^cite_note-253]

- On 30 July 2005, Air India Flight 127, a [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) (registered as VT-EVJ) on lease from [Korean Air](315411.md), flying from [HAL Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_Airport) in Bengaluru to [Chicago](248449.md) with stops at [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport) and [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport), skidded while landing at Mumbai on the wet runway 14/32 due to hydroplaning, and damaged the nose wheel landing gear after hitting a few runway lights. While there were no injuries, the plane, which was immobilised after being stuck at the end of runway 32, sustained substantial damage and was taken to an Air India hangar for repairs.

  [^cite_note-254]

  [^cite_note-255]

  &#x20;The incident took place 4 days after the airport was closed due to [flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_floods_of_2005) in the Mumbai, and 2 days after reopening. An alternative Boeing 747-400 was arranged to continue the next legs of the flight, which took off after a delay of four hours.

- On 4 September 2009, during the pre-takeoff phase at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport in Mumbai, Air India Flight 829, a [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400) (registered as VT-ESM), experienced an engine fire, which spread to the wing. As the damage caused by the fire was substantial by spreading to the wings and engine pylon, the plane was declared a total loss and 21 of the 229 individuals on board were injured while evacuating. The Boeing 747 was scrapped for parts in May 2011.

  [^cite_note-256]

  &#x20;This particular 747 was the first 747-400 to enter Air India's fleet.

  [^cite_note-257]

- On 4 August 2026, Air India Flight 2379, an Airbus A320 operating from Phuket to New Delhi experienced a sudden altitude loss during cruise, reportedly following turbulence. The aircraft lost around 300 feet of altitude during which 17 people (including 13 passengers and 4 crew members) sustained injuries ranging from minor to serious. The aircraft continued the flight and landed safely in Delhi with passengers given immediate medical attention. The incident as of 10 August 2026 has been classified as a 'serious incident'&#x20;

  [^cite_note-258]

  and is currently under investigation by India's [Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Accident_Investigation_Bureau_\(India\))

  [^cite_note-259]
