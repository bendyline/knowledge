The **Gulf of Aden** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): خليج عدن; [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language): *Gacanka Cadmeed*) is a deepwater gulf of the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) between [Yemen](350939.md) to the north, the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the east, [Djibouti](17207794.md) to the west, and the [Guardafui Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardafui_Channel), the [Socotra Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Archipelago), [Puntland](233569.md) in [Somalia](27358.md) and [Somaliland](51425.md) to the south.[^cite_note-2] In the northwest, it connects with the [Red Sea](26191.md) through the [Bab-el-Mandeb](62138.md) strait, and it connects with the Arabian Sea to the east. To the west, it narrows into the [Gulf of Tadjoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tadjoura) in Djibouti. The [Aden Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Ridge) lies along the middle of the gulf, and tectonic activity at the ridge is causing the gulf to [widen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafloor_spreading) by about 15 mm (0.59 in) per year.&#x20;

The ancient Greeks regarded the gulf as one of the most important parts of the "[Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythraean_Sea)". It later came to be dominated by Muslims, as the area around the gulf converted to [Islam](6037917.md). From the late 1960s onwards, there was an increased [Soviet naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy) presence in the Gulf. The importance of the Gulf of Aden declined while the [Suez Canal](29323.md) was closed, but it was revitalized when the canal was reopened in 1975, after being deepened and widened by the [Egyptian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Egypt).&#x20;

The waterway is part of the important Suez Canal shipping route between the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) in the Indian Ocean, with 21,000 ships crossing the gulf annually.[^cite_note-yah1-3] This route is often used for the delivery of [Persian Gulf](24761.md) [oil](23195.md), making the gulf an integral waterway in the [world economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy).[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-\:1-5] Important cities along the Gulf of Aden include the namesake [Aden](38050.md) in [Yemen](350939.md). Other Yemeni cities are [Zinjibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinjibar), [Shuqrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuqrah), [Ahwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahwar), [Balhaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balhaf), [Mukalla](2414800.md), [Ash-Shihr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash-Shihr), [al-Hami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hami,_Yemen), [Hadibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu), and [Qulensya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qulensya). On the African side are the cities of [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_\(city\)), [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) and [Bosaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaso).&#x20;

Despite a lack of large-scale commercial fishing facilities, the coastline supports many isolated fishing towns and villages. The Gulf of Aden is richly supplied with fish, turtles, and lobsters.[^cite_note-6] Local fishing takes place close to the shore; sardines, tuna, kingfish, and mackerel make up the bulk of the annual catches. Crayfish and sharks are also fished locally.&#x20;

## Historical names

In [antiquity](252905.md), the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) viewed what is now called the Gulf of Aden as an extension of the [Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythraean_Sea) (Red Sea) [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἐρυθρὰ Θάλασσα, *Erythrà Thálassa*. They named several of the islands in the gulf, including one they called Stratonis, although it is no longer clear which Greek name referred to which island.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]&#x20;

In [Abu'l-Fida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu%27l-Fida)'s *A Sketch of the Countries* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): تقويم البلدان), the present-day Gulf of Aden was called the Gulf of Berbera, which shows how important [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) was in both regional and international trade during the medieval period.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]&#x20;

Legendary navigator [Ibn Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_M%C4%81jid) also referred to the Gulf of Aden as the Gulf of Berbera in his 15th century magnum opus *The Book of the Benefits of the Principles and Foundations of Seamanship*.[^cite_note-11] Berbera has been a prominent port since antiquity.[^cite_note-Periplus-12]&#x20;

### Hydrography

The temperature of the Gulf of Aden varies between 15 and 28 °C (59 and 82 °F), depending on the season and the [monsoons](57630.md). The [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity) of the gulf at 10 metres (33 ft) depth varies from 35.3[‰](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E2%80%B0) along the eastern Somali coast to as high as 37.3‰ in the gulf's centre,[^cite_note-fao-14] while its oxygen content at the same depth is typically between 4.0 and 5.0 mg/L.[^cite_note-fao-14]&#x20;

The Gulf of Aden is a vital waterway for shipping, especially for [Persian Gulf](24761.md) oil, making it an integral waterway in the [world economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy).[^cite_note-\:0-4] Approximately 11% of the world's seaborne [petroleum](23195.md) passes through the Gulf of Aden on its way to the [Suez Canal](29323.md) or to regional refineries.[^cite_note-\:1-5] The main ports along the gulf are [Aden](38050.md), [Balhaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balhaf), [Bir Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi%27r_Ali), [Mukalla](2414800.md), and Shokra in [Yemen](350939.md); [Djibouti City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_City) in [Djibouti](17207794.md); [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila) and [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) in [Somaliland](51425.md), and [Bosaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaso) in [Somalia](27358.md).&#x20;

In [antiquity](252905.md), the gulf was a thriving area of international trade between [Ptolemaic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt) and [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the west and [Classical India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_India), [its Indonesian colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Indonesia#Early_kingdoms), and [Han China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) in the east. It was not limited to [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment), as Yemeni [incense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense), [tortoiseshell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoiseshell), and other goods were in high demand in both directions. After Egyptian sailors discovered the [monsoon winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_winds) and began to trade directly with India, [caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_\(travellers\)) routes and their associated kingdoms began to collapse, leading to a rise in [piracy](50715.md) in the area. The 1st-century *[Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea)* documents one Egyptian captain's experiences during this era.&#x20;

After the collapse of the Roman economy, direct trade ceased but the [Awsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awsan) I port [Crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_\(Yemen\)), located just south of the modern city of [Aden](38050.md), remained an important regional centre. In late antiquity and the early medieval period, there were several invasions of [Yemen](350939.md) from [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksum); after the rise of [Islam](6037917.md), the gulf permitted repeated migrations of northwest Africa by Arab settlers.&#x20;

In the first decade of the 2000s, especially during the [war in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)), the gulf evolved into a hub of [pirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_Somalia) activity. By 2013, attacks in the waters had steadily declined due to private security and international navy patrols.[^cite_note-Wapstoas-19] [India](14533.md) receives US\$50 billion in imports—and sends US\$60 billion in exports—through this area annually. To protect its trade and that of other countries, India keeps a warship escort in the area.[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

## Ecology

A geologically young body of water, the Gulf of Aden has a unique [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) that includes many varieties of [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral), [seabirds](147019.md) and [invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate). This rich ecological diversity has benefited from a relative lack of [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) by humans in the past. However, environmental groups fear that the lack of a coordinated effort to control pollution may jeopardize the gulf's [ecosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_spheres).[^cite_note-21] [Whales](33777.md), [dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin), and [dugongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugong)[^cite_note-22] were once common[^cite_note-23] before being severely reduced by commercial hunts, including by mass illegal hunts by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Japan](15573.md) in the 1960s and 1970s.[^cite_note-24] The now critically endangered Arabian [humpback whales](231728.md) were once seen here in large numbers,[^cite_note-25] but only a few large whales still appear in the gulf waters, including [Bryde's whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryde%27s_whale),[^cite_note-26] [blue whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_whale),[^cite_note-27] and deep-sea toothed whales such as [sperm whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperm_whale)[^cite_note-28] and [tropical bottlenose whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_bottlenose_whale).[^cite_note-IndieSightings-29]&#x20;
