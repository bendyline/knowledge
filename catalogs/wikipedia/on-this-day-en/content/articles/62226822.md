**John Matthew Martinis** (born 1958) is an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and Professor of Physics at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara).[^cite_note-\:4-2][^cite_note-3] He led a team to develop a [superconducting quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computer) at the [Quantum AI Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_AI_Lab). With the [Sycamore processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycamore_processor), the team claimed the first evidence of [quantum supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_supremacy) in 2019. He shared the 2025 [Nobel Prize in Physics](52497.md) with [John Clarke](17245653.md) and [Michel Devoret](51263177.md) for joint work on [macroscopic quantum phenomena](35543722.md) in [superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity).[^cite_note-\:0-4]&#x20;

## Early life and education

John Matthew Martinis was born in 1958 and raised in [San Pedro, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro,_Los_Angeles).[^cite_note-\:3-5] His father, a [Croat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croat) from [Komiža](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi%C5%BEa) on the island of [Vis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vis_\(island\)) near [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia), Croatia,[^cite_note-6] immigrated to the United States from Yugoslavia to escape [the communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia).[^cite_note-\:3-5] His mother was born in San Pedro to parents who had also emigrated from Croatia.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

Martinis attended the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he received a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in Physics in 1980, and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Physics in 1987.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

&#x20;During his doctoral studies, Martinis investigated the quantum behaviour of a macroscopic variable, the phase difference across a [Josephson tunnel junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-\:1-10][^cite_note-11] His doctoral advisor was [John Clarke](17245653.md).[^cite_note-\:1-10] During this time, he collaborated with [Michel Devoret](51263177.md), a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at the time.[^cite_note-\:1-10]

Martinis giving a lecture in 2017

In 1985, Clarke, Devoret, and Martinis presented their analysis of microwave pulses that demonstrated the [quantized energy levels of a Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit#Quantized_energy_levels).[^cite_note-\:1-10] This work would later become the basis for [superconducting quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing).[^cite_note-\:1-10]&#x20;

On September 21, 2026, the [Prime Minister of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Croatia), [Andrej Plenković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrej_Plenkovi%C4%87), granted Martinis Croatian citizenship.[^cite_note-12]&#x20;

## Career

Martinis joined the [CEA Paris-Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Paris-Saclay) in France[^cite_note-\:2-13] for a first postdoc, and then the Electromagnetic Technology division at the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology) (NIST) in Boulder, where he worked on [superconducting quantum interference device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQUID) (SQUIDs) amplifiers.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:2-13]&#x20;

Since 2004, Martinis has served on the faculty of the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara).[^cite_note-\:4-2] He held the title of Susan and Bruce Worster Chair in Experimental Physics for many years. The quantum device he developed in collaboration with UCSB colleagues was named [*Science* magazine's](193513.md) 2010 Breakthrough of the Year.[^cite_note-\:4-2] [Google Quantum AI Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Artificial_Intelligence_Lab), a partnership between UC Santa Barbara and Google, announced in 2014 that it had hired Martinis and his team in a multimillion dollar deal to build a [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing) using superconducting [qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit).[^cite_note-forbes2020-15][^cite_note-16] He and his team published a paper in *[Nature](43427.md)* in 2019,[^cite_note-17] where they presented how they achieved [quantum supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_supremacy) for the first time using the [Sycamore processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycamore_processor), a 53-qubit quantum processor.[^cite_note-18] Martinis resigned from Google in April 2020 after being reassigned to an advisory role.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-forbes2020-15]&#x20;

In 2020, Martinis joined [Silicon Quantum Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Quantum_Computing), a start-up founded in Australia by Professor [Michelle Simmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Simmons).[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

In 2026, he was appointed to the [President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President%27s_Council_of_Advisors_on_Science_and_Technology) (PCAST) by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md).[^cite_note-21]&#x20;

### Qolab

In 2022, together with CEO Alan Ho, he founded Qolab, a quantum computing private company based on semiconductor chip manufacturing.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

Since 2025, Qolab and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) are co-leading a consortium for the [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (QBI). The team is tasked with building an industrially useful quantum supercomputer by 2033. Within this partnership, Martinis and Qolab lead the hardware build, designing and fabricating the core superconducting quantum processors.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Quantum_Insider-24] In December 2025, Qolab launched Qolab Start, a superconducting qubit platform for hardware research and workforce development.[^cite_note-Quantum_Insider-24] Built for universities, research labs, and workforce training, it eliminates the need to build custom labs by providing pre-packaged hardware and direct access to quantum processors. Researchers can access these systems via an on-premise installation or through the cloud.[^cite_note-25] In February 2026, Singapore's National Quantum Federated Foundry (NQFF) and Qolab formed a research collaboration to build integrated cryogenic low-pass filters (LPFs) for quantum processors.[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

Martinis is the co-leader of Quantum Scaling Alliance (QSA), a global group that develops quantum computing technology and include Qolab as well as others such as [Applied Materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Materials), HPE, Synopsys.[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

## Honors and awards

In 2014, he shared the [Fritz London Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_London_Memorial_Prize) with [Michel Devoret](51263177.md) and [Robert J. Schoelkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Schoelkopf).[^cite_note-28]&#x20;

Martinis was chosen for [*Nature*'s 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature%27s_10), a list of people who mattered for science in 2019.[^cite_note-29]&#x20;

In 2021, he received the [John Stewart Bell Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_Bell_Prize) for Research on Fundamental Issues in Quantum Mechanics and Their Applications.[^cite_note-John_Stewart_Bell_Prize-30]&#x20;

In 2025, he received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](52497.md) alongside his doctoral advisor [John Clarke](17245653.md) and [Michel Devoret](51263177.md) for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-31]&#x20;

In 2026, he was decorated with the [Grand Order of King Dmitar Zvonimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Order_of_King_Dmitar_Zvonimir) by the [Croatian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Croatia) [Zoran Milanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_Milanovi%C4%87) for exceptional contribution to the development and promotion of quantum physics and technology on a global level, proud emphasis and preservation of Croatian origin, and special merit in transferring cutting-edge scientific knowledge to the Croatian academic community.[^cite_note-32]&#x20;
