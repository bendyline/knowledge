The **Tanzania People's Defence Force** (**TPDF**) ([Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language): *Jeshi la Ulinzi la Wananchi wa Tanzania*) is the [military force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) of the [United Republic of Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Republic_of_Tanzania). It was established in September 1964, following a mutiny by the former colonial military force, the [Tanganyika Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_Rifles). From its inception, it was ingrained in the troops of the new TPDF that they were a people's force under civilian control. Unlike some of its neighbouring countries, Tanzania has never suffered a coup d'état or civil war.

The TPDF's mission is to defend [Tanzania](30118.md) and every Tanzanian, especially the people and their political ideology. Conscripts are obligated to serve 2 years as of 2004.[^cite_note-1]

## History



Zanzibar, 12 Jan. 2004, celebration of 40 years of the revolution

After an aborted [mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_Rifles#Mutiny) in January 1964, the existing army was disbanded. The new force was titled the 'Tanganyika Military Force', from 25 January 1964 - 26 April 1964.[^cite_note-\:0-2] The Tanzanian government concluded that the former British model was not appropriate for the needs of an independent African state.[^cite_note-3] Fresh recruits were sourced from the [Tanganyika African National Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_African_National_Union) youth wing.[^cite_note-4] After the merge of [Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_\(1961%E2%80%931964\)) and [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar), the force was renamed the United Republic Military Force on 27 April 1964.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

For the first few years of the TPDF, the army was even smaller than the disbanded 2,000-strong Tanganyika Rifles, the air force was minuscule, and no navy had yet been formed. It appears that the new TPDF had three battalions by August 1965, stationed at [Nachingwea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachingwea), Colito Barracks (now called [Lugalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugalo_Military_Base)) five miles outside Dar es Salaam, and [Tabora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabora), plus the yet to be fully integrated Zanzibari force of about 1,000.[^cite_note-5] However the army was four battalions strong by 1967.[^cite_note-6]

From 1964 to 1974, the TPDF was commanded by [Mrisho S.H. Sarakikya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrisho_Sarakikya), trained at the [Royal Military Academy Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst), who was promoted from lieutenant to brigadier in 1964 and became the force's first commander.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaplan1978248–249-7][^cite_note-8] He was succeeded by Major General [Abdallah Twalipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_Twalipo?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1974.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaplan1978249-9] Twalipo was still a major general in 1975, but then promoted to lieutenant general by 1978[^cite_note-10] and then later full general.

In 1972, the [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) (IISS) listed the army with 10,000 personnel, four infantry battalions, 20 [Type 59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_59_tank) [main battle tanks](26595097.md), 14 [Type 62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_62) light tanks, some [BTR-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-40) and [BTR-152](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-152) [armoured personnel carriers](19278686.md), and Soviet [field artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_artillery) and Chinese [mortars](324499.md). 'Spares \[were] short and not all equipment was serviceable.'[^cite_note-11]

[War broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda%E2%80%93Tanzania_War) between Uganda and Tanzania in October 1978, with several Ugandan attacks across the border culminating in [an invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kagera) of the Kagera Salient.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198354,_58,_61-12] President [Julius Nyerere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Nyerere) ordered Tanzania to undertake full [mobilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilisation) for war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198363-13] In a few weeks, the Tanzanian army was expanded from less than 40,000 troops[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELupogo200183-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201527-15] to over 150,000, including about 40,000 [militiamen](20622.md)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201527-15] as well as members of the police, prison services, and the [national service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_service).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELupogo200184-16] Fighting in December was mostly limited to "[trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare)"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELagarde19795-17] along the border, marked by sporadic clashes and air raids.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201528-18] By early January 1979 all Ugandan troops had been ejected from Kagera.[^cite_note-lubega2-19]

Nyerere decided that Tanzanian forces should occupy southern Uganda as revenge for the devastation wrought by Ugandan troops in his country and to incite a rebellion against Ugandan dictator [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198377–79-20] The Tanzanians launched their offensive in mid-February 1979.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201529-21] Major General [David Musuguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Musuguri) was appointed commander of the TPDF's 20th Division and tasked with overseeing the advance into Uganda.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198378–79-22] They steadily advanced, killing dozens of Ugandan soldiers and destroying large amounts of their material.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198379–82-23] Following the capture of [Masaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaka,_Uganda) and [Mbarara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbarara), the TPDF halted to reorganise. [Silas Mayunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silas_Mayunga) was promoted to major general and given charge of a newly formed "Task Force", a unit consisting of the 206th Brigade and the Minziro Brigade, which was to operate semi-autonomously from the 20th Division.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198386-24] While the 20th Division moved out of southeast Uganda and attacked major locations in the country, the Task Force advanced north into western Uganda in the following months, engaging Ugandan troops conducting [rearguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rearguard) defensive actions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney1983174-25]

The 20th Division captured [Kampala](56689.md) on April 11 and overthrew Amin's government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201536–37-26] The fall of Kampala marked the first time in the post-colonial history of the continent that an African state seized the capital of another African country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney1983124-27] The war ended on June 3, 1979; after Tanzanian forces occupied Uganda's border region with [Sudan](27421.md) and [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney1983195–196-28] Some Western military analysts attributed Tanzania's victory to the collapse of the Uganda Army, arguing that the TPDF would have been defeated by most other African armies.[^cite_note-29] Others felt that the TPDF's success indicated substantial improvements in African military capabilities over the previous years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThom198852–53-30]

When the TPDF began returning en masse to Tanzania, only a small number of soldiers were demobilised, contrary to public expectations. Military commanders then began making accommodations to render the wartime expansions of the army permanent, creating new units and divisional headquarters. Some in the military hierarchy expressed disapproval in light of Tanzania's bleak financial situation, and the country's depressed economy eventually forced the TPDF to disband many of the extra units.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELupogo200184-16] Nevertheless, the TPDF retained a large number of officers in the standing army, with the assumption that they could be used to command militiamen in the event they needed to be called back into service.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvirganHoney198372-31] The post-war size of the TPDF remained larger than the pre-war size throughout the next decade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrancis1994160-32]

In 1992, the IISS listed the army with 45,000 personnel (some 20,000 conscripts), 3 division headquarters, 8 infantry brigades, one tank brigade, two field artillery battalions, two [anti-aircraft artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_artillery) battalions (6 batteries), two mortar, two anti-tank battalions, one engineer regiment (battalion-sized), and one [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) battalion with [SA-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-125_Neva/Pechora) and [SA-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2K12_Kub) missiles.[^cite_note-33] Equipment included 30 Chinese Type 59 and 32 [T-54/55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-54/55) main battle tanks.

In 2007 Tanzania pledged forces for the [SADC Standby Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SADC_Standby_Brigade) of the [African Standby Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Standby_Force).[^cite_note-34]

## Land Force Command

The Land Force Command was separated from the Army and an official commander was appointed to run the operations of the Land Forces.[^cite_note-35] In 2013, the other separated half of the army was officially incorporated as a new branch of the military to oversee strategic planning and administration of all the branches of the military called the Defence Force HQ Command.[^cite_note-36]

As of 2012, the army is gradually modernising and restructuring. Much of the inventory is in storage or unreliable.[^cite_note-37]

- 5 × infantry brigades

- 1 × armoured brigade

- 3 × artillery battalions

- 2 × air defence artillery battalions

- 1 × mortar battalion

- 2 × anti-tank battalions

- 121st Engineer Regiment (battalion size; unit identification from usaraf.army.mil and [Flickr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flickr))

- 1 × central logistic/support group

## Air Force Command

Main article: [Tanzania Air Force Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Air_Force_Command)



TPDF honour guard

Tanzania established its air force as the "Air Wing" ([Kiswahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiswahili): *Usafirishaji wa Anga*) of the TPDF Air Defence Command in 1965.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201514-38] As it was following an international policy of [non-alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201513-39] Tanzania procured aircraft and trainers from a variety of countries, most notably [China](5405.md), [Canada](5042916.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201514–15-40] and the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201519-41] By 1978, the Tanzanian Air Wing possessed 14 [MiG-21MFs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21), two MiG-21UMs, 22 [Shenyang F-5s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang_J-5), 12 [Shenyang F-6s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang_J-6), as well as several [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft) and [trainer aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201519-41][^cite_note-44] Furthermore, the country's Air Defence had access to SA-3 [surface-to-air missiles](182664.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201519-41] [SA-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K32_Strela-2) [MANPADS](1389714.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201525-45] 14.5mm and 37mm anti-aircraft guns,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELagarde19798-42][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELegum1981B-333-46] and ground support equipment—including [early-warning radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early-warning_radar).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201519-41]

The Air Wing was eventually organised into three *Kikosi cha Jeshi* or KJ Brigades, with each brigade focusing on one particular element of air warfare: aircraft and helicopters (601 KJ), technical support (602 KJ), and air defence (603 KJ). The fighter aircraft unit of 601 KJ, known as "Squadron 601", was based at [Mwanza Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwanza_Airport) (MiG-21s) and [Ngerengere Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngerengere_Air_Force_Base) (F-5s, F-6s).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECooperFontanellaz201520-47] In 1978 the Air Defence Command employed approximately 1,000 personnel.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaxton20161169-43][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELagarde19798-42]

The current commander of the now-[Tanzania Air Force Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Air_Force_Command) is Major General William Ingram, who replaced Major General Joseph Kapwani upon the latter's retirement in January 2016. During a visit to Zimbabwe in March 2014, Kapwani commended Zimbabweans for 'remaining resolute and firmly safeguarding the country's sovereignty despite the suffering brought on by illegal Western sanctions.'[^cite_note-48] He made the remarks when he paid a courtesy call on [Air Force of Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_of_Zimbabwe) Commander Air Marshal [Perrance Shiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perrance_Shiri) at AFZ headquarters in [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare) on 12 March 2014. General Kapwani, who was then the chair of the SADC Standing Aviation Committee, said he was in Zimbabwe to share experiences and strengthen relations.

A few of the Tanzanian air wing's transport remain serviceable. However, its Shenyang F-5s, and [Chengdu F-7s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) are reported to fly only on rare occasions because of airworthiness problems.[^cite_note-Tanzanian_military_aviation_OrBat-49] Tanzania's long coastline means that transports are also used for patrol flights.



A TPDF soldier

On 14 November 2013, Helmoed-Römer Heitman reported for [Jane's Defence Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Defence_Weekly) that a 'usually reliable source' had informed Jane's that the TPDF had replaced its 12 old CAC J-7 fighters with 14 new J-7s, twelve single-seat and two dual-seat. Deliveries were completed in 2011. Heitman also reported that the aircraft were fully operational at Dar es Salaam and Mwanza air bases.[^cite_note-50]

Recent estimates (2014) suggest that Tanzania's air force command operates 32 aircraft in 3 different types. It is believed they are operating 14 [fighters](10929.md), 11 fixed-wing [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft) and 7 transport aircraft. On October 1, 2015 a [K-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-8_Karakorum) trainer jet of Tanzania Air Force Command crashed into the sea killing both pilots.[^cite_note-51]

## Naval Command

Main article: [Tanzania Naval Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Naval_Command)

The navy operates 9 [fast attack craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_attack_craft) and 12 [patrol boats](536773.md).

The current commander of the Naval Command is Rear Admiral [Ramson Godwin Mwaisaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramson_Godwin_Mwaisaka?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The closing ceremony of the joint Tanzanian-Chinese exercise Beyond/Transcend 2014 was held on November 14, 2014, at [Kigamboni Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigamboni_Naval_Base) attended by guests that included China’s ambassador to Tanzania, the Chief of the TPDF, and the heads of the navy and air force. The exercise between the Chinese [People’s Liberation Army Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%E2%80%99s_Liberation_Army_Navy) and the TPDF began on October 16 in [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), with more than 100 navy officers and seamen participating.

[Jane's Defence Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Defence_Weekly) wrote in August 2017 that '..a People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) flotilla consisting of a destroyer, a frigate, and a supply vessel visited Dar es Salaam on 16–20 August.'[^cite_note-52] Rear Admiral Makanzo said during the visit that Tanzania currently has two marine infantry companies, both of which were trained by the PLAN, with the training of a third company planned to begin with Chinese assistance. The admiral said that Tanzanian marines were deployed at the time in peacekeeping operations in the Central African Republic, the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), and South Sudan.
