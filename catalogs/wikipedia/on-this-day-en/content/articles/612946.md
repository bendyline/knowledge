The **Palme d'Or** (French pronunciation: [\[palm(ə) dɔʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); English: **Golden Palm**) is the highest prize awarded to the director of the Best Feature Film of the Official Competition at the [Cannes Film Festival](101460.md). It was introduced in 1955 by the festival's organizing committee. Previously, from 1939 to 1954, the festival's highest prize was the **Grand Prix du Festival International du Film**. In 1964, the Palme d'Or was replaced again by the Grand Prix, before being reintroduced in 1975.[^cite_note-Brief_History-1]

The Palme d'Or is widely considered one of the [film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_industry)'s most prestigious awards.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## History



The Commune of Cannes coat of arms

In 1954, the festival decided to present an award annually, titled the Grand Prix of the International Film Festival, with a new design each year from a contemporary artist.[^cite_note-Brief_History-1] The festival's board of directors invited several jewellers to submit designs for a [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae), in tribute to the coat of arms of the city of [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes), evoking the famous legend of [Saint Honorat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoratus) and the palm trees lining the famous [Promenade de la Croisette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade_de_la_Croisette).[^cite_note-Brief_History-1] The original design by Parisian jeweller Lucienne Lazon, inspired by a sketch by director [Jean Cocteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Cocteau), had the [bevelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bevel) lower extremity of the [stem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_stem) forming a heart, and the pedestal a sculpture in [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta) by the artist [Sébastien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien).[^cite_note-6]

&#x20;In 1955, the first Palme d'Or was awarded to [Delbert Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delbert_Mann) for his film *[Marty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-Brief_History-1] From 1964 to 1974, the festival temporarily resumed a Grand Prix.[^cite_note-Brief_History-1] In 1975, the Palme d'Or was reintroduced and has since remained the festival's symbol, awarded each year to the director of the winning film, presented in a case of pure red [Morocco leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_leather) lined with white [suede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suede).[^cite_note-Brief_History-1]



Palme d'Or awarded to *[Apocalypse Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse_Now)* at the [1979 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Cannes_Film_Festival)

Since its reintroduction, the prize has been redesigned several times. At the beginning of the 1980s, the rounded shape of the pedestal, bearing the palm has gradually transformed to become pyramidal in 1984. In 1992, Thierry de Bourqueney redesigned the Palme and its pedestal in hand-[cut crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_glass). In 1997, [Caroline Scheufele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Scheufele) redesigned the statuette; since then, it has been manufactured by the Swiss jewellery firm [Chopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopard). The palm is made from 4.16 oz (118 g) of [18-carat yellow gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carat_\(purity\)) while the branch's base forms a small heart.[^cite_note-7] A single piece of cut crystal forms a cushion for the palm, which is hand-cast into a wax mould and now presented in a case of blue [Morocco leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_leather). In 1998, [Theo Angelopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Angelopoulos) was the first director to win the *Palme d'or* as it appears today, for his film *[Eternity and a Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity_and_a_Day)*.[^cite_note-Brief_History-1]

In 2013, when *[Blue Is the Warmest Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Is_the_Warmest_Colour)* won the Palme d'Or, the jury headed by [Steven Spielberg](26940.md) awarded it to the film's actresses [Adèle Exarchopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad%C3%A8le_Exarchopoulos) and [Léa Seydoux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9a_Seydoux), as well as the director [Abdellatif Kechiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdellatif_Kechiche).[^cite_note-8] This remains the only instance where multiple Palme d'Or trophies were presented.[^cite_note-The_Hollywood_Reporter-9] The jury decided to include the actresses in the recognition due to a Cannes policy that forbids the Palme d'Or-winning film from receiving any additional awards. This policy would have prevented the jury from acknowledging the actresses separately.[^cite_note-10] Regarding the unorthodox decision, Spielberg commented, "Had the casting been 3% wrong, \[the film] wouldn't have worked like it did for us".[^cite_note-11] Subsequently, Kechiche auctioned off his Palme d'Or trophy to fund his new feature film. In an interview with *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*, he expressed dissatisfaction with the festival's decision to award multiple trophies, stating that he felt they had "publicly insulted" him. He added, "Liberating myself from this Palme d'Or is a way of washing my hands of this sorry affair".[^cite_note-The_Hollywood_Reporter-9]

The 2014 award ceremony which awarded the Palme d'Or to *[Winter Sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Sleep_\(film\))*, a Turkish film by [Nuri Bilge Ceylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuri_Bilge_Ceylan), occurred during the 100th anniversary year of [Turkish cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Turkey). On receiving the award, Ceylan dedicated it to the "young people" involved in Turkey's ongoing political unrest, and the workers killed in the [Soma mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soma_mine_disaster), which occurred on the day before the commencement of the awards event.[^cite_note-12] In 2017, the award was redesigned to celebrate the festival's [70th anniversary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Cannes_Film_Festival). The diamonds were provided by an ethical supplier certified by the Responsible Jewellery Council.[^cite_note-Brief_History-1]

The [2020 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Cannes_Film_Festival) was cancelled due to the ongoing [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). 56 films were announced as official selections by the festival, but no awards were presented for the first time since 1968.[^cite_note-LA_Times-13]

From 2026, the non-English language winners will also be automatically eligible for the [Academy Award for Best International Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film).[^cite_note-14]

&#x20;As of 2026, [Jane Campion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Campion), [Julia Ducournau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Ducournau) and [Justine Triet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justine_Triet) are the only female directors to have won the prize (for *[The Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Piano)*, *[Titane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titane)*, and *[Anatomy of a Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy_of_a_Fall)*, respectively). However, [Bodil Ipsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodil_Ipsen) won the Grand Prix du Festival International du Film for *[The Red Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Red_Meadows)* (along with [Lau Lauritzen Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lau_Lauritzen_Jr.)) as part of an 11-way tie at the inaugural 1946 festival.



[Roberto Rossellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Rossellini) won in 1946.

[Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino) won in 1994.



[Abbas Kiarostami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Kiarostami) won in 1997.



[Jean-Pierre & Luc Dardenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardenne_brothers) won twice, in 1999 and 2005.



[Ken Loach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Loach) won twice, in 2006 and 2016.



[Cristian Mungiu](11409466.md) won twice, in 2007 and 2026.



[Michael Haneke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Haneke) won twice, in 2009 and 2012.



[Ruben Östlund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_%C3%96stlund) won twice, in 2017 and 2022.



[Bong Joon Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bong_Joon_Ho) won in 2019.



[Jean-Luc Godard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Godard) won the only "Special Palme d'Or" ever awarded, in 2018.



[Julia Ducournau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Ducournau) won in 2021, becoming the first woman to win it solo.



[Sean Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Baker) won in 2024.



[Jafar Panahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafar_Panahi) won in 2025.

## Honorary Palme d'Or

Main article: [Honorary Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Palme_d'Or)

At the [1997 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Cannes_Film_Festival) by the festival's organizing committee, on the occasion of the 50th anniversary of the Festival as the "Palme des Palmes", a homage to Swedish filmmaker [Ingmar Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingmar_Bergman) who had never been awarded a competitive Palme.[^cite_note-Rooney1997-129]

Since 2002, the festival awards the prize regularly to individuals who have achieved a notable body of work but who had never won a competitive Palme d'Or.[^cite_note-130] In 2024, [Studio Ghibli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_Ghibli) became the first and only studio to date to win it.[^cite_note-131]
