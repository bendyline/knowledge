**Tarlac City**, officially the **City of Tarlac** ([Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language): *Lakanbalen ning Tarlac*; [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language): *Siyudad na Tarlac*; [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Siudad ti Tarlac*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lungsod ng Tarlac* [\[tɐɾˈlak\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)), is a first-class component city and capital of the [province](165100.md) of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac), Philippines. According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 401,892 people, making it the most populous in the province.[^cite_note-5]

## History



Aerial view of Tarlac, circa 1930s



Historical marker of José Rizal's stay in "Tarlak" in 1892

Tarlac's first settlers came from [Bacolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolor), [Pampanga](255602.md). They cleared the area, fertilised the soil, and then established their settlement here in 1788. Tarlac's name is a Hispanized derivation from a *talahib* weed called *tarlak*, an [Aeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeta) term.[^cite_note-tar-6] The area around the current capital city was described as *matarlak* or *malatarlak*, a word meaning "abundant with *tarlak* grass."[^cite_note-tar1-7] This small community of settlers experienced rapid population growth, as settlers from [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan), [Pampanga](255602.md) and [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) moved into the area. The [Kapampangan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), which is the language of [Pampanga](255602.md), became the native language of this town. Roads and barrios were built over the following decades through hard work of its residents. Following the foundation of the province of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) in 1872, Tarlac was designated as the capital of the new province.

### Cityhood

Main article: [Cities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines)

On June 21, 1969, President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) signed and approved the Republic Act 5907, converting this municipality into a city. However, its plebiscite was unsuccessful, showing their voters rejected cityhood.

On April 21, 1990, the barangays of Burgos, David, Iba, Labney, Lawacamulag, Lubigan, Maamot, Mababanaba, Moriones, Pao, San Juan de Valdez, Sula, and Villa Aglipay, originally part of the then-municipality of Tarlac, separated and formed to be the part of [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_Tarlac). Tarlac now retains its 274.66 km<sup>2</sup> (106.05 sq mi) area.

Finally, on March 12, 1998, Republic Act No. 8593 was signed by President [Fidel V. Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_V._Ramos), making Tarlac as the component city of the province of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac). On April 18, 1998, its residents approved the conversion of the municipality into a city. Tarlac was proclaimed as the component city by [COMELEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMELEC) on the next day, on April 19, 1998.

#### Highly Urbanized City

On October 27, 2005, President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) signed Proclamation No. 940, classifying Tarlac City as a highly urbanized city (HUC) in the province.[^cite_note-proc940-8] However, the provincial government opposed the city's campaign for conversion into HUC.[^cite_note-9] The majority of voters rejected the conversion in the plebiscite on February 11, 2006.

## Geography

Tarlac City is located 58 kilometres (36 mi) north of [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon)'s regional center [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga), 124 kilometres (77 mi) north of [Manila](184334.md), and 126 kilometres (78 mi) from [Baguio](162480.md).

The city is situated at the center of the province of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac), along the [Tarlac River](4462599.md). To its north are [Gerona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerona,_Tarlac) and [Santa Ignacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ignacia), to the west is [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_Tarlac), to the south are [Capas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capas) and [Concepcion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concepcion,_Tarlac), and to the east are [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Tarlac) and [La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz,_Tarlac).

Tarlac City is approximately 24 metres (79 ft) above sea level on some parts but reaching even 50 metres (160 ft) on large western portions. Tarlac City was historically a part of what is now [Porac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porac). Parts of Tarlac City are claimed to be among the few portions of land in the province which was not created by ancient eruptions from [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo).

### Languages

Being at the meeting point of both [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language) and [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language) languages, cultures, and ethnicities, both languages are predominantly spoken in the city and environs. [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language) and [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) are also used, especially those with [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) and/or [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people) ethnicity/ancestry, respectively, with the latter language also serving as a medium for inter-ethnic communications. As the majority of the residents are Kapampangans, most of the Pangasinans, Ilocanos, and Tagalogs speak Kapampangan as first language.

### Religion

According to statistics compiled by the Philippine government, the most dominant religion in the city is Christianity. The majority of Christians are Roman Catholics followed by a large concentration of [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) (Church of Christ). Other Christian groups belong to various Protestant denominations. There are some being non-Christian such as Muslims, etc.

## Education



Aquino Center and Museum

As the educational center of Tarlac, Tarlac City houses the main campus of [Tarlac State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac_State_University), as well as other higher educational institutions.

The Tarlac City Schools Division of the Department of Education operates 87 elementary schools and 13 high schools as of 2013.

Some private schools in Tarlac City are the [Don Bosco Technical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bosco_Technical_Institute,_Tarlac), [College of the Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_the_Holy_Spirit_of_Tarlac), [Tarlac Montessori School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac_Montessori_School), Kian Tiak School, Ecumenical Christian College and Creston Academy

## Tourism

The Malatarlak Festival, celebrated every January in Tarlac City, is one of the most remarkable festivals in the province. In 2011, the city mayor then changed the name of the festival to Melting Pot Festival, but it is still remembered by its former name. The festival is a commemoration to the first people who built civilization in the province, the Aetas. The names and themes of the festivals in Tarlac City have changed over the years depending on the city's leadership. For the current administration (since July 2016), the local fiesta or festival has been called Kaisa Festival derived from the word *magkaisa* (to unite).

Tarlac City has two major economic zones that serve as a tourist destination.

## Transportation



MacArthur Highway in Tarlac

Tarlac City is the usual bus stop for commuters traveling from the South to the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region) and [Cordillera provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region). Bus companies that take a route through the city include [Pangasinan Solid North Transit, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionisio_R._De_Leon_Express), [Dagupan Bus Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan_Bus_Company), [Philippine Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Rabbit), [Victory Liner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Liner), [Five Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Star_Bus_Company), [First North Luzon Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_North_Luzon_Transit), Luzon Cisco Transport, Genesis, Santrans, [Viron Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viron_Transit), [Partas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partas), and many others. Many of the bus companies' rest stops can be found along MacArthur Highway including Siesta and Motorway.

The [MacArthur Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Highway) goes from the southern to the northern end of the city. There are a series of roads leading to [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales) and [Pangasinan](1691800.md) as well as [Baguio](162480.md). Most buses passing through the town of [Camiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camiling,_Tarlac) onwards to Pangasinan usually take the Romulo Highway which forks from MacArthur Highway along Barangay San Roque.

[Subic–Clark–Tarlac Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic%E2%80%93Clark%E2%80%93Tarlac_Expressway) (SCTEx) connects with [Tarlac–Pangasinan–La Union Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac%E2%80%93Pangasinan%E2%80%93La_Union_Expressway) (TPLEx) and [Central Luzon Link Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon_Link_Expressway) (CLLEx) within the capital of the province. The 3 expressways serves as an alternate route for motorists going to the other parts of Northern Luzon area such as [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley), [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)), [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), [Pangasinan](1691800.md), [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union) and [Baguio](162480.md) while in the Marcos Highway via TPLEX and Kennon Road from McArthur Highway.
