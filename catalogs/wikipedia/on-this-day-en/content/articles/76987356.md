The **2026 UEFA Women's Champions League final** was the final match of the [2025–26 UEFA Women's Champions League](74122415.md), the 25th season of Europe's premier women's club [football](10568.md) tournament organised by [UEFA](32332.md), and the 17th season since it was renamed from the UEFA Women's Cup to the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md). It was played at the [Ullevaal Stadion](712728.md) in [Oslo](22309.md), Norway,[^cite_note-appointment-5] on 23 May 2026 between Spanish club [Barcelona](24763114.md) and French club [OL Lyonnes](8587183.md).

Barcelona won the match 4–0 for their fourth UEFA Women's Champions League title.[^cite_note-6] As winners, they qualified for the [2027 FIFA Women's Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_FIFA_Women's_Champions_Cup) semi-finals and the [2028 FIFA Women's Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_FIFA_Women's_Club_World_Cup). With the win, they completed their second continental [quadruple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_association_football_teams_to_have_won_four_or_more_trophies_in_one_season#Women).

## Background

[Barcelona](24763114.md) reached their seventh UEFA Women's Cup/Women's Champions League final overall and sixth consecutively, winning in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final) while losing in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final).

[OL Lyonnes](8587183.md) reached their record-extending twelfth UEFA Women's Cup/Women's Champions League final, having won a record eight titles in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), 2019, [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), and 2022 while losing in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), and 2024.

The clubs have previously met in three finals; in 2019, 2022, and 2024. The first two were won by OL Lyonnes, while the most recent was won by Barcelona. This final marked a record-equaling fourth UEFA Women's Cup/Women's Champions League final between the same two sides, matching the previous record of finals contested by OL Lyonnes and [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg_\(women\)).[^cite_note-7]

## Venue

### Host selection

On 17 May 2023, UEFA opened the bidding process for the final, which was held in parallel with that of the [2027 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final). Interested bidders could bid for either one or both of the finals. Additionally, bidding associations could only be appointed one UEFA final in a given year. The proposed venues had to include natural grass and be ranked as a [UEFA category four stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_stadium_categories), with a gross capacity of 30,000 to 60,000 preferred. The bidding timeline was as follows:[^cite_note-invitation_to_bid-8]

- 17 May 2023: Applications formally invited

- 17 July 2023: Closing date for registering intention to bid

- 26 July 2023: Bid requirements made available to bidders

- 15 November 2023: Submission of preliminary bid dossier

- 21 February 2024: Submission of final bid dossier

- 22 May 2024: Appointment of host

UEFA announced on 18 July 2023 that three associations had expressed interest in hosting the 2026 and 2027 finals during the first bidding process.[^cite_note-9]

## Match

### Summary

In the first half OL Lyonnes threatened the Barcelona goal a number of times, and after 15 minutes scored a goal through [Lindsey Heaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsey_Heaps) which was disallowed by [VAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) for offside.[^cite_note-BBCMR-12] For Barcelona, [Ewa Pajor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewa_Pajor) then had a shot on goal which narrowly went wide.[^cite_note-BBCMR-12][^cite_note-GUMR-13] In the second half Barcelona took control, and in the 55th minute Pajor scored the opening goal when she ran on to a pass from [Patri Guijarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patri_Guijarro) and struck a left-footed shot past goalkeeper [Christiane Endler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiane_Endler).[^cite_note-BBCMR-12][^cite_note-GUMR-13] 14 minutes later, Pajor doubled the lead for Barcelona by converting a cross from [Salma Paralluelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salma_Paralluelo).[^cite_note-BBCMR-12] OL Lyonnes tried to fight back, and Barcelona goalkeeper [Cata Coll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cata_Coll) kept out a shot from [Vicki Becho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicki_Becho) before preventing [Tabitha Chawinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabitha_Chawinga) from converting when she had only Coll to beat.[^cite_note-BBCMR-12] With the game almost over, Barcelona scored twice more through two goals from Paralluelo; the first with a long shot, before a break resulted in her being left with only the goalkeeper to beat.[^cite_note-GUMR-13] Barcelona thus regained the trophy that they had lost to [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) in 2025.[^cite_note-GUMR-13]
