The **Royal Australian Navy** (**RAN**) is the [naval branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) of the [Australian Defence Force](67538.md) (ADF). The professional head of the RAN is the [Chief of Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Navy_\(Australia\)) (CN),[^cite_note-2] Vice Admiral [Matthew Buckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Buckley). The Chief of Navy is also jointly responsible to the [Minister for Defence](593152.md) (MINDEF) and the [Chief of the Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Force_\(Australia\)) (CDF). The [Department of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defence_\(Australia\)), which is a part of the [Australian Public Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Public_Service), supports the ADF, and ergo, the Royal Australian Navy.[^cite_note-3]

The navy was formed in 1901 as the **Commonwealth Naval Forces** (**CNF**) through the amalgamation of the [colonial navies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_navies_of_Australia) following the [federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia). Although it was originally intended for local defence, it became increasingly responsible for regional defence as the British Empire started to diminish its influence in the South Pacific.

The Royal Australian Navy was initially a [green-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-water_navy), as the [Royal Navy](26061.md) provided a [blue-water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy) force to the [Australian Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Squadron), which the Australian and New Zealand governments helped to fund; the squadron was assigned to the [Australia Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Station). This period lasted until 1913, when naval ships purchased from Britain arrived, although the [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty) continued to provide blue-water defence capability in the Pacific and Indian Oceans up to the early years of the Second World War.[^cite_note-Oxcom-4]

During its history, the Royal Australian Navy has participated in a number of major wars, including the First and Second World Wars, Korean War, Malayan Emergency, Indonesia-Malaysia Confrontation and the Vietnam War. As of 2025, the RAN consists of 36 commissioned vessels, 12 non-commissioned vessels and over 16,000 personnel. The navy is one of the largest and most sophisticated naval forces in the South [Pacific region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim), with a significant presence in the Indian Ocean and worldwide operations in support of military campaigns and peacekeeping missions.

## History

Main article: [History of the Royal Australian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Royal_Australian_Navy)

### Formation

The Commonwealth Naval Forces were established on 1 March 1901, with the amalgamation of the six separate [colonial naval forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_navies_of_Australia), following the [Federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia).[^cite_note-5] The Commonwealth Navy initially consisted of the former New South Wales, Victorian, Queensland, Western Australian, South Australian and Tasmanian ships and resources of their disbanded navies.

The *[Defence Act 1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Act_1903)* established the operation and command structure of the Commonwealth Navy.[^cite_note-6] When policymakers sought to determine the newly established force's requirements and purpose, there were arguments about whether Australia's naval force would be structured mainly for local defence or designed to serve as a fleet unit within a larger imperial force, controlled centrally by the [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty).[^cite_note-Dennis516-7] In 1908–09, a compromise solution was pursued, with the Australian government agreeing to establish a force for local defence but that would be capable of forming a fleet unit within the Royal Navy, albeit without central control. As a result, the navy's force structure was set at "one battlecruiser, three light cruisers, six destroyers and three submarines". The first of the Navy's new vessels, the destroyer [HMAS Yarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Yarra_\(I\)), was completed in September 1910, and by the outbreak of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) the majority of the planned fleet had been realised.[^cite_note-Whitley17-8] On 10 July 1911, the Navy was renamed the Royal Australian Navy, with the approval of King [George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V).[^cite_note-9]

### World War I

#### Pacific

Following [the British Empire's declaration of war on Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_entry_into_World_War_I), the [British War Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_War_Office) tasked the capture of [German New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_New_Guinea) to the [Australian Government](1222540.md). This was to deprive the [Imperial German Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Navy)'s [East Asia Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Squadron) of regional intelligence by removing their access to wireless stations. On 11 August, three destroyers and [HMAS *Sydney*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Sydney_\(I\)) prepared to engage the squadron at German Anchorages in New Guinea, which did not eventuate as the vessels were not present. Landing parties were placed on [Rabaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabaul) and [Herbertshohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbertshohe) to destroy its German wireless station; however, the objective was found to be further inland and an expeditionary force was required. Meanwhile, [HMAS *Australia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Australia_\(I\)) was tasked with scouring the Pacific Ocean for the German squadron.

The [Australian Naval and Military Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Naval_and_Military_Expeditionary_Force) (ANMEF) began recruiting on the same day that the taskforce arrived in [New Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Britain), and consisted of two battalions: one of 1,000 men, and the other with 500 serving and former seamen. On 19 August, the ANMEF departed [Sydney](27862.md) for training in [Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville) before the rendezvous with other RAN vessels in [Port Moresby](57387.md).[^cite_note-\:2-10] On 29 August, four cruisers and HMAS *Australia* assisted New Zealand's [Samoa Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa_Expeditionary_Force) in landing at [Apia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apia), and committing a bloodless takeover of German Samoa. Additionally, the RAN captured German merchant vessels, disrupting German merchant shipping in the Pacific. On 7 September, the ANMEF, now including HMAS *Australia*, three destroyers, and two each of cruisers and submarines, departed for Rabaul.

A few days later, on 9 September, [HMAS *Melbourne*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Melbourne_\(I\)) landed a party to destroy the island's wireless station, though the German administration promptly surrendered. Between 11 and 12 September, landings were put ashore at Kabakaul, Rabaul and Herbertshohe; it was during this period that the first Australian casualties and deaths of the war occurred. On 14 September, [HMAS *Encounter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Encounter_\(1902\)) barraged an enemy position at Toma with shells; it was the first time the RAN had fired upon an enemy and had shelled an inland location. On 17 September, German New Guinea surrendered to the encroaching ANMEF, with the overall campaign a success and exceeded the objectives set by the War Office. However, the RAN submarine [HMAS *AE1*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_AE1) became the first ever vessel of the new navy to be sunk.[^cite_note-\:2-10] The Australian Squadron was placed under control of the [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty),[^cite_note-11] and was moreover tasked with protecting Australian shipping.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

On 1 November, the RAN escorted the [First Australian Imperial Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Australian_Imperial_Force) convoy from [Albany, WA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_WA) and set for the [Khedivate of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khedivate_of_Egypt), which was soon to become the [Sultanate of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Egypt). On 9 November, HMAS *Sydney* began hunting for [SMS *Emden*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Emden), a troublesome German coastal raider. The SMS *Emden* and HMAS *Sydney* met in the Battle of Cocos, the *Emden* was destroyed in Australia's first naval victory. Following the almost complete destruction of the East Asia Squadron in the [Battle of the Falklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Falklands) by the Royal Navy, the RAN became able to be reassigned to other naval theatres of the war.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

#### Atlantic and Mediterranean

On 28 February 1915, the [Royal Australian Naval Bridging Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Naval_Bridging_Train) (RANBT) was formed with members of the [Royal Australian Naval Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Naval_Reserve) who could not find billets in the RAN.[^cite_note-12] Following the entrance of the Ottoman Empire in alliance with the Central Powers, [HMAS *AE2*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_AE2) was committed to the initial naval operation of the [Gallipoli campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_campaign). After the failure of the naval strategy, an amphibious assault was planned to enable the Allies' warships to pass through the [Dardanelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanelles) and capture [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople). The RANBT was sent ashore, along with the invasion, for engineering duties.[^cite_note-13]

Later in the war, most of the RAN's major ships operated as part of [Royal Navy](26061.md) forces in the Mediterranean and North Seas, and then later in the Adriatic, and then the Black Sea following the surrender of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-Whitley17-8]

### Interwar years

In 1919, the RAN received a force of six destroyers, three sloops and six submarines from the Royal Navy,[^cite_note-14] but throughout the 1920s and early 1930s, the RAN was drastically reduced in size due to a variety of factors including political apathy and economic hardship as a result of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression).[^cite_note-Gillett61-15] In this time the focus of Australia's naval policy shifted from defence against invasion to trade protection,[^cite_note-Dennis518-16] and several fleet units were sunk as targets or scrapped. By 1923, the size of the navy had fallen to eight vessels,[^cite_note-Gillett61-15] and by the end of the decade it had fallen further to five, with just 3,500 personnel.[^cite_note-Dennis518-16] In the late 1930s, as international tensions increased, the RAN was modernised and expanded, with the service receiving primacy of funding over the [Army](2795.md) and [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) during this time as Australia began to prepare for war.[^cite_note-Dennis518-16]

### World War II

Early in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), RAN ships again operated as part of Royal Navy formations, many serving with distinction in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Mediterranean), the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Campaign_\(World_War_II\)), the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War), the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Madagascar), and off the [West African coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dakar).[^cite_note-17] Following the outbreak of the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) and the virtual destruction of Allied naval forces in [Southeast Asia](28741.md), the RAN operated more independently, defending against [Axis naval activity in Australian waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_naval_activity_in_Australian_waters), or participating in [United States Navy](20518076.md) offensives. As the navy took on an even greater role, it was expanded significantly and at its height the RAN was the fourth-largest navy in the world, with 39,650 personnel operating 337 warships, but no active submarines.[^cite_note-Dennis518-16] A total of 34 vessels were lost during the war, including three cruisers and four destroyers.[^cite_note-18]

### Post war to present



RAN Wessex helicopter in 1962

After the Second World War, the size of the RAN was again reduced, but it gained new capabilities with the acquisition of two aircraft carriers, [*Sydney*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Sydney_\(R17\)) and [*Melbourne*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Melbourne_\(R21\)).[^cite_note-19] The RAN saw action in many [Cold War](325329.md)–era conflicts in the Asia-Pacific region and operated alongside the Royal Navy and United States Navy off Korea, Malaysia, and Vietnam.[^cite_note-20] Since the end of the Cold War, the RAN has been part of Coalition forces in the Persian Gulf and Indian Ocean, operating in support of [Operation Slipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Slipper) and undertaking counter piracy operations. It was also deployed in support of Australian peacekeeping operations in [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INTERFET) and the [Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAMSI).[^cite_note-workingpaper-21]

The high demand for personnel in the Second World War led to the establishment of the [Women's Royal Australian Naval Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Royal_Australian_Naval_Service) (WRANS) branch in 1942, where over 3,000 women served in shore-based positions. The WRANS was disbanded in 1947, but then re-established in 1951 during the Cold War. It was given permanent status in 1959, and the RAN was the final branch to integrate [women in the Australian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Australian_military) in 1985.[^cite_note-Dennis607-22]

In April 2023, following the release of the *Defence Strategic Review*, the government sought an *[Independent analysis into Navy's surface fleet combatant fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Surface_Fleet_Review)*.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] In February 2024, the government released its response to the independent analysis.[^cite_note-25]

## Structure

### Command structure

The strategic command structure of the RAN was overhauled during the New Generation Navy changes.[^cite_note-26] The RAN is commanded through Naval Headquarters (NHQ) in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra).[^cite_note-27] NHQ is responsible for implementing policy decisions handed down from the [Department of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defence_\(Australia\)) and for overseeing tactical and operational issues that are the purview of the subordinate commands.[^cite_note-28]

Beneath NHQ are two subordinate commands:

- **Fleet Command**: fleet command is led by [Commander Australian Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_Australian_Fleet) (COMAUSFLT). COMAUSFLT holds the rank of [rear admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_admiral_\(Australia\)); previously, this post was Flag Officer Commanding HM's Australian Fleet (FOCAF), created in 1911,

  [^cite_note-29]

  &#x20;but the title was changed in 1988 to the Maritime Commander Australia. On 1 February 2007, the title changed again, becoming Commander Australian Fleet.

  [^cite_note-30]

  &#x20;The nominated at-sea commander is Commodore Warfare (COMWAR), a one-star deployable task group commander. Fleet command has responsibility to CN for the full command of assigned assets, and to Joint Operations command for the provision of operationally ready forces.

- **[Navy Strategic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Strategic_Command)**: the administrative element overseeing the RAN's training, engineering and logistical support needs. Instituted in 2000, the Systems Commander was appointed at the rank of [commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)); in June 2008, the position was upgraded to the rank of rear admiral.

Fleet Command was previously made up of seven [Force Element Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Element_Group), but after the New Generation Navy changes, this was restructured into four Force Commands:[^cite_note-31]

- [Fleet Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm_\(RAN\)) (previously known as the Australian Navy Aviation Group), responsible for the navy's aviation assets and capability. As of 2018, the FAA consists of two front line helicopter squadrons (one focused on anti-submarine and anti-shipping warfare and the other a transport unit), two training squadrons and a trials squadron.

  [^cite_note-32]

- Mine Warfare, Clearance Diving, Hydrographic, Meteorological and Patrol Forces, an amalgamation of the previous [Patrol Boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Patrol_Boat_Group), [Hydrographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Navy_Hydrographic_Service), and Mine Warfare and Clearance Diving Forces, operating what are collectively termed the RAN's "minor war vessels"

- [Submarine Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Navy_Submarine_Service), (Royal Australian Navy Submarine Service) operating the [*Collins*-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins-class_submarine)

- Surface Force, covering the RAN's surface combatants (generally ships of [frigate](11464.md) size or larger)

### Fleet

The Royal Australian Navy consists of over 50 commissioned vessels and over 16,000 personnel.[^cite_note-33] Ships commissioned into the RAN are given the prefix HMAS ([His/Her Majesty's Australian Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty's_Australian_Ship)).[^cite_note-34]

The RAN has two primary bases for its fleet: the first, [Fleet Base East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Base_East), is located at [HMAS *Kuttabul*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Kuttabul_\(naval_base\)), Sydney and the second, [Fleet Base West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Base_West), is located at [HMAS *Stirling*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Stirling), near Perth.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] In addition, three other bases are home to the majority of the RAN's minor war vessels: [HMAS *Cairns*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Cairns_\(naval_base\)), in Cairns, [HMAS *Coonawarra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Coonawarra), in Darwin, and [HMAS *Waterhen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Waterhen_\(naval_base\)), in Sydney.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

### Clearance Diving Branch

Main article: [Clearance Diving Branch (RAN)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearance_Diving_Branch_\(RAN\))



Australian Clearance Diving Team One conduct direct-action tactical manoeuvring during HYDRACRAB

The Clearance Diving Branch is composed of two *Clearance Diving Teams* (CDT) that serve as parent units for naval clearance divers:

- Clearance Diving Team 1 (AUSCDT ONE), based at HMAS *Waterhen* in [New South Wales](21654.md); and

- Clearance Diving Team 4 (AUSCDT FOUR), based at HMAS *Stirling* in Western Australia.

When clearance divers are sent into combat, Clearance Diving Team Three (AUSCDT THREE) is formed.

The CDTs have two primary roles:

- Mine counter-measures (MCM) and explosive ordnance disposal (EOD); and

- Maritime tactical operations.

## Personnel



A female RAN sailor in 2016. Women serve in the RAN in combat roles and at sea.

As of June 2023, the RAN has 14,745 permanent full-time personnel, 172 gap-year personnel, and 4,607 reserve personnel.[^cite_note-\:0-40] The permanent full-time trained force consists of 3,070 commissioned officers, and 9,695 enlisted personnel.[^cite_note-\:3-41] Male personnel made up 75.9% of the permanent full-time force, while female personnel made up 24%. The RAN has the second-highest percentage of women in the permanent forces, compared to the RAAF's 26.6% and the Army's 15.3%.[^cite_note-\:3-41] Throughout the 2022-23 financial year 1,141 enlisted in the RAN on a permanent basis while 1,354 left, representing a net loss of 213 personnel.[^cite_note-\:0-40]

Commissioned officers of the Australian Navy have pay grades ranging from S-1 to O-10.[^cite_note-Ranks-44] The highest rank achievable in the current Royal Australian Navy structure is O-10, an admiral who serves as the Chief of the Defence Force (CDF) when the position is held by a Naval Officer. The navy has a O-11 position [Admiral of the Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_fleet_\(Australia\)) that is honorary and is currently held by [Charles III](125248.md), [King of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Australia).[^cite_note-Ranks-44][^cite_note-45]

O-8 (rear admiral) to O-11 (admiral of the fleet) are referred to as [flag officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_officer), O-5 (commander) and above are referred to as [senior officers](150237.md#Field_or_senior_officers), while S-1 (midshipman) to O-4 (lieutenant commander) are referred to as [junior officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_officer). All RAN Officers are issued a commission by the [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General_of_Australia) as [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief) on behalf of His Majesty King Charles III.

Naval officers are trained at the [Royal Australian Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Naval_College) (HMAS *Creswell*) in [Jervis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay_Territory) as well as the [Australian Defence Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Defence_Force_Academy) in Canberra.[^cite_note-46]

### Rate insignia



Royal Australian Navy sailors from HMAS *Sydney* during Operation Northern Trident 2009

Royal Australian Navy sailors wear "right arm rates" insignia, called "Category Insignia" to indicate specialty training qualifications.[^cite_note-RAN_Website-50] This is a holdover from the Royal Navy.

#### Special insignia

The [Warrant Officer of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_Officer_of_the_Navy) (WO-N) is an appointment held by the most senior sailor in the RAN and holds the rank of warrant officer (WO). However, the WO-N does not wear the WO rank insignia; instead, they wear the special insignia of the appointment.[^cite_note-51] The WO-N appointment has similar equivalent appointments in the other services, each holding the rank of warrant officer, each being the most senior sailor/soldier/airman in that service, and each wearing their own special insignia rather than their rank insignia. The Australian Army equivalent is the [Regimental Sergeant Major of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regimental_Sergeant_Major_of_the_Army) (RSM-A)[^cite_note-52] and the Royal Australian Air Force equivalent is the [Warrant Officer of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_Officer_of_the_Air_Force) (WOFF-AF).[^cite_note-53]

#### Chaplains and Maritime Spiritual Wellbeing Officers



Rank Insignia for Royal Australian Navy Chaplains and Maritime Spiritual Wellbeing Officers (from 2021).

Chaplains in the Royal Australian Navy are commissioned officers who complete the same training as other officers in the RAN at the Royal Australian Naval College, HMAS Creswell. From July 2020, Maritime Spiritual Wellbeing Officers (MSWOs) were introduced to the Navy Chaplaincy Branch, designed to give Navy people and their families with professional, non-religious pastoral care and spiritual support.[^cite_note-54]

In the Royal Australian Navy, Chaplains and MSWOs are commissioned officers without rank. For reasons of protocol, ceremonial occasions and for saluting purposes, they are, where appropriate, normally grouped with Commanders (O-5).[^cite_note-55] The more senior Division 4 Senior Chaplains are grouped with Captains (O-6) and Division 5 Principal Chaplains are grouped with Commodores (O-7), but their rank slide remains the same. Principal Chaplains and MSWOs, however, have gold braid on the peak of their white service cap.

From January 2021, MSWOs and all chaplains wear the branch's new non-faith-specific rank insignia of a fouled anchor overlaying a compass rose, which represents a united team front, encompassing all faiths and purpose. Chaplains and MSWOs have insignia that reflect their religion on collar mounted patches (Cross for Christian, Crescent for Muslim etc, Compass rose for MSWOs.)[^cite_note-56]

### Future

Main article: [Future of the Royal Australian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_the_Royal_Australian_Navy)

There are currently several major projects underway that will see upgrades to RAN capabilities.

- *Project SEA 1180 Phase 1* is building six [*Arafura*-class offshore patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura-class_offshore_patrol_vessel) based on the [Lürssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCrssen) OPV80 design, to replace *Armidale*-class patrol boats. Construction started in November 2018, with the first vessel HMAS *Arafura* entering service in 2025.

  [^cite_note-\:1-73]

- *Project SEA 1445 Phase 1* is the acquisition of ten Evolved [*Cape* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape-class_patrol_boat) patrol boats to be built by [Austal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austal) in [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Western_Australia).

## Current operations

Main article: [Current Australian Defence Force deployments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_Australian_Defence_Force_deployments)

The RAN currently has forces deployed on seven major operations:[^cite_note-99]

- Operation Resolute: border protection and fisheries patrol.

- Operation Manitou: counter-piracy, counterterrorism and maritime stability in the Middle East and

- Operation Accordion: support operation to provide sustainment to forces deployed on Operation Manitou.

- Operation Solania: contribute to maritime surveillance in the Pacific.

- Operation Gateway: operate maritime patrols in the Indian Ocean and South China Sea, alongside Malaysia.

- Operation Render Safe: safely dispose of remnant explosives of [World War II](32927.md) on Pacific nations.

- Operation Dyurra: integrate space capabilities into other operations.
