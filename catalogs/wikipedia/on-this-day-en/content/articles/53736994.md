**Cale Douglas Makar**[^cite_note-1] (born October 30, 1998) is a Canadian professional [ice hockey](14790.md) player who is a [defenceman](567684.md) and [alternate captain](502359.md#Alternate_captains) for the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL). He was selected with the fourth overall pick by the Avalanche in the [2017 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-2]

After two seasons in the [National Collegiate Athletic Association](60706.md) (NCAA) with the [UMass Minutemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMass_Minutemen_ice_hockey), Makar debuted in the NHL during the [2019 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). Playing his first full season in [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_NHL_season), he made an immediate impact and won the [Calder Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy) as rookie of the year. In his second professional season he was nominated for the [James Norris Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Norris_Memorial_Trophy), awarded to the league's best defenceman, and won the Norris in the following [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NHL_season); he later won a second Norris in the [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NHL_season), in his fifth nomination. Makar won the [Stanley Cup](66968.md) with the Avalanche in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Stanley_Cup_Final), winning the [Conn Smythe Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy) as the most valuable player in the [2022 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). He has been widely cited as one of the best defencemen and best players in the modern NHL.[^cite_note-3]

Internationally, Makar has played for the [Canadian junior team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_men's_national_junior_ice_hockey_team), winning gold at the [2018 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_World_Junior_Ice_Hockey_Championships). With the [senior national team](744439.md), he won gold at the [2025 4 Nations Face-Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_4_Nations_Face-Off) and silver at the [2026 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics_-_Men's_tournament).

## Playing career

### Junior

Makar, a [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary) native, first played within the Crowchild Blackhawks minor-hockey program before moving to play with the Northwest Calgary Athletic Association (NWCAA) Bruins at the [Bantam level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_ice_hockey#Age_categories) in 2011. He also played bantam with the Calgary Flames before moving to minor midget with the NWCAA Stampeders.[^cite_note-4] Makar was originally drafted at the [major junior level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey) by the [Western Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hockey_League) (WHL)'s [Medicine Hat Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine_Hat_Tigers) in the eighth round, 164th overall, of the 2013 WHL Bantam Draft.[^cite_note-5]

Having returned to the Calgary Flames for the minor midget level, in his only full season with the club in 2014–15, Makar led the team in scoring with 23 points in 34 league games, being named the team's Most Valuable Player and selected to the [Alberta Midget Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Midget_Hockey_League) First All-Star Team. To retain his [NCAA](60706.md) eligibility, Makar joined as an affiliate player to the [Brooks Bandits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks_Bandits) of the [Alberta Junior Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Junior_Hockey_League) (AJHL) at the tail end of the season, recording five points in three regular season games. In helping the Bandits reach the finals, Makar was third among defenceman in scoring with 7 points in 20 games. Having agreed to return with the Bandits in the forthcoming seasons, Makar announced his commitment to play collegiate hockey with the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMass_Minutemen_ice_hockey) of the [Hockey East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_East) NCAA conference on August 29, 2015.[^cite_note-6]

As a 17-year-old, Makar established himself as a standout defenceman with the Bandits in 2015–16, logging 55 points in 54 games, en route to earning AJHL All-League and All-Rookie recognition. He scored 14 points in 13 games to help the Bandits claim the AJHL championship. His productive season collected AJHL and CJHL Rookie of the Year Awards, the Western Canada Cup Top Defenceman Award, and the RBC Cup Top Defenceman, Top Scorer and Most Valuable Player awards.[^cite_note-AJHL-7]

Makar sustained and built upon his previous success in the 2016–17 season to lead all defencemen and finished sixth among all skaters in the AJHL with 75 points (24 goals and 51 assists) in 54 games, collecting the league's MVP and top defenceman accolades. He had 16 points in 13 playoff games to help Brooks to a second straight AJHL championship and six points in five games to help the Bandits to a second-place finish in the [Royal Bank Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bank_Cup). He was chosen as the RBC Cup Top Defenceman and Most Valuable Player, the first player to ever be named RBC Cup MVP in back-to-back seasons. He received the CJHL Most Valuable Player Award and won the prestigious RBC National Junior A Player of the Year Award.[^cite_note-8]



Makar selected by the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) at the [2017 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NHL_entry_draft).

Through Makar's rapid rise up the rankings before the [2017 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NHL_entry_draft), he was considered a top prospect and one of the top defencemen available.[^cite_note-9] He was described as a dynamic skater that creates offence whenever he is on the ice.[^cite_note-10] Makar was ultimately selected fourth overall by the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md), the second defenceman selected in the draft, after third overall selection [Miro Heiskanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miro_Heiskanen). Makar became the highest-drafted player to come straight from the AJHL, and just the second in the first round since [Joe Colborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Colborne) in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-11]

### College

Despite his blue-chip prospect status, Makar opted to remain loyal to his commitment to joining the rebuilding UMass Minuteman program for the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NCAA_Division_I_men's_ice_hockey_season).[^cite_note-develop-12] As a freshman, he immediately assumed a top-pairing role, alongside Jake McLaughlin, and recorded his first collegiate point in his debut against [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_Sun_Devils_men's_ice_hockey) on October 6, 2017. He recorded his first goal in a 4–0 victory over [Merrimack College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrimack_Warriors_men's_ice_hockey) on October 27, 2017.[^cite_note-13]

While showing an adjustment period to the collegiate level, Makar raised his game through the new year to help the Minutemen to turnaround their program to make the postseason before suffering a 7–2 defeat to the hands of [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Huskies_men's_ice_hockey). He finished fifth on the team in scoring, second amongst defenceman, with 5 goals and 16 assists for 21 points in 34 games.[^cite_note-14] He was selected with co-Rookie of the Year honors by the New England Hockey Writers Association and by finishing ninth in defenceman scoring in the [Hockey East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_East), he was selected to the conference's All-Rookie and Third All-Star Teams.[^cite_note-HE-15]

With the season concluded and despite interest from the Colorado Avalanche in turning pro, Makar opted to continue his development in returning for his sophomore season with UMass in [2018–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_NCAA_Division_I_men's_ice_hockey_season).[^cite_note-16] That season, Makar became the first Minutemen honored as [Hockey East Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hockey_East_Player_of_the_Year) after he led the league in scoring and finished second in the conference.[^cite_note-POY-17] Makar was also selected for the [Hockey East First All-Star Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_All-Hockey_East_Teams#First_Team)[^cite_note-2019_All-Star-18] and named a [Hobey Baker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobey_Baker_Award) finalist.[^cite_note-19] On April 12, Makar was announced the winner of the Hobey Baker.[^cite_note-Hobey_Baker-20] The following night, Makar and the Minutemen lost 3–0 to [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota%E2%80%93Duluth_Bulldogs_men's_ice_hockey) in the [Frozen Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NCAA_Division_I_Men's_Ice_Hockey_Tournament) final.[^cite_note-21]

### Professional

#### Debut and Calder Trophy (2019–2021)

On April 14, 2019, the Avalanche signed Makar to a three-year, entry-level contract.[^cite_note-22] He scored his first career NHL goal with his first shot in the league in his debut a day later, during game 3 of the first round of the [2019 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) against the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames). In so doing, he became the first defenceman to score a playoff goal in his NHL debut.[^cite_note-23]

Makar made the Avalanche opening lineup for the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_NHL_season) and he scored his first career regular season goal in a 6–1 win over the [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md).[^cite_note-24] He was subsequently named a Third Star of the Week for the week of November 10, becoming the first Avalanche rookie to earn the honor.[^cite_note-25] On November 12, 2019, in a 4–0 road win against the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets), Makar became the second Avalanche defenceman to record 18 points in 18 games as a rookie.[^cite_note-26] However, he suffered an upper-body injury in a game against the [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) on December 7 and was listed as day-to-day. At the time of his injury, he was leading rookies in scoring with 28 points in 29 games.[^cite_note-27] Makar returned to the Avalanche's lineup on December 27, where he recorded an assist in the 6–4 loss to the [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild).[^cite_note-28] On January 18, 2020, Makar set a new Avalanche franchise record with his 11th goal of the season, besting [John-Michael Liles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John-Michael_Liles) record for most goals by a rookie defenceman.[^cite_note-29] As a result of his play, he was named a [Calder Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy) finalist alongside [Quinn Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinn_Hughes) and [Dominik Kubalík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Kubal%C3%ADk).[^cite_note-30] During the second round of the NHL playoffs, Makar surpassed the record for most points in a postseason by a rookie defenceman, before it was beaten by Hughes the following night.[^cite_note-31] Following the conclusion of the regular season, Makar was awarded the Calder Memorial Trophy as the rookie of the year.[^cite_note-32]

After the [pandemic](62750956.md)-shortened 2020–21 season, which saw the Avalanche win the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy), Makar was named a finalist for the [James Norris Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Norris_Memorial_Trophy) and finished second in the voting for the trophy, behind [Adam Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Fox) of the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers).[^cite_note-33]

#### Stanley Cup, Norris and Conn Smythe trophies (2021–present)

On July 24, 2021, Makar signed a six-year, \$54 million extension with the Avalanche.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] On October 28, Makar scored two points (one goal and one assist) in a 4–3 win against the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues), in doing so, he scored his 100th point in just 108 games, which tied him with [Sergei Zubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Zubov) as the sixth-fastest defenceman to accomplish this feat.[^cite_note-36] On March 25, 2022, Makar scored his 24th goal of the 2021–22 season in a 6–3 win against the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers), breaking the Avalanche's single-season record for goals scored by a defenceman.[^cite_note-37] He finished the season with 28 goals, the most by a defenceman in the NHL since [Brent Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Burns) in the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NHL_season), and 58 assists. 19 of his 28 goals were at even strength. Makar was named a James Norris Memorial Trophy finalist for the second consecutive season.[^cite_note-38] He won the Norris Trophy, the first Avalanche defenceman to do so, and finished eighth in voting for the [Hart Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Memorial_Trophy), awarded by the [Professional Hockey Writers' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Hockey_Writers'_Association) to the league's [most valuable player](235000.md).[^cite_note-39] The Avalanche finished first in the [Western Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Conference_\(NHL\)), and second in the league overall, advancing into the [2022 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) to meet the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators) in the first round. Makar scored 10 points in a four-game sweep of the Predators, setting a record for points by a defenceman in the first four games of a postseason. Teammate [Nathan MacKinnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_MacKinnon) remarked, "he might be the best player in the league right now."[^cite_note-40] He would amass a further 19 points in the next three rounds, as the Avalanche ousted the Blues and the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) before defeating the two-time defending champion [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning) in the [2022 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Stanley_Cup_Final), winning his first Stanley Cup. Makar finished the playoffs with a team-leading 29 points (eight goals and 21 assists), the fourth-most for a defenceman in a single postseason in league history.[^cite_note-41] He was awarded the [Conn Smythe Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy) as the most valuable player in the postseason, and became only the third defenceman to win the Norris and Conn Smythe Trophies in the same season, after [Bobby Orr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Orr) ([1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_NHL_season) and [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_NHL_season)) and [Nicklas Lidström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicklas_Lidstr%C3%B6m) ([2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_NHL_season)).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Makar recorded 20 points in the first 17 games of the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NHL_season), his 20th being a power play goal scored in a November 21, 2022, game against the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars). This was his 200th career point in 195 games, the fastest pace of any NHL defenceman to that milestone.[^cite_note-44] Previous record holder [Sergei Zubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Zubov) reached the mark in 207 games.[^cite_note-45] He attracted notice when, during a December 19 game against the [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders), he convinced the referee to rescind a tripping penalty that had been incorrectly assessed against Islanders forward [Mathew Barzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_Barzal), with Makar clarifying that he had fallen on his own rather than being tripped. Barzal remarked that it was "obviously, good sportsmanship on his part. I don't know if I would have done the same, to be honest with you."[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Injuries caused Makar to miss 22 games in the regular season, but he finished with 17 goals and 49 assists in 60 games, and was named a Norris Trophy finalist for the third consecutive year.[^cite_note-48]



Makar after hitting [Jared McCann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_McCann) during game four of the [2023 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).

During game four of the first round of the [2023 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), Makar was involved in a controversial on-ice incident after delivering what many deemed to be a hard, late hit into the boards on [Seattle Kraken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Kraken) forward [Jared McCann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_McCann). Although the play had not officially been whistled dead, the puck had been deflected into the upper safety netting and McCann had slowed his pace in anticipation of a whistle when Makar delivered the hit. The officials initially appeared to assess Makar for a five-minute major penalty; he ultimately received a two-minute interference call. McCann, who was ruled out for game five, was injured as a result of the hit and did not return for the rest of the game, which was ultimately a 3–2 overtime victory for the Kraken. After the game, Makar stated that he had not intended to injure McCann and that he was unsure if the puck was still in play at the time.[^cite_note-49] The next day, Makar received a one-game suspension for the hit on McCann; this was the first suspension (and first disciplinary offense) of Makar's NHL career.[^cite_note-50] Makar returned for the remainder of the series, which saw the Avalanche upset by the Kraken in seven games.[^cite_note-51]

On March 6, 2024, Makar recorded his first career NHL [hat trick](411541.md#Hockey) in a 7–2 victory over the [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings); becoming the first Avalanche defenceman to do so since [Sandis Ozoliņš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandis_Ozoli%C5%86%C5%A1) back on December 6, 1999.[^cite_note-52] He was voted a Norris Trophy finalist for the fourth consecutive season, having finished second in points and first in points-per-game among defencemen.[^cite_note-53]

The following [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NHL_season) would see Makar reach new offensive achievements. On April 3, 2025, he recorded his 30th goal of the season, becoming the ninth defenceman in NHL history to reach that mark, and only second in the post-[lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout) era.[^cite_note-54] In 80 games, he finished the season with 92 points (30 goals, 62 assists), ninth in league scoring overall. In addition to being a Norris Trophy finalist for the fifth time, he was for the first time a finalist for the [Ted Lindsay Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lindsay_Award), awarded by the [National Hockey League Players' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League_Players'_Association) to the "most outstanding" player in the NHL, ultimately going to Tampa Bay Lightning's forward [Nikita Kucherov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Kucherov).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Makar was subsequently awarded his second career Norris Trophy on June 11, 2025, receiving 176 of 191 first-place votes.[^cite_note-2025Norris-57]

During a March 18, 2026, game against the Dallas Stars, Makar scored his 20th goal of the [2025–26 NHL season](79321758.md), becoming the first defenceman since [Phil Housley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Housley) to score 20 goals in three straight seasons.[^cite_note-58] He scored the 500th point of his career when he assisted on a [Brock Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Nelson) goal in a 4–2 loss to the Winnipeg Jets on March 28.[^cite_note-59] Makar finished the regular season with 20 goals and 59 assists in 75 games, helping the Avalanche to win the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy) for the second time in his tenure. He was a Norris Trophy finalist for the sixth year in a row.[^cite_note-60]

Entering the [2026–27 season](82019540.md), the final year of his contract, Makar signed an eight-year extension on August 28, 2026. With a total value of \$163.2 million and an average annual value of \$20.4 million, it was the most expensive contract in NHL history, and the first with an average annual value in excess of \$20 million.[^cite_note-61] This milestone came at a time of explosive projected growth in the league's [salary cap](364837.md) as a result of revenue increases.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Makar was first selected by [Hockey Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Canada) to compete for Canada West at the [World Junior A Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_A_Challenge) in 2015 and 2016. He was selected to the Tournament All-Star Team in both years and helped Canada West to a gold medal in 2015.[^cite_note-64] Unable to defend the gold medal, Makar followed up by setting a tournament single-game record for points by a defenceman in 2016.[^cite_note-65] Makar appeared for Canada at the World Junior Summer Showcase. He had one goal and one assist during the exhibition. His single goal came at a pivotal time on August 4, 2017, against Sweden, tying the game 4–4 in the third period. The marker re-energized the Canadians as they registered three more goals to pull off a 7–4 win.[^cite_note-develop-12]

Makar was selected to the [Canada national junior team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_men's_national_junior_ice_hockey_team) for the [2018 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_World_Junior_Ice_Hockey_Championships), winning gold. Makar finished the tournament leading all defenceman with three goals and eight points in seven games, and was the only Canadian named to the All-Tournament Team.[^cite_note-WJC-66]

Following the World Juniors, Makar reportedly turned down an invite to join the [Canada senior team](744439.md) for the [2018 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2018_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament) to focus on his season at UMass, as he would have had to miss three weeks for the tournament.[^cite_note-67]

With NHL players not being permitted to play in the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics), and Colorado seeing extensive postseason success after Makar's NHL breakthrough, he did not play for Canada's national team until the [4 Nations Face-Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Nations_Face-Off) in 2025. After missing Canada's group stage game against the [United States](945923.md) due to illness,[^cite_note-68] he was able to return in time for the subsequent game against [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_men's_national_ice_hockey_team).[^cite_note-69] In the event final, a rematch against the United States, Makar had an assist on [Connor McDavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_McDavid)'s winning goal in overtime, earning his first senior international title.[^cite_note-70]

Makar was one of the first six players named to [Canada's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) for the [2026 Winter Olympics](77611835.md), the first edition with NHL participation since [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2014_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament).[^cite_note-71] He was also selected as one of the team's alternate captains.[^cite_note-72] Canada reached the final against the United States, where Makar scored the game-tying goal in the second period, but the Americans ultimately prevailed 2–1 in overtime to win the gold medal.[^cite_note-73] He was named to the Olympic All-Star Team.[^cite_note-2026Olympics-74]

## Personal life

Makar is the son of Gary Makar and Laura MacGregor.[^cite_note-AJHL-7] He was named after former NHL player [Cale Hulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cale_Hulse).[^cite_note-Name-75] His younger brother [Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Makar) is a prospect for the Avalanche after signing a one-year, entry-level contract on March 31, 2025, after spending three years with the Minutemen, and was also drafted by the Avalanche in the seventh round of the [2021 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Makar's cousin, Mark Logan, played from 2015 to 2019 at the [Rochester Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIT_Tigers_men's_ice_hockey).[^cite_note-79] [Tom Lysiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Lysiak) was his father's cousin.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] He is of Ukrainian descent on his father's side.[^cite_note-82] Growing up, he was a fan of the hometown [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames).[^cite_note-Name-75]

Makar married his longtime girlfriend, Tracy Evans, in August 2024.[^cite_note-83]
