The **1991 Rhineland-Palatinate state election** in Germany was held on 21 April 1991 to elect the members of the [Landtag of Rhineland-Palatinate](583737.md). The incumbent [coalition government](6036.md) of the [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU) and [Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_\(Germany\)) (FDP) led by [Minister-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ministers-President_of_Rhineland-Palatinate) [Carl-Ludwig Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl-Ludwig_Wagner) was defeated, losing its majority.[^cite_note-1] The [Social Democratic Party](60585.md) (SPD) moved into first place, forming a coalition with the FDP. SPD leader [Rudolf Scharping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Scharping) was subsequently elected as Minister-President.[^cite_note-2]

This election marked several changes in the state history:&#x20;

First, a change in the state election system. Previous elections were held purely proportionally, however, from that election on, the elections were held more in line with the federal [mixed-member proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-member_proportional_representation), with both direct constituencies and proportional representation.

Secondly, from 1991 on, the Landtag became elected for five years instead of the previous four.

Thirdly, the election marked a shift in the state's political alignment: for 44 years (1947-1991) the state had been governed by CDU, with CDU coming first in every election.[^cite_note-3] After this election, CDU would not come first nor partake in any state governments again before [2026](70945023.md).[^cite_note-4]
