**Algiers** ([/ælˈdʒɪərz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*al-JEERZ*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجزائر, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Jazāʾir*, pronounced [\[al.d͡ʒazaːʔir\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/9a/LL-Q13955\_%28ara%29-Reda_Kerbouche-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1.wav/LL-Q13955\_%28ara%29-Reda_Kerbouche-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/9a/LL-Q13955_%28ara%29-Reda_Kerbouche-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1.wav/LL-Q13955_%28ara%29-Reda_Kerbouche-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [locally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Arabic) [\[(ɪ)ˈd͡ːzaːjir\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Alger*, pronounced [\[alʒe\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8e/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Alger.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Alger.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8e/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Alger.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Alger.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Algerian Tamazight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Algerian_Amazigh): ⴷⵣⴰⵢⵔ, romanized: *Dzayer*), officially **Capital City of Algiers** (Arabic: الجزائر العاصمة, romanized: *al-Jazāʾir al-ʿĀṣima*; Algerian Tamazight: ⴷⵣⴰⵢⵔ ⵜⴰⵎⴰⵏⴰⵖⵜ, romanized: *Dzayer Tamaneɣt*), is the capital and [largest city of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Algeria), located on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) in the north-central portion of the country. In 2025, an estimated 4.325 million people resided within the urban area.[^cite_note-2] Algiers is the [third-largest city on the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coastal_settlements_of_the_Mediterranean_Sea), the [tenth-largest city in the Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_in_the_Arab_world), and the [19th-largest city in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Africa_by_population) by population. Algiers is the capital of the [Algiers Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Province); it extends over many [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Algeria) without having its own separate governing body. It extends along the Bay of Algiers surrounded by the [Mitidja Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitidja_Plain) and major mountain ranges. Its favorable location made it the center of [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_of_Algiers) and French influences for the region, shaping it to be a diverse [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis).

Algiers was formally founded in 972 AD by [Buluggin ibn Ziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluggin_ibn_Ziri), though its history goes back to between 1200 and 250 BC as a [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) trading settlement. Over time, it came under the control of several powers, including [Numidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia), the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), and various [Islamic caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_caliphates). In 1516, it became the capital of the Ottoman [Regency of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_of_Algiers), a status it held until the [French invasion in 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Algiers_in_1830), after which it served as the capital of [French Algeria](292341.md). During [World War II](32927.md), it briefly functioned as the administrative center of [Free France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) from 1942 to 1944 before returning to French colonial rule. It has remained the capital of the modern Algerian state since the [Algerian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War) in 1962.

Algiers is the main tourist destination in Algeria, known for its numerous museums, art galleries, and cultural institutions. Most notable is the historic [Casbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casbah_of_Algiers), a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) featuring traditional Algerian, Ottoman, and [Andalusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) architecture. The city also contains a larger French-built section that showcases diverse architectural styles and trends. Commonly referred to as *al bidha* ("the white") for its whitewashed buildings, Algiers blends colonial and indigenous urban influences. It hosted the [1975 Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Mediterranean_Games) and various major international sporting events, and it serves as the seat of the Consultative Council of the [Arab Maghreb Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Maghreb_Union). Numerous Algerian multinational companies and institutions are based in the city, such as [Sonatrach](1749631.md) Petroleum Corporation, [Air Algérie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Alg%C3%A9rie), and [Bank of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Algeria).

## Toponymy and name origin

&#x20;The name of the city in Arabic is *al-Jazāʾir* (الجزائر), and in [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Algerian_Amazigh) *Dzayer* (ⴷⵣⴰⵢⵔ). Due to its similarity to the name of the country in local languages, it is officially specified as "Capital City of Algiers" (Arabic: الجزائر العاصمة, romanized: *al-Jazāʾir al-ʿĀṣima*; Berber: ⴷⵣⴰⵢⵔ ⵜⴰⵎⴰⵏⴰⵖⵜ, romanized: *Dzayer Tamaneɣt*; both lit. 'Algiers the Capital').[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

The Arabic name *al-Jazāʾir* (الجزائر), meaning 'the Islands', this name's origin is related to the 4 main islands off the western [cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_\(geography\)) where people settled, looking on a map one can notice that the islands were eventually connected to the mainland in 1525 AD via a pier now named *Kheireddine pier*. This name is a truncated form of the name that was used first by [Buluggin ibn Ziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluggin_ibn_Ziri) when he established the modern city in 972 AD (on the ruins of the [Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic) city of [Icosium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosium))[^cite_note-5] which was *Jazāʾir Banī Mazghanna* (جزائر بني مزغانة), meaning 'islands of [Banu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_\(Arabic\)) Mazghanna', this term was used by the [Hammadid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammadid_dynasty) as well as early medieval geographers such as [Muhammad al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) and [Yaqut al-Hamawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqut_al-Hamawi). Before that, from French and [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) *Alger*[^cite_note-leschi-6] from the Arabic name, *al-Jazāʾir*. During [Ottoman](22278.md) rule, the name of the capital, *al-Jazāʾir*, was extended over the entire country, giving it the English name *Algeria* derived from the French name *Algérie*.[^cite_note-7]

In [classical antiquity](252905.md), the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Graeco-Roman_geographers) knew the town as *Ikósion* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἰκόσιον), which was [Latinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinization_of_names) as Icosium under [Roman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic). The Greeks [explained the name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology) as coming from their word for 'twenty' (εἴκοσι, *eíkosi*), supposedly because it had been founded by 20 companions of [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules) when he visited the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains) during [his labors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labors_of_Hercules).[^cite_note-backtalk-8]

Algiers is also known as *el-Behdja* (البهجة, 'the Joyous') *El Mahrussa* ('the Good-guarded'), or "Algiers the White" (French: *Alger la Blanche*) for its whitewashed buildings.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

The local name *Dzayer* is the origin of the [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym) *dziri*, which could be connected with the [Zirids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirids), the founders of the city, as suggested by Tassadit Yacine.[^cite_note-11] Academic discussions often omit that the name that both Arabic- and Berber-speaking inhabitants have always given to their city is *Dzayer*. However, *Dzayer* raises the question of the origin of the [affricate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affricate) \[[dz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_alveolar_affricate)], which neither Arabic nor Berber possesses in its consonantal system. One might wonder if the sound /[z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_alveolar_fricative)/, replacing /[ʒ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_postalveolar_fricative)/, is a persistent trace of Berber. Since, as mentioned, the sound is found in the name of *Zirids*, some imagine that *Dzayer* is the result of the contraction of /aθˈziri/, meaning 'those of Ziri'. In addition, /z/ is represented by the [Tifinagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifinagh) letter [ⵣ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E2%B5%A3), which is a symbol of [Berber identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) and culture and is prominently featured on [their flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_flag).[^cite_note-12]

### Early history

Main article: [Icosium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosium)

The city's history is believed to date back to 1200 BC, but it was a small settlement without any significance until around the 3rd century BC when "Ikosim" became a small port town in [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage) where [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) were trading with other Mediterranean ports. After the [Battle of Cirta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cirta), [Numidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia) got a hold of the town along with its neighboring regions at around 202 BC, after which the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) started weakening the [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) nation. On 104 BC, following the capturing of [Jughurta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jughurta) and executing him in Rome, the western half of his nation was given to [Mauretania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauretania) under the rule of [Bocchus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocchus_I). At around 42 AD, [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius) divided Mauretania into two provinces, [Mauretania Caesariensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauretania_Caesariensis) that included [Icosium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosium) as one of its towns; the second province was [Mauretania Tingitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauretania_Tingitana) and were deemed as Roman [Municipiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipium), additionally they were given [Latin rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_rights) by the emperor [Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian). In 371-373 AD, Mauretania revolted with the help of [Firmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmus_\(4th-century_usurper\)), in hopes of establishing an independent state. Icosium was raided and damaged. Some clues show the presence of [bishops](4092.md) in the region at this time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911655-13]

In 435 AD, the [Vandal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandal_Kingdom) took control of [northern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Africa) along the coasts of today's [Tunisia](30188.md) and [Algeria](358.md). The [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) that was ruling the area allowed the Vandals to settle when it became clear that they could not be defeated by Roman military forces. Though the city was damaged again due to the fighting between the two armies, the town was still slowly growing in population.

### Medieval history

In 534 AD, the Vandal kingdom was subjugated by the general [Belisarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisarius) of the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire), making Icosium a part of the empire. In the early 7th century, "Beni Mezghenna" who are a [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) tribe belonging to the [Sanhaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanhaja) as cited by [Ibn Khaldoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldoun), settled on the plains of [Icosium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosium) and the surrounding areas.[^cite_note-14] Shortly after, in the late 7th century, the [Muslim conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb) brought the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) into the region, but were faced with resistance from Berber forces led by [Kahina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahina) and [Kusaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusaila) in the 680s, who opposed the advancing Islamic armies. However, [Hassan ibn al-Nu'man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_ibn_al-Nu'man) and [Musa ibn Nusayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_ibn_Nusayr) later defeated both Berber leaders, killing Kusaila at the [Battle of Mamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mamma) (688) and killing Dihya at the [Battle of Tabarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tabarka) (702), leading to the subjugation of the Berber tribes, bringing Islamic rule into North Africa. The [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) succeeded [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) at around 750 AD. Independence movements across the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) resulted in the breaking of two nations, the [Idrisid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idrisid_dynasty) and the [Aghlabid Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghlabid_Emirate) but acted as agents of the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasids) in [Baghdad](4492.md). Icosium fell into the hands of Aghlabids and abandoned the town. They were then overthrown by the [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) in 909 AD, who went on to control all of [Ifriqiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifriqiya) by 969 AD.

The present city was re-founded in 972 AD by [Buluggin ibn Ziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluggin_ibn_Ziri), who was appointed by the Faṭimid caliph [al-Muʿizz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mu%CA%BFizz) as governor of [al-Qayrawān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qayraw%C4%81n) and any other territory his nation, the [Zirid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirid_Dynasty) might reclaim from its enemies, the [Zenata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenata) tribesmen. His state accordingly expanded its boundaries westward. In approximately 1014 AD, under the reign of [Badis ibn al-Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badis_ibn_al-Mansur), the dynasty was divided between the Zirids at al-Qayrawan in the east, and the [Hammadid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammadid_dynasty) at [Qal'at Bani Hammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qal'at_Bani_Hammad); "Jazaʾir Banī Mazghanna", commonly known as "Algiers" as the new name of [Icosium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosium) was absorbed into the [Hammadid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammadid_dynasty)[^cite_note-Ruedy2005-15] who in 1067 AD relocated to [Béjaïa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9ja%C3%AFa) and carried on a lively trade while most of North Africa was under a state of [anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy).

In 1079 AD, [Ibn Tashfin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Tashfin), a Sanhaja leader of the [Almoravid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid_Empire) sent an army of 20,000 men from Marrakesh to push towards what is now [Tlemcen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlemcen) to attack the "Banu Ya'la", the [Zenata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenata) tribe occupying the area. Led by [Mazdali ibn Tilankan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazdali_ibn_Tilankan), the army defeated the Banu Ya'la in battle near the valley of the [Moulouya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulouya_River) and executed their commander, the son of Tlemcen's ruler. However, [Mazdali ibn Tilankan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazdali_ibn_Tilankan) did not push to Tlemcen right away as the city of [Oujda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oujda) was too strong to capture. Instead, Ibn Tashfin himself returned with an army in 1081 AD that captured Oujda and then conquered Tlemcen, massacring the [Maghrawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrawa_\(tribe\)) forces there and their leader; He pressed on and by 1082 AD he had captured "Jazaʾir Banī Mazghanna".

In 1151 AD, [Abd al-Mu'min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Mu'min) launched an expedition to the east, conquering [Béjaïa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9ja%C3%AFa) in August 1152, the capital of the Hammadids; on their way, Beni Mezghanna did not succumb and was now under the [Almohad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate)'s control. The caliphate suffered from states breaking out of its rule, most notably, the [Kingdom of Tlemcen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tlemcen) in 1235 AD. The town once again came under the dominion of the [Ziyanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyanid) [sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultans) of the Kingdom but experienced a large measure of independence under [Thaaliba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaaliba) [amirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amirs) who settled the [Mitidja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitidja) plain at around 1200 AD.

### Early modern history



Illustration of the [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet) off the coast of Algiers before Pier "Kheireddine" was built.

The [Kingdom of Tlemcen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tlemcen) was the target of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire)'s and the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire)'s campaigns and conquests against its coasts, beginning in 1501 AD. However, Algiers continued to be of comparatively little importance until after the [expulsion of the Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista#Conversions_and_expulsions) from Spain, many of whom sought asylum in the city, after which the Spanish led by [Pedro Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Navarro) established a fortified base and [garrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison) on one of the [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet) off the coast of Algiers, and named it "Peñón de Argel" or Peñón of Algiers, . By that time, Algiers had an [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir), Salim al-Thumi who had to "swear obedience and loyalty" to [Ferdinand II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon) who also imposed a levy intended to suppress the [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911655-13][^cite_note-Çelik1997-16]

### Ottoman rule



Algiers by [Antonio Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Salamanca), circa 1540, published in [Civitates Orbis Terrarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitates_Orbis_Terrarum)



[Abraham Duquesne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Duquesne) delivering Christian captives in Algiers after the bombing in 1683.

In 1516, the amir of Algiers, Selim b. Teumi, invited the corsair brothers [Oruç Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oru%C3%A7_Reis) and [Hayreddin Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Barbarossa) to expel the [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain). Oruç Reis came to Algiers, ordered the assassination of Selim, and seized the town and ousted the Spanish in the [Capture of Algiers (1516)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Algiers_\(1516\)). Hayreddin, succeeding Oruç after the latter was killed in battle against the Spaniards in the [1518 fall of Tlemcen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Tlemcen_\(1518\)), was the founder of the *[pashaluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashaluk)*, which subsequently became the *[beylik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey)*, of Algeria. Barbarossa lost Algiers in 1524 but regained it with the [1529 Capture of Peñón of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_of_Algiers_\(1529\)), and then formally invited the Sultan [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent) to accept sovereignty over the territory and to annex Algiers to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).



Historic map of Algiers by [Piri Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piri_Reis)

&#x20;Algiers from this time became the chief seat of the [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates). In October 1541 in the [Algiers expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_expedition_\(1541\)), the King of Spain and [Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) sought to capture the city, but a storm destroyed a great number of his ships, and his army of some 30,000, chiefly made up of Spaniards, was defeated by the Algerians under their [pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha), Hassan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911655-13]



Ornate [Ottoman cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_weapons) found in Algiers on 8 October 1581 by Ca'fer el-Mu'allim. Length: 385 cm, cal\:178 mm, weight: 2910 kg, stone projectile. Seized by France during the [invasion of Algiers in 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Algiers_in_1830). [Army Museum, Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Museum_\(Paris\)).

Formally part of the Ottoman Empire but essentially free from Ottoman control, starting in the 16th century Algiers turned to piracy and ransoming. Due to its location on the periphery of both the Ottoman and European economic spheres, and depending for its existence on a Mediterranean that was increasingly controlled by European shipping, backed by European navies, piracy became the primary economic activity. Repeated attempts were made by various nations to subdue the pirates that disturbed shipping in the western Mediterranean and engaged in slave raids as far north as Iceland.[^cite_note-17] By the 17th century, up to 40% of the city's 100,000 inhabitants were enslaved Europeans.[^cite_note-18] The United States fought two wars (the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Barbary_War) and [Second Barbary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Barbary_War)) over Algiers' attacks on shipping.

Among the notable people held for ransom was the future Spanish novelist, [Miguel de Cervantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_de_Cervantes), who was held captive in Algiers for almost five years, and wrote two plays set in Algiers of the period. The primary source for knowledge of Algiers of this period, since there are no contemporary local sources, is the *Topografía e historia general de Argel* (1612, but written earlier), published by Diego de Haedo, but whose authorship is disputed.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] This work describes in detail the city, the behavior of its inhabitants, and its military defenses, with the unsuccessful hope of facilitating an attack by Spain so as to end the piracy.

A significant number of renegades lived in Algiers at the time, Christians converted voluntarily to Islam, many fleeing the law or other problems at home. Once converted to Islam, they were safe in Algiers. Many occupied positions of authority, such as [Samson Rowlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samson_Rowlie), an Englishman who became Treasurer of Algiers.[^cite_note-21]



The [Casbah of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casbah_of_Algiers) under Ottoman rule in 1690.

The city under Ottoman control was enclosed by a wall on all sides, including along the seafront. In this wall, five gates allowed access to the city, with five roads from each gate dividing the city and meeting in front of the Ketchaoua Mosque. In 1556, a citadel, [Palace of the Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Dey) was constructed at the highest point in the wall. A major road running north to south divided the city in two: The upper city (al-Gabal, or 'the mountain') which consisted of about fifty small quarters of [Andalusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusians), [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Jews), [Moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) and [Kabyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabyle_people) communities, and the lower city (al-Wata, or 'the plains') which was the administrative, military and commercial centre of the city, mostly inhabited by [Ottoman Turkish dignitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouloughlis) and other upper-class families.[^cite_note-22]



The [bombardment of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Algiers_\(1816\)) under [Viscount Exmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Pellew,_1st_Viscount_Exmouth), August 1816, painted by [Thomas Luny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Luny)

On 27 August 1816, the [Bombardment of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Algiers_\(1816\)) took place city by a British squadron under [Lord Exmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Pellew,_1st_Viscount_Exmouth) (a descendant of Thomas Pellew, taken in an Algerian slave raid in 1715[^cite_note-23]), assisted by men-of-war from the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands), destroying the corsair fleet harboured in Algiers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911655-13]



Portrait of [Hussein Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Dey), the last [Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dey) of the [Deylik of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deylik_of_Algiers).

France and the [Regency of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_of_Algiers) had a commercial–political conflict called the [Bakri-Busnach affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakri-Busnach_affair) which has been bothering both nations in the 19th century. On 29 April 1827, foreign consuls and diplomatic agents gathered in the [Palace of the Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Dey) for a conference with the [Regency of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_of_Algiers) ruler [Hussein Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Dey). Tensions were high because of France's failure to pay outstanding debts. In a heated moment later referred to as "fly-whisk incident", the [Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dey) struck the French consul in the face with the handle of a [fly-whisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-whisk).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

In an attempt by [Charles X of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_X_of_France) to increase his popularity amongst the French, he sought to bolster [patriotic sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_sentiment), and turn eyes away from his domestic policies, by treating the incident as a public insult and demanded an apology. Failure to respond was met by operations against the dey.[^cite_note-EncBrit-26] A naval siege on the port of Algiers by the [French Navy](383256.md) began the following days which lasted 3 years and impacted the French and Algerian economies due to their former extensive trade treaties.



The keys of the city of Algiers, which were handed to the [French Army](611740.md) on 5 July 1830.



Bombardment of Algiers by sea on 3 July 1830

Tensions only continued rising while the [French Armed Forces](10724.md) were preparing for the [1830 invasion of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Algiers_in_1830). The naval fleet departed from [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon) on 25 May 1830, and successfully reached the western coast of the Regency near what is today [Sidi Fredj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Fredj) on 14 June 1830. The Algerian forces met their French opponents in the [Battle of Staouéli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Staou%C3%A9li) on 19 June 1830, to which the [Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dey)'s forces were defeated, this enabled the colonial army to advance into the city and made [Hussein Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Dey) surrender to French [General de Bourmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Auguste-Victor,_Count_de_Ghaisnes_de_Bourmont) on 5 July 1830.

### French rule

Under French rule, Algiers became the capital of [French Algeria](292341.md), "an integral part of the [French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Republic)" according to a formal annexation declared on 22 June 1834.[^cite_note-27] Following this, interest turned into the completion of the [French conquest of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_conquest_of_Algeria) that shared goals with its [pacification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacification_of_Algeria) efforts; Establishing a European [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Europe), economic and political presence in Africa. Plans to transform the face of the city to match French standards and architectural trends began shortly after obtaining the city. Originally, the Casbah extended to the sea, but it was pushed back to the hills above after demolishing the walls and lower half of the old city and erecting the current "Place des Martyrs",[^cite_note-harvsp_Driss_2002_p45-28] constructing [promenades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade) and boulevards that circle the city or face the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean), tracing new streets and building apartments that are characterized by their "Haussmanian" Style.[^cite_note-Almi70-29]

Settlers of [European descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_descent) marked a majority of the city's population,[^cite_note-30] some constituted a minority of "[Pieds-noirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieds-noirs)" who were granted [French citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_citizenship) and rights under the [Crémieux Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9mieux_Decree). On the other hand, [Code de l'indigénat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_de_l'indig%C3%A9nat) enforced inferiority of the "Arabs" and "Muslims" which were getting forcibly removed from their homes and were banned from entering various parts of "Alger" to segregate by race, religion and language.[^cite_note-AlloucheLaloum1987-31][^cite_note-32] Added to that, [mosques](19894.md) were repurposed to [churches](6325.md), [stables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stables), or demolished/closed permanently, examples of this are [Ketchoua Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchoua_Mosque) and [Ali Bitchin Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Bitchin_Mosque).[^cite_note-Almi36-33]

During the 1930s, the architect [Le Corbusier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier) drew up plans for a complete redesign of the colonial city. Le Corbusier was highly critical of the urban style of Algiers, describing the European district as "nothing but crumbling walls and devastated nature, the whole a sullied blot". He also criticised the difference in living standards he perceived between the European and African residents of the city, describing a situation in which "the 'civilised' live like rats in holes" whereas "the 'barbarians' live in solitude, in well-being".[^cite_note-36] However, these plans were ultimately ignored by the French administration.

During World War II, Algiers was the first city to be seized from the Axis by the Allies in [Operation Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Terminal), a part of [Operation Torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch).

### Algerian War

The "tense truce" between Algerian rebels, French army and the OAS in 1962

Algiers also played a pivotal role in the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War) (1954–1962), a bloody independence struggle in which hundreds of thousands (estimates range between 350,000 and 1,500,000) died (mostly Algerians but also French and [pieds-noirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieds-noirs)). In particular, it saw the [Battle of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War#Battle_of_Algiers) when the 10th Parachute Division of the French Army, starting on 7 January 1957, and on the orders of the French Minister of Justice [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand) (who authorized any means "to eliminate the insurrectionists"), led attacks against the Algerian fighters for independence. Algiers remains marked by this battle, which was characterized by merciless fighting between FLN forces which carried out a guerrilla campaign against the French military and police and pro-French Algerian soldiers, and the French Army which responded with a bloody repression, torture and blanket terrorism against the native population. The demonstrations of 13 May during the crisis of 1958 provoked the fall of the [Fourth Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fourth_Republic) in France, as well as the return of General [de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) to power.

### Independence

Algeria achieved [independence](62450.md) on 5 July 1962, with Algiers as its capital. Since then, despite losing its entire *[pied-noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied-noir)* population, the city has expanded massively. It now has about five million inhabitants, or 10 percent of Algeria's population—and its suburbs now cover most of the surrounding [Mitidja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Algeria) plain. Run by the FLN that had secured independence, Algiers became a member of [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) during the [Cold War](325329.md). In October 1988, one year before the fall of the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall), Algiers was the site of demonstrations demanding the end of the single-party system and the creation of a *real* [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) baptized the "Spring of Algiers". The demonstrators were repressed by the authorities (more than 300 dead), but the movement constituted a turning point in the political history of modern Algeria. The [1989 Algerian constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Algerian_constitutional_referendum) took place and a new constitution was adopted that put an end to the one-party rule and saw the creation of more than fifty political parties, as well as official freedom of the press. The [Kasbah of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasbah_of_Algiers) was enlisted as a cultural [world heritage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_heritage_site) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in December 1992.

### Crisis of the 1990s

The city became the theatre of many political demonstrations of all descriptions until 1993. In 1991, a political entity dominated by religious conservatives called the [Islamic Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Salvation_Front) engaged in a political test of wills with the authorities. In the 1992 elections for the Algerian National Assembly, the Islamists garnered a large amount of support in the first round. Fearing an eventual win by the Islamists, the army canceled the election process, setting off the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War) between the State and armed religious conservatives which would last for a decade.

On 11 December 2007, two car [bombs exploded in Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_December_2007_Algiers_bombings). One bomb targeted two [United Nations](31769.md) office buildings and the other targeted a government building housing the [Supreme Court of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Algeria). The death toll was at least 62, with over two hundred injured in the attacks.[^cite_note-37] However, only 26 remained hospitalized the following day.[^cite_note-38] As of 2008, it is speculated that the attack was carried out by the [Al Qaeda](1921.md) cell within the city.[^cite_note-39]

Indigenous terrorist groups have been actively [operating in Algeria since around 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_insurgency_in_Algeria_\(2002%E2%80%93present\)#2007).

## Geography

### Location



[Satellite image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_image) of Algiers

Algiers is located in the north-central part of [Algeria](358.md). Relative to the Bay of Algiers, the historical center was founded and extended on the "Algiers Sahel", coastal hills of the east, though the city grew exponentially on top of the hills eventually extending as far as towns such as [Aïn Bénian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFn_B%C3%A9nian,_Algiers) in the east, and [Bordj El Bahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordj_El_Bahri) in the west, and towards the [Mitidja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitidja) Plain in its northern, central, and western parts.

Algiers is crossed by several rivers and waterways that are indifferently called [Oued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oued). All the rivers that cross it flow into the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) making it specific to its environment. The Bouzaréah massif, known for its rugged relief, has a very dense hydrographic network, drained by eight main waterways (Baranès, Sidi Medjber, Frais vallon, jaubert, Scotto Nadal, Chemin du Fort, Birtraria and Oued Koriche or Oued Atoun). Half of its waterways have been artificialized and channeled by buried collectors. To the west, one finds the Mazafran and Beni Messous [Wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadis). To the east, the El Harrach (the main Oued dividing the city into an eastern [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and historical side, and a western [suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban) side), El Hamiz and Réghaïa Wadis.

Considerable [land reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation) along the waterfront allowed for developments such as "Marina Mall" in 2012, "Sablettes Promenade" in 2016, El kettani waterfront in 2025 and ongoing projects like the Algiers Medina, New bay of Algiers, Wadis new arrangements.

The city's downtown sits at an elevation of 2 m (at the [Algiers Central Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Central_Post_Office)), while it averages 45 m across the city's [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), and tops at 407 m on the [Bouzaréah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouzar%C3%A9ah) peak. [Algiers Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Province), the administrative area that includes the city's urban and suburban centers covers 1,190 km2 (460 sq mi).

### Climate

Algiers has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*). Its proximity to the Mediterranean aids in moderating the city's temperatures. As a result, Algiers usually does not see the extreme temperatures that are experienced in the adjacent interior. Algiers on average receives roughly 685.6 millimetres (27 in) of rain per year, the bulk of which is seen between October and April. The precipitation is higher than in most of coastal Mediterranean Spain, and similar to most of coastal Mediterranean France, as opposed to the interior North African semi-arid or arid climate.

The rainiest months are November and December, in a single month (November) it can rain more than [Riyadh](51554.md) annually. On average, the city sees 2.4 freezing nights a year.[^cite_note-40]

&#x20;\
&#x20;Snow is very rare; the last snowfall was in 2012, when the city received 100 millimetres (4 in) of snowfall, its first snowfall in eight years.[^cite_note-41] The hottest temperature ever recorded in Algiers was 48.7 °C (119.7 °F) on 21 July 2023,[^cite_note-42] and the coldest temperature ever recorded in Algiers was −3.3 °C (26.1 °F).

#### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimated that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#RCP_4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Algiers in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Perth](24355.md) in Australia. The annual temperature would increase by 2.6 °C (4.7 °F), and the temperature of the warmest month by 1.9 °C (3.4 °F), while the temperature of the coldest month would be 3.8 °C (6.8 °F) higher.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches Representative Concentration Pathway (RCP) 4.5.[^cite_note-49]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Algiers is one of 12 major African cities ([Abidjan](21371767.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), Algiers, [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Dakar](44251.md), [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), [Durban](313650.md), [Lagos](85232.md), [Lomé](57067.md), [Luanda](18402.md) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) which would be the most severely affected by the future [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damages of U\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion for the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damages, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to \$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP 4.5, \$206 billion for RCP 8.5 and \$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-AR6Ch9-50] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-51] The Casbah is on a list of 10 African [World Heritage Site](44940.md) most threatened by sea level rise.[^cite_note-AR6Ch9-50]

## Administration

### Administrative changes over time

#### During French rule

In 1830, the city of Algiers was a [Casbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casbah_of_Algiers), a fortified city built on the ruins of traders and old civilisations that inhabited the area, but after the French conquest its walls were demolished and the city was expanded into a european side which eventually unified both in 1832 into a municipality.

Following this in 1835 was the creation of 14 additional communes around Algiers,[^cite_note-52] the communes of [El Biar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Biar) and Mustapha (currently [Sidi M'Hamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_M'Hamed)) were attached to it before being detached in 1870. In 1904, the commune of Mustapha was definitively integrated into the city of Algiers which was divided into 12 districts for a total area of 15.64 km².

In 1959, Greater Algiers was created with the grouping of 9 municipalities (Algiers, Saint-Eugène, Bouzareah, El Biar, Dely Brahim, Birmendreis, Kouba, Hussein-Dey and Maison-Carrée). This area was divided into 10 districts and a territory of 186 km², it was governed by a general administrator appointed by decree and a municipal council of 75 members, each district being governed by a deputy mayor.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

#### After Independence



Divisions of the city of Algiers (in red) over time (1959/1967-1977-1985) within the Wilaya of Algiers (in white).

The city was maintained until 1967[^cite_note-55] when the role of a general administrator was removed. In 1974, two districts were added ([Bouzareah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouzareah) and [Bir Mourad Raïs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Mourad_Ra%C3%AFs)).[^cite_note-56] In 1977, the districts became fully-fledged municipalities, but the People's Council of the City of Algiers (CPVA) was created, bringing together the former districts to continue the prerogatives of the former municipality of Algiers. A new entity was added to the CPVA, namely [Baraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraki,_Algiers), bringing the total to 13 municipalities.[^cite_note-57]

Following the administrative division of 1984, the city was once again reorganized in 1985, increasing to 15 communes, but the area was divided by three, increasing to 58.5 km², by shedding the peripheral territories, to the east around [El Harrach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Harrach), to the west ([Bouzareah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouzareah)) and to the south ([Bir Mourad Raïs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Mourad_Ra%C3%AFs)). It continued to be managed jointly by the communes and the CPVA, but the latter is placed under the supervision of the [wilaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilaya_of_Algiers).[^cite_note-58]

Since the postponement of the municipal elections of 1989,[^cite_note-59] the CPVA ceased to exist. It was first replaced by a *Provisional Municipal Council of the Urban Agglomeration of Algiers* (CCPAUA),[^cite_note-60] but a few a months later, in April 1990, two new laws relating to the [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Municipal_Assembly) and the [wilaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilaya) were adopted,[^cite_note-61] and the *Urban Coordination Councils of the Wilaya of Algiers* (CUC) was created,[^cite_note-62] the former municipalities forming the city of Algiers having been grouped under the name "Intercommunal Council of Algiers". From that moment on, the administration of the wilaya definitively replaced that of the city. Thus, the technical departments and services linked to the CPVA were placed under the supervision of the Wilaya before becoming *Public Industrial and Commercial Establishment in Algeria* (EPIC).

In 1997, after expanding to include 24 new municipalities, the [Wilaya of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilaya_of_Algiers) was granted a special status and became the "Governorate of Greater Algiers" (GGA), headed by a minister-governor, in this case [Cherif Rahmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherif_Rahmani). It would be organized into 28 urban communes, called urban districts, and into 29 simple communes.[^cite_note-63] This new status did not last long, since in 2000, the GGA was dissolved, having been deemed unconstitutional.[^cite_note-64]

#### Immediate urban districts

**Sidi M'Hamed**, located on the eastern shores of the bay, it is the geographically smallest and most densely populated district. The district is known for its numerous architectural styles ranging from [moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_revival) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Revival_architecture), to [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture) and [moderne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderne_architecture) styles, emphasising the city's stance as a melting-pot for architectural experimentation. It contains the most well-known landmarks such as [Maqam Echahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maqam_Echahid), [Central Postal Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Poste_d'Alger), [Sacred Heart Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cath%C3%A9drale_du_Sacr%C3%A9-C%C5%93ur_d'Alger), the [Government Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Palace_\(Algiers\)), El Aurassi Hotel, [Aérohabitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rohabitat) residency, [the Cultural Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_and_Arts_\(Algeria\)), [Bardo Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardo_National_Museum_\(Algiers\)) and [University of Algiers 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Algiers_1). Sidi M'Hamed has always been the hub for expression, and chants for change; after all it has witnessed the [Battle of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Algiers_\(1956%E2%80%931957\)), the [mass riots of 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_October_Riots), mass protests from 1990/1991 leading up into the [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War), a result of a political and societal crisis, [Hirak Echaabi](60179677.md) in 2019.

The district is serviced by various modes of transport, 3 [commuter rail](6286.md) stations, the Algiers Central Train Station "Algiers Station" in the "Alger-Centre" municipality as well as the "Agha Station" in the "Sidi M'Hamed" (Previously "Mustapha") municipality and the "Ateliers Station" in the "Belouizdad" (Previously "Belcourt") municipality, 5 [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Metro) stations, 2 [Gondola lift](298681.md) lines are planned, Algiers' Maritime Terminal, and the Port of Algiers (among the biggest [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports) of the country). [Algerian Television (EPTV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Establishment_of_Television), the Algerian Radio, the [Bank of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Algeria), *Algiers Financial City* (AFC) (construction halted) are based in Sidi M'Hamed.

The historic center of the city, [Casbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casbah_of_Algiers) is a primary representation of its vibes, from Square Port-Saïd and Martyrs' Square that lead to Algiers' biggest markets and museums, which reflect the region's rich history and culture, as well as old religious diversity of Judaism, Islam and [Catholic Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Christianity), to Landmarks that include the Roman ruins near the [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_metro) [Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_des_Martyrs_Metro_Station) and [Rais' Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_des_Rais), [Dar Hassan Pacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_Hassan_Pacha), [Palace of the Dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Dey), and "Climat de France", a housing project developed by the French; the [Great Synagogue of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Synagogue_of_Algiers), [Basilica of Our Lady of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Africa), and [Ketchaoua Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchaoua_Mosque); which affirms the architectural diversity of Algiers. ## Local architecture

Botanical Garden Hamma

There are many public buildings of interest, including the whole [Kasbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasbah) quarter, Martyrs Square (*Sahat ech-Chouhada* ساحة الشهداء), the government offices (formerly the British consulate), the "[Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamaa_el_Kebir)", "[New](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamaa_el_Djedid)", and [Ketchaoua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchaoua_Mosque) Mosques, the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) cathedral of [Notre Dame d'Afrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_d'Afrique), the [Bardo Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardo_National_Museum_of_Prehistory_and_Ethnography), the old *Bibliothèque Nationale d'Alger*—a [Moorish palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_architecture) built in 1799–1800 and the new [National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Algeria), built in a style reminiscent of the [British Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Library).

The main building in the Kasbah was begun in 1516 on the site of an older building, and served as the palace of the deys until the French conquest. A road has been cut through the centre of the building, the mosque turned into [barracks](1033489.md), and the hall of audience allowed to fall into ruin. There still remains a [minaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minaret) and some marble arches and columns. Traces exist of the vaults in which were stored the treasures of the dey.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]

[Djamaa el Kebir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamaa_el_Kebir) (*Jamaa-el-Kebir* الجامع الكبير) is the oldest mosque in Algiers. It was first built by [Yusuf ibn Tashfin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_ibn_Tashfin), but reconstructed many times. The pulpit (*[minbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbar)* منبر) bears an inscription showing that the building existed in 1097. The minaret was built by the sultan of [Tlemcen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlemcen), in 1324.[^cite_note-66] The interior of the mosque is square and is divided into aisles by columns joined by [Moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) arches.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]

The [New Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamaa_el_Djedid) (*Jamaa-el-Jedid* الجامع الجديد), dating from the 17th century, is in the form of a [Greek cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cross), surmounted by a large white cupola, with four small cupolas at the corners. The minaret is 27 metres (89 ft) high. The interior resembles that of the Grand Mosque.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]

The church of the Holy Trinity (built in 1870) stands at the southern end of the *rue d'Isly* near the site of the demolished Fort Bab Azoun باب عزون. The interior is richly decorated with various coloured marbles. Many of these marbles contain memorial inscriptions relating to the British residents (voluntary and involuntary) of Algiers from the time of John Tipton, the first English consul, in 1580 (NB Some sources give 1585). One tablet records that in 1631 two Algerine pirate crews landed in Ireland, sacked [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_County_Cork), and enslaved its inhabitants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]



The Ketchaoua Mosque



&#x20;Mosque in the City of Algiers.

The [Ketchaoua Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchaoua_Mosque) (*Djamaa Ketchaoua* جامع كتشاوة), at the foot of the Casbah, was before independence in 1962 the cathedral of St Philippe, itself made in 1845 from a mosque dating from 1612. The principal entrance, reached by a flight of 23 steps, is ornamented with a [portico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico) supported by four black-veined marble columns. The roof of the nave is of [Moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_Empire) [plaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaster) work. It rests on a series of arcades supported by white marble columns. Several of these columns belonged to the original mosque. In one of the chapels was a tomb containing the bones of [Geronimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geronimo_\(martyr\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65] The building seems a curious blend of Moorish and [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) styles.

Algiers possesses a college with schools of law, medicine, science and letters. The college buildings are large and handsome. The [Bardo Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardo_National_Museum_\(Algiers\)) holds some of the ancient sculptures and mosaics discovered in Algeria, together with medals and Algerian money.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]

The port of Algiers is sheltered from all winds. There are two harbours, both artificial—the old or northern harbour and the southern or Agha harbour. The northern harbour covers an area of 95 hectares (235 acres). An opening in the south [jetty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetty) affords an entrance into Agha harbour, constructed in Agha Bay. Agha harbour has also an independent entrance on its southern side. The inner harbour was begun in 1518 by [Khair-ad-Din Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khair-ad-Din_Barbarossa) (see History, below), who, to accommodate his pirate vessels, caused the island on which was Fort Penon to be connected with the mainland by a [mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_\(architecture\)). The lighthouse which occupies the site of [Fort Penon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Penon?action=edit\&redlink=1) was built in 1544.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]

Algiers was a walled city from the time of the deys until the close of the 19th century. The French, after their occupation of the city (1830), built a [rampart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall), [parapet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapet) and [ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditch_\(fortification\)), with two terminal forts, [Bab Azoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_Azoun) باب عزون to the south and [Bab-el-Oued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab-el-Oued) اد to the north. The forts and part of the ramparts were demolished at the beginning of the 20th century, when a line of forts occupying the heights of [Bouzaréah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouzar%C3%A9ah) بوزريعة (at an elevation of 396 metres (1,299 ft) above the sea) took their place.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911654-65]

[Notre Dame d'Afrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_d'Afrique), a church built (1858–1872) in a mixture of the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) styles, is conspicuously situated overlooking the sea, on the shoulder of the [Bouzaréah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouzar%C3%A9ah) hills, 3 km (2 mi) to the north of the city. Above the altar is a statue of the [Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary,_the_mother_of_Jesus) depicted as a black woman. The church also contains a solid silver statue of the [archangel Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archangel_Michael), belonging to the confraternity of [Neapolitan](55880.md) fishermen.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911655-13]

[Villa Abd-el-Tif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Abd-el-Tif), former residence of the [dey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dey), was used during the French period, to accommodate French artists, chiefly painters, and winners of the [Abd-el-Tif prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd-el-Tif_prize), among whom [Maurice Boitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Boitel), for a while of two years. Nowadays, Algerian artists are back in the villa's studios.

## Economy



Ministry of Finance of Algeria

Algiers is an important economic, commercial and financial center, with a stock exchange capitalized at 60 million euros. Algiers contributes to 20% of Algeria's GDP (51 Billions \$ in 2024.) The city has the highest cost of living of any city in North Africa, as well as the 50th highest worldwide, as of March 2007, having gained one position compared to the previous year.[^cite_note-69]

Mohamed Ben Ali El Abbar, president of the Council of Administration of the Emirate Group EMAAR, presented five "megaprojects" to Algerian President [Abdelaziz Bouteflika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelaziz_Bouteflika), during a ceremony which took place Saturday, 15 July, in the [People's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Palace_\(Algiers\)) of Algiers. These projects will transform the city of Algiers and its surroundings by equipping them with a retail area and restoration and leisure facilities.

The first project will concentrate on the reorganization and the development of the infrastructures of the railway station "Aga" located in the downtown area. The ultramodern station intended to accommodate more than 80.000 passengers per day, will become a centre of circulation in the heart of the grid system, surrounded by commercial offices and buildings and hotels intended for travellers in transit. A shopping centre and three high-rise office buildings rising with the top of the commercial zone will accompany the project.

The second project will not relate to the bay of Algiers and aims to revitalize the sea front. The development of the 44 km (27 mi) sea front will include marinas, channels, luxury hotels, offices, apartments of great standing, luxury stores and leisure amenities. A crescent-shaped peninsula will be set up on the open sea. The project of the bay of Algiers will also comprise six small islands, of which four of round form, connected to each other by bridges and marinas and will include tourist and residential complexes.



[Air Algérie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Alg%C3%A9rie) head office in Place [Audin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Audin) near the [University of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Algiers), in Alger-Centre

The third project will relate to restructuring an area of Algiers, qualified by the originators of the project of "city of wellness". El Abbar indicated to the journalists that the complex would be "agreeable for all those which will want to combine tourism and well-being or tourism and relaxation". The complex will include a university, a research center and a medical centre. It should also include a hospital complex, a care centre, a hotel zone, an urban centre and a thermal spa with villas and apartments. The university will include a medical school and a school for care male nurses which will be able to accommodate 500 students. The university campus will have the possibility of seeing setting up broad ranges of buildings of research laboratories and residences.

Another project relates to technological implantation of a campus in Sidi Abdellah, 25 km (16 mi) south-east from Algiers. This 90 hectares (222 acres) site will include shopping centres, residential zones with high standard apartments and a golf course surrounded by villas and hotels. Two other residential zones, including 1.800 apartments and 40 high standard villas, will be built on the surrounding hills.

The fifth project is that of the tourist complex Colonel Abbès, which will be located 25 km (16 mi) west from Algiers. This complex will include several retail zones, meeting places, and residential zones composed of apartments and villas with views of the sea.[^cite_note-70]

There is another project under construction, by the name of Algiers Medina. The first step of the project is nearly complete.

A [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) office for French-speaking countries in Africa is in Algiers.[^cite_note-71]

Algiers metro

#### Railways

Opened on 1 November 2011, [Algiers Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Metro) is the city's rapid transit system (Metro) that is currently servicing 5 districts of the city (Bab El Oued, Sidi M'Hamed, Hussein Dey, El Harrach, Bir Mourad Raïs) by Line 1 providing 19 stations with a total length of 18.5 km. It has played a vital role in easing traffic congestions and transit demand and connectivity in urbansised Algiers from downtown to neighbourhoods in the western and southern areas of the city.
