**Milan**[^cite_note-7] is the capital and largest city of the region of [Lombardy](43807.md), in [northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy), and the seat of the [Metropolitan City of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan). It is the [second-largest city in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Italy) after the country’s capital, [Rome](25458.md), with a population of 1,362,863 in 2026.[^cite_note-population-2] The city's [wider metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_metropolitan_area) is the largest in Italy, and the [fourth-largest in the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Europe), with an estimated population of 6.55 million.[^cite_note-CityPopulation.de-3] Milan is considered Italy's economic capital,[^cite_note-8] and its metropolitan area accounts for about 20% of [the country's GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Italy).[^cite_note-prologis-9]

Founded around 590 BC[^cite_note-cronologia-10] by a [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) tribe, Milan [was conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_expansion_in_Italy) by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) in 222 BC, who [Latinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinisation_of_names) the name of the city into [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum).[^cite_note-cronologia-10][^cite_note-Tellier-11] From AD 284 to 402, it served as capital of the [western part of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-12] In the Late Medieval period, the wealthy [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan) was one of the greatest forces behind the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).[^cite_note-\:0-13][^cite_note-14] As a major center of the [Italian Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Enlightenment) during the [Early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period), Milan's cultural and political struggle against [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) domination was crucial in the reunification of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy). From the 19th century onwards, Milan led the industrial and financial development of Italy.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

Milan is a major international center of industry, finance,[^cite_note-17] science,[^cite_note-18] communications, fashion, art and tourism. Milan was classified as an "Alpha" city by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-19] Milan's business district hosts [Borsa Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borsa_Italiana), Italy's main stock exchange (part of the [Euronext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronext) consortium, the world's [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_major_stock_exchanges) by market capitalization) and the headquarters of numerous national corporations, including eight [*Fortune* 500 Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_Italian_companies) companies.[^cite_note-20] As of 2023, Milan and its [special metropolitan authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan) have the largest GDP and the highest per-capita GDP of any Italian province.[^cite_note-21]

Milan is a global fashion capital[^cite_note-Breward-22] and a major international tourist destination, being one of the most visited cities in the world, ranked second in Italy after Rome, fifth in Europe and sixteenth in the world.[^cite_note-ilgiorno-23][^cite_note-newsroom-24] The city is a major cultural center, with museums and art galleries that feature some of the most important collections in the world, including major works by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Milan hosts numerous educational institutions, including academies and universities that account for 11% of the national total of enrolled students.[^cite_note-University_and_research_in_Milan-27][^cite_note-28] Milan hosts several international events and fairs, including [Milan Fashion Week](18486693.md) and the [Milan Furniture Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Furniture_Fair), which are among the world's largest in terms of revenue, visitors and growth.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Milan has many luxury hotels and is the fifth most starred in the world by [Michelin Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Guide).[^cite_note-32] It hosted the [Universal Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair) in [1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_International) and [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2015). In the field of sports, Milan is home to two of Europe's most successful football teams, [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) and [Internazionale Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internazionale_Milano) (Inter Milan), and one of Europe's main basketball teams, [Olimpia Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimpia_Milano). The city also co-hosted the [Winter Olympic](38416091.md) and [Paralympic](41045653.md) games in 2026.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## Etymology



A bas-relief on the [Palazzo della Ragione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Ragione,_Milan) of the *[scrofa semilanuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrofa_semilanuta)* ("half-woolly sow") from which, according to a theory, the city's toponym may derive

Milan was founded with the Celtic name of Medhelanon,[^cite_note-Tellier-11][^cite_note-cronologia-10] later [Latinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinisation_of_names) by the [ancient Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans) into [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum). In [Celtic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_language) *medhe-* meant "middle, center" and the name element *-lanon* is the Celtic equivalent of Latin *-planum* "plain", meant "(settlement) in the midst of the plain",\[^cite_note-Delamarre_2003\_[https://archive.org/details/dictionnairedela00dela_348/page/n219_221–222-36](https://archive.org/details/dictionnairedela00dela_348/page/n219_221%E2%80%93222-36)][^cite_note-Quintela-37] or of "place between watercourses" (Celtic *medhe* = "in the middle, central"; *land* or *lan* = "land"), given the presence of the [Olona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olona), [Lambro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambro), [Seveso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seveso_\(river\)) rivers and the Nirone and Pudiga streams.[^cite_note-CraccoRuggini17-38]

The [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) name *Mediolanum* comes from the Latin words *medio* (in the middle) and *planus* (plain).[^cite_note-39] Some scholars believe that *lanum* comes from the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) root *lan*, meaning an enclosure or demarcated territory (source of the [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) word *[llan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llan_\(placename\))*, meaning "a sanctuary or church", ultimately cognate to English/German *Land*) in which Celtic communities used to build shrines.[^cite_note-40]

Hence *Mediolanum* could signify the central town or sanctuary of a Celtic tribe. About sixty Gallo-Roman sites in France bore the name "Mediolanum", for example: [Saintes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saintes,_Charente-Maritime) (*[Mediolanum Santonum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum_Santonum)*) and [Évreux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vreux) (*[Mediolanum Aulercorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum_Aulercorum)*).[^cite_note-41] Another theory links the name to the *[scrofa semilanuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrofa_semilanuta)* ("half-woolly sow") an ancient emblem of the city, fancifully accounted for in [Andrea Alciato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Alciato)'s *Emblemata* (1584), beneath a woodcut of the first raising of the city walls, where a boar is seen lifted from the excavation, and the etymology of *Mediolanum* given as "half-wool",[^cite_note-42] explained in Latin and in French.

According to this theory, the foundation of Milan is credited to two [Celtic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts), the [Bituriges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituriges_Cubi) and the [Aedui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aedui), having as their [emblems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem) a ram and a boar.[^cite_note-43] Therefore "The [city's symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbols_of_Milan) is a wool-bearing boar, an animal of double form, here with sharp bristles, there with sleek wool."[^cite_note-44] Alciato credits [Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose) for his account.[^cite_note-45]

## History

Main article: [History of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Milan)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Milan).

### Celtic era



[Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) artifacts from the period before the Roman conquest (3rd–2nd centuries BC), preserved in the [Civic Archaeological Museum of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum,_Milan)

Around 590 BC[^cite_note-cronologia-10] a [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) tribe belonging to the [Insubres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubre) group and belonging to the [Golasecca culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golasecca_culture) settled the city under the name Medhelanon.[^cite_note-Tellier-11][^cite_note-cronologia-10] According to the legend reported by [Livy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livy) (writing between 27 and 9 BC), the [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul) king [Ambicatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambicatus) sent his nephew [Bellovesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellovesus) into northern Italy at the head of a party drawn from various Gaulish tribes; Bellovesus allegedly founded the settlement in the times of the Roman monarchy, during the reign of [Tarquinius Priscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Tarquinius_Priscus). Tarquin is traditionally recorded as reigning from 616 to 579 BC, according to ancient Roman historian Titus Livy.[^cite_note-46]

Medhelanon, in particular, was developed around a [sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary), which was the oldest area of the village.[^cite_note-Tosi-47] The sanctuary, which consisted of a wooded area in the shape of an ellipse with a central clearing, was aligned according to precise astronomical points. For this reason, it was used for religious gatherings, especially in particular celebratory moments. The sanctuary of Medhelanon was an ellipse with axes of 443 m (1,453 ft) and 323 m (1,060 ft) located near [Piazza della Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_della_Scala).[^cite_note-Tosi-47]

The urban planning profile was based on these early paths, and on the shape of the sanctuary, reached, in some cases, up to the 19th century and even beyond. For example, the route of the modern Corso Vittorio Emanuele, [Piazza del Duomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Duomo,_Milan), [Piazza Cordusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Cordusio) and Via Broletto, which is curvilinear, could correspond to the south side of the ellipse of the ancient sanctuary of Medhelanon.[^cite_note-Tosi-47]

One axis of the Medhelanon sanctuary was aligned towards the [heliacal rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliacal_rising) of [Antares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antares), while the other towards the heliacal rising of [Capella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capella). The latter coincided with a Celtic spring festival celebrated on 24 March, while the heliacal rising of Antares corresponded with 11 November, which opened and closed the Celtic year and which coincided with the point where the Sun rose on the winter [solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solstice).[^cite_note-Tosi-47] About two centuries after the creation of the Celtic sanctuary, the first residential settlements began to be built around it. Medhelanon then transformed from a simple religious center to an urban and then military centre, thus becoming a real village.[^cite_note-Tosi-47]

The first homes were built just south of the Celtic sanctuary, near the modern [Royal Palace of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Milan).[^cite_note-Tosi-47] Subsequently, with the growth of the town centre, other important buildings for the Medhelanon community were built. First, a temple dedicated to the goddess [Belisama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisama) was built, which was located near the modern [Milan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Cathedral). Then, near the modern Via Moneta, which is located near today's [Piazza San Sepolcro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_San_Sepolcro), a fortified building with military functions was built which was surrounded by a defensive moat.[^cite_note-Tosi-47]

### Roman times

Main article: [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum)



Roman ruins in Milan: the [Columns of San Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonne_di_San_Lorenzo)

During the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic), the Romans, led by consul [Gnaeus Cornelius Scipio Calvus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnaeus_Cornelius_Scipio_Calvus), fought the [Insubres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubres) and captured the settlement in 222 BC. The chief of the Insubres then submitted to Rome, giving the Romans control of the settlement.[^cite_note-48] The Romans eventually [conquered the entirety of the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_expansion_in_Italy), calling the new [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) "[Cisalpine Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Gaul)" ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Gallia Cisalpina*)—"Gaul this side of the Alps"—and may have given the city its Latinized name of Mediolanum: in [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulish) *\*medio-* meant "middle, centre" and the name element *-lanon* is the Celtic equivalent of Latin *-planum* "plain", thus *\*Mediolanon* (Latinized as *Mediolānum*) meant "(settlement) in the midst of the plain".[^cite_note-Quintela-37]\[^cite_note-Delamarre_2003\_[https://archive.org/details/dictionnairedela00dela_348/page/n219_221–222-36](https://archive.org/details/dictionnairedela00dela_348/page/n219_221%E2%80%93222-36)] Mediolanum became the most important center of Cisalpine Gaul and, in the wake of economic development, in 49 BC, was elevated, within the *[Lex Roscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Roscia)*, to the status of *[municipium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipium)*.[^cite_note-49]



Ruins of the Emperor's palace in Milan located in Via Gorani, where [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great) and [Licinius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licinius) issued the [Edict of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Milan)



Remains of the [Roman walls of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Milan#Roman_walls) located inside the [Civic Archaeological Museum of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum,_Milan)

The ancient Celtic settlement was, from a [topographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) point of view, superimposed and replaced by the Roman one. The Roman city was then gradually superimposed and replaced by the medieval one. The urban center of Milan has therefore grown constantly and rapidly, until modern times, around the first Celtic nucleus. The original Celtic toponym Medhelanon then changed, as evidenced by a graffiti in Celtic language present on a section of the Roman walls of Milan which dates back to a period following the Roman conquest of the Celtic village, in Mesiolano.[^cite_note-romanoimpero-50] In 286, the Roman Emperor [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian) moved the capital of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) from Rome to Mediolanum.[^cite_note-51] Diocletian himself chose to reside at [Nicomedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomedia) in the Eastern Empire, leaving his colleague [Maximian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximian) at Milan.

During the Augustan age Mediolanum was famous for its schools; it possessed a theatre and an [amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_amphitheatre) (129.5 x 109.3 m), the third largest in [Roman Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Italy) after the [Colosseum](49603.md) in Rome and the vast amphitheatre in [Capua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capua).[^cite_note-52] A large stone wall encircled the city in Caesar's time, and later was expanded in the late third century AD, by Maximian. Maximian built several gigantic monuments including the large [circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_circus) (470 × 85 metres) and the *[thermae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermae)* or [Baths of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baths_of_Hercules), a large complex of imperial palaces and other services and buildings of which few visible traces remain. Maximian increased the city area to 375 acres by surrounding it with a new, larger stone wall (about 4.5 km long) with many 24-sided towers. The monumental area had twin towers; the one included later in the construction of the convent of [San Maurizio Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Maurizio_al_Monastero_Maggiore) remains 16.6 m high.

It was from Mediolanum that the [Emperor Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great) issued what is now known as the [Edict of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Milan) in AD 313, granting tolerance to all religions within the Empire, thus paving the way for Christianity to become the dominant religion of the Empire. Constantine was in Mediolanum to celebrate the wedding of his sister to the Eastern Emperor, [Licinius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licinius). In 402, the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) besieged the city and the [Emperor Honorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorius_\(emperor\)) moved the Imperial residence to [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna).[^cite_note-53] In 452, [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila) besieged the city, but the real break with the city's Imperial past came in 539, during the [Gothic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_\(535%E2%80%93554\)), when [Uraias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraias) (a nephew of [Witiges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitiges), formerly King of the [Italian Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom)) carried out a [siege of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Milan_\(538%E2%80%93539\)), and after capitulation, according to [Procopius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procopius), 300,000 male citizens were executed and the women sold to the allies of the Goths for assisting in the siege.[^cite_note-54] The [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) took [Ticinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticinum) as their capital in 572 (renaming it *Papia* – the modern [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia)), and left [early-medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages) Milan to the governance of its [archbishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan).

### Middle Ages

Main article: [Lordship of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordship_of_Milan)



The 12th century [Medieval Porta Ticinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Ticinese_\(Medieval_Gate_of_Milan\)) is one of the city's three medieval gates that still exist in modern Milan.



[Piazza Mercanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Mercanti) was the heart of the city in the Middle Ages.

After the siege of the city by the Visigoths in 402, the imperial residence moved to Ravenna. Attila, King of the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns), [sacked and devastated the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Milan_\(452\)) in 452 AD. In 539 the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) conquered and destroyed Milan during the Gothic War against [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) Emperor [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I). In the summer of 569 the Lombards (from whom the name of the Italian region [Lombardy](43807.md) derives), conquered Milan, overpowering the small [Byzantine garrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_army) left for its defence. Some Roman structures remained in use in Milan under Lombard rule.[^cite_note-55] Milan surrendered to [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) and the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) in 774.

The 11th century saw a reaction against the control of the [Holy Roman Emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor). City-states emerged in northern Italy, an expression of the new political power of the cities and their will to fight against all feudal powers. Milan was no exception. It did not take long, however, for the Italian city-states to begin fighting each other to try to limit neighbouring powers.[^cite_note-56] The Milanese destroyed Lodi and continuously warred with Pavia, Cremona and Como, who in turn asked [Frederick I Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I_Barbarossa) for help. In a sally they captured [Empress Beatrice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_I,_Countess_of_Burgundy) and forced her to [ride a donkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parading_on_donkey) backward through the city until getting out. Frederick I Barbarossa brought the destruction of much of Milan in 1162.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]



The [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan) (1395–1447 and 1450–1796) at the time of [Gian Galeazzo Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Galeazzo_Visconti)'s death in 1402. The [Golden Ambrosian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Ambrosian_Republic) (1447–1450) briefly existed between the end of the [Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_of_Milan) period in 1447 and the start of the [Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Sforza) period in 1450.

A period of peace followed and Milan prospered as a centre of trade due to its geographical position. During this time, the city was considered one of the largest European cities.[^cite_note-\:0-13] As a result of the independence that the Lombard cities gained in the [Peace of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance) in 1183, Milan returned to the commune form of local government first established in the 11th century.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

In 1395, [Gian Galeazzo Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Galeazzo_Visconti) became the first [Duke of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Milan) upon receiving the title from [Wenceslaus, King of the Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslaus,_King_of_the_Romans). In 1447, [Filippo Maria Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Maria_Visconti), Duke of Milan, died without a male heir. Following the end of the Visconti line, the [Ambrosian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Ambrosian_Republic) was established; it took its name from St. Ambrose, the popular patron saint of the city.[^cite_note-lucas\:268-61] Both the Guelph and the Ghibelline factions worked together to bring about the Ambrosian Republic in Milan. In 1450, the Republic collapsed when Milan was conquered by [Francesco I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_I_Sforza) of the [House of Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Sforza), which made Milan one of the leading cities of the Italian [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).[^cite_note-lucas\:268-61][^cite_note-InternationalStudent-62] Under the House of Sforza, Milan experienced a period of great prosperity, which in particular saw the development of mulberry cultivation and silk processing.[^cite_note-63]

Following this economic growth, works such as the [Sforza Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sforza_Castle) (already existing in the Visconti era under the name of Porta Giovia Castle, but re-adapted, enlarged and completed by the Sforza family) and the [Ospedale Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policlinico_of_Milan) were completed. The Sforzas also managed to attract to Milan personalities such as [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci), who redesigned and improved the function of the [navigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigli) and painted *[The Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_\(Leonardo\))*, and [Bramante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramante), who worked on the [church of Santa Maria presso San Satiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_presso_San_Satiro), on the [basilica of Sant'Ambrogio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant'Ambrogio) and to the [church of Santa Maria delle Grazie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_delle_Grazie,_Milan), influencing the development of the [Lombard Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_in_Lombardy).

### Early modern

Main article: [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan)



A 1680 map of Milan, with the [Spanish walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Milan#Spanish_walls) encircling the city



Milan was the capital of the [Napoleonic Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)), highlighted in yellow



Historic *[palazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo)* on [Corso Venezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corso_Venezia)

Milan's last independent ruler, [Lodovico il Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodovico_il_Moro), requested the aid of [Charles VIII of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VIII_of_France) against the other [Italian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_states), eventually unleashing the [Italian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars). The king's cousin, [Louis of Orléans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XII), took part in the expedition and realized most of Italy was virtually defenseless. This prompted him to come back a few years later in 1500, and claim the Duchy of Milan for himself, his grandmother having been a member of the ruling [Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_of_Milan) family. At that time, Milan was also defended by [Swiss mercenaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_mercenaries). After the victory of Louis's successor François I over the Swiss at the [Battle of Marignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marignan), the duchy was promised to the French king [François I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_I_of_France). When the Emperor [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) defeated Francis I at the [Battle of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pavia) in 1525, French rule in the Duchy of Milan came to an end. In 1535, the Sforza line went extinct.[^cite_note-64]

In 1556, Charles V abdicated in favour of his son [Philip II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) and his brother [Ferdinand I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). Charles's Italian possessions, including Milan, passed to Philip II and the line of the Spanish Habsburgs, while Ferdinand's Austrian line of Habsburgs ruled the Holy Roman Empire. The [Great Plague of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_plague_of_1629%E2%80%931631) in 1629–31, that claimed the lives of an estimated 60,000 people out of a population of 130,000, caused unprecedented devastation in the city and was effectively described by [Alessandro Manzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni) in his masterpiece *[The Betrothed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Betrothed)*. This episode was seen by many as the symbol of Spanish bad rule and decadence and is considered one of the last outbreaks of the centuries-long [pandemic](24255.md) of plague that began with the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death).[^cite_note-65]

In 1700, the Spanish line of Habsburgs was extinguished with the death of [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_Spain). After his death, the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) began in 1701. In 1706, the French were defeated in [Ramillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ramillies) and [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Turin) and were forced to yield northern Italy to the [Austrian Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy). In 1713 and 1714, the Treaties of [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht) and [Rastatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rastatt) formally confirmed Austrian sovereignty over most of Habsburg Spain's Italian possessions including Lombardy and its capital, Milan.

[Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) invaded Italy in 1796, and Milan was declared capital of the [Cisalpine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic). Later, he declared Milan capital of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)) and was crowned King of Italy in the cathedral. After Napoleon's occupation ended, the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) returned Lombardy and Milan to Austrian control in 1815.[^cite_note-66]

### Late modern and contemporary



A popular print depicting the "[Five Days of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Days_of_Milan)" (18–22 March 1848) uprising against Austrian rule

On 18 March 1848, Milan effectively rebelled against Austrian rule, during the so-called "[Five Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Days_of_Milan)" ([Italian](14708.md): *Le Cinque Giornate*), that forced Field Marshal [Radetzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Radetzky_von_Radetz) to temporarily withdraw from the city. The bordering [Kingdom of Piedmont–Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_\(1720%E2%80%931861\)) sent troops to protect the insurgents and organised a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) that ratified by a huge majority the unification of Lombardy with Piedmont–Sardinia. But just a few months later the Austrians were able to send fresh forces that routed the Piedmontese army at the [Battle of Custoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Custoza_\(1848\)) on 24 July and to reassert Austrian control over northern Italy. About ten years later, however, Italian nationalist politicians, officers and intellectuals such as [Cavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Benso,_Count_of_Cavour), [Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi) and [Mazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mazzini) were able to gather a huge consensus and to pressure the monarchy to forge an alliance with the new [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire) of [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) to defeat Austria and establish a large Italian state in the region. At the [Battle of Solferino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Solferino) in 1859, French and Italian troops heavily defeated the Austrians that retreated under the [Quadrilateral line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilatero).[^cite_note-Solferino-67] Following this battle, Milan and the rest of Lombardy were incorporated into Piedmont-Sardinia, which then proceeded to annex all the other Italian statelets and proclaim the birth of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) on 17 March 1861.



[Piazza Cordusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Cordusio) in Milan

The political [unification of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy) enhanced Milan's economic dominance over northern Italy. A dense rail network, whose construction had started under Austrian patronage, was completed in a brief time, making Milan the rail hub of northern Italy and, with the opening of the [Gotthard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Rail_Tunnel) (1882) and [Simplon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_Tunnel) (1906) railway tunnels, the major South European rail hub for goods and passenger transport. Indeed, Milan and Venice were among the main stops of the [Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express) that started operating from 1919.[^cite_note-68] Abundant hydroelectric resources allowed the development of a strong steel and textile sector and, as Milanese banks dominated Italy's financial sphere, the city became the country's leading financial centre. In May 1898, Milan was shaken by the [Bava Beccaris massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bava_Beccaris_massacre), a riot related to soaring cost of living.[^cite_note-69]



The [Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Vittorio_Emanuele_II) destroyed by [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) bombings, 1943

Milan's northern location in Italy closer to Europe, secured also a leading role for the city on the political scene. It was in Milan that [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) built his political and journalistic careers, and his fascist [Blackshirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackshirts) rallied for the first time in the city's Piazza San Sepolcro; here the future [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) dictator launched his [March on Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_on_Rome) on 28 October 1922. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) Milan's large industrial and transport facilities [suffered extensive damage from Allied bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Milan_in_World_War_II) that often also hit residential districts.[^cite_note-70] When Italy [surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_between_Italy_and_Allied_armed_forces) in 1943, German forces occupied and plundered most of northern Italy, fueling the birth of a massive resistance guerrilla movement.[^cite_note-Cooke-71] On 29 April 1945, the American [1st Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Armored_Division_\(United_States\)) was advancing on Milan but, before it arrived, the Italian resistance seized control of the city and [executed Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Benito_Mussolini) along with his mistress and several regime officers, that were later hanged and exposed in [Piazzale Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazzale_Loreto), where one year before some resistance members had been executed.



Corso Vittorio Emanuele II, one of the main shopping streets in Milan, with the [Duomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Cathedral) at left and [La Rinascente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rinascente) department store at right

During the post-war economic boom, the reconstruction effort and the [Italian economic miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_economic_miracle) attracted a large wave of internal migration (especially from rural areas of [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy)) to Milan. The population grew from 1.3 million in 1951 to 1.7 million in 1967.[^cite_note-Ginsborg-72] During this period, Milan was rapidly rebuilt, with the construction of several innovative and modernist skyscrapers, such as the [Torre Velasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Velasca) and the [Pirelli Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli_Tower), that soon became the symbols of this new era of prosperity.[^cite_note-Foot-73] The economic prosperity was, however, overshadowed in the late 1960s and early 1970s during the so-called [Years of lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_\(Italy\)), when Milan witnessed an unprecedented wave of street violence, [labour strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_strike) and [political terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_terrorism). The apex of this period of turmoil occurred on 12 December 1969, when [a bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Fontana_bombing) exploded at the National Agrarian Bank in Piazza Fontana, killing 17 people and injuring 88.

In the 1980s, with the international success of Milanese houses (like [Armani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armani), [Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prada), [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace), [Moschino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moschino) and [Dolce & Gabbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_&_Gabbana)), Milan became one of the world's fashion capitals. The city saw also a marked rise in [international tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_tourism), notably from America and Japan, while the stock exchange increased its market capitalisation more than five-fold.[^cite_note-74] This period led the mass media to nickname the metropolis *"Milano da bere"*, literally "Milan to be drunk".[^cite_note-75]

In the 1990s, Milan was badly affected by [Tangentopoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangentopoli), a political scandal in which many politicians and businessmen were tried for corruption. The city was also affected by a severe financial crisis and a steady decline in textiles, automobile and steel production.[^cite_note-Foot-73] Berlusconi's Milano 2 and Milano 3 projects were the most important housing projects of the 1980s and 1990s in Milan and brought to the city new economical and social energy.



[Via Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Dante,_Milan) in Milan, located between [Piazza Cordusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Cordusio) and the [Sforza Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sforza_Castle)

In the early 21st, century Milan underwent a series of sweeping redevelopments over huge former industrial areas.[^cite_note-76] Two new business districts, [Porta Nuova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Nuova_\(Milan\)) and [CityLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityLife_\(Milan\)), were built in the space of a decade, radically changing the skyline of the city. Its exhibition centre moved to a much larger site in [Rho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho,_Lombardy).[^cite_note-77] The long decline in traditional manufacturing has been overshadowed by a great expansion of publishing, finance, banking, fashion design, information technology, logistics and tourism.[^cite_note-78] The city's decades-long population decline seems to have partially reverted in recent years, as the *comune* gained about 100,000 new residents since the last census. The successful re-branding of the city as a global capital of innovation has been instrumental in its successful bids for hosting large international events such as [2015 Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Expo) and [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md).

## Geography



The northward [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) since the mid-20th century is visible in this satellite image of Milan



[Navigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigli), a system of interconnected canals in and around Milan, dating back to the Middle Ages

Milan is located in the north-western section of the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), approximately halfway between the river [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)) to the south and the foothills of the [Alps](981.md) with the great lakes ([Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como), [Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore), and [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano)) to the north, the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) river to the west and the [Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_\(river\)) to the east. The city's land is flat and the [elevation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevation) is relatively low; its highest point is 122 m (400 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).

The administrative *comune*, or city proper, covers an area of about 181 square kilometres (70 sq mi), with a population, in 2025, of 1,366,155 and a population density of 7,520 inhabitants per square kilometre (19,500/sq mi). Milan's continuous urban area extends beyond the city limits. The administrative [Metropolitan City of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan), a special-status [provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Italy) authority, covers 1,575 km<sup>2</sup> (608 sq mi) and in 2024 had a population estimated at 3,245,459, with a resulting density of 2,067/km<sup>2</sup> (5,350/sq mi),[^cite_note-Annuario_2025._I_dati_dell’area_metropolitana_di_Milano-79] while its wider metropolitan had an estimated population in excess of 6.1 million as of 2025.[^cite_note-CityPopulation.de-3]

The concentric layout of the city centre reflects the *Navigli*, an ancient system of navigable and interconnected canals, now mostly covered.[^cite_note-80] The suburbs of the city have expanded mainly to the north, swallowing up many *comuni* along the roads towards Varese, Como, Lecco, and Bergamo.[^cite_note-81] In the 21st century, the Navigli region of Milan is a highly active area with a large number of residential units, bars, and restaurants; it is also a well-known centre for artists.[^cite_note-82]

### Climate



The monthly precipitation in Milan from 1940 to 2023. Data from open-meteo.com

Milan features a mid-latitude, four-season [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*), according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). Milan's climate is similar to much of Northern Italy's inland plains, with hot, humid summers and cold, foggy winters. The Alps and [Apennine Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_Mountains) form a natural barrier that protects the city from the major circulations coming from northern Europe and the sea.[^cite_note-83]

During winter daily average temperatures can fall below freezing (0 °C \[32 °F]) and accumulations of snow can occur: the historic average of Milan's area is 25 centimetres (10 in) in the period between 1961 and 1990, with a record of 90 centimetres (35 in) in January 1985. In the suburbs the average can reach 36 centimetres (14 in).[^cite_note-84] The city receives on average seven days of snow per year.[^cite_note-85]

The city was often shrouded in thick cloud or fog during winter, although the removal of rice paddies from the southern neighbourhoods and the [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) effect have greatly reduced this occurrence since the turn of the 21st century. Occasionally, the [Foehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foehn) winds cause the temperatures to rise unexpectedly: on 22 January 2012 the daily high reached 16 °C (61 °F) while on 22 February 2012 it reached 21 °C (70 °F).[^cite_note-86] Air pollution levels rise significantly in wintertime when [temperature inversions form and persist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_inversion), causing Milan to be one of Europe's most polluted cities.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

Summers in Milan are hot and humidity levels are high with peak temperatures reaching above 35 °C (95 °F). Due to the high humidity, urban heat effect and lack of wind, nighttimes often remain muggy during the summer months.[^cite_note-89] Usually the summer enjoys clearer skies with an average of more than 13 hours of daylight:[^cite_note-WeatherSpark-90] when precipitation occurs though, it is more likely to be accompanied by thunderstorms and [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail).[^cite_note-WeatherSpark-90]

Springs and autumns are generally pleasant, with temperatures ranging between 10 and 20 °C (50 and 68 °F); these seasons are characterized by higher rainfall, especially in April and May.[^cite_note-91] Relative humidity typically ranges between 45% (comfortable) and 95% (very humid) throughout the year, rarely dropping below 27% (dry) and reaching as high as 100%.[^cite_note-WeatherSpark-90]

Wind is generally absent: over the course of the year typical wind speeds vary from 0 to 14 km/h (0 to 9 mph) (calm to gentle breeze), rarely exceeding 29 km/h (18 mph) (fresh breeze), except during summer thunderstorms when winds can blow strong. In the spring, gale-force windstorms may happen, generated either by [Tramontane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramontane) blowing from the Alps or by [Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_\(wind\))-like winds from the north. Due to its geographic location surrounded by mountains on 3 sides, Milan is among the least windy cities in Europe.[^cite_note-WeatherSpark-90]

As of 2026, the population is 1,362,863, of which 48.7% are male, and 51.3% are female. Minors make up 14.2% of the population, and seniors make up 22.7%.[^cite_note-population-2]

The population of Milan today is lower than its historical peak. With rapid industrialization in post-war years, the population of Milan peaked at 1,743,427 in 1973.[^cite_note-99] Thereafter, during the following decades, about one third of the population moved to the outer belt of suburbs and new satellite settlements that grew around the city proper.

Today, Milan's conurbation extends well beyond the borders of the city proper and of its special-status provincial authority: its contiguous built-up urban area was home to 5.27 million people in 2015,[^cite_note-Demographia-100] while its wider [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_metropolitan_area), the largest in Italy and fourth largest in the EU, is estimated to have a population in excess of 6.1 million as of 2025.[^cite_note-CityPopulation.de-3]

After World War II, Milan experienced two main waves of immigration: the first, dating from the 1950s to the early 1970s, saw a large influx of migrants from poorer and rural areas within Italy; the second, starting from the late 1980s, has been characterized by the preponderance of foreign-born immigrants.[^cite_note-102]

The early period coincided with the so-called Italian economic miracle of postwar years, an era of extraordinary growth based on rapid industrial expansion and great public works, that brought to the city a large influx of over 400,000 people, mainly from rural and underdeveloped Southern Italy.[^cite_note-Foot-73]

Decades of continuing high immigration have made the city one of the most cosmopolitan and multicultural in Italy. Immigrants came mainly from Africa (in particular [Eritreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritreans_in_Italy), [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptians_in_Italy), [Moroccans](26282995.md), [Senegalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese_people_in_Italy) and [Nigerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_people_in_Italy)), and the former socialist countries of Eastern Europe (notably [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Italy), [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians_in_Italy), [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Italy), [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_diaspora), [Moldovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovans), and [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_italy?action=edit\&redlink=1)), in addition to a growing number of Asians (in particular [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_Italy), [Sri Lankans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankans_in_Italy) and [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Italians)) and Latin Americans (Mainly South Americans). At the beginning of the 1990s, Milan already had a population of foreign-born residents of approximately 58,000 (or 4% of the then population), that rose rapidly to over 117,000 by the end of the decade (about 9% of the total).[^cite_note-103]

Milan is home to the second-largest [East Asian](19605700.md) community in Europe after [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_community_in_Paris), with the Philippines and China, making up about a quarter of its foreign population (around 76,000 out of 301,000 in 2023). Another 4,000 foreigners come from other East Asian countries; notably, Milan hosts more than 2,000 Japanese nationals and 1,000 Koreans, excluding those who also hold Italian citizenship.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-\:1-105] Foreigners holding East-Asian citizenship thus make up around 5.36% of the city's population. Milan notably hosts the oldest and largest (along with [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato)) [Chinese community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Milan) in Italy, with around 37,000 people in 2023, excluding Italians of Chinese descent such as immigrants who have acquired Italian citizenship or their descendants. Situated in the [8th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Milan), and centered on [Via Paolo Sarpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Paolo_Sarpi), an important commercial avenue, the Milanese Chinatown was originally established in the 1920s by immigrants from [Wencheng County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wencheng_County), in the [Zhejiang](96602.md), and used to operate small textile and leather workshops.[^cite_note-106] Milan also hosts a Japanese International school as well as various Chinese schools throughout the city.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

The city also hosts an historical African community originating from the [Horn of Africa](62142.md). As of 2023, there were around 4,000 Eritrean, Ethiopian or Somali-born people living in Milan, the overwhelming majority being double-citizens of Italy, and not counting second and third generation migrants. The three countries were all Italian colonies at a time, from 1869 ([Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea))[^cite_note-EBAb-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] to 1943 ([East African campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_\(World_War_II\))). Due to the historical links with Italy, a small community originating from the Horn of Africa has established its presence near Porta Venezia district starting from the 1970s.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] It is estimated that in the "Asmarina" area (Little Asmara) there are around 2,000-2,500 people from the Horn of Africa still living there, along with multiple restaurants, institutes as well as an Ethiopian Church.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

Another notable area with a large presence of foreign residents coming from a specific country is the so-called "quadrilatero di San Siro" or "San Siro casbah" in reference to the large Arab-speaking population living in the area.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] The neighbourhood, consisting of around 6,000 municipal flats, is characterised by the fact of having an estimated 25% share of Arab-speaking inhabitants, mostly hailing from Egypt.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] The area has often been described as a *[banlieue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banlieue)* within Milan and has historically had a higher crime rate than the rest of the city. Nevertheless, in recent years many projects have been presented so as to mitigate the marginalisation of its inhabitants. Other areas hosting large Arabic-speaking populations include Maciachini-Imbonati, Corvetto, Comasina and piazza Arcole.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

Milan has a substantial English-speaking community (around 4,500 US citizens, British, Irish and Australian expatriates, excluding double-citizens), and several English schools and English-language publications, such as *Hello Milano*, *Where Milano* and *[Easy Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Milano)*.[^cite_note-\:1-105]

### Religion



[Santa Maria delle Grazie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_delle_Grazie,_Milan) was completed in 1497



[St. Ambrose Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_Sant'Ambrogio) dates back to AD 379–386.

Milan's population, like that of Italy as a whole, is mostly Catholic.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-PEW2018-RESEARCH-133] It is the seat of the Archdiocese of Milan. Greater Milan is also home to [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant), [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md), Jewish, [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Sikh](27964.md) and [Buddhist](3267529.md) communities.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

Milan has been a Christian-majority city since the late Roman Empire.[^cite_note-139] Its religious history was marked by the figure of St. Ambrose, whose heritage includes the [Ambrosian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite) (Italian: *Rito ambrosiano*), used by some five million Catholics in the greater part of the [Archdiocese of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milan),[^cite_note-140] which consider the largest in Europe.[^cite_note-141] The Rite varies slightly from the canonical [Roman Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rite) [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_liturgy), with differences in the mass, liturgical year ([Lent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent) starts four days later than in the Roman Rite), baptism, rite of funerals, priest clothes and sacred music (use of the [Ambrosian chant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrosian_chant) rather than Gregorian).[^cite_note-142]

In addition, the city is home to the largest Orthodox community in Italy. Lombardy is the seat of at least 78 Orthodox parishes and monasteries, the vast majority of them located in the area of Milan.[^cite_note-143] The main [Romanian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Orthodox) church in Milan is the Catholic church of Our Lady of Victory (Chiesa di Santa Maria della Vittoria), currently granted for use to the local Romanian community.[^cite_note-144] Similarly, the point of reference for the followers of the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) is the Catholic church of [San Vito in Pasquirolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vito_in_Pasquirolo,_Milan).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

The Jewish community of Milan is the second largest in Italy after Rome, with about 10,000 members, mainly [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi).[^cite_note-147] The main city synagogue, Hechal David u-Mordechai Temple, was built by architect [Luca Beltrami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Beltrami) in 1892 and is also the community's main headquarters, is located in Via della Guastalla. The interior was renovated in 1997.

Milan hosts also one of the largest Muslim communities in Italy,[^cite_note-148] and the city saw the construction of the country's first new [mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_Segrate) featuring a dome and minaret, since the destruction of the ancient mosques of [Lucera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucera) in the year 1300. In 2014 the City Council agreed on the construction of a new mosque amid bitter political debate, since it is strenuously opposed by right-wing parties such as the [Northern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_League_\(Italy\)).[^cite_note-149] As of 2018, the Muslim population is estimated at 9% of the city's population.[^cite_note-150]

Currently, accurate statistics on the Hindu and Sikh presence in Milan metro area are not available; however, various sources estimate that about 40% of the total Indian population living in Italy, or about 50,000 individuals, reside in Lombardy,[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] where a number of Hindu and Sikh temples exist and where they form the largest such communities in Europe after the ones in Britain.[^cite_note-153]

## Administration

### Municipal government

See also: [Mayor of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Milan), [City Council of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Council_of_Milan), [Elections in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Milan), and [Boroughs of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_Milan)



[Palazzo Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Marino), Milan City Hall



The city's nine *municipi* ("boroughs")

The legislative body of the Italian *comuni* is the [City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Council_of_Milan) (*Consiglio Comunale*), which in cities with more than one million population is composed by 48 councillors elected every five years with a proportional system, at the same time of the mayoral elections. The executive body is the City Committee (*Giunta Comunale*), composed by 12 [assessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assessor_\(Italy\)), that is nominated and presided over by a directly elected [Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Milan). The current mayor of Milan is [Giuseppe Sala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Sala_\(politician\)), an independent leading a centre-left alliance led by the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)).

The municipality of Milan is subdivided into nine administrative Borough Councils (*Consigli di Municipio*), down from the former twenty districts before the 1999 administrative reform.[^cite_note-154] Each Borough Council is governed by a Council (*Consiglio*) and a President, elected contextually to the city Mayor. The urban organisation is governed by the Italian Constitution (art. 114), the Municipal Statute[^cite_note-155] and several laws, notably the Legislative Decree 267/2000 or Unified Text on Local Administration (*Testo Unico degli Enti Locali*).[^cite_note-156] After the 2016 administrative reform, the Borough Councils have the power to advise the Mayor with nonbinding opinions on a large spectrum of topics and are responsible for running most local services, such as schools, social services, waste collection, roads, parks, libraries and local commerce; in addition they are supplied with an autonomous funding to finance local activities.

### Metropolitan city



[Palazzo Isimbardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Isimbardi) is the seat of the Council of the [Metropolitan City of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan).

Milan is the capital of the eponymous [Metropolitan city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan). According to the last governmental dispositions concerning administrative reorganisation, the urban area of Milan is one of the 15 [Metropolitan municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_cities_of_Italy) (*città metropolitane*), new administrative bodies fully operative since 1 January 2015.[^cite_note-157] The new Metro municipalities, giving large urban areas the administrative powers of a province, are conceived for improving the performance of local administrations and to slash local spending by better co-ordinating the municipalities in providing basic services (including transport, school and social programs) and environment protection.[^cite_note-Ferri-158] In this policy framework, the Mayor of Milan is designated to exercise the functions of Metropolitan mayor (*Sindaco metropolitano*), presiding over a Metropolitan Council formed by 24 mayors of municipalities within the Metro municipality. The Metropolitan City of Milan is headed by the Metropolitan Mayor (*Sindaco metropolitano*) and by the Metropolitan Council (*Consiglio metropolitano*). Since 21 June 2016, Giuseppe Sala, as mayor of the capital city, has been the mayor of the Metropolitan City.

### Regional government

Milan is also the capital of Lombardy, one of the twenty [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of Italy. Lombardy is by far the most populated region of Italy, with more than ten million inhabitants, almost one sixth of the national total. It is governed by a Regional Council, composed of 80 members elected for a five-year term. On 26 March 2018, a list of candidates of the [centre-right coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_coalition_\(Italy\)), a coalition of centrist and right-wing parties, led by Attilio Fontana, largely won the regional election, defeating a coalition of socialists, liberals and ecologists and a third-party candidate from the populist [Five Stars Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Stars_Movement). The conservatives have governed the region almost uninterruptedly since 1970. The regional council has 48 members from the centre-right coalition, 18 from the [centre-left coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_coalition_\(Italy\)) and 13 from the Five Star Movement. The seat of the regional government is [Palazzo Lombardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Lombardia) that, standing at 161.3 metres (529 feet),[^cite_note-159] is the fifth-tallest building in Milan.

The [architectural and artistic presence in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_architecture_and_art_in_Milan) represents one of the attractions of the Lombard capital. Milan has been among the most important Italian centers in the [history of architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_architecture), has made important contributions to the development of art history, and has been the cradle of a number of [modern art](163543.md) movements.

There are only few remains of the ancient Roman city, notably the well-preserved [Colonne di San Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonne_di_San_Lorenzo). During the second half of the 4th century, Saint Ambrose, as bishop of Milan, had a strong influence on the layout of the city, reshaping the centre (although the cathedral and baptistery built in Roman times are now lost) and building the great basilicas at the city gates: Sant'Ambrogio, [San Nazaro in Brolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Nazaro_in_Brolo), [San Simpliciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Simpliciano) and [Sant'Eustorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant'Eustorgio), which still stand, refurbished over the centuries, as some of the finest and most important churches in Milan. [Milan's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duomo_di_Milano), built between 1386 and 1877, is the largest church in Italy—the larger [St. Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica) is in the [State of Vatican City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Vatican_City), a sovereign state—and the [third largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_churches),[^cite_note-ReferenceA-160] as well as the most important example of [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) in Italy. The gilt bronze statue of the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonnina_\(statue\)), placed in 1774 on the highest pinnacle of the Duomo, soon became one of the most enduring symbols of Milan.[^cite_note-162]

In the 15th century, when the Sforza ruled the city, an old Viscontean fortress was enlarged and embellished to become the Castello Sforzesco, the seat of an elegant Renaissance court surrounded by a walled hunting park. Notable architects involved in the project included the [Florentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) [Filarete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filarete), who was commissioned to build the high central entrance tower, and the military specialist Bartolomeo Gadio.[^cite_note-163] The alliance between Francesco Sforza and Florence's [Cosimo de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosimo_de'_Medici) bore to Milan Tuscan models of Renaissance architecture, apparent in the [Ospedale Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ospedale_Maggiore) and Bramante's work in the city, which includes Santa Maria presso San Satiro (a reconstruction of a small 9th-century church), the tribune of [Santa Maria delle Grazie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_delle_Grazie_\(Milan\)) and three cloisters for Sant'Ambrogio.[^cite_note-murray-164] The [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation) in the 16th to 17th centuries was also the period of [Spanish domination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) and was marked by two powerful figures: [Saint Charles Borromeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Borromeo) and his cousin, [Cardinal Federico Borromeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Borromeo). Not only did they impose themselves as moral guides to the people of Milan, but they also gave a great impulse to culture, with the creation of the [Biblioteca Ambrosiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Ambrosiana), in a building designed by [Francesco Maria Richini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Richini), and the nearby Pinacoteca Ambrosiana. Many notable churches and Baroque mansions were built in the city during this period by the architects [Pellegrino Tibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pellegrino_Tibaldi), [Galeazzo Alessi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeazzo_Alessi) and Richini himself. [^cite_note-ReferenceC-165] Empress [Maria Theresa of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa_of_Austria) was responsible for the significant renovations carried out in Milan during the 18th century.[^cite_note-166] This urban and artistic renewal included the establishment of [Teatro alla Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Scala), inaugurated in 1778, and the renovation of the Royal Palace. The late 1700s [Palazzo Belgioioso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Belgioioso) by [Giuseppe Piermarini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Piermarini) and [Royal Villa of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Villa_of_Milan) by [Leopoldo Pollack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_Pollack), later the official residence of Austrian viceroys, are often regarded among the best examples of [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) in Lombardy.[^cite_note-167] From 1805 to 1814, during the [Napoleonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) rule of the city, Milan was established as the capital of a satellite Kingdom of Italy and steps were taken to reshape it in accordince with its newly acquired status. This included construction of large boulevards, new squares ([Porta Ticinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Ticinese) by [Luigi Cagnola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Cagnola) and Foro Bonaparte by [Giovanni Antonio Antolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Antolini)), and cultural institutions ([Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinacoteca_di_Brera) and the [Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brera_Academy)).[^cite_note-168] The massive [Arch of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Peace), situated at the bottom of Corso Sempione, is often compared to the [Arc de Triomphe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_de_Triomphe) in Paris. In the second half of the 19th century, Milan quickly became the main industrial centre of the new Italian nation, drawing inspiration from the great European capitals that were hubs of the [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution). The great [Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Vittorio_Emanuele_II), realised by [Giuseppe Mengoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mengoni) between 1865 and 1877 to celebrate [Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_II_of_Italy), is a covered passage with a glass and cast iron roof, inspired by the [Burlington Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Arcade) in London. Several other arcades such as the [Galleria del Corso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_del_Corso), built between 1923 and 1931, complement it. Another late-19th-century eclectic monument in the city is the [Cimitero Monumentale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimitero_Monumentale) graveyard, built in a [Neo-Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival_architecture) style between 1863 and 1866.

The tumultuous period of early 20th century brought several, radical innovations in Milanese architecture. [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau), also known as *[Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_style)* in Italy, is recognisable in [Palazzo Castiglioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Castiglioni_\(Milan\)), built by architect [Giuseppe Sommaruga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Sommaruga) between 1901 and 1903.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-165] Other examples include Hotel Corso,[^cite_note-ReferenceC-165] [Casa Guazzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Guazzoni) with its wrought iron and staircase, and Berri-Meregalli house, the latter built in a traditional Milanese Art Nouveau style combined with elements of neo-Romanesque and Gothic revival architecture, regarded as one of the last such types of architecture in the city.[^cite_note-169] A new, more eclectic form of architecture can be seen in buildings such as Castello Cova, built the 1910s in a distinctly neo-medieval style, evoking the architectural trends of the past.[^cite_note-170] An important example of [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco), which blended such styles with [Fascist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_architecture), is the huge [Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Centrale) inaugurated in 1931.[^cite_note-171]

The post-World War II period saw rapid reconstruction and fast economic growth, accompanied by a nearly two-fold increase in population. In the 1950s and 1960s, a strong demand for new residential and commercial areas drove to extreme urban expansion, that has produced some of the major milestones in the city's architectural history, including [Gio Ponti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gi%C3%B2_Ponti)'s Pirelli Tower (1956–60), Velasca Tower (1956–58), and the creation of brand new residential satellite towns, as well as huge amounts of low-quality public housings. In recent years, de-industrialization, [urban decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_decay) and gentrification led to a vast urban renewal of former industrial areas, that have been transformed into modern residential and financial districts, notably Porta Nuova in downtown Milan and [FieraMilano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FieraMilano) in the suburb of [Rho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho_\(Italy\)). In addition, the old exhibition area is being completely reshaped according to the Citylife regeneration project, featuring residencial areas, museums, an urban park and three skyscrapers designed by international architects, and after whom they are named: the 202-metre (663-foot) [Isozaki Arata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isozaki_Arata) (when completed, the tallest building in Italy),[^cite_note-Isozaki_Tower_Citylife-172] the twisted [Hadid Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaha_Hadid),[^cite_note-Hadid_Tower_Citylife-173] and the curved [Libeskind Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Libeskind).[^cite_note-Liebskind_Tower_Citylife-174]

Two business districts dominate Milan's skyline: *Porta Nuova* in the north-east (boroughs No. 9 and 2) and *CityLife* (borough No. 8) in the north-west part of the commune. The [tallest buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Milan) include the [Unicredit Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicredit_Tower) at 231 m (though only 162 m without the spire), and the 209 m [Allianz Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Tower), a 50-story tower.

### Parks and gardens



[Sempione Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Sempione)



[Montanelli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giardini_Pubblici_Indro_Montanelli)

The largest parks in the central area of Milan are [Sempione Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Sempione), at the north-western edge, and [Montanelli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giardini_Pubblici_Indro_Montanelli), situated north-east of the city. English-style Sempione Park, built in 1890, contains the [Civic Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Civica), the [Civic Aquarium of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Aquarium_of_Milan) (which is the third oldest aquarium in [Europe](9239.md)[^cite_note-175]), a steel lattice panoramic tower, an art exhibition centre, a Japanese garden and a public library.[^cite_note-176] The Montanelli gardens, created in the 18th century, hosts the [Natural History Museum of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Civico_di_Storia_Naturale_di_Milano) and a [planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetario_di_Milano).[^cite_note-177] Slightly away from the city centre, heading east, Forlanini Park is characterised by a large pond and a few preserved shacks which remind of the area's agricultural past.[^cite_note-178] In recent years Milan's authorities pledged to develop its green areas: they planned to create twenty new urban parks and extend the already existing ones, and announced plans to plant three million trees by 2030.

Also notable is [Monte Stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Stella_\(Milan\)) ("Starmount"), also informally called Montagnetta di San Siro ("Little mountain of San Siro"), an artificial hill and surrounding city park in Milan. The hill was created using the debris from the buildings that were bombed during [World War II](32927.md), as well as from the last remnants of the [Spanish walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Milan) of the city, demolished in the mid 20th century. Even at only 25 m (82 ft) height, the hill provides a panoramic view of the city and hinterland, and in a clear day, the Alps and Apennines can be distinguished from atop. A notable area of the park is called "Giardino dei Giusti" (Garden of the Just), which is a memorial to distinguished opponents of genocide and crimes against humanity; each tree in the garden is dedicated to one such person. Notable people who have been dedicated a tree in the Giardino dei Giusti include [Moshe Bejski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Bejski), [Andrej Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrej_Sakharov), [Svetlana Broz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetlana_Broz), and [Pietro Kuciukian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Kuciukian).

The [Orto Botanico di Brera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orto_Botanico_di_Brera) a [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) located behind [Palazzo Brera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Brera) at Via Brera 28 in the center of Milan, is another major park in the city. The garden consists primarily of rectangular flower-beds, trimmed in brick, with elliptical ponds from the 18th century, and specula and [greenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse) from the 19th century (now used by the Academy of Fine Arts). It contains one of the oldest *[Ginkgo biloba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginkgo_biloba)* trees in Europe, as well as mature specimens of *[Firmiana platanifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmiana_platanifolia)*, *[Juglans nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juglans_nigra)*, *[Pterocarya fraxinifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterocarya_fraxinifolia)*, and *[Tilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilia)*.

In addition, even though Milan is located in one of the most urbanised regions of Italy, it is surrounded by a belt of green areas and features numerous gardens even in its very centre. The farmlands and woodlands north (Parco Nord Milano since 1975) and south ([Parco Agricolo Sud Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Agricolo_Sud_Milano) since 1990) of the urban area have been protected as regional parks.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180] West of the city, the Parco delle Cave (Sand pit park) has been established on a neglected site where gravel and sand used to be extracted, featuring artificial lakes and woods.[^cite_note-181]

### Museums and art galleries

Main article: [List of museums in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Milan)



[Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s *[The Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_\(Leonardo_da_Vinci\))*, together with the church of [Santa Maria delle Grazie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_delle_Grazie_\(Milan\)), is a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).



[Pinacoteca di Brera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinacoteca_di_Brera) is the city's largest art gallery.



The [Royal Palace of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Milan) is a major venue for international art exhibitions.



The [Biblioteca Ambrosiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Ambrosiana) and [Pinacoteca Ambrosiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Ambrosiana) are among the city's most important libraries and art galleries, with a large collection of historic books, manuscripts, sketches and paintings from the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).

Milan is home to many cultural institutions, museums and art galleries, that account for about a tenth of the national total of visitors and receipts.[^cite_note-182] The [Pinacoteca di Brera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinacoteca_di_Brera) is one of Milan's most important art galleries. It contains one of the foremost collections of Italian painting, including masterpieces such as the *[Brera Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brera_Madonna)* by [Piero della Francesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_della_Francesca). The [Castello Sforzesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castello_Sforzesco) hosts numerous art collections and exhibitions, especially statues, ancient arms and furnitures, as well as the [Pinacoteca del Castello Sforzesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sforza_Castle_Pinacoteca), with an art collection including [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo)'s last sculpture, the *[Rondanini Pietà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondanini_Piet%C3%A0)*, [Andrea Mantegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Mantegna)'s *[Trivulzio Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivulzio_Madonna)* and Leonardo da Vinci's *[Codex Trivulzianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Trivulzianus)* manuscript. The Castello complex also includes [The Museum of Ancient Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_d'Arte_Antica), The Furniture Museum, The [Museum of Musical Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Musical_Instruments_\(Milan\)) and the [Applied Arts Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Arts_Collection_\(Milan\)), [The Egyptian and Prehistoric sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum_\(Milan\)) of the [Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_\(Milan\)) and the Achille Bertarelli Print Collection (Civica Raccolta delle Stampe Bertarelli).

Milan's figurative art flourished in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), and with the Visconti family being major patrons of the arts, the city became an important centre of [Gothic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art) and architecture (Milan Cathedral being the city's most formidable work of Gothic architecture). Leonardo worked in Milan from 1482 until 1499. He was commissioned to paint the *[Virgin of the Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_of_the_Rocks)* for the Confraternity of the Immaculate Conception and *[The Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_\(Leonardo\))* for the monastery of [Santa Maria delle Grazie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_delle_Grazie_\(Milan\)).[^cite_note-Kemp-183]

The city was [affected by the Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_in_Milan) in the 17th and 18th centuries, and hosted numerous formidable artists, architects and painters of that period, such as [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio) and [Francesco Hayez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Hayez), which several important works are hosted in Brera Academy. The [Museum of Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_Risorgimento_\(Milan\)) is specialised on the history of [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification) Its collections include iconic paintings like [Baldassare Verazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldassare_Verazzi)'s *Episode from the Five Days* and Francesco Hayez's 1840 *Portrait of Emperor [Ferdinand I of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Austria)*. The [Triennale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triennale) is a design museum and events venue located in Palazzo dell'Arte, in [Sempione Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Sempione). It hosts exhibitions and events highlighting contemporary Italian design, urban planning, architecture, music and media arts, emphasising the relationship between art and industry.

Milan in the 20th century was the epicentre of the [futurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism) artistic movement. [Filippo Marinetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Tommaso_Marinetti), the founder of Italian Futurism wrote in his 1909 "*[Manifesto of Futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto_of_Futurism)*" (in Italian, *Manifesto Futuristico*), that Milan was "*grande...tradizionale e futurista*" ("*grand...traditional and futuristic*", in English). [Umberto Boccioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_Boccioni) was also an important Futurism artist who worked in the city. Today, Milan remains a major international hub of modern and contemporary art, with numerous modern art galleries. The [Modern Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Art_Gallery_\(Milan\)), situated in the Royal Villa, hosts collections of Italian and European painting from the 18th to the early 20th centuries.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186] The [Museo del Novecento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Museum_of_Twentieth_Century_\(Museo_del_Novecento\)), situated in the [Palazzo dell'Arengario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_dell'Arengario), is one of the most important art galleries in Italy about 20th-century art; of particular relevance are the sections dedicated to Futurism, [Spatialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatialism) and [Arte povera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte_povera). In the early 1990s architect [David Chipperfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chipperfield) was invited to convert the premises of the former Ansaldo Factory into a Museum. Museo delle Culture (MUDEC) opened in April 2015.[^cite_note-187] The [Gallerie di Piazza Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallerie_di_Piazza_Scala), a modern and contemporary museum located in Piazza della Scala in the Palazzo Brentani and the Palazzo Anguissola, hosts 195 artworks from the collections of [Fondazione Cariplo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fondazione_Cariplo) with a strong representation of nineteenth-century Lombard painters and sculptors, including [Antonio Canova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Canova) and Umberto Boccioni. A new section was opened in the Palazzo della Banca Commerciale Italiana in 2012. Other private ventures dedicated to contemporary art include the exhibiting spaces of the [Prada Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prada_Foundation) and [HangarBicocca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HangarBicocca). The [Nicola Trussardi Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Trussardi_Foundation) is renewed for organising temporary exhibition in venues around the city. Milan is also home to many public art projects, with a variety of works that range from sculptures to murals to pieces by internationally renowned artists, including [Arman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arman), [Kengiro Azuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengiro_Azuma), [Francesco Barzaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Barzaghi), [Alberto Burri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Burri), [Pietro Cascella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Cascella), [Maurizio Cattelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Cattelan), Leonardo da Vinci, [Giorgio de Chirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_de_Chirico), [Kris Ruhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Ruhs), [Emilio Isgrò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Isgr%C3%B2), [Fausto Melotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fausto_Melotti), [Joan Miró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mir%C3%B3), Carlo Mo, [Claes Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claes_Oldenburg), [Igor Mitoraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Mitoraj), Gianfranco Pardi, [Michelangelo Pistoletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo_Pistoletto), [Arnaldo Pomodoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_Pomodoro), Carlo Ramous, [Aldo Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Rossi), [Aligi Sassu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligi_Sassu), [Giuseppe Spagnulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Spagnulo) and [Domenico Trentacoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Trentacoste).

### Music

See also: [Music of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Milan)



Inaugurated in 1778, [La Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Scala) is among the world's most famous opera houses.

Milan is considered an international capital of the performing arts. The city hosts [La Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Scala) opera house, widely considered one of the world's most prestigious,[^cite_note-188] with a 200-year history of [premieres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Exhibitions) of major operas, such as *[Nabucco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabucco)* by [Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi) in 1842, *[La Gioconda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gioconda_\(opera\))* by [Amilcare Ponchielli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amilcare_Ponchielli), *[Madama Butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madama_Butterfly)* by [Giacomo Puccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Puccini) in 1904, *[Turandot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turandot)* by Puccini in 1926, and more recently *[Teneke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teneke_\(opera\))*, by [Fabio Vacchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Vacchi) in 2007. Other relevant theatres in Milan include [Teatro degli Arcimboldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_degli_Arcimboldi), [Teatro Dal Verme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Dal_Verme), [Teatro Lirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Lirico_\(Milan\)). The city is also the seat of a renowned [symphony orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestra_Sinfonica_di_Milano_Giuseppe_Verdi) and [musical conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Conservatory), and has been, throughout history, a major centre for musical composition: numerous famous composers and musicians such as [Gioseppe Caimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioseppe_Caimo), [Simon Boyleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Boyleau), [Hoste da Reggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoste_da_Reggio), Giuseppe Verdi, [Giulio Gatti-Casazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Gatti-Casazza), [Paolo Cherici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Cherici) and [Alice Edun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Edun) lived and worked in Milan. The city is also the birthplace of many modern ensembles and bands, including [I Camaleonti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Camaleonti), [Camerata Mediolanense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camerata_Mediolanense), [Gli Spioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gli_Spioni), [Dynamis Ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamis_Ensemble), [Elio e le Storie Tese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_e_le_Storie_Tese), [Krisma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krisma), [Premiata Forneria Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiata_Forneria_Marconi), [Quartetto Cetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartetto_Cetra), [Stormy Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormy_Six), [Le Vibrazioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Vibrazioni) and [Lacuna Coil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacuna_Coil).

### Fashion and design

Main article: [Fashion in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_in_Milan)





A 1952 [Alfa Romeo 1900 C52 Disco Volante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_Disco_Volante) (left) and a 1967 [Alfa Romeo 33 Stradale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_33_Stradale) (right) at the [Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Alfa_Romeo) of [Alfa Romeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo), established in Milan in 1910. The company's logo features the [flag of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Milan) and the [Biscione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscione), a symbol of the city, originally on the coat-of-arms of the [Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_of_Milan) and [Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Sforza) dynasties.

Milan is widely regarded as a global capital in industrial design, fashion and architecture.[^cite_note-189] In the 1950s and 60s, as the main industrial centre of Italy and one of Europe's most dynamic cities, Milan became a world capital of design and architecture. There was such a revolutionary change that Milan's fashion exports accounted for US\$726 million in 1952, and by 1955 that number grew to US\$72.5 billion.[^cite_note-190]

Modern skyscrapers, such as the [Pirelli Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli_Tower) and the [Torre Velasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Velasca) were built, and artists such as [Bruno Munari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Munari), [Lucio Fontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucio_Fontana), [Enrico Castellani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Castellani) and [Piero Manzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Manzoni) gathered in the city.[^cite_note-191] Today, Milan is still particularly well known for its high-quality furniture and interior design industry. The city is home to [Fiera Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiera_Milano), Europe's largest permanent trade exhibition, and Salone Internazionale del Mobile, one of the most prestigious international furniture and design fairs.[^cite_note-192]

Milan is also regarded as one of the fashion capitals of the world, along with [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Fashion_Week), [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Fashion_Week) and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Fashion_Week).[^cite_note-193] Milan is synonymous with the Italian prêt-à-porter industry,[^cite_note-194] as many of the most famous [Italian fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fashion) brands, such as [Valentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_SpA), [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace), [Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prada), [Armani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armani) and [Dolce & Gabbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_&_Gabbana), are headquartered in the city. Numerous international fashion labels also operate shops in Milan. Furthermore, the city hosts the [Milan Fashion Week](18486693.md) twice a year, one of the most important events in the international fashion system.[^cite_note-195] Milan's main upscale fashion district, *[quadrilatero della moda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilatero_della_moda)*, is home to the city's most prestigious shopping streets ([Via Monte Napoleone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Monte_Napoleone), [Via della Spiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_della_Spiga), [Via Sant'Andrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Sant'Andrea), [Via Manzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Manzoni,_Milan) and [Corso Venezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corso_Venezia)), in addition to [Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Vittorio_Emanuele_II), one of the world's oldest shopping malls.[^cite_note-196] The term *[sciura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciura)* encapsulates the look and culture of fashionable, elderly Milanese women.

### Languages and literature

Main article: [Milanese literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milanese_literature)



[Alessandro Manzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni) is famous for the novel *[The Betrothed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Betrothed)* (1827), generally ranked among the masterpieces of [world literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_literature).

[^cite_note-britannica-197]

&#x20;This novel is a fundamental milestone in the development of the modern, unified Italian language.

[^cite_note-198]

In the late 18th century and throughout the 19th, Milan was an important centre for intellectual discussion and literary creativity. The [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) found here a fertile ground. [Cesare, Marquis of Beccaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Beccaria), with his famous *[Dei delitti e delle pene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei_delitti_e_delle_pene)*, and Count [Pietro Verri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Verri), with the periodical *Il Caffè* were able to exert a considerable influence over the new [middle-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class) culture.

In the first years of the 19th century, the ideals of the [Romantic movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) made their impact on the cultural life of the city and its major writers debated the primacy of Classical versus [Romantic poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_poetry). Additionally, [Giuseppe Parini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Parini) and [Ugo Foscolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Foscolo) published their most important works, and were admired by younger poets as masters of ethics, as well as of literary craftsmanship.

In the third decade of the 19th century, [Alessandro Manzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni) wrote his novel *[The Betrothed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Betrothed)*, considered the manifesto of Italian Romanticism, which found in Milan its centre; in the same period [Carlo Porta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Porta), reputed the most renowned local vernacular poet, wrote his poems in [Lombard Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_Language). The periodical *[Il Conciliatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Conciliatore)* published articles by [Silvio Pellico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Pellico), [Giovanni Berchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Berchet), [Ludovico di Breme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovico_di_Breme), who were both Romantic in poetry and patriotic in politics.

After the Unification of Italy in 1861, Milan retained a sort of central position in cultural debates. New ideas and movements from other countries of Europe were accepted and discussed: thus [Realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_\(arts\)) and [Naturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalism_\(literature\)) gave birth to prewar Italian movement of *[Verismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verismo)* in Southern Italy, its greatest *Verista* novelist [Giovanni Verga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Verga) formed in Sicily who wrote his most important books in Milan.

In addition to Italian, approximately 2 million people in Northern Italy can speak the [Milanese dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milanese_dialect) or other [Western Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_dialects_of_Lombard_language) varieties.[^cite_note-199]

### Media

Milan is an important national and international media center. [RCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCS_MediaGroup), a major international multimedia publishing group, is the publisher of both *[Corriere della Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriere_della_Sera)* (established in 1876, it is one of the oldest Italian newspapers) and *[La Gazzetta dello Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gazzetta_dello_Sport)* (a daily dedicated to sports, and the most widely-read in Italy). Other local dailies are the general broadsheets *[Il Giorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Giorno_\(newspaper\))*, *[Il Giornale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Giornale)*, the Catholic newspaper *[Avvenire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avvenire)*, and *[Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Sole_24_Ore)*, a daily business newspaper owned by [Confindustria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confindustria) (the Italian employers' federation). Free daily newspapers include *[Leggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leggo)* and *[Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_\(Italian_newspaper\))*. Milan is also home to many architecture, art and fashion periodicals, including *[Abitare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitare)*, *[Casabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casabella)*, *[Domus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus_\(magazine\))*, *[Flash Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_Art)*, *[Gioia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioia_\(magazine\))*, *[Grazia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazia)* and *[Vogue Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_Italia)*. *[Panorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama_\(magazine\))* and *[Oggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oggi_\(magazine\))*, two of Italy's most important weekly news magazines, are also published in Milan. Several major television networks have their national headquarters in Milan and its metropolitan area, including [Mediaset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediaset) (the largest commercial broadcaster in the country), [Sky Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Italia), the Italian division of [Paramount Skydance](79917576.md),[^cite_note-200] [Telelombardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telelombardia). National radio stations based in Milan include [Radio DeeJay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_DeeJay), [Radio 105 Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_105_Network), [R101 (Italy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R101_\(Italy\)), [Radio Popolare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Popolare), [RTL 102.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTL_102.5), [Radio Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Capital) and [Virgin Radio Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Radio_Italia).

### Cuisine

Main article: [Lombard cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_cuisine)



[Ossobuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossobuco) served with *[risotto alla milanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risotto_alla_milanese)*



[Panettone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panettone)



The [Antica trattoria Bagutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antica_trattoria_Bagutto) in Milan, the oldest restaurant in Italy and the second oldest in Europe.

[^cite_note-\:2-201]

Like most cities in Italy, Milan has developed its own local culinary tradition, which, as it is typical for North Italian cuisines, uses more frequently rice than pasta, butter than [vegetable oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil) and features almost no tomato or [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_as_food). Milanese traditional dishes include *[cotoletta alla milanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotoletta)*, a breaded veal (pork and turkey can be used) cutlet pan-fried in butter (similar to Viennese [Wiener Schnitzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Schnitzel)). Other typical dishes are *[cassoeula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassoeula)* (stewed pork rib chops and sausage with [Savoy cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage)), *[ossobuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossobuco)* (braised [veal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veal) shank served with a condiment called *[gremolata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gremolata)*), *[risotto alla milanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risotto)* (with [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron) and beef marrow), *busecca* (stewed [tripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripe) with beans), *[mondeghili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondeghili)* ([meatballs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meatballs) made with leftover meat fried in butter) and *brasato* (stewed beef or pork with wine and potatoes).

Season-related pastries include *chiacchiere* (flat fritters dusted with sugar) and *tortelli* (fried spherical cookies) for [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival), *colomba* (glazed cake shaped as a dove) for Easter, *pane dei morti* ("bread of the (Day of the) Dead", cookies flavoured with [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon)) for [All Souls' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls'_Day) and [panettone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panettone) for Christmas. The *salame Milano*, a [salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salami) with a very fine grain, is widespread throughout Italy. Renowned Milanese cheeses are [gorgonzola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgonzola) (from the [namesake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgonzola,_Milan) village nearby), [mascarpone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascarpone), used in pastry-making, [taleggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taleggio_cheese) and [quartirolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartirolo_lombardo).

The *[comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune)* of [San Colombano al Lambro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Colombano_al_Lambro), located about 40 kilometres (25 mi) south-east of Milan, is home to the *[Denominazione di origine controllata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominazione_di_origine_controllata)* (DOC) wine which includes 100 hectares (250 acres) producing a single red wine. The finished wine must attain a minimum [alcohol level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_level) of 11% to be [labelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labelled_\(wine\)) with the San Colombano DOC designation.[^cite_note-Saunders-202]

Milan is well known for its world-class restaurants and cafés, characterised by innovative cuisine and design.[^cite_note-203] As of 2014, Milan has 157 Michelin-selected places, including three 1-Michelin-starred restaurants;[^cite_note-204] these include [Cracco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracco_in_Galleria), Sadler and il Luogo di Aimo e Nadia.[^cite_note-205] Many historical restaurants and bars are found in the historic centre, the Brera and Navigli districts. Milan is home to the oldest restaurant in Italy and the second in Europe, the [Antica trattoria Bagutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antica_trattoria_Bagutto), which has existed since at least 1284.[^cite_note-\:2-201][^cite_note-206] One of the city's oldest surviving cafés, [Caffè Cova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caff%C3%A8_Cova), was established in 1817.[^cite_note-207] In total, Milan has 15 cafés, bars and restaurants registered among the Historical Places of Italy, continuously operating for at least 70 years.[^cite_note-208]

Whereas Rome is Italy's political capital, Milan is the country's industrial and financial heart, and it is widely considered the economic capital of Italy[^cite_note-prologis-9] and one of the wealthiest cities in the country in terms of per-capita GDP.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] According to [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat), Milan's metropolitan region GDP was €228 billion (\$265 billion) in 2024,[^cite_note-Eurostat_metro_area_GDP_data-215] while the whole Lombardy's [gross regional product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) was €490 billion (\$568 billion),[^cite_note-216] the [second-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Union_regions_by_GDP) among EU regions.

Milan is a member of the [Blue Banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Banana) corridor and of the [Four Motors for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Motors_for_Europe) among Europe's economic leaders. The urban region of Milan is home to about 45% of businesses in the Lombardy region and more than 8 percent of all businesses in Italy, including three [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies.[^cite_note-217] According to the [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md), Milan was the 11th-most-expensive city in Europe and the 22nd-most-expensive city in the world in 2019.[^cite_note-218] [Via Monte Napoleone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Monte_Napoleone) has been defined as the most-expensive street in the world (2024).[^cite_note-219]

Milan is one of the [fashion capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_capital) of the world, where the sector can count on 12,000 companies, 800 show rooms and 6,000 sales outlets; the city hosts the headquarters of global fashion houses such as Armani, [Bottega Veneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottega_Veneta), [Costume National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costume_National), Dolce & Gabbana, [Dsquared²](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_and_Dan_Caten), [Etro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etro), [Jil Sander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jil_Sander_\(brand\)), [Loro Piana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loro_Piana), [Luxottica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxottica), [Marni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marni_\(brand\)), [Moncler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moncler), Moschino, Prada, Versace, [Valentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_Garavani), [Trussardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trussardi) and [Zegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zegna) and four weeks a year are dedicated to fashion events.[^cite_note-City_profile-220]

Since the late 1800s, the area of Milan has been a major industrial and manufacturing center. [Alfa Romeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo) automobile company and [Falck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falck_Group) steel group employed thousands of workers in the city until the closure of their sites in [Arese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arese) in 2004 and [Sesto San Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesto_San_Giovanni) in 1995.

Other global industrial companies, such as [Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_\(company\)), [Prysmian Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prysmian_Group), [Riva Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppo_Riva), [Saras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saras_S.p.A.), [Saipem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saipem), Luxottica, [Pirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli), [UniCredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniCredit) and [Techint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techint), maintain their headquarters and significant employment in the city and its suburbs. Other relevant industries active in metro Milan include chemicals (e.g. [Mapei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapei), [Versalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versalis), [Tamoil Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamoil)), home appliances (e.g. [Candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candy_\(company\))), hospitality ([UNA Hotels & Resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNA_Hotels_&_Resorts)), food & beverages (e.g. [Bertolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertolli), [Campari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campari_Group)), machinery, medical technologies (e.g. [Amplifon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplifon), [Bracco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracco_\(company\))), plastics and textiles. The construction (e.g. [Webuild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webuild)), retail (e.g. [Esselunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esselunga), [La Rinascente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rinascente)) and utilities (e.g. [A2A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2A), Edison S.p.A., [Snam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snam), [Sorgenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorgenia?action=edit\&redlink=1)) sectors are also large employers in the Greater Milan. Other major companies based in Milan include [Fininvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fininvest), [TIM Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIM_Group) and [Banco BPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_BPM).

The main national insurance companies and banking groups (for a total of 198 companies) and over forty foreign insurance and banking companies are located in the city,[^cite_note-City_profile-220] as well as a number of asset management companies, including [Anima Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anima_Holding), [Azimut Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azimut_Holding), [ARCA SGR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_SGR) and [Eurizon Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurizon_Capital). The [Associazione Bancaria Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associazione_Bancaria_Italiana) representing the Italian banking system, and [Milan Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borsa_Italiana) (225 companies listed on the stock exchange) are both located in the city. Porta Nuova, the main [business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_district) of Milan and one of the most important in Europe, hosts the Italian headquarters of numerous global companies, such as [Accenture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accenture), [Axa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axa), [Bank of America](347756.md), [BNP Paribas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNP_Paribas), [Celgene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celgene), [China Construction Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Construction_Bank), [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank_\(Italy\)), [FM Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_Global), [Herbalife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbalife), [Amazon](90451.md), [Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad_Italia), [KPMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPMG), [Maire Tecnimont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maire_Tecnimont), [Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_UFJ_Financial_Group), [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic), [Pirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli), [Ubisoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft_Milan), [Shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_\(pharmaceutical_company\)), [Tata Consultancy Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Consultancy_Services), [Telecom Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecom_Italia), UniCredit and [UnipolSai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UnipolSai).

Other large multinational service companies, such as [Allianz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz), [Generali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assicurazioni_Generali), [Alleanza Assicurazioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alleanza_Assicurazioni) and [PwC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PwC), have their headquarters in the CityLife business district, a new 900-acre-wide (3.6 km<sup>2</sup>) development project designed by prominent modernist architects Zaha Hadid, Daniel Liebskind and Arata Isozaki.

The city is home to numerous media and advertising agencies, [national newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_newspapers) and telecommunication companies, including both the public service broadcaster [RAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI) and private television companies like [Mediaset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediaset) and [Sky Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Italia). In addition, it hosts the headquarters of the largest Italian publishing companies, such as [Feltrinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feltrinelli_\(publisher\)), [Giunti Editore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giunti_Editore), [Messaggerie Italiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messaggerie_Italiane?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mondadori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondadori), [RCS Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCS_Media_Group) and [Rusconi Libri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusconi_Libri?action=edit\&redlink=1). Milan has also seen a rapid increase in the presence of [IT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) companies, with both domestic and international companies such as [Altavista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altavista), Google, [Italtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italtel), [Lycos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycos), [Microsoft](19001.md),[^cite_note-221] [Virgilio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgilio.it) and [Yahoo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!) establishing their Italian operations in the city.

The city is also a global hub for event management and trade fairs. [Fiera Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiera_Milano) operates the most important trade fair organiser in Italy and the world's fourth-largest[^cite_note-Fiera-209] exhibition hall in Rho, were international exhibitions like [Milan Furniture Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Furniture_Fair), [EICMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EICMA), [EMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMO_\(trade_show\)) take place on 400,000 square metres of exhibition areas with more than 4 million visitors in 2018.[^cite_note-222]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Milan)

Milan is one of the key transport nodes of Italy and southern Europe. Its [central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Centrale_railway_station) is Italy's second, after [Rome Termini railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Termini_railway_station), and Europe's eighth busiest.[^cite_note-grandistazioni-228][^cite_note-229] The [Malpensa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Malpensa_Airport), [Linate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linate_Airport) and [Orio al Serio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orio_al_Serio_International_Airport) airports serve the Greater Milan, the largest metropolitan area in Italy.

[Azienda Trasporti Milanesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azienda_Trasporti_Milanesi) (ATM) is the Milanese municipal transport company; it operates five [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) lines, 18 tram lines, 131 bus lines, four [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) lines and one [people mover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_mover) line, carrying about 776 million passengers in 2018.[^cite_note-230] Overall the network covers nearly 1,500 km (932 mi) reaching 46 municipalities.[^cite_note-231] Besides public transport, ATM manages the interchange [parking lots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_lot) and other transport services, including [bike sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BikeMi) and [carsharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsharing) systems.[^cite_note-232]

### Rail

#### Underground



[Milan Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro) is the largest rapid transit system in Italy in terms of length, number of stations and ridership; and the fifth longest in the [European Union](9317.md) and the eighth in the [Europe](9239.md).

[^cite_note-233]

The [Milan Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro) is the [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system serving the city and surrounding municipalities. The network consists of five lines ([M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_1), [M2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_2), [M3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_3), [M4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_4) and [M5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_5)), with a total network length of 112 kilometres (70 mi), and a total of [125 stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Milan_Metro_stations), mostly underground.[^cite_note-Milan-figs-234] It has a daily ridership of 1.15 million,[^cite_note-rep3sep-235] the largest in Italy as well as one of the largest in Europe.

The architectural project of the Milan Metro, created by [Franco Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Albini) and [Franca Helg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franca_Helg), and the signs, designed by [Bob Noorda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Noorda), received the [Compasso d'Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compasso_d'Oro) award in 1964.[^cite_note-236] In the European Union, it is the seventh-largest network in terms of kilometres.[^cite_note-237]

#### Suburban

As of September 2025, the [Milan suburban railway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_suburban_railway_service), operated by [Trenord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenord), comprises 12 [S lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-train) connecting the metropolitan area with the city centre, with possible transfers to all the metro lines. Most S lines run through the [Milan Passerby Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Passerby_Railway), commonly referred to as "il Passante" and mostly served by double-decker trains every 4/8 minutes in the central underground section, between Porta Vittoria and Lancetti.[^cite_note-238]

#### National and international trains



[Milano Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Centrale_railway_station)

Milan Central station, with 110 million passengers per year, is the largest and [eighth-busiest railway station in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_railway_stations_in_Europe) and the second busiest in Italy after [Roma Termini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini).[^cite_note-grandistazioni-228] Milano Centrale railway station is the largest railway station in Europe by volume.[^cite_note-239] [Milano Cadorna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Cadorna_railway_station) and [Milano Porta Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Porta_Garibaldi_railway_station) stations are, respectively, the seventh- and the eleventh-busiest stations in Italy, mostly served by regional and suburban trains.[^cite_note-grandistazioni-228]

Since the end of 2009, two [high-speed train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) lines link Milan to Rome, [Naples](55880.md) and [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin%E2%80%93Milan_high-speed_railway), considerably shortening travel times with other major cities in Italy. Since 2016, a further high-speed line connects the city to Brescia,[^cite_note-240] as part of the line towards Verona and Venice, which is still under construction,[^cite_note-241] while parts of the line towards Genoa are underway, as of May 2025.[^cite_note-242] Milan is served by direct international trains to Nice, Marseille, Lyon, Paris, Lugano, Geneva, Bern, Basel, Zurich and Frankfurt, and by overnight sleeper services to Munich and Vienna (ÖBB).[^cite_note-243] Since 2011, [Trenord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenord) has operated both Trenitalia and LeNord regional trains in Lombardy, carrying over 750,000 passengers on more than 50 routes every day.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]

[Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia) and [S.N.C.F.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.N.C.F.) provide high speed services to [Paris](22989.md), stopping at [Lyon](8638634.md) and [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin).[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

### Cycling

The bicycle is becoming an increasingly important mode of transportation in Milan. Since 2008, the implementation of a city-wide network of bike paths has been initiated, to fight congestion and air pollution. During the COVID pandemic in 2019, 35 km of bike lanes were realized on short notice, to relieve pressure on the subway occupation.[^cite_note-266]

The [bike sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_sharing) system BikeMi operates throughout most of the city and has become increasingly popular. In addition, several commercial dockless bicycle and scooter sharing services are widely available.

Milan hosted matches at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1990](157232.md), the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980), and more recently held the [2003 World Rowing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Rowing_Championships), the [2009 World Boxing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_AIBA_World_Boxing_Championships), and some games of the [Men's Volleyball World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_Men's_World_Championship) in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIVB_Volleyball_Men's_World_Championship), as well as the final games of the [Women's Volleyball World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_Women's_World_Championship) in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIVB_Volleyball_Women's_World_Championship). In 2018, Milan hosted the [World Figure Skating Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Figure_Skating_Championships). Milan also hosted the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md) as well as the [2026 Winter Paralympics](41045653.md) jointly with [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md).

Milan, along with [Manchester](20206.md), is one of only two cities in Europe that is home to two [European Cup/Champions League](44220.md) winning teams: [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) football clubs [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) and [Inter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan). They are two of the most successful clubs in the world of football in terms of international trophies. Both teams have also won the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) (formerly the [Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_\(1960%E2%80%932004\))). With a combined ten Champions League titles, Milan is only second to Madrid as the city with the most European Cups. Both teams play at the [UEFA](32332.md) 5-star-rated Giuseppe Meazza Stadium, more commonly known as the [San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Siro), that is one of the biggest stadiums in Europe, with a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of over 80,000.[^cite_note-size-294] The Meazza Stadium has hosted four [European Cup/Champions League finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Cup_and_UEFA_Champions_League_finals), most recently in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_UEFA_Champions_League_final), when [Real Madrid](26413.md) defeated [Atlético Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid) 5–3 in a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md). A third team, [Brera Calcio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brera_Calcio), plays in [Prima Categoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prima_Categoria), the seventh tier of Italian football.[^cite_note-295] Another team, [Milano City FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_City_FC) (a successor of Bustese Calcio),[^cite_note-296] plays in [Serie D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_D), the fourth level.

Milan was one of the host cities of the [EuroBasket 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2022). There are currently four professional [Lega Basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Basket) clubs in Milan: [Olimpia Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimpia_Milano), Pallacanestro Milano 1958, Società Canottieri Milano and A.S.S.I. Milano. Olimpia is the most decorated basketball club in Italy, having won 31 [Italian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Basket_Serie_A) championships, eight [Italian National Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Basketball_Cup), five [Italian Super Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Basketball_Supercup), three [European Champions Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroLeague), one [FIBA Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Intercontinental_Cup), three [FIBA Saporta Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Saporta_Cup), two [FIBA Korać Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Kora%C4%87_Cup) and many junior titles. The team play at the [Mediolanum Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum_Forum), with a capacity of 12,700, where it has been hosted the final of the [2013–14 Euroleague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Euroleague). In some cases the team also plays at the [PalaDesio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PalaDesio), with a capacity of 6,700.

Milan is also home to Italy's oldest American football team: [Rhinos Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinos_Milano), who have won five Italian Super Bowls. The team plays at the [Velodromo Vigorelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velodromo_Vigorelli), with a capacity of 8,000. Another [American football](18951490.md) team that use the same venue is the [Seamen Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Seamen), who joined the professional [European League of Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_League_of_Football) in 2023. Milan also has two cricket teams: Milano Fiori, currently competing in the second division, and Kingsgrove Milan, who won the Serie A championship in 2014. [Amatori Rugby Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amatori_Rugby_Milano), the most decorated rugby team in Italy, was founded in Milan in 1927. The [Monza Circuit](952083.md), located near Milan, hosts the Formula One [Italian Grand Prix](511451.md).[^cite_note-297] The circuit is located inside the [Royal Villa of Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Villa_of_Monza) park. It is one of the world's oldest [car racing](1022.md) circuits. The capacity for the [Formula One](10854.md) races is currently over 113,000. It has hosted a Formula One race nearly every year since the first year of competition, with the exception of 1980.

In [road cycling](1755460.md), Milan hosts the start of the annual [Milan–San Remo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93San_Remo) [classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_cycle_races) one-day race and the annual [Milano–Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano%E2%80%93Torino) one day race. Milan is also the traditional finish for the final stage of the [Giro d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_d'Italia), which, along with the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) and the [Vuelta a España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuelta_a_Espa%C3%B1a), is one of cycling's three [Grand Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)).
