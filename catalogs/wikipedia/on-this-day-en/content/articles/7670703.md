**Petite-Rivière-de-l'Artibonite** (French pronunciation: [\[pətit ʁivjɛʁ də laʁtibɔnit\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Haitian Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Creole_language): *Ti Rivyè Latibonit*) is a [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_communes_of_Haiti) in the [Dessalines Arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dessalines_Arrondissement), in the [Artibonite](489220.md) [department](10937444.md) of [Haiti](13373.md). It is located in the [Artibonite Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artibonite_Valley), with the center of the town on a bluff overlooking the [Artibonite River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artibonite_River).

One of the important battles of the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) (1791–1804) was fought here at [Crete Pierrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cr%C3%AAte-%C3%A0-Pierrot); [Jean-Jacques Dessalines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Dessalines) ravaged the French army led by [Rochambeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatien-Marie-Joseph_de_Vimeur,_vicomte_de_Rochambeau).

The town is the site of [Palais de la Belle Rivière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_de_la_Belle_Rivi%C3%A8re), a palace built from 1816 to 1820 by [Henri Christophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Christophe). Its construction was never finished. Despite a popular urban legend, the palace does not have 365 doors and 52 windows. Following the [2010 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake), the palace was restored from its dilapidated state.[^cite_note-2]

In April 2025 the area was attacked by a gang.[^cite_note-3]
