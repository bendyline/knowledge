On 19 and 20 September 2023, [Azerbaijan](746.md) launched a large-scale military offensive against the [self-declared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) breakaway state of [Artsakh](1000530.md), violating the [ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) signed in the aftermath of the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) in 2020.[^cite_note-\:1-12][^cite_note-\:4-13] The offensive took place in the disputed region of [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md), which is *[de jure](43594.md)* a part of Azerbaijan, and was a *de facto* independent republic. The stated goal of the offensive was the complete disarmament and [unconditional surrender](415242.md) of Artsakh, as well as the withdrawal of all Armenian soldiers present in the region.[^cite_note-\:8-14] The offensive occurred in the midst of an escalating crisis caused by Azerbaijan's 10-month-long [blockade of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh), which resulted in significant scarcities of essential supplies such as food, medicine, and other goods.[^cite_note-\:0-15]

One day after the offensive started on 20 September, a [ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) described as a written agreement for the [surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_\(military\)) of Artsakh was reached at the mediation of the [Russian peacekeeping contingent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh#Russian_peacekeeping_contingent) where it was agreed that the [Artsakh Defence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artsakh_Defence_Army) would be disarmed.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Ceasefire violations by Azerbaijan were nonetheless reported by both Artsakhi residents and officials until early October.[^cite_note-ceasefirevio1-20][^cite_note-ceasefirevio2-21] On 28 September, the president of Artsakh, [Samvel Shahramanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvel_Shahramanyan) signed a decree to dissolve all state institutions by 1 January 2024, bringing the existence of the breakaway state to an end.[^cite_note-22]

The offensive and subsequent surrender resulted in the [expulsion of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians](74922032.md), in which nearly the entire population fled to [Armenia](10918072.md) through the [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Human rights organizations and experts in genocide prevention issued multiple alerts[^cite_note-34] that the region's Armenian population was at risk or actively being subjected to ethnic cleansing and genocide,[^cite_note-\:0222-35][^cite_note-OCM-36] as well as war crimes and crimes against humanity.[^cite_note-37]

## Background

Main article: [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](4695860.md)

> All the above efforts have only one goal: ensure irreversible normalization of relations between Baku and Yerevan for the benefit of all populations on the ground. It is now time for courageous compromise solutions, also in light of today's escalation.

— Spokesperson of [Charles Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Michel), [President of the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council), on 1 September 2023.

[^cite_note-michel-statement-38]

The Nagorno-Karabakh conflict is an [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_conflict) and [territorial conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_conflict) between [Armenia](10918072.md) and [Azerbaijan](746.md) over the region of [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md), inhabited mostly by ethnic [Armenians](387816.md) until the offensive.[^cite_note-39] The Nagorno-Karabakh region was once entirely claimed by, and partially *de facto* controlled by the [breakaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md),[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] but is recognized internationally as part of Azerbaijan.

The conflict escalated in 1988, when the [Karabakh Armenians demanded the transfer of the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_movement) from [Soviet Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) to [Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), triggering the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) that ended in 1994 with Azerbaijan losing control of about 13.6% of its territory,[^cite_note-42] including Nagorno-Karabakh, to Karabakh Armenians and the [army of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Armenia). In 1993, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) adopted [four resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_Nations_Security_Council_resolutions_on_the_Nagorno-Karabakh_conflict) that supported the [territorial integrity](31335.md) of Azerbaijan and demanded the immediate withdrawal of the occupying Armenian forces from Azerbaijan, which was reaffirmed by the 2008 [United Nations General Assembly Resolution 62/243](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_62/243).[^cite_note-43]

In late 2020, the large-scale [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) resulted in thousands of casualties and a significant Azerbaijani victory. An armistice was established by a tripartite [ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) on 10 November, resulting in Armenia and Artsakh ceding the territories surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh as well as one-third of Nagorno-Karabakh itself to Azerbaijan[^cite_note-44] [Ceasefire violations in Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh#Ceasefire_violations) and [on the Armenian–Azerbaijani border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Azerbaijan_border_crisis_\(2021%E2%80%93present\)) occurred following the 2020 war, leading to several casualties.

Subsequent to the 2020 war, Azerbaijan rescinded its offer of granting special status and autonomy to its ethnic Armenian residents and insisted on their "integration" into Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Several international mediators and human rights organizations advocated for [self-determination](29269.md) for the local Armenian population[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] and did not believe that Artsakh Armenians could live safely under the authoritarian regime[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] of Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md),[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] which is [institutionally anti-Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan).[^cite_note-\:19-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

## Prelude

Further information: [Armenia–Azerbaijan border crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Azerbaijan_border_crisis) and [Blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh)

In December 2022, Azerbaijan began [blockading the Republic of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Republic_of_Artsakh_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)) from the outside world, in violation of the 2020 ceasefire agreement and international legal rulings.[^cite_note-56] The Azerbaijiani government [seized territory around the Lachin corridor both within Artsakh and Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Azerbaijan_border_crisis_\(2021%E2%80%93present\)#Armenian_territory_occupied_by_Azerbaijan), blocked alternative bypass routes, and installed a military checkpoint.[^cite_note-57] Azerbaijan also sabotaged critical civilian infrastructure of Artsakh, including gas, electricity, and internet access.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

The blockade created a [humanitarian crisis](1251700.md) for the population of Artsakh. Imports of essential goods, including humanitarian convoys of the [Red Cross](15487.md) and the [Russian peacekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh) were blocked. 120,000 residents of the region were trapped.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] There were widespread shortages of essential goods, including electricity, fuel, and water reserves, and emergency reserves were rationed, alongside massive unemployment, and closures of schools and public transportation.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Azerbaijan claimed its actions were aimed at preventing the transportation of weapons and natural resources.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] It also said its goal was for Artsakh to "integrate" into Azerbaijan, despite opposition from the population, and threatened military action if the [Artsakh government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Artsakh) did not disband.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The spokesperson for [Charles Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Michel), [President of the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council) called for the "irreversible normalization" of Armenian-Azerbaijani relations and "courageous compromise solutions".[^cite_note-michel-statement-38]

Numerous countries, international organizations, and human rights observers condemned Azerbaijan's blockade and considered it to be a form of [hybrid warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_warfare)[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] and [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing).[^cite_note-70] Multiple international observers also considered the blockade and the inaction of the Russian peacekeepers to be violations of the tripartite [2020 Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) signed between Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Russia, which ended the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) and guaranteed safe passage for Armenia through the [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Azerbaijan and Russia ignored calls from various countries and international organizations to restore freedom of movement to Armenia through the corridor.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

Two weeks before the clashes, the [Lemkin Institute for Genocide Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemkin_Institute_for_Genocide_Prevention) issued a report stating that there was "alarming evidence that President \[Ilham] Aliyev may be planning a military assault on Artsakh in the very near future." The report noted that Aliyev had recently signed a new decree ordering all eligible citizens 18 years of age or older to report for military service between 1 and 31 October 2023. The Lemkin Institute also warned that a "military assault on Artsakh could lead to the mass murder stage of [genocide](12441.md). It would almost assuredly result in the forced displacement of [Armenians](387816.md) from Artsakh and the widespread commission of genocidal atrocities...\[and]...Artsakh's Armenians would lose their distinct identity as Artsakhsis, an identity that [has been forged through centuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nagorno-Karabakh)—millennia—of independent cultural flourishing in their mountains and valleys."[^cite_note-75]

## Offensive

### 19 September



Azerbaijani soldiers in trenches

On 19 September 2023, in violation of the [2020 ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement),[^cite_note-\:1-12][^cite_note-\:4-13] [Azerbaijan](746.md) launched a large-scale offensive against [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md). The [Azerbaijan Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Azerbaijan\)) claimed to be undertaking "local anti-terrorist activities" and cited [land mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_mines) allegedly planted by Armenians killing two Azerbaijani civilians and four police officers as a pretext for the offensive.[^cite_note-eurasianet-76][^cite_note-Alarabiyah3-77] The ministry demanded the disarmament and withdrawal of all ethnic Armenian soldiers, as well as the [unconditional surrender](415242.md) and dissolution of the Republic of Artsakh.[^cite_note-78] The statement from the ministry claimed that the [Russian peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh) contingent and [the joint Russian-Turkish Monitoring Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_joint_Russian-Turkish_Monitoring_Centre) were informed about the operation.[^cite_note-\:8-14] Russia denied this claim, stating that its peacekeepers were only informed of the offensive a "few minutes" before it started.[^cite_note-apkarabakh22-79]

Azerbaijan claimed that no civilian targets were attacked with weaponry, but witnesses reported strikes conducted in close proximity to large cities and densely populated areas.[^cite_note-\:0-15] The attacks occurred in the midst of Azerbaijan's ongoing blockade of the region.[^cite_note-\:0-15] Azerbaijan said that it had set up "[humanitarian corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_corridors) and reception points on the [Lachin road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor) and in other directions" which would "ensure the evacuation of the population from the dangerous area".[^cite_note-\:0222-35] These announcements were disseminated through text messages, leaflets, and social media, triggering fears of [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) amongst the residents.[^cite_note-80] Artsakhi authorities warned its residents that "the Azerbaijani propaganda machine uses large-scale information and psychological influence measures."[^cite_note-auto-81][^cite_note-82] The [Cyber Security Service of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_Security_Service_\(Azerbaijan\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) temporarily [restricted access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restrictions_on_TikTok) to [TikTok](56822861.md) in Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

Late in the afternoon, Nagorno-Karabakh's leadership offered to negotiate with Azerbaijan after it launched its military offensive and called on Azerbaijan to "immediately cease the hostilities and sit down at the negotiation table with the aim of settling the situation". The [Presidential Administration of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Administration_of_Azerbaijan) responded that it was ready to meet with representatives of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians in the Azerbaijani town of [Yevlakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevlakh).[^cite_note-eurasianet-76] It stressed at the same time that the Azerbaijani offensive would continue unless Artsakh disband its government bodies and armed forces.[^cite_note-85] The Azerbaijani Defence Ministry later stated that its forces had captured more than 60 military posts and destroyed up to 20 military vehicles.[^cite_note-86] The Armenian daily *[Azg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azg_\(daily\))* reported claims that Azerbaijan had captured the villages of [Charektar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charektar) and [Getavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getavan,_Nagorno-Karabakh).[^cite_note-87] The [Prosecutor General's Office of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecutor_General's_Office_of_Azerbaijan) claimed that Armenian forces attacked [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha) with large-caliber weapons, killing one civilian.[^cite_note-88]

Artsakh authorities stated that the state's de facto capital, [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert), and other cities were being heavily shelled, accusing Azerbaijan of attempted ethnic cleansing.[^cite_note-\:5-89][^cite_note-\:6-90] Artsakh's [human rights](13831.md) ombudsman Gegham Stepanyan said two civilians, including a child, were killed,[^cite_note-91] while 11 others were injured,[^cite_note-92] eight of whom were children.[^cite_note-apkarabakh22-79] By the end of the day, Artsakh reported that 27 people had been killed and more than 200 were injured.[^cite_note-halt-93]

Artsakh authorities reported that they had evacuated over 7,000 people from 16 rural settlements,[^cite_note-94] while Russian peacekeepers evacuated 5,000 others.[^cite_note-bbc-95] [Russian Foreign Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Foreign_Ministry) spokesperson [Maria Zakharova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Zakharova) stated that Russian food and medicine arrived in Artsakh via the Lachin and Agdam routes.[^cite_note-96]

### 20 September



Wounded Armenian civilian



Armenian children hiding during the Azerbaijani attacks

See also: [2023 Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement)

Armenian sources reported that Aznavur Saghyan, the mayor of [Martuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martuni,_Nagorno-Karabakh), was killed[^cite_note-97] by an Azerbaijani sniper.[^cite_note-98] It was also reported that the [Amaras Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaras_Monastery) near [Sos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sos,_Khojavend) had fallen under Azerbaijani control.[^cite_note-99] *Azg* reported that Azerbaijani forces had captured the settlements of [Drmbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drmbon,_Nagorno-Karabakh) and [Harav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harav);[^cite_note-100] later that day, [Chankatagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chankatagh), [Chapar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapar,_Nagorno-Karabakh), [Karmir Shuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmir_Shuka), [Khachmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachmach), [Machkalashen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machkalashen), [Sarushen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarushen,_Nagorno-Karabakh), [Shosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shosh,_Nagorno-Karabakh) and [Vaghuhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaghuhas) were also reported to have been captured.[^cite_note-101] Furthermore, the Armenian daily *[Aravot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravot)* reported that the Kashen mine,[^cite_note-kashen-102] one of the biggest sources of tax revenue for the Artsakhi government,[^cite_note-103] had fallen under Azerbaijani control.[^cite_note-kashen-102] Artsakhi president [Samvel Shahramanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvel_Shahramanyan) said "[Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md) will have to take relevant steps to ensure physical security of population".[^cite_note-104]

Artsakhi authorities agreed to a proposal by [Russian peacekeeping forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh) to establish a ceasefire from 13\:00 on 20 September.[^cite_note-105] Under the terms of the agreement, the government of the [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md) agreed to disarm and to enter into talks with the government of [Azerbaijan](746.md) regarding the reintegration of the territory.[^cite_note-106] Among the Azerbaijani demands was a requirement for Artsakh and [Armenia](10918072.md) to surrender a list of individuals to Azerbaijan for prosecution and trial, including former and current Artsakhi civilian and military leaders.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] Large masses of [Armenian](387816.md) civilians began fleeing Artsakh after the ceasefire announcement, with many of them gathering at [Stepanakert Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert_Airport).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] Later, [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) commented on this that "it is obvious to me that this is being done to cause internal political upheavals and chaos".[^cite_note-111] The Armenian government said it was not involved in the drafting of the ceasefire agreement, while Azerbaijani presidential envoy [Elchin Amirbeyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elchin_Amirbeyov?action=edit\&redlink=1) said that Russian peacekeepers helped facilitate the ceasefire.[^cite_note-bbc-95]

Colonel [Anar Eyvazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anar_Eyvazov?action=edit\&redlink=1), Spokesperson for the [Azerbaijani Defense Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Defense_Ministry), announced that during the operation, Azerbaijan had captured 90 combat positions. He also said that Azerbaijani forces had captured seven combat vehicles, one tank, four mortars and two infantry fighting vehicles from Armenian military units as trophies.[^cite_note-tass90pos-112]

Shelling of Stepanakert continued until the city's electrical grid was knocked out several hours after the ceasefire was supposed to go into effect.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] According to a statement from the [Russian Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ministry_of_Defense), several peacekeepers were killed near the village of [Chankatagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chankatagh) in [Tartar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartar_District) after their vehicle [was attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_killing_of_Russian_peacekeepers_in_Nagorno-Karabakh?action=edit\&redlink=1) while they were returning from an observation post.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] Azerbaijani official [Elin Suleymanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elin_Suleymanov) admitted that Azeri forces had killed the peacekeepers, calling it an accident, expressed condolences to Russia, and vowed to launch a probe into what happened.[^cite_note-peacekeepers-killed-10][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] Among those killed was Captain First Rank Ivan Kovgan, the deputy commander of Russia's [Northern Fleet submarine forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet#Submarines),[^cite_note-122] and a concurrent deputy commander of the peacekeeping force.[^cite_note-123] With Azerbaijani cooperation, Russian peacekeepers detained suspects, and an Azerbaijani commander was suspended.[^cite_note-124] President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) subsequently apologized over phone to Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) for the incident.[^cite_note-125] According to the [Prosecutor General's Office of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecutor_General's_Office_of_Azerbaijan), a group of military personnel of the Azerbaijan army opened fire on a car with Russian peacekeepers, mistaken believing they were Armenian forces due to the difficult terrain and foggy-rainy weather conditions. As a result, five peacekeepers were killed.[^cite_note-trendazpeacekeepers-126][^cite_note-127]



Ilham Aliyev addressing the nation on 20 September 2023

Armenia accused Azerbaijan of firing at its soldiers in the border town of [Sotk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotk), which Azerbaijan denied.[^cite_note-bbc-95]

In a televised address that evening, President Aliyev reiterated that "Karabakh is Azerbaijan", adding that his "iron fist" had consigned the idea of Karabakh being a separate Armenian state to history.[^cite_note-128]

## Aftermath



Armenians evacuated from their homes

### Expulsion of Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh

Main article: [Expulsion of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians](74922032.md)

After the fighting ended on 21 September, Azerbaijan opened the border for the first time in months to allow people to leave, and tens of thousands of Armenians began to flee out through the military checkpoint at the Lachin corridor. On 24 September, Azerbaijan opened the [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor) for residents of Nagorno-Karabakh to travel one-way to Armenia despite its ongoing [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Republic_of_Artsakh_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-129] A mass evacuation of ethnic Armenian civilians from the region then started, fearing persecution and [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) if they remain.[^cite_note-Light-Reuters-130] The first group of refugees arrived in Armenia through the border post at [Kornidzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kornidzor).[^cite_note-131] By the end of the day, the Armenian government said 1,050 refugees had arrived in the country.[^cite_note-132]

The last scheduled evacuation bus out of Nagorno-Karabakh left on 2 October carrying 15 refugees.[^cite_note-apebbs-133] On the same day, the Artsakhi government said that some officials would stay on until search operations for people killed and missing were completed. Journalists who visited Stepanakert said the city was virtually abandoned except for a few residents, Azerbaijani police officers and Russian peacekeepers. They also added that Azerbaijani telecommunications firms and signals were already present in the city.[^cite_note-134] A United Nations report released the same day found that there were between 50 and 1,000 ethnic Armenians left in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-135] The Armenian government said the number of refugees from Nagorno-Karabakh had reached 100,617 on 3 October,[^cite_note-a617-8] nearly the entire current population of Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-HRW1-136][^cite_note-cnn100-137] A total of 21,043 vehicles were recorded to have crossed the Hakari Bridge going to Armenia in the week since the exodus began.[^cite_note-138]

The government of Artsakh said that families left homeless due to Azerbaijan's military offensive and who expressed a desire to leave Artsakh will be transferred to Armenia, accompanied by Russian peacekeepers. It was reported that 23 ambulances, accompanied by specialists and the Red Cross, were transporting 23 severely injured people from Artsakh to Armenia.[^cite_note-\:33-139] In Armenia, the theatre in the city of [Goris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goris) was converted into a base for the Red Cross to accommodate refugees.[^cite_note-140] A secondary hub was later opened in [Vayk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayk).[^cite_note-141] Armenian Health Minister [Anahit Avanesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahit_Avanesian) said some refugees died in transit due to exhaustion brought about by malnutrition, the lack of medicines and the travel time which took up to 40 hours.[^cite_note-uncertain-142] The Armenian government promised a one-off payment to each refugee of 100,000 drams and provide a monthly subsidy of 50,000 drams for at least six months, adding that it had so far managed to provided temporary accommodation for 53,000 refugees.[^cite_note-143]

During the movement of refugees, at least 170 people were killed[^cite_note-144] and more than 290 people were injured after [an explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkadzor_explosion) at a fuel storage facility in [Berkadzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkadzor), 6 kilometres (3.7 miles) from Stepanakert on 25 September. Most of the victims were queueing to obtain fuel for their vehicles while on their way to Armenia.[^cite_note-explosion-145] Victims with various degrees of burns were treated in the Republican Medical Center of Artsakh, facilities of the Arevik community organization, medical facilities in Ivanyan, and the medical station of Russian peacekeepers.[^cite_note-146] The [Presidential Administration of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Administration_of_Azerbaijan) said it sent an ambulance carrying medical supplies.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] 142 of the injured were later brought to Armenia.[^cite_note-uncertain-142]

### Arrests of Artsakhi officials

Azerbaijani forces were reported to be screening refugees fleeing to Armenia as part of its search for "war criminals" in border checkpoints.[^cite_note-149] The [Azerbaijani State Border Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Border_Service_of_Azerbaijan) detained several Artsakhi officials in the Lachin corridor while attempting to cross into Armenia, including former army commander and [defence minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Artsakh\)) [Levon Mnatsakanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Mnatsakanyan),[^cite_note-150] former deputy army commander Davit Manukyan,[^cite_note-cnn100-137] and Russian-Armenian billionaire [Ruben Vardanyan](8841045.md), who was the former [state minister](59831143.md).[^cite_note-151] Former [foreign minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Artsakh#Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs) [David Babayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Babayan) surrendered to Azerbaijani authorities in Shusha.[^cite_note-152] Azerbaijani presidential adviser [Hikmat Hajiyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikmat_Hajiyev) later confirmed that up to six people had been arrested on charges of committing "war crimes".[^cite_note-153]

On 1 October, Azerbaijani Prosecutor-General [Kamran Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamran_Aliyev_\(lawyer\)) issued arrest warrants against former Artsakhi President [Arayik Harutyunyan](13280780.md) and military commander [Jalal Harutyunyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_Harutyunyan) over their role in the [2020 Ganja missile attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Ganja_missile_attacks) during the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War,[^cite_note-154] adding that 300 other separatist officials were under investigation for alleged war crimes.[^cite_note-155]

On 3 October, the [State Security Service of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Security_Service_of_Azerbaijan) detained former [presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Artsakh) [Arkadi Ghukasyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkadi_Ghukasyan), [Bako Sahakyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bako_Sahakyan)[^cite_note-formerpresidents-156] and Arayik Harutyunyan,[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] as well as [President of the National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Artsakh) [Davit Ishkhanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davit_Ishkhanyan).[^cite_note-formerpresidents-156][^cite_note-159]

### Negotiations and dissolution of Artsakh

On 21 September, negotiations between representatives of Nagorno-Karabakh and Azerbaijan took place in [Yevlakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevlakh) to discuss security, rights and "issues of re-integration".[^cite_note-160] The Artsakhi delegation consisted of Sergey Martirosyan and [Davit Melkumyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davit_Melkumyan?action=edit\&redlink=1), and was escorted by [Russian peacekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh). The Azerbaijani delegation consisted of [Ramin Mammadov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramin_Mammadov), Bashir Hajiyev and Ilkin Sultanov.[^cite_note-161] The head of the [Joint Russian-Turkish Monitoring Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Russian-Turkish_Monitoring_Center), Oleg Semyonov, was also present.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] The talks, which lasted two hours, ended without a formal agreement, however a statement from the Azerbaijani government said that they were "constructive and positive" and that further negotiations would continue in October.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] According to the Artsakhi delegation, the negotiations stalled due to a "whole host of questions", but specifically when they requested security guarantees that the Azerbaijani government would not force ethnic Armenians to [leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) the [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md) region, however, both sides agreed on the cessation of military action.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] Azerbaijan agreed to send food and fuel supplies to the region.[^cite_note-168]

On 22 September, [Hikmet Hajiyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikmet_Hajiyev), foreign policy adviser to Azerbaijani president [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md), stated that his government would ensure that civilians can travel safely in their own vehicles on roads that connect [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md) to [Armenia](10918072.md).[^cite_note-169] He also suggested that an [amnesty](18940624.md) would be offered to former combatants who agree to disarm. Some ethnic [Armenian](387816.md) armed groups vowed to continue fighting.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Rumors spread on some Internet news publications that these included former colonel [Karen Jalavyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Jalavyan) and his subordinates. A journalist from *Hraparak* contacted Jalavyan and asked him if the rumors were true, but he did not answer the question directly. Noticeably upset, he stated that "the entire nation has washed its hands from the people of Karabakh" and told Pashinyan to "go defend Artsakh".[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

The second day of negotiations also ended in deadlock despite the amnesty offer to Artsakhi commanders and fighters as the Artsakhi delegation stated the questions of humanitarian aid, security guarantees, and a promise from the Azerbaijani delegation to not drive Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh were unresolved.[^cite_note-174] According to Armenian journalist [Tatul Hakobyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatul_Hakobyan), a meeting between the head of [State Security Service of Azerbaijan](54147626.md), [Ali Naghiyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Naghiyev), and Artsakh's president [Samvel Shahramanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvel_Shahramanyan) took place in Shusha.[^cite_note-175]

Elements of the Artsakh defense force began transferring their weapons to Russian peacekeepers,[^cite_note-176] with the Russian defence ministry confirming the turnover of six armored vehicles, more than 800 small arms units and 5,000 rounds of ammunition.[^cite_note-177] The memorial tank monument, a [restored T-72 tank of Gagik Avsharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shusha_\(1992\)#Commemorations) commemorating the [capture of Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shusha_\(1992\)) and located on Shusha road, was reportedly dismantled by Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] Additionally, a convoy of 15 trucks from the Russian peacekeepers left Armenia for Nagorno-Karabakh, crossing the Lachin corridor.[^cite_note-180] More than 50 tonnes of humanitarian aid was delivered to Nagorno-Karabakh by Russian peacekeepers, according to the Russian defence ministry.[^cite_note-181] On 24 September, the town of [Martakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martakert) was reported to have come under Azerbaijani military control.[^cite_note-182]

A second round of negotiations between representatives of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians and Azerbaijan took place in [Khojaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khojaly_\(town\)), where according to Azerbaijan, humanitarian issues were discussed.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184] The two sides agreed to a third meeting taking place in a few days time.[^cite_note-185]

On 26 September, Azerbaijani forces reportedly took control of [Martuni (Khojavend)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martuni,_Nagorno-Karabakh).[^cite_note-186]

On 28 September, Artsakh president [Samvel Shahramanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvel_Shahramanyan) signed a decree stating that all state institutions would be dissolved by 1 January 2024, bringing the existence of the breakaway republic to an end.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] However, on 22 December, Shahramanyan, who was by then in exile in the Armenian capital [Yerevan](34352.md), said that there was "no document... of the Republic of Artsakh (Karabakh) stipulating the dissolution of government institutions," while his office said that the decree issued in September was "empty paper," adding that "no document can lead to the dissolution of the republic, which was established by people's will."[^cite_note-189]

Azerbaijani media reported on 29 September that a third meeting between representatives of the Karabakh Armenian community and officials from the government of Azerbaijan took place in [Yevlakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevlakh).[^cite_note-190] Azerbaijani police vehicles were seen in Stepanakert[^cite_note-191] and officials placed the [Azerbaijani flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Azerbaijan) on the city's *[We Are Our Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Our_Mountains)* monument.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193]

A fourth round of talks was held in Stepanakert on 2 October, during which Azerbaijani authorities outlined their plans for the "reintegration" of the region.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195]

On 3 October, Azerbaijani officials were reported to have reissued a 2021 map of Stepanakert renaming one of its streets after [Enver Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Pasha), one of the main perpetrators of the [Armenian genocide](140376.md).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] When approached for comment, the State Service on Property Issues of the [Ministry of Economy of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_of_Azerbaijan) and the [State Committee on Urban Planning and Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Committee_on_Urban_Planning_and_Architecture_\(Azerbaijan\)) of Azerbaijan stated they were unaware of any street renaming initiated by Azerbaijani authorities in Stepanakert.[^cite_note-198]

On 15 October, President Ilham Aliyev visited Stepanakert and raised the [Azerbaijani flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_flag).[^cite_note-199] A video was also released showing him stepping on an [Artsakhi flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Artsakh) placed on the floor in the building that housed the former government.[^cite_note-200]



Ilham Aliyev raising the flag of Azerbaijan in [Khojaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khojaly,_Azerbaijan), a town previously controlled by Artsakh until the offensive

### Ceasefire violations

The Artsakh Ministry of Internal Affairs stated on 21 September that the Azerbaijani military violated the ceasefire and continued to shell Stepanakert "with different types of small arms".[^cite_note-ceasefirevio1-20] *[Rheinische Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinische_Post)* reported ceasefire violations by Azerbaijani forces and gunfire in Stepanakert, according to residents.[^cite_note-ceasefirevio2-21] It was reported that electricity in Artsakh cannot be supplied because a number of substations that feed the electrical grid were under Azerbaijani control, with "Artsakhenergo" CJSC carrying out restoration works in Stepanakert.[^cite_note-201] Russia recorded two instances of ceasefire violations in [Shusha District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_District) and three other instances in [Mardakert District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardakert_District_\(NKAO\)).[^cite_note-202] [Artak Beglaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artak_Beglaryan), former Artsakhi Minister of State, reported that [Karkijahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karkijahan), a suburb of Stepanakert, was "empty" following a ceasefire violation. Many residents took refuge in Stepanakert itself.[^cite_note-203] He stated that Azerbaijani troops entered several houses in the neighborhood.[^cite_note-beglaryan-204] To avoid further tensions, Russian peacekeepers were deployed in the outskirts of Stepanakert, including in Karkijahan.[^cite_note-205] Artsakhi armed forces were also deployed to the area.[^cite_note-beglaryan-204]

On 22 September, [Artsakhi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Artsakh) spokesperson Armine Hayrapetyan told [Agence-France Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence-France_Presse) that Azerbaijani forces had positioned themselves around Stepanakert, prompting residents to hide in basements for fear of killings. She also added that since the Azerbaijani offensive, Stepanakert and other parts of Nagorno-Karabakh have lost most basic services such as electricity, gas, food, fuel, internet and telephone connections.[^cite_note-\:2-206]

On 23 September, the Russian Ministry of Defense said one ceasefire violation was recorded in the Mardakert region, which resulted in one Azerbaijani soldier being injured following a shootout.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

On 24 September, the Azerbaijani Defence Ministry claimed that two of its soldiers were killed after their truck hit a landmine.[^cite_note-explosion-145]

On 30 September, Azerbaijan claimed that one of its soldiers was killed by an Armenian army sniper in [Aşağı Ayrım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C5%9Fa%C4%9F%C4%B1_Ayr%C4%B1m), [Kalbajar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalbajar_District), which Armenia immediately denied.[^cite_note-209]

On 2 October, Armenia accused Azerbaijani forces of opening fire on a vehicle delivering food to military outposts around the village of [Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kut,_Armenia),[^cite_note-210] killing a soldier and injuring two others. On the same day, the Russian Defence Ministry said that a joint Russian-Azerbaijani patrol in Stepanakert was shot at by an unidentified sniper without causing any injury.[^cite_note-211]

On 5 October, Armenia and Azerbaijan accused each other of firing at military targets on each other's territory.[^cite_note-\:39-212]

### Humanitarian aid

On 22 September, Azerbaijan said it had sent two 20-tonne trucks with food and hygiene products and well as two trucks with bread to Nagorno-Karabakh from [Aghdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghdam).[^cite_note-\:2-206]

For the first time since the Azerbaijani offensive, a [Red Cross](5023832.md) aid convoy was spotted at the Armenia–Azerbaijan border heading for Nagorno-Karabakh on 23 September.[^cite_note-213] The Red Cross later evacuated 17 people who were injured during the clashes.[^cite_note-214] In a separate delivery, Russia sent 50 tons of aid to Stepanakert.[^cite_note-215]

On 28 September, [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID) sent a Disaster Assistance Response Team to the region to help coordinate the U.S. humanitarian response.[^cite_note-216]

Other countries and international organizations that have provided humanitarian assistance to civilians in Nagorno-Karabakh and to ethnic Armenians that have fled the region include Argentina,[^cite_note-217] Australia,[^cite_note-218] Canada,[^cite_note-219] the Czech Republic,[^cite_note-220] Denmark,[^cite_note-221] the European Union,[^cite_note-222] France,[^cite_note-223] Greece,[^cite_note-224] Hungary,[^cite_note-225] Japan,[^cite_note-226] Iran,[^cite_note-227] Italy,[^cite_note-228] Norway,[^cite_note-229] Poland,[^cite_note-230] Spain,[^cite_note-231] Sweden,[^cite_note-232] Switzerland,[^cite_note-233] and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-234] [Cyprus](5593.md) offered to shelter some of the displaced Armenians who have fled the region.[^cite_note-235]

### Diplomatic efforts



Armenian protesters in London

At a meeting of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md), Armenia proposed that a United Nations peacekeeping mission should be deployed to Nagorno-Karabakh and called for the establishment of an international mechanism for dialogue between Azerbaijan and representatives of the ethnic Armenian community in the region.[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

On 26 September, a meeting between [Armen Grigoryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Grigoryan_\(politician\)), head of Armenia's Security Council and Hikmat Hajiyev, Foreign Policy Advisor to the President of Azerbaijan, was hosted by the [European Union](9317.md) in [Brussels](3708.md). At the meeting the EU emphasized the need for access for humanitarian and human rights organizations into Nagorno-Karabakh. It also wanted more details about the Azerbaijani government's plans for the future of Karabakh Armenians in Azerbaijan. Azerbaijan outlined its plans to provide humanitarian assistance and security to the local population in the region.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239] On the same day, [United States Secretary of State](32293.md) [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) spoke with President Aliyev by telephone and urged him to refrain from further hostilities and to allow an international observer mission into Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-240] German foreign minister [Annalena Baerbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annalena_Baerbock) also urged that independent observers be allowed into the region.[^cite_note-241] Russia insisted that any international observer mission in the region can only happen with permission from the government of Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-242]

On 27 September, US State Department spokesperson [Matthew Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Miller_\(spokesperson\)) announced the government of Azerbaijan had given assurances that it would welcome an international monitoring mission to the region and that the United States and its allies would discuss the composition and mandate of such a mission in the coming days.[^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]

Azerbaijan announced on 29 September that it would facilitate a visit by [United Nations](31769.md) observers to the region "in the coming days".[^cite_note-246] The UN later confirmed that it would send a mission to Nagorno-Karabakh for the first time in 30 years to address humanitarian needs. On the same day, Armenia formally requested the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) to order Azerbaijan to withdraw its troops from civilian areas of Nagorno-Karabakh to ensure safe access by UN personnel.[^cite_note-247] It later petitioned the court to impose 10 "provisional measures" aimed at protecting the rights of ethnic Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh and prevent Azerbaijan's "ethnic cleansing" of the region from becoming irreversible.[^cite_note-248] On 17 November, the court ruled that Azerbaijan had an obligation to allow the return of residents who fled following the offensive and protect those who had stayed behind.[^cite_note-249]

The United Nations in Azerbaijan, led by the UN Resident Coordinator in Azerbaijan Vladanka Andreeva, sent a mission to Nagorno-Karabakh to address humanitarian needs on 1 October. While visiting Stepanakert, the team claimed to have found no evidence of damage to public infrastructure in the areas it visited. The mission saw that the Azerbaijani government was preparing for the resumption of health services and some utilities.[^cite_note-UN_team_completes_mission_to_Karabakh-250] The mission reportedly "was struck by the sudden manner in which the local population left their homes". It was reported to the team that between 50 and 1,000 ethnic Armenians remain in the region. It encountered no civilian vehicles on the Lachin road to the border crossing used by the refugees. The mission also had limited access to rural areas.[^cite_note-UN_team_completes_mission_to_Karabakh-250][^cite_note-251] The mission was criticized for failing to mention the numerous casualties and injuries among Armenian civilians, the targeting of civilian infrastructure by Azerbaijan, and [blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) by Azerbaijan over the previous nine months along with the humanitarian crisis it caused.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254] The delegation returned to the region on 9 October.[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256]

On 4 January 2024, the US State Department placed Azerbaijan on its watchlist on religious freedom, following a recommendation by the [US Commission on International Religious Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Commission_on_International_Religious_Freedom) over concerns for the preservation of Christian religious sites in Nagorno-Karabakh following the Azerbaijani takeover and government oversight in religious activities.[^cite_note-257]

#### Normalisation efforts

On 7 December, Armenia and Azerbaijan agreed to hold a prisoner exchange and move towards normalising relations, stating in a joint statement their agreement to seize "a historical chance to achieve a long-awaited peace in the region".[^cite_note-258] The exchange was conducted on 13 December, with two Azerbaijani soldiers being released in return for 32 Armenian soldiers.[^cite_note-259]

Several countries have offered themselves to become a mediator between both sides in their peace talks and normalisation efforts, such as Georgia, Iran, the European Union and the United States.[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-Kenare-261][^cite_note-\:9-262] Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan and Azerbaijan President Ilham Aliyev have met several times for talks mediated by the EU, but progress towards normalisation stalled when Aliyev refused to attend French-mediated talks in Spain on October and U.S.-mediated talks in Washington D.C. on November, claiming both mediators had a "biased" position.[^cite_note-\:9-262]

Iran hosted talks between the foreign ministries of Armenia and Azerbaijan in Tehran on October, with Turkey and Russia also participating. Georgia was invited to the talks but refused to attend. Iranian president [Ebrahim Raisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebrahim_Raisi) emphasized Iran's rejection of "interference by Western powers in the Caucasus," signifying the country's discontent with U.S.- and EU–mediated talks.[^cite_note-Kenare-261]

## Risk of genocide

Main article: [Expulsion of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians](74922032.md)

Further information: [Blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh § Genocide risk factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh#Genocide_risk_factors)



Armenians seeking refuge due to attacks by Azerbaijani forces

Human rights organizations and [experts in genocide prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_studies) issued alerts stating that the ethnic Armenian population in Nagorno-Karabakh was at risk of [genocide](12441.md),[^cite_note-263] while others stated that Azerbaijan was already carrying out such actions.[^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265][^cite_note-auto1-266] The Lemkin Institute for Genocide Prevention states "There is no doubt in the minds of experts in genocide prevention – at the Lemkin Institute, but also at [Genocide Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Stanton#Genocide_Watch), the [International Association of Genocide Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Genocide_Scholars), and among legal experts such as former ICC chief prosecutor [Luis Moreno Ocampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Moreno_Ocampo) – that what Armenians are facing from Azerbaijan is genocide."[^cite_note-267] Experts in genocide prevention have stated that Azerbaijan's ongoing blockade of Artsakh and sabotage of public infrastructure constitutes genocide under Article II, (c) of the [Genocide Convention](143988.md), "Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction". There are various indicators that Azerbaijan possesses [genocidal intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocidal_intent): President [Aliyev's public statements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan#Official_statements), his regime's [openly Armenophobic practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan#Destruction_of_cultural_heritage) and noncompliance with the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) orders to end the blockade.[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270]

[Christian Solidarity International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Solidarity_International) emphasized that the "[United States](3434750.md) has had ample notice of the forced deportation that is now unfolding in Nagorno Karabakh, starting with Genocide Watch Emergency Alerts in 2020, 2021, 2022, and a Genocide Warning issued by the Save Karabakh Coalition in December 2022. These warnings were followed by two Genocide Emergency Alerts issued by Genocide Watch in 2023, and a declaration of genocide by the first Chief [Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court](33903993.md), Luis Moreno Ocampo."[^cite_note-auto1-266] [Thomas de Waal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_de_Waal), a senior fellow at [Carnegie Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Europe), commented on Azerbaijan's ongoing nine-month long blockade of the region: "Armenians fear that this is a prelude to an Azerbaijani attempt to fully drive them all out of their homeland."[^cite_note-\:90-271]

### Reports of violence against Armenians

Kavita Belani, [United Nations Refugee Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Refugee_Agency) Representative in Armenia, who spoke with Karabakh Armenians in Armenia, stated on 29 September 2023 that "there were no recorded incidents or cases of mistreatment against people on the move".[^cite_note-272] A UN mission that visited Nagorno-Karabakh on 1 October 2023 reported that "they did not come across any reports — either from the local population or from others — of violence against civilians following the latest ceasefire."[^cite_note-273]

Lawyer Anoush Baghdassarian was onsite in [Goris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goris) to interview Armenian refugees and document possible war crimes and crimes against humanity.[^cite_note-274] On 19 October 2023, [Anahit Manasyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahit_Manasyan), the [Human Rights Defender of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Defender_of_Armenia), announced that the bodies of numerous Armenian civilians massacred in Nagorno-Karabakh had been transported to Armenia. Manasyan stated that the bodies showed signs of torture and included women and children.[^cite_note-275]

## International aspects

### Reactions

#### Armenia

Further information: [2023 Armenian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Armenian_protests)



Protesters in front of the Russian embassy in Yerevan carried the Artsakh flag.

Armenian Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) reiterated that the situation in the Armenia-Azerbaijan border was stable and said that Azerbaijan was trying to [ethnically cleanse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnically_cleanse) the region.[^cite_note-\:5-89] Pashinyan also said that Azerbaijan's motivation for the attack was to draw Armenia into a military confrontation.[^cite_note-auto-81] Following the ceasefire on 20 September, Pashinyan said the country was preparing for an influx of 40,000 families from Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-276] The [Armenian foreign ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Armenia\)) accused Azerbaijan of unleashing "large-scale aggression" against Karabakh and attempting "ethnic cleansing" in the region.[^cite_note-\:5-89][^cite_note-\:6-90] Armenia called on the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) and Russia to take action to end the military operation, while Pashinyan called an emergency meeting of the country's National Security Council.[^cite_note-\:5-89] The council's secretary, [Armen Grigoryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Grigoryan_\(politician\)), accused [Russian peacekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh) of failing to protect Nagorno-Karabakh,[^cite_note-cnn-277] which was also echoed by Pashinyan.[^cite_note-278] Following the announcement of the ceasefire, protests erupted in the capital [Yerevan](34352.md) over the impending loss of Nagorno-Karabakh. The pro-Western National Democratic Alliance blamed the situation on Russia's failure to intervene, while members of the pro-Russian political opposition blamed Pashinyan for the defeat and accused him of betraying Nagorno-Karabakh's residents "in favor of the interests of the West."[^cite_note-279]

On 3 October, the [National Assembly of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Armenia) voted 60–22 in favor of ratifying the [Rome Statute](68762.md), which would enable Armenia to join the [International Criminal Court](14880.md). Although the government claimed that the move to create additional guarantees for Armenia in response to Azerbaijani aggression, it was also seen as a sign of worsening relations with Russia, whose president, [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), is wanted by the court on charges of war crimes in the [invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-280] The measure was signed into law by President [Vahagn Khachaturyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahagn_Khachaturyan) on 14 October.[^cite_note-281]

On 28 November 2023, the president of the [National Assembly of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Armenia) [Alen Simonyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alen_Simonyan) stated that the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict is over and Armenia should not prioritize the quick return of the recently displaced Karabakh Armenians to their homes in peace talks with Azerbaijan. Simonyan confirmed that "Armenia fully recognizes the territorial integrity of Azerbaijan, including Nagorno-Karabakh."[^cite_note-282] On 1 February 2024, Armenia became the 124th member of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md).[^cite_note-283]

##### Armenian diaspora



Armenians protesting in front of the [UN headquarters](188955.md)

In [Los Angeles](18110.md), members of the [Armenian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Armenian_Americans_in_Los_Angeles) used a trailer truck to block a major freeway for several hours to draw attention to the situation in Nagorno-Karabakh on 19 September, causing major traffic jams. #### Russia

[Russian foreign ministry](4432213.md) spokeswoman [Maria Zakharova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Zakharova) said that Russia was "deeply alarmed by the sharp escalation."[^cite_note-\:5-89][^cite_note-290] Chairman of the [Security Council of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council_of_Russia) [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) said that Russia will not defend [Armenia](10918072.md) from the [Azerbaijani](746.md) offensive, while strongly criticizing Armenian Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) for "\[flirting] with NATO" and "defiantly going to \[Russia's] enemies with cookies".[^cite_note-291] This comes despite Russia and Armenia both being members of the [Collective Security Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization) mutual defense pact and Russia stationing several thousand soldiers in Armenia and Nagorno-Karabakh as [peacekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh).[^cite_note-292]

Presidential spokesperson [Dmitry Peskov](9214683.md) denied accusations from Armenia that the country's peacekeepers had failed to protect Nagorno-Karabakh from the Azerbaijani attack, calling them "unfounded".[^cite_note-cnn-277] Peskov later said that the future of the peacekeeping mission was to be decided between Russia and Armenia now that their area of operation was effectively in Azerbaijani territory.[^cite_note-293] A week before the fighting, President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) said that the country could do nothing if Armenia had already recognized Nagorno-Karabakh as part of Azerbaijan, referring to statements made by Pashinyan in May that appeared to recognize Azerbaijani sovereignty over Nagorno-Karabakh in exchange for security guarantees towards the Armenian population.[^cite_note-bbc-95]

The independent Russian media outlet *[Meduza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meduza_\(website\))* said it had obtained a guidance document from the Kremlin circulated on 19 September to state media outlets that recommended blaming Armenia and the West, rather than Azerbaijan, for the escalation of the conflict in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-cnn-277] Speaking at the 78th [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) in [New York City](645042.md) on 23 September, Foreign Minister [Sergei Lavrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Lavrov) accused the Armenian leadership of adding "fuel to the fire", referring to the conflict, and reiterated that [agreements made following the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma-Ata_Protocol) stated that the existing borders of its former constituent republics were inviolable, hereby recognizing Nagorno-Karabakh as part of Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-294]

#### Turkey

Imposition of another status \[to the region] will never be accepted," and that "\[Turkey] support\[s] the steps taken by Azerbaijan — with whom we act together with the motto of [one nation, two states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan%E2%80%93Turkey_relations) — to defend its territorial integrity."[^cite_note-296] Erdoğan also met with Aliyev in the [Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic](385358.md).[^cite_note-297]

#### European Union

The [President of the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council) of the European Union [Charles Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Michel) condemned Azerbaijan's hostility and urged the country to immediately stop its military activities and return to dialogue, through a social media post.[^cite_note-\:52-298]

On 21 September, [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell), the High Representative of the European Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy released a statement which condemned the military operation by Azerbaijan against the Armenian population of Nagorno-Karabakh and deplored the casualties and loss of life caused by the offensive.[^cite_note-299] According to [RFE/RL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFE/RL)'s journalist Rikard Jozwiak, this was not issued as a joint statement of the entire bloc because it was [vetoed](74661.md) by [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] Hungarian [Foreign Minister](25144043.md) [Péter Szijjártó](26166814.md) denied this and said that the country, "along with several other member states, provided feedback and proposed amendments for a joint statement." However, he said that a consensus was not reached, which led Borrell to issue the statement on his own behalf.[^cite_note-302]

The [President of the European Commission](24468.md), [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) condemned "the military operation by Azerbaijan against the Armenian population of Nagorno-Karabakh and reaffirmed the need to respect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Armenia". #### United Nations

United Nations spokesman [Stéphane Dujarric](10302459.md) told [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) that the situation in Nagorno-Karabakh was "very concerning". He urged both sides to halt hostilities and go back to "sustained dialogue".[^cite_note-\:52-298] The UN Secretary-General's Special Adviser on the Prevention of Genocide, [Alice Wairimu Nderitu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Wairimu_Nderitu), expressed alarm at the military escalation by Azerbaijan, saying that: "Military action can only contribute to escalate what is already a tense situation and to put the civilian population in the area at risk of violence, including risk of genocide and related atrocity crimes."[^cite_note-305]

The [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) adopted a joint statement on 12 October, signed by delegates from countries including Armenia, United Kingdom, Germany, Canada, United States, France and Japan[^cite_note-306] expressing concern over the humanitarian and human rights crisis in Nagorno-Karabakh. Urging both Armenia and Azerbaijan to offer the [OHCHR](22463.md) assistance and ensure the rights and safety of those remaining in the region. They also urged compliance with interim measures adopted by the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) and provisional measures of the International Court. Lastly, the statement called for continued humanitarian assistance to those displaced by the crisis and for international access to Nagorno-Karabakh, supporting dialogue among all parties to ensure comprehensive and lasting peace.[^cite_note-307][^cite_note-308]

#### Council of Europe

The [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) stated that it was "extremely concerned about the serious humanitarian and human rights situation in Nagorno-Karabakh".[^cite_note-309]

The Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights [Dunja Mijatović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunja_Mijatovi%C4%87) visited Armenia and Azerbaijan, including the Karabakh region, from 16 to 23 October 2023, and published on 12 January 2024 her observations. Testimonies provided to the Commissioner by Karabakh Armenians reveal a deep-rooted fear for their lives and future amid armed conflicts, exacerbated by Azerbaijan's control resulting from unresolved past atrocities and ongoing intimidation. Feeling abandoned by all parties and with no security guarantees, the heightened vulnerability experienced during the blockade, and the unexpected reopening of the Lachin corridor in late September 2023, prompted Karabakh Armenians to believe that leaving the region immediately was the only option available to ensure their survival and future well-being. The Commissioner also stated that all displaced persons have the right to return to their homes, regardless of whether they have been displaced internally or across borders, and that both Armenia and Azerbaijan have the obligation to ensure that any return is voluntary, safe and dignified.[^cite_note-310][^cite_note-311]

### Foreign involvement

#### Belarus

According to leaked documents, Belarus provided advanced weaponry to Azerbaijan, including artillery and drones, that were used in this conflict, prompting Armenian withdrawal from the [Collective Security Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization) in June 2024.[^cite_note-354]

#### Israel

[Israel](9282173.md) is one of the major arms exporters to Azerbaijan, accounting for 27% of Azerbaijan's major arms imports from 2011 to 2020, and 69% of Azerbaijan's major imports from 2016 to 2020, according to research from the [Stockholm International Peace Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_International_Peace_Research_Institute).[^cite_note-355] According to Efraim Inbar, an expert on [Israel-Azerbaijan relations](11716339.md) and president of the [Jerusalem Institute for Strategy and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Institute_for_Strategy_and_Security), ties between the two countries have grown stronger since the [2020 war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War).[^cite_note-356]

The offensive involving heavy artillery, rocket launchers and drones were suspected to be largely supplied by Israel alongside Turkey. Weeks before the offensive, Azerbaijani military cargo planes repeatedly flew between a southern Israeli airbase and an airfield near Nagorno-Karabakh, according to flight tracking data and Armenian diplomats. Armenia's ambassador to Israel Arman Akopian said that he voiced his concerns about Israeli weaponry shipments to the Israeli government.[^cite_note-357][^cite_note-358]

#### Turkey

An official of the [Turkish Ministry of National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defense_\(Turkey\)) stated that while [Turkey](11125639.md) was using "all means" to support Azerbaijan, including the modernisation of the Azerbaijani military and military training, denied that the country played a direct role in the offensive,[^cite_note-359] instead limiting its support for Azerbaijan to diplomatic means.[^cite_note-360]

#### Armenia

Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan stated that the [Armenian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Armed_Forces) were not involved in the fighting and that its forces were not stationed in [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md).[^cite_note-\:5-89] Armenia's [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_of_Armenia) accused Azerbaijani officials of spreading misinformation, saying that there is no Armenian military equipment or personnel present in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-\:5-89][^cite_note-361]

## Analysis

Various political analysts and Artsakh residents consider Azerbaijan's underlying goal for the offensive to be [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing).[^cite_note-\:0222-35][^cite_note-OCM-36]

[Luis Moreno Ocampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Moreno_Ocampo), the first [Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court](33903993.md), warned that another [Armenian genocide](140376.md) was about to take place. Citing the 1948 [Genocide Convention](143988.md), he stated that Azerbaijan was purposely inflicting living conditions to physically destroy a specific group by blocking the Lachin corridor, and was proceeding to kill and cause "serious bodily and mental harm". Moreno Ocampo stated the inaction of the international community would encourage Azerbaijan in the belief that it would face no serious consequences for committing [genocide](12441.md). He also discredited Aliyev's claims that his regime was not seeking ethnic cleansing, pointing out that Aliyev regularly referred to Armenia as "[Western Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Azerbaijan_\(political_concept\))" and claimed "present-day Armenia is our land".[^cite_note-washingtonpost-genocide-362]

[Thomas de Waal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_de_Waal) said that Azerbaijan was emboldened to start its offensive during a downturn in [relations between Russia and Armenia](13875647.md), and the loss of the Russian peacekeeping force's "best commanders" to the [invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). He also said that Russia could use such a crisis to instigate regime change in Armenia.[^cite_note-apkarabakh22-79]

A group of 123 Turkish academics released a statement warning the international community about Azerbaijan's intentions to commit genocide in Nagorno-Karabakh. In response, the Federation of Eurasian Turkic Associations called for the academics to have their citizenship revoked and for them to be expelled to Armenia.[^cite_note-363]

Belarusian Nobel Prize laureate [Ales Bialiatski](31971174.md) coined the term "Azerbaijanization" to describe [NATO](21133.md) member countries' tendency to ignore violations of human rights when it suits their interests.[^cite_note-364]

[Sergey Radchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Radchenko), a historian on [Cold War](325329.md) politics and professor at [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University), questioned the lack of international intervention in Nagorno-Karabakh, comparing the situation to that of [NATO's intervention in Kosovo in 1999](322473.md).[^cite_note-365]

Nikolay Mitrokhin, a researcher at the [University of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bremen), attributed the speed of Azerbaijan's victory to its demographic superiority over both Nagorno-Karabakh and Armenia and the failure of the latter to strengthen their defenses and strategies against the modernized Azerbaijani military. He also attributed the collapse of the Nagorno-Karabakh military to the weakness and corruption of both the Nagorno-Karabakh and [Armenian economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Armenia) which turned away potential investors in their militaries and noted that these factors had already contributed to the previous Azerbaijani victory in 2020.[^cite_note-366]
