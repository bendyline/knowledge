In September 2025, large-scale [anti-corruption](58111406.md) protests and [demonstrations](6787314.md) took place all across [Nepal](171166.md), predominantly organized by [Generation Z](4015968.md) students and young citizens.[^cite_note-9] Known as the "**Gen Z Revolution**"[^cite_note-10] or "**Third People's Movement**",[^cite_note-\:1-11] they began parallel to a nationwide ban on numerous social media platforms including [YouTube](3524766.md), [Instagram](31591547.md), [TikTok](56822861.md), [X](9988187.md), [Facebook](7529378.md), and [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp), and were motivated by the public's frustration with [corruption](69277289.md) and display of wealth by [government](19233409.md) officials and their families, as well as allegations of mismanagement of [public funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_funds).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The movement expanded to encompass broader issues of governance, transparency, and political accountability, and became notable for the central role that social media and digital platforms played in organizing demonstrations and facilitating public deliberation during the political transition.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-apnews1-17][^cite_note-18] The situation escalated, with [police violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_violence) against children and hospitals,[^cite_note-lancet-19] protests against public officials, and vandalism of government and political buildings taking place throughout the country.

According to a report[^cite_note-20] by [Himal Southasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himal_Southasian), this event marked the highest single-day death toll from a public protest in Nepal's history.

On 9 September 2025, Prime Minister [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md), along with a few government ministers, resigned. On 12 September, [Sushila Karki](50200885.md) was appointed the interim [prime minister of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Nepal). The protests subsided by 13 September.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

There were 76 deaths. According to the [Nepali Army](12593188.md)'s official report, of those people, 22 were protesters, 3 were police officers, and 10 were prisoners who were shot to prevent their escape.[^cite_note-23]

## Background

On 4 September 2025, the [Government of Nepal](19233409.md) ordered the shutdown of 26 social media platforms, including [Facebook](7529378.md), [X](9988187.md), [YouTube](3524766.md), [LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn), [Reddit](3829005.md), [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_\(software\)), and [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat), for failing to register under the [Ministry of Communication and Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Communications_\(Nepal\))'s new rules.[^cite_note-apnews1-17] According to *People's Dispatch*, the registration requirement had been motivated in part as a way to enable the enforcement of a new Digital Services Tax and stricter [value-added tax](52177473.md) rules on foreign e-service providers in an effort to boost revenue.[^cite_note-peoplesdispatch-24] However, critics alleged the shutdown was prompted by a [social media trend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Internet_phenomena#Politics) highlighting [nepotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepotism), focusing on the privileges enjoyed by the children and relatives—nicknamed "[Nepo Babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepo_baby)" or "Nepo Kids"—of influential political leaders.[^cite_note-nk1-25]

According to *People's Dispatch*, the media platform ban had a significant impact on Nepalese [political economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy). Of the Nepalese GDP, 33% comes from [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance), with hundreds of thousands of exit permits being issued, alongside 20.8% youth unemployment. These remittances keep thousands of households afloat and pay import bills, and also indicate a lack of structural transformation in the domestic economy toward an employment-first model, pushing the youth into work in online spaces.[^cite_note-peoplesdispatch-24] Banning social media thus threatened youth livelihood.

Prior to the protests, the average Nepali citizen made US\$1,400 per year,[^cite_note-AlJa-26] while families of the country's ruling elite displayed their wealth on social media. This "Nepo Kid" trend prompted significant public anger, particularly from [Generation Z](4015968.md) users.[^cite_note-nk1-25][^cite_note-27] The median age of Nepal's population is 25, meaning that a large part of the population is in Gen Z, the age group that uses social media the most. This relatively young population, combined with the country's largely rural, rough terrain and substantial migration abroad, leads to Nepal having one of the highest social media usage rates in South Asia, about 48% of Nepalese using social media.[^cite_note-28]

According to *[Himal Southasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himal_Southasian)*, the protests were "a long time coming," fueled by a "long series of corruption scandals." involving the political and business establishment, which reinforced a "dismal view of the system." The report further noted that while Nepal has progressed since the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Civil_War), the process has been "slow and tortuous," with public services remaining poor despite high tax burdens.[^cite_note-29]

## Digital coordination

Youth participatory groups, particularly Hami Nepal[^cite_note-30] (a [non-governmental organization](46539.md)), used [Discord online communities](49271581.md#Servers) (called "servers" in Discord)[^cite_note-31] and [Instagram](31591547.md) channels as central organizing tools in the Gen Z protests.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-AJE_GenZ_Discord_PM-33] According to *Green European Journal*, the Discord server rapidly evolved into the movement's primary communications hub before the demonstrations. Volunteers organized dedicated channels for media outreach, legal assistance, medical response, fact-checking, documentation, regional coordination and newcomer support.[^cite_note-\:11-34] Participants also collaboratively prepared protest guidelines, assigned volunteer responsibilities and discussed contingency plans for possible internet disruptions.

[Shaswot Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaswot_Lamichhane?action=edit\&redlink=1), who established the Discord server[^cite_note-NYT_politics_chat_room-35] and served as the [moderator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderator_\(communications\)) manager,[^cite_note-36] was a recent graduate with a background in science and technology.[^cite_note-ICTFrame-37] He was involved in the technical coordination that underpinned these exercises in digital democracy, explaining that the discussions were meant to simulate a "mini-election", emphasizing that it was intended to gauge people's preferences rather than constitute a binding election or formal appointment process[^cite_note-NYT_politics_chat_room-35][^cite_note-\:11-34].&#x20;

Organizers incorporated [QR codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QR_code) into protest banners so that photographs distributed by news articles would also direct viewers to the Discord server.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-\:11-34] They additionally coordinated outreach to approximately 140 social media influencers before the protests to broaden participation despite the government's social media restrictions. [VPNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VPN) and posted flyers were also used to evade the social media ban.[^cite_note-39]

According to several news outlets, messages in the **Youth Against Corruption** Discord channel advocated for violence against the Nepalese leadership.[^cite_note-indiatoday-6][^cite_note-40] Tactical discussions on Discord included procurement and use of [Molotov cocktails](20922.md), suggestions to seize ammunition from police stations, and instructions on disabling airplane tires using [acetylene gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylene). After police response led to 19 deaths, organizers in some Discord servers asked members to stop attending classes. They called for an indefinite closure of colleges and schools until the government accepted accountability for the fatalities.[^cite_note-41] However, *Green European Journal*, based on an analysis of the archived Discord logs, reported that the server had initially functioned as the movement's principle coordination hub, with dedicated channels for logistics, legal support, medical response, media outreach and fact-checking. It also reported that moderators urged protesters to remain peaceful and attempted to regain control after violence broke out, but that the rapid influx of members following the police shootings overwhelmed moderation efforts and allowed increasingly violent messages to proliferate.[^cite_note-\:11-34]

After Oli's resignation,[^cite_note-pm-oli-resigns-2025-sepetember-1] the army's chiefs met with Hami Nepal and asked them to suggest nominees for an interim leader who would oversee national elections. On a Discord server with over 100,000 members,[^cite_note-NYT_politics_chat_room-35] more than 10,000 users[^cite_note-AJE_GenZ_Discord_PM-33] met virtually in a Discord channel[^cite_note-42] to debate. The discord server had sub channels for constitutional law, fact-checking and candidate research.[^cite_note-\:11-34] After discussions, several polls, and the use of sub-channels for [fact-checking](595273.md),[^cite_note-AJE_GenZ_Discord_PM-33] the members settled on [Sushila Karki](50200885.md).

Supporters of the process were seen by *Al Jazeera English* as viewing the online process as "a revolutionary counter to the traditional practice of politicians choosing leaders behind closed doors, which had displayed little transparency".[^cite_note-AJE_GenZ_Discord_PM-33]

Sudan Gurung, founder of the youth organization Hami Nepal coordinated online discussions through Discord and served as a liaison between protest organizers and the Nepali Army during the transition period that followed Prime Minister K. P. Sharma Oli's resignation.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

### Digital privacy and nonviolent tactics

Scholars of nonviolent resistance argue that digital literacy and secure communication have become increasingly central to sustaining nonviolent campaigns in the twenty-first century.[^cite_note-\:5-46] In Nepal, the 2025 Gen Z protests began as youth-led demonstrations against a government ban on major social media platforms and long-standing corruption and inequality, and were initially framed by organizers as peaceful rallies and symbolic actions.[^cite_note-\:6-47]

The shutdown of 26 platforms, including Facebook, YouTube and WhatsApp, heightened fears of surveillance and content removal.[^cite_note-\:6-47][^cite_note-\:7-48] Drawing on wider patterns of “digital repression”, activists reported that mainstream platforms felt heavily monitored and vulnerable to takedowns.[^cite_note-\:7-48] Youth organizations therefore relied on platforms such as Discord and Instagram as central tools for coordination, using Discord's server structure, channels and role-based permissions to manage access, share logistical information and hold large-scale discussions about strategy.[^cite_note-\:6-47] Some organizers also used flyers with QR codes and virtual private networks (VPNs) to direct supporters to these spaces and to bypass platform restrictions.[^cite_note-\:6-47]

At the same time, reports on Discord chat logs indicate that some users also discussed more confrontational tactics, including Molotov cocktails and attacks on infrastructure, revealing tensions within the movement over how far to escalate.[^cite_note-\:6-47] Analysts of nonviolent resistance note that such debates are common in contemporary protest waves, where movements often combine mass peaceful mobilization with episodes of rioting or property destruction.[^cite_note-\:5-46] The Nepalese case highlights how Gen Z activists have intertwined nonviolent ideals with concerns about digital security, seeking to protect both physical protesters and their online communities while challenging entrenched political elites.[^cite_note-\:5-46][^cite_note-\:6-47][^cite_note-\:7-48]

## International media outreach and narrative framing

Using [open-source intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_intelligence) (OSINT) methodology, the Nepalese media watchdog platform *Media Kurakani* analyzed over 60 TikTok videos to document how youth citizen journalism drove global media coverage during the demonstrations.[^cite_note-MediaKurakaniOSINT-49] As police responded with force on 8 September 2025, protesters livestreamed unedited smartphone footage and created English-language videos directly appealing to international outlets—including [CNN](62028.md), the [BBC](19344654.md), [The New York Times](30680.md), [Al Jazeera](30204052.md), and [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News)—demanding media attention as it is.[^cite_note-MediaKurakaniOSINT-49]

Although major global outlets initially reported the events primarily as a reaction to the government's social media ban, Gen Z activists conducted an online campaign targeting foreign journalists to correct the framing.[^cite_note-MediaKurakaniOSINT-49] Demonstrators asserted that the primary drivers of the movement were systemic government corruption, political nepotism, and police brutality, prompting international outlets to update their coverage accordingly.[^cite_note-MediaKurakaniOSINT-49] Digital appeals directed at international human rights bodies also contributed to public statements from the [United Nations](31769.md) calling for transparent investigations into police conduct.[^cite_note-MediaKurakaniOSINT-49]

## Timeline

### 8 September

Large gatherings took place in [Kathmandu](17168.md),[^cite_note-\:1-11] particularly at [Maitighar Mandala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitighar_Mandala) and around the [federal parliament building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_Centre,_Nepal), New Baneshwor, with tens of thousands of participants.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The protests were originally organized as a peaceful rally by Hami Nepal,[^cite_note-indiatoday-6] an [NGO](46539.md)[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] whose website highlights earthquake relief projects.[^cite_note-54] Anil Baniya, one of the protest organizers from Hami Nepal, said the government cracked down on the protesters after some protesters began to climb the walls of the parliament complex and others threw stones. Baniya stated that what began as a peaceful protest was hijacked by "external forces and political party cadres" but that regardless, the government should not have responded to stones being thrown with live ammunition from the armed police.[^cite_note-AlJa-26] The [Straw Hat Pirates' Jolly Roger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_Hat_Pirates'_Jolly_Roger) flag from the [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga) series *[One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)* was used by some protesters, in similar fashion to the [2025 Indonesian protests](79252449.md).[^cite_note-55]

Emerging as a decentralized movement, the protest grew and evolved to a movement with no formal leadership, individuals joined voluntarily in opposition to corruption and the social media ban. The demonstrations escalated when protesters attempted to enter the [Federal Parliament of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Parliament_of_Nepal), leading [security forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Armed_Forces) to respond with tear gas, water cannons, rubber bullets, and live ammunition.[^cite_note-bbc-56] Video Geolocation published in the evening showed numerous victims killed in front of the [parliament building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_Centre,_Nepal). Victims were seen lying inanimate on the ground, many with serious or fatal wounds to the head and torso, many wearing school uniforms. Videos showed victims with their faces blown off. An expert identified bullets fired as coming from a lightweight [7.62x51mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7.62%C3%9751mm_NATO) automatic rifle.[^cite_note-france24-19sep-injuryupdate-57] The Kathmandu District Administration Office imposed a [curfew](297681.md) in parts of the capital near government buildings. However, protests continued.[^cite_note-AlJa-26]

The confrontations resulted in at least 19 people killed and more than 347 people injured.[^cite_note-kathmandu-post-58][^cite_note-bbc-56][^cite_note-59] Seventeen of those protesters were killed in the capital.[^cite_note-france24-19sep-injuryupdate-57] The [National Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Human_Rights_Commission_\(Nepal\)) called on authorities not to use excessive force and to "show restraint in handling the protests."[^cite_note-60]

In the evening, the government lifted the ban on social media platforms.[^cite_note-ie2025-61] Home Minister [Ramesh Lekhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesh_Lekhak) resigned and got his passport seized.[^cite_note-fp2025-62] A curfew was imposed in several major cities including Kathmandu, [Birgunj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgunj), [Bhairahawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairahawa), [Butwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butwal), [Pokhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara), [Itahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itahari), and [Damak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damak_Municipality).[^cite_note-id202502-63] Following this, protests for the complete dissolution of parliament began. This was primarily caused by the initial government response to the original protests.[^cite_note-AlJa-26]

### 9 September



Nepalese Parliament building damaged of fire set by protesters on 20 September 2025.

Prime Minister [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md) began the day with a call to all parties to cease violent action, but he did not respond to the corruption allegations.[^cite_note-R3sign-2] Then he directed his [Communist Party of Nepal (Unified Marxist–Leninist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Nepal_\(Unified_Marxist%E2%80%93Leninist\)) ministers not to resign their posts and resigned his own office.[^cite_note-R3sign-2][^cite_note-r1-64] Afterwards, Oli fled to an army barracks outside of Kathmandu Valley as per his interview in Kantipur's fireside.[^cite_note-65] Videos surfaced of him escaping in a helicopter, with rumors of a potential escape to India or Dubai. There were conflicting reports on whether President [Ram Chandra Paudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Chandra_Paudel) resigned as well, as the Nepalese Army denied reports of Paudel's resignation.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] Despite this, protests continued nationwide. Protestors set fire to many government buildings: part of [Singha Durbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singha_Durbar), the administrative headquarters of Nepal;[^cite_note-69] the adjacent building of the [Supreme Court of Nepal](2707227.md);[^cite_note-70] the president's residence at [Sital Niwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sital_Niwas);[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] the prime minister's residence at [Baluwatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluwatar,_Kathmandu);[^cite_note-74] and the headquarters of the Communist Party UML.[^cite_note-next-75] Multiple reports allege that Nepali Army commander [Ashok Raj Sigdel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashok_Raj_Sigdel) advised Oli to resign in order for the Army to restore peace.[^cite_note-76]

Protest actions targeted several prominent locations in Kathmandu, including the residences of the prime minister and the president, as well as the homes of various government ministers and members of parliament, which were set ablaze by protesters.[^cite_note-77] The parliament building was set on fire and looted.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] The headquarters of the UML and [Nepali Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_Congress) was vandalized, with party flags stripped and burned.[^cite_note-80] Security forces, including the [Nepali Army](12593188.md), facilitated the safe evacuation of politicians from affected areas and evacuated to [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) (TIA), while attempting to control the escalating incidents of arson and destruction.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Minister of Agriculture [Ram Nath Adhikari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Nath_Adhikari) and Health Minister [Pradip Paudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pradip_Paudel) along with 21 MPs from the [Rastriya Swatantra Party](72312845.md) resigned,[^cite_note-news242025-83] along with all the MPs of the [Rastriya Prajatantra Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastriya_Prajatantra_Party).[^cite_note-84] Several members left Oli's UML party.[^cite_note-85] Protestors went to TIA to block the political leaders from fleeing. The airport was closed and occupied by the Nepali Army.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] There was ensuing confusion as hundreds of travelers were left stranded in the TIA prompted airlines to order personnel to keep all individuals sheltered within the airport.[^cite_note-\:2-88][^cite_note-89] Scheduled international flights were diverted to Pokhara or cancelled.[^cite_note-90] *[Kantipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantipur_\(daily\))* media house was set on fire in the early evening.[^cite_note-91] The online news portal was stopped due to damage in its server, forcing it to post in Facebook as an alternative.[^cite_note-92] Other buildings burned by protesters were the new [Hilton Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Hotel) in Kathmandu and the [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Population_\(Nepal\)) building in Ramshahpath.[^cite_note-93]

Several political leaders and their residences were attacked. Former prime minister [Sher Bahadur Deuba](623301.md) and his wife, foreign minister [Arzu Rana Deuba](23500386.md), were heavily injured while their house was set on fire where protestors found large amounts of US Dollars and Nepali Rupees. They were handed over to the police by protestors.[^cite_note-94] Deputy prime minister [Prakash Man Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakash_Man_Singh)'s house and vehicle were set on fire,[^cite_note-95] followed by the house of former president [Bidya Devi Bhandari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidya_Devi_Bhandari) at Bhangal, Kathmandu.[^cite_note-96] Former prime minister [Jhala Nath Khanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhala_Nath_Khanal)'s residence was also set on fire, critically injuring his wife Ravi Laxmi Chitrakar.[^cite_note-97] The residence of Ramesh Lekhak was also set on fire in Naikap, Kathmandu.[^cite_note-98] In [Rupandehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupandehi) district, the houses of local politicians [Bal Krishna Khand](16975806.md), Bhoj Prasad Shrestha and several mayors were burned.[^cite_note-99] In [Chitwan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitwan_District), the house of former prime minister [Prachanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prachanda) was set on fire.[^cite_note-100] His daughter Ganga Dahal's house was also set on fire at Lalitpur. A person was found dead the next day in the house.[^cite_note-सेनाले-101] In [Hetauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetauda), several government offices were vandalized and set on fire.[^cite_note-102] In [Karnali Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnali_Province), the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnali_Provincial_Assembly) building and the residence of chief minister [Yam Lal Kandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_Lal_Kandel) was set on fire.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] In [Biratnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biratnagar), the capital of [Koshi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshi_Province), the district office and district court were set on fire, followed by the residence of the chief minister and cabinet ministers.[^cite_note-105]

The prison in [Kailali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailali) district was attacked by the protestors. All inmates fled.[^cite_note-106] [Rabi Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Lamichhane) was set free from the [Nakhu Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhu_Jail) in Kathmandu, the prison was later set on fire; all prisoners fled.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] [Kaski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaski_District) prison was also set on fire, resulting in the escape of 773 inmates.[^cite_note-109] In [Banke District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banke_District), five inmates were killed after security forces opened fire during a jailbreak at a juvenile correctional facility.[^cite_note-banke-110] The building of the [Road Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Roads_\(Nepal\)) was severely damaged after being set on fire by protestors,[^cite_note-111] along with the office of [Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority](40082311.md).[^cite_note-112] The lower station of the [Chandragiri Cable Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragiri_Cable_Car) was also set on fire, along with the CG Electronics Digital Park, Balambu and Thankot Police Station.[^cite_note-113]

Several protesters were injured. Thirty-three were treated in the Teaching Hospital.[^cite_note-114] Three policemen died in [Koteshwor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koteshwor).[^cite_note-115]

Curfew was imposed in several cities including [Kathmandu](17168.md), [Birgunj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgunj), [Bhairahawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairahawa), [Butwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butwal), [Pokhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara), [Itahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itahari), and [Damak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damak_Municipality).[^cite_note-id202502-63] Additionally, the police and armed forces began going house-by-house near the protests searching for protesters, and raided the [Civil Service Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Service_Hospital) where wounded and dying protesters were taken after being shot or otherwise beaten by government forces.[^cite_note-AlJa-26] Tear gas was shot into the hospital.[^cite_note-lancet-19]

A friendly football match between Nepal and [Bangladesh](3454.md) that was scheduled for 9 September at the Dasharath Stadium was cancelled because of the events in Nepal.[^cite_note-116]

Talks about restoring the [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Nepal) re-emerged, with former king [Gyanendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyanendra_of_Nepal) emerging as a "symbol of resistance for those disillusioned with the current political system", particularly from the royalist opposition to the post [Nepali civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_civil_war) governments. Earlier in the year, [monarchists protested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nepalese_pro-monarchy_protests), calling for a restoration, ending with a heavy crackdown that caused at least two deaths.[^cite_note-117] However, organizers urged not to resurface pro-monarchy sentiment, nor hold the Nepali flag as they believed it was a royal form of "ultranationalism." Authors and scholars began to debate the actual intention of these comments, and whether protesters really wanted a restoration of the monarchy.[^cite_note-118]

At around 22\:00 [NPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Standard_Time), the Nepali Army stated that it would "take charge" of the country in the absence of the prime minister to ensure "law and order" is maintained.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] The press release called for cooperation as they deployed troops later that evening into the early hours of the following day.[^cite_note-121] Later in the evening, the Chief of Army Staff Ashok Raj Sigdel appealed to the agitating groups for talks.[^cite_note-122]

### 10 September



Burnt out revenue office in Chitwan, 9 September 2025

In the early morning, [Nepali Army](12593188.md) forces were seen patrolling neighborhoods in critical areas.[^cite_note-123] General Sigdel again encouraged protesters to remain peaceful as the government attempts to restore peace.[^cite_note-\:2-88][^cite_note-124] Between approximately 10\:00 NPT on 9 September and 22\:00 NPT on 10 September, the Army announced detention of 27 individuals on counts of looting, arson, and other "destructive and anarchic activities" and seizing weaponry.[^cite_note-125] Later reports counted 31 firearms.[^cite_note-banke-110]

In some places, protestors continued raiding government buildings and politicians' homes through the night, with one video showing a group of protestors taking firearms from homes, including what is believed to be a [GSG-522](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Sport_Guns_GSG-5) carbine and a bolt-action rifle.[^cite_note-banke-110][^cite_note-126] The pace was slowed down by noon. A group of protestors started cleaning the street near the parliament building that was set on fire the previous day.[^cite_note-auto-127] Many army weapons were captured by protesters. One video included [armor vests](149058.md), [L1A1 SLR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L1A1_Self-Loading_Rifle) rifles, 37mm [Webley & Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webley_&_Scott) "Schermuly" [grenade launchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenade_launcher), [INSAS 1B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSAS_rifle) rifles, [Lee–Enfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee%E2%80%93Enfield) rifles, and [Ishapore 1A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishapore_1A1) rifles.[^cite_note-128]

Protestors held online discussions on [Discord](49271581.md) to select an interim leader. Around 10\:00 pm NPT, the group voted and selected [Sushila Karki](50200885.md).[^cite_note-129] [Balen Shah](70833999.md), mayor of Kathmandu, and [Kul Man Ghising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kul_Man_Ghising) were other candidates. Balen reportedly did not pick up the phone.[^cite_note-130] [Durga Prasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Prasai) separately met with the army chief, but no concrete conclusion was met.[^cite_note-131] Karki confirmed her willingness to accept the role in an interview with [News18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network18_Group).[^cite_note-132]

In Pokhara, two dead bodies were found inside a burned house at Nayabazar and Lakeside.[^cite_note-auto-127]

Police stated that as of 10 September, more than 13,500 prisoners had escaped from prisons nationwide of them, one was Rabi Lamichhane, former home minister of Nepal and convicted to prison for fraud.[^cite_note-133] Soldiers arrested 303 of those prisoners and handed them over to police. Seventy-three rifles were also returned.[^cite_note-सेनाले-101] In [Dhading District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhading_District), two inmates were shot dead and seven injured by soldiers when they tried to flee,[^cite_note-134] while soldiers also thwarted an attempted jailbreak in Kathmandu's main prison.[^cite_note-135]

TIA's closure was extended late in the morning, but eventually resumed operations.[^cite_note-banke-110][^cite_note-136]

*[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that more than 100,000 citizens were using [Discord](49271581.md), an online communication software, to select a nominee for interim leader. The server, described as "the Parliament of Nepal", had jumped to 145,000 members. The server had reportedly settled on Sushila Karki, the country's former chief justice, and proposed her name for meetings with the Army.[^cite_note-NYT_politics_chat_room-35]

### 11 September

In the morning a meeting was held that included President [Ram Chandra Paudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Chandra_Paudel), Nepali Army Chief [Ashok Raj Sigdel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashok_Raj_Sigdel) and Gen Z representatives. The meeting was held at Bhadrakali army headquarters as part of efforts to select an interim leader. The Gen Z group advocated for [Sushila Karki](50200885.md) to be interim prime minister. [Balendra Shah](70833999.md), Kul Man Ghising, and mayor of [Dharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharan), [Harka Raj Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harka_Raj_Rai) were also considered.[^cite_note-137]

The [Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist Centre)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Nepal_\(Maoist_Centre\)) announced its support for the protests, with party chairperson [Pushpa Kamal Dahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushpa_Kamal_Dahal) urging protestors to work within the democratic framework.[^cite_note-138]

Meanwhile, the death toll increased to 34, while more than 1,300 people were injured, according to [Nepal's Ministry of Health and Population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Population_\(Nepal\)) (MoHP).[^cite_note-139]

### 12 September

According to police spokesperson Binod Ghimire, the dead include 21 protesters, nine prisoners, three police officers, and 18 others.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] Ghimire confirmed that more than 12,500 fugitives remain at large, after escaping from various prisons in Nepal. Some tried to cross into India, where they were apprehended by [Indian border forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sashastra_Seema_Bal).[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]



Map of reported protest locations following 4 September ban

[Sushila Karki](50200885.md), a former Supreme Court chief justice, was inaugurated as interim prime minister. She became the first [female prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_elected_and_appointed_female_heads_of_state_and_government) in the history of Nepal.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-\:3-145] Shortly afterwards, president Poudel dissolved parliament and set 5 March as the date for [the next election](69672716.md).[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

### 13 September

The curfew was lifted in the Kathmandu area.[^cite_note-148] Prime Minister Karki began visiting injured protesters in hospitals.[^cite_note-149] [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle) reported that calm appeared to have returned to the streets of the capital, and that normal daily activities had resumed.[^cite_note-150]

## Aftermath

### Casualties and emergency response

At least 76 people were killed in the protests and its aftermath.[^cite_note-\:76-7] According to the Nepal Army's official report, of the 76 killed, 22 were protesters, three police officers, and 10 prisoners who were shot to prevent their escape.[^cite_note-151] On 1 May 2026, Nepal Government officially confirmed that 2,660 were injured during the protest.[^cite_note-152]

Search teams were dispatched to recover bodies from all structures set on fire, according to the MoHP.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] A day of national mourning for the victims was held on 17 September 2025.[^cite_note-155]

Protests destroyed numerous public offices, destroying equipment of essential services. Spared offices continued to operate normally while burned offices pitched tents and scraped together materials to resume services.[^cite_note-156]

In February 2026, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md), [Amnesty International](18947898.md), and the [International Commission of Jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_of_Jurists) called on the interim government and all parties participating in the March 2026 elections to "commit to end impunity for rights abuses and corruption by upholding the rule of law, including by successfully completing the transitional justice process." Reports on human rights abuses leading up to the Gen Z protests as well as reports on abuses during and since the protests have not been published according to Human Rights Watch.[^cite_note-157]

#### Selected individual injuries

**Shantanu Dhakal**, an 18-year-old student, was shot through his left jaw by police using [live ammunition](18940621.md) during an anti-corruption protest in Itahari, Nepal, on September 8, 2025. Dhakal was immediately taken to the Teaching Hospital for treatment.

According to Dhakal's report on the YouTube channel EON, he had finished school that day and went to join a peaceful protest in Itahari. He was standing in front of the Itahari Police Station. The protest was held at the Itahari Sub-Metropolitan Office area, and it was calm at first. However, the situation suddenly changed when Dhakal heard gunfire a few meters away. The crowd became unstable, and two students were killed. One died on the spot near the Itahari Sub-Metropolitan City Office, while the other was taken to BP Koirala Institute of Health Sciences in Dharan and later died. Dhakal heard another wave of gunshots and ran about 70 to 80 meters away.[^cite_note-\:8-158] He was shot in the left jaw and his left side molars were crushed, but he maintained consciousness.[^cite_note-\:9-159][^cite_note-New_Normal-160][^cite_note-\:10-161][^cite_note-\:8-158] He was taken to the nearest medical center for first aid and then moved to Birat Teaching Hospital by ambulance.[^cite_note-\:8-158] He walked to the ambulance while still bleeding. After being discharged from the hospital, he said to his friends who were with him: "There is a limit of pain in human minds, if that exceed any part of human body doesn't feel pain at all".[^cite_note-\:9-159][^cite_note-New_Normal-160][^cite_note-\:10-161] He could not speak for a few weeks because of the injury.[^cite_note-\:8-158] He spent three weeks in hospital for surgeries and treatment. Once back home, as he spent time in recovery, he decided to document online his [near-death experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-death_experience) and life in the aftermath of the protests.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-New_Normal-160]

During his recovery, Dhakal used social media to share his story, including images of himself with a [tracheostomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracheotomy) tube and cheek wound. His posts, which included details about the challenges of basic activities like brushing his teeth, provided a raw and personal look at the aftermath of political violence. And it suddenly grabbed the attention of people and the media.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-New_Normal-160] Popular Nepali singer Eleena Chauhan met Shantanu Dhakal on her Facebook, she wrote: "Despite being seriously injured in the Gen Z movement, you survived, brave Shantanu Dhakal. The courage you showed for the country and the pain you endured are greater than words. You sacrificed, raised your voice, and stood strong but sadly, not everyone has yet recognized such dedication. The bravery you showed will never go in vain, and history will remember it. Meeting you today fills me with respect! Shantanu, your life force is an inspiration for the new generation."[^cite_note-164]

### Damage to private property

In addition to public infrastructure, several private businesses were damaged during these protests. Multiple outlets of [Bhatbhateni Supermarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhat-Bhateni_Supermarket), Nepal's largest retail chains, were vandalized, looted, and set on fire in different locations, partially motivated by the owner of Bhatbhateni [Min Bahadur Gurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Bahadur_Gurung?action=edit\&redlink=1), having gifted land and a building to the UML party. Kantipur, Nepal's largest TV and radio broadcasters headquarter was also set on fire. Media reports documented extensive structural damage, financial losses, and casualties at some branches.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

### Interim government response

According to officials from Sushila Karki's interim government, any family who lost a member in the protests would receive monetary compensation of 1 million [Nepalese rupees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_rupees) (equivalent to about US\$7,000 as of September 2025), the maximum amount allowed by the law.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] Those who were injured during the protests would also be compensated. At the same meeting, she called out for unity in order to rebuild the country.[^cite_note-\:3-145]

The day Karki took office, on 14 September, she "urged calm and cooperation to rebuild" following the protests.[^cite_note-\:4-169] She also condemned the destruction caused by protests and claimed that some of the arson was premeditated. She also criticised those who caused damage and pledged to investigate conspiracies behind the violence, intending to spare nobody.[^cite_note-170] Karki said that she would remain in power for no more than six months.[^cite_note-\:4-169]

### New elections

Main article: [2026 Nepalese general election](69672716.md)

Nationwide elections were set for 5 March 2026. These elections marked the end of the interim government and the formation of a new government. A new online voter-registration system had seen over 915,000 first-time voters sign up, with more than two-thirds of them young people. There was also a surge in new political parties, and the election had 120 different parties participating. Much of the campaigning focused on new candidates seeking to win the Gen Z vote, but established politicians, including former Prime Minister Oli, were on the ballot. There were also three other former prime ministers who were leading major parties.[^cite_note-171] Approximately two-thirds of the lawmakers from the previous parliament (those serving prior to the formation of the interim government) did not run in these elections.[^cite_note-172]

Following its conclusion, [The New York Times](30680.md) described the protest as "by far Gen Z's biggest win to date".[^cite_note-173]

### Arrest of Oli and Lekhak

The newly formed [Balendra Shah](70833999.md)'s government (of the [Rastriya Swatantra Party](72312845.md) political party) arrested former Prime Minister [KP Sharma Oli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KP_Sharma_Oli) and former Home Minister [Ramesh Lekhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesh_Lekhak) over their involvement in crackdown of the protesters[^cite_note-174] based on the recommendation by Karki Commission Report.[^cite_note-175] However, on April 9, 2026, they were both released from custody on a general date following a mandamus order from the [Supreme Court of Nepal](2707227.md).[^cite_note-176]

## Reactions

### Domestic

#### Government

Former Minister of Justice [Gobinda Bandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobinda_Bandi) said the social media ban goes against the constitution and basic freedoms, including rights protected by Nepal's constitution and the [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights) (ICCPR).[^cite_note-\:0-177]

The [Rastriya Prajatantra Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastriya_Prajatantra_Party) (RPP), part of the opposition, condemned the social media ban and the police response to the protests, and called for the immediate dissolution of the [current government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Oli_cabinet).[^cite_note-178]

In a Facebook post, Kathmandu's mayor (now Prime Minister) [Balendra Shah](70833999.md) said he could not attend the protests because of the age limit set by the organizers. However, he believed it was important to listen to their voices.[^cite_note-179] President [Ram Chandra Paudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Chandra_Paudel) and the Nepalese army appealed for calm and political dialogue.[^cite_note-180]

#### Others

[Gyanendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyanendra_of_Nepal), the former [King of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Nepal), issued a statement condemning the violent crackdown on the protests and calling on the government to listen to the demands of the protesters. He further urged the protest movement to remain peaceful and avoid being hijacked by criminal elements.[^cite_note-181] Various hotels in Pokhara's lakeside area were set on fire during the protests. The Hotel Association Pokhara Nepal called for an investigation on the vandalism and for government compensation, as Pokhara's tourism industry was heavily hit.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] The Nepalese Gen-Z uprising has challenged the old-elite political system.

In October, Nepalese physicians pleaded to the [UN Human Rights Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Human_Rights_Council) to investigate the [human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_violation) against children and health-care personnel. They have asked the UN International Children's Emergency Fund and the [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) to help the victims.[^cite_note-lancet-19]

### International

#### Foreign governments

The unrest in Nepal impacted activities along the [India–Nepal border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Nepal_border). Local sources noted that markets had fallen silent, causing larger economic stagnation across neighbouring regions. Shortly after the worries, the [Maharashtra State](20629.md) Emergency Operations Centre (SEOC) issued a travel advisory on 9 September requesting Indian citizens not to travel until further notice. It made emergency helplines available and encouraged both Indian and Nepali citizens in Nepal to also remain in place.[^cite_note-\:2-88][^cite_note-184] Early in the morning of 10 September, [Air India](26457880.md), [IndiGo](2085799.md), [SpiceJet](1701189.md), and several other Indian airlines officially cancelled all flights in and out of [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport), commonly known as TIA.[^cite_note-\:2-88] After TIA was reopened later that day, IndiGo resumed flights.[^cite_note-185] However, sources from inside TIA reported that the airport was still at a standstill, with little communication of updates from authorities.[^cite_note-186]

India's [Ministry of External Affairs](10302410.md) said it was closely monitoring the protests, expressing grief over the casualties and extending condolences to the victims' families. It also wished a speedy recovery for the injured and advised Indian citizens in Nepal to follow curfew orders and safety guidelines.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

The Bangladeshi [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Bangladesh\)) stated that it was closely monitoring the situation in Nepal, while expressing condolences to the families of the deceased and wishing recovery to the injured. It also stated that it hoped a "peaceful and constructive dialogue" among "all relevant parties" in the country to resolve any kind of differences.[^cite_note-190]

The military leader of Myanmar, [Min Aung Hlaing](38278640.md), stated during a meeting with the [State Security and Peace Commission](80587873.md) that "\[lessons should be learned from the] outside interference \[in Nepal]."[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]

#### Intergovernmental organizations

United Nations Secretary-General [António Guterres](471806.md)'s spokesman, [Stéphane Dujarric](10302459.md), said the secretary-general was "deeply saddened by the loss of life" and called on the authorities to comply with international human rights law and for the protestors to respect life and property.[^cite_note-193]

[Volker Türk](71721726.md), the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, said he was "appalled" by the escalating violence and called on the security forces to exercise the utmost restraint.[^cite_note-194] He also called for an investigation into conduct of the security forces, particularly the deaths of protestors and reports of "disproportionate use of force".[^cite_note-195]

#### Human rights groups

[Amnesty International](18947898.md) condemned the crackdown and called for independent investigation and accountability on 8 September. It called for a de-escalation while urging authorities to "exercise maximum restraint" in its use of force. It supported the protesters rights to protest peacefully and demanded the government heed the "legitimate demands of the youth to end corruption" to reestablish civil liberties.[^cite_note-196]

[Human Rights Watch](46530.md) did not immediately make a statement on the protests, but suggested certain actions in a news report on 9 September. Its statements were supported by findings that the Nepali government has "a record of silencing online speech," which fueled growing discontent in younger population. Citing the ban's lifting and rampant militaristic [police violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_violence) it suggested that "Nepali authorities should promptly and impartially investigate the police use of force and appropriately discipline or prosecute all those responsible for abuses, regardless of rank." Its Asia director, Meenakshi Ganguly, underscored that the police shootings "shows the administration's appalling disregard for the lives of its own citizens and desperate need to suppress criticism." It further cited the [UN](31769.md) Basic Principles on the Use of Force and Firearms and tracking of the [UN Human Rights Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Rights_Committee) to argue that the government's use of live ammunition was extrajudicial.[^cite_note-197]

The [International Federation for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Human_Rights) and [World Organisation Against Torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Organisation_Against_Torture) released a joint statement condemning the crackdown on peaceful protests on 9 September. It called for the government to "immediately halt all forms of violation and brutality by authorities against protesters," ensure that the ban rollback was permanent through legal means, and to uphold its state party status to the [Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights](22463.md) (OHCHR) and the [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights) (ICCPR) by maintaining "respect \[for] the fundamental rights of its people–both online and offline."[^cite_note-198]
