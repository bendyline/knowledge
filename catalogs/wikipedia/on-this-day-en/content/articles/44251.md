**Dakar** ([/dɑːˈkɑːr, dæ-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [UK also](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˈdækɑːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-4] French: [\[dakaʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language): *Ndakaaru*)[^cite_note-5] is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Senegal) of [Senegal](27278.md). The [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Senegal) of Dakar has a population of 1,278,469, and the population of the Dakar metropolitan area was at 4.0 million in 2023.

Dakar is situated on the [Cap-Vert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Vert) peninsula, the westernmost point of mainland Africa.[^cite_note-6] Cap-Vert was colonized by the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) in the early 15th century. The Portuguese established a presence on the island of [Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e) off the coast of Cap-Vert and used it as a base for the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md). [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) took over the island in 1677. Following the abolition of the slave trade and French annexation of the mainland area in the 19th century, Dakar grew into a major regional port and a major city of the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire). In 1902, Dakar replaced [Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Louis,_Senegal) as the capital of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa). From 1959 to 1960, Dakar was the capital of the short-lived [Mali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation). In 1960, it became the capital of the independent Republic of Senegal. Dakar will host the [2026 Summer Youth Olympics](64557687.md).

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Dakar).

### Foundation and European Trade

The [Cap-Vert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Vert) peninsula was settled no later than the 15th century by the [Lebu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebu_people), an aquacultural subgroup of the Wolof ethnic group. The original villages—[Ouakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouakam), [Ngor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngor,_Dakar), [Yoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoff) and [Hann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hann,_Senegal)—still constitute distinctively [Lebou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebou) neighborhoods of the city today.&#x20;

In 1444, the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) reached the Bay of Dakar.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Peaceful contact was finally opened in 1456 by [Diogo Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_Gomes), and the bay was subsequently referred to as the "*Angra de Bezeguiche*" (after the name of the local ruler).[^cite_note-mota-10] The bay of "Bezeguiche" would go on to serve as a critical stop for the [Portuguese India Armadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India_Armadas) of the early 16th century, where large fleets would routinely stop, both on their outward and return journeys from India, to repair, collect fresh water from the rivulets and wells along the Cap-Vert shore and trade for provisions with the local people for their remaining voyage.[^cite_note-mota-10] (It was famously during [one of these stops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Portuguese_India_Armada_\(Cabral,_1500\)#Conference_at_Bezeguiche), in 1501, where the Florentine navigator [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci) began to construct his "[New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World)" hypothesis about America.[^cite_note-11]) A town grew on the bay that was one of the major ports of [Cayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayor), a constituent kingdom of the [Jolof Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolof_Empire) that [broke away in 1549](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Danki).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoulegue2013124-12]

The Portuguese founded a settlement on the island of [Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e) (then known as the island of Bezeguiche or Palma), which by 1536 they began to use as a base for slave exportation. A new Lebou village, called Ndakaaru, was established directly across from Gorée in the 17th century to service the European [trading factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_factory) with food and drinking water. Gorée was captured by the [United Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) in 1588, which gave it its present name (spelled *Goeree*, after [Goeree-Overflakkee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goeree-Overflakkee) in the Netherlands). The island switched hands between the Portuguese and Dutch several more times before falling to the English under [Admiral Robert Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Holmes_\(admiral\)) on January 23, 1664, and finally to the French in 1677. Though under continuous French administration since, multiracial people, descended from Dutch and French traders and African wives, dominated the slave trade. The infamous "[House of Slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Slaves)" was built at Gorée in 1776.

### Lebou Republic

By the 1780s, Dakar was one of the largest towns in Cayor.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoulegue2013440-13] In 1795, the [marabouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marabout) of Cayor revolted against the [Damel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damel), inspired by the [Futanke revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamate_of_Futa_Jallon) in [Futa Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futa_Toro). The Lebou community joined the uprising under the leadership of the Diop, a Muslim clerical family originally from [Coki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coki). After some initial victories, the Muslim armies were defeated at Loofe, and the Lebou fell back on the Cap Vert.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoulegue2013458-14] They built a series of defensive walls out of [laterite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterite) blocks, the longest of which stretched across the entire peninsula from Yoff to Hann, and successfully repulsed the Damel's army. With this victory, [Jaal Joop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaal_Joop?action=edit\&redlink=1) declared a new, independent, theocratic state, subsequently called the "Lebou Republic" by the French, with the capital at Ndakaaru.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoulegue2013459-62-15] The [Serigne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marabout) of Ndakaaru is still recognized as the traditional political authority of the Lebou by the Senegalese State today.

The slave trade was abolished by France in February 1794. However, [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) reinstated the slave trade in May 1802. The slave trade continued at Gorée until 1848, when it was finally abolished throughout all French territories. To replace trade in slaves, the French promoted peanut cultivation on the mainland. As the peanut trade boomed, tiny Gorée Island, whose population had grown to 6,000 residents, proved ineffectual as a port. Traders from Gorée decided to move to the mainland, and a "factory" with warehouses was established in [Rufisque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufisque) in 1840. In 1857, the French established a military post at Ndakaaru (which they called "Dakar") and annexed the Lebou Republic, though its institutions continued to function nominally.

### Colonial Dakar



A public water well, 1899

Large public expenditure for infrastructure was allocated by the colonial authorities to Dakar's development. The port facilities were improved with jetties, a telegraph line was established along the coast to [Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Louis,_Senegal), and the [Dakar-Saint-Louis railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Saint-Louis_railway) was completed in 1885, at which point the city became an important base for the conquest of the [Western Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sudan).

Gorée, including Dakar, was recognised as a French *commune* in 1872. Dakar itself was split off from Gorée as a separate *commune* in 1887. The citizens of the city elected their own mayor and municipal council, and helped send an elected representative to the [National Assembly](299616.md) in Paris. Dakar replaced Saint-Louis as the capital of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) in 1902.[^cite_note-16] A second major railroad, the [Dakar-Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Niger_railway) built from 1906 to 1923, linked Dakar to Bamako and consolidated the city's position at the head of France's West African empire. In 1929, the *commune* of Gorée Island, now with only a few hundred inhabitants, was merged into Dakar.



Dakar Entrepôt. c. 1900

Urbanization during the colonial period was marked by forms of racial and social segregation—often expressed in terms of health and hygiene—which continue to structure the city today. Following a plague epidemic in 1914, the authorities forced most of the African population out of old neighborhoods, or "Plateau", and into a new quarter, called Médina, separated from it by a "sanitary cordon". As first occupants of the land, the Lebou inhabitants of the city successfully resisted this expropriation. They were supported by [Blaise Diagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Diagne), the first African to be elected Deputy to the National Assembly. Nonetheless, the Plateau thereafter became an administrative, commercial, and residential district increasingly reserved for Europeans and served as a model for similar exclusionary administrative enclaves in French Africa's other colonial capitals (Bamako, Conakry, Abidjan, Brazzaville). Meanwhile, the Layene Sufi order, established by [Seydina Mouhammadou Limamou Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seydina_Mouhammadou_Limamou_Laye), was thriving among the Lebou in Yoff and in a new village called Cambérène. Since independence, urbanization has sprawled eastward past Pikine, a commuter suburb whose population (2001 est. 1,200,000) is greater than that of Dakar proper, to Rufisque, creating a [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) of almost 3 million (over a quarter of the national population).



Harbor Dakar, 1905

In its colonial heyday, Dakar was one of the major cities of the French Empire, comparable to [Hanoi](56667.md) or [Beirut](37428.md). French trading firms established branch offices there, and industrial investments (mills, breweries, refineries, canneries) were attracted by its port and rail facilities. It was also strategically important to France, which maintained an important naval base and coaling station in its harbor, and which integrated it into its earliest air force and airmail circuits, most notably with the legendary Mermoz airfield (no longer extant).

Main article: [Battle of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dakar)

In 1940, Dakar became involved in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WW_II) when [General de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle), leader of the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) Forces, sought to make the city the base of his resistance operations. The object was to raise the Free French flag in West Africa, to occupy Dakar and thus start to consolidate the French resistance of its colonies in Africa. The plan had British naval support when fighting alone against the Axis powers. However, due to delays and the plan becoming known, Dakar had already come under the influence of the German-controlled will of the [Vichy government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France). With the arrival of French naval forces under Vichy control and faced by stubborn defences onshore, de Gaulle's proposals were resisted, and the Battle of Dakar ensued off the coast, lasting three days 23–25 September 1940, between the Vichy defences and the attack of the Free French and British navy. The enterprise was abandoned after appreciable naval losses. Although the initiative on Dakar failed, General de Gaulle was able to establish himself at [Douala](19997263.md) in the Cameroons, which became the rallying point for the resistance of the Free French cause.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]



Mural in Dakar commemorating the [Thiaroye massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiaroye_massacre). It reads "Thiaroye '44, an unforgettable event".

In November 1944, West African conscripts in the French army mutinied against poor conditions at the Thiaroye camp, on the outskirts of the city. The mutiny was seen as an indictment of the colonial system and constituted a watershed for the nationalist movement. On 1 December 1944, French soldiers guarding the camp [opened fire on the West African soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiaroye_massacre). Accounts of the death toll range from around 35 (the official French account) to over 300 (army veterans active at the time).

### Independence

Dakar was the capital of the short-lived [Mali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation) from 1959 to 1960, after which it became the capital of Senegal. The poet, philosopher and first President of [Senegal](27278.md) [Léopold Sédar Senghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor) tried to transform Dakar into the "Sub-Saharan African Athens" (l'Athènes de l'Afrique subsaharienne),[^cite_note-20] as his vision was for it.

Dakar is a major financial centre, home to a dozen national and regional banks (including the [Central Bank of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_West_African_States) (BCEAO) which manages the unified [West African CFA franc](3791898.md) currency), and to numerous international organizations, NGOs and international research centers. Dakar has a large [Lebanese community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people_in_Senegal) (concentrated in the import-export sector) that dates to the 1920s, a community of Moroccan businesspeople, as well as Mauritanian, [Cape Verdean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdeans_in_Senegal), and Guinean communities. The city is home to as many as 20,000 French expatriates. France still maintains an air force base at Yoff, and the French fleet is serviced in Dakar's port.

Beginning 1978 and until 2007, Dakar was frequently the ending point of the [Dakar Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Rally).

## Geography



View of Dakar from the [International Space Station](15043.md) in 2011

Dakar is located on the [Cap-Vert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Vert) peninsula on the [Atlantic](698.md) coast and is the westernmost city on the African mainland.

### Climate

Dakar has an ocean-influenced tropical [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSh*), with a short rainy season and a lengthy dry season. Dakar's rainy season lasts from July to October, while the dry season covers the remaining eight months. The city sees approximately 411 mm (16.2 in) of rainfall per year.

Dakar between December and May is usually very warm with daily temperatures around 25–28 °C (77.0–82.4 °F). Nights during this time of the year are warm, some 18–20 °C (64.4–68.0 °F). However, between May and November, the city becomes decidedly hotter with daily highs reaching 29–31 °C (84.2–87.8 °F) and night lows a little bit above 23–25 °C (73.4–77.0 °F). Notwithstanding this hotter season, Dakar's weather is far from being so hot as experienced in inland Sahelian cities like [Niamey](57061.md) and [N'Djamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Djamena), where temperatures hover above 36 °C (96.8 °F) for much of the year. Dakar is cooled year-round by sea breezes.

#### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimated that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Dakar in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praia) in [Cape Verde](18962637.md). The annual temperature would increase by 1.5 °C (2.7 °F), and the temperature of the warmest and the coldest month by 1.4 °C (2.5 °F) and 1.6 °C (2.9 °F), respectively.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches RCP 4.5.[^cite_note-26]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Dakar is one of 12 major African cities ([Abidjan](21371767.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Algiers](1644.md), [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), Dakar, [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), [Durban](313650.md), [Lagos](85232.md), [Lomé](57067.md), [Luanda](18402.md) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) which would be the most severely affected by future [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damages of US\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion for the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damages, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to US\$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP4.5, US\$206 billion for RCP8.5 and US\$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-27] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-28]

The city of Dakar is a [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_\(subnational_entity\)) (also sometimes known as *commune de ville*), one of the 125 communes of Senegal. The commune of Dakar was created by the French colonial administration on 17 June 1887, by detaching it from the commune of [Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e). The commune of Gorée, created in 1872, was itself one of the oldest Western-style municipalities in Africa (along with the municipalities of [Algeria](358.md) and [South Africa](17416221.md)).

The commune of Dakar has been in continuous existence since 1887, being preserved by the new state of Senegal after independence in 1960, although its limits have varied considerably over time. The limits of the commune of Dakar have been unchanged since 1983. The commune of Dakar is ruled by a democratically elected municipal council (*conseil municipal*) serving five years, and a mayor elected by the municipal council. There have been 20 mayors in Dakar since 1887. The first black mayor was [Blaise Diagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Diagne), mayor of Dakar from 1924 to 1934. The longest-serving mayor was [Mamadou Diop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamadou_Diop_\(politician\)), mayor for 18 years between 1984 and 2002.

The commune of Dakar is also a *[department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_\(country_subdivision\))*, one of the 45 [departments of Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Senegal). This situation is quite similar to [Paris](22989.md), which is both a commune and a department. However, contrary to French departments, departments in Senegal have no political power (no departmental assembly), and are merely local administrative structures of the central state, in charge of carrying out some administrative services as well as controlling the activities of the communes within the department.

The department of Dakar is divided into four [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Senegal): [Almadies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almadies_Arrondissement), [Grand Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Dakar_Arrondissement), [Parcelles Assainies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parcelles_Assainies_Arrondissement) (which literally means "drained lots"; this is the most populous arrondissement of Dakar), and [Plateau/Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau/Gor%C3%A9e_Arrondissement) (downtown Dakar).[^cite_note-29] These arrondissements are quite different from the [arrondissements of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Paris), being merely local administrative structures of the central state, like the Senegalese *departments*, and are thus more comparable to French [departmental arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement_in_France).



The Assemblée nationale on the Plateau, the heart of old Dakar

In 1996, a massive reform of the administrative and political divisions of Senegal was voted by the Parliament of Senegal. The commune of Dakar, whose population approached 1 million inhabitants, was deemed too large and too populated to be properly managed by a central municipality, and thus on August 30, 1996, Dakar was divided into 19 *communes d'arrondissement*. These *communes d'arrondissement* were given extensive powers and are very much like regular communes. They have more powers than the arrondissements of Paris and are more akin to the [London boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_borough). The commune of Dakar was maintained above these 19 *communes d'arrondissement*, and it coordinates the activities of the *communes d'arrondissement*, much as [Greater London](45367389.md) coordinates the activities of the London boroughs. The 19 *communes d'arrondissement* belong to either of the four arrondissements of Dakar, and the *[sous-préfet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect)* of each arrondissement is in charge of controlling the activities of the *communes d'arrondissement* in his arrondissement.



Dakar Central Station

The *commune d'arrondissement* of [Dakar-Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Plateau) (34,626 inhabitants), in the arrondissement of Plateau/Gorée, is the historical heart of the city, and most ministries and public administrations are located there. The densest and most populous *commune d'arrondissement* is Médina (136,697 inhabitants), in the arrondissement of Plateau/Gorée. The *commune d'arrondissement* of [Yoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoff) (55,995 inhabitants), in the arrondissement of Almadies, is the largest one, while the smallest one is the *commune d'arrondissement* of [Île de Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e) (1,034 inhabitants), in the arrondissement of Plateau/Gorée.

Dakar is one of the 14 [*régions of Senegal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Senegal). The Dakar *région* encompasses the city of Dakar and all its suburbs along the [Cape Verde Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_Vert). Its territory is thus roughly the same as the territory of the metropolitan area of Dakar. Since the administrative reforms of 1996, the *régions* of Senegal, which until then were merely local administrative structures of the central state, have been turned into full-fledged political units, with democratically elected regional councils, and regional presidents. They were given extensive powers, and manage economic development, transportation, or environmental protection issues at the regional level, thus coordinating the actions of the communes below them.

## Notable sites



[Deux Mamelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deux_Mamelles) seen from afar, with the [African Renaissance Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Renaissance_Monument) on the left and [Les Mamelles Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mamelles_Lighthouse) on the right

The city of Dakar is a member of the [Organization of World Heritage Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_World_Heritage_Cities) and contains several landmarks. One of the most notable is [Deux Mamelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deux_Mamelles), twin hills located in [Ouakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouakam) commune. The hills are the only high ground in the city, providing views of the entire area and sweeping views of the city. The first hill is topped with [Mamelles Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mamelles_Lighthouse) built in 1864. The second hill has the newly completed [African Renaissance Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Renaissance_Monument) built on top, which is considered the [tallest statue in Africa](9830701.md).[^cite_note-30]



The African Renaissance Monument

Other landmarks of the city include the [medina quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina_quarter) located in [Médina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9dina,_Dakar) commune. Médina is originally built as a township for local populace during the French colonial era. Today, it is a traditional commercial center packed with tailors' shops. The most notable street market is Soumbédioune, which is also a major tourist attraction. The quarter also houses [Dakar Grand Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Grand_Mosque) at the heart of the commune, which is built in 1964 and one of the prominent landmarks of the city.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]



Beach in Dakar

Dakar is flanked by four small islands, île de Yoff, Île de N'Gor, [Îles de la Madeleine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_de_la_Madeleine_\(Senegal\)) and [Île de Gorée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gor%C3%A9e). Île de N'Gor is on the northern shore of [N'Gor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngor,_Dakar) commune, with beaches providing attractions such as surfing. N'Gor commune also has other popular beach resorts such as Plage de N'Gor.[^cite_note-33] Île de Gorée, formerly a slave island, is today a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) which preserves the colonial era architectures and facilities. Some notable places on the island include the Gorée Memorial, which is a memorial for the slaves, and the [House of Slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Slaves), which is a museum dedicated to the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md). Today, the island is also hosting the art scene of the hundreds of local artists who line up their works at the outdoor exhibitions.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-Je-35]

Some other notable places include Layen Mausoleum which entombs the founder of the [Layene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layene) Sufi tariqa, Palais Présidentiel which is the seat of the government constructed in 1907,[^cite_note-36] Place de l'Indépendance which is the central square of Dakar, [Dakar Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Victories_Cathedral,_Dakar), and [Cheikh Anta Diop University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheikh_Anta_Diop_University) also known as the [University of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dakar), which was established in 1957.

## Places of worship

The most common [places of worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Places_of_worship) in Dakar are [Muslim](6037917.md) mosques.[^cite_note-37] There are also [Christian](5211.md) churches: [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Dakar) ([Catholic Church](606848.md)), [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God), [Universal Church of the Kingdom of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Church_of_the_Kingdom_of_God).

Dakar was selected as the Capital of Islamic Culture for African Region for the year 2007 by the [Islamic Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Educational,_Scientific_and_Cultural_Organization) (ISESCO), honoring its Islamic heritage.[^cite_note-38] ISESCO and its parent organization [Organization of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) (OIC) have held several regional and international conferences in the city,[^cite_note-39] best known for adoption of Dakar Declaration in 1991, which aimed at fostering cooperation between the member states.[^cite_note-ifrc-40] Dakar is also known as the birthplace of the [Layene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layene) Brotherhood, a [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) [tariqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqa) founded by [Seydina Mouhammadou Limamou Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seydina_Mouhammadou_Limamou_Laye) in 1883 at the commune of Yoff. Seydina is buried in the Layen Mausoleum, which is among the major landmarks of Dakar.[^cite_note-41] Today, Layene Brotherhood is consisted mostly of the [Lebou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebou) people and based in the Cap-Vert area. It is also the third-biggest Sufi order in Senegal.

Prominent worshiping sites for Muslims in Dakar include the Grand Mosque of Dakar, built in 1964, which is situated at Allée Pape Gueye Fall of Medina, the [Mosque of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_Divinity), constructed in 1973, situated in Ouakam, with the characteristic triangular windows, and Omarienne Mosque with [minarets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minaret) topped by green orbs.[^cite_note-Je-35][^cite_note-at-42]

## Culture



N'dawrabine dance of [Ngor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngor,_Dakar)

In [Senegal](27278.md), the traditional culture is very centred around the idea of family. This even includes the way that they eat. When it is time to eat a typical meal, someone will say "kay lekk" which means 'come eat'. Everyone will come together and sit around the plate and eat with their hands.[^cite_note-43] Some famous dishes include Cebbu Jën (Tiéboudienne) and Yassa. The etiquette of people in Dakar is very simple but very vital. To not greet someone upon sight is to portray rudeness and oftentimes ignorance. Due to French colonialism, the children of Dakar have a unique school system. The school will get a break at about midday and return home to get some rest. Since the population is majority Muslim, there are numerous daily Islamic activities ongoing, such as participating in noon prayer at the nearest mosque and attending the local mosque on Fridays. Music has a big influence on the youth, with famous artists like Daara J Family who use their voice to represent the problems in their communities.[^cite_note-44]

Dakar is home to multiple national and international festivals, like [World Festival of Black Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Festival_of_Black_Arts), Festival international du film de quartier de Dakar, [Dakar Biennale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Biennale). It was also the location of [Taf Taf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taf_Taf), an international [artist residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist_residency) program.[^cite_note-45]

Dakar Railway Station (Gare de Dakar)



The [Train Express Regional Dakar-AIBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Express_Regional_Dakar-AIBD) (TER)

The [Conseil Exécutif des Transports Urbains Durables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_Ex%C3%A9cutif_des_Transports_Urbains_Durables?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CETUD) was founded in 1997 to coordinate transportation in the Dakar region.[^cite_note-49] A 2021 study by CETUD revealed that the national economy suffers an annual loss of nearly CFA francs 900 billion (approximately US\$1.4 billion) as a result of poor air quality, traffic congestion, hazardous roads, and noise pollution. This represents nearly 6% of the country's yearly GDP.[^cite_note-50]

The town is home to the [Autonomous Port of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Port_of_Dakar) and the terminus of the non-functioning [Dakar-Niger railroad line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Niger_railroad_line).



Place indépendance Dakar 1

Three [Trans-African Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-African_Highway_network) (TAHs) start from Dakar: the [Cairo-Dakar Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo%E2%80%93Dakar_Highway) (TAH 1), the [Dakar-Ndjamena Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Sahelian_Highway) (TAH 5), and the [Dakar-Lagos Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans%E2%80%93West_African_Coastal_Highway) (TAH 7).

The [Train Express Regional Dakar-AIBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Express_Regional_Dakar-AIBD) (TER) will connect Dakar with [Blaise Diagne International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Diagne_International_Airport) (AIBD). An initial 36 kilometres (22 mi) will link Dakar to Diamniadio and a second phase of 19 kilometres (12 mi) would connect Dakar to the Blaise Diagne airport. A total of 14 train stations will be served and the fastest end-to-end journey will take 45 minutes. The railway is expected to carry 115,000 passengers per day. The TER's first test run launched on 14 January 2019 and the first passenger train ran in December 2021.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

The city has a 100% electric [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) (BRT) network: [Sunu BRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunu_BRT), which currently has two routes in operation, with two more planned.[^cite_note-\:1-54][^cite_note-\:0-55] The electric bus fleet consists of 144 articulated e-buses, dedicated right-of-way and 32 feeder bus routes.[^cite_note-\:0-55] The construction of the Dakar BRT was funded by the World Bank and the European Investment Bank, and the Government of Senegal.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-\:0-55] The bus fleet was financed, acquired, and is operated by the private sector under a 15-year concession agreement established in 2021. The private sector entity, Dakar Mobilité, is a partnership between Meridiam and the Fonds d'Investissement du Sénégal.[^cite_note-\:0-55] The BRT development created 1,000 direct jobs, 35-45% of which were roles for women.[^cite_note-57] The BRT system is expected to carry approximately 300,000 passengers per day and reduce travel times from 95 to 45 minutes[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] After the development of the system, 69% of the city's population can access the city centre in under an hour, compared with just 12% before the BRT.[^cite_note-60]

[Blaise Diagne International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Diagne_International_Airport) is the city's international airport; it handles flights by several airlines, including [Air France](14998012.md), [Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines), [Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Airline) and [Emirates Sky Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Sky_Cargo), [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia_Airlines), [TAP Air Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAP_Air_Portugal) and [Turkish](167925.md), and is the hub of Senegal's flag carrier, [Air Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Senegal).
