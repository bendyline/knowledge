**USS *Mason* (DDG-87)** is an [*Arleigh Burke*-class](197341.md) ([Flight IIA](197341.md#Characteristics)) [Aegis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_combat_system) [guided-missile destroyer](232005.md) in the [United States Navy](20518076.md). She is named in honor of the black crewmembers who served on board [USS *Mason* (DE-529)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Mason_\(DE-529\)) during the period of [racial segregation in the United States Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States_Armed_Forces).[^cite_note-1]

*Mason* was the 21st ship of this class to be built at [Bath Iron Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_Iron_Works) in [Bath, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Maine), and construction began on 19 January 2000. She was launched and christened on 23 June 2001. On 12 April 2003, a commissioning ceremony was held at [Port Canaveral, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Canaveral). She is currently homeported at the [Naval Station Mayport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Mayport) in [Mayport, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayport,_Florida).

## Etymology

This is the third U.S. Navy warship with the name USS *Mason*. The first [*Mason* (DD-191)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Mason_\(DD-191\)), in service from 1920 to 1941, was named for [John Young Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Young_Mason), well known for his service as the [secretary of the navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_navy) for two American Presidents. The second [*Mason* (DE-529)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Mason_\(DE-529\)) was named for Ensign [Newton Henry Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_Henry_Mason), a naval aviator who posthumously was awarded the [Distinguished Flying Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Flying_Cross_\(USA\)). This ship is named for the crew of the second [*Mason* (DE-529)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Mason_\(DE-529\)), as this was the first ship in the US Navy with the distinction of having a predominantly black crew.[^cite_note-2]

*&#x20;Mason* in the Atlantic, 2011

USS *Mason* conducted her maiden deployment with the [USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman) Carrier Strike Group in support of [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom)/[Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in late 2004. *Mason* returned home after six months on 18 April 2005.[^cite_note-3]

On 3 October 2006, *Mason* departed [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md) for a seven-month deployment to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) in support of the global [war on terrorism](13425800.md). She participated in Exercise Neon Falcon. *Mason* returned home in May 2007.[^cite_note-4]

*Mason* deployed with the [aircraft carrier](2219.md) [USS *Theodore Roosevelt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Theodore_Roosevelt_\(CVN-71\)) on 12 September 2008 for a scheduled deployment.[^cite_note-5] *Mason* made scheduled port calls in 2008-2009 to ports in [Mykonos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos), [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), [Aqaba](328399.md), [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Dubai](211583.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md), and [Jebel Ali](1875391.md). *Mason* conducted operations in the Persian Gulf in support of operation enduring freedom.

On 12 March 2011, she sailed through the [Suez Canal](29323.md) en route to the Mediterranean, to support possible humanitarian or military action in response to the [Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_civil_war).[^cite_note-6] In April 2011, a boarding team from the ship successfully liberated five Yemeni hostages from 11 [Somali pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_pirate) who had taken over the Yemeni-flagged ship F/V *Nasri*. The pirates had seriously injured two other fishermen in their attack, left the wounded ashore, and then taken *Nasri* to sea as a pirate [mothership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothership). [Assault weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_weapon), ammunition, [rocket propelled grenades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_propelled_grenade), and launchers were destroyed by the boarding team.[^cite_note-7]

On 22 July 2013, she deployed to the [Fifth Fleet](418867.md) and [Sixth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Sixth_Fleet) area of responsibility as part of the *Harry S. Truman* Carrier Strike Group. *Mason* returned to her homeport on 18 April 2014.



*Mason* with Chinese destroyer [*Jinan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_destroyer_Jinan_\(152\)), 6 November 2015

On 7 November 2015, *Mason*, acting as the [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship) for Destroyer Squadron 26, completed the first East Coast Passing Exercise with the [People's Republic of China](5405.md)'s [People's Liberation Army](66890.md)-Navy ships.

### 2016 attacks off the coast of Yemen

On 3 October 2016, following a guided-missile attack by [Houthi](17240332.md) rebels on the United Arab Emirates-operated [HSV-2 *Swift*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSV-2_Swift), *Mason* was deployed off the coast of [war-torn Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen), along with destroyers [USS *Nitze*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Nitze) and [USS *Roosevelt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Roosevelt_\(DDG-80\)), and [USS *Ponce*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ponce_\(LPD-15\)), an [amphibious transport dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_transport_dock) being used as a forward landing base and laser [test bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_bed).[^cite_note-8] According to an unnamed U.S. [Department of Defense](7279897.md) official, the purpose for sending the ships was "to ensure that shipping continues unimpeded in the strait and the vicinity."[^cite_note-Tomlinson,_2016-9]

On 9 October 2016, *Mason*, operating near the [Bab-el-Mandeb](62138.md) strait, was targeted by two missiles fired from Houthi-controlled territory. Both missiles fell short and crashed into the water.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The Houthi insurgency denied launching the attack on the warship.[^cite_note-12] The [United States Naval Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Institute) reported that *Mason* fired two [SM-2 Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard) missiles and one [RIM-162 ESSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM) missile to intercept the two missiles, and deployed her [Nulka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nulka) missile decoy. One of two U.S. defense officials cited anonymously added that whether the incoming missiles had been shot down or crashed into the water on their own was not clear .[^cite_note-13] This marked the first recorded instance of ship-based [anti-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-air) missiles being fired from vertical launching cells in combat in response to an actual inbound missile threat.[^cite_note-14]

On 12 October 2016 *Mason* was again targeted by missiles fired from Yemeni territory, while she was operating in the Bab el-Mandeb strait.[^cite_note-2nd_attack-15] *Mason* was not hit by the two missiles, which were fired from near the southern Yemen city of [Al Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah).[^cite_note-2nd_attack-15] While the Navy was not certain whether the first incoming missile was intercepted or instead just fell into the sea, officials said *Mason* successfully intercepted the second missile at a distance around 8 miles (13 km),[^cite_note-16] marking the first time in history a warship destroyed an inbound [antiship missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiship_missile) with a [Surface-to-air missile](182664.md) in actual self-defense. On 13 October 2016, the U.S. attacked three [radar](25676.md) sites in Houthi-held territory, which had been involved in the earlier missile attacks, with [cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) launched from *Nitze*.[^cite_note-17] The Pentagon assessed that all three sites were destroyed.[^cite_note-18]

On 15 October 2016, *Mason* was targeted in a third attack by Houthi rebels based in Yemen, by five antiship [cruise missiles](6590.md) while operating in the [Red Sea](26191.md) north of the Bab el-Mandeb strait. *Mason* fired a radar [decoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoy), an [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) decoy, and several [SM-2 Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM-2_Standard) missiles in response, either neutralizing or intercepting four of the five incoming missiles. The Navy reported the fifth incoming missile was neutralized by a radar decoy launched from *Nitze*, after *Mason* alerted her to the threat.[^cite_note-19]

### 2017–2022

On 23 May 2017, *Mason* was awarded the 2016 [Battenberg Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battenberg_Cup), signifying she was the best all-around ship or submarine in the United States Navy's [Atlantic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Fleet_\(United_States\)) based on crew achievements. *Mason* was only the fifth destroyer in the prior 111 years to receive the award.[^cite_note-20]

On 22 August 2022, *Mason* completed a homeport shift to [Naval Station Mayport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Mayport), in [Jacksonville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida).[^cite_note-21]

### Gaza war

On 14 October 2023, US Secretary of Defense [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin) directed nuclear-powered [aircraft carrier](2219.md) [*Dwight D. Eisenhower*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Dwight_D._Eisenhower) and her [carrier strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_strike_group), which includes the [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) [*Philippine Sea*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Philippine_Sea_\(CG-58\)), along with *Mason* and sister destroyers [*Laboon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Laboon) and [*Gravely*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Gravely), to the [eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean) in response to the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-22] This is the second carrier strike group to be sent to the region in response to the conflict, following aircraft carrier [*Gerald R. Ford*](4050542.md) and her group, which had been dispatched six days earlier.[^cite_note-23]

On 26 November 2023, *Mason* responded to a [distress call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distress_call) from the commercial tanker *Central Park*, which was seized by gunmen in the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md). Five gunmen tried to escape on a fast boat, but were chased and eventually surrendered to a search-and-seizure team dispatched by *Mason*. Two [ballistic missiles](38025.md) were fired from Houthi-controlled parts of Yemen towards the general direction of *Mason* and *Central Park*, but they landed about 10 [nautical miles](21289.md) away from the ships, with no reported injuries or damage.[^cite_note-24]

On 6 December 2023, *Mason* shot down an air drone headed toward *Mason* from Yemen, which officials attributed to Houthi rebels, as it was in the southern Red Sea.[^cite_note-25]

After the Norwegian-flagged oil and chemical tanker *Strinda*, traveling from Malaysia and carrying a cargo of [palm oil](57561.md), was struck by an antiship cruise missile while passing through the [Bab-el-Mandeb](62138.md) strait on 11 December 2023, *Mason* responded to the ship's distress call and rendered assistance.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

On 13 December 2023, *Mason* shot down in self-defense a drone launched from a Houthi-controlled area of Yemen, which was heading directly towards *Mason*.[^cite_note-auto-28][^cite_note-auto1-29] *Mason* was at the time responding to the distress call issued by the tanker *Ardmore Encounter*.[^cite_note-auto1-29] Houthi forces had unsuccessfully attempted to board the tanker on skiffs, exchanged fire with the ship’s armed guards, and then fired two missiles in the *Ardmore Encounter*'s direction, prompting the distress call.[^cite_note-auto1-29] The tanker was carrying jet fuel from India to the Netherlands and Sweden.[^cite_note-auto1-29]

## Coat of arms



Crest

The shield has background of white with a double chevron across the center. Above are opposing lions and below is a gold trident. The traditional U.S. Navy colors were chosen for the shield because dark blue and gold represents the sea and excellence, respectively. The colors red, white, and blue represent the United States. The double chevron is to honor DD 191 and DE 529, the former ships named USS *Mason*. The facing lions, adapted from the Mason family coat of arms, denote the Atlantic and Pacific campaigns of World War II. The trident, symbol of sea prowess, represents *Mason*'s modern warfare capabilities, which include the AEGIS weapon system (including [Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System](249526.md) from [theater ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_ballistic_missile)) and [Cooperative Engagement Capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_Engagement_Capability).

The crest consists of a helm, crossing swords behind, and a combined anchor and cross surrounded by a wreath. The helm is symbolic to strong defense with power projection. The anchor refers to the namesake of DD 191, [John Y. Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Y._Mason), who was the Secretary of the Navy under Presidents [John Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyler) and [James K. Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_K._Polk). The cross is in reference to [Newton Henry Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_Henry_Mason)'s Distinguished Flying Cross award. The wreath represents all the awards, honors, and achievements of the past ships with the namesake Mason and crews who served them.

The motto is written on a scroll of white with a red trim. The ship's motto is "Proudly We Serve". The motto is in honor of the high achievement of the African-American crew of DE-529 who made history with their selfless bravery in defense of the U.S. in World War II, and marks their contribution to the eventual desegregation of the U.S. Navy.

The coat of arms in full color as in the blazon, upon a white background enclosed within a dark blue oval border edged on the outside with a gold rope and bearing the inscription "USS Mason" at the top and "DDG 87" in the base, all gold.
