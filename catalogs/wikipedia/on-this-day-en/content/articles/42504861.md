**Sara Zimmerman Duterte-Carpio** (English: [/dəˈtɜːrteɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), Filipino: [\[dʊˈtɛɾtɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog); born May 31, 1978), commonly known as **Inday Sara**,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] is a Filipino lawyer and politician who is the 15th and current [vice president of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_the_Philippines). She is the third female vice president (after [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) and [Leni Robredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leni_Robredo)), the third vice president to come from [Mindanao](20546.md), and the [youngest ever Philippine vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vice_presidents_of_the_Philippines_by_age). A daughter of the 16th president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md), she is the leading figure of [Dutertismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutertismo), the political ideology associated with her father. Duterte previously served as the [mayor of Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Davao_City) from 2010 to 2013, and from 2016 to 2022. Duterte was also Davao City's vice mayor from 2007 to 2010.

Duterte graduated from [San Pedro College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_College), initially aiming to pursue a medical career. She attended classes in the College of Law of [San Beda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Beda_University) and eventually graduated in [San Sebastian College – Recoletos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebastian_College_%E2%80%93_Recoletos). She was elected as Davao City's vice mayor in 2007, before eventually serving as the city's mayor from 2010 until 2013, succeeding her father and becoming the youngest and the first female mayor of the city. After her first term ended, she took a brief hiatus, returning to politics in 2016 after being elected again as Davao City mayor and was reelected in 2019. During her second stint as mayor, she initiated the *Byaheng DO30* and Peace 911 programs in the city, as well as overseeing the [city government's COVID-19 pandemic response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Davao_Region#Davao_City). Duterte was also an influential figure in national politics during her father's presidency, forming alliances with several political parties and playing a key role in the ouster of [Pantaleon Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantaleon_Alvarez) as the [Speaker of the House of Representatives](7835723.md) in 2018.

Duterte ran for the vice presidency in 2022 under [Lakas–CMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakas%E2%80%93CMD), becoming the running mate of [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) from the [Partido Federal ng Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_Federal_ng_Pilipinas) under the [UniTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniTeam) alliance. Their ticket won in a landslide victory, becoming the first running mate pair to win together since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Philippine_presidential_election), and the first to be elected by a majority since the establishment of the Fifth Republic in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Philippine_presidential_election). Duterte became the vice president on June 30, 2022, having her [inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration_of_Bongbong_Marcos#Duterte's_inaugural_ceremony) held 11 days earlier in Davao City.

As vice president, Duterte concurrently served as the [secretary of education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Education_\(Philippines\)) amidst a national learning crisis[^cite_note-6] and as vice chairperson of [an anti-insurgency task force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Task_Force_to_End_Local_Communist_Armed_Conflict) (NTF-ELCAC), but resigned from both positions on June 19, 2024. Despite her initial electoral alliance with Marcos, political observers noted her increasing absence from public appearances with the president in 2024 correlating with a growing rift in the Marcos and Duterte political families.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] By late August 2024, during police operations to [arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_of_Apollo_Quiboloy) her father's ally [Apollo Quiboloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Quiboloy), she openly admitted her regret to supporting Marcos in the presidential election,[^cite_note-11] while Marcos himself later acknowledged that they have not spoken to each other since she handed in her resignation.[^cite_note-12] After her father was [arrested](79430656.md) and brought to [The Hague](30269.md) in March 2025, her tenure has been characterized by multiple trips abroad that aim to consolidate her family's overseas supporters.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

On February 5, 2025, Duterte was [impeached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_impeachment_of_Sara_Duterte) by the [House of Representatives](293605.md) after concerns were raised regarding her use of [confidential funds](#Confidential_funds_controversy) and her [assassination threat](#ZoomPressCon) against President Marcos, his wife [Liza](70769981.md), and his cousin, House Speaker [Martin Romualdez](45505193.md).[^cite_note-impeachement-16] She became the first sitting vice president, as well as the fifth official in Philippine history, to be impeached,[^cite_note-17] and by May 2026, became the first Philippine official to be [impeached twice](82338655.md).[^cite_note-18] However, Duterte remains in office as a [Senate trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_trial_of_Sara_Duterte) is ongoing since July 2026 to determine if she should be acquitted or removed from her position as vice president.[^cite_note-19] On February 18, 2026, she announced her [2028 run for president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte_2028_presidential_campaign).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Early years

Sara Zimmerman Duterte was born at [Davao Doctors Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Doctors_Hospital) in [Davao City](489401.md) on May 31, 1978.[^cite_note-22] She is the second child to then-lawyer later president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) and flight attendant [Elizabeth Zimmerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Abellana_Zimmerman). Her maternal great-grandfather was German American, having fled to the Philippines from the United States.[^cite_note-Sales-2023-23]

Growing up in what she called a "broken family",[^cite_note-MayorsDuterte-24] Duterte has spoken of her "love-hate relationship" with her father when she was a student due to her disapproval for his womanizing tendencies and late night habits.[^cite_note-spotlight-25][^cite_note-eyes-26] On the evening of February 25, 1986, at age seven, she and her siblings were brought by Rodrigo to the [San Pedro Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Cathedral) to join in celebrations of the ouster of President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) by the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution), in which her grandmother [Soledad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad_Duterte) took part, with Sara remembering her father to have said: "Timan-i ninyo ning gabhiona ni. Ayaw ninyo kalimti" (lit. 'Remember this night. Do not forget it').[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-RapplerFeb25-28] In 1994, her brother Paolo left the family without explanation to raise his first son ([Omar Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Duterte)) with Lovelie Sangkola for the next five years.[^cite_note-29] Once Rodrigo left his wife Elizabeth in the 1990s, Sara stayed with her mother, and up to the time she was mayor, Sara considered her relationship with her father as "not very close".[^cite_note-MayorsDuterte-24] Despite this, Rodrigo considered Sara to be his favorite child,[^cite_note-spotlight-25] and placed high value on the education she and her brothers received.[^cite_note-eyes-26][^cite_note-30]

### Education and law career

Duterte attended [San Pedro College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_College), majoring in [BS Respiratory Therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science_in_Respiratory_Care), and graduated in 1999;[^cite_note-31] in her inaugural speech as [mayor of Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Davao_City), Duterte said she originally wanted to be a pediatrician instead of a politician.[^cite_note-32] She later took up a law degree from [San Beda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Beda_University) but transferred to the [San Sebastian College – Recoletos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebastian_College_%E2%80%93_Recoletos), where she graduated in May 2005.[^cite_note-Unregistered-33] In 2005, Duterte passed the [Philippine Bar examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Bar_examination), with a score of 80 percent.[^cite_note-34] She then worked for a few months as a court attorney at the office of [Supreme Court Associate Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines) [Romeo Callejo Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Callejo_Sr.)[^cite_note-Unregistered-33]

### Alleged Jewish ethnicity



Duterte's maternal great-grandfather, George John Zimmerman

On September 30, 2016, Rodrigo Duterte sparked outrage after stating that "[Hitler](2731583.md) massacred [three million Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust)... there's three million [drug addicts](51032591.md). I'd be happy to [slaughter them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killings_and_forced_disappearances_in_the_Philippines)."[^cite_note-35] Rodrigo Duterte claimed on October 3, 2016, during [Rosh Hashanah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_Hashanah), that the Zimmerman family is of [patrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrilineal_descent) [Jewish descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Is_a_Jew%3F),[^cite_note-ranada2016-36][^cite_note-mendez2016-37] with Elizabeth Zimmerman's father supposedly having fled to the [Philippines](23440.md) from [Nazi Germany](21212.md).[^cite_note-sharon2018-38] In the speech, Duterte said "\[..] my wife is a Zimmerman. She is a descendant of an American Jew. \[..] So why would I defile the memory of the Jews? What would I get if I insult the Jewish people \[..]"[^cite_note-39]

Contrary to popular belief and many Philippine and Israeli media reports repeating Rodrigo Duterte's claims, the Zimmermans are not Jewish. In a statement released on June 27, 2023, six years after both of Rodrigo's remarks were made, then-Press Secretary [Trixie Cruz-Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trixie_Cruz-Angeles) reiterated that the claims that the Zimmermans were Jewish are false, saying "\[t]he Zimmermans arrived in the PH in the 1930s and were not refugees during [World War II](32927.md). They are not European Jews, but came from the US."[^cite_note-40] On June 28, 2023, historian Todd Sales Lucero (a columnist from [*The Freeman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freeman_\(newspaper\)), a newspaper based in [Cebu](160278.md)), reported that Zimmerman's grandfather, George J. Zimmerman, is an American German [Lutheran](23371382.md) who hails from Unterlangau, [Bavaria](3764.md), who is non-Jewish. While there are German Jews with the name Zimmermann, this does not mean all Zimmermans are Jewish in origin. [DNA testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dna_testing) in the Zimmermans has further disproven the claim of Jewish heritage. It is likely that Rodrigo made the claim of Jewish ancestry in order to justify his previous remarks, after the fact.[^cite_note-41]

## Vice mayor of Davao City (2007–2010)



Duterte being interviewed on March 12, 2009, while vice mayor

Sara's father Rodrigo had admitted to initially keeping his family members from entering "the messy and difficult world of politics", but by December 2006, he expressed that he would like for Sara to run as his running mate in the 2007 mayoral elections due to his concern for corrupt politicians taking over and ruining his legacy.[^cite_note-42] Sara Duterte was elected vice mayor in 2007 with over 330,000 votes, defeating lone opponent Jeff Ho and succeeding Luis Bonguyan.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Duterte served under her father for one term until 2010, and concurrently served as a regional officer of the National Movement of Young Legislators from 2007 to 2010. It was during this time when lawyer [Zuleika T. Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuleika_T._Lopez) was first hired by Duterte to be a staff member of the city government, receiving the position of city council secretary.[^cite_note-EDCarillo-45]

As vice mayor, Duterte's landmark project was 'Inday para sa Barangay', a [whole-of-government approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole-of-government_approach) servicing yearly in each of the city's barangays. It was later called the Caravan of Government Services when she transferred it to the Office of the City Mayor.[^cite_note-ovpbio-46]

In 2008, Duterte's stated net worth jumped to ₱18.49 million from the previous year's ₱7.25 million (another source provides ₱13.88 million for 2007) based on her filed [statements of assets, liabilities, and net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_of_assets,_liabilities,_and_net_worth) (SALN), above the net worth declared by her father, Mayor Rodrigo Duterte (₱15.32 million) in 2008.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

## First term as mayor of Davao City (2010–2013)

In 2010, Duterte was elected mayor, becoming the first female mayor of the city, as well as being the youngest to have been elected, as she turned 32 years old just weeks after being proclaimed winner.[^cite_note-49] She won over [House Speaker](7835723.md) [Prospero Nograles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospero_Nograles), her father's political rival, in a lead of 200,000 votes in the 2010 elections. Nograles earlier filed a protest at the Commission on Elections in [Manila](184334.md) questioning the results, stating that there was a conspiracy of local poll officials.[^cite_note-50] Vowing to be "useful and to serve the country at all times", she assumed the post that her father Rodrigo held for over 20 years.[^cite_note-51] After Duterte appointed [Zuleika T. Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuleika_T._Lopez) as city administrator a few days into her term, her decision was challenged by the city council led by Rodrigo, then the vice mayor of Davao, who argued that Lopez was unqualified for the position.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In response, Sara withdrew her request for the council's concurrence in Lopez's appointment and threatened to halt city hall's engagements with the city council, stating that the council "should learn to respect" her office as mayor.[^cite_note-SSJCZ-54]

During her first term, she also served as officer in the National Executive Board of the League of Cities Philippines from 2010 to 2013.

On December 2, 2010, President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) appointed her as Chairperson of the Regional Development Council (RDC) Region XI. She was the first woman to hold the position and the first to be appointed from the government sector since the reorganization of the council in 1986.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] As chairperson, she saw the growth of [Davao Region](296162.md)’s Gross Regional Domestic Product from 3.9 percent in 2011 to 7.1 percent in 2012.[^cite_note-ovpbio-46]

On July 1, 2011, Duterte gained national attention when she punched Abe Andres, a Davao City Regional Trial Court sheriff, over the demolition of shanties in Barangay Kapitan Tomas Monteverde Sr., Agdao, Davao City. Earlier, she had asked the court and the demolition team to delay the demolition; Andres declined her request and pushed through with the demolition which infuriated her.[^cite_note-57] On July 11, Sheriffs Confederation of the Philippines Inc. filed a complaint against Duterte in connection to the incident.[^cite_note-58] On June 28, 2012, almost a year after the incident, she publicly apologized to Andres and his family.[^cite_note-59] By May 2022, Andres openly expressed his support for the Marcos-Duterte electoral team.[^cite_note-60]

### Policies

Duterte promoted the city as a tourism and investment destination, introducing the "Davao: Life Is Here" tagline to market the city in 2011.[^cite_note-61]

## Political hiatus (2013–2016)

Duterte decided not to seek re-election in 2013 to give way to her father Rodrigo.[^cite_note-62] She also turned down her father's offer to run for representative of [Davao City's 1st district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City's_1st_congressional_district).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

## Second and third term as mayor of Davao City (2016–2022)



Sara Duterte (center) filing her certificate of candidacy for mayor of Davao City accompanied by his father Mayor [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) (left) and her husband [Mans Carpio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mans_Carpio) (right).

Duterte ran again for the mayoralty post for Davao City in the [2016 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_general_election) and won the position, succeeding her father for the second time. She had her older brother [Paolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Duterte), and later Bernard Al-ag, as her vice mayors during this term.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] According to lawyer [Salvador Panelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Panelo), Duterte had been reluctant in running for mayor, wishing to stay away from politics and instead continue her legal practice, but was eventually convinced by her father.[^cite_note-67]



Portrait during her second term as mayor

In her first year back as mayor, the [Davao City bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Davao_City_bombing) took place on September 2, 2016, with Duterte's private nurse Kristia Bisnon, who accompanied Duterte during hospital checkups in July for her pregnancy with triplets, among the victims who perished.[^cite_note-InqDavao-68] Three days later, Duterte's doctor informed her that she had a miscarriage with two of her three fetuses, which she revealed during a speech on September 8, 2016.[^cite_note-InqDavao-68][^cite_note-69]

On October 23, 2017, Mayor Duterte launched the Tapang at Malasakit (lit. 'Courage and Compassion') Alliance for the Philippines (TMAP) at the Marquis Events Place in [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig), composed of Duterte supporters and allies, where she announced her intention to run for the [Davao City 1st district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City's_1st_congressional_district) seat at the [House of Representatives](293605.md) and called on people to "fight global terrorism by quickly rebuilding Marawi, focus on our poverty that has fueled narco-politicians, set aside our personal politics" in support of President Duterte.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]



Duterte during [Rizal Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Day) 2018

On February 23, 2018, she launched a new regional party called [Hugpong ng Pagbabago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugpong_ng_Pagbabago) (HNP).[^cite_note-73] The party was later approved on July 4, 2018, ahead of the 2019 general elections.[^cite_note-74] HNP also stood as an electoral alliance, fielding candidates from different parties for the [senatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_Senate_election). Nine out of 13 senatorial candidates won in the election.

Following the creation of HNP, a feud began between Duterte and one of her father's allies, then [House Speaker](7835723.md) [Pantaleon Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantaleon_Alvarez), citing its formation as an example of the entrenchment of political dynasties in the country. In response, Duterte publicly berated Alvarez and alleged that he has been calling mayors in the [Davao Region](296162.md) to tag her as part of the opposition and brag about having the ability to impeach her father, all of which Alvarez denied.[^cite_note-75] Duterte also remarked that "the Philippines will be a better country if he is not Speaker."[^cite_note-76] Months later, Alvarez was unseated as the House Speaker in the [17th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Congress_of_the_Philippines), with former president and [Pampanga's 2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga's_2nd_congressional_district) representative [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) taking his place. According to her father, she was behind the ousting of Alvarez as speaker, stating that "honest to God, it was Inday who maneuvered it."[^cite_note-77]

Although she previously intended to run for Congress in 2019 after her second term, Mayor Duterte stated in September 2018 that she is willing to serve a third term as mayor,[^cite_note-78] with her brother Paolo (by then resigned from the vice mayorship) to run for the congressional seat instead.[^cite_note-79] In the [May 2019 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_general_election), Duterte defeated her lone opponent, a 67-year-old [sari-sari store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari-sari_store) owner named Jun Marcellones, by a lead of over 500,000 votes and succeeded reelection,[^cite_note-80] with her brother and running mate [Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Duterte) also elected vice mayor.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] After the election, congressman-elect [Alan Peter Cayetano](10220741.md) advised Duterte against endorsing [Marinduque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinduque's_at-large_congressional_district) representative [Lord Allan Velasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Allan_Velasco) for the speakership due to its potential to "break up the 'group'" and be detrimental to the Dutertes' allies in the 2022 presidential election, which Duterte deemed as a "veiled threat".[^cite_note-84] In 2020, Duterte did not declare any cash deposits or cash on hand for her 2019 [statement of assets, liabilities, and net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_of_assets,_liabilities,_and_net_worth) (SALN), which she later repeated for the succeeding five years.[^cite_note-85]

### Policies

During her second stint as mayor, Duterte initiated *Byaheng DO30*, consisting of 30 projects addressing ten priority sectors which include "education, health, poverty alleviation, infrastructure development, solid waste management and environment, agriculture, investment and tourism, traffic and transport management, disaster risk reduction and management, and peace and order".[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] Duterte also established the Peace 911 program to address the local [communist rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines) through a "human-centered" approach, bringing several government services to far-flung areas.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-auto-89] The program was first implemented in the Paquibato district before being expanded to other barangays.[^cite_note-auto-89][^cite_note-90] Her administration also established the Kean Gabriel Hotline for reporting child abuse anonymously.[^cite_note-91]

As Davao City mayor, Duterte garnered an approval rating of 93% according to an independent survey by the RP-Mission and Development Foundation, exceeding her father's highest approval rating when he was mayor at 86% in 2010, and getting the highest rating throughout the country.[^cite_note-92]

### COVID-19 pandemic response

See also: [COVID-19 pandemic in the Davao Region § Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Davao_Region#Davao_City)

In early 2020, the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) reached the city. In response, Duterte created a city task force for COVID-19 and other public health emergencies and instituted several measures to limit the spread of the disease and address the crisis. The city government cancelled several citywide events, ordered the closure of several establishments, and restricted travel and movement in the city.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] To aid residents affected by the restrictions, the local government provided food packs; most of the city's [Bayanihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayanihan_to_Heal_as_One_Act) grant was spent on the purchase of food packs distributed to the city's barangays and for medical workers.[^cite_note-95] Travel restrictions were eased as the number of cases lowered. The local government also established COVID-19 testing centers and isolation facilities.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] Her administration also oversaw the city's COVID-19 vaccination program when the national government began its [vaccination program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination_in_the_Philippines) in early 2021.[^cite_note-98]

## 2022 vice presidential election

Further information: [2022 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election) and [Bongbong Marcos 2022 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos_2022_presidential_campaign)

Duterte was considered by many political commentators as her father's successor after her role as a power broker in the 2019 midterm elections.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] As early as April 2021, an organization called Inday Sara Is My President (ISIP Pilipinas Movement) was formed by national convenor and Duterte security aide [Ramil Madriaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramil_Madriaga) and lawyer Ryan Rey S. Quilala, the latter of whom being Duterte's former professor at [San Sebastian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebastian_College_%E2%80%93_Recoletos).[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] On July 7, 2021, the [People's Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Reform_Party) (PRP) signaled their support early on for Duterte's potential presidential run,[^cite_note-104] with Duterte stating two days later that she was open to running for president.[^cite_note-105] On September 9, she said she would not, since her father, the sitting president, would run for vice president, and they agreed that only one should run for a national position.[^cite_note-106] However, he did not file his candidacy for vice president by October 2,[^cite_note-107] but she did not file a candidacy for any national position, running instead for reelection as mayor of Davao City.[^cite_note-\:0-108] She later withdrew her candidacy for reelection as Davao City mayor on November 9.[^cite_note-109] Her brother [Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Duterte), incumbent vice mayor of Davao City, ran in her stead.[^cite_note-110] On November 11, she resigned from Hugpong ng Pagbabago and joined [Lakas–CMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakas%E2%80%93CMD) in [Silang, Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silang,_Cavite).[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-lakas-112] There, she sponsored the wedding of Jed Patricio and Gianna Revilla, the daughter of party chairman and senator [Bong Revilla](8452762.md).[^cite_note-113] Six days later, she became the chairperson of Lakas–CMD, succeeding Revilla.[^cite_note-114] On November 19, she rejoined Hugpong ng Pagbabago as its chairperson.[^cite_note-115]

### Candidacy and campaign



Marcos and Duterte with the UniTeam slate during their proclamation rally at the [Philippine Arena](32817630.md) on February 8, 2022

On November 13, she filed her candidacy for [Vice President of the Philippines](2271639.md) under Lakas–CMD for the [2022 Philippine vice presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election).[^cite_note-116] She said this was to meet her supporters halfway, who had been previously asking her to run for president, and credited Senator [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos) with convincing her to run for the position.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-ABSCBNNewsCruz-119] She was then adopted and endorsed by [Partido Federal ng Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_Federal_ng_Pilipinas) as the running mate of former senator [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md),[^cite_note-120] and soon afterwards was endorsed by the PRP.[^cite_note-121] Their alliance was named [UniTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniTeam).[^cite_note-ABSCBNNewsCruz-119]

Duterte became the vice president-elect, winning the election with 32,208,417 votes with a margin of 22 million over her closest rival, Senator [Francis Pangilinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Pangilinan).[^cite_note-122] She became the first vice president to be elected by a majority since the [1986 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Philippine_presidential_election) and the largest majority since [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_presidential_election). She also earned the most votes for any office in a single-winner election in Philippine history.[^cite_note-WaPo-123] She was also the first vice president from Davao City,[^cite_note-124] the youngest to become vice president at the age of 44,[^cite_note-125] the third woman to hold the post after Gloria Macapagal Arroyo and [Leni Robredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leni_Robredo), the third vice president who is a child of a president after [Salvador Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Laurel) and Arroyo, the third vice president to come from [Mindanao](20546.md) after [Emmanuel Pelaez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Pelaez) and [Teofisto Guingona Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teofisto_Guingona_Jr.), and the fourth Cebuano-speaking vice president overall (after [Sergio Osmeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a), [Carlos P. Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_P._Garcia), and Pelaez). She and Marcos were also the first presidential ticket to win together since the [2004 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Philippine_presidential_election).[^cite_note-proclamation-may25-126][^cite_note-127]

### Programs and policies



Duterte taking her oath of office before Associate Justice [Ramon Paul Hernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Paul_Hernando) in Davao City on June 19, 2022, with her parents [Elizabeth Zimmerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Zimmerman) and outgoing President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) as witnesses



Duterte with U.S. Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) in [Manila](184334.md) on November 21, 2022

In her first day as the vice president, Duterte established satellite offices for the [Office of the Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Vice_President_of_the_Philippines) (OVP) in [Cebu City](285868.md), [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan), [Davao City](489401.md), [Tacloban](746499.md), [Tandag](877402.md), and [Zamboanga City](25167165.md).[^cite_note-134] The seat of the Office of the Vice President would then be transferred from [Quezon City Reception House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_City_Reception_House) to [Cybergate Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinsons_Cybergate) in [Mandaluyong](359686.md),[^cite_note-135] which is closer to the headquarters of the [Department of Education](5464548.md) (DepEd) in [Pasig](359702.md) where she also held office as its secretary.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

On August 3, 2022, Duterte, alongside the [Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation_\(Philippines\)), launched the Libreng Sakay Program (lit. transl. Free Rides Program) of the OVP, providing free rides as an effort to de-congest the roads during peak hours. It initially launched five buses bestowed by the department,[^cite_note-138] deploying two in [Metro Manila](72340.md) (plying the [EDSA Carousel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Carousel) route) and one each in [Bacolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod), [Cebu](160278.md) (plying [Mandaue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaue), [Lapu-Lapu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapu-Lapu_City), and Cebu City), and Davao City.[^cite_note-139]

On October 9, 2022, the OVP, in collaboration with the DepEd and the [Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_\(Philippines\)), began deploying "Kalusugan Food Trucks" (lit. transl. Health Food Trucks) in select areas nationwide to address malnutrition among school children, wherein the trucks regularly stop at identified schools and institutions with malnutrition and hunger problems among children, with the project following a 120-day feeding program menu recommended by the DOH's National Nutrition Council.[^cite_note-140]

On March 9, 2023, Duterte launched the "Mag Negosyo Ta 'Day" program which aims to financially support women and [LGBTQIA+](78174325.md) members, alongside other marginalized sectors, by facilitating a business-friendly environment and encouraging the growth of micro, small, and medium enterprises. The program was adopted by the OVP from Duterte's similar initiative during her tenure as mayor of Davao City.[^cite_note-141]

Following her designation as the co-vice chairperson of the [National Task Force to End Local Communist Armed Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Task_Force_to_End_Local_Communist_Armed_Conflict) (NTF-ELCAC) on May 11, 2023, she issued a warning to communist rebels and said the rebels' "so-called protracted war" must end.[^cite_note-142] On May 29, Duterte kicked off a nationwide distribution of one million bags containing school supplies and dental kits as well as the planting of one million trees under the OVP's “PagbaBAGo: A Million Learners and Trees” program in partnership with DepEd and the [Department of Environment and Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Environment_and_Natural_Resources). As of October 2023, a total of 64,860 PagbaBAGo bags were distributed by the OVP to school children and planted 178,167 trees.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-\:3-144]

From July 2022 to October 2023, Duterte's OVP was able to provide ₱1.2 billion worth of medical assistance to 106,958 beneficiaries, with the office's burial assistance program providing ₱130.3 million worth of assistance to 22,470 families. Within the same time period, the office's Libreng Sakay Program served 523,263 commuters in Metro Manila, 89,605 commuters in Bacolod City, 60,409 commuters in Davao City, and 99,633 commuters in Cebu City. The OVP's Disaster Operations Center also conducted 162 relief operations and extended assistance worth ₱69.1 million to 115,045 families in times of disasters and calamities.[^cite_note-\:3-144]

### Secretary of Education (2022–2024)



Duterte and her predecessor as Secretary of Education, [Leonor Briones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonor_Briones), during the turnover ceremony at the DepEd headquarters in [Pasig](359702.md)



Duterte with [Brunei Minister of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Brunei\)) Romaizah Salleh

As the concurrent Education Secretary, Duterte was responsible for planning further the transition to the resumption of mandatory face-to-face classes at all basic education schools in the Philippines, which was put on hold since 2020 due to the risks brought out by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Philippines), for the school year 2022–2023.[^cite_note-152] She issued her first department order, the Department Order No. 034 dated July 11, 2022, wherein schools may either opt for five-day in-person classes or blended modality from the opening of classes on August 22 to October 31 before shifting to mandatory in-person classes observing [physical distancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_distancing) when necessary by November 2 onwards.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] She also declared that school uniforms and vaccination among students would be optional for the upcoming school year.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] She also mulled institutionalizing [blended learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blended_learning) only in select schools and areas with special circumstances, including schools with possibly unrepaired or unfinished buildings.[^cite_note-157] Earlier, before taking office, Duterte also called the reinstatement of the mandatory [Reserve Officers’ Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps_\(Philippines\)) (ROTC), which would later gain more support especially from legislators and government officials.[^cite_note-158]

On November 25, 2022, Duterte established the Learner Rights and Protection Office (LRPO) under DepEd and launched its Telesafe Contact Center Helpline to address child abuse and strengthen child protection.[^cite_note-159]

On January 20, 2023, Duterte presented the “current state” of the country's basic education through the Basic Education Report (BER) 2023, wherein she identified the challenges faced by the department when it came to the delivery of basic education and outlined the plans that are set to be implemented by the department.[^cite_note-160] As the Secretary of Education, Duterte was elected as the President of the [Southeast Asian Ministers of Education Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Ministers_of_Education_Organization) (SEAMEO) succeeding Singapore's Education Minister, Chan Chun Sing, on February 8, 2023, during the 52nd SEAMEO Council Meeting held in Manila.[^cite_note-161]

To maintain a learning-focused environment and ensure teachers are not burdened with non-work-related matters, in March 2023, Duterte signed a department order prohibiting teachers from joining volunteer work and extracurricular activities during school hours.[^cite_note-162] In May that year, she said she intended to include mandatory [scouting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouting) in the K-12 curriculum for young Filipino boys to instill in them an enduring love for the country.[^cite_note-163] In July 2023, Duterte signed a department order starting a National Learning Recovery Program designed to improve students’ numeracy and literacy and strengthen the education sector's learning recovery.[^cite_note-164]



Duterte during the 2nd [PSHS-ZPRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Science_High_School_Zamboanga_Peninsula_Region_Campus) Commencement Exercises in [Dipolog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipolog) on June 6, 2023

In August 2023, DepEd launched a new "less congested" and revised version of K–12, called the "*Matatag* curriculum". This version of the K–12 reduced the learning areas for students from seven to five and removed Mother Tongue as a separate subject; it also emphasized a "*Makabansa*" learning area to instill Filipino identity and [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_nationalism) among students. It will be implemented in phases from 2024 to 2028 on Kinder and Grades 1 to 10.[^cite_note-165] The *Matatag* curriculum pilot implementation was well received by teachers, students, and parents.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] The *Matatag* Agenda and the Basic Education Development Plan 2030 as formulated by the DepEd under Duterte was later approved by President Marcos as the national policy and plan for basic education on April 27, 2024.[^cite_note-168]

In September 5, 2023, Duterte announced that school teachers nationwide would have a 30-day straight break without volunteer work for the School Year 2023–2024.[^cite_note-169] In the same month, the DepEd also signed a memorandum of agreement with the [Government Service Insurance System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Service_Insurance_System) (GSIS) establishing more express lanes in all GSIS offices for teachers and DepEd personnel.[^cite_note-170] Following reports citing the Commission on Audit which flagged DepEd for failing to remit nearly P5 billion in premium contributions and loan amortizations to the GSIS in 2022, DepEd stated in December 2023 that it has conducted reconciliation activities with the GSIS regarding its balance at all levels of the department.[^cite_note-171]

Other reforms implemented by Duterte as education secretary was the establishment of a procurement strand within the department to streamline service delivery and resource acquisition, as well as the creation of a school infrastructure and facilities strand to tackle the shortage of classrooms and equipment, which resulted in the construction of 2,201 classrooms, 45 Last Mile School classrooms, and 880 health facilities by the end of 2023,[^cite_note-\:1-172] although this fell short of the 5,000 to 6,000 classroom target previously set by the department for the year.[^cite_note-173] She had also signed a memorandum of agreement with non-profit organization Go Negosyo in November 2023 to teach Filipino youth about business and agriculture through various programs.[^cite_note-174]

Under her tenure, DepEd also aimed to enhance the digital infrastructure of schools. Satellite internet was provided to 2,000 schools, while 25 schools were selected for [Starlink](45111627.md) connections as of 2023. The DepEd also introduced "Digital Education Learning Carts" and mobile computer labs equipped with laptops, charging carts, and [Smart TVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_TV).[^cite_note-\:1-172] However, the budget for the department's Computerization Fund remained underutilized, with only ₱10.2 billion being obligated out of the ₱20.5 billion allocation in the 2023 budget.[^cite_note-175] Additionally, only ₱2.18 billion out of ₱11.36 billion allocated for computers, laptops, and television sets had been utilized. Thousands of laptops intended for personnel were also not delivered. By 2023, the ratio between the number of teachers for each computer remained at 1 to 30, while the student to computer ratio was 1 to 9.[^cite_note-176]

Under Duterte's leadership, the Philippines ranked bottom among 64 countries in the creative thinking assessment of the [Programme for International Student Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment) (PISA), with a mean score of 14, compared to the global average of 33. The assessment for which the rankings are based on were done in May 2022, before she took office as education secretary.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] Duterte said that the results revealed an “uncomfortable truth” about the state of country’s education system, but that efforts were in place to improve future PISA results.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

On January 25, 2024, Duterte delivered her second BER.[^cite_note-182] The following day, Duterte signed a department order which removed administrative tasks from teachers’ workloads with the aim of enabling teachers to maximize their time in actual classroom teaching.[^cite_note-183] This was then augmented by the signing of an additional order on April 30 which limited teachers' work hours to eight hours a day, six of which are devoted to actual classroom teaching while the remaining two is reserved for ancillary tasks.[^cite_note-184]

On May 10, 2024, the DepEd signed a Joint Memorandum Circular with the [Technical Education and Skills Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_Education_and_Skills_Development_Authority) (TESDA), [Commission on Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Higher_Education) (CHED), and [Department of Labor and Employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Labor_and_Employment_\(Philippines\)) (DOLE) for the country's senior high school graduates to be provided with free national certification assessments, the accreditation of DepEd senior high schools as assessment centers, along with the training and certification of technical vocational livelihood (TVL) teachers as Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) competency assessors, as a means of boosting the employment opportunities of the country's senior high school students.[^cite_note-185]

Amid a [worsening rift between the Marcos and Duterte political clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_constitutional_reform_attempts_in_the_Philippines), on June 19, 2024, Duterte resigned as Education Secretary. She formally handed over the post to her successor, Senator [Sonny Angara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Angara), on July 18, 2024.[^cite_note-186]

### Rift with Marcos and the House



Duterte (left) and President Marcos (right) during the 45th [Philippine National Police Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Police_Academy) Commencement Exercises, Camp General Mariano N. Castañeda, [Silang, Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silang,_Cavite), April 19, 2024

On May 19, 2023, Duterte resigned from [Lakas–CMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakas%E2%80%93CMD), stating that her leadership "cannot be poisoned by political toxicity." Her resignation came a few days after her political ally [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo), was demoted from her senior [deputy speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Speaker_of_the_House_of_Representatives_of_the_Philippines) position in the House of Representatives[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] following rumors that Arroyo allegedly planned to unseat Bongbong Marcos' cousin, [House Speaker](7835723.md) [Martin Romualdez](45505193.md).[^cite_note-189] In a speech in June 2023, Duterte acknowledged Marcos' support for her but stated that Marcos' middle name, Romualdez, was "unmentionable" amid reports of a falling out between her and the Speaker.[^cite_note-190]

Duterte's relationship with the House worsened when her office's, as well as the education department's, use of confidential funds was questioned by the [Philippine House Committee on Appropriations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_House_Committee_on_Appropriations) and the [Philippine Senate Committee on Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Finance).[^cite_note-VPRequestsFunds-191] The House would later remove her requested confidential funds from the proposed 2024 budget,[^cite_note-192] while Duterte later rescinded her request for confidential funds, citing their "divisiveness."[^cite_note-193]

In January 2024, former senator [Antonio Trillanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Trillanes) claimed that Duterte was included as a “secondary respondent" in a crime against humanity case pending before the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) (ICC). Arturo Lascañas, a retired police officer in Davao City, implicated Duterte as having had a role in [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing) (EJKs) after succeeding her father, Rodrigo Duterte, to become Davao City mayor in 2010. Duterte responded by saying that she never engaged with the [Davao Death Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Death_Squad) (DDS) throughout her terms as Davao City mayor and vice mayor, and that she would face any charges only before judges and courts in the Philippines.[^cite_note-194]

In early 2024, Duterte lamented that she was being subjected to an intensified "demolition job" meant to destroy her reputation as a public servant.[^cite_note-195] Amid a [worsening rift between the Marcos and Duterte political clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_constitutional_reform_attempts_in_the_Philippines), on June 19, 2024, Duterte resigned as Secretary of Education and co-vice chairperson of the NTF-ELCAC.[^cite_note-SaraResignsDepEd-196] Shortly after, Marcos announced that Senator [Sonny Angara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Angara) would replace Duterte as the new DepEd Secretary.[^cite_note-197] Duterte's resignation took effect on July 19, 2024.[^cite_note-SaraResignsDepEd-196]

Duterte skipped Marcos' [State of the Nation Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_State_of_the_Nation_Address_\(Philippines\)) (SONA) on July 22, 2024, claiming to have appointed herself as the "[designated survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_survivor)", a term she did not elaborate but is related to a contingency plan in the [United States presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_line_of_succession).[^cite_note-designated-198] She went to [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol) for Bohol Day and attended the wake of Bohol Vice Governor Dionisio Victor Balite, who died on July 17.[^cite_note-199] A day after Marcos' SONA, the [Philippine National Police](1313055.md) (PNP) relieved all 75 police officers previously assigned for Duterte's security,[^cite_note-200] reducing her from a record-high of around 400, the most for any Philippine vice president in history.[^cite_note-201] Despite the recall, she still retains over 300 security personnel, more than those assigned to Marcos.[^cite_note-202] She described the recall as “a clear case of political harassment” and accused PNP [Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Philippine_National_Police) [Rommel Marbil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rommel_Marbil) of lying. Marbil added that the recall was due to the absence of security threats against her and denied any "political pressure" influencing the decision.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204]

On July 24, 2024, Duterte left the country for [Germany](11867.md) for a personal trip with her mother, husband, and children some hours prior to the southwestern monsoon, enhanced by [Typhoon Gaemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Gaemi) (locally called Carina), which caused significant rainfall and affected [Luzon](69375.md). She had received travel authority from the Office of the President on July 9. The OVP noted that while the trip's timing was unfortunate, the Disaster Operations Center remained ready to aid those affected by the typhoon, including a relief operation in [Quezon City](284223.md).[^cite_note-205] Duterte revealed that during her trip to Germany, she spent her time with family and met with the Filipino community group Hakbang ng Maisug International Germany (HMIG) in [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-206] Her departure faced criticism from netizens and Representative [Edcel Lagman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edcel_Lagman) ([Albay–1st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albay's_1st_congressional_district)) for perceived insensitivity during the crisis,[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208] though Senator [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos) defended her, stating that Duterte didn't know that there would be a storm.[^cite_note-209]

On August 7, 2024, Duterte publicly criticized the Marcos government and the House of Representatives over issues such as failures in disaster management, policing, healthcare reform, and the possible involvement of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) in investigating the [Philippine drug war](51032591.md) spearheaded by her father as president.[^cite_note-210] Later that month, during the budget hearing for the OVP's 2025 budget, Duterte refused to answer questions regarding her office's budget.[^cite_note-211] Unlike in previous years, where the House swiftly approved the OVP budget,[^cite_note-212] the approval of the 2025 budget was deferred, while some congressmen criticized Duterte for her behavior during the hearing.[^cite_note-213] Duterte nor any representative from her office did not attend the second budget hearing on September 10, and approval for the OVP budget was deferred again.[^cite_note-214]



Duterte (far right) refused to take an oath at a congressional hearing on her [office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Vice_President_of_the_Philippines)'s budget utilization in September 2024.

On September 18, 2024, Duterte attended the House's inquiry about her office's alleged misuse of funds and refused to take an oath and answer questions.[^cite_note-215] Additionally, when asked about her friendship with Marcos, she said they were never friends and merely running mates in the 2022 elections.[^cite_note-216] Marcos responded, expressing he was "deceived" about their supposed friendship.[^cite_note-217] On October 18, 2024, Duterte held a press conference at her office, where she discussed her fallout with Marcos, claiming he "does not know how to be president" and has led the country "on the road to hell."[^cite_note-218] She also revealed that she once told Senator Imee Marcos that if attacks against her persisted, she would exhume former President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos)' remains and discard them in the [West Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philippine_Sea).[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

The lower chamber's probe against Duterte and her office's alleged misuse of funds continued by November 2024. Several of Duterte's staff were cited in contempt for refusing to attend these hearings.[^cite_note-222] On the sixth hearing, Duterte's [chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_to_the_Vice_President_of_the_Philippines), [Zuleika T. Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuleika_T._Lopez), was cited in contempt for undue interference; she would later be detained at a detention facility within the [Batasang Pambansa Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa_Complex).[^cite_note-223] Duterte stayed with Lopez overnight on November 21 and 22, despite only being allowed by the House to visit Lopez and not stay with her for the duration of her detainment.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] When Lopez was due to be transferred on the evening of November 22 by House authorities to the [Correctional Institution for Women](46469171.md), Duterte obstructed the proceeding and stayed in the room where Lopez is detained,[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227] after which Lopez initiated a midnight press conference through [Zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_\(software\)).[^cite_note-228]

### Threat to assassinate Marcos

&#x20;In the early morning of November 23, Duterte made an expletive-laden[^cite_note-\$ABS_241123_sara-229][^cite_note-\$ABS_241123_sara2-230][^cite_note-polto-231] tirade against the Marcos administration during Lopez's press conference, wherein she claimed to have spoken with a [contract killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_killing) to target President Marcos, his wife [Liza](70769981.md), and House Speaker Romualdez in the event of her assassination.[^cite_note-ABS-CBNDomingo-232][^cite_note-INQSigales-233] She made the claim in response to a vlogger's question over her security.[^cite_note-234] Later that day, the administration deemed her threat against the president to be "clear and unequivocal" and thus treated as an "active threat" against the government.[^cite_note-ABS-CBNDomingo-232][^cite_note-INQSigales-233] Duterte later clarified that her remarks were not serious threats but rather a reflection of her fear for her personal safety after hearing threats against her.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] Marcos pledged to prevent any "criminal attempts," but in response, Duterte stated she would not overlook the actions of the Marcos administration against her. She also claimed that the [Marcos family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_family) was behind the [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ninoy_Aquino) of former senator [Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino).[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] Romualdez called Duterte's claim of hiring a contract killer a direct threat to democracy. He also referred to the threat as "alarming and unprecedented."[^cite_note-239] On November 26, 2024, the [National Bureau of Investigation](2489044.md) issued a [subpoena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subpoena) to Duterte regarding her alleged assassination threat,[^cite_note-240] which she eventually ignored.[^cite_note-241] On September 4, 2026, a court in [Quezon City](284223.md) issued an arrest warrant for grave threats against Duterte over her statements, posting bail the following day.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

### Confidential funds controversy

Main article: [Sara Duterte confidential funds misuse allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte_confidential_funds_misuse_allegations)

In 2022 and 2023, Duterte made use of confidential and intelligence funds for both the OVP and DepEd; the OVP under her predecessor did not make use of confidential funds. According to Duterte's chief of staff, due to the OVP's compartmentalized structure, she handled her confidential funds in direct coordination with her office's special disbursing officer Gina F. Acosta without any intermediary.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-stashed-245][^cite_note-246]

In 2022, the OVP had requested ₱250 million, although only half, ₱125 million, was granted.[^cite_note-247] The report of the [Commission on Audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Audit) (COA) later revealed that the entire fund was spent within 11 days.[^cite_note-248] COA has also issued a notice of disallowance on the ₱73 million worth of such confidential funds in 2022.[^cite_note-249] In 2023, the OVP was the fourth highest spender of confidential funds, spending a total of ₱375 million within the year, more than the combined total of confidential funds from the [National Intelligence Coordinating Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Coordinating_Agency), [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(Philippines\)), and the [National Bureau of Investigation](2489044.md).[^cite_note-250]

During the plenary deliberations for the 2024 national budget in September 2023, Duterte requested Congress for confidential and intelligence funds worth ₱500 million for the OVP and ₱150 million for the DepEd. DepEd defended the use of confidential funds, stating that it was for gathering information and intelligence. The request would later be scrutinized by both chambers of Congress. Duterte dismissed claims that these funds were used inappropriately.[^cite_note-VPRequestsFunds-191] She also defended the use of confidential funds, and branded critics against it as enemies of peace and the nation.[^cite_note-251] In November 2023, Duterte eventually dropped both of her requests for confidential funds in response to the request being "divisive".[^cite_note-VPGivesUpFunds-252]

In 2024, the lower chamber's [Committee on Good Government and Public Accountability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_House_Committee_on_Good_Government_and_Public_Accountability) started investigations against the vice president's alleged misuse of funds after the approval of the 2025 OVP budget was deferred. On September 25, a former DepEd undersecretary alleged that Duterte gave out cash gifts worth ₱50 thousand monthly;[^cite_note-253] later on, another former official also came forward, claiming that they received similar envelopes but had stopped by late 2023, around the same time the issue of confidential funds was raised.[^cite_note-254] By November 2024, acknowledgement receipts from her offices submitted to the COA were revealed. Several lawmakers have expressed their doubts on the authenticity of the receipts, highlighting irregularities such as uncommon and allegedly fictitious names as well as discrepancies in the dates and signatures.[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256] Duterte claimed that she has not seen the receipts.[^cite_note-257] On December 9, 2024, the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority) reported that the names of 60% of the 677 individuals that were named as recipients of confidential funds from DepEd had no records in the national [civil registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_registry).[^cite_note-258]

On December 5, 2025, [Commission on Audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Audit) released the 2024 audit report, that there were no findings of loss or wastage of government funds or property of the financial statements of Duterte as of December 31, 2024.[^cite_note-259] In April 2026, [Ramil Madriaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramil_Madriaga), a former security aide of vice president Duterte, claimed that Duterte's 125 million confidential funds from her December 2022 withdrawal were used within one day, and not 11 days as indicated by the records.[^cite_note-260]

Gadon stated that President Marcos did not support the filing of the disbarment case against Duterte. ### Impeachments

#### First impeachment

Main article: [First impeachment of Sara Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_impeachment_of_Sara_Duterte)

On December 2, 2024, an impeachment complaint was filed in the House of Representatives against Duterte for 24 offenses including failure to account for her spending of confidential funds, involvement in extrajudicial killings and threatening the assassination of President Marcos and Romualdez.[^cite_note-262] A second impeachment complaint was filed against her on December 4 for misuse of confidential funds.[^cite_note-263] On December 19, a third impeachment complaint was filed against Duterte for betrayal of public trust.[^cite_note-264]

Despite growing calls for Duterte's impeachment,[^cite_note-265] Marcos has publicly voiced his opinion against impeaching her, calling it "a storm in a teacup" and considering the move inconsequential to the lives of Filipinos.[^cite_note-266] Consequently, the [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) on December 4 has announced that it plans to hold a rally to oppose the impeachment efforts concurring with President Marcos' stance in November 2024 that efforts to remove Duterte from office is unconstructive.[^cite_note-267] On January 13, 2025, more than 1.5 million INC members went to the "[National Rally for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rally_for_Peace)" at the [Quirino Grandstand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirino_Grandstand) in Manila;[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] 12 other sites also staged the rally across the country.[^cite_note-270]

On February 5, 2025, the [House of Representatives](293605.md) consolidated the three filed complaints into one complaint and voted to impeach Duterte on charges that include corruption, plotting to assassinate President Marcos, involvement in [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing) and incitement to insurrection and public disorder. The measure passed after 215 lawmakers voted in favor, significantly above the minimum 102 votes needed in the chamber.[^cite_note-impeachement-16] Although the verified complaint was immediately submitted to the Senate within the same day, Senate President [Francis Escudero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Escudero) was noted to have taken a controversial interpretation of the constitutional term "forthwith" in holding an impeachment trial in the Senate, delaying the potential start of the trial to June when congressional sessions are resumed.[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273][^cite_note-274]

On June 10, 2025, the Senate convened as an impeachment court, but soon remanded the complaint back to the House of Representatives, requesting that the latter first confirm the impeachment's constitutionality and that it ask the then-upcoming [20th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Congress_of_the_Philippines) if it was willing to continue the impeachment.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276] A day later, the House adopted a resolution affirming that their impeachment complaint did not violate the constitution, and carried a motion deferring its acceptance of the remanded impeachment articles.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] The [Supreme Court](1335534.md) later unanimously nullified the complaint on July 25, 2025, ruling that it was "unconstitutional" for alleged procedural issues and not allowing Duterte to defend herself when the impeachment was filed.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280] The decision, as written by Justice [Marvic Leonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvic_Leonen), has been heavily criticized by legal experts and former Supreme Court justices due to a conspicuous factual error and the addition of criteria for impeachment, warning that the latter would effectively make the future filing of impeachment complaints against government officials more difficult.[^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] Due to constitutional restrictions, no further impeachment complaint can be filed against Duterte until February 6, 2026, one year after her impeachment.[^cite_note-284] On August 4, 2025, the House of Representatives filed a motion for reconsideration to the Supreme Court regarding its ruling,[^cite_note-285] which was upheld in January 2026.[^cite_note-286] Despite the motion, the Senate voted to archive Duterte's impeachment on August 6, 2025, with Escudero arguing that the case can be revived when the Supreme Court reverses its initial decision.[^cite_note-287]

#### Second impeachment

Main article: [Second impeachment of Sara Duterte](82338655.md)

On February 2, 2026, two new impeachment complaints were filed against Duterte in the House of Representatives on charges of betrayal of public trust in the usage of confidential funds.[^cite_note-288] On May 11, 2026, a majority of the House of Representatives voted to impeach Duterte, sending the case to a Senate trial.[^cite_note-289]

Ahead of the trial, a pro-Duterte Senate majority installed the Duterte-friendly [Alan Peter Cayetano](10220741.md) as Senate president.[^cite_note-290] Senator [Ronald dela Rosa](50966529.md), who was [evading arrest on ICC charges in the Senate building](83172418.md), cast the deciding vote.[^cite_note-291]

### Arrest of Rodrigo Duterte

Main article: [Arrest of Rodrigo Duterte](79430656.md)



Duterte addresses supporters at [Malieveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malieveld) in [The Hague](30269.md), Netherlands, on March 23, 2025, calling for her father's release and return to the Philippines.

On March 11, 2025, Sara's father, former Philippine president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md), was arrested in Manila on the basis of an [International Criminal Court](14880.md) (ICC) warrant charging him with [crimes against humanity](61099.md).[^cite_note-292] Sara Duterte released a statement that her father's detention was "a blatant affront to our sovereignty and an insult to every Filipino who believes in our nation’s independence".[^cite_note-293] Shortly after 11 p.m. [PHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time) ([UTC+08\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+08\:00)), a government-chartered private jet[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295] carrying Duterte along with his legal counsel, [Salvador Medialdea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Medialdea), departed from [Ninoy Aquino International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport). Minutes after, President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) held a televised press conference where he revealed that the flight would eventually continue to [The Hague](30269.md) in the Netherlands, where Duterte was set to be arraigned for the indictment on charges of crimes against humanity at the ICC.[^cite_note-\:0-108][^cite_note-296] The following morning, Sara Duterte herself departed Manila for [Amsterdam](844.md) to support her father.[^cite_note-297] On March 14, 2025, Duterte publicly disclosed that she would not return to Philippines for an unspecified period, stating that she would focus on forming her father's legal team.[^cite_note-saraleaveofabsence-298] The next day, Duterte stated that she would not return to the Philippines until another family relative could arrive in [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Netherlands)[^cite_note-nextofkinstatement-299] to also ensure that her father would not be left alone.[^cite_note-nextofkinstatement-299] On April 7, 2025, she returned to the Philippines after a month of being with her detained father.[^cite_note-returnandtrialexpectationstart-300] Two weeks later, Sara Duterte formally endorsed [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos) and [Camille Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Villar) in the [Senate elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Philippine_Senate_election).[^cite_note-301]

## Publications

Duterte wrote the children's book *Isang Kaibigan* (lit. transl. A Friend), launched in November 2023. With her office proposing a budget of ₱10 million for the distribution of 200,000 copies to public schools as part of the OVP's "PagbaBAGo" campaign, the book has gained controversy[^cite_note-303] over the potential use of public funds to promote her own book and allegations of plagiarism due to similarities with Andy Runton's *Owly: Just a Little Blue* and the main character's similarities with [Canva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canva)'s graphics.[^cite_note-betrayal-304] The OVP has asserted the originality and copyright registration of the book.[^cite_note-305] While intended for educational purposes, critics argued that the book serves as a political tool to enhance Duterte's image, a notion she denied by highlighting the book's focus on children who are not yet eligible to vote.[^cite_note-306] Duterte later announced that she will write another book, this time about "a friend's betrayal."[^cite_note-betrayal-304]

## Personal life



Duterte with her father

Duterte met [Manases "Mans" Carpio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mans_Carpio) while she was attending [San Beda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Beda_University) (now San Beda University); they married at the Santuario de San Antonio in [Forbes Park, Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Park,_Makati), on October 27, 2007.[^cite_note-spotlight-25] Although news reports have often mentioned that they have three children,[^cite_note-307][^cite_note-308][^cite_note-309][^cite_note-310] Duterte had issued a statement in July 2024 mentioning that they have four children.[^cite_note-ABS-CBNVillaruel-2] Manases, a nephew of [Ombudsman](1866220.md) [Conchita Carpio-Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchita_Carpio-Morales) and Supreme Court Senior Associate Justice [Antonio Carpio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Carpio),[^cite_note-311][^cite_note-312] is a legal counsel for Lapanday Foods Corporation.[^cite_note-313][^cite_note-314]

She previously hosted television programs like *[Una Ka BAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_Ka_BAI)* and *[Byaheng DO30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byaheng_DO30)* on [GMA Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DXMJ-TV), a local station of [GMA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMA_Network). *Byaheng DO30* also expanded its airing across Mindanao on GMA Regional TV and internationally via [GMA News TV International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMA_News_TV_International).[^cite_note-315] Duterte is also a fan of [Korean dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_drama).[^cite_note-316][^cite_note-317] In 2017, she stated that her only talent was in writing.[^cite_note-RapplerFeb25-28]

During her political hiatus from 2013 to 2016, Duterte devoted her time as one of the partner lawyers of Carpio & Duterte Lawyers. Planning to join the judiciary, she also passed the Pre-Judicature Program of the Philippine Judicial Academy.[^cite_note-ovpbio-46] Duterte was elected as one of the governors of the [Philippine Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Red_Cross) in 2014.[^cite_note-318] Duterte is a reserve officer in the [Armed Forces of the Philippines](23448.md) with the rank of colonel, being confirmed on March 11, 2020.[^cite_note-\:0-108]

In October 2015, to convince her father to run for president in the [2016 Philippine presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_presidential_election) despite his reluctance due to lack of campaign funds and political machinery, she shaved her head.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-320] On April 18, 2016, in connection with the [rape remark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Duterte_presidential_campaign,_2016#Rape_during_1989_Davao_hostage_crisis) made by her father Rodrigo on one of his presidential candidacy campaigns, Duterte took to her [Instagram](31591547.md) account to admit that she was once a rape victim.[^cite_note-321] However, Rodrigo Duterte dismissed his daughter's admission and referred to her as a "drama queen".[^cite_note-322]

On March 10, 2022, she told supporters at a meet-and-greet that she was part of the LGBT community, with her gender expression being male.[^cite_note-323] Duterte's statement was met with some skepticism by the left-wing LGBT rights group [Bahaghari Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahaghari_Philippines).[^cite_note-324]

In September 2022, Duterte received criticism for allegations that she was using the official presidential helicopter, a [Bell 412](3101264.md), for personal trips to visit her children in Davao. These allegations surfaced after Duterte thanked President Marcos and the [250th Presidential Airlift Wing](27918720.md#Tactical_Commands_and_Air_Wings) in a [Facebook](7529378.md) post for ensuring that she could be at home in time with her children "wherever she may be found in the country". The Philippine Air Force clarified that the Vice President is authorized to use helicopters from the 250th for official functions, denying rumors that Duterte used the helicopter to visit her children in Davao.[^cite_note-VPAuthorizedToUsePresHeli-325]

On June 12, 2024, a neck scar was first noticed on Duterte while she was in [Davao City](489401.md) attending its [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Philippines\)) flag-raising ceremony, which she later claimed as coming from an attempted knife attack ("gurgur") against her. When reporters asked for clarification, she stated that her answer was "very straightforward" and added in jest that the attack was done by "all of those people" ("Silang lahat").[^cite_note-326]
