The **2026 UEFA Champions League final** was the final match of the [2025–26 UEFA Champions League](74127997.md), the 71st season of Europe's premier club [football](10568.md) tournament organised by [UEFA](32332.md), and the 34th season since it was renamed from the [European Champion Clubs' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Champion_Clubs'_Cup) to the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md). It was played at the [Puskás Aréna](27530864.md) in [Budapest](36787.md), Hungary,[^cite_note-appointment-5] on 30 May 2026 between French club [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md), the defending champions, and English club [Arsenal](2174.md). This was the first final to include a titleholder team since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_UEFA_Champions_League_final).

Paris Saint-Germain won the match 4–3 on [penalties](511302.md), following a 1–1 draw after [extra time](633242.md#Association_football), for their second UEFA Champions League title, becoming the second club to win back-to-back titles in the UEFA Champions League era, matching [Real Madrid](26413.md)'s feat in the [2016–17 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_UEFA_Champions_League).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] As winners, Paris Saint-Germain earned the right to play against [Aston Villa](42173.md), the winners of the [2025–26 UEFA Europa League](78224540.md), in the [2026 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_UEFA_Super_Cup). They will also compete in the final of the [2026 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_Intercontinental_Cup), and qualified for the [2029 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2029_FIFA_Club_World_Cup).

## Background

[Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) reached their third European Cup/Champions League final overall and second consecutively, having lost to [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UEFA_Champions_League_final) before winning the title in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Champions_League_final). They also became the first team since [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_FC) in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_UEFA_Champions_League_final) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Champions_League_final) to reach two consecutive Champions League finals. This was their seventh UEFA competition final overall, having also triumphed in the [1996 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final) and [2025 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Super_Cup). They lost the Cup Winners' Cup final in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final) as holders, as well as losing the [1996 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_UEFA_Super_Cup). Paris Saint-Germain was the first French team to appear in three European Cup/Champions League finals, surpassing their [rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique) [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), who lost in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_European_Cup_final) before their victory in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_UEFA_Champions_League_final), as well as [Stade Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_Reims), who lost both the [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_European_Cup_final) and [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_European_Cup_final) European Cup finals to [Real Madrid](26413.md).

[Arsenal](2174.md) reached their second European Cup/Champions League final, having lost to [Barcelona](68187.md) in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_UEFA_Champions_League_final). This was their eighth UEFA competition final overall, having triumphed in the [1994 European Cup Winners' Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final). They lost the Cup Winners' Cup finals in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final) and [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final)—the latter as holders, as well as losing the [1994 European Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_European_Super_Cup), [2000 UEFA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_UEFA_Cup_final), and [2019 UEFA Europa League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Europa_League_final).

Both clubs won their respective leagues and were eliminated from other cup competitions, thus aiming for a [continental double](88184.md#Continental_double). This was the first time since [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UEFA_Champions_League_final) where both Champions League finalists were champions of their respective domestic leagues. The two teams met in the 2025 semi-finals, with Paris Saint-Germain winning both legs (1–0 at the [Emirates Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Stadium) and then 2–1 at the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes)).

The 2026 final was the fourth European Cup final between sides from two different capital cities after [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_European_Cup_final), [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_European_Cup_final) and [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_European_Cup_final).[^cite_note-8]

The "home" team (for administrative purposes) was predetermined as the winners of semi-final 1 (Paris Saint-Germain). This was the first Champions League final to kick off at 18\:00 [CEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Summer_Time), three hours earlier than previous editions. UEFA stated that the change was made to "enhance the matchday experience and benefit fans, teams and host cities".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Venue



The [Puskás Aréna](27530864.md) in [Budapest](36787.md) hosted the final.

### Host selection

On 17 May 2023, UEFA opened the bidding process for the final, which was held in parallel with that of the [2027 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_UEFA_Champions_League_final). Interested bidders could bid for either one or both of the finals. Additionally, bidding associations could only be appointed one UEFA final in a given year. The proposed venues had to include natural grass and be ranked as a [UEFA category four stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_stadium_categories), with a gross capacity of at least 70,000 preferred. The bidding timeline was as follows:[^cite_note-invitation_to_bid-11]

- 17 May 2023: Applications formally invited

- 17 July 2023: Closing date for registering intention to bid

- 26 July 2023: Bid requirements made available to bidders

- 15 November 2023: Submission of preliminary bid dossier

- 21 February 2024: Submission of final bid dossier

- 22 May 2024: Appointment of host

UEFA announced on 18 July 2023 that two associations had expressed interest in hosting the 2026 and 2027 finals during the first bidding process.[^cite_note-12]

## Match

### Summary





Paris Saint-Germain midfielder [Vitinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitinha_\(footballer,_born_February_2000\)) (left) was named [man of the match](906698.md#Association_football), while his opponent midfielder [Kai Havertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Havertz) (right) scored the earliest goal of the match.

In the sixth minute of the match, an attempted [clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#C) by Paris Saint-Germain defender [Marquinhos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquinhos) deflected off Arsenal's [Leandro Trossard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Trossard). The deflected ball fell to Arsenal striker [Kai Havertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Havertz), who beat the Paris Saint-Germain goalkeeper [Matvey Safonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matvey_Safonov) from a tight angle, putting Arsenal 1–0 ahead. It would be their only shot on target throughout the match.[^cite_note-15]

In the 61st minute, Arsenal's [Cristhian Mosquera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristhian_Mosquera) fouled [Khvicha Kvaratskhelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khvicha_Kvaratskhelia) inside the [penalty box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_box). [Ousmane Dembélé](48621084.md) converted the resulting penalty kick, tying the score at 1–1. In the 77th minute, a shot by Kvaratskhelia was deflected onto the post by [Myles Lewis-Skelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myles_Lewis-Skelly).[^cite_note-16] By the end of the second half, Paris Saint-Germain had made 18 attempts on goal (four on target), while Arsenal had made four attempts (one on target). With the score still tied, the game went to [extra time](633242.md#Association_football).

In the 103rd minute, Paris Saint-Germain's [Nuno Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuno_Mendes_\(footballer,_born_2002\)) tackled [Noni Madueke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noni_Madueke) to the ground in his team's penalty area, prompting appeals for a penalty kick from Arsenal players. However, the referee awarded a [free kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_kick) to Paris Saint-Germain instead, adjudging that Madueke had grabbed Mendes' arm,[^cite_note-17] and issued [yellow cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_card#Association_football) to Arsenal player [Declan Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declan_Rice) and manager [Mikel Arteta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Arteta) for their protests.[^cite_note-18] Extra time ended with the score tied 1–1 and the match proceeded to a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md).

Paris Saint-Germain went first in the shoot-out, with [Gonçalo Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Ramos) converting his penalty. This was followed by successful attempts from Arsenal's [Viktor Gyökeres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Gy%C3%B6keres) and Paris Saint-Germain's [Désiré Doué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Dou%C3%A9). The first miss was by Arsenal's [Eberechi Eze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberechi_Eze), who shot wide left, which was immediately followed by Arsenal goalkeeper [David Raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Raya) saving Mendes' attempt. Four successful attempts followed, two for each team, by Rice, [Achraf Hakimi](52004386.md), [Gabriel Martinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Martinelli), and [Lucas Beraldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Beraldo), leaving Paris Saint-Germain ahead 4–3 with one penalty attempt to come before the [sudden-death round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_death_\(sport\)). [Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Magalh%C3%A3es) sent his shot over the bar, giving Paris Saint-Germain the victory.[^cite_note-19]

## Aftermath

Main article: [2026 Paris Saint-Germain celebration riots](83313054.md)

Following PSG's victory, tens of thousands of fans celebrated on the streets of Paris. Violent clashes erupted as the celebrations developed into rioting; there were 283 arrests in Paris and another 133 in the rest of France.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The [French Interior Ministry](871703.md) deployed 22,000 security officers across the country, including 8,000 in the capital. Similar riots [had occurred the previous year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Paris_Saint-Germain_celebration_riots) following [PSG's victory in the 2025 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Champions_League_final#Celebrations_and_riots).[^cite_note-25]
