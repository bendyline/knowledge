**Lashkar-e-Taiba** (**LeT**)[^cite_note-51] is a Pakistani [Islamist](15012.md) [Jihadist](7826589.md) [militant](310790.md) organisation driven by a [Salafi jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) ideology.[^cite_note-Gunaratna-52][^cite_note-Freeman-53] The organisation's primary stated objective is to merge the whole of [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) with Pakistan.[^cite_note-54] It also seeks the destruction of [India](14533.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), and [Judaism](15624.md) through [jihad](16203.md).[^cite_note-\:0-55][^cite_note-\:1-56] It was founded in 1985–1986 by [Hafiz Saeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Muhammad_Saeed), Zafar Iqbal Shehbaz, [Abdullah Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Yusuf_Azzam) and several other Islamist [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen)[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] with funding from [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden)[^cite_note-Atkins-59][^cite_note-Globalecco-34] during the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War). It has been [designated as a terrorist group](63578.md) by the [United Nations](31769.md) and numerous other countries and been responsible for terrorist attacks on civilians in India, such as the [2000 Red Fort attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Red_Fort_attack), [2005 Delhi bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Delhi_bombings), [2006 Mumbai train bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Mumbai_train_bombings), [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks) and the [2025 Pahalgam attack](79778862.md).

It has been supported by Pakistan’s intelligence agency, the [Inter-Services Intelligence](2159021.md) (ISI), and is often viewed as a proxy militant organisation used by [Pakistan](23235.md) against [India](14533.md) in the [insurgency](1915235.md) in Indian-administered [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

Its affiliated front organisations include the [Milli Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milli_Muslim_League), a political party, and Jamat-ud-Dawa (JuD), the group's "charity wing". The group differs from most other militant groups in Pakistan in following the Islamic interpretation of [Ahl-i Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl-i_Hadith) (which is similar to [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) and [Salafism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism)), and in foreswearing attacks on the government of Pakistan and sectarian attacks on Pakistanis "who have professed faith" in Islam.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9][^cite_note-Ishfaq-2019-19][^cite_note-Macander-CSIS-2021-62]

## Objectives

While the primary area of operations of LeT's jihadist activities is the [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley), their professed goal is not limited to challenging India's sovereignty over [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md). LeT sees the issue of [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Conflict) as part of a wider global struggle.[^cite_note-randjan2009-63] Once Kashmir is liberated, LeT seeks to use it "as a base of operations to conquer India and force Muslim rule to the Indian subcontinent."[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

LeT’s ideology is fundamentally anti-Western, with the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) held responsible for the decline of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). Consequently, LeT opposes any form of Western or British influence in Pakistan and the broader South Asian region. In its publications and on various platforms, the organisation has consistently articulated its primary political goals, which include the destruction of India, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), and [Judaism](15624.md). The organisation considers jihad a religious duty for all Muslims, with the establishment of a caliphate as its central religious and political objective.[^cite_note-\:0-55][^cite_note-\:1-56]

[C. Christine Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Christine_Fair), who has analyzed LeT propaganda since 1995, notes that the militant organisation has consistently condemned what it describes as a "Brahmanic-Talmudic-Crusader" alliance of Hindus, Jews, and Christians, whom it accuses of collaborating to undermine the [Ummah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah).[^cite_note-64]

Its followers are proponents of the South Asian group Ahl-e-Hadith (AeH) Islam, which is considered Salafist.[^cite_note-Macander-CSIS-2021-62] It has adopted maximalist agenda of global [jihad](16203.md) including attacks on civilians. The group justifies its ideology on [verse 2\:216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihad_verse) of the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran).

> Fighting has been made obligatory upon you ˹believers˺, though you dislike it. Perhaps you dislike something which is good for you and like something which is bad for you. Allah knows and you do not know.[^cite_note-Q.2\:216-65]

Extrapolating from this verse, the group asserts that military jihad is a religious obligation of all Muslims and defines the many circumstances under which it must be carried out. In a pamphlet entitled "Why Are We Waging Jihad?", the group states that all of India along with many other countries were once ruled by Muslims and were Muslim lands, which is their duty to take it back from the non-Muslims. It declared United States, India, and Israel as "existential enemies of Islam".[^cite_note-haqqani-32][^cite_note-66] LeT believes that jihad is the duty of all Muslims and must be waged until eight objectives are met: Establishing Islam as the dominant way of life in the world, forcing disbelievers to pay [jizya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizya), exacting revenge for killed Muslims, punishing enemies for violating oaths and treaties, defending all Muslim states, and recapturing occupied Muslim territory. The group construes lands once ruled by Muslims as Muslim lands and considers it as their duty to get them back. It embraces a pan-Islamist rationale for military action.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9][^cite_note-haqqani-32]

In the wake of the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks) investigations of computer and email accounts revealed a list of 320 locations worldwide deemed as possible targets for attack. Analysts believed that the list was a statement of intent rather than a list of locations where LeT cells had been established and were ready to strike.[^cite_note-67]

Unlike other Pakistan-based Salafi-jihadist militant organisations, LeT has "publicly \[renounced] sectarian violence against other Islamic sects".[^cite_note-Macander-CSIS-2021-62] While it has waged violent jihad outside of Pakistan, *inside* the country, the group has spent considerable effort and resources on "preaching and social welfare".[^cite_note-tankel201104-9] This along with its professed opposition to not fighting "those who have professed Faith" in Islam (where [thousands of Muslims have been killed in sectarian attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectarian_violence_in_Pakistan)), has built up significant goodwill among Pakistanis, especially pious Muslims and the poor (helping to protect the group from foreign pressure on the Pakistan government to stop LeT's killing of foreigners).[^cite_note-tankel201104-9] Although it views Pakistan's ruling powers as hypocrites (self-proclaimed but insincere Muslims), it doesn't support revolutionary jihad at home because the struggle in Pakistan "is not a struggle between Islam and disbelief". The pamphlet "Why do we do Jihad?" states, "If we declare war against those who have professed Faith, we cannot do war with those who haven’t." The group instead seeks to reform errant Muslims through [dawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawah). It aims to bring Pakistanis to LeT's interpretation of Ahl-e-Hadith Islam and thus, transforming the society in which they live.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

LeT's leaders have argued that Indian-administered [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) was the closest occupied land, and observed that the ratio of occupying forces to the population there was one of the highest in the world, meaning this was among the most substantial occupations of Muslim land. Thus, LeT cadres could volunteer to fight on other fronts but were obligated to fight in Indian-administered Kashmir.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

In January 2009, LeT publicly declared that it would pursue a peaceful resolution in the Kashmir issue and that it did not have global jihadist aims, but the group is still believed to be active in several other spheres of anti-Indian militancy.[^cite_note-Roul-A-68] The disclosures of [Abu Jundal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jundal), who was sent to India by the Saudi Arabian government, however, revealed that LeT is planning to revive militancy in Jammu and Kashmir and conduct major attacks in India.

## History

### Formation

In 1985, [Hafiz Mohammed Saeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Mohammed_Saeed) and Zafar Iqbal[^cite_note-Zafar_Iqbal-1] formed the *Jamaat-ud-Dawa* (Organisation for Preaching, or JuD) as a small missionary group dedicated to promoting an [Ahl-e-Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl-e-Hadith) version of Islam. In the next year, Zaki-ur Rehman Lakvi merged his group of anti-Soviet jihadists with the JuD to form the *Markaz-ud Dawa-wal-Irshad* (Center for Preaching and Guidance, or MDI). The MDI had 17 founders originally, and notable among them was [Abdullah Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Azzam). Azzam would be killed in a [car bombing](155282.md) orchestrated by [Khad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHAD) in 1989.

The LeT was formed in Afghanistan's [Kunar province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_province) in 1990[^cite_note-satp-4] and gained prominence in the early 1990s as a military offshoot of MDI.[^cite_note-cronin-5] MDI's primary concerns were [dawah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawah) and the LeT focused on jihad although the members did not distinguish between the two groups' functions. According to Hafiz Saeed, "Islam propounds both dawa\[h] and jihad. Both are equally important and inseparable. Since our life revolves around Islam, therefore both dawa and jihad are essential; we cannot prefer one over the other."[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

Most of these training camps were located in [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province) (NWFP) and many were shifted to Pakistan-administered [Azad Kashmir](2745.md) for the sole purpose of training volunteers for terrorism in India. From 1991 onward, militancy surged in India, as many Lashkar-e-Taiba volunteers were infiltrated into Indian-administered [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) from Pakistan-administered Azad Kashmir with the help of the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) and ISI.[^cite_note-tellis2010f-42] As of 2010, the degree of control that Pakistani intelligence retains over LeT's operations is not known.

### Designation as terrorist group

On 28 March 2001, in Statutory Instrument 2001 No. 1261, British [Home Secretary](149104.md) [Jack Straw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Straw) designated the group a [Proscribed Terrorist Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2000) under the [Terrorism Act 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2000).[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

On 5 December 2001, the group was added to the [Terrorist Exclusion List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_Act_Terrorist_Exclusion_List). In a notification dated 26 December 2001, [United States Secretary of State](32293.md) [Colin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Powell), designated Lashkar-e-Taiba a [Foreign Terrorist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._State_Department_list_of_Foreign_Terrorist_Organizations).[^cite_note-satp-4]

Lashkar-e-Taiba was banned in Pakistan on 12 January 2002.[^cite_note-bbcprofile-85]

It is banned in India as a designated terrorist group under the [Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlawful_Activities_\(Prevention\)_Act).

It was [listed as a terrorist organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_terrorist_organisations_in_Australia) in Australia under the *Security Legislation Amendment (Terrorism) Act 2002* on 11 April 2003 and was re-listed on 11 April 2005 and 31 March 2007.[^cite_note-ANS1-86][^cite_note-87]

On 2 May 2008, it was placed on the Consolidated List established and maintained by the committee established by the [United Nations Security Council Resolution 1267](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1267) as an entity associated with [al-Qaeda](1921.md). The report also proscribed Jamaat-ud-Dawa as a front group of the LeT.[^cite_note-88] [Bruce Riedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Riedel), an expert on terrorism, believes that LeT with the support of its Pakistani backers is more dangerous than al-Qaeda.[^cite_note-89]

### Aftermath of Mumbai attacks



Pakistani street art of the Lashkar e Taiba

According to a media report, the US accused JuD of being the front group for the prime suspects of the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks), the Lashkar-e-Taiba, the organisation that trained the 10 gunmen involved in these attacks.[^cite_note-90]

On 7 December 2008, under pressure from the US and India, [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) launched an operation against LeT and raided a *markaz* (center) of the LeT at Shawai Nullah, 5 km from [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) in Pakistan administered [Azad Kashmir](2745.md). The army arrested more than twenty members of the LeT including [Zaki-ur-Rehman Lakhvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaki-ur-Rehman_Lakhvi), the alleged mastermind of the Mumbai attacks. They are said to have sealed off the center, which included a [madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah) and a mosque alongside offices of the LeT according to the government of Pakistan.[^cite_note-91]

On 10 December 2008, India formally requested the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) to designate JuD as a terrorist organisation. Subsequently, Pakistan's ambassador to the United Nations Abdullah Hussain gave an undertaking, saying,[^cite_note-92]

> After the designation of Jamaat-ud-Dawah (JUD) under (resolution) 1267, the government on receiving communication from the Security Council shall proscribe the JUD and take other consequential actions, as required, including the freezing of assets.

A similar assurance was given by Pakistan in 2002 when it clamped down on the LeT; however, the LeT was covertly allowed to function under the guise of the JuD. While arrests have been made, the Pakistani government has categorically refused to allow any foreign investigators access to Hafiz Muhammad Saeed.

On 11 December 2008, the United Nations Security Council imposed sanctions on JuD, declaring it a global terrorist group. Saeed, the chief of JuD, declared that his group would challenge the sanctions imposed on it in all forums. Pakistan's government also banned the JuD on the same day and issued an order to seal the JuD in all [four provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Pakistan), as well as Pakistan-administered [Azad Kashmir](2745.md).[^cite_note-93] Before the ban JuD, ran a weekly newspaper named *Ghazwah*, two monthly magazines called *Majalla Tud Dawaa* and *Zarb e Taiba*, and a fortnightly magazine for children, *Nanhe Mujahid*. The publications have since been banned by the Pakistani government. In addition to the prohibition of JuD's print publications, the organisation's websites were also shut down by the Pakistani government.

After the UNSC ban, [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) minority groups in Pakistan came out in support of JuD. At protest marches in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_\(Pakistan\)), Hindu groups said that JuD does charity work such as setting up water wells in desert regions and providing food to the poor.[^cite_note-Reuters_Pak_Hindus_Rally-94][^cite_note-Hindus_rally_for_Muslim_charity-95] However, according to the [BBC](19344654.md), the credibility of the level of support for the protest was questionable as protesters on their way to what they believed was a rally against price rises had been handed signs in support of JuD.[^cite_note-Hindus_rally_for_Muslim_charity-95] The JuD ban has been met with heavy criticism in many Pakistani circles, as JuD was the first to react to the [Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake) and the [Ziarat earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Ziarat_earthquake). It also ran over 160 schools with thousands of students and provided aid in hospitals as well.[^cite_note-aj20100218-96]

In January 2009, JuD spokesperson, [Abdullah Muntazir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Muntazir), stressed that the group did not have global jihadist aspirations and would welcome a peaceful resolution of the Kashmir issue. He also publicly disowned LeT commanders Zaki-ur-Rehman Lakhvi and [Zarrar Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarrar_Shah), who have both been accused of being the masterminds behind the Mumbai attacks.[^cite_note-Roul-A-68]

In response to the UN resolution and the government ban, the JuD reorganised itself under the name of Tehreek-e-Tahafuz Qibla Awal (TTQA).[^cite_note-Roul-A-68]

On 25 June 2014, the United States added several of LeT affiliates including Jamaat-ud-Dawa, Al-Anfal Trust, Tehrik-e-Hurmat-e-Rasool, and Tehrik-e-Tahafuz Qibla Awwal to the list of foreign terrorist organisations.[^cite_note-97]

&#x20;According to Stephen Tankel, writing in 2011,&#x20;

> despite the "chorus" of diplomats, security officials and military officers" calling for Pakistan to clamp down on LeT, Pakistan has and will continue to resist. This is because LeT is "one of the few militant outfits that officially refrain from launching attacks in Pakistan", which, with the group's trained fighters and resources would be very bad for the stability of Pakistan if it did. Secondly, the Pakistani army and its powerful [Inter-Services Intelligence Directorate](2159021.md) (ISI) have long considered LeT to be the country’s most reliable proxy against India and the group still provides utility in this regard as well as the potential for leverage at the negotiating table. Thus, the consensus is that, at least in the short-term, taking steps to dismantle the group would chiefly benefit India, while Pakistan would be left to deal with the costs.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

### Milli Muslim League

Main article: [Milli Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milli_Muslim_League)

[Jamaat-ud-Dawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-ud-Dawa) members on 7 August 2017 announced the creation of a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) called *Milli Muslim League*. Tabish Qayoum, a JuD activist working as the party spokesman, stated they had filed registration papers for a new party with Pakistan's electoral commission.[^cite_note-98] Later in August, JuD under the banner of the party fielded a candidate for the 2017 by-election of [Constituency NA-120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency_NA-120). Muhammad Yaqoob Sheikh filed his nomination papers as an independent candidate.[^cite_note-99]

The registration application of the party was rejected by ECP on 12 October.[^cite_note-100] [Hafiz Saeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Saeed) announced in December, a few days after release from house arrest on 24 November, that his organisation will contest the [2018 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistani_general_election).[^cite_note-101]

### Name changes

In February 2019, after the Pulwama attack, the Pakistan government placed the ban once again on Jamat-ud-Dawa and its charity organisation [Falah-e-Insaniat Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falah-e-Insaniat_Foundation) (FIF).[^cite_note-TH_Feb2019-102] To evade the ban, their names were changed to Al Madina and Aisar Foundation respectively and they continued their work as before.[^cite_note-DailyTimesRename-103]

### People's Anti-Fascist Front

Main article: [People's Anti-Fascist Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Anti-Fascist_Front)

The PAFF was originally thought to be a faction of Lashkar-e-Taiba according to Indian officials.[^cite_note-104] The Indian police claimed it is an offshoot of [Jaish-e-Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Muhammad).[^cite_note-105] The PAFF was created during the wake of the [2019 Kashmir Protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Kashmir_Protests) after the [revocation of autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revocation_of_the_special_status_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir) of the [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] The PAFF has claimed responsibility of many attacks in Kashmir against Indian forces.[^cite_note-108]

### Influence in Kashmir

Main article: [Insurgency in Jammu and Kashmir](1915235.md)

After the [Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) victory against the [Soviet Union occupation in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet-Afghan_war), Lashkar-e-Taiba and Mujahideen fighters, with the aid of [Pakistan](23235.md), slowly infiltrated [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) with the goal of spreading a radical [Islamist Ideology](15012.md) to [Jihad](16203.md) against [Indian administration in Jammu and Kashmir](1915235.md).[^cite_note-Dawn-Who_is_Lashkar-e-Tayyiba-109]

In July 2025, [Indian security forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Security_Forces) carried out a high-profile counter-terrorism operation near [Srinagar](294148.md), killing three [Pakistani](28171281.md) terrorists affiliated with Lashkar-e-Taiba. Among those neutralised was Suleiman Shah, identified as the mastermind behind the 22 April [Pahalgam terror attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahalgam_attack) that claimed 26 civilian lives. The operation, codenamed Operation Mahadev, also eliminated two other LeT operatives—Abu Hamza and Yasir, both linked to the same attack.

Suleiman, a former [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) soldier, had a ₹20 lakh (2,000,000) bounty on his head and was classified as a high-value target. The joint operation was launched by the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), [CRPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRPF_Valley_QAT), and [Jammu and Kashmir Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Police) based on specific intelligence in the Mulnar area of Harwan. The success of the operation is seen as a significant setback to Lashkar-e-Taiba, coming shortly after the [United Nations](31769.md) banned [The Resistance Front](74821509.md) (TRF), a proxy outfit of LeT.[^cite_note-110]

## Activities

The group operates terrorist training camps and does humanitarian work. Across Pakistan, the organisation runs 16 Islamic institutions, 135 secondary schools, an ambulance service, mobile clinics, blood banks and seminaries according to the South Asia Terrorism Portal.[^cite_note-satp-4]

In 2002, the group's annual religious congregation (*[ijtema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijtema)*) was the second-largest gathering among Pakistan's religious parties, with around 1 million participants, after [Tablighi Jamaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablighi_Jamaat) (2 million) but ahead of other traditional Islamist parties such as the [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_Pakistan) (500,000).[^cite_note-shafqat2002-111]

The group actively carries out suicide attacks on [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md) in Jammu and Kashmir.

Some breakaway Lashkar-e-Taiba members have been accused of carrying out attacks in Pakistan, particularly in [Karachi](17123.md), to mark its opposition to the policies of former president [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf).[^cite_note-bbcprofile-85][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

### Publications

[Christine Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Fair) estimates that, through its editing house Dar al Andalus, "LeT is perhaps the most prolific producer of *[jihadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadi)* literature in Pakistan." By the end of the 90s, the Urdu monthly magazine *Mujallah al-Dawah* had a circulation of 100 000, another monthly magazine, *Ghazwa*, of 20 000, while other weekly and monthly publications target students (*Zarb-e-Tayyaba*), female students (*Tayyabaat*), children and those who are literate in English (*Voice of Islam* and *Invite*) or Arabic (*al-Ribat*). It also published, every year, around 100 booklets, in many languages.[^cite_note-114] It has been described as a "profitable department, selling [lacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh) of books every year."[^cite_note-115]

### Antisemitism

Lashkar-e-Taiba, the Pakistan-based [Islamic terrorist](566821.md) group, has also expressed antisemitic views. The organisation declares the [Jewish people](25955086.md) to be "Enemies of [Islam](6037917.md)" and designates Israel as an "Enemy of Pakistan".[^cite_note-116]

The [Hasidic Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Judaism) ([Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad)) religious centre [Nariman House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nariman_House) in [Mumbai](19189.md) was hit by Lashkar-e-Taiba militants during the 2008 Mumbai attacks in India. The sole surviving perpetrator of the attack, [Ajmal Kasab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmal_Kasab), was eventually captured by Indian security forces.[^cite_note-PakAdmitsNYT-117][^cite_note-118] Antisemitic views were evident from the testimonies of Kasab following his arrest and trial.[^cite_note-Rubenstein2011-119]

### Markaz-e-Taiba

The LeT headquarters called *Markaz-e-Taiba* is in Nangal Saday, about 5 km north of [Muridke](19493547.md), on the eastern side of the G.T. road; about 30 km from [Lahore](125315.md), [Punjab](24751.md). Established in 1988, it is spread over 200 acres (0.81 km<sup>2</sup>) and contains a madrassa, hospital, market, residences, a fish farm and agricultural tracts. The initial sectarian religious training, *Daura-e-Sufa* is imparted here to the militants.[^cite_note-telegraphManipur-120] Supporters of Pakistan-based militant groups, including Lashkar-e-Taiba and the al-Qaeda-linked **313 Brigade**, posted videos on TikTok, YouTube, and Google from here. The videos, verified and geolocated by [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News), showed armed men and children engaging in martial arts training, and captions promoting jihad. Hashtags like "#313" and terms such as "mujahid" were used to signal affiliation with banned groups. Analysts noted that the posts formed part of a coordinated propaganda campaign aimed at recruitment and spreading extremist ideology.[^cite_note-Sky_12_May_2025-121]

In May 2025, the [Indian Airforce](403355.md) bombed the camp as part of [Operation Sindoor](79896622.md) in retaliation for the [Pahalgam attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahalgam_attack).[^cite_note-Sky_12_May_2025-121][^cite_note-122] In a viral video, Lashkar-e-Taiba commander Qaasim vowed to rebuild the destroyed Markaz camp in Muridke "even bigger" following its destruction. Standing amid the rubble, he admitted the camp's role in training numerous Mujahideen and Talaba, and declared that they would reconstruct the facility on a larger scale, reaffirming its importance in producing fighters who, according to him, had achieved “Faiz (Victory).”[^cite_note-123]

### Training camps

The LeT militant training camps are in a number of locations in Pakistan. These camps, which include the base camp, Markaz-e-Taiba in [Muridke](19493547.md) near [Lahore](125315.md) (mentioned above) and the one near Manshera, are used to impart training to militants. In these camps, the following trainings are imparted:

- the 21-day religious course (*Daura-e-Sufa*)

  [^cite_note-telegraphManipur-120]

- the 21-day basic combat course (*Daura-e-Aam*)

  [^cite_note-hindu20081206-36]

- the three-month advanced combat course (*Daura-e-Khaas*)

  [^cite_note-hindu20081206-36]

  [^cite_note-saAnalysis-37]

A [26/11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks) conspirator, [Zabiuddin Ansari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabiuddin_Ansari), alias Abu Jundal, was arrested in 2012 by Indian intelligence agencies and was reported to have disclosed that paragliding training was also included in the training curriculum of LeT cadres at is camps in [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md) in Pakistan-administered [Azad Kashmir](2745.md).[^cite_note-124]

These camps have been tolerated since inception by Pakistan's [Inter-Services Intelligence](2159021.md) (ISI) agency because of their usefulness against India and in Afghanistan although as of 2006 they had been instructed not to mount any operations.[^cite_note-125] A French anti-terrorism expert, Jean-Louis Bruguière, in his *Some Things that I Wasn't Able to Say* has stated that the regular Pakistani army officers trained the militants in the LeT militant training camps until recently. He reached this conclusion after interrogating a French militant, Willy Brigitte, who had been trained by the LeT and arrested in Australia in 2003.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

#### Other training camps

In 1987, LeT established two militant training camps in [Afghanistan](737.md). The first one was the Muaskar-e-Taiba at [Jaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaji_District) in [Paktia Province](457274.md) and the second one was the Muaskar-e-Aqsa in [Kunar Province](2159600.md).[^cite_note-128] US [intelligence analysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_analysts) justify the [extrajudicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial) detention of at least one [Guantanamo detainee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detainment_camp) because they allege he attended a LeT training camp in Afghanistan. A memorandum summarising the factors for and against the continued detention of [Bader Al Bakri Al Samiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_detainees_at_Guantanamo_Bay) asserts that he attended a LeT [training camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_training_camp).

[Mariam Abou Zahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariam_Abou_Zahab) and [Olivier Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Roy_\(political_scientist\)) in their *Islamist Networks: The Afghan-Pakistan Connection* (London: [C. Hurst & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Hurst_&_Co.), 2004) mentioned three training camps in Pakistan-administered [Azad Kashmir](2745.md), the principal one is the Umm-al-Qura training camp at [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md). Every month five hundred militants are trained in these militant camps. Muhammad Amir Rana in his *A to Z of Jehadi Organizations in Pakistan* (Lahore: Mashal, 2004) listed five training camps. Four of them, the Muaskar-e-Taiba, the Muaskar-e-Aqsa, the Muaskar Umm-al-Qura and the Muaskar Abdullah bin Masood are in Pakistan-administered Azad Kashmir and the Markaz Mohammed bin Qasim training camp is in [Sanghar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanghar_District) of [Sindh](28972.md). Ten thousand militants had been trained in these militant camps up to 2004.

As of 2022, Lashkar-e-Taiba maintains multiple training camps in Afghanistan alongside [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed) which are used for both training and recruitment.[^cite_note-JG-129][^cite_note-UR-130]

### Funding

The government of Pakistan began to fund the LeT during the early 1990s and by around 1995 the funding had grown considerably. During this time the army and the ISI helped establish the LeT's military structure with the specific intent to use the militant group against Indians. The LeT also obtained funds through efforts of the MDI's Department of Finance.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

Until 2002, the LeT collected funds through public fundraising events usually using charity boxes in shops. The group also received money through donations at MDI offices, through personal donations collected at public celebrations of an operative's martyrdom, and through its website.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9] The LeT also collected donations from the Pakistani immigrant community in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and United Kingdom, Islamic Non-Governmental Organizations, and Pakistani and Kashmiri businessmen.[^cite_note-satp-4][^cite_note-tankel201104-9][^cite_note-131] LeT operatives have also been apprehended in India, where they had been obtaining funds from sections of the Muslim community.[^cite_note-132]

Although many of the funds collected went towards legitimate uses, e.g. factories and other businesses, a significant portion was dedicated to military activities. According to US intelligence, the LeT had a military budget of more than \$5 million by 2009.[^cite_note-tankel201104-9]

#### Drug and arms trade

The group also maintains a vast smuggling network, smuggling weapons and drugs from Afghanistan, through Pakistan, to India.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-TI-134] These are likely brought from Lashkar-e-Taiyaba training camps in Afghanistan[^cite_note-JG-129][^cite_note-UR-130] and stored in their Pakistani counterparts before being brought to India.[^cite_note-TI-134]

Lashkar-e-Taiba's drugs include [heroin](14034.md) and [methamphetamine](11038318.md) while weapons include American-made [M4 Carabine assault rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_carbine) among other drugs and weapons.[^cite_note-140] Indian security forces have been seizing arms cashes almost daily.[^cite_note-144] The investigations done by India's NIA (National Intelligence Agency) have exposed major drug operations conducted by Lashkar-e-Taiba.[^cite_note-IU-142][^cite_note-TI-134]

This smuggling has only strengthened the group's relation's with the Indian [D-Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Company).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-TI-134]

#### Use of charity aid to fund relief operations

LeT assisted victims after the [2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake).[^cite_note-bbc20061005-146] In many instances, they were the first on the scene, arriving before the army or other civilians.[^cite_note-147]

A large amount of funds collected among the Pakistani expatriate community in Britain to aid victims of the earthquake were funnelled for the activities of LeT although the donors were unaware. About [£](270673.md)5 million were collected, but more than half of the funds were directed towards LeT rather than towards relief efforts. Intelligence officials stated that some of the funds were used to prepare for [an attack that would have detonated explosives on board transatlantic airflights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_transatlantic_aircraft_plot).[^cite_note-148] Other investigations also indicated the aid received for earthquake relief was used to increase fighter recruitment.[^cite_note-149]

### The Resistance Front (TRF)

Main article: [The Resistance Front](74821509.md)

[The Resistance Front](74821509.md) (TRF) is believed to be a proxy organisation associated with the Lashkar-e-Taiba. Established in 2019, TRF has been accused by the [Indian government](553883.md) of engaging in various activities that threaten peace and security in the [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) region. These activities include planning attacks on security forces and civilians, coordinating weapon transportation for proscribed terrorist groups, recruitment of militants, infiltration across borders, and smuggling of weapons and narcotics. In January 2023, TRF was banned under the [Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlawful_Activities_\(Prevention\)_Act) (UAPA), and its commander, [Sheikh Sajjad Gul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Sajjad_Gul), was designated as a terrorist. This action followed suspicions of TRF's involvement in the conspiracy to assassinate Kashmiri journalist [Shujaat Bukhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shujaat_Bukhari) in June 2018.[^cite_note-174] In April 2025, TRF claimed responsibility for [2025 Pahalgam attack](79778862.md)[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-aljazeera20250423-176][^cite_note-cnn20250424-177] and they later retracted their claim of responsibility.[^cite_note-178]

## External relationships

### Support from Saudi Arabia

See also: [Saudi Arabia and terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_and_terrorism) and [State-sponsored terrorism § Saudi Arabia](317645.md#Saudi_Arabia)

According to a secret December 2010 paper signed by the US secretary of state, "[Saudi Arabia](349303.md) remains a critical financial support base for [Al-Qaeda](1921.md), the [Taliban](30635.md), LeT and other terrorist groups."[^cite_note-202] LeT used a Saudi-based front company to fund its activities in 2005.[^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-203][^cite_note-204]

### Role in India-Pakistan relations

LeT attacks have increased tensions in the already contentious [relationship between India and Pakistan](4500730.md).[^cite_note-cfrprofile-205][^cite_note-206] Part of the LeT strategy may be to deflect the attention of Pakistan's military away from the tribal areas and towards its border with India. Attacks in India also aim to exacerbate tensions between India's Hindu and Muslim communities and help LeT recruitment strategies in India.[^cite_note-randjan2009-63]

LeT cadres have also been arrested in different cities of India. On 27 May, a LeT militant was arrested from Hajipur in Gujarat. On 15 August 2001, a LeT militant was arrested from [Bhatinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhatinda) in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India).[^cite_note-207] Mumbai police's interrogation of LeT operative, Abu Jundal revealed that LeT has planned 10 more terror attacks across India and he had agreed to participate in these attacks.[^cite_note-208] A top US counter-terrorism official, Daniel Benjamin, in a news conference on 31 July 2012, told that LeT was a threat to the stability in South Asia urging Pakistan to take strong action against the terror outfit.[^cite_note-209] Interrogation of Jundal revealed that LeT was planning to carry out aerial attacks on Indian cities and had trained 150 paragliders for this. He knew of these plans when he visited a huge bungalow in eastern Karachi where top LeT men, supervised by a man called Yakub were planning aerial and sea route attacks on India.[^cite_note-210]

In August 2026, Rizwan Hanif, an LeT operative, publicly admitted while addressing his supporters in a speech that Indian agents killed more than 30 LeT operatives across various parts of Pakistan.[^cite_note-211]

#### Inter-Services Intelligence involvement

The [ISI](2159021.md) have provided financial and material support to LeT.[^cite_note-Atkins-59] In 2010, [Interpol](14338129.md) issued warrants for the arrest of two serving officers in the Pakistan Army for alleged involvement in the 2008 Mumbai attacks.[^cite_note-212] The LeT was also reported to have been directed by the ISI to widen its network in the Jammu region where a considerable section of the populace comprised Punjabis. The LeT has a large number of activists who hail from Indian Punjab and can thus effectively penetrate into Jammu society.[^cite_note-213] A 13 December 2001 news report cited a LeT spokesperson as saying that LeT wanted to avoid a clash with the Pakistani government. He claimed a clash was possible because of the suddenly conflicting interests of the government and of the militant outfits active in Jammu and Kashmir even though the government had been an ardent supporter of Muslim freedom movements, particularly that of Kashmir.

Pakistan denies giving orders to LeT's activities. However, the Indian government and many non-governmental think-tanks allege that the Pakistani ISI is involved with the group.[^cite_note-satp-4] The situation with LeT causes considerable strain in [Indo-Pakistani relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_relations), which are already mired in suspicion and mutual distrust.

### Role in Afghanistan

The LeT was created to participate in the [Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) conflict against the [Najibullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Najibullah) regime in [Afghanistan](737.md). In the process, the outfit developed deep linkages with Afghanistan and has several Afghan nationals in its cadre. The outfit had also cultivated links with the [first Taliban regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)) in Afghanistan and also with [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) and his [al-Qaeda](1921.md) network. Lashkar-e-Taiba is also a Punjabi group, but its [Ahl-e Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl-e_Hadith) faith ([Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement)) and close relationship with the Pakistani military establishment have contributed to a historically rocky relationship with Deobandi militant groups and other Anti-Salafi Taliban elements,[^cite_note-Salafi-33] thus, the Taliban has a less ideological interest in letting LeT operate from Afghan soil.[^cite_note-214] Even while refraining from openly displaying these links, the LeT office in [Muridke](19493547.md) was reportedly used as a transit camp for third country recruits heading for Afghanistan.

Guantanamo detainee [Khalid Bin Abdullah Mishal Thamer Al Hameydani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_Bin_Abdullah_Mishal_Thamer_Al_Hameydani)'s [Combatant Status Review Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatant_Status_Review_Tribunal) said that he had received training via Lashkar-e-Taiba.[^cite_note-215]

Lashkar-e-Taiba's directed attacks against Indian targets in Afghanistan. Three major attacks occurred against Indian government employees and private workers in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-216]

The Combatant Status Review Tribunals of [Taj Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_Mohammed_\(Guantanamo_detainee\)) and [Rafiq Bin Bashir Bin Jalud Al Hami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafiq_Bin_Bashir_Bin_Jalud_Al_Hami), and the [Administrative Review Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_Review_Board) hearing of [Abdullah Mujahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Mujahid) and [Zia Ul Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Ul_Shah) allege that they too were members or former members of Lashkar-e-Taiba.[^cite_note-CsrtMohammed-217][^cite_note-CsrtAlHami-218][^cite_note-ArbMujahid-219][^cite_note-ArbShah-220]

After the Taliban's restoration in 2021, it cracked down on Salafi groups in Afghanistan due to the conflict with the [Islamic State](9087364.md).[^cite_note-221] Unlike the Deobandi group Jaish-e-Mohammed, despite claims of Taliban links, the LeT's ties to Salafism have led to evidence of a strengthened alliance with [IS-KP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IS-KP), a sworn enemy of the Taliban.[^cite_note-222]

### Role in Nepal

At the seminar held in July 2025 titled "Terrorism in South Asia: Challenges to Regional Peace and Security", organised by the Nepal Institute for International Cooperation and Engagement in Kathmandu, [Sunil Bahadur Thapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Bahadur_Thapa), Advisor to the [President of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Nepal), warned that groups like Lashkar-e-Taiba and [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed), pose serious threats to India and could potentially use Nepal as a transit route.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225]

### Links with other militant groups

While the primary focus for the Lashkar is the operations in Indian Kashmir, it has frequently provided support to other international terrorist groups. Primary among these is the al-Qaeda Network in Afghanistan. LeT members also have been reported to have engaged in conflicts in the [Philippines](23440.md), [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia), the Middle East and [Chechnya](6095.md).[^cite_note-226] There are also allegations that members of the [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam) conducted arms transfers and made deals with LeT in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-archives.dawn.com-227]

#### Ties to Khalistan militants

In the aftermath of the 2008 Mumbai attacks, India submitted a list of 20 individuals to Pakistan, including known members of the Lashkar-e-Taiba (LeT) and Khalistani militant outfits such as the [International Sikh Youth Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Sikh_Youth_Federation) (ISYF). Among them was ISYF chief [Lakhbir Singh Rode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhbir_Singh_Rode), based in Lahore, wanted for arms smuggling and inciting communal violence. The ISYF, along with [Babbar Khalsa International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babbar_Khalsa) (BKI), had reportedly maintained ties with Pakistan’s Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) and received training and logistical support from Islamist groups like LeT since the early 2000s.[^cite_note-Jamestown_Sikh_Militants-229]

Beginning in the mid-1990s, ISYF operatives are believed to have trained alongside LeT cadres, with both groups sharing facilities and resources in Pakistan. By 2005, Indian intelligence warned of renewed militancy, citing collaboration between ISYF, LeT, and ISI in training militants for infiltration and attacks in Punjab and Jammu & Kashmir. The ISYF is believed to have been the first Sikh militant group to establish contact with ideologues from Markaz-e-Dawat-ul-Irshad, the Salafist organisation that served as the parent body of Lashkar-e-Taiba (LeT).[^cite_note-Jamestown_Sikh_Militants-229]

### Impact on Pakistani external relations

In 2015 the Pakistan Army ordered 12 [Bell AH-Z Viper](10805384.md) attack helicopters, with an option of 3 more to replace its ageing [AH-1F Cobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-1F_Cobra) and were to be delivered by 2017.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] Following cancellation of \$300 million military aid to Pakistan by the US government in 2018, the helicopters were put into storage at [Davis-Monthan AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis%E2%80%93Monthan_Air_Force_Base), [Arizona](21883824.md).[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] The reasoning for this cancellation by Trump was due Pakistan's support for groups like Lashkar-e-Tayyiba. In 2020 Pakistan considered ditching the Bell AH-Z Viper deal for either Turkish or Chinese helicopters.[^cite_note-239] By 2022 Pakistan fully ditched this deal for Turkish [T129 ATAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI/AgustaWestland_T129_ATAK) and Chinese [Z-10ME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changhe_Z-10) Helicopters.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] The 12 Bell AH-Z Vipers which were meant for Pakistan, are now planned to be sold to Ukraine instead as of 2024.[^cite_note-242]

In 2025 Germany turned down Pakistan’s request for AIP technology for its submarines due to its support for Lashkar-e-Taiba. Germany instead, sold this technology to India for the submarines of its own navy.[^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244]
