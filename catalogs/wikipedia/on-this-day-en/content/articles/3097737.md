The **Piper PA-31 Navajo** is a family of twin-engined low-wing tricycle gear [utility aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_aircraft) designed and built by [Piper Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_Aircraft) for small [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_airline) and [feeder airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airline), and as a [corporate aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_aircraft). Production ran from 1967 to 1984. It was license-built in a number of [Latin American](18524.md) countries.

## Development



Early Navajo with two-bladed propellers and two-part entry door

In 1962, Piper began developing a six- to eight-seat twin-engined corporate and commuter transport aircraft under the project name *Inca*, at the request of company founder [William T. Piper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_T._Piper).[^cite_note-ALDN-2][^cite_note-PA31ann-4] Looking like a scaled-up [PA-30 Twin Comanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-30_Twin_Comanche), the PA-31 made its first flight on 30 September 1964, and was announced later that year.[^cite_note-Janes_76_p354-3][^cite_note-PA31ann-4] It is a low-wing monoplane with a conventional tail, powered by two 310 hp (231 kW) [Lycoming TIO-540-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-540) turbocharged engines in "tiger shark" cowlings, a feature shared with the Twin Comanche and the [PA-23 Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-23).[^cite_note-PA31ann-4][^cite_note-lambert-5][^cite_note-range-6]

As testing proceeded, two cabin windows were added to each fuselage side and the engines were moved further forward.[^cite_note-range-6][^cite_note-FD-7] The PA-31, named "[Navajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_people)" after the native American tribe, was [certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) by the [FAA](11186.md) on 24 February 1966, again in mid-1966 with an increase in [maximum takeoff weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_takeoff_weight) (MTOW) from 6,200 to 6,500 lb (2,812 to 2,948 kg), and deliveries began in 1967.[^cite_note-ALDN-2][^cite_note-A20SO-8]

The PA-31-300 was certified by the FAA in June 1967, the only variant without [turbocharged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbocharger) engines: 300 hp (224 kW) Lycoming IO-540-M1A5 engines driving two-bladed propellers.[^cite_note-A20SO-8] Unofficially, the initial model was referred to as the PA-31-310. Only 14 PA-31-300 were built in 1968 and 1969: the smallest variant production.[^cite_note-ALDN-2][^cite_note-PCSI-9]



Pressurized PA-31P with fewer and smaller windows

In January 1966, development of the PA-31P Pressurized Navajo had begun : Piper's first [pressurized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization) aircraft.[^cite_note-HFN-10] The PA-31P (or PA-31P-425 unofficially) was certified in late 1969.[^cite_note-A8EA-11] It was powered by 425 hp (317 kW) [Lycoming TIGO-541-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_TIO-541) engines, had a longer nose, fewer and smaller windows, 25 US gal (95 L; 21 imp gal) fuel tanks in the [engine nacelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine_nacelle) and a one-piece [airstair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstair) cabin entry door instead of the split pair of doors.[^cite_note-ALDN-2][^cite_note-A8EA-11][^cite_note-HFN-10] MTOW was increased to 7,800 lb (3,538 kg).[^cite_note-A8EA-11] The PA-31P was produced from 1970 to 1977.[^cite_note-ALDN-2]

The 1971 Navajo B featured air conditioning, new storage lockers in the rear of the engine nacelles, increased baggage space, a third door next to the cabin doors for easier baggage loading, and an optional separate door for the pilot to enter the cockpit.[^cite_note-peperell-1][^cite_note-NBAA71-12]



The PA-31-350 Chieftain, stretched by 2 ft (61 cm)

In September 1972, Piper unveiled the PA-31-350 Navajo Chieftain, a Navajo B stretched by 2 ft (61 cm) for up to ten seats, with more powerful engines and [counter-rotating propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-rotating_propellers) to prevent [critical engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_engine) handling problems.[^cite_note-A20SO-8][^cite_note-NBAA72-13] The Chieftain was powered by 350 hp (261 kW) Lycoming TIO-540 variants, with an opposite-rotation LTIO-540 on the right-hand wing, and MTOW was increased to 7,000 lb (3,175 kg).[^cite_note-A20SO-8] Deliveries started in 1973, after a delay due to a flood caused by [Hurricane Agnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Agnes) at Piper's factory in [Lock Haven, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_Haven,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-NBAA72-13][^cite_note-ALDC-14]

The 1974 PA-31-325 Navajo C/R was based on the Navajo B.[^cite_note-NavIPC-15] The Navajo C/R had 325 hp (242 kW), lower rated versions of the Chieftain's counter-rotating engines.[^cite_note-A20SO-8] It was certified in May 1974, and production commenced in the 1975 model year.[^cite_note-NavIPC-15] The Navajo B was also superseded in 1975 by the Navajo C.[^cite_note-NavIPC-15]

In May 1981, Piper established its T1000 Airliner Division at its [Lakeland, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeland,_Florida), factory.[^cite_note-Levy1-16] The PA-31-350T1020 (or T1020) was a PA-31-350 Chieftain optimized for and marketed for the commuter airline market, without the 40 US gal (151 L; 33 imp gal) auxiliary fuel tanks in each wing.[^cite_note-A20SO-8] Up to eleven seats could be fitted, and baggage capacity was reduced from 700 to 600 lb (318 to 272 kg) maximum.[^cite_note-A20SO-8] The first T1020 was delivered in December 1981.[^cite_note-Levy2-17]

The PA-31T3 (T1040) was a hybrid with the PA-31-350T1020 main fuselage, and the nose and tail of the [PA-31T1 Cheyenne I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-31T_Cheyenne).[^cite_note-Barnett-18] The wings were similar to the Cheyenne I's, but with reduced fuel capacity and baggage lockers in the engine nacelles similar to those of the Chieftain.[^cite_note-A8EA-11] An optional underbelly cargo pod was also available.[^cite_note-A8EA-11][^cite_note-Barnett-18] The [Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6A-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6) [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) engines were the same as those of the Cheyenne I.[^cite_note-A8EA-11] Deliveries began in July 1982.[^cite_note-Levy2-17] A T1050 variant was proposed, with a fuselage stretch of 11 ft 6 in (3.51 m) and seating capacity for 17, but did not proceed.[^cite_note-Barnett-18]



Pressurized PA-31P-350 Mojave

The PA-31P-350 Mojave was also a hybrid, a piston-engined Cheyenne.[^cite_note-test-19] The Mojave combined the Cheyenne I fuselage with the Chieftain tail.[^cite_note-test-19] The Chieftain's wings were strengthened, their span was 4 ft (1.2 m) wider and the fuel capacity was enlarged to 243 US gal (920 L; 202 imp gal).[^cite_note-test-19] The engines variants had [intercoolers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercooler), and the rear part of the nacelles were baggage lockers.[^cite_note-test-19] The Mojave's MTOW rose by 200 lb (91 kg) to 7,200 lb (3,266 kg).[^cite_note-A20SO-8][^cite_note-A8EA-11] Certified in 1983, like the T1020 and T1040, the Mojave was produced in 1983 and 1984; combined production with the T1020 and T1040 was below 100 aircraft.[^cite_note-A8EA-11][^cite_note-ALDC-14][^cite_note-Barnett-18] Two experimental PA-31-353s were also built in the mid-1980s.[^cite_note-ALDC-14]

### Licensed manufacture

The PA-31 series was manufactured under licence in several countries from kits of parts supplied by Piper.[^cite_note-Chincul-20][^cite_note-EMB820-21] [Chincul SACAIFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chincul_SACAIFI) in Argentina assembled most of the series as the PA-A-31, PA-A-31-325, PA-A-31P and PA-A-31-350 and [Aero Industrial Colombiana SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_Industrial_Colombiana_SA?action=edit\&redlink=1) (AICSA) in Colombia assembled PA-31, PA-31-325 and PA-31-350 aircraft.[^cite_note-Eurocontrol-22] The PA-31-350 Chieftain was also assembled under licence in Brazil by [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer) as the EMB 820C Navajo.[^cite_note-Bonelli-23][^cite_note-EMB820-21] In 1984, Embraer subsidiary company [Indústria Aeronáutica Neiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ind%C3%BAstria_Aeron%C3%A1utica_Neiva) began converting Embraer EMB 820Cs by installing [Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6) turboprop engines; Neiva called the converted aircraft the Carajá.[^cite_note-Caraja85-24]

## Accidents and incidents

- July 13, 1972: An [Ansett Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansett_Australia) Piper PA-31 Navajo aircraft, registered as VH-CIZ, crashed near [Golden Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Grove,_South_Australia) shortly after departing Adelaide for a charter flight to [Moomba Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moomba_Airport). All eight people on board, including the pilot and seven passengers, were killed.

  [^cite_note-51]

- December 18, 1979: A Piper PA-31 Navajo light plane working a courier service crashed at Halifax airport, killing two and destroying many documents detailing transactions from 59 banks in Prince Edward Island and Cape Breton.

  [^cite_note-1979crash-52]

- December 3, 1983: [SouthCentral Air Flight 59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SouthCentral_Air_Flight_59), a PA-31-350 registered *N35206*, carrying eight passengers and one pilot, was on the takeoff roll at [Anchorage International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchorage_International_Airport) when it collided head-on with [Korean Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines) Flight 084, a [McDonnell Douglas DC-10-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10-30) freighter *HL7339*. The Piper struck the DC-10's left and center main landing gear and three passengers sustained minor injuries; the DC-10 overran the runway and the three crew suffered serious injuries. Investigators determined that the Korean Air Lines pilot had become disoriented taxiing in fog, failed to follow correct procedures and confirm his position, and accidentally initiated takeoff from the wrong runway. Both aircraft were severely damaged and were written off.

  [^cite_note-53]

- October 14, 1984: [Wapiti Aviation Flight 402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wapiti_Aviation_Flight_402) collided with terrain while landing at [High Prairie Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Prairie_Airport) killing six of the 10 occupants onboard including Canadian politician [Grant Notley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Notley).

- January 11, 1987: An [Eagle Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Air_\(Iceland\)) Piper Chieftain with registration TF-ORN, crashed in the ocean outside of [Skutulsfjörður](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Dsafj%C3%B6r%C3%B0ur) while on approach to [Ísafjörður Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Dsafj%C3%B6r%C3%B0ur_Airport), in bad weather, killing the pilot.

  [^cite_note-54]

- April 27, 1994: A Piper PA-31-350 Navajo Chieftain, registration *N990RA*, operated by Action Air Charters, overshot the 6-24 runway at [Sikorsky Memorial Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Memorial_Airport), Stratford, Connecticut, and struck a fence, killing eight of the nine aboard.

  [^cite_note-55]

  [^cite_note-56]

- October 8, 1998: A Castle Aviation owned and operated Piper Chieftain (N3453A) was flying from [Detroit Metropolitan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Metropolitan_Airport) to [Portage County Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage_County_Regional_Airport) when it was destroyed after a forced landing in a wooded area near Ravenna, Ohio, approximately 5 miles from the destination airport due to fuel starvation. The airplane had been suffering issues with the landing gear status lights during the trip which caused a circuit breaker to open. The pilot flew four more legs with the airplane in this condition, not realizing the fuel gauges were also disabled by that circuit breaker.

  [^cite_note-57]

- May 31, 2000: [Whyalla Airlines Flight 904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whyalla_Airlines_Flight_904) was a scheduled commuter flight, operated by a Piper PA-31 Navajo which crashed while attempting to ditch in the [Spencer Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Gulf), [South Australia](26716.md) after suffering failures of both engines on the evening of 31 May 2000. All 8 people on board the aircraft were killed as a result of the accident. The findings of a subsequent investigation by the [Australian Transport Safety Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Transport_Safety_Bureau) highlighting the airline's operating procedures as a key factor leading to the accident were later overturned after they were contradicted by evidence presented at a [coronial inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronial_inquiry) into the deaths of those on board the flight.

  [^cite_note-coroner-58]

  &#x20;The accident led to a recall by engine manufacturer [Textron Lycoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textron_Lycoming) which saw close to 1000 aircraft grounded worldwide while defects were rectified at an estimated cost of [\$A](81215.md)66 million. Australia's aviation regulator, the [Civil Aviation Safety Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Safety_Authority) also mandated life jackets be carried on all aircraft operating over water after the crash.

  [^cite_note-Age-59]

- 6 June 2003: An Air Adventures New Zealand Ltd Piper PA-31 Chieftain (registered ZK-NCA) crashed into trees and terrain 1.2 nmi (2.2 km; 1.4 mi) short of runway 20, killing the pilot and seven of the nine passengers on board. The aircraft was flying too low during an ILS approach in foggy weather.

  [^cite_note-60]

- July 9, 2009: At approximately 10\:08 pm, a Piper PA-31 Navajo airplane originating from [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia) crashed into an industrial area in [Richmond, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_British_Columbia). The two pilots were killed. It was owned and operated by Canadian Air Charters and was carrying units of blood for [Canadian Blood Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Blood_Services) at the time. Officials say that wake turbulence was the main cause of the crash. Fatigue, along with diminished depth perception in darkness, was also a factor.

  [^cite_note-wake-61]

- March 15, 2013: Three people were killed when a Piper PA-31 Navajo aircraft crashed shortly after takeoff into a parking lot of a business near the departure end of the runway 9. All three occupants of the aircraft were killed, and the cause of the crash was listed as pilot error.

  [^cite_note-62]

  [^cite_note-63]

- August 1, 2016: Thai Regional Airline Flight 106, a Piper PA-31 Navajo (HS-FGB) arriving from [Nakhon Ratchasima Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhon_Ratchasima_Airport) with two passengers, crashed at [Lam Phak Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lam_Phak_Chi), [Nong Chok district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nong_Chok_district), [Bangkok](56615.md). In the incident, there was one fatality (the captain in command) and four injuries.

  [^cite_note-64]

- February 13, 2023: A Piper PA-31 Navajo aircraft owned by Murowa (Private) Limited force-landed near [Beatrice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice,_Zimbabwe). The aircraft was transporting diamonds under armed guard from the Murowa mine to [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare) when it developed an apparent engine fault. Five people were injured in the crash, including the pilot and security personnel. The diamond consignment was secured following the incident.

  [^cite_note-herald-65]

- July 26, 2025: A Piper PA-31-310 Navajo C registered *C-GYYP*, operated by Kîsik Aerial Survey crashed approximately three minutes after takeoff from [Deer Lake Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_Lake_Regional_Airport), the pilot who was the sole occupant onboard was killed.

  [^cite_note-66]

- January 10, 2026: A Piper PA-31-310 (N325FA) crashed after takeoff from [Juan José Rondón Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_Rond%C3%B3n_Airport) in Paipa, Colombia, the pilot and five passengers, including Colombian singer [Yeison Jiménez](82082223.md), were killed.

  [^cite_note-67]

- **Empty weight:** 3,930 lb (1,783 kg)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 6,500 lb (2,948 kg)

- **Fuel capacity:** 190 US gal (160 imp gal; 720 L) total, 187 US gal (156 imp gal; 710 L) useable

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [Lycoming TIO-540-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-540) air-cooled six-cylinder horizontally opposed [piston engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_engine), 310 hp (230 kW) each

- **Propellers:** 3-bladed Hartzell metal, fully feathering propellers, 6 ft 8 in (2.03 m) diameter

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 261 mph (420 km/h, 227 kn) at 15,000 ft (4,600 m)

- **Cruise speed:** 238 mph (383 km/h, 207 kn) at 20,000 ft (6,100 m) (econ. cruise)

- **Stall speed:** 73 mph (117 km/h, 63 kn) (flaps down)

- **[Never exceed speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_speeds#VNE):** 272 mph (438 km/h, 236 kn)&#x20;

  [^cite_note-A20SO-8]

- **Range:** 1,165 mi (1,875 km, 1,012 nmi) at 20,000 ft (6,100 m) (econ. cruise speed)

- **Service ceiling:** 26,300 ft (8,000 m)

- **Rate of climb:** 1,445 ft/min (7.34 m/s)
