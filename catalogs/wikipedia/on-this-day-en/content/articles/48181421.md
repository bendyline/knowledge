**Luis de la Fuente Castillo** (born 21 June 1961) is a Spanish professional [football](10568.md) [manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manager_\(association_football\)) and former player who played as a [left-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-back). He is the manager of the [Spain national team](609825.md).

He amassed [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) totals of 254 matches and six goals over 13 seasons, with [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao) and [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC), winning two league titles with the former including a [double](88184.md) with the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_del_Rey) in 1984.[^cite_note-4]

De la Fuente began working with [Spanish youth teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_youth_football_team) in 2013, managing the [under-19s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-19_football_team) to victory in the [2015 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_UEFA_European_Under-19_Championship) and the [under-21 side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-21_football_team) to the [2019 equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship). He coached the [Olympic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-23_football_team) to the silver medal at the [2020 games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament) and took over his country's [seniors](609825.md) in 2022, winning the [2023 Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals), [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) and the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md), becoming the first manager to claim all three major trophies.[^cite_note-5]

## Playing career

Born in [Haro, La Rioja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haro,_La_Rioja), de la Fuente graduated from [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao)'s youth system,[^cite_note-Success-6][^cite_note-7] and made his senior debut with [the reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao_B) in 1978, in the [Segunda División B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B). On 8 March 1981, he made his first-team – and [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) – debut, coming on as a second-half [substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_\(association_football\)) in a 0–0 away draw against [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_CF).[^cite_note-8]

De la Fuente was promoted to the main squad in the summer of 1982. He scored his first professional goal on 26 March 1983, closing the 4–0 home win over [Celta de Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Celta_de_Vigo).[^cite_note-9]

In July 1987, de la Fuente signed for fellow top-flight club [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC),[^cite_note-10] and continued to appear regularly the following campaigns in defence or as a [left winger](548981.md#Winger).[^cite_note-11] In 1991, he returned to Athletic for a 20 million [pesetas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_peseta) fee,[^cite_note-12] featuring rarely under coach [Jupp Heynckes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupp_Heynckes) who arrived one year later.[^cite_note-13]

De la Fuente joined [Alavés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_Alav%C3%A9s) in 1993, with the side in the third tier. After [one season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B), he retired at the age of 33.[^cite_note-Success-6]

De la Fuente earned [caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_\(sport\)) for [Spain at youth level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_youth_football_team). He also represented the [under-23 side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-23_football_team) in qualification for the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-14]

## Coaching career

### Early years

De la Fuente's first managerial job was at [Portugalete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Portugalete) in the [regional leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisiones_Regionales_de_F%C3%BAtbol_in_the_Basque_Country). In summer 2000, he was appointed at [Segunda División B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B) club [Aurrerá de Vitoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Aurrer%C3%A1_de_Vitoria),[^cite_note-15] but was sacked in March of the following year in spite of a seventh place in the table.[^cite_note-16]

After a spell back at Sevilla (academy), de la Fuente returned to Athletic.[^cite_note-17] Initially a manager of the youths and the reserves,[^cite_note-18] he also acted as match delegate for two years[^cite_note-19] before returning to his previous duties.[^cite_note-20]

On 13 July 2011, de la Fuente was named Alavés coach,[^cite_note-21] being dismissed on 17 October.[^cite_note-22]

### Spain youths

On 5 May 2013, de la Fuente was appointed at the helm of the [Spain under-19 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-19_football_team),[^cite_note-23] who won the [2015 UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_UEFA_European_Under-19_Championship) in Greece.[^cite_note-U19-24] He became manager of the [under-21 side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-21_football_team) in July 2018, after [Albert Celades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Celades) resigned.[^cite_note-25] His first competition was the [2019 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship) in Italy, conquered after the [2–1 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship_Final) defeat of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_national_under-21_football_team) in [Udine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udine).[^cite_note-U21-26]

On 8 June 2021, de la Fuente and his team filled in as the [Spain senior side](609825.md) for a [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) [friendly](977090.md) against [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_national_football_team), after the aforementioned squad had gone into isolation when [Sergio Busquets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Busquets) tested positive for [COVID-19](63030231.md).[^cite_note-27] They won 4–0 in [Leganés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legan%C3%A9s).[^cite_note-28]

De la Fuente was also in charge of the [Spanish Olympic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-23_football_team) at the delayed [2020 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament) in Tokyo.[^cite_note-29] His side won the silver medal, losing 2–1 to [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_under-23_football_team) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament_%E2%80%93_Final).[^cite_note-OG-30]

### Spain senior

On 8 December 2022, de la Fuente was appointed head coach of the senior side, as [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique) resigned following a round-of-16 [penalty shootout](511302.md) elimination at the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) by [Morocco](1024865.md).[^cite_note-31] He was officially presented four days later, with a contract running until [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) with the option to be extended.[^cite_note-32]

De la Fuente won 3–0 in a [European qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying) at home to [Norway](203277.md) in his first game on 25 March 2023, with two goals by 32-year-old debutant [Joselu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joselu).[^cite_note-33] He led the country to victory in the [2022–23 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Nations_League) (a first ever), defeating [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) 5–4 on penalties following a [0–0 draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Nations_League_final) in [Rotterdam](26049.md).[^cite_note-UNL-34]

Spain finished the Euro 2024 group stage in first place, scoring five goals and conceding none.[^cite_note-35] De la Fuente's side went on to claim the trophy in [Berlin](3354.md) with seven wins in as many matches, beating [England](9904.md) 2–1 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_final).[^cite_note-EURO-36]

In January 2025, de la Fuente extended his contract until the end of [Euro 2028](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2028).[^cite_note-37] After finishing first in [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_E) for the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md),[^cite_note-38] the team topped [Group H](81660199.md) in the finals[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] and eventually reached [the final](75845879.md), their first since [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final), with 13 goals for and only one against, scored by [Belgium](679716.md)'s [Charles De Ketelaere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_De_Ketelaere) in the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] In the final, Spain defeated [Argentina](454699.md) 1–0 after [extra time](633242.md) to win their second World Cup, with him becoming the oldest manager to win the tournament at 65 years and 28 days old, surpassing the previous record held by his compatriot [Vicente del Bosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_del_Bosque).[^cite_note-47] Starting in March 2024, he guided his side to an all-time world record for the longest undefeated streak in men's international football, 38 consecutive matches without a loss in regulation time, including all at his first European Championship and World Cup tournaments.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 21 September 2026, the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) announced that de la Fuente had agreed to a new deal, running until [Euro 2032](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2032);[^cite_note-50] he reportedly earned an annual salary of [€](9472.md)5 million, being one of the highest-paid national team coaches in the world.[^cite_note-51]

## Style of coaching

Due to his background coaching in youth setups, de la Fuente liked to work with younger players familiar to him and not high-maintenance. [Mikel Merino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Merino), [Dani Olmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Olmo), [Mikel Oyarzabal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Oyarzabal), [Fabián Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabi%C3%A1n_Ruiz) and [Unai Simón](58209770.md) won the under-21 European championship title during his tenure, and later represented the full team under the same manager.[^cite_note-TA-52] He explained his philosophy by stating that "I come from a grassroots background. Our commitment, to the people we trust in the youth system, is not a pose, it is a conviction".[^cite_note-53]

Spain often dominated possession under de la Fuente, also starting to use a more traditional [centre-forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-forward) and delivering more [crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_\(association_football\)) to the box.[^cite_note-TA-52]

## Personal life

De la Fuente married a woman from [Andalusia](2736.md), with whom he had three children. One son, Alberto, was also involved in the sport, and at one point worked with his father in the Spanish Federation as an analyst.[^cite_note-54]

Raised Catholic yet lapsed for some time, de la Fuente ultimately embraced the religion again in adulthood.[^cite_note-55]
