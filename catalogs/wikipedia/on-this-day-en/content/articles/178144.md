**Drammen** (Norwegian pronunciation: [\[ˈdrɑ̀mːən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Norwegian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a9/Drammen.ogg/Drammen.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a9/Drammen.ogg/Drammen.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city and municipality in [Buskerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buskerud) county, [Norway](21241.md). The port and river city of Drammen is centrally located in the south-eastern and most populated part of Norway. Drammen municipality also includes smaller towns and villages such as [Konnerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konnerud), [Svelvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svelvik), [Mjøndalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mj%C3%B8ndalen) and [Skoger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skoger).

## Location

Drammen is located west of the [Oslofjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslofjord) inlet, situated approximately 44 km (27 mi) southwest of the capital city of Oslo. There are more than 100,000 inhabitants in the municipality, though the city is the regional capital of an area with around 82,000 residents. Drammen, and its adjacent communities, are experiencing more socioeconomic and population growth than ever before. The city makes good use of the [Drammensfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammensfjord), a waterway utilised for recreation, fishing and other activities, as well as waterfront housing.

## Name and coat of arms

The [Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse) form of the city's name was *Drafn*, and this was originally the name of the inner part of [Drammensfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammensfjord). The [fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord) is, however, probably named after the river [Drammenselva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammenselva) (Norse *Drǫfn*), and this again is derived from *drǫfn* f '[wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave)'.[^cite_note-2] The [coat-of-arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) is from modern times. They were granted on 17 November 1960. The arms has a silver-colored column on top a silver-colored bedrock crossed with a silver-colored key and sword against a blue background.[^cite_note-3] It is based upon the old seal dating from 1723 for [Bragernes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bragernes), one of the central parts of Drammen. The [motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motto) for Bragernes (in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)) was *In Fide Et Justitia Fortitudo* (English: in faith and justice is strength), and the items in the seal are referring to this: key = faith, sword = justice, column on rocks = strength.[^cite_note-4]

## Governance

On 1 January 2020, the municipalities of [Svelvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svelvik_Municipality), [Nedre Eiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nedre_Eiker) and Drammen merged to form «The New Drammen Municipality». The new municipality, which is now called Drammen, is Norway's seventh largest with over 100,000 residents. The main seat of the Greater Municipality is located in the city of Drammen. Drammen city had a population of 82,875 inhabitants in 2021, but serves as a regional centre for an area that had a population of 110,236 inhabitants in 2021.[^cite_note-5] The city is the country's record champion in environment and urban development prizes, having won 21 since 2003, consisting of 16 national and 5 international.

## History

[Rock carvings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) at Åskollen and Austad are 6000 to 7000 years old, and are the first signs of human activity in the area. The largest rock carving at Åskollen depicts a [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose).

Drammen originally consisted of three [small seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town#Norway): [Bragernes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bragernes) (on the northern side of the [Drammenselva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammenselva) river) and [Strømsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Str%C3%B8ms%C3%B8) and [Tangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangen,_Drammen?action=edit\&redlink=1) (both on the southern side of the river). For trade purposes, small seaports were placed under market towns. Despite their geographical proximity, Bragernes was placed under [Christiania](22309.md) and Strømsø under [Tønsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8nsberg). For this reason, cooperation between the adjacent seaport towns was almost impossible.

In 1662, a merger was proposed to unite Strømsø and Bragernes to form a market town with the name Frederiksstrøm. The proposal was rejected by King [Frederick III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III_of_Denmark). Bragernes received limited market town rights in 1715, and merged with Strømsø to gain status as a single [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Norway) on 19 June 1811.[^cite_note-7]

Its geographical location made the city favorable for seafaring, [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding), [log driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_driving), timber trade. During the 19th century, paper and pulp industries were developed. Large parts of the city were ruined in the great fire of 12–13 July 1866,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETingle1866-8] which led to the reconstruction of the city centre, including the characteristic town square and Bragernes church. The [Drammen Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen_Line) (*Drammenbanen* ) opened in 1872 providing rail service between Drammen and Oslo.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In 1909, Drammen got the first [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) system in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), the [Drammen trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Drammen). The lines ran until 1967. For many years the centre of Drammen suffered from heavy traffic. In 1970, [Drammen Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen_Bridge) with two lanes on European route E18 was built (expanded to four lanes in 2006) and in 1999 the opening of the Bragernes tunnel (*Bragernestunnelen*) diverted additional traffic away from the centre of the city.[^cite_note-11]

In recent years, the city centre has seen the introduction of new housing, shopping facilities, restaurants, cafes and bars, as well as a public pathway along the [Drammenselva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammenselva) river.[^cite_note-12]

In 2011, Drammen observed its 200th anniversary with many citywide jubilee celebrations.[^cite_note-13] Drammen's district heating system was upgraded to use [water-sourced heat pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump#Water-source_heat_pump), drawing on local fjord water, to support population growth in the city.[^cite_note-bbc-20150310-14]

As of August 2026, Drammen is the fifth largest city in Norway in terms of its population; 126.230 inhabitans.[^cite_note-15]

## Geography

Drammen is one of the larger cities in Norway, and lies about 40 kilometres (25 mi) from the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) of Norway, [Oslo](22309.md). The city centre lies at the end of a [valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley), on both sides of the Drammenselva river, and where the river meets the [Drammensfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammensfjord). The [Holmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen_\(Drammen\)) island in Drammen is also the main [harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor) for car and fruit import in Norway. [Skimtheia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skimtheia) at 554 meters above sea level is the highest point of the Drammensmarka forest.[^cite_note-16]

The Drammen region is part of the metropolitan region around [Oslo](22309.md) and the country's fifth largest urban area, it extends far beyond the municipal boundaries; into the municipalities of [Lier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lier,_Norway), [Asker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asker), [Øvre Eiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98vre_Eiker) and [Holmestrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmestrand). The total population of the settlement is 110,236 inhabitants as of 1 January 2021.[^cite_note-17]

In 2008 Drammen won the prestigious prize for the best city development in Europe.[^cite_note-18]

## Climate

Drammen has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Dfb), characterized by fairly cold winters and mild summers. Located at a sheltered location in a valley at the head of a narrow fjord branch of innermost Oslofjord, Drammen is one of the warmest cities in Scandinavia in summer. The warmest month on record was July 2018 with mean 22.3 °C (72.1 °F), average daily high 29.3 °C (84.7 °F) and 11 days with highs at or above 30 °C (86 °F). The all-time high 35 °C (95 °F) was recorded August 3, 1982, and is a tie with the national high for the month. The September record high is the national record. The all-time low −28 °C (−18 °F) was recorded in January 1987, which is the coldest month on record with mean −13.8 °C (7.2 °F) and average daily low −17.1 °C (1.2 °F). In more recent years, December 2010 was almost as cold. The average date for first overnight freeze (low below 0 °C (32 °F)) in autumn is 13 October (1981–2010 average).[^cite_note-19] The Drammen-Berskog weather station has been operating since 2004. The station is located about 5 km from the city centre, which is more influenced by the maritime climate of Drammensfjorden. As a result, the city centre tends to experience slightly milder winters and somewhat cooler summers compared to the station. An earlier weather station named Drammen-Marienlyst (3 m) operated from 1966 to 2003.

### Aass Brewery

Main article: [Aass Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aass_Brewery)

Aass Brewery is the oldest surviving [brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewery) in Norway, and has won acclaim for both its [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer) and its well-conserved building. Founded in 1834, the brewery's primary products are soft drinks, beer and aquavit.[^cite_note-32]

### Drammen Museum

Main article: [Drammen Museum of Art and Cultural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen_Museum_of_Art_and_Cultural_History)

The Drammen Museum of Art and Cultural History includes Marienlyst, a manor house from ca. 1770, museum building from 1930 with the museum's administration, permanent exhibitions and collections, and Lyche pavilion from 1990 with the gallery, temporary exhibitions and museum café, Halling yard, with 5 old buildings, the oldest from 1760s. The museum also includes the two largest preserved like farms in Drammen, [Gulskogen Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulskogen_Manor?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Austad farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austad_farm?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-39]

### Drammen Spiral

The *[Drammen Spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen_Spiral)* is a road tunnel that allows access to the [Skansen Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skansen_Ridge?action=edit\&redlink=1), 180 m (591 ft) above the town. It opened in 1961 on the site of a former quarry.[^cite_note-40]

### Drammen Theater

Drammen Theater in Bragernes was built in 1869 and was designed by architect [Emil Victor Langlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Victor_Langlet). The theater was the first modern theater in the country. It was designed in a complex Renaissance style with symmetrical facades and round arched windows. After Drammen Theater suffered total destruction by fire in December 1993, a new theater was rebuilt on the model of the original house. It was finished during February 1997.[^cite_note-41]

### Drammensbadet

Drammensbadet is a public swimming and training facility located in Marienlyst, Drammen. It was one of the largest in Norway when it opened 1 September 2008. They have five indoor and four outdoor pools.[^cite_note-42]

### Bragernes Torg (town square)

Bragernes Torg is the largest town square in Norway and one of the longest in the Nordics.[^cite_note-43]

## Transport

The city does not have its own airport. The nearest airports are [Sandefjord Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandefjord_Airport,_Torp) which is located 79 km and [Oslo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Airport,_Gardermoen) which is located 92 km away from Drammen.
