# Telecommunications in Syria

The [Syrian Ministry of Communications and Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Communications_and_Information_Technology_\(Syria\)) retains governmental authority over the internet in [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-1] Prior to the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), **telecommunications in Syria** were slowly moving towards liberalization, with a number of licenses awarded and services launched in the Internet service provision market. The initiative reflected the government's change in attitude towards liberalization, following its promise to the [European Union](9317.md) to liberalize markets by 2010. All other forms of fixed-line communications are provided by the state-owned operator, [Syrian Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Telecom) (STE).[^cite_note-IWS-Syria-2]

## Telecommunications system



Telecommunication map of Syria, 1949

**[International dialing code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_dialing_code)**: +963[^cite_note-BBC-Syria-3]

Further information: [Telephone numbers in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_numbers_in_Syria)

**[Landline telephones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landline)** in use: 2.821 million (2021 est.).[^cite_note-CIAWFB-Syria-4]

**[Mobile phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellphone)** in use: 16.991 million (2021 est.).[^cite_note-CIAWFB-Syria-4] The mobile operators are [Syriatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriatel) and [MTN Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Syria).[^cite_note-5] There is mobile phone coverage in most parts of Syria providing access to 96% of the population. Call quality ranges from good to acceptable. Many international calls fail or are less clear over the mobile network compared to the landline network.

Further information: [List of radio stations in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_radio_stations_in_Syria)

**[Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio):** 14 AM, 11 FM, and 1 shortwave stations in 1998. The radio operators are the state owned [Radio Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Damascus) and [Al-Madina FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Madina_FM), the first private radio station, launched in March 2005.[^cite_note-BBC-Syria-3] Other private radios are for example: Melody FM, Sham FM, Radio Arabesque, Mix FM Syria, Rotana FM, Fuse FM, Farah FM). Private radio stations can also transmit news or political content.

Further information: [Television in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Syria)

**[Television](29831.md):** There are three television operators: the state owned [GART](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_for_Radio_and_Television_\(Syria\)) which operates five satellite channels ([Al-Souriya TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Souriya_TV), [Syrian News Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_News_Channel)), broadcasting in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and the private TV Stations like [Syria TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_TV_\(Fadaat_Media\)), [Al Thania TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Thania_TV) or [Massaya TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massaya_TV). There are no restrictions on the use of satellite receivers and many viewers watch pan-Arab TV stations.[^cite_note-BBC-Syria-3] Roughly two-thirds of Syrian homes have a satellite dish providing access to foreign TV broadcasts.[^cite_note-CIAWFB-Syria-4]

Opposition satellite stations broadcast from abroad; they include London-based [Barada TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barada_TV), [Orient TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_TV), which operates from the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE), and Istanbul-based [Syria TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_TV_\(Turkey\)).[^cite_note-BBC-Syria-3]

## Internet

**[Country code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_code)**: The top level domain for Syria is [.sy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.sy).

There were 8,500,000 Internet users in Syria as of March 2021 for a 46.5% Internet penetration rate.[^cite_note-6] Syria ranks 13th out of 14 countries in the Middle East region, just behind [Iraq](7515928.md) (59.6%) and ahead of Yemen (25.9%). The growth of Internet users has been rapid since 2016: [^cite_note-7][^cite_note-IWS-Syria-2]

| Year | Internet users | % of population |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2000 | 30,000 | 0.2% |
| 2002 | 220,000 | 1.2% |
| 2005 | 800,000 | 4.2% |
| 2009 | 3,565,000 | 16.4% |
| 2010 | 3,935,000 | 17.7% |
| 2011 | 4,469,000 | 19.8% |
| 2016 | 5,502,250 | 29.6% |
| 2021 | 8,500,000 | 46.5% |

The internet first appeared around 1998. 35 organs of the Syrian government by July 1998 were connected to the internet.[^cite_note-8]

There were 420 Syrian Internet hosts in 2010, placing Syria 187th out of 231 in the world.[^cite_note-CIAWFB-Syria-4]

With a measured download speed that averages 4.60 Mbit/s in May 2024, the speed of the Internet in Syria is relatively slow as it ranks 179 out of 181 in terms of speed.[^cite_note-9]

[ADSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADSL) service in [Syria](7515849.md) has been available since 2003.[^cite_note-10] However, ADSL is not available in all locations and, where available, the local telco may not have enough ports for immediate activation. Through 2009 broadband Internet access had reached less than 0.2% of the Syrian population.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

[3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) wireless Internet is available in all major cities as well as cities with significant tourism. [2.5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2.5G) [EDGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_\(telecommunication\)) wireless Internet is available through mobile network operators, SyriaTel and MTN. Wireless Internet is accessed using a [USB stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB_stick) purchased from the mobile operators. In addition, 3G [SIM cards](197489.md) for use on mobile phones may be purchased with a data plan. However, only [WCDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCDMA) phones support data at the moment. SyriaTel and MTN provide [4G](486547.md) [LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTE_\(telecommunication\)) coverage.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-renamed_from_8_on_20240615212043-14]

High-speed Internet is also available through many [Internet cafes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_cafes).

### Internet censorship in Ba'athist Syria

| --- | --- |

Main article: [Internet censorship in Ba'athist Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_Ba'athist_Syria)

Internet filtering in Ba'athist Syria was found to be pervasive in the political and Internet tools areas, and selective in the social and conflict/security areas by the [OpenNet Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenNet_Initiative) in August 2009.[^cite_note-ONI-Syria-15] Syria has been on [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) Enemy-of-the-Internet list since 2006 when the list was established.[^cite_note-RWBEnemies-16] In 2009, the Committee to Protect Journalists named Syria number three in a list of the ten worst countries in which to be a blogger, given the arrests, harassment, and restrictions which online writers in Syria have faced.[^cite_note-17]

Syria has banned websites for political reasons and arrested people accessing them. In addition to filtering a wide range of Web content, the Syrian government monitors Internet use very closely and has detained citizens "for expressing their opinions or reporting information online." Vague and broadly worded laws invite government abuse and have prompted Internet users to engage in self-censoring and self-monitoring to avoid the state's ambiguous grounds for arrest.[^cite_note-ONI-Syria-15][^cite_note-18]

In February 2011, Syria stopped filtering [YouTube](3524766.md), [Facebook](7529378.md), and [Twitter](9988187.md).[^cite_note-19]

[Voice over Internet Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_over_Internet_Protocol) ([VoIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VoIP)) is blocked completely and requires a proxy or [Virtual Private Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_Private_Network) ([VPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VPN)) to work around it.[^cite_note-20] However, VoIP operators that utilize non-standard [Session Initiation Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Initiation_Protocol) (SIP) ports may function behind Syria's proxy.

[Internet cafes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_cafes), which are widespread and accessible to the public for a fee, can be used to access blocked sites.[^cite_note-ISN2008-21] However, more restrictions have been placed on internet cafes, all public internet centers need operating approval from the security services, are required to keep detailed records of their customers' surfing habits, and people have been arrested after accessing blocked content.[^cite_note-22]

### Shutdown of Syrian Internet

In 2012, it was reported that all Internet connectivity between Syria and the outside world appeared to have ceased, as of 29 November 2012. This coincided with reported intense rebel activity inside Syria.[^cite_note-23] [Matthew Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Prince), CEO of [Cloudflare](32760230.md), reported that three [undersea communication cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undersea_communication_cable) in Tartous, Syria and a fourth land cable through [Turkey](11125639.md) were connecting Syria to the internet prior to the event.[^cite_note-24] However, according to an August 2014 interview with [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden), the Internet blackout in Syria was related to a failed attempt by the [U.S. National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Security_Agency) (NSA) to infiltrate [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware) on a core router of one of the country's main ISPs.[^cite_note-25]
