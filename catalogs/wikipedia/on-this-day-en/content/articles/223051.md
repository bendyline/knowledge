**Tak** ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): ตาก, pronounced [\[tàːk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Thai)) is one of Thailand's seventy-seven [provinces](204637.md) (*changwat*) and lies in [Western Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Thailand). Neighbouring provinces are (from north clockwise) [Mae Hong Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Hong_Son_province), [Chiang Mai](211630.md), [Lamphun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamphun_province), [Lampang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampang_province), [Sukhothai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhothai_province), [Kamphaeng Phet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamphaeng_Phet_province), [Nakhon Sawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhon_Sawan_province), [Uthai Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthai_Thani_province) and [Kanchanaburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanchanaburi_province). The western edge of the province has a long boundary with [Kayin State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayin_State) of Myanmar ([Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma)).

## Geography

The [Bhumibol Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumibol_Dam) (named after [King Bhumibol Adulyadej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Bhumibol_Adulyadej), the old name was *Yanhee Dam*) is in Khao Kaew [Tambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambon) (sub-district), [Sam Ngao District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Ngao_District) of Tak and was built from 1958 to 1964.[^cite_note-RidTh-5] It stops the [river Ping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_River), one of the two sources of the [Chao Phraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Phraya) River.[^cite_note-RID_River_Gauges-6][^cite_note-WorldWater-7][^cite_note-WorldWaterDetail-8] The artificial lake created covers an area of 300 km<sup>2</sup> and is the largest in Thailand. [Taksin Maharat National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taksin_Maharat_National_Park), [Namtok Pha Charoen National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namtok_Pha_Charoen_National_Park), [Lan Sang National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Sang_National_Park), and [Khun Phawo National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khun_Phawo_National_Park) are all in the province. [Thungyai Naresuan Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thungyai_Naresuan_Wildlife_Sanctuary) shares half of the lake front with Kanchanaburi and [Huai Kha Khaeng Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai_Kha_Khaeng_Wildlife_Sanctuary) at the border with Uthai Thani and are [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-UNEPT-9][^cite_note-UNEP-10]

On the western side of Tak province the [Tenasserim Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenasserim_Hills) meet the [Dawna Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawna_Range). One of the few transnational roads and cross-border points into [Myanmar](19457.md) is at [Mae Sot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Sot). Northwest of Mae Sot the main road on the Thai side skirts the border until it turns straight north towards [Mae Hong Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Hong_Son).

Tak province occupies 17,303 km<sup>2</sup> (6,681 sq mi) and lies 426 km north of Bangkok. The total forest area is 12,455 km<sup>2</sup> (4,809 sq mi) or 72 percent of provincial area.[^cite_note-AREA-1]

## History

Tak was a historical kingdom built over 2,000 years ago, even before the [Sukhothai period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhothai_kingdom). The ancient kingdom had its peak around the 1st century. By the 5th century the capital of this kingdom was moved south to Lavo (present day [Lopburi province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopburi_province)). A city named [Ban Tak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphoe_Ban_Tak) was established by [Jamadevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamadevi) (พระนางจามเทวี), princess of the Lavo kingdom, around 663 CE. It became part of the [Sukhothai kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhothai_kingdom) through battles led by [Ramkhamhaeng the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramkhamhaeng_the_Great) and formed the main fortress on the western front. The city was moved further west and renamed *Mueang Rahang* when the [Ayuthaya kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayuthaya_kingdom) was lost to [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) during [King Maha Thammaracha's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahathammarachathirat_\(king_of_Ayutthaya\)) reign. The city was moved back to the east side of the [Ping River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_River) during the early [Bangkok period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_period).[^cite_note-Jamadevi-14] Chinese stoneware and porcelain of the Song, Yuan and Ming periods have been recorded as grave goods at highland burial grounds in [Umphang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umphang_district) and [Phop Phra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phop_Phra_district).[^cite_note-matsong2019-15]<sup>: 217–223 </sup> Srisak Vallibhotama counts Tak among the seven modern provinces that covered the core of Sukhothai.[^cite_note-siroch2016-16]<sup>: 41 </sup> In Dhida Saraya's reconstruction of Sukhothai's trade routes, the western route ran through Chot, a district of the present Tak province, to [Martaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mottama) and [Pegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bago,_Myanmar).[^cite_note-siroch2016-16]<sup>: 44 </sup> The Mae Lamao transit post, in the present [Mae Sot district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Sot_district), was the route by which [Bayinnaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayinnaung) entered Siam in 1563 and again in November 1568.[^cite_note-siroch2016-16]<sup>: 126, 135 </sup>

King [Taksin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taksin) was vice-governor of Tak before the Ayutthaya kingdom fell during the war with Burma. As his name was *Sin*, he became called *Tak-Sin* during his time in Tak.[^cite_note-Taksin-17]

## Demographics

About a quarter of the population belongs to one of Thailand's tribe [hill tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_tribe_\(Thailand\)): [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)), [Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people) ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language) *Kariang*), [Akha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akha_language) (Thai *Akha*), [Lahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahu_people) (Thai *Musoe*), [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people) (Thai *Mong*), and [Lisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisu_people) (Thai *Lisaw*).[^cite_note-Tribes-18] The largest tribe in Tak is Karen.[^cite_note-Karen-19]

## Refugees

According to the [UNHCR](31964.md) data of 2008, nearly 95,000 of Thailand's 121,000 registered [refugees](45547.md) from [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) are housed in several refugee camps in Tak province of which [Mae La camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_La_refugee_camp) is the largest with around 45,000 [Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people) refugees.[^cite_note-UNrefugee-20]

## Symbols



Bhumibol Dam

The [provincial seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seals_of_the_provinces_of_Thailand) shows King [Naresuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naresuan) on the royal elephant.[^cite_note-Tak_province-21] Sometimes below the elephant a [garuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda) is depicted, as the garuda is the state symbol of Thailand. King Naresuan is shown pouring consecrated water on the ground, a symbolic act to declare independence. This refers to the war of 1584 with [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma), when Tak was the first border town to be liberated from Burmese control.

The provincial slogan is, "A town of wonderful nature, huge Bhumiphol Dam, King Taksin The Great and beautiful forests".[^cite_note-Tak_province-21]

The [provincial tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Thai_provincial_trees) is the [Asian Jatoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Jatoba?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*[Xylia xylocarpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylia_xylocarpa)* var. *kerrii*), and the provincial flower is the Orchid tree ([*Bauhinia* sp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhinia)). The cyprinid fish *[Hypsibarbus malcolmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypsibarbus_malcolmi)* is the provincial aquatic life.

## Health

There are two main public hospitals in Tak: [Somdejphrajaotaksin Maharaj Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somdejphrajaotaksin_Maharaj_Hospital) and Mae Sot Hospital, both operated by the [Ministry of Public Health](23194319.md).

## Economy



Cornfields and the mountains of Burma south of Mae Sot

Agriculture is a major part of the Tak economy. The province of Tak produces [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable), [fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit), [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef), [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia), and other foods. Industries in Tak include [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) quarrying and [jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewelry). Zinc mining was formerly conducted in [Mae Sot District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Sot_District).[^cite_note-24]

[Handicrafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft) and [Myanmar](19457.md) products are also important for trade. The [Bhumibol Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumibol_Dam) in the northern part of Tak is its most popular tourist attraction. Tourism, especially [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism), in the southern part is seasonal with popular destinations such as the [Thi Lo Su Waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thi_Lo_Su_Waterfall), Thi Lo Le Waterfall, [hiking](147313.md) and [white water rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_water_rafting) in its various forest reserves. Tak is also known for its [Loi Krathong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loi_Krathong) festival where *krathong sai* (กระทงสาย) consisting of many krathongs are floated in a long line down the river.[^cite_note-25] The Loi Krathong festival is held on the [Ping River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_River) in [Mueang Tak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mueang_Tak_District) on Loi Krathong night.

## Transportation



Thai-Myanmar Friendship Bridge over Moei River, part of AH1

### Roads

Tak is a key communication and transportation centre of the north, with three [Asian highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_highway) passing through the province. [AH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH1) enters through the Myanmar-Thai border at [Mae Sot District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Sot_District)[^cite_note-Highways-26] [AH2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH2) passes through the province from north to south. Also [AH16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH16) terminates at Tak.

### Air

Tak province is served by [Mae Sot Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Sot_Airport) and [Tak Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tak_Airport). There have been no airlines serving Tak Airport since March 1994, but the Tak Airport is still in operation.
