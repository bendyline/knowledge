The **Explosives Act 1875** ([38 & 39 Vict.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38_&_39_Vict.) c. 17) is an [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament_\(United_Kingdom\)) of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](13964.md) that consolidated and amended statutes relating to [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_fire_safety_legislation_in_the_United_Kingdom).

## Background

On 2 October 1874, a barge carrying gunpowder ignited and exploded near the Macclesfield Bridge on the [Regent's Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent's_Canal) in London, which became known as the [Macclesfield Bridge disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent's_Canal#Macclesfield_Bridge_disaster).[^cite_note-\:1-3] Four people were killed and the bridge was destroyed. The disaster led to efforts to reform [explosives law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_fire_safety_legislation_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-\:1-3]

## Passage

Leave to bring in the Explosive Substances Bill was granted to the [Home Secretary](149104.md), [R. A. Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Cross) [MP](3776360.md), [Sir Henry Selwin-Ibbetson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Henry_Selwin-Ibbetson) [MP](3776360.md), and [William Henry Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Smith_\(1825%E2%80%931891\)) [MP](3776360.md) on 25 February 1875.[^cite_note-\:02-4] The bill had its [first reading](4062253.md) in the [House of Commons](13828.md) on 25 February 1875, presented by the home secretary, R. A. Cross [MP](3776360.md).[^cite_note-\:02-4] During debate, the home secretary described the key changes of the Bill, including:[^cite_note-5]

- Requiring manufacturers to submit plans to the Home Secretary before seeking local licensing

- Introducing registration requirements for retailers

- Creating a tiered system for storage amounts:&#x20;

  - Retailers: Up to 200 lbs

  - Personal use: Up to 30 lbs (no registration needed)

  - Consumer stores: 2 cwt to 2 tons

- Establishing government inspectors

- Granting factory owners more power to remove people causing safety risks

The bill had its [second reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_reading) in the [House of Commons](13828.md) on 16 March 1875 and was committed to a [committee of the whole house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_the_Whole_House_\(United_Kingdom\)),[^cite_note-\:02-4] which met on 5 April 1875, 8 April 1875 and 12 April 1875.[^cite_note-\:02-4] During debate, a proposed amendment by [John Philip Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Philip_Nolan) to change "grand jury cess" to "poor rates" for [Ireland](147575.md) was defeated.[^cite_note-6]

The consideration of the Bill was adjourned after the debate on 19 April 1875,[^cite_note-\:02-4] and the committee met again on 23 April 1875, with amendments.[^cite_note-\:02-4] The committee was discharged and the Bill was re-committed to a [committee of the whole house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_the_Whole_House_\(United_Kingdom\)) on 27 April 1875 in respect of amended clause 110 of the Bill, which met and reported immediately on 27 April 1875, with amendments.[^cite_note-\:02-4] The amended bill had its [third reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_reading) in the [House of Commons](13828.md) on 28 April 1875 and passed, without amendments.[^cite_note-\:02-4]

The bill had its [first reading](4062253.md) in the [House of Lords](13658.md) on 29 April 1875.[^cite_note-\:3-7] The bill had its [second reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_reading) in the House of Lords on 3 May 1875, introduced by [Frederick Lygon, 6th Earl Beauchamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Lygon,_6th_Earl_Beauchamp),[^cite_note-8] and was committed to a [committee of the whole house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_the_Whole_House_\(United_Kingdom\)),[^cite_note-\:3-7] which met and reported on 7 May 1875, with amendments.[^cite_note-\:3-7] The amended bill had its [third reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_reading) in the House of Lords on 13 May 1875 and passed, with amendments.[^cite_note-\:3-7]

The amended bill was considered and agreed to by the [House of Commons](13828.md) on 21 May 1875.[^cite_note-\:02-4]

The bill was granted [royal assent](153067.md) on 14 June 1875.[^cite_note-\:3-7]

## Subsequent developments

[Courtenay Ilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtenay_Ilbert) described the act as a [Consolidation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidation_Act), given that the act, which amended the law with respect to [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives), also consolidated various enactments relating to that particular branch of law.[^cite_note-10]

Section 42 of the act was repealed by section 745 of, and the twenty-second schedule to, the [Merchant Shipping Act 1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Shipping_Act_1894) ([57 & 58 Vict.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57_&_58_Vict.) c. 60), which came into force on 1 January 1895.[^cite_note-\:03-11]

Section 99 of the act was repealed by section 1(1) of, and part XI of schedule 1 to, the [Statute Law (Repeals) Act 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_Law_\(Repeals\)_Act_1977), which came into force on 16 June 1977.[^cite_note-12]

The act was partly repealed when the [Manufacture and Storage of Explosives Regulations 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacture_and_Storage_of_Explosives_Regulations_2005?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([SI 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_2005)/1082) was brought into being but the inspection and licensing remained much the same.[^cite_note-13]

According to the astronomer and broadcaster [Patrick Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Moore), the act had a negative effect upon the development of rocketry in Britain:[^cite_note-14]

> The situation in the early part of 1975 was that many hundreds of satellites had been launched, mainly by Russia and America but also by a few other nations: Japan, China, Italy, France and Britain. It is sad to have to put Britain at the tail-end of the list, but we are still suffering from the after-effects of the restrictions imposed by the Explosives Act of 1875, which so effectively stopped the rocket pioneers here from carrying out practical experiments in the vital early period when von Braun and his colleagues were hard at work, first at their Rocket Flying Field near Berlin and then at Peenemünde.

— Patrick Moore, *The Next Fifty Years in Space* (1976)
