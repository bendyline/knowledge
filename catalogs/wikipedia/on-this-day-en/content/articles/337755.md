An **unmanned combat aerial vehicle** (**UCAV**), also known as a **combat drone**, **fighter drone** or **battlefield UAV**, is an [unmanned aerial vehicle](58900.md) (UAV) that carries [aircraft ordnance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_ordnance) such as [air-to-surface missiles](1544116.md), [anti-tank guided missiles](37517.md) (ATGMs), and/or [aerial bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb) on [hardpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint) or within [weapons bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_bay), allowing it to perform [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactic) [attacks](15077993.md) known as [drone strikes](38212231.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Advancements in Turkey’s and Australia’s UCAV programs have recently demonstrated some [multi-role combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft) characteristics by combining beyond-visual-range air-to-air combat and ground-attack capabilities.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Australian_MQ-28_CCA_Downs_Drone_in_Milestone_Air-to-Air_Test-5] UCAVs are used for [intelligence, surveillance, target acquisition and reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence,_surveillance,_target_acquisition_and_reconnaissance), attacking [high-value targets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-value_target) and prolonged [loitering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loiter_\(aeronautics\)) before needing to return to base, unlike [loitering munitions](53703349.md) and [one-way attack drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone), which are single-use drones made for physically [ramming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramming) into a target and exploding on impact; or [surveillance drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_surveillance_and_reconnaissance_aerial_vehicle), which are unarmed drones used only for [aerial reconnaissance](20338498.md) and gathering intelligence. In March 2026 the British government indicated that it had aerial minesweeping drones and was considering deploying them in the strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-6]

Aircraft of this type have no onboard human pilot,[^cite_note-7] but are usually under real-time [remote control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleoperation) by human operators, with varying levels of [automation](5071866.md).[^cite_note-8] As the operator runs the vehicle from a remote terminal via [radio control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_control), equipment necessary to support an on-board human pilot is not needed, resulting in a lower weight and a smaller size than a manned aircraft for the same [payload](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload). Many countries have operational domestic UCAVs, and many more have imported fighter drones or are in the process of developing them.[^cite_note-9]

Technological advancements are rapidly expanding the capabilities of UCAVs with a primary focus on enhanced autonomy and [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI) integration.[^cite_note-AI’s_Oppenheimer_moment:_autonomous_weapons_enter_the_battlefield-10][^cite_note-TAI_Anka-3_TAI_official-11] Several UCAVs can perform complex functions such as autonomous sensor fusion, real-time target identification, and dynamic mission re-planning reducing the cognitive load on human operators.[^cite_note-That’s_a_tank:_Army_introduces_AI-aided_target_recognition_to_next-gen_C2_prototype-12][^cite_note-Skunk_Works_AI_Mission_Contingency-13] This evolution is central to modern network-centric warfare as UCAVs seamlessly integrate into combined architectures.[^cite_note-Networking_Models_in_Flying_Ad-Hoc_Networks-14][^cite_note-The_pilot_of_an_F-22_just_controlled_a_drone_wingman_in_flight-15] By sharing data across platforms from satellites to ground units they create a fused, comprehensive battlespace picture that enables dramatically faster decision cycles which is a critical advantage in modern strategy.[^cite_note-Networking_Models_in_Flying_Ad-Hoc_Networks-14][^cite_note-JADC2_Network_Clear_to_Managing_Forces_Across_the_World,_Says_Panel-16] A significant new doctrinal is embodied in the development of [loyal wingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned-unmanned_teaming) capable UCAVs, designed to operate in collaborative teams with manned fighter jets.[^cite_note-The_pilot_of_an_F-22_just_controlled_a_drone_wingman_in_flight-15][^cite_note-TAI_Anka-3_TAI_official-11] These drones can perform high-risk missions like electronic attack, forward reconnaissance or weapons delivery thereby shielding human pilots and acting as force multipliers.[^cite_note-AI’s_Oppenheimer_moment:_autonomous_weapons_enter_the_battlefield-10][^cite_note-TAI_Anka-3_TAI_official-11] As global militaries increasingly invest in and deploy unmanned systems an arms race in countermeasures is concurrently accelerating.[^cite_note-AI’s_Oppenheimer_moment:_autonomous_weapons_enter_the_battlefield-10] This includes the development of advanced electronic warfare (EW) suites, directed-energy weapons and anti-drone systems to disrupt, deceive or destroy UCAVs.[^cite_note-Anti-drone_'shoot-out'_lets_experienced_soldiers_wring_out_latest_gear-17][^cite_note-Turkish_defense_firm_MKE_introduces_anti-drone_system_TOLGA-18][^cite_note-Drones_in_Modern_Warfare-19][^cite_note-Iran_Hacked_the_Stealthiest_Aircraft:_How_the_Loss_of_the_RQ-170_Sentinel_Was_a_Major_Setback-20] This arms race ensuring that the battlefield of the future will be defined by this competition between UCAV platforms and the measures designed to defeat them.[^cite_note-Drones_&_Counter-Drone_Systems-21][^cite_note-Anti-drone_'shoot-out'_lets_experienced_soldiers_wring_out_latest_gear-17][^cite_note-The_future_of_unmanned_combat_aerial_vehicles:_An_analysis_using_the_Three_Horizons_framework-22]

## History

Main articles: [History of unmanned combat aerial vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_unmanned_combat_aerial_vehicles), [Drone warfare](38212231.md), and [Civilian casualties from U.S. drone strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_casualties_from_U.S._drone_strikes)



1972: [Ryan Firebee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Firebee) with 2 Maverick missiles

### First Attempts - 1940-1970

One of the earliest explorations of the concept of the combat drone was pioneered by two men: [Lee de Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_de_Forest) who was an inventor and developer of early radio controlled devices and [U.A. Sanabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulises_Armand_Sanabria) who was an electrical engineer in the emerging field of television and commercial broadcasting. Their idea for a radio-command vehicle was featured in an article in a 1940 publication of *Popular Mechanics*.[^cite_note-23] The modern military drone was the brainchild of [John Stuart Foster Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Foster_Jr.), a nuclear physicist of [Lawrence Livermore Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Livermore_National_Laboratory).[^cite_note-kaplan-24] In 1971, Foster was a [model airplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_airplane) hobbyist and had the idea this hobby could be applied to building weapons.[^cite_note-kaplan-24] He drew up plans and by 1973 [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) (Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency) built two prototypes called "Prairie" and "Calera". They were powered by a modified lawn-mower engine and could stay aloft for two hours while carrying a 28-pound (13 kg) load.[^cite_note-kaplan-24]

It should be noted, however, that the pioneers of military or combat UAV development were, in order, the United States, [Israel](9282173.md), and Iran. The U.S. and Israel began developing combat drones in the 1970s, followed by [Iran](14653.md) in the 1980s. The primary reason for this simultaneous development in the industry was that the United States, Iran, and Israel were strategic allies prior to 1979.

### Israel - 1973-1982

In the 1973 [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War), [Israel](9282173.md) used unarmed U.S. [Ryan Firebee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Firebee) target drones to spur [Egypt](8087628.md) into firing its entire arsenal of anti-aircraft missiles.[^cite_note-25] This mission was accomplished with no injuries to Israeli pilots, who soon exploited the depleted Egyptian defences. In the late 1970s and 80s, Israel developed the [Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Scout) and the [Pioneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAI_RQ-2_Pioneer), which represented a shift toward the modern lighter, glider-type model of UAV. Israel pioneered the use of [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) (UAVs) for real-time surveillance, electronic warfare, and decoys.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The images and radar decoying provided by these UAVs helped Israel to completely neutralize the Syrian [air defenses](146640.md) in [Operation Mole Cricket 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mole_Cricket_19) at the start of the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War), resulting in no pilots downed.[^cite_note-WSJLevinson-29]

### US and Iran - 1980-1991

In the late 1980s, [Iran](14653.md) deployed a drone armed with six [RPG-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7) rounds in the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War).[^cite_note-30]

Impressed by Israel's success, the US quickly acquired a number of UAVs, and its Hunter and Pioneer systems are direct derivatives of Israeli models. The first 'UAV war' was the first [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War): according to a May 1991 Department of the Navy report: "At least one UAV was airborne at all times during Desert Storm." After the Persian Gulf War successfully demonstrated its utility, global militaries invested widely in the domestic development of combat UAVs.[^cite_note-31]

### Later development - 2000-2020

The first "kill" by an American UAV was on October 7, 2001, in [Kandahar](17260.md).[^cite_note-32]

Between 2004 and late 2012, the U.S. increased its use of [drone strikes against targets in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes_in_Pakistan) and elsewhere as part of the [war on terror](13425800.md). In January 2014, it was estimated that 2,400 people were killed by U.S. drone strikes in five years.[^cite_note-33] In June 2015, the total death toll of U.S. drone strikes was estimated to exceed 6,000.[^cite_note-pilkington-34]

In 2020, Turkey became the first country to use UCAVs in a large, coordinated attack on a conventional battlefield when it attacked forces in Syria. They were used to attack enemy positions, to provide cover for ground forces and to scout for artillery.[^cite_note-35] Drones were used extensively in the [2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_war) between Azerbaijan and Armenia.[^cite_note-36] Azerbaijan's use of cheaper Turkish [TB2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_TB2) drones was seen as crucial to their victory against the Armenian forces.[^cite_note-37]

### Ukraine and Russia - 2022 onwards



During the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md), use of Russian drones (unmanned aerial vehicles) increased about tenfold from early 2024 through summer 2025.

[^cite_note-NYTimes_20250908-38]



[D1L-Duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D1L-Duck) drone

Drones were also used extensively during the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md).[^cite_note-39] The war was widely described as the first full-scale war featuring the large-scale use of small and commercial-grade UAVs in military settings.[^cite_note-40] Consumer [quadcopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadcopters) and [first-person view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_view_\(radio_control\)) (FPV) drones, modified with sensors and explosives, were being used for military missions. The affordability and availability of small UAVs have shifted modern warfare and given rise to new offensive and defensive strategies.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Usage of small combat drones offers a cost advantage,[^cite_note-43] and their high performance, reliability, and commercial availability for repair parts also contributed to their popularity.[^cite_note-nyt_202303-44][^cite_note-fp_2302-45][^cite_note-46]

## By country

### China



UCAVs at [2025 China Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China_Victory_Day_Parade)

China manufactures and exports combat drones on a large scale, and the country is the source of most commercial UAV components.[^cite_note-47] The [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) military operates multiple types of unmanned combat aerial vehicles.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-ch-49] Since 2010, Chinese drone companies have begun to export large quantities of drones to the global military market. Of the 18 countries that are known to have received military drones between 2010 and 2019, the top 12 all purchased their drones from China.[^cite_note-ch-49][^cite_note-50] The shift accelerated in the 2020s due to China's advancement in drone technologies and manufacturing, compounded by market demand from the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and the [Israel-Gaza conflict](74998519.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-wsj_drone-52][^cite_note-53] Chinese companies that manufactures military unmanned aerial vehicles include [China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Aerospace_Science_and_Technology_Corporation) (CASC) and [Chengdu Aircraft Industry Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Aircraft_Industry_Group), etc.[^cite_note-ch-49]

China is known to be developing various "[loyal wingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyal_wingman)" UCAVs, such as [AVIC Dark Sword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVIC_Dark_Sword),[^cite_note-afm_j20s-54] which is a concept first revealed in 2006.[^cite_note-janes_image-55] Stealth UCAV, such as [Hongdu GJ-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_GJ-11) unmanned combat aerial vehicles, are designed to be controlled by the [Chengdu J-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-20) fighter, forming [manned-unmanned aircraft teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned-unmanned_teaming).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Other stealth UCAVs include [Feihong FH-97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feihong_FH-97) UCAV developed by [China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Aerospace_Science_and_Technology_Corporation) (CASC). It was designed to suppress air defenses with [electronic countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_countermeasure), fly ahead of aircraft to provide early warning, act as an expandable decoy, as well as provide reconnaissance and damage evaluation.[^cite_note-58] [Feihong FH-97A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feihong_FH-97A), a loyal wingman drone designed to fly alongside the fighter aircraft.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

In August and September 2025, China unveiled multiple different types of UCAV and loyal wingmen prototype under development, and the naval variant of the [Hongdu GJ-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_GJ-11) UCAV.[^cite_note-twz_250902-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] These Chinese UCAV designs have different wing configurations and engine options, with some large UCAVs aimed to perform air superiority mission independently or collaboratively with manned aircraft.[^cite_note-av_250903-64][^cite_note-aw_250903-65][^cite_note-fg_250903-66]

In 2025, the first pictures of two very large stealthy long range drones appeared. They have been unofficially designated as the “WZ-X” and “GJ-X”.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

### Turkey

#### TAI Anka-3

Main article: [TAI Anka-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka-3)



A [TAI Anka-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka-3) (center) performing [manned-unmanned teaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned-unmanned_teaming) with manned aircraft [TAI Hürjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_H%C3%BCrjet) (left) and a [TAI Hürkuş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_H%C3%BCrku%C5%9F) (right) in 2024

TAI Anka 3 is the code-name for the new single engine UCAV being developed by TAI.[^cite_note-70] It will have a [flying wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_wing) form,[^cite_note-71] and will feature low-observable stealth technology. The role of the aircraft will be [suppression of enemy air defenses (SEAD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppression_of_Enemy_Air_Defenses), [penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetrator_\(aircraft\)) and bomber.[^cite_note-72]

#### TAI Aksungur

Main article: [TAI Aksungur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Aksungur)

TAI Aksungur is a built by [Turkish Aerospace Industries (TAI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Aerospace_Industries) for the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md).[^cite_note-j360-73][^cite_note-dn1-74][^cite_note-tai-75] Using existing technology from the [TAI Anka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka) series of drones, it is the manufacturer's largest drone,[^cite_note-FlightglobalDebut-76] with payload capacity for mission-specific equipment. #### Bayraktar Kizilelma

Main article: [Bayraktar Kızılelma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_K%C4%B1z%C4%B1lelma)

Bayraktar Kızılelma is a proposed jet-powered, single-engine, low-observable, supersonic, carrier-capable unmanned combat aircraft in development by [Baykar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar), famous for its [Bayraktar TB2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_TB2).[^cite_note-Baykar_MIUS_Official-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] On 12 March 2022, [Selçuk Bayraktar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sel%C3%A7uk_Bayraktar), CTO of Baykar announced that the first prototype of the Bayraktar Kızılelma has entered the production line.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] On November 30, 2025, the Bayraktar Kızılelma successfully completed a landmark flight and firing test campaign, achieving a global first among Unmanned Combat Aerial Vehicles (UCAVs). The platform successfully launched [Gökdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6kdo%C4%9Fan_\(air-to-air_missile\)), a Beyond-Visual-Range (BVR) air-to-air missile, which is equipped with an active radar seeker head, utilizing the autonomous guidance capabilities of its own integrated [MURAD AESA Radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MURAD_AESA_Radar) system. With this unparalleled operational demonstration, the Kızılelma has officially secured its position as the world's first unmanned fighter jet to validate this critical BVR engagement capability on a fully autonomous platform.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

### United Kingdom

#### BAE Systems Taranis

Main article: [BAE Systems Taranis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Taranis)

Taranis is a British demonstrator program for unmanned combat air vehicle (UCAV) technology. It is part of the UK's Strategic Unmanned Air Vehicle (Experimental) (SUAV\[E]) program. BAE describes Taranis's role in this context as following: "This £124m, four -year programme is part of the UK Government's Strategic Unmanned Air Vehicle Experiment (SUAVE) and will result in a UCAV demonstrator with fully integrated autonomous systems and low observable features."

The Taranis demonstrator will have an MTOW (Maximum Takeoff Weight) of about 8000 kilograms and be of comparable size to the [BAE Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Hawk) – making it one of the world's largest UAVs. It will be stealthy, fast, and able to deploy a range of munitions over a number of targets, as well as being capable of defending itself against manned and other unmanned enemy aircraft. The first steel was cut in September 2007 and ground testing started in early 2009. The first flight of the Taranis took place in August 2013 in Woomera, Australia.[^cite_note-86] The demonstrator will have two internal weapons bays. With the inclusion of "full autonomy" the intention is thus for this platform to be able to "think for itself" for a large part of the mission.

#### J-UCAS

Main article: [Joint Unmanned Combat Air Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Unmanned_Combat_Air_Systems)

- [Boeing X-45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_X-45) UCAV (TD)

- [Northrop-Grumman X-47 Pegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_X-47A_Pegasus)

**Joint Unmanned Combat Air Systems**, or **J-UCAS**, was the name for the joint [U.S. Navy](20518076.md)/[U.S. Air Force](32090.md) unmanned combat air vehicle procurement project. J-UCAS was managed by DARPA, the [Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Advanced_Research_Projects_Agency). In the 2006 [Quadrennial Defense Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrennial_Defense_Review), the J-UCAS program was terminated. The program would have used stealth technologies and allowed UCAVs to be armed with precision-guided weapons such as [Joint Direct Attack Munition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munition) (JDAM) or precision miniature munitions, such as the [Small-Diameter Bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-Diameter_Bomb), which are used to suppress enemy air defenses. Controllers could have used real-time data sources, including satellites, to plan for and respond to changes on and around the battlefield.



A [X-47B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_X-47B) UCAV technology demonstrator

The program was later revitalized into [UCAS-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCAS-D), a [United States Navy](20518076.md) program designed to develop a carrier-based unmanned aircraft.[^cite_note-87]

#### N-UCAS

[UCAS-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCAS-D) and [Northrop Grumman X-47B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_X-47B) are the U.S. Navy-only successors to the J-UCAS, which was canceled in 2006. Boeing is also working on the [X-45N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_X-45#X-45N) in this sector.

In a New Year 2011 editorial titled "China's Naval Ambitions", *[The New York Times](30680.md)* editorial board argued that "\[t]he Pentagon must accelerate efforts to make American naval forces in Asia less vulnerable to [Chinese missile threats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_killer) by giving them the means to project their deterrent power from further offshore. Cutting back purchases of the Navy's [DDG-1000 destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt_class_destroyer) (with its deficient missile defense system) was a first step. A bigger one would be to reduce the Navy's reliance on short-range manned strike aircraft like the [F-18](845012.md) and the [F-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35C), in favor of the carrier-launched N-UCAS ...."[^cite_note-88]

On 6 January 2011, the DOD announced that this would be one area of additional investment in the 2012 budget request.[^cite_note-89]

The [United States Air Force](32090.md) has shifted its UCAV program from medium-range [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics) [strike aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_aircraft) to [long-range strategic bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_B-21_Raider). The technology of the Long Range Strike program is based on the [Lockheed Martin Polecat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_Polecat) demonstrator.

### Multinational

- **EADS Surveyor**: The EADS "Surveyor" is still in preliminary investigation phase. It will be a fixed-wing, jet-powered UAV and is being positioned as a replacement for the CL-289. [EADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS) is currently working on a demonstrator, the "Carapas", modified from an Italian Mirach 100 drone. The production Surveyor would be a stealthy machine with a top speed of 850 km/h (530 mph), an endurance of up to three hours, and capable of carrying a sophisticated sensor payload, including SIGINT gear. It would also be able to carry external loads, such as air-dropped sensors or light munitions.

### Non-state actors

In the mid-2010s, the [Islamic State](9087364.md) terrorist group began attaching explosives to commercially available [quadcopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadcopter) such as the Chinese-made [DJI Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_\(unmanned_aerial_vehicle_series\)) to bomb military targets in [Iraq and Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_the_Islamic_State).[^cite_note-90] During the [2016–17 battle of Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mosul_\(2016%E2%80%932017\)), the Islamic State reportedly used drones as surveillance and weapons delivery platforms, using improvised cradles to drop grenades and other explosives.[^cite_note-91] An Islamic State drone facility was notably targeted by [Royal Air Force](25679.md) strike aircraft during the battle.[^cite_note-92]

Militant groups during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) have also reportedly used UAVs in attacks, one example being a swarm of drones armed with bombs attacking Russian bases in western Syria in early January 2018.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Starting in the 2020s, [Mexican drug cartels](197015.md#Mexico) began dropping reportedly hundreds of drone-carried bombs targeting both security forces and enemy gangs during turf wars.[^cite_note-95]

## Safety and security

### Counter-UAS

See also: [Counter unmanned air system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_unmanned_air_system)

There has been widespread use of drones in the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-lessons-from-use-of-drones-in-the-ukraine-war-96] Ukrainian soldiers use GPS signals to guide a drone to find Russian artillery and to guide Ukrainian artillery. Jamming these drone GPS signals cause drones to operate less effectively,[^cite_note-97] as the operators of drones have to rely on pre-programmed routes through areas of jamming until communications can be restored. Other systems supplied by the West rely on automation.[^cite_note-98] Systems like the [AeroVironment Switchblade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AeroVironment_Switchblade) can find targets autonomously, requiring human permission only to engage found targets.[^cite_note-lessons-from-use-of-drones-in-the-ukraine-war-96] In October 2022 a video appeared on the web showing two drones colliding and one being rendered unflyable as a result.[^cite_note-Newdick-99] It was claimed that the filming drone was Ukrainian and the one destroyed was Russian.[^cite_note-Newdick-99] If this is the case it would be the first recorded case of drone on drone combat.[^cite_note-Newdick-99]

On 27 December 2022 North Korea sent five drones over its border with South Korea. One reaching Seoul, all five returned to the North, despite a five-hour chase involving fighter jets and attack helicopters with some 100 rounds being fired. A South Korean [KAI KT-1 Woongbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_KT-1_Woongbi) crashed although both crew survived. The [Joint Chiefs of Staff (South Korea)](14470718.md) released a statement in which it said that while it can stop attack drones, its ability to stop smaller spy drones is "limited". A senior official, Kang Shin-chul, said: "Our military's lack of preparedness has caused a lot of concern to the people…actively employ detection devices to spot the enemy's drone from an early stage and aggressively deploy strike assets". The South Korean President [Yoon Suk-yeol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoon_Suk-yeol) has indicated that South Korea will invest in stealthy drones that could penetrate North Korea, with the creation of a new military unit.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

The South Korean Defence Ministry announced a new series of anti-drone measures, planning to spend some 560 billion won over the next five years. The money will go towards four new initiatives. One is an airborne laser that will be used to destroy larger drones, whilst a jammer would be used on smaller drones. A new counter drone unit, made up of two squadrons, would also be created. The laser is already in the test phase and is expected to become operational in 2027. The jamming system has been described as "soft kill".[^cite_note-102]

## Ethics and laws

See also: [Targeted killing](33928336.md)

### Civilian casualties

#### Israel

In March 2009, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported allegations that Israeli UAVs armed with missiles killed 48 [Palestinian civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), including two small children in a field and a group of women and girls in an otherwise empty street.[^cite_note-103] In June, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) investigated six UAV attacks that were reported to have resulted in civilian casualties and alleged that Israeli forces either failed to take all feasible precautions to verify that the targets were combatants or failed to distinguish between combatants and civilians.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

[Hamas](13913.md) used drones in its [terror attack](74998317.md) on Israel of Oct. 7, 2023.[^cite_note-107]

#### United States

Main articles: [Disposition Matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposition_Matrix) and [Civilian casualties from U.S. drone strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_casualties_from_U.S._drone_strikes)

[Collateral damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_damage) of [civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilians) still takes place with drone combat, although some (like [John O. Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_O._Brennan)) have argued that it greatly reduces the likelihood.[^cite_note-Wilson_Center-108] Although drones enable advanced tactical surveillance and up-to-the-minute data, flaws can become apparent.[^cite_note-109] The U.S. drone program in Pakistan has killed several dozen civilians accidentally.[^cite_note-latime2-110] An example is the operation in February 2010 near Khod, in [Uruzgan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urozgan_Province), [Afghanistan](737.md). Over ten civilians in a three-vehicle convoy travelling from [Daykundi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daykundi_Province) were accidentally killed after a drone crew misidentified the civilians as hostile threats. A force of [Bell OH-58 Kiowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_OH-58_Kiowa) helicopters, who were attempting to protect ground troops fighting several kilometers away, fired [AGM-114 Hellfire](38151.md) missiles at the vehicles.[^cite_note-latime1-111][^cite_note-wapo1-112]

In 2009, the [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution) reported that in the US-led [drone attacks in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes_in_Pakistan), ten civilians died for every militant killed.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] A former ambassador of Pakistan said that American UAV attacks were turning Pakistani opinion against the United States.[^cite_note-115] The website PakistanBodyCount.Org reported 1,065 civilian deaths between 2004 and 2010.[^cite_note-116] According to a 2010 analysis by the [New America Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_America_Foundation) 114 UAV-based missile strikes in northwest Pakistan from 2004 killed between 830 and 1,210 individuals, around 550 to 850 of whom were militants.[^cite_note-117] In October 2013, the Pakistani government revealed that since 2008 317 drone strikes had killed 2,160 Islamic militants and 67 civilians – far less than previous government and independent organization calculations.[^cite_note-118]

In July 2013, former Pentagon lawyer [Jeh Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeh_Johnson) said, on a panel at the [Aspen Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen_Institute)'s Security Forum, that he felt an emotional reaction upon reading [Nasser al-Awlaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_al-Awlaki)'s account of how his 16-year-old grandson was killed by a U.S. drone.[^cite_note-119]

In December 2013, a U.S. drone strike in [Radda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rada'_District), capital of Yemen's [Bayda province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bayda'_Governorate), killed members of a wedding party.[^cite_note-120] The following February, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) published a 28-page report reviewing the strike and its legality, among other things. Titled "A Wedding That Became A Funeral", the report concludes that some (but not necessarily all) of the casualties were civilians, not the intended regional [Al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Arabian_Peninsula) targets. The organization demanded US and Yemeni investigations into the attack. In its research, HRW "found no evidence that the individuals taking part in the wedding procession posed an imminent threat to life. In the absence of an armed conflict, killing them would be a violation of international human rights law."[^cite_note-121]

### Political effects

As a new weapon, drones are having unforeseen political effects. Some scholars have argued that the extensive use of drones will undermine the popular legitimacy of local governments, which are blamed for permitting the strikes.[^cite_note-Smith_–_5_Sept_2012-122]

On August 6, 2020, U.S. Senators [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul) (R-KY), [Mike Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Lee) (R-UT), [Chris Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Murphy) (D-CT), [Chris Coons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Coons) (D-DE), and [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) (I-VT) introduced a bill to ban sales, transfers, and exports of large armed drones to countries outside of [NATO](21133.md) amid concerns that civilians were killed with American-made weapons used by Saudi Arabia and the UAE during the [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen). Congress had previously passed a similar measure with bipartisan support, but failed to overcome President Donald Trump's veto.[^cite_note-123]

### Psychological effects

See also: [PlayStation mentality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_mentality)

Controllers can also experience psychological stress from the combat they are involved in. A few may even experience [posttraumatic stress disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posttraumatic_stress_disorder) (PTSD).[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] There are some reports of drone pilots struggling with post traumatic stress disorder after they have killed civilians, especially children. Unlike bomber pilots, moreover, drone operators linger long after the explosives strike and see its effects on human bodies in stark detail. The intense training that US drone operators undergo "works to dehumanise the 'enemy' people below whilst glorifying and celebrating the killing process."[^cite_note-Drone:_Robot_Imperium_–_Longreads-126]

Professor Shannon E. French, the director of the Center for Ethics and Excellence at [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University) and a former professor at the [U.S. Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Academy), wonders if the PTSD may be rooted in a suspicion that something else was at stake. According to Professor French, the author of the 2003 book *The Code of the Warrior*:[^cite_note-127]

> If \[I'm] in the field risking and taking a life, there's a sense that I'm putting [skin in the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_in_the_game_\(phrase\)) ... I'm taking a risk so it feels more honorable. Someone who kills at a distance—it can make them doubt. Am I truly [honorable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honour)?

The [Missile Technology Control Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Technology_Control_Regime) applies to UCAVs.

On 28 October 2009, [United Nations Special Rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Special_Rapporteur) on extrajudicial, summary or arbitrary executions, [Philip Alston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Alston), presented a report to the Third Committee (social, humanitarian and cultural) of the [General Assembly](31957.md) arguing that the use of unmanned combat air vehicles for [targeted killings](33928336.md) should be regarded as a breach of [international law](8195726.md) unless the United States can demonstrate appropriate precautions and accountability mechanisms are in place.[^cite_note-128]

In June 2015, forty-five former US military personnel issued a joint appeal to pilots of aerial drones operating in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Pakistan and elsewhere urging them to refuse to fly and indicated that their missions "profoundly violate domestic and international laws." They noted that these drone attacks also undermine principles of human rights.[^cite_note-pilkington-34]

Stanford's ‘Living Under Drones’ researchers, meanwhile, have shown that civilians in Pakistan and Afghanistan are reluctant to help those hit by the first strikes because rescuers themselves have often been killed by follow-on drone strikes. Injured relatives in the rubble of the first strike have been known to tell their relatives not to help rescue them because of the frequency of these so-called ‘double-tap’ strikes. People also avoid gathering in groups in visible places. Many children are permanently kept indoors and often no longer attend school.[^cite_note-Drone:_Robot_Imperium_–_Longreads-126]

Writer [Mark Bowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bowden) has disputed this viewpoint, saying in his [The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic) article, "But flying a drone, \[the pilot] sees the carnage close-up, in real time—the blood and severed body parts, the arrival of emergency responders, the anguish of friends and family. Often, he’s been watching the people he kills for a long time before pulling the trigger. Drone pilots become familiar with their victims.[^cite_note-129] They see them in the ordinary rhythms of their lives—with their wives and friends, with their children. War by remote control turns out to be intimate and disturbing. Pilots are sometimes shaken."[^cite_note-130]

&#x20;This assessment is corroborated by a sensor operator's account:&#x20;

> The smoke clears, and there are pieces of the two guys around the crater. And there’s this guy over here, and he’s missing his right leg above his knee. He’s holding it, and he’s rolling around, and the blood is squirting out of his leg … It took him a long time to die. I just watched him.

— [Airman First Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airman_First_Class) [Brandon Bryant (whistleblower)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Bryant_\(whistleblower\)) in GQ[^cite_note-131]

Back in the United States, a combination of "lower-class" status in the military, overwork, and psychological trauma may be taking a mental toll on drone pilots. These psychological, cultural and career issues appear to have led to a shortfall in USAF drone operators, which is seen as a "dead end job".[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

### Stand-off attacks

The "unmanned" aspect of armed UAVs has raised moral concerns about their use in combat and law enforcement contexts. Attacking humans with remote-controlled machines is even more abstract than the use of other "stand-off" weaponry, such as missiles, artillery, and aerial bombardment, possibly depersonalizing the decision to attack. By contrast, UAVs and other stand-off systems reduce casualties among the attackers.[^cite_note-134]

### Wrongful targeting

There are only estimates of the magnitude of the errors in target selection. However, they do occur, and some of them become known.

One fatal "error" happened in December 2023, when the [Nigerian army](14943749.md) accidentally hit a village in [northwestern Nigeria](2227637.md), killing 85 civilians celebrating a Muslim festival.[^cite_note-135] The army said they thought the people were rebels.[^cite_note-136]

### Autonomous attacks

The picture is further complicated if the UAV can initiate an attack autonomously, without direct human involvement. Such UAVs could possibly react more quickly and without bias, but would lack human sensibility.[^cite_note-Foust2013-137] Heather Roff replies that [lethal autonomous robots](36973733.md) (LARs) may not be appropriate for complex conflicts and targeted populations would likely react angrily against them.[^cite_note-Foust2013-137] [Will McCants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_McCants) argues that the public would be more outraged by machine failures than [human error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_error), making LARs politically implausible.[^cite_note-Foust2013-137] According to Mark Gubrud, claims that drones can be hacked are overblown and misleading and moreover, drones are *more* likely to be hacked if they're autonomous, because otherwise the human operator would take control: "Giving weapon systems autonomous capabilities is a good way to lose control of them, either due to a programming error, unanticipated circumstances, malfunction, or hack and then not be able to regain control short of blowing them up, hopefully before they've blown up too many other things and people."[^cite_note-Gubrud2013-138] Others have argued that the technological possibility of autonomy should not obscure the continuing moral responsibilities humans have at every stage.[^cite_note-Robillard2018-139] There is an ongoing debate as to whether the attribution of moral responsibility can be apportioned appropriately under existing international humanitarian law, which is based on four principles: military necessity, distinction between military and civilian objects, prohibition of unnecessary suffering, and proportionality.[^cite_note-Kanwar2011-140]

### International regulation

Under the framework of the [Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons](3111840.md), states have discussed lethal autonomous weapon systems since 2014. In 2016, the treaty's states parties established an open-ended [Group of Governmental Experts on Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Governmental_Experts_on_Lethal_Autonomous_Weapons_Systems) to continue those discussions.[^cite_note-141] The discussions have addressed international humanitarian law, accountability, possible prohibitions and regulations, and the extent of human control required over AI-enabled weapons.[^cite_note-142]

## Public opinion

Main article: [Public opinion about U.S. drone attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_opinion_about_U.S._drone_attacks)

In 2013, a [Fairleigh Dickinson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairleigh_Dickinson_University) poll asked registered voters whether they "approve or disapprove of the U.S. military using drones to carry out attacks abroad on people and other targets deemed a threat to the U.S.?" The results showed that three in every four voters (75%) approved of the U.S. military using drones to carry out attacks, while (13%) disapproved.[^cite_note-143] A poll conducted by the Huffington Post in 2013 also showed a majority supporting targeted killings using drones, though by a smaller margin.[^cite_note-144] A 2015 poll showed Republicans and men are more likely to support U.S. drone strikes, while Democrats, independents, women, young people, and minorities are less supportive.[^cite_note-145]

Outside America, there is widespread opposition to US drone killings. A July 2014 report found a majority or plurality of respondents in 39 of 44 countries surveyed opposed U.S. drone strikes in countries such as Pakistan, Yemen, and Somalia. The U.S., Kenya, and Israel were the only countries where at least half the population supported drone strikes. Venezuela was found to be the most anti-drone country, where 92% of respondents disagreed with U.S. drone strikes, followed closely by Jordan, where 90% disagreed; Israel was shown as the most pro-drone, with 65% in favor of U.S. drone strikes and 27% opposed.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

The drone strikes conducted by the Malian army have had devastating consequences on the civilian population in the Azawad region. In March 2024, attacks killed 13 civilians, including seven children, in Amasrakad, Gao region, sparking outrage and calls for justice from organizations like Amnesty International. These incidents highlighted the lack of transparency and accountability in drone use, exacerbating local and international tensions.[^cite_note-148]’[^cite_note-149]
