The former headquarters in [Minami-Aoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minami-Aoyama), Minato, Tokyo, in use from 1985 to 2025. The building will be demolished to make room for a new building, scheduled for completion by the 2030 Japanese fiscal year.

**Honda Motor Co., Ltd.**,[^cite_note-3] commonly known as **Honda**, is a Japanese [multinational](214491.md) [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(company\)) automotive manufacturer headquartered at the Toranomon Alcea Tower in [Toranomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toranomon), [Minato, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato,_Tokyo), Japan.

Founded in October 1946 by [Soichiro Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soichiro_Honda), Honda has been the world's largest [motorcycle](19876.md) manufacturer since 1959,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] reaching a production of 500 million as of May 2025.[^cite_note-6] It is the world's largest manufacturer of [internal combustion engines](41228673.md) measured by number of units, producing more than 14 million internal combustion engines each year.[^cite_note-7] Honda became the second-largest Japanese automobile manufacturer in 2001.[^cite_note-8] As of 2026, Honda is one of the world's major manufacturers, with an estimated market capitalization of around US\$32,000,000,000.[^cite_note-9] Honda was the eighth largest automobile manufacturer in the world.[^cite_note-10] Honda has built and sold the most produced motor vehicle in history, the [Honda Super Cub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Super_Cub).[^cite_note-11]

Honda was the first Japanese automobile manufacturer to release a dedicated luxury brand, [Acura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura), in March 1986. Aside from its core automobile and motorcycle businesses, Honda also manufactures garden equipment, marine engines, [personal watercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_watercraft), power generators, and other products. Since 1986, Honda has been involved with artificial intelligence/robotics research and released its [ASIMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASIMO) robot in 2000. It has ventured into aerospace with the establishment of [GE Honda Aero Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Honda_Aero_Engines) in 2004 and the [Honda HA-420 HondaJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_HA-420_HondaJet), which began production in 2012. Honda has four joint-ventures in China: [Dongfeng Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongfeng_Honda), [GAC Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAC_Honda), [Wuyang Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyang_Honda) and [Sundiro Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundiro_Honda?action=edit\&redlink=1).

In 2013, Honda invested about 5.7% (US\$6.8 billion) of its revenues into research and development.[^cite_note-12] Also in 2013, Honda became the first Japanese automaker to be a net exporter from the United States, exporting 108,705 Honda and Acura models, while importing only 88,357.[^cite_note-13]

## History



Honda's foray into four-wheelers started with the [Honda T360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_T360) in 1963.

Throughout his life, Honda's founder, [Soichiro Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soichiro_Honda) (1906–1991), had an interest in automobiles. He worked as a mechanic at the Art Shokai garage, where he tuned cars and entered them in races. In 1937, with financing from his acquaintance Kato Shichirō, Honda founded [Tōkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkai_region) Seiki (Eastern Sea Precision Machine Company) to make [piston rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_ring) working out of the Art Shokai garage.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] After initial failures, Tōkai Seiki won a contract to supply piston rings to [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota), but lost the contract due to the poor quality of their products.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] After attending engineering school without graduating, and visiting factories around Japan to better understand Toyota's quality control processes known as "[five whys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys)", by 1941, Honda was able to mass-produce piston rings acceptable to Toyota, using an automated process that could employ even unskilled wartime laborers.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14][^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 16–19 </sup>

[Tōkai Seiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkai_Seiki) was placed under the control of the [Ministry of Commerce and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_and_Industry_\(Japan\)) (called the Ministry of Munitions after 1943) at the start of World War II, and Soichiro Honda was demoted from president to senior managing director after Toyota took a 40% stake in the company.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] Honda also aided the war effort by assisting other companies in automating the production of military aircraft propellers.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14]

The relationships Honda cultivated with personnel at Toyota, [Nakajima Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakajima_Aircraft_Company), and the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) were instrumental in the postwar period.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] A US [B-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29) bomber attack destroyed Tōkai Seiki's Yamashita plant in 1944, and the Itawa plant collapsed on 13 January during the [1945 Mikawa earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_Mikawa_earthquake). Soichiro Honda sold the salvageable remains of the company to Toyota after the war for ¥450,000 and used the proceeds to found the Honda Technical Research Institute in October 1946.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14][^cite_note-Falloon2005-16]

With a staff of 12 men working in a 16 m<sup>2</sup> (170 sq ft) shack, they built and sold improvised [motorized bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorized_bicycles), using a supply of 500 [two-stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stroke) *50 cc* [Tohatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohatsu) [war surplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_surplus) radio [generator engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine-generator).[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14][^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 19 </sup>[^cite_note-Sakiya1982-17] When the engines ran out, Honda began building their own copy of the Tohatsu engine, and supplying these to customers to attach to their bicycles.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14][^cite_note-Sakiya1982-17] This was the Honda A-Type, nicknamed the Bata Bata for the sound the engine made.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] In 1949, the Honda Technical Research Institute was liquidated for ¥1,000,000, or about US\$5,000 today; these funds were used to incorporate Honda Motor Co., Ltd.[^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 21 </sup> At about the same time Honda hired engineer Kihachiro Kawashima, and [Takeo Fujisawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo_Fujisawa) who provided indispensable business and marketing expertise to complement Soichiro Honda's technical bent.[^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 21 </sup> The close partnership between Soichiro Honda and Fujisawa lasted until they stepped down together in October 1973.[^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 21 </sup>

The first complete motorcycle with both the frame and engine made by Honda was the 1949 [D-Type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_D-Type), the first Honda to go by the name Dream.[^cite_note-Falloon2005-16][^cite_note-18] In 1961, Honda achieved its first Grand Prix victories and World Championships in the 125 cc and 250 cc categories.[^cite_note-19] Honda Motor Company grew in a short time to become the world's largest manufacturer of motorcycles by 1964.[^cite_note-20]

The first production automobile from Honda was the [T360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_T360) mini pick-up truck, which went on sale in August 1963.[^cite_note-21] Powered by a small 356 cc straight-4 gasoline engine, it was classified under the cheaper [Kei car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_car) tax bracket.[^cite_note-22] The second production car from Honda was the [S500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_S500) sports car, which followed the T360 into production in October 1963. Its chain-driven rear wheels pointed to Honda's motorcycle origins.[^cite_note-23]

Over the next few decades, Honda worked to expand its product line, operations, and exports to numerous countries around the world. In 1986, Honda introduced the successful [Acura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura) brand to the American market in an attempt to gain ground in the [luxury vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_vehicle) market. The year 1991 saw the introduction of the [Honda NSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_NSX) supercar, the first all-aluminum monocoque vehicle that incorporated a [mid-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-engine_design) V6 with [variable-valve timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_valve_timing).[^cite_note-24]

In 1990, CEO [Tadashi Kume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadashi_Kume) was succeeded by [Nobuhiko Kawamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuhiko_Kawamoto). Kawamoto was selected over [Shoichiro Irimajiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoichiro_Irimajiri), who oversaw the successful establishment of Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. in [Marysville, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marysville,_Ohio). Irimajiri and Kawamoto shared a friendly rivalry within Honda; owing to health issues, Irimajiri would resign in 1992.

Following the death of [Soichiro Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soichiro_Honda) and the departure of Irimajiri, Honda found itself quickly being outpaced in product development by other Japanese automakers and was caught off-guard by the truck and [sport utility vehicle](47696.md) boom of the 1990s, all of which took a toll on the profitability of the company. Japanese media reported in 1992 and 1993 that Honda was at serious risk of an unwanted and hostile takeover by [Mitsubishi Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors), which at the time was a larger automaker by volume and was flush with profits from its successful [Pajero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Pajero) and [Diamante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Diamante) models.[^cite_note-25]

Kawamoto acted quickly to change Honda's corporate culture, rushing through market-driven product development that resulted in recreational vehicles such as the first-generation [Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Odyssey_\(international\)#First_generation_\(RA1-RA5;_1994\)) and the [CR-V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_CR-V), and a refocusing away from some of the numerous sedans and coupes that were popular with the company's engineers but not with the buying public. The most shocking change to Honda came when Kawamoto ended [the company's successful participation in Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_in_Formula_One) after the 1992 season, citing costs in light of the takeover threat from Mitsubishi as well as the desire to create a more environmentally friendly company image.[^cite_note-26]

The [Honda Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Aircraft_Company) was established in 2006 as a wholly owned subsidiary to manufacture and sell the [HondaJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HondaJet) family of aircraft.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The first deliveries to customers began in December 2015.[^cite_note-29] In February 2015, Honda announced that CEO and President [Takanobu Ito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takanobu_Ito) would step down and be replaced by Takahiro Hachigo in June 2015. Additional retirements by senior managers and directors were expected.[^cite_note-30]

In October 2019, Honda was reported to be in talks with [Hitachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi) to merge the two companies' car parts businesses, creating a components supplier with almost \$17 billion in annual sales.[^cite_note-31]

In January 2020, Honda announced that it would be withdrawing employees working in the city of [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), [Hubei](83319.md), China due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-32] On 23 March 2020 due to the global spread of the virus, Honda became the first major automaker with operations in the US to suspend production in its factories. It resumed automobile, engine and transmission production at its US plants on 11 May 2020.[^cite_note-33] Honda and General Motors announced in September 2020 a North American alliance to begin in 2021.[^cite_note-34] According to The Detroit Free Press, "The proposed alliance will include sharing a range of vehicles, to be sold under each company's distinct brands, as well as cooperation in purchasing, research and development, and connected services."[^cite_note-35] In 2021, Honda announced its intention to become the world's first carmaker to sell a vehicle with level 3 self-driving technology.[^cite_note-36]

In March 2022, Honda announced it would develop and build electric vehicles in a joint venture with electronics giant [Sony](26989.md). The latter is set to provide its imaging, sensing, network and other technologies while Honda would be responsible for the car manufacturing processes.[^cite_note-37] The [Sony Honda Mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Honda_Mobility) company was officially announced on 13 October 2022[^cite_note-38] with pre-orders said to open in 2025 and the release of the first EVs scheduled for 2026 in the US under the "Afeela" brand.[^cite_note-39]

In February 2023, Honda announced a deal with American car company [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) to produce cars using a new hydrogen fuel system. The aim is to ramp up the hydrogen powered cells in their Electric vehicles as well as trucks, construction machinery, and power stations.[^cite_note-40] In March 2023, Honda recalled 500,000 vehicles in the United States and Canada due to an issue with seat belts in the car not latching correctly. Among the models recalled were the 2017-2020 CR-V, the 2018 and 2019 Accord, the 2018-2020 Odyssey, the 2019 Insight, and the Acura RDX from 2019 and 2020. According to the recall, the seat belts in the front seats would break open on impact increasing the risk of injury in a crash.[^cite_note-41] On 21 December 2023, Honda announced a global recall of about 4.5 million vehicles, including 2.54 million in the US, over fuel pump failures, following earlier recalls in 2021 and 2020 for the same issue.[^cite_note-42]

In March 2026, Honda announced the cancellation of the [0 Saloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_0_Saloon), [0 SUV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_0_SUV) and [Acura RSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura_RSX#Coupe_SUV) due to profit losses, shifting its focus towards [hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle).[^cite_note-43]

### Attempted merger with Nissan

On 23 December 2024, Honda announced an MOU had been entered to merge with fellow automaker [Nissan](21240.md), to become the 3rd largest auto company by sales. [Mitsubishi Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors), in which Nissan has 24% ownership, also agreed to join the talks of integration.[^cite_note-44] The merger was set with a deadline of 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-45] Mitsubishi announced it would make a decision on merging with the new company by the end of January 2025.[^cite_note-\:0-45]

In February 2025, Honda and Nissan announced that their boards had voted to end talks to merge. Nissan reportedly backed out of the talks with larger rival Honda after negotiations were complicated by growing differences, including Honda's proposal that Nissan become a subsidiary.[^cite_note-46]. Despite ending the merger talks, Honda and Nissan continued their technical partnership. In August 2026, the companies signed a joint-development agreement to standardize core electronic control units, an in-vehicle operating system, middleware and vehicle-control software for future software-defined vehicles, with deployment targeted from fiscal 2029.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

Honda is headquartered in [Minato, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato,_Tokyo), Japan. Their shares trade on the [Tokyo Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Stock_Exchange) and the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md), as well as exchanges in [Osaka](52230.md), [Nagoya](61720.md), [Sapporo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapporo), [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto), [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stock_Exchange), [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronext_Paris), and Switzerland.

Honda has assembly plants around the globe. These plants are located in China, the United States, Pakistan, Canada, England, Japan, Belgium, Brazil, México, New Zealand, Malaysia, Indonesia, India, Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam, Turkey, Taiwan, Perú and Argentina. As of July 2010, 89% of Honda and Acura vehicles sold in the United States were built in North American plants, up from 82.2% a year earlier. This shields profits from the yen's advance to a 15-year high against the dollar.[^cite_note-58]

[American Honda Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Honda_Motor_Company) is based in [Torrance, California](107690.md). [Honda Racing Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Racing_Corporation) (HRC) is Honda's motorsport division. [Honda Canada Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Canada_Inc.) is headquartered in [Markham, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markham,_Ontario),[^cite_note-59] it was originally planned to be located in [Richmond Hill, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Hill,_Ontario), but delays led it to look elsewhere. Its manufacturing division, [Honda of Canada Manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_of_Canada_Manufacturing), is based in [Alliston, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliston,_Ontario). Honda has also created joint ventures around the world, such as [Honda Siel Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Siel_Cars_India) and [Hero Honda Motorcycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_Honda) in India,[^cite_note-60] [Guangzhou Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou_Automobile_Industry_Group) and [Dongfeng Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongfeng_Honda) in China, Boon Siew Honda in Malaysia and [Honda Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Atlas) in Pakistan.&#x20;

Honda runs a business innovation initiative called **Honda Xcelerator**, to build relationships with innovators, partner with Silicon Valley startups and entrepreneurs, and help other companies work on prototypes. Xcelerator had worked with reportedly 40 companies as of January 2019. Xcelerator and a developer studio are part of the **Honda Innovations** group, formed in Spring 2017 and based in [Mountain View, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_California).[^cite_note-61] Through [Honda Mobilityland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Mobilityland), Honda operates the [Suzuka Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course) and [Twin Ring Motegi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Ring_Motegi) racing tracks.

Following the [2011 Tohoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami) in Japan, Honda announced plans to halve production at its UK plants.[^cite_note-62] The decision was made to put staff at the Swindon plant on a 2-day week until the end of May as the manufacturer struggled to source supplies from Japan. It's thought around 22,500 cars were produced during this period.

Honda's automotive manufacturing ambitions can be traced back to 1963, with the [Honda T360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_T360), a [Kei truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_truck) built for the Japanese market.[^cite_note-65] This was followed by the two-door [roadster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadster_\(automobile\)), the [Honda S500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_S500) also introduced in 1963. In 1965, Honda built a two-door commercial delivery van, named the [Honda L700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_L700). Honda's first four-door sedan was not the [Honda Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Accord), but the air-cooled, four-cylinder, gasoline-powered [Honda 1300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_1300) which was introduced in 1969. The Civic was a hatchback that gained wide popularity internationally, but it wasn't the first two-door hatchback built by Honda. That was the [Honda N360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_N360), a [Kei car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_car) that was adapted for international sale as the N600. The Civic, which appeared in 1972 and replaced the N600 also had a smaller sibling that replaced the air-cooled N360, called the [Honda Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Life), which was water-cooled.

The Honda Life represented Honda's efforts in competing in the *kei* car segment, offering sedan, delivery van and small pick-up platforms on a shared chassis. The [Life Step Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Life#Life_Step_Van) had a novel approach that, while not initially a commercial success, appeared to be an influence to vehicles with the front passengers sitting behind the engine, a large cargo area with a flat roof and a liftgate installed in back, and utilizing a transversely installed engine with a front-wheel-drive powertrain.

As Honda entered into automobile manufacturing in the late 1960s where Japanese manufacturers such as Toyota and Nissan had been making cars since before WWII, Honda instilled a sense of doing things a little differently than its Japanese competitors. Its mainstay products like the Accord and Civic (with the exception of its USA-market 1993–97 Passport which was part of a vehicle exchange program with Isuzu (part of the Subaru-Isuzu joint venture)) have always employed [Front-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front-wheel_drive) powertrain implementation, which is currently a long-held Honda tradition. Honda also installed new technologies into its products, first as optional equipment, then later standard, like [anti-lock brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-lock_brakes), [speed-sensitive power steering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_steering), and [multi-port fuel injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-port_fuel_injection) in the early 1980s. This desire to be the first to try new approaches is evident with the creation of the first Japanese luxury chain [Acura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura), and was also evident with the all-aluminum, mid-engined sports car, the [Honda NSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_NSX), which also introduced [variable valve timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_valve_timing) technology, which Honda calls [VTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTEC).

The Civic family is a line of [compact cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_car) developed and manufactured by Honda. In North America, the Civic is the second-longest continuously running nameplate from a Japanese manufacturer; only its perennial rival, the [Toyota Corolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Corolla), introduced in 1966, has been in production longer.[^cite_note-66] The Civic, along with the Accord and [Prelude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Prelude), comprised Honda's vehicles sold in North America until the 1990s, when the model lineup was expanded. Having gone through several generational changes, the Civic has become larger and more [upmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upmarket), and it currently slots between the [Fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Fit) and Accord.

Honda's first [hybrid electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle) was the 1999 [Insight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Insight). The Civic was first offered as a hybrid in 2001, and the Accord followed in 2004. In 2008, the company launched the [Clarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Clarity), a [fuel cell car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell_car).

In 2008, Honda increased global production to meet the demand for small cars and [hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle) in the US and emerging markets. The company shuffled US production to keep factories busy and boost car output while building fewer [minivans](20828.md) and [sport utility vehicles](47696.md) as [light truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-truck) sales fell.[^cite_note-67] Its first entrance into the pickup segment, the light-duty Ridgeline, won Truck of the Year from *[Motor Trend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Trend)* magazine in 2006. Also in 2006, the redesigned Civic won [Car of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Trend_Car_of_the_Year) from the magazine, giving Honda a rare double win of Motor Trend honors. It is reported that Honda plans to increase hybrid sales in Japan to more than 20% of its total sales in the fiscal year 2011, from 14.8% in the previous year.[^cite_note-68]

Five of [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md)'s top ten most fuel-efficient cars from 1984 to 2010 come from Honda, more than any other automakers. The five models are: 2000–2006 Honda Insight (53 mpg<sub>‑US</sub> or 4.4 L/100 km or 64 mpg<sub>‑imp</sub> combined), 1986–1987 Honda Civic Coupe HF (46 mpg<sub>‑US</sub> or 5.1 L/100 km or 55 mpg<sub>‑imp</sub> combined), 1994–1995 Honda Civic hatchback VX (43 mpg<sub>‑US</sub> or 5.5 L/100 km or 52 mpg<sub>‑imp</sub> combined), 2006– Honda Civic Hybrid (42 mpg<sub>‑US</sub> or 5.6 L/100 km or 50 mpg<sub>‑imp</sub> combined), and 2010– Honda Insight (41 mpg<sub>‑US</sub> or 5.7 L/100 km or 49 mpg<sub>‑imp</sub> combined).[^cite_note-69] The [ACEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Council_for_an_Energy-Efficient_Economy) has also rated the [Civic GX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Civic_GX) as the greenest car in America for seven consecutive years.[^cite_note-70]

Honda currently builds vehicles in factories located in Japan, the United States of America, Canada, China, Pakistan, the United Kingdom, Malaysia, Belgium, Brazil, Indonesia, India, Thailand, Turkey, Argentina, Mexico, Taiwan, and the Philippines.

### Motorcycles

For a list of motorcycle products, see [list of Honda motorcycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Honda_motorcycles).



1953 Honda Cub on display at the [Barber Vintage Motorsports Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_Vintage_Motorsports_Museum), Birmingham, Alabama. The two-stroke single-cylinder motorcycle had a displacement of 58 cc and a top speed of 40 km/h (25 mph).



Honda [Gold Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Wing) bike

Honda is the largest motorcycle manufacturer in Japan and has been since it started production in 1955.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] At its peak in 1982, Honda manufactured almost three million motorcycles annually. By 2006, this figure had been reduced to around 550,000 but was still higher than its three domestic competitors.[^cite_note-Alexander2008-14] In 2017, India became the largest motorcycle market for Honda.[^cite_note-71] In India, Honda is leading in the scooters segment, with 59% market share.[^cite_note-72]

During the 1960s when it was a small manufacturer, Honda broke out of the Japanese motorcycle market and began exporting to the United States. Working with the advertising agency [Grey Advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_Advertising), Honda created an innovative marketing campaign, using the slogan "[You meet the nicest people on a Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_meet_the_nicest_people_on_a_Honda)." In contrast to the prevailing negative stereotypes of motorcyclists in America as tough, antisocial rebels, this campaign suggested that Honda motorcycles were made for the everyman. The campaign was hugely successful; the ads ran for three years, and by the end of 1963 alone, Honda had sold 90,000 motorcycles.[^cite_note-Frank2003-15]

Taking Honda's story as an archetype of the smaller manufacturer entering a new market already occupied by highly dominant competitors, the story of their market entry, and their subsequent huge success in the US and around the world has been the subject of some academic controversy. Competing explanations have been advanced to explain Honda's strategy and the reasons for their success.[^cite_note-73]

The first of these explanations was put forward when, in 1975, the [Boston Consulting Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Consulting_Group) (BCG) was commissioned by the UK government to write a report explaining why and how the British motorcycle industry had been out-competed by its Japanese competitors. The report concluded that the Japanese firms, including Honda, had sought a very high scale of production (they had made a large number of motorbikes) in order to benefit from [economies of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale) and [learning curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_curve) effects. It blamed the decline of the British motorcycle industry on the failure of British managers to invest enough in their businesses to profit from economies of scale and [scope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scope).[^cite_note-74]



2004 [Honda Super Cub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Super_Cub)

The second explanation was offered in 1984 by Richard Pascale, who had interviewed the Honda executives responsible for the firm's entry into the US market. As opposed to the tightly focused strategy of low cost and high scale that BCG accredited to Honda, Pascale found that its entry into the US market was a story of "miscalculation, serendipity, and organizational learning" – in other words, Honda's success was due to the adaptability and hard work of its staff, rather than any long-term strategy.[^cite_note-75] For example, Honda's initial plan on entering the US market was to compete in large motorcycles, around 300 cc. Honda's motorcycles in this class suffered performance and reliability problems when ridden the relatively long distances of the US highways.[^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 41–43 </sup> When the team found that the scooters they were using to get themselves around their US base of [San Francisco](49728.md) attracted positive interest from consumers they fell back on selling the [Super Cub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Super_Cub) instead.[^cite_note-Frank2003-15]<sup>: 41–43 </sup>

The most recent school of thought on Honda's strategy was put forward by [Gary Hamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Hamel) and [C. K. Prahalad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._K._Prahalad) in 1989. Creating the concept of [core competencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_competency) with Honda as an example, they argued that Honda's success was due to its focus on leadership in the technology of internal combustion engines.[^cite_note-76] For example, the high power-to-weight ratio engines Honda produced for its racing bikes provided technology and expertise which was transferable into mopeds. Honda's entry into the US motorcycle market during the 1960s is used as a [case study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_study) for teaching introductory strategy at [business schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_school) worldwide.[^cite_note-77]

### Engines



Honda [Outboard motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outboard_motor)

Honda engines powered the entire 33-car starting field of the [2010 Indianapolis 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indianapolis_500)[^cite_note-84] and for the fifth consecutive race, there were no engine-related retirements during the running of the Memorial Day Classic.[^cite_note-85] In the 1980s Honda developed the [GY6 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GY6_engine) for use in motor scooters. Although no longer manufactured by Honda, it's still commonly used in many Chinese, Korean and Taiwanese light vehicles.[^cite_note-86]

Honda, despite being known as an engine company, has never built a [V8 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine) for passenger vehicles. In the late 1990s, the company resisted considerable pressure from its American dealers for a V8 engine (which would have seen use in top-of-the-line Honda SUVs and [Acuras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura)), with American Honda reportedly sending one dealer a shipment of [V8 beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_\(beverage\)) to silence them.[^cite_note-87] Honda considered starting V8 production in the mid-2000s for larger Acura sedans, a new version of the high-end NSX sports car (which previously used DOHC V6 engines with VTEC to achieve its high power output) and possible future ventures into the American full-size truck and SUV segment for both the Acura and Honda brands, but this was canceled in late 2008, with Honda citing environmental and worldwide economic conditions as reasons for the termination of this project.[^cite_note-88]

### Robots



[ASIMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASIMO) at [Expo 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2005)

[ASIMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASIMO) is part of Honda's Research & Development robotics program. It's the eleventh in a line of successive builds starting in 1986 with [Honda E0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_E0) moving through the ensuing [Honda E series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_E_series) and the [Honda P series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_P_series). Weighing 54 kilograms and standing 130 centimeters tall, ASIMO resembles a small [astronaut](664.md) wearing a backpack, and can walk on [two feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biped) in a manner resembling human [locomotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking), at up to 6 km/h (3.7 mph). ASIMO is the world's only humanoid robot able to ascend and descend stairs independently.[^cite_note-89] However, human motions such as climbing stairs are difficult to mimic with a machine, which ASIMO has demonstrated by taking two plunges off a staircase.

ASIMO is able to walk, dance and navigate steps. In 2010, Honda developed a machine capable of reading a user's brainwaves to move ASIMO. The system uses a helmet covered with [electroencephalography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography) and [near-infrared spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-infrared_spectroscopy) sensors that monitor electrical brainwaves and cerebral blood flow signals that alter slightly during the human thought process. The user thinks of one of the limited number of gestures it wants from the robot, which has been fitted with a Brain-Machine Interface.[^cite_note-90]

### Automobiles

See also: [Honda in Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_in_Formula_One)



[Max Verstappen](41758713.md) won the [2021 Formula One World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship) with a Honda power unit.

Honda entered [Formula One](10854.md) for the first time in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Formula_One_season), just one year after starting the production of road cars, making both engine and chassis. Honda achieved its first victory at the [1965 Mexican Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Mexican_Grand_Prix), and another win at the [1967 Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Italian_Grand_Prix), before it withdrew after the [1968 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Formula_One_season). They returned to the sport in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Formula_One_World_Championship) as an engine manufacturer, remaining until [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Formula_One_World_Championship). This period saw Honda dominate Grand Prix racing,[^cite_note-101] as between [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Formula_One_World_Championship) it won five consecutive Drivers' Championships with [Nelson Piquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Piquet), [Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) and [Alain Prost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Prost), and six Constructors' titles with [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Grand_Prix_Engineering) and [McLaren](20994.md).[^cite_note-102] A third stint from [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Formula_One_World_Championship), initially as engine maker and later also as team owner, yielded 17 podiums, including one win, and second place in the [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Formula_One_World_Championship) constructors' standings. They returned as a power unit supplier for the second year of the hybrid era in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Formula_One_World_Championship) and initially struggled, but intense development saw them become race winners again by [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Formula_One_World_Championship), and in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship) it won the World Drivers' Championship with [Max Verstappen](41758713.md) and [Red Bull Racing](1173193.md).[^cite_note-103] Honda formally left Formula One after 2021 to focus its resources on [carbon neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality) technologies, but an arrangement was made for it to extend power unit supply for Red Bull until 2025.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] While no longer a works team, Red Bull Racing still displayed Honda on its engine cover in this extended deal. As the series introduced more sustainable regulations, Honda announced it will formally rejoin in 2026 to provide power units to [Aston Martin](63473867.md) as a works team.[^cite_note-106]

Honda debuted in the [CART IndyCar World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championship_Auto_Racing_Teams) as an engine supplier in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_PPG_Indy_Car_World_Series), and the company won six consecutive Drivers' Championships and four Manufacturers' Championships between [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_PPG_Indy_Car_World_Series) and [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_CART_season).[^cite_note-IndyCar.com-107] In 2003, Honda transferred its effort to the IRL IndyCar Series. In [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_IndyCar_Series), Honda won the [Indianapolis 500](159018.md) for the first time and claimed the Drivers' and Manufacturers' Championships, a feat which it repeated in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_IndyCar_Series).[^cite_note-IndyCar.com-107] From [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_IndyCar_Series) to [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_IndyCar_Series), Honda was the series' lone manufacturer, before manufacturer competition returned for [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_IndyCar_Series). Since 2012, Honda's turbocharged V6 engines have won the Indianapolis 500 several times as well as claimed multiple Drivers' and Manufacturers' titles.[^cite_note-108] In the Japanese [Super Formula Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Formula_Championship), Honda-powered cars have won the championship numerous times since 1981, with its title tally in the double digits. In [Formula Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Two), Honda engines dominated the premier series in 1966 and scored multiple titles in the early 1980s.

In [sports car racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_car_racing), Honda won the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) in the GT2 class,[^cite_note-109] and in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) they won in the LMP2 category.[^cite_note-110] Honda made its factory debut in the [Super GT Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_GT) (previously known as the [All-Japan GT Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Japan_Grand_Touring_Car_Championship)) in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_All_Japan_Grand_Touring_Car_Championship), and in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_All_Japan_Grand_Touring_Car_Championship) it won its first championships.\[^cite_note-[www.dailysportscar.com-111](http://www.dailysportscar.com-111/)] Since then, it has won several further titles, uniquely with both mid- and front-engined cars.\[^cite_note-[www.dailysportscar.com-111](http://www.dailysportscar.com-111/)] Through their [Acura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura) and [HPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Performance_Development) divisions, Honda has also competed in [sports prototype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_prototype) racing, beginning with the Spice-Acura prototypes that won the [IMSA GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_GT_Championship) Lights championship in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_IMSA_GT_Championship), [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_IMSA_GT_Championship) and [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_IMSA_GT_Championship). Acura joined the [American Le Mans Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Le_Mans_Series) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_American_Le_Mans_Series) and won the [12 Hours of Sebring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Hours_of_Sebring) in class on their debut, before winning the championship in both the LMP1 and LMP2 classes in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_American_Le_Mans_Series). The cars were rebranded as HPDs for [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_American_Le_Mans_Series), after which they won multiple titles in the ALMS and also won the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) in the LMP2 class. Acura returned to prototype racing in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_IMSA_SportsCar_Championship) in the DPi class of the [IMSA SportsCar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_SportsCar_Championship), winning championship titles in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_IMSA_SportsCar_Championship), [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_IMSA_SportsCar_Championship) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_IMSA_SportsCar_Championship) as well as the [24 Hours of Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Hours_of_Daytona) overall in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_24_Hours_of_Daytona), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_24_Hours_of_Daytona), and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_24_Hours_of_Daytona).[^cite_note-112] Honda's [GT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_GT3) car won both the [IMSA GTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_SportsCar_Championship) and [Super GT GT300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_GT) titles.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

During the [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_A) era of the [Japanese Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Touring_Car_Championship), Honda won seven manufacturers' titles and six drivers' titles in the sub-1,600 cc division between 1986 and [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Japanese_Touring_Car_Championship).[^cite_note-115] The following [Super Touring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Touring) era of [touring car racing](52243.md) saw Honda win the Japanese and [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Touring_Car_Championship) championships in 1996 and 1997, while in Europe Honda's Super Touring cars claimed over 40 wins across the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Touring_Car_Championship), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Tourenwagen_Cup) and [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Touring_Car_Championship) series. After the collapse of the Super Touring regulations in the early 2000s, Honda remained involved in the British Touring Car Championship, where its cars would win multiple championships in the mid-2000s and throughout the 2010s. Honda entered the [World Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Touring_Car_Championship) in late [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_World_Touring_Car_Championship), and in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Touring_Car_Championship) it won the Manufacturers' World Championship. Honda's [TCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_Touring_Car) car won the global [TCR Model of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCR_Touring_Car#TCR_Model_of_the_Year) award in 2019, 2020, and 2024.[^cite_note-116]

### Motorcycles



[Honda RC212V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_RC212V) raced by [Dani Pedrosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Pedrosa)

HRC combines participation in motorcycle races throughout the world with the development of high-potential racing machines. Its racing activities are an important source for the creation of leading-edge technologies used in the development of Honda motorcycles. HRC also contributes to the advancement of motorcycle sports through a range of activities that include sales of production racing motorcycles, support for satellite teams, and rider education programs.

Soichiro Honda, being a race driver himself, could not stay out of international [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport). In 1959, Honda entered five motorcycles into the [Isle of Man TT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man_TT) race, the most prestigious motorcycle race in the world. While always having powerful engines, it took until 1961 for Honda to tune its chassis well enough to allow [Mike Hailwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Hailwood) to claim its first [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) victories in the 125 and 250 [cc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_centimetre) classes. Hailwood would later pick up its first Senior TT wins in 1966 and [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Isle_of_Man_TT). Honda's race bikes were known for their "sleek & stylish design" and exotic engine configurations, such as the 5-cylinder, 22,000 rpm, 125 cc bike and their 6-cylinder 250 cc and 297 cc bikes.

In 1979, Honda returned to [Grand Prix motorcycle racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) with the [monocoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocoque)-framed, [four-stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine) [NR500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_NR500). The [FIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Internationale_de_Motocyclisme) rules limited engines to four cylinders, so the NR500 had non-circular, 'race-track', cylinders, each with 8 valves and two connecting rods, in order to provide sufficient valve area to compete with the dominant [two-stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stroke_engine) racers. The experiment failed. For the 1982 season, Honda debuted its first two-stroke race bike, the NS500 and in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing_season), Honda won its first 500 cc [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) World Championship with [Freddie Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddie_Spencer). Since then, Honda has become a dominant marque in motorcycle Grand Prix racing, winning a plethora of top-level titles with riders such as [Mick Doohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Doohan) and [Valentino Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_Rossi). Honda also head the number of wins at the [Isle of Man TT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man_TT) having notched up 227 victories in the solo classes and [Sidecar TT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidecar_TT),[^cite_note-117] including [Ian Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Hutchinson_\(motorcycle_racer\))'s clean sweep at the [2010 races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Isle_of_Man_TT).[^cite_note-118]

The outright lap record on the [Snaefell Mountain Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snaefell_Mountain_Course) was held by Honda, set at the 2015 TT by [John McGuinness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McGuinness_\(motorcycle_racer\)) at an average speed of 132.701 mph (213.562 km/h) on a [Honda CBR1000RR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_CBR1000RR),[^cite_note-119] bettered the next year by [Michael Dunlop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dunlop) on a [BMW S1000RR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_S1000RR) at 133.962 mph (215.591 km/h).[^cite_note-120]

In the [Motocross World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motocross_World_Championship), Honda has claimed seventeen world championships. In the [World Enduro Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Enduro_Championship), Honda has captured eight titles, most recently with [Stefan Merriman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Merriman) in 2003 and with [Mika Ahola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mika_Ahola) from 2007 to 2010. In [motorcycle trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_trials), Honda has claimed three world championships with Belgian rider [Eddy Lejeune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddy_Lejeune).

### Compressed natural gas



Two clean vehicle versions of the [Honda Civic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Civic).\

The [Honda Civic GX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Civic_GX) was for a long time the only purpose-built [natural gas vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_vehicle) (NGV) commercially available in some parts of the US.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] The Honda Civic GX first appeared in 1998 as a factory-modified Civic LX that had been designed to run exclusively on [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas). The car looks and drives just like a contemporary [Honda Civic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Civic) LX, but does not run on gasoline. In 2001, the Civic GX was rated the cleanest-burning internal combustion engine in the world by the [US Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) (EPA).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

First leased to the City of Los Angeles, in 2005, Honda started offering the GX directly to the public through factory trained dealers certified to service the GX. Before that, only [fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_vehicles) were eligible to purchase a new Civic GX. In 2006, the Civic GX was released in New York, making it the second state where the consumer is able to buy the car.[^cite_note-125]

In June 2015, Honda announced its decision to phase out the commercialization of natural-gas powered vehicles to focus on the development of a new generation of [electric vehicles](279350.md) such as [hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle), [plug-in electric cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_electric_car) and hydrogen-powered [fuel cell vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell_vehicle). Since 2008, Honda has sold about 16,000 natural-gas vehicles, mainly to taxi and commercial fleets.[^cite_note-126]

### Flexible-fuel

Honda's Brazilian subsidiary launched [flexible-fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible-fuel_vehicle) versions for the [Honda Civic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Civic) and [Honda Fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Fit) in late 2006. As other Brazilian flex-fuel vehicles, these models run on any blend of [hydrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrous) ethanol ([E100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neat_alcohol_fuel)) and [E20-E25 gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E20_fuel).[^cite_note-HondaFlex-127][^cite_note-4Rodas-128] Initially, and in order to test the market preferences, the carmaker decided to produce a limited share of the vehicles with flex-fuel engines, 33 percent of the Civic production and 28 percent of the Fit models.[^cite_note-HondaFlex-127][^cite_note-4Rodas-128] Also, the sale price for the flex-fuel version was higher than the respective gasoline versions, around US\$1,000 premium for the Civic, and US\$650 for the Fit, despite the fact that all other flex-fuel vehicles sold in Brazil had the same tag price as their gasoline versions.[^cite_note-4Rodas-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] In July 2009, Honda launched in the Brazilian market its third flexible-fuel car, the [Honda City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_City).[^cite_note-131]

During the last two months of 2006, both flex-fuel models sold 2,427 cars against 8,546 gasoline-powered automobiles,[^cite_note-132] jumping to 41,990 flex-fuel cars in 2007,[^cite_note-133] and reaching 93,361 in 2008.[^cite_note-134] Due to the success of the flex versions, by early 2009 a hundred percent of Honda's automobile production for the Brazilian market is now flexible-fuel, and only a small percentage of gasoline version is produced in Brazil for exports.[^cite_note-135]

In March 2009, Honda introduced the world's first flex-fuel motorcycle in the Brazilian market. Manufactured by its Brazilian subsidiary, Moto Honda da Amazônia, the CG 150 Titan Mix is priced at approximately US\$2,700.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

At \$19,800 as a five-door hatchback it will be the least expensive hybrid available in the US.[^cite_note-142]

### Hydrogen fuel cell



[Honda FCX Clarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_FCX_Clarity) [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) [fuel cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell) vehicle

In [Takanezawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takanezawa), Japan, on 16 June 2008, Honda Motors produced the first assembly-line [FCX Clarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_FCX_Clarity), a [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle) [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_vehicle) [fuel cell vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell_vehicle). More efficient than a [gas-electric hybrid vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle), the FCX Clarity combines [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) and [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) from ordinary air to generate electricity for an electric motor. In July 2014 Honda announced the end of production of the [Honda FCX Clarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_FCX_Clarity) for the 2015 model.[^cite_note-\:2-149] The vehicle itself does not emit any [pollutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollutants) and its only by-products are heat and water. The FCX Clarity also has an advantage over gas-electric hybrids in that it does not use an [internal combustion engine](41228673.md) to propel itself. Like a gas-electric hybrid, it uses a [lithium ion battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_ion_battery) to assist the fuel cell during acceleration and capture energy through [regenerative braking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regenerative_braking), thus improving fuel efficiency. The lack of hydrogen filling stations throughout developed countries will keep production volumes low.[^cite_note-150] Honda will release the vehicle in groups of 150. California is the only US market with infrastructure for fueling such a vehicle, though the number of stations is still limited. Building more stations is expensive, as the [California Air Resources Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Air_Resources_Board) (CARB) granted \$6.8 million for four H2 fueling stations, costing US\$1.7 million each.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] Honda views hydrogen fuel cell vehicles as the long-term replacement of piston cars, not battery cars.[^cite_note-154]

Honda introduced the [CR-V e\:FCEV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_CR-V_\(sixth_generation\)#CR-V_e\:FCEV) in February 2024 in the US. It is a plug-in hybrid fuel cell version of the CR-V that is equipped with an electric motor, two high-pressure [hydrogen tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_tank) with a total capacity of 4.3 kg (9.5 lb) and a 17.7 kWh battery with plug-in charging capability.\[^cite_note-[www.dailysportscar.com-111](http://www.dailysportscar.com-111/)] It was also launched in Japan as the only version of the CR-V sold in the country, imported from the Marysville, Ohio assembly plant in the US. This model began production on 5 June 2024.[^cite_note-155] The later revealed to includes an H2 credit.[^cite_note-156]

Following this, the [Honda e\:Ny1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_e\:Ny1) was launched in 2023, with an [electric range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-electric_range) of 256 mi (412 km) on the top spec model. It is Honda's first electric SUV. In April 2022, Honda and [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) announced a joint venture to develop low-cost electric vehicles based on GM's [Ultium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultium) architecture in order to beat [Tesla](5533631.md) vehicles in sales.

In October 2023, the two companies announced that the joint venture has been cancelled due to slower-than-expected demand of electric vehicles and changing market conditions.[^cite_note-169] Although the upcoming [Honda Prologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Prologue) and [Acura ZDX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acura_ZDX) will use the Ultium architecture and will be manufactured by General Motors, future Honda electric vehicles will be designed solely by Honda and will be manufactured in Honda assembly plants.[^cite_note-170]

### Batteries

In August 2022, Honda and [LG Energy Solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Energy_Solution) revealed a joint venture (L-H Battery Co.) to establish a new lithium-ion battery factory in the United States, specifically for Honda and Acura electric vehicles. The initial goal was to produce 40 gigawatt hours of battery capacity.[^cite_note-171] In December 2025, LGES sold its ownership of the building to Honda amidst shifting market demands.[^cite_note-172] Following the cancellation of the Acura RSX and Honda 0 Series, L-H Battery began producing cells for LGES JF2 DC-LINK [energy storage systems (BESS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_energy_storage_system) in July 2026.[^cite_note-173] Some of the remaining capacity for EV batteries will be reallocated for Honda’s new generation of hybrid models.&#x20;

### EV Write Downs

In March 2026, Honda announced it expected to realize up to \$15.7B in expenses and losses related to its reassessment of its EV strategy. The company announced it decided to cancel the launches and development of certain models due to a slowdown in the North American EV market and increased competition in China.[^cite_note-174]

## Marketing

### Sales channels in Japan

Main article: [Honda automobile dealerships (Japan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_automobile_dealerships_\(Japan\))



Honda Primo ([Osaka](52230.md))

From 1978 to 2006, Honda sold vehicles in Japan through three separate dealership networks, which are Verno, Clio, and Primo. Each offered a distinct range of models, mirroring a similar strategy used by rivals [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_dealerships_\(Japan\)) and [Nissan](21240.md#Japan). Honda Verno, established alongside the [Honda Prelude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Prelude), sold sportier and better-equipped vehicles; Honda Clio carried models such as the [Honda Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Accord); and Honda Primo sold smaller cars including the [Honda Civic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Civic) and kei cars. The three networks were consolidated into a single *Honda Cars* brand in March 2006.

### International marketing



A Honda dealership in [Ontario](22218.md), Canada



A Honda dealership in [Dreghorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreghorn), Scotland

In 2003, Honda released its *[Cog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cog_\(television_commercial\))* advertisement in the UK and on the Internet. To make the ad, the engineers at Honda constructed a [Rube Goldberg Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rube_Goldberg_Machine) made entirely out of car parts from a [Honda Accord Touring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Accord_\(Japan_and_Europe_seventh_generation\)). To the chagrin of the engineers at Honda, all the parts were taken from two of only six hand-assembled [pre-production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-production_car) models of the Accord. The advertisement depicted a single cog which sets off a chain of events that ends with the Honda Accord moving and [Garrison Keillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Keillor) speaking the tagline, "Isn't it nice when things just... work?" It took 100 takes to create the ad.[^cite_note-175]

Honda has done humor marketing such as its 1985 four-page "How to fit six Hondas in a two-car garage" print ad[^cite_note-176] or "descending so low in a parking garage, they pass [stalagmites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalagmite) and a [Gollum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golem)-like figure."[^cite_note-177]

In 2004, it produced the *[Grrr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grrr_\(advertisement\))* advert, usually immediately followed by a shortened version of the 2005 *Impossible Dream* advert. In December 2005, Honda released *[The Impossible Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impossible_Dream_\(Honda_advert\))* a two-minute panoramic advertisement filmed in New Zealand, Japan, and Argentina which illustrates the founder's dream to build performance vehicles. While singing the song "[Impossible Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Impossible_Dream_\(The_Quest\))", a man reaches for his racing helmet, leaves his trailer on a minibike, then rides a succession of vintage Honda vehicles: a motorcycle, then a car, then a powerboat, then goes over a waterfall only to reappear piloting a [hot air balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon), with Garrison Keillor saying "I couldn't have put it better myself" as the song ends. The song is from the 1960s musical *[Man of La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_La_Mancha)*, sung by [Andy Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Williams).

In 2006, Honda released its *Choir* advertisement, for the UK and the internet. This had a 60-person choir who sang the car noises as the film of the Honda Civic is shown.

In the mid to late 2000s in the United States, during model close-out sales for the current year before the start of the new [model year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_year), Honda's advertising has had an [animated character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_character) known simply as [Mr. Opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Opportunity), voiced by [Rob Paulsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Paulsen). The casual-looking man talked about various deals offered by Honda and ended with the phrase "I'm Mr. Opportunity, and I'm knockin'", followed by him "knocking" on the television screen or "thumping" the speaker at the end of radio ads. In addition, commercials for Honda's international hatchback, the Jazz, are parodies of well-known pop culture images such as *[Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris)* and [Thomas the Tank Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Tank_Engine).

In late 2006, Honda released an ad with [ASIMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASIMO) exploring a museum, looking at the exhibits with almost childlike wonderment (spreading out its arms in the aerospace exhibit, waving hello to an [astronaut](664.md) suit that resembles him, etc.), while Garrison Keillor ruminates on progress. It concludes with the tagline: "More forwards please". Honda also sponsored [ITV's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Sport) coverage of [Formula One](10854.md) in the UK for 2007. However, it had announced that it would not continue in 2008 due to the sponsorship price requested by ITV being too high.

In May 2007, focuses on its strengths in racing and the use of the Red H badge – a symbol of what is termed as "Hondamentalism". The campaign highlights the lengths that Honda engineers go to in order to get the most out of an engine, whether it is for bikes, cars, powerboats – even lawnmowers. Honda released its Hondamentalism campaign. In the TV spot, Garrison Keillor says, "An engineer once said to build something great is like swimming in honey", while Honda engineers in white suits walk and run towards a great light, battling strong winds and flying debris, holding on to anything that will keep them from being blown away. Finally one of the engineers walks towards a red light, his hand outstretched. A web address is shown for the Hondamentalism website. The digital campaign aims to show how visitors to the site share many of the Hondamentalist characteristics.

At the beginning of 2008, Honda released – the *Problem Playground*. The advert outlines Honda's environmental responsibility, demonstrating a hybrid engine, more efficient solar panels, and the [FCX Clarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FCX_Clarity), a hydrogen-powered car. The 90-second advert has large-scale puzzles, involving [Rubik's Cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubik's_Cube), large shapes, and a 3-dimensional puzzle. On 29 May 2008, Honda, in partnership with [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4), broadcast a live advertisement. It showed [skydivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydivers) jumping from an airplane over Spain and forming the letters H, O, N, D, and A in mid-air. This live advertisement is generally agreed to be the first of its kind on British television. The ad lasted three minutes.[^cite_note-178]

In 2009, American Honda released the *Dream the Impossible* documentary series, a collection of 5- to 8-minute web vignettes that focus on the core philosophies of Honda. Current short films include *Failure: The Secret to Success*, *Kick Out the Ladder* and *Mobility 2088*. They have Honda employees as well as [Danica Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danica_Patrick), [Christopher Guest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Guest), [Ben Bova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bova), Chee Pearlman, [Joe Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Johnston) and [Orson Scott Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orson_Scott_Card). The film series plays at dreams.honda.com. In the UK, national television ads feature voice-overs from American radio host [Garrison Keillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Keillor), while in the US the voice of Honda commercials is actor and wrestler [John Cena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cena). In the North American market, Honda starts all of its commercials with a two-tone jingle since the mid-2010s.

### Sports

[Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna), the late F1 driver, once remarked that Honda played a pivotal role in his three world championships. He held deep respect for the company's founder, Soichiro Honda, and maintained a strong relationship with Nobuhiko Kawamoto, the chairman of Honda at the time. Senna even referred to Honda as "the greatest company in the world."[^cite_note-179]

As part of its marketing campaign, Honda is an official partner and sponsor of the North American [National Hockey League](21809.md), the [Anaheim Ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Ducks) of the NHL, and the arena named after it: [Honda Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Center). Honda also sponsored [The Honda Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Honda_Classic) golf tournament in the United States until 2023 and is a sponsor of the United States [Major League Soccer](71802.md). The "[Honda Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Player_of_the_Year)" award is presented in United States soccer. The "[Honda Sports Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Sports_Award)" is given to the best female athlete in each of twelve college sports in the United States. One of the twelve Honda Sports Award winners is chosen to receive the [Honda-Broderick Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda-Broderick_Cup), as "Collegiate Woman Athlete of the Year".

Honda sponsored [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) club [Valencia CF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_CF) starting from [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_La_Liga).[^cite_note-180] Honda has been a presenting sponsor of the [Los Angeles Marathon](1574891.md) since 2010 in a three-year sponsorship deal, with winners of the LA Marathon receiving a free [Honda Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Accord). Since 1989, the [Honda Campus All-Star Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Campus_All-Star_Challenge) has been a [quiz bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiz_bowl) tournament for [Historically black colleges and universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_colleges_and_universities).
