**Minamitorishima** (南鳥島; lit. "Southern Bird Island"), sometimes known as **Minami-Tori-shima** or **Minami-Torishima** and also known as **Marcus Island**, is an isolated [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_of_Japan) [coral atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_atoll) in the northwestern [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). It is the easternmost and most isolated [territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_\(country_subdivision\)) belonging to [Japan](15573.md), and the only Japanese territory on the [Pacific Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Plate), past the [Japan Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Trench). Although small (151 ha or 370 acres),[^cite_note-gaiyou-1] it is of strategic importance, as it enables Japan to claim a 428,875 km<sup>2</sup> (125,040 sq nmi) [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) in the surrounding waters.&#x20;

It is the easternmost territory of Tokyo, being administratively part of [Ogasawara Subprefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogasawara_Subprefecture). No [civilians](145804.md) live there, except personnel of the [Japan Meteorological Agency](457886.md), [Japan Self-Defense Forces](2785204.md) (JSDF), and [Japan Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard) serving temporary tours of duty on the island.[^cite_note-travelbook-2]

## Access

The island is off limits to civilians except for Japan Meteorological Agency staff. Reporters, documentary makers, and scientific researchers can sometimes get an entry permit. No commercial boat tours or flights visit the island, and civilians are currently not allowed access to Minamitorishima for tours or sightseeing, due to its use by the JSDF as an observation station.[^cite_note-travelbook-2]

## Geography and geology



A map of Minamitorishima

Minamitorishima is very remote. There is no other land for over 1,000 km (540 nmi; 621 mi) in any direction.&#x20;

Minamitorishima is located some 1,848 km (998 nmi; 1,148 mi) southeast of [Tokyo](30057.md) and 1,267 km (684 nmi; 787 mi) east of the closest Japanese island, [South Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Iwo_Jima) of the [Volcano Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano_Islands), and nearly on a straight line between mainland Tokyo and [Wake Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Island), 1,415 km (764 nmi; 879 mi) further to the east-southeast. The closest island to Minamitorishima is [East Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maug_Islands) in the [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands), which is 1,015 km (548 nmi; 631 mi) to the west-southwest.

The island is triangular in shape, and has a saucer-like profile, with a raised outer rim of between 5 and 9 m (16 and 30 ft) above sea level. The central area of the island is 1 m (3 ft) below sea level. Minamitorishima is surrounded by fringing [reefs](240844.md), which range from 50 to 300 m (164 to 984 ft) in width, enclosing a shallow lagoon, which is connected with the open ocean by narrow passages on the southern and northeastern sides.

Outside the reef, the ocean depths quickly plunge to over 1,000 m (3,300 ft). The island has a total land area of 1.51 km<sup>2</sup> (1 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-gaiyou-1] It takes about 45 minutes to walk around the island.

The sea is clear in the shallow area around Minamitorishima. At night, no [light pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_pollution) occurs, so rarely noticed stars are clearly visible in the sky.[^cite_note-travelbook-2]

The island does not have soil adequate to produce substantial crops, so food is brought in by supply ships and planes. The only foods grown on the island are [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), [mustard greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_greens), and [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconuts). [Saltwater fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_fish) are caught offshore.[^cite_note-travelbook-2]

### Minamitorishima area rare-earth deposits

After China restricted exports of strategic [rare-earth oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_element) (REOs) in 2009, Japan started to explore its seabeds for deposits.[^cite_note-3]

In January 2013, a deep-sea research vessel of the [Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Agency_for_Marine-Earth_Science_and_Technology) obtained seven deep-sea mud core samples from the seafloor at 5,600 to 5,800 m (18,400 to 19,000 ft) depth, about 250 km (130 nmi; 160 mi) south of the island.[^cite_note-4] The research team found a mud layer 2 to 4 m (6.6 to 13.1 ft) beneath the seabed that is extremely concentrated in REO. Analytical results showed that the maximum REO content in the mud was up to 0.66%.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In 2018, a scientific study of the seabed mud estimated that 16 million tons of REO mineralised sediments were within the studied area. The calculated rare-earth element and [yttrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yttrium) content for the research area was more than 16 million tons (average = 964 ppm).[^cite_note-Yutaro_etal_2018-7][^cite_note-8]

In February 2026, the Japanese government announced the retrieval of sediment containing rare earth minerals at a depth of 6,000 m (20,000 ft) below sea level off the island's coast.[^cite_note-9]

## Natural resources

In January 2026, the deep-sea scientific drilling vessel *[Chikyū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiky%C5%AB)*, operated by the [Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Agency_for_Marine-Earth_Science_and_Technology) (JAMSTEC), conducted the world's first trial extraction of [rare earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_element)-rich mud from approximately 6,000 m (20,000 ft) below the sea surface near the island, as part of Japan's Strategic Innovation Promotion Program.[^cite_note-10]

The mission lasted until 14 February 2026 and targeted the retrieval of approximately 35 t (34 long tons) of seabed mud, each tonne estimated to contain around 2 kg (4.4 lb) of rare earth elements. Japan's Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary Masanao Ozaki described the successful retrieval as "a meaningful achievement from the perspectives of economic security and comprehensive ocean development."[^cite_note-11]

The *[Japan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Times)* reported that full industrialisation of the process is targeted by 2028, requiring demonstrated viability from mining through separation and refining.[^cite_note-12] Environmental groups raised concerns about potential damage to deep-sea [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem). The government stated it would collect ecological data throughout the trial.

## Wildlife

A species in the [gecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko) family [Gekkonidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gekkonidae), *[Perochirus ateles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perochirus_ateles)*, inhabits the island. In Japan, these are found only in Minamitorishima and [South Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Iwo_Jima). They are thought to have arrived from [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia) on [driftwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftwood).

A large number of land snails, *[Achatina fulica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achatina_fulica)*, have parasites that are harmful to humans. [Marine life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life) inhabits the ocean around the island, including [sea snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_snake), [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna), [sharks](43617.md), and some rare fish. Small fish are in the shallow area around the island.[^cite_note-travelbook-2]

## Population

No local residents live on Minamitorishima. Civilians are not allowed to reside there, and the personnel of the Japan Meteorological Agency, JSDF, and the Japan Coast Guard, only serve on the island for a limited time, and in limited numbers.[^cite_note-travelbook-2]

### First known sightings

The first discovery and mention of an island in this area was made by a Spanish [Manila galleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon) captain, Andrés de Arriola, in 1694.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWelsch2001-13] It was charted in Spanish maps as *Sebastián López*, after Spanish Admiral Sebastián López, victorious in the [battles of La Naval de Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_La_Naval_de_Manila) in 1646 against the Dutch. Its exact location was left unrecorded until further sightings in the 19th century.

Captain [Bourn Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourn_Russell) (1794–1880) in the *Lady Rowena* departed [Sydney](27862.md), NSW, 2 November 1830 on a Pacific whaling voyage. On his return on 27 June 1832, he reported an island, not on his charts, which he named "William the Fourth's Island". The *[Sydney Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Herald)* reported Russell's description of the size, shape, and orientation of the island and its reef, but misspelled his name and gave the island a Southern Hemisphere latitude.[^cite_note-14]

The island was sighted again on 16 December 1864 by Captain Charles Gelett of the Hawaiian Evangelical Association's missionary ship *Morning Star*, and was called "Weeks Island" by him. Its position was recorded by a United States survey ship in 1874 and first sighted by a Japanese national, Kiozaemon Saito, in 1879.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Colonization and sovereignty contention

On 30 June 1886, Japanese explorer [Shinroku Mizutani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinroku_Mizutani?action=edit\&redlink=1) (水谷新六, 1850–1921) led a group of 46 colonists from [Haha-jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haha-jima) in the [Bonin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonin_Islands) to settle on Marcus Island. The settlement was named "Mizutani" after the leader of the expedition. The [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) officially annexed the island 24 July 1898,[^cite_note-18] the previous United States claim from 1889 according to the [Guano Islands Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guano_Islands_Act) not being officially acknowledged. The island was officially named "Minamitorishima" and placed administratively under the [Ogasawara Subprefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogasawara_Subprefecture) of Tokyo (*Tōkyō-fu*).

Sovereignty over the island before [World War I](4764461.md) was apparently disputed, as various sources from the time move the island from the American to Japanese domain without specific explanation. In 1902, the United States dispatched a warship from Hawaii to enforce its claims, but withdrew on finding the island still inhabited by Japanese, with a Japanese warship patrolling nearby.&#x20;

In 1914, [William D. Boyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_D._Boyce) included Marcus Island as an obviously American island in his book, *The Colonies and Dependencies of the United States*. In 1933, by orders of the Japanese government, the civilian inhabitants of Minamitorishima were evacuated. In 1935, the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) established a meteorological station on the island and built an airstrip.

### World War II



Minamitorishima under attack on 31 August 1943

After the start of [World War II](32927.md), the Japanese [garrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison) stationed on the island consisted of the 742-man Minamitorishima Guard Unit, under the command of [Rear Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_Admiral) [Masata Matsubara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masata_Matsubara?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the 2,005-man [12th Independent Mixed Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Independent_Mixed_Regiment?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army), under the command of [Colonel](199023.md) [Yoshiichi Sakata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshiichi_Sakata?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-19] The [United States Navy](20518076.md) bombed it repeatedly in 1942[^cite_note-20] and in 1943,[^cite_note-21] but never attempted to capture it. The island was featured in the 1944 U.S. film *[The Fighting Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fighting_Lady)*.&#x20;

Japan was able to resupply the garrison by [submarine](28825.md), using a channel, still visible today, cut through the reef on the northwest side of the island. The island was subject to repeated U.S. air attacks during World War II, and finally surrendered when the [destroyer](8779.md) [USS *Bagley*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Bagley_\(DD-386\)) arrived on 31 August 1945.[^cite_note-22]

### U.S. occupation



Minamitorishima in 1975

The [Treaty of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_San_Francisco) transferred the island to American control in 1952.[^cite_note-23] The island was returned to Japanese control in 1968, but the Americans retained control of the airstrip and [LORAN-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LORAN-C) station.

In 1964, after some delays caused by storms that ravaged the island during construction, the [U.S. Coast Guard](32223.md) opened a [LORAN-C navigation station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Island_LORAN-C_transmitter) on Minamitorishima, whose mast was until 1985 one of the tallest structures in the Pacific area, at 411 m (1,350 ft). Before replacing Loran A for general marine navigation, Loran C was used by submarine-launched Polaris missile systems. The existence and location of Loran C stations was classified.&#x20;

LORANSTA Marcus Island was billeted for 23 US Coast Guard personnel. The commissioning commanding officer was U.S. Coast Guard [Lieutenant Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_officer_rank_insignia) Louis. C. Snell. A detachment of [SeaBees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaBees) remained on the island for several months making repairs to the island's airstrip.

Coast Guardsmen stationed on the island served one-year tours that were later modified to allow an R\&R visit to mainland Japan at the six-month point. At the end of this isolated tour of duty, crew members received an additional 30 days of compensatory leave.

While under U.S. administration, the island was resupplied by air. During the [Vietnam War](32611.md), the weekly logistic flight was a [DC-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-6) flight conducted by the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Central_Intelligence_Agency)-operated [Air America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_America_\(airline\)). Later, a [C-130 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules) from the [345th Tactical Airlift Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/345th_Tactical_Airlift_Squadron), [Yokota Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokota_Air_Base), Japan, resupplied the island on missions every Thursday. Coast Guardsmen often amused themselves by judging aircraft landings, raising placards painted with large numbers.&#x20;

An unusually long four-hour ground time was scheduled to allow technicians who flew in to perform maintenance on the transmitter, and to offload extra fuel from the C-130 to power the island's generator. It allowed the Coast Guardsmen to read and answer letters, while aircrews [snorkeled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snorkeling) and collected green glass fishing buoys that had washed up on the shore.

### Resumed Japanese administration

The Marcus Island station was transferred from the U.S. Coast Guard to the [Japan Maritime Self Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Maritime_Self_Defense_Force) (JMSDF) in September 1993 and was closed in December 2009.

The island is used for weather observation and has a radio station, but little else. The JMSDF garrison was supplied by C-130s from [Iruma Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iruma_Air_Base), or by C-130s from [Haneda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport) or [Atsugi Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsugi_Air_Base), with flights via [Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwo_Jima) on a weekly basis. The runway of [Minami Torishima Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minami_Torishima_Airport) is only 1,300 m (4,300 ft) long and cannot handle large aircraft.

## Climate

Minamitorishima has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*), with warm to hot temperatures throughout the year. The wettest months are July and August. The driest months are February and March. It has the highest average temperature in Japan, at 25 °C (77 °F).[^cite_note-24] Temperature extremes range from 13.8 °C (56.8 °F) on February 10, 1976, to the highest, at 35.6 °C (96.1 °F) on July 17, 1951.
