**Tourism in Russia** represents 2.5% of the [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) of Russia. In 2024, foreign visitors to Russia for the purpose of tourism rose to 4.2 million, led by growth in visitors from China, the UAE, and Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-1]

Tourist routes include travel around the [Golden Ring of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Ring_of_Russia), cruises on the rivers including the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), and long journeys on the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md).

[Russian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cuisine), [Russian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_people), [Maslenitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslenitsa), [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars), [Sabantuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabantuy), [Siberia](29639.md), and [shamanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanist) rituals are popular with tourists.

## Tourist destinations

### Culture

Not including [Crimea](163045.md), the country has 23 sites on the [List of World Heritage Sites in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Russia) by [UNESCO](21786641.md).



Grand Cascade in [Peterhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhof_Palace) in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md)

Tourist destinations in Russia include Saint Petersburg (which appeared in the list of top visited cities of Europe in 2010) and Moscow, the current and the former capitals of the country. Moscow and Saint Petersburg museums such as [Hermitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Museum) and [Tretyakov Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tretyakov_Gallery), theaters including [Bolshoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshoi_Theatre) and [Mariinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariinsky_Theater), churches such as [Saint Basil's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Basil's_Cathedral), [Cathedral of Christ the Saviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Christ_the_Saviour), [Saint Isaac's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Isaac's_Cathedral) and [Church of the Savior on Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Savior_on_Blood), fortifications such as the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) and [Peter and Paul Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_and_Paul_Fortress), squares such as [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) and [Palace Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Square), and streets such as [Tverskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tverskaya_Street) and [Nevsky Prospect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevsky_Prospect). Palaces and parks are found in the former [imperial residences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template\:Imperial_palaces_in_Russia) in the suburbs of Moscow ([Kolomenskoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomenskoye), [Tsaritsyno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaritsyno_Park)) and Saint Petersburg ([Peterhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petergof), [Strelna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strelna), [Oranienbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranienbaum,_Russia), [Gatchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatchina_Palace), [Pavlovsk Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlovsk_Palace), [Tsarskoye Selo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsarskoye_Selo)). Moscow contains Soviet-era buildings along with the [Moscow International Business Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_International_Business_Center). Saint Petersburg has classical architecture, rivers, channels and [bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_in_Saint_Petersburg).

[Nizhny Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Novgorod) is the capital of the [Volga region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Federal_District). Nizhny Novgorod is divided into two parts by the [Oka River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oka_River). The *[Upper City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Nizhny_Novgorod#Upper_City)* is its historical part. The *[Lower City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Nizhny_Novgorod#Lower_City)* is its industrial and commercial part. Here are the [Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Novgorod_Fair), the old [Sormovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sormovsky_City_District) and Kanavino, [GAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAZ) and Sotsgorod (the so-called "city in the city"), the [railway terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Novgorod_railway_station), and the [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strigino_International_Airport).

Kazan, the capital of [Tatarstan](67135.md), shows a mix of Christian Russian and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) cultures.

[Sakha Republic](57905.md) proposes to use former forced labour camps as a tourist attraction.[^cite_note-2] Tourists visit places of Communist crimes, e.g., of the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) and [Solovetsky Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solovetsky_Islands).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

#### Museums

See also: [List of museums in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Russia)

Russia is home to museums that include the [Tretyakov Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tretyakov_Gallery), the [Kremlin Armoury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin_Armoury) and the [State Historical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Historical_Museum) in Moscow, the [Hermitage Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Museum), and the [Russian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Museum) in St Petersburg, the [Kazan Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Kremlin) in Kazan, etc. Russia has museums related to its literary and classical music heritage, such as [Yasnaya Polyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasnaya_Polyana) associated with [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy), the [Mikhaylovskoye Museum Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhaylovskoye_Museum_Reserve) associated with [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin), the [Dostoyevsky Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dostoyevsky_Museum), the [Tchaikovsky State House-Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchaikovsky_State_House-Museum), and the [Rimsky-Korsakov Apartment and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimsky-Korsakov_Apartment_and_Museum).

Museums related to Russia's military history and military hardware include the [Central Museum of the Great Patriotic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_Great_Patriotic_War,_Moscow) on [Poklonnaya Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poklonnaya_Hill), the [Central Naval Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Naval_Museum) in St Petersburg, the [Battle of Stalingrad Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamayev_Kurgan) in Volgograd. Museums related to science and technology include the [Polytechnic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Museum) of Moscow, and the [Memorial Museum of Cosmonautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Museum_of_Cosmonautics).

The GULAG History Museum tells the story about the [GULAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GULAG) camps in [Siberia](29639.md).[^cite_note-5]

### Nature

In Russia, Nature Reserves have history and it has its own word of definition Zapovedniks ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): заповедник, plural заповедники) more than [100 Nature Reserves exist in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapovednik#List_of_Nature_Reserves_\(%22Zapovedniks%22\)_in_Russia) and more than [50 National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Parks_of_Russia).

National parks and sanctuaries of Russia include the [Baikal Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal_Nature_Reserve), the [Altai Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altai_Nature_Reserve), the [Lazovsky Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazovsky_Nature_Reserve), the [Kedrovaya Pad Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedrovaya_Pad_Nature_Reserve), the [Curonian Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curonian_Spit) National Park, the [Valdaysky National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdaysky_National_Park), the [Baikal-Lena Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal-Lena_Nature_Reserve), the [Ilmen Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilmen_Nature_Reserve).

The [Seven Wonders of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Wonders_of_Russia) include [Lake Baikal](56865.md), [Valley of Geysers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Geysers), [Manpupuner rock formations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manpupuner_rock_formations), [Kizhi Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizhi_Island), and [Mount Elbrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Elbrus).

Other areas include [Republic of Adygea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Adygea) where [Fisht Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisht_Mountain) is located, Chechnya Republic where [Lake Kezenoyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kezenoyam) is located.[^cite_note-6]

### Spas



Narzan Gallery building in Kislovodsk housing a dozen fountains of both hot and cold mineral-rich water

[Mineral spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_spa) resorts have been established across Russia in such regions as [Kamchatka Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Krai), [Altai Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altai_Krai), [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md), [Stavropol Krai](474074.md), [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus) region of Russia.[^cite_note-7] Some cities have natural hot spring water during winter and some of Russian cities are called Russian Spa town, including [Pyatigorsk](1433382.md), [Yessentuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yessentuki), [Kislovodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kislovodsk), [Zheleznovodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheleznovodsk) and [Mineralnye Vody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralnye_Vody); these towns are jointly known as the [Caucasian Mineral Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanatoriums_of_the_Caucasian_Mineral_Waters).

Russia has one of the largest water borders in world, but only the more Southern regions are suitable for resort tourism. The warm subtropical Black Sea coast of Russia is the site for some seaside resorts such as [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi) and [Tuapse](1623160.md).

### Medical tourism

Russia is a destination for medical tourism. A factor in its popularity was the relatively weak ruble [post-2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_financial_crisis_\(2014%E2%80%932016\)), which saw the industry grow from some 110,000 clients in 2017[^cite_note-forbes-medical-tourism-9] to some 728,000 clients in the first five months of 2020.[^cite_note-ratanews-10] Stomatology is the most used (44% of patients), genecology and urology follow (25% taken together), the other services are plastic surgery (10%), ophthalmology (10%), and cardiology (5%).[^cite_note-forbes-medical-tourism-9] Most clients come from [the CIS states](36870.md), where receiving high-tech medical assistance can be problematic, particularly from Central Asia, which amounts for 62% of all patients; but also from Eastern Europe (32%), South and East Asia (5%).[^cite_note-11] In addition to price and accessibility of complex manipulations, the difference in regulations between Russia and the clients' own nations is a driving factor for receiving care in Russia: for instance, [in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization) is illegal in China, but legal in Russia.[^cite_note-ratanews-10]

### Religious tourism

Religious tourism has two main subtypes: pilgrimage, as travel done for religious or spiritual purposes, and the viewing of religious monuments and artefacts for sightseeing. The former is relatively insignificant for the Russian tourism industry, amounting for approximately 100 thousands pilgrims yearly.[^cite_note-12] The latter is more important. Orthodox Christianity being the most common religion in Russia, it also accounts for most religious monuments across the country.
