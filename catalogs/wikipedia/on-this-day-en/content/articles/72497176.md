**Unidad por Chile** (**UpCh**, lit. 'Unity for Chile'), known informally as the **Government Alliance** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Alianza de Gobierno*) until 2026, is a Chilean coalition of politiсal parties that were part of the government of president [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md).[^cite_note-2] The coalition was formed on 6 November 2022 after a meeting called by Boric at the [Palace of Cerro Castillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Cerro_Castillo).[^cite_note-radio-3]

The coalition is made up of the member parties of the two coalitions that supported Boric's candidacy and entered his administration: [Apruebo Dignidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apruebo_Dignidad) and [Democratic Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialism_\(Chile\)).[^cite_note-4] The alliance agreed to have a common and rotating spokesperson, the first spokeswoman for the coalition being the president of the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Chile), [Paulina Vodanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulina_Vodanovic).

## History



Official leaders in Cerro Castillo on 6 November 2022 announcing the creation of the "Government Alliance".

In March 2021, the primary elections of the [Apruebo Dignidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apruebo_Dignidad) pact, a coalition made up of the [Broad Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_\(Chilean_political_coalition\)) and [Chile Digno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_Digno), took place. The Broad Front candidate [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md) won the election, running as the sole candidate of Apruebo Dignidad in the first round of the presidential elections. As the result of this presidential election, he would participate in the runoff election.

After the defeat of the [Christian Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_\(Chile\)) candidate [Yasna Provoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasna_Provoste), the [New Social Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Pact) coalition formed that year for the elections, bringing together the parties from the [Concertación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concertaci%C3%B3n) and [Nueva Mayoría](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Mayor%C3%ADa). These parties had governed the country for 24 years previously. The New Social Pact supported the candidacy of Boric, the Apruebo Dignidad candidate, who won in the runoff.

After Boric's victory in the presidential elections, and given how the congress was composed after the parliamentary elections[^cite_note-5]—Apruebo Dignidad only managed to elect 37 of the 150 [deputies](716259.md) and 5 of the 50 [senators](683411.md)—Gabriel Boric included in the government the parties that made up the New Social Pact coalition, with the exception of the [Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_\(Chile\)). These parties would later adopt the nomination of [Democratic Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialism_\(Chile\)).

The government of Gabriel Boric has been supported by these two political blocs (or “two souls”, as the local press has called them): Democratic Socialists and Apruebo Dignidad.[^cite_note-6] The difficult coexistence of both coalitions within the ruling party, added to the defeat of the option supported by the government in the [plebiscite on 4 September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Chilean_constitutional_referendum). This gave rise to the political committee on 17 October 2022, calling an official conclave, with the purpose of analyzing the course that the government should take going forward.[^cite_note-7] The conclave took place on 6 November 2022 in the Presidential Palace of Cerro Castillo, where the leaders of the ruling parties met.[^cite_note-radio-3] Since then, the coalition has met regularly to form an electoral and content committee,[^cite_note-8] and has replicated the first national conclave in various [regions](299550.md) of the country.[^cite_note-9]

On 30 April 2025, the coalition was formalized under the name Unidad por Chile, to run in the primaries and presidential elections of that year.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]
