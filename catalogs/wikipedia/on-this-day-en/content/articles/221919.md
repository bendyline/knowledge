**Ōita Prefecture** (大分県, *Ōita-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[oꜜː.i.ta, oː-, oː.i.taꜜ.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located on the island of [Kyūshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%ABsh%C5%AB).[^cite_note-3] Ōita Prefecture has a population of 1,081,646 (1 February 2025) and has a geographic area of 6,340 km<sup>2</sup> (2,450 sq mi). Ōita Prefecture borders [Fukuoka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Prefecture) to the northwest, [Kumamoto Prefecture](220713.md) to the southwest, and [Miyazaki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_Prefecture) to the south.

[Ōita](6793355.md) is the capital and largest city of Ōita Prefecture, with other major cities including [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu), [Nakatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakatsu,_%C5%8Cita), and [Saiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiki,_%C5%8Cita).[^cite_note-4] Ōita Prefecture is located in the northeast of Kyūshū on the [Bungo Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Channel), connecting the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) and [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea), across from [Ehime Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehime_Prefecture) on the island of [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku). Ōita Prefecture is famous for its [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring) and is a popular tourist destination in Japan for its *[onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen)* and *[ryokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryokan_\(inn\))*, particularly in and around the city of Beppu. It has more *onsen* than any other [Japanese prefecture](74517.md).[^cite_note-5]

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Ōita Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_%C5%8Cita_Prefecture)

Around the 6th century, [Kyushu](16914.md) consisted of four regions: [Tsukushi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukushi_Province), [Hi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi_Province), [Kumaso Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumaso_Province) and [Toyo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyo_Province).

Toyo Province was later divided into two regions, upper and lower Toyo Province, called [Bungo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Province) and [Buzen Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzen_Province).&#x20;

After the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), districts from [Bungo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Province) and [Buzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzen_Province) provinces were combined to form Ōita Prefecture.[^cite_note-6] These provinces were divided among many local *[daimyōs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D)* and thus a large castle town never formed in Ōita. From this time that whole area became known as "Toyo-no-Kuni", which means "Land of Abundance".

The origins of the name Ōita are documented in a report from the early 8th century called the Chronicles of Bungo ([豊後国風土記](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%B1%8A%E5%BE%8C%E5%9B%BD%E9%A2%A8%E5%9C%9F%E8%A8%98), *_bungonokuni-fudoki_*).[^cite_note-oita20-7] According to the document, when Emperor Keikō visited the Kyushu region, stopping first in Toyo-no-Kuni, he exclaimed that 'This is a vast land, indeed. It shall be known as Okita-Kuni!' Okita-Kuni, meaning "Land of the Great Fields", later came to be written as "Ōita". Present day interpretations based on Ōita's topography state that Oita's name comes from "Okita", meaning "many fields", rather than "vast" or "great" field, because of Ōita's complex terrain.[^cite_note-oita20-7]

In the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) (1603–1867) the town of [Hita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hita) was the government seat for the entire domain of [Kyushu](16914.md), which was directly controlled by the national government or *[shōgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgun)* at that time. The region became well known for the money-lending industry based out of [Hita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hita). Merchants in Hita's Mameda and Kuma districts worked with the national government to create this money-lending industry known as Hita-kin.

### Shrines and temples

*[Sasamuta-jinja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasamuta-jinja)* and *[Yusuhara Hachiman-gū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuhara_Hachiman-g%C5%AB)* are the chief [Shinto shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_shrine) (*[ichinomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichinomiya)*) in the prefecture.[^cite_note-8]

[Usa Jjingū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usa_Jing%C5%AB), the head shrine of more than 40,000 [Hachiman shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachiman_shrine), is located in [Usa, Ōita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usa,_%C5%8Cita).[^cite_note-9]

## Geography

Ōita Prefecture is on the north-eastern section of the island of [Kyūshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%ABsh%C5%AB). It is 119 kilometres (74 mi) from east to west, and 106 kilometres (66 mi) from north to south, with a total area of 6,340.71 square kilometers.

Surrounded by the [Suo Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suo_Channel?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Honshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honsh%C5%AB) Island to the north, the [Iyo Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyo_Channel?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku) Island to the east, it is bordered by [Miyazaki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_Prefecture) to the south, and [Fukuoka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Prefecture) and [Kumamoto Prefecture](220713.md) to the west. It is divided between north and south by a major tectonic line running from [Usuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuki) City in Ōita Prefecture to Yatsushiro City in [Kumamoto](6793346.md) Prefecture, which is to the west of Ōita. There are several other tectonic lines running from east to west through the prefecture. The northern part of the prefecture features [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and [metamorphic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rocks), while the southern area features [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), which is the foundation for the [Tsukumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukumi) cement industry, and several limestone caves. The Kirishima Range is a [volcanic belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_belt) that runs vertically through the prefecture and contributes to the many hot spring sources that make the region a popular tourist attraction and makes Ōita the prefecture with the largest number of hot springs in the whole country.[^cite_note-10]

The mountain ranges include the peaks of [Mount Yufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Yufu), [Mount Tsurumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tsurumi), [Mount Sobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sobo), [Mount Katamuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Katamuki), and [Mount Kujū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kuj%C5%AB) (which is called the "roof of Kyushu"). These mountain ranges contribute to the fact that 70% of Oita is covered by forests, and the rivers and streams that flow from these ranges give the prefecture rich water sources. The prefecture's major water sources are the [Yamakuni River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamakuni_River) (with the [Heisei Ozeki Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisei_Ozeki_Dam) and [Yabakei Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yabakei_Dam) on it), Yakkan River, Ōita River, [Ōno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cno_River) (with [Hakusui Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakusui_Dam) on it), and Banjō River. The coastline features include [Beppu Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu_Bay) and the [Bungo Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Channel).&#x20;



[Kujū Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kuj%C5%AB)

[Mount Kujū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kuj%C5%AB), a volcano, is surrounded by highlands called the Kujū Highlands and the Handa Highlands. The main agriculture activity on the plateau grasslands north and south of Mt. Kujū is [dairy farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming). There are open plains throughout the prefecture with Nakatsu Plain in the north, Oita Plain in the center, and Saiki Plain in the south. The inland areas consist of basin valleys in [Hita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hita), [Kusu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusu_District,_%C5%8Cita), [Yufuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yufuin) and [Taketa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taketa), which were formed by [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) buildup in combination with river [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion).&#x20;

Ōita has a 759 km (472 mi) coastline that has shoals in the north, Beppu Bay in the center, and a jagged or sawtooth "rias coastline" in the south. Sea cliffs, caves, and sedimentary rock formations that can be found in [Saiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiki) City's Yakata Island are considered very rare outside of coral reef areas. Ōita's coastal waters contribute to a prosperous fishing industry.

As of April 1, 2014, 28% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely the [Aso Kujū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aso_Kuj%C5%AB_National_Park) and [Setonaikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setonaikai_National_Park) National Parks; the [Nippō Kaigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipp%C5%8D_Kaigan_Quasi-National_Park), [Sobo-Katamuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobo-Katamuki_Quasi-National_Park), and [Yaba-Hita-Hikosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaba-Hita-Hikosan_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and the [Bungo Suidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Suid%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Jinkakuji Serikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinkakuji_Serikawa_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Kunisaki Hantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki_Hant%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Sobo Katamuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobo_Katamuki_Prefectural_Natural_Park_\(%C5%8Cita\)), and [Tsue Sankei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsue_Sankei_Prefectural_Natural_Park) Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-11]

Currently, the prefecture has 14 cities, 3 districts, 3 towns, and one village. From 2005 to 2006, all municipalities but [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu), [Tsukumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukumi), [Himeshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himeshima), [Hiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiji), and all towns in [Kusu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusu_District,_%C5%8Cita) District, were merged, and the total municipalities went down from 58 on December 31, 2004, to 18 after the creation of the city of [Kunisaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki) by merging with 4 towns from Higashikunisaki District on March 31, 2006. As a result, the prefecture became the one with the fewest municipalities within Kyūshū, and the fourth fewest in Japan. However, Ōita Prefecture now has the fewest towns (3) and fewest towns and villages combined (4) in all of Japan.

## Economy

Ōita Prefecture's industrial activity is centered on agricultural products. Fishery products and manufactured goods rank second and third respectively in terms of Ōita's industrial activity.

Ōita is Japan's number one producer of the following products:[^cite_note-12]

**Kabosu limes:** Ōita Prefecture is Japan's number one producer of [kabosu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabosu), a citrus fruit that is similar to a lime. Kabosu are available year-round but peak season for taste and quality is from August to October. [Kabosu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabosu) are rich in vitamin C and contain beneficial acids, such as citric acid. Kabosu have been produced in the [Taketa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taketa) and [Usuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuki) areas for many years.

**Dried shiitake mushrooms:** Ōita is the largest producer of dried [shiitake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiitake) mushrooms in Japan, and the cultivation of [shiitake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiitake) mushrooms is said to have originated in Ōita. Ōita's dried shiitake rank number one in Japan in production quantity and quality. [Shiitake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiitake) are said to be beneficial in the prevention of high blood pressure and [arterial sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arterial_sclerosis).

**Saffron:** [Saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron) has been cultivated in Ōita since the late 19th century, and Ōita's [Taketa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taketa) area produces 80% of Japan's total amount of [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron), making Ōita the top producer of [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron). The quality of Ōita [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron) has gained international acclaim as active component levels are several times higher than foreign saffron. [Saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron) is said to have many benefits including circulation enhancement and is used as a food coloring and natural medicine.

**Galingale:** Ōita is Japan's top producer of galingale ([シチトウ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B7%E3%83%81%E3%83%88%E3%82%A6)|shichitō), a kind of rush plant, a grass with a distinctive triangular profile, belonging to the family [Cyperaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperaceae). It is grown in [Kunisaki Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki_Peninsula) of Ōita Prefecture and is used in the production of Ryukyu-style [tatami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatami) mats, as it is dust and moisture absorbent and has a pleasant scent.

**Madake bamboo:** The madake variety of bamboo makes up 60% of Japan's cultivated bamboo, and Ōita is Japan's top producer. It is the most popular variety of bamboo used in handiwork and traditional crafts since it is very flexible and pressure resistant.

Ōita ranks number one in Japan (and second in the world next to America's Yellowstone National Park) for the amount of hot-spring output and geothermal power. Ōita also ranks number one in Japan for the amount of limestone production.

## Demographics



Oita prefecture population pyramid in 2020

As of October 1, 2008, Ōita's total population was 1,201,715. Broken down into age groups, it was determined that 13.3% of the population was between the age of newborn and 14 years old, while 60.6% of the population was between the ages of 15 years and 64 years old, and 25.8% of the population was 65 years old or older.

As of November 1, 2009, there were 4,160 foreign students in Ōita total, from 101 different countries and territories, with the majority being from Asia (e.g. China and South Korea).[^cite_note-15]

## Culture

Oita is in the northeast corner of the island of Kyushu. Its coastal areas, farmland, highland, and mountains lend to a mix of different farming and fishing culture. There are many festivals throughout the year to pray for healthy harvests and abundant crops.

As of May 2006, 146 cultural assets in Ōita were designated by the national government (Agency for Cultural Affairs), four of which are designated as [National Treasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Treasures_of_Japan). Additionally, the prefecture itself has designated almost 700 traditions, properties, landmarks etc. as cultural assets.

### National treasures

1. [Fuki-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuki-ji) Temple: The only wooden structure in Kyushu to remain intact since the [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period) (794–1192). Fukiji Temple is in [Bungotakada.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungotakada,_%C5%8Cita)

   

   [Fuki-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuki-ji)'s Main Hall

2. The Main Building of [Usa Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usa_Shrine): A building which is said to be a prototype for *[hachiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachiman_shrine)* style architecture located in [Usa City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usa,_%C5%8Cita).

3. Peacock Buddhist Altar Fitting: This is a Buddhist altar fitting called *Kujaku Monkei* with engraved peacocks and an inscription dated 1209. The information on it tells of the relationship between Usa Shrine and the former Mirokuji, a [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temples_in_Japan) once connected to the shrine as a [jingū-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing%C5%AB-ji).

4. [Usuki Stone Buddhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuki_Stone_Buddhas): Approximately 60 cliff carvings of Buddha that were crafted between the Heian period (794–1192) and the [Kamakura period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_period) (1185–1333) are the only rock carvings of their kind to have received a "[National Treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Treasure_\(Japan\))" designation. They are in [Usuki City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuki,_%C5%8Cita).&#x20;

   

   One of the [Usuki Stone Buddhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuki_Stone_Buddhas)

Below are some of Oita's cultural traditions that are designated by the Agency for Cultural Affairs as [Important Intangible Cultural Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_Cultural_Property_\(Japan\)):

1. Shujo Onie Fire Festival: An event held to pray for national security, health, and longevity on the first day of the Chinese New Year at temples of Tendai Buddhist denomination in the Kunisaki Peninsula area. The three temples that continue to hold this event are the Tennenji-temple (Bungotakata City), Iwatoji-temple (Kunisaki Town) and Jobutsuji-temple (Kunisaki Town).

2. Koyo Shrine Puppet Show: This is a puppet show also known as "Kitabaru Puppet Show" which uses *kugutsu* puppets that perform dance and sumo wrestling matches. Oita's Hachiman Kohyo Shrine is in Nakatsu City.

3. Hita's Gion Festival: A festival held in Hita City in July with parade floats that are up to 12 meters high. Although the festival only takes place once a year, the magnificent floats are on display year-round at the Gion Festival Float Museum in Kuma Town, Hita City.

4. Yoshihiro Gaku Traditional Performing Art: Dance performed along with traditional song and music in Musashi Town in Kunisaki Peninsula

5. Manufacturing Process of Sulfur "Flowers" at Myoban Hot Spring: Thatched huts at Myoban Hot Spring are used to produce *yu-no-hana* or sulfur "flowers" which are crystals that develop naturally on the ground around the springs. The manufacturing and collection process of the sulfur flowers has remained largely unchanged since the Edo period and thus the manufacturing process itself is designated as an ethno-cultural asset. The crystals are used as the main component of bath salts sold in Myoban, which are a popular souvenir that is used to help heal skin conditions.

### Dance

**[Kagura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagura)** is a sacred dance performed at festivals and celebrations throughout the prefecture.

**Shonai kagura** is a festive dance that has been practiced for over 200 years and is representative of Oita Prefecture. Another kagura, the **Ondake-style Kagura**, was nationally designated as an "Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property" in 2007. There is also traditional song and music known as "*gaku*" that is performed in the Kunisaki Peninsula Area and is accompanied by characteristic dances such as the *Yoshihirogaku* in Musashi Town. Dancers wear grass skirts and dance with a drum tied to their front and a flag tied to their backs to pray to the Buddhist God Amida Buddha. In the Ono district there are 80 groups of *Shishimai* or dancers who perform a lion dance with roots based on the Ondake-style dance.

### Crafts

**[Onta Pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onta_ware)** is the name of a type of stoneware pottery made for everyday usage – typically called 'mingei' (folk art or craft) in Japanese. The community is situated in the Hiko mountain range, about 17 kilometres from the centre of Hita City, and is said to have been established in 1705 to make large wares – lidded jars for pickled vegetables and fruit, water crocks, ash burners, and pouring vessels with small spouts – for local farmhouses. At the time potters were themselves farmers, who produced pots during the 'off season' in agriculture. These they fired in a cooperative kiln (kyōdō noborigama).&#x20;



[Onta ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onta_ware) *[tokkuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokkuri)*

Onta pottery is now produced full-time by ten families in Sarayama, five of whom continue to share and fire an eight chambered climbing kiln. The other five households fire independent climbing kilns of four or five chambers, which they fire approximately six times a year. The potters use clays that they dig locally and obtain natural materials (notably, wood ash, rice straw ash, feldspar, iron oxide and, occasionally, copper) with which to mix their glazes. Sarayama is famous for the 'karausu' clay pounders lining its two streams and powered by the water therein. The fact that the clay pounders prepare only enough clay for two people to work with full-time at the wheel has determined both household structure and the number of houses able to take up pottery in Sarayama.[^cite_note-16]

For anything other than small pots, potters use a kick wheel on which to throw their wares, which they decorate typically with hakeme and tobiganna slipware decoration techniques. In April 1995, the Agency for Cultural Affairs announced the designation of Onta Pottery as an "Important Intangible Cultural Property" in 1995.[^cite_note-17] This designation is for the actual techniques used in making the pottery and not the actual pots themselves. Precisely because the designation is for the process rather than the product, it is regarded as an "intangible" property and is the only stoneware pottery-making process so designated in Japan.

**Bamboo Crafts** were started in the late 14th century to create baskets for travelling goods salesmen. During the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) (1600–1868) [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu) thrived as a tourist town and bamboo baskets and goods were used in the daily lives of the local people for everything from cooking to washing in order to meet the demands of the thriving tourist population. The bamboo items soon became a souvenir that tourists purchased to take home and this solidified making Beppu a center for Bamboo crafts production. In 1903 a training center for bamboo workers was established and present-day visitors to Beppu can enjoy learning about the history of bamboo, and hands-on classes at the Beppu City Traditional Bamboo Crafts Center. Festivals using bamboo shoots as candle holders are carried out yearly in the autumn in [Usuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usuki), [Taketa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taketa), and [Hita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hita) Cities.

### Religion

The [Kunisaki Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki_Peninsula) has been called "Buddha's Village" and many [Buddhist statues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_sculpture) and [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temples_in_Japan) fill the areas surrounding [Bungotakada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungotakada,_%C5%8Cita) and [Kunisaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki). The most notable of these are the *[Rokugō Manzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokug%C5%8D_Manzan)* (六郷満山) temples. *[Shinbutsu-shūgō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinbutsu-sh%C5%ABg%C5%8D)* (神仏習合), or the syncretism of [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Japan) and [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto), is said to have begun in this region out of the unique *Rokugō Manzan* culture.[^cite_note-Shinbutsu_Shugo-18][^cite_note-Manzan-19]

[Usa Jingū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usa_Jing%C5%AB) in [Usa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usa,_%C5%8Cita) is the head shrine of [Hachiman Shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachiman_shrine) in Japan. It is said to be the birthplace of [mikoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoshi).[^cite_note-mikoshi-20]

### Architecture

The [Agency for Cultural Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agency_for_Cultural_Affairs) also designates certain areas for preservation as [Groups of Traditional Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_Traditional_Buildings). The merchant quarter of [Hita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hita), Mameda Town, is one of 83 districts (as of April 1, 2009) throughout the country designated as "Important Preservation Districts for Groups of Traditional Buildings".[^cite_note-21] Old samurai residences throughout the prefecture are points of architectural interest. Nioza Historical Road in Usuki is also lined with buildings dating back to the 16th century and also in Usuki the Inaba-Family Villa is a former samurai residence open to the public.

[Arata Isozaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arata_Isozaki) is a world-renowned architect who is from Oita. The former Oita Prefectural Library (now Oita Art Plaza) won an award for architectural design in 1967. Other works of his can be found throughout the prefecture including B-con Plaza in Beppu, Bungo-No-Kuni Information Library, the Audio-Visual Center in Oita City, and Yufu Train Station.

### Music

The [Martha Argerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Argerich) music festival "Argerich's Meeting Point in Beppu" is an annual event held in [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu) City. Martha Argerich is the General Director of the festival and the event is supported by a large number of volunteers. It takes place over 10 days and includes recitals and also lessons. This international event welcomes music lovers from all over the world to Beppu.

### Arts

The Oita-Asian Sculpture Exhibition (see external link below) is a biennial event that takes place in Asaji Town in Bungo-Ono City. This exhibition is carried out to commemorate Oita sculptor Asakura Fumio, and to encourage rising artists throughout Asia. Applicants are accepted from within Japan and from several Asian countries. Exhibition winners are given generous prizes and their works are kept on display at the Asakura Fumio Memorial Museum in Bungo-Ono City.

Ōita Prefecture is famous for its [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_springs), particularly those in and around the city of [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu). The most well-known of Beppu's hot springs are known as the [Hells of Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hells_of_Beppu). These "hells" are for viewing only and cannot be used for bathing.[^cite_note-hell_list-22] Beppu also has eight major [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_gradient) [hotspots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotspot_\(geology\)) known as [*Beppu Hattō*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu_Onsen) (別府八湯).[^cite_note-Hatto-23] Within the city of Beppu, there are more than 150 individual *[onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen)* bathing facilities.[^cite_note-stamps-24]



Umi Jigoku (Sea Hell), one of the [Beppu Hells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hells_of_Beppu)

The city's [ryokans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryokan_\(inn\)) and public onsen are amply supplied by the same volcanic source. When [Chiba University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_University) and Tokyo's [Institute for Sustainable Energy Policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Sustainable_Energy_Policies_\(Japan\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) compiled a list of prefectures meeting demands for reusable energy, Oita ranked number one with a 25.2% rate of self-sufficiency through the use of geothermal energy and hot spring heat.[^cite_note-25]

The city of [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu) is a busy passenger port with regular ferry links through the inland sea to [Osaka](52230.md) and several other destinations, and offers a variety of cultural experiences. For instance, the public aquarium "[Umi-tamago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oita_Marine_Palace_Aquarium)" on the shoreline outside Beppu features basketballing [sea otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_otter), performing [archer fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_fish), and puzzle-solving octopuses, along with more naturalistic displays of freshwater and marine fish from around the world.

Also in Beppu, there is the Kijima Kogen [Amusement Park](137327.md) ([城島高原パーク](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9F%8E%E5%B3%B6%E9%AB%98%E5%8E%9F%E3%83%91%E3%83%BC%E3%82%AF)). In [Hiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiji), there is a [Sanrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanrio) theme park called [Harmonyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonyland?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([ハーモニーランド](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A2%E3%83%8B%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A9%E3%83%B3%E3%83%89)).&#x20;

[Yufuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yufuin,_%C5%8Cita) is another popular destination for tourists. It is known for its *[onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen)*, [shopping streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dtengai), *[ryokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryokan)*, [Mount Yufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Yufu) (由布岳, *Yufudake*), and Lake Kinrin ([金鱗湖](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%87%91%E9%B1%97%E6%B9%96), *Kinrin-ko*).[^cite_note-26]

The [Kunisaki Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki_Peninsula) is well known for its hiking trails, many mountain valleys, and the [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temples_in_Japan) and [Buddhist statues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_sculpture) located throughout the area.[^cite_note-27]



[Onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen) in [Yufuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yufuin,_%C5%8Cita) with a view of [Mount Yufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Yufu)&#x20;

Another attraction is [Mount Takasaki Monkey Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takasakiyama_Natural_Zoological_Garden), a park featuring two distinct troupes of wild [Japanese macaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_macaque) monkeys that make regular visits to the feeding grounds. The reserve was initially established to prevent the monkeys from raiding the region's fruit crops, a behavior that brought them into conflict with local farmers. The wild macaques can be observed up close in the park during the feeding sessions.&#x20;



Feeding the monkeys at [Mount Takasaki Monkey Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takasakiyama_Natural_Zoological_Garden)

Previously, [Mt. Hachimen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hachimen) in [Nakatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakatsu,_%C5%8Cita) was home to [Concert on the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_on_the_Rock), a music festival held as an annual charity event which saw over 30 international acts performing over a weekend in June 2004. The event was held again in 2005 but has since been discontinued.

In 2017, the creators of [Pokémon Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Go) held an event from March 4 to 13 featuring newly spawned [Pokémon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon) Snorlax for players to check areas hit by the [2016 Kumamoto earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kumamoto_earthquakes) and help support the local economy.[^cite_note-\:0-28] A spokeswoman for the company said that the game event would provide many opportunities for players to come and see the sights.[^cite_note-\:0-28] The event followed a similar event that spawned the Pokémon Lapras in the [Miyagi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyagi_Prefecture) in November 2016 in areas that were severely damaged by the [2011 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami).[^cite_note-29]

## Notable people

- [Ōtomo Sōrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ctomo_S%C5%8Drin) (1530–1587): The Otomo family ruled over the [Funai Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funai_Domain), which is present day [Ōita City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cita,_%C5%8Cita), in the 16th century. Funai was a very internationalized city which engaged in trade and exchange with other nations. Sōrin, the 21st leader of the [Ōtomo clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ctomo_clan), embraced Western culture enthusiastically and invited the missionary [Francis Xavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Xavier) to the city to promote Christianity. Sōrin dreamed of creating a Christian nation; he was baptized and given the name "Don Francisco". Sōrin died in [Tsukumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukumi).

  [^cite_note-32]

- [Miura Baien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_Baien) (September 1, 1723 – April 9, 1789): A scholar originally known as Susumu but called Baien after the name of his private school where he educated many scholars. Miura developed his own system of logic and wrote many works including his three famous words, Deep Words (玄語, *_gengo_*), Redundant Words (贅語, *_zeigo_*), and Bold Words (敢語, *_kango_*). He also worked in a hospital and had a good knowledge of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy). He hand made an astronomical globe that was passed down through many generations.

  [^cite_note-oita20-7]

  &#x20;He spent his entire life in Tominaga Village which is the present day area of [Aki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_District,_K%C5%8Dchi) Town in [Kunisaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunisaki) City. Miura Baien is considered one of Ōita's three sages along with Hoashi Banri and Hirose Tansō.

- [Hoashi Banri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoashi_Banri?action=edit\&redlink=1) (帆足万里; February 11, 1778 – July 30, 1852): Miura Baien's pupil who expanded his academic ability into many fields including [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism), natural sciences, medicine and language. He taught himself Dutch to reference scientific publications for his eight-volume work *Kyuritsu*, which was considered the top work of Western natural science in Japan at that time. In 1832 he was made Minister for the Feudal Lord to fix the financial problems of the Hiji clan.

  [^cite_note-oita20-7]

  &#x20;Banri Hoashi is considered one of Ōita's three sages along with Miura Baien and Hirose Tansō.

- [Hirose Tansō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirose_Tans%C5%8D) (広瀬淡窓; May 22, 1782 – November 28, 1856): A Confucian scholar, poet and educator from a money-lending family in Hita. Ōita's current governor [Katsusada Hirose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katsusada_Hirose) is a descendant of Tansō Hirose. In Edo period Japan, education was limited to samurai families and the rich. However, Hirose Tansō opened a school called Kangien ([咸宜園](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%92%B8%E5%AE%9C%E5%9C%92)) meaning "all are welcome" and admitted students regardless of social status, age, or education level. The school's methodology of a "self-administered work-study policy" is said to have had great influence on the modern day education system in Japan. Former Prime Minister [Kiyoura Keigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyoura_Keigo) was educated here, with other students who went on to become influential scholars, artists and politicians. The school remains were designated a historical site in 1932 and are a couple blocks from the original Hirose family house, now the Hirose Museum. There, Tansō Hirose and other family members' works are on display, with other original Hirose artifacts, China dolls, tea ceremony utensils and more. Both are in Mameda Town, about a 10-minute walk from [Hita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hita) Station. Tansō Hirose is considered one of the Oita's three sages along with Miura Baien and Hoashi Banri. An asteroid called [10009 Hirosetanso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10009_Hirosetanso) discovered by the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1977 was named after Tansō Hirose.

- [Fukuzawa Yukichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuzawa_Yukichi) (1834–1901): Founded Japan's oldest institute of higher education, [Keio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keio_University) in Tokyo. Fukuzawa Yukichi grew up in the [Nakatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakatsu) domain and is pictured on the 10,000 yen bill. He was influential in Japan's education system by promoting independence and self-reliance of the Japanese people at his classes as [Keio-Gijuku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keio-Gijuku_University), known as present day Keio University, originally a school for Western studies. The university now educates in a range of fields and produces influential and prominent alumni.

- [Hiroshi Nagahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshi_Nagahama) (born 1970): Veteran [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime) industry luminary who began his career in 1990 with [Madhouse Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhouse_\(company\)) as an animator and went on to direct *[Mushishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushishi)*, *[Detroit Metal City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Metal_City)*, *[The Flowers of Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flowers_of_Evil_\(manga\))* and *[The Reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reflection_\(TV_series\))*, as well as serving as art director of *[Revolutionary Girl Utena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Girl_Utena)*.

- [Yamamoto Tatsuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamamoto_Tatsuo_\(politician\)), once governor of the [Bank of Japan](188155.md) from 1898 to 1903, was from here.
