The **Musketeers of the military household of the King of France** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Mousquetaires de la maison militaire du roi de France* or *compagnie des mousquetaires du roi*), also known as the **Musketeers of the Guard** (*Mousquetaires de la garde*) or **King's Musketeers** (*Mousquetaires du roi*), were an elite fighting company of the [military branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [Maison du Roi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_du_Roi), the royal household of the [French monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_monarchs).

## History

They were founded in 1622 when [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII) furnished a company of [light cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cavalry) (the *carabins*, created by Louis' father [Henry IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France)) with [muskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskets). The Musketeers fought in battles both on foot ([infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry)) and on horseback ([cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry)). They formed the [royal guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_guard) for the king while he was outside of the royal residences (within the royal residences, the king's guard was the *[Garde du corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garde_du_Corps_\(France\))* and the *[Gardes suisses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardes_suisses)*). The Musketeers of the Guard wore an early type of military uniform with a [tabard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabard) (known as *soubreveste*), indicating that they "belonged" to the King, and an embroidered white cross denoting the fact that they were formed during the [Huguenot rebellions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot_rebellions) in support of the Catholic cause.



Uniforms of Musketeers of the Guard, 1660–1814

Shortly after the Musketeers were established, a second company was founded to report to [Cardinal Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu). At the cardinal's death in 1642, the company passed to his successor [Cardinal Mazarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Mazarin), who disbanded his Musketeers in 1646. He revived the Musketeers in 1657 with a company of 150 men. Upon Mazarin's death in 1661, the cardinal's Musketeers passed to [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV).

In 1664, the two companies were reorganized: one company took the name "Grey Musketeers" (*mousquetaires gris*) from the color of their matched horses, while the second were called "Black Musketeers" (*mousquetaires noirs*), mounted on black horses. At roughly the same time, the size of the Musketeer companies was doubled.

The Musketeers were among the most prestigious of the military companies of the [Ancien Régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime), and in principle membership in the companies was reserved for nobles. With the reforms of [Michel le Tellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_le_Tellier) – which mandated a certain number of years of military service before nobles could attain the rank of officer – many nobles sought to do this service in the privileged Musketeer companies.

In 1776, the Musketeers were disbanded by [Louis XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVI) for budgetary reasons. Reformed in 1789, they were disbanded again shortly after the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution). They were reformed on 6 July 1814 and definitively disbanded on 1 January 1816.

## Notable Musketeers of the Guard

The following are some of the notable Musketeers:

- [Charles de Batz de Castelmore d'Artagnan](299489.md) (the historical basis of [Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Dumas)' character [d'Artagnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Artagnan) in his 1844 novel *[The Three Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers)*)

- [Henri d'Aramitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_d'Aramitz) (the historical basis of Dumas' character [Aramis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramis) in *[The Three Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers)*)

- [Armand d'Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_d'Athos) (the historical basis of Dumas' character [Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athos_\(character\)) in *[The Three Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers)*)

- [Isaac de Porthau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_de_Porthau) (the historical basis of Dumas' character [Porthos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porthos) in *[The Three Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers)*)

- [Jean-Armand du Peyrer de Troisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comte_de_Troisville) (the historical basis of Dumas' character Monsieur de Tréville in *[The Three Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers)*)

- [Gilbert du Motier, Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette) (later American Revolutionary War general)

- [Louis de Rouvroy, duc de Saint-Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Rouvroy,_duc_de_Saint-Simon)

- [Étienne de Boré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne_de_Bor%C3%A9), first [Mayor of New Orleans](3094181.md)
