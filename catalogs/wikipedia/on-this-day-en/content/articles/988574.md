**Bannu District** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): بنو ولسوالۍ, [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): ضلع بنوں) is a district in the [Bannu Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannu_Division) of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), Pakistan. Its status as a district was formally recorded in 1861 during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj).[^cite_note-gazetteer-3][^cite_note-thor-4]<sup>: 3 </sup>

Cloth weaving, sugar mills and the manufacturing of cotton fabrics, machinery and equipment are the major industries in Bannu. It is also known for its weekly *Jumma* fair.

## Geography

District borders [North Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Waziristan) to the northwest, [Karak](1106087.md) to the northeast, [Lakki Marwat](2799819.md) and [Bettani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettani) to the southeast, and [South Waziristan](4175082.md) to the southwest. It is represented in the [provincial assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) by four [MPAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Provincial_Assembly).[^cite_note-thor-4]<sup>: 3–16 </sup> It is surrounded by rugged and dry mountains, it is a fertile place, and early English visitors had been known to refer to it as a "paradise" – see the description by Edwardes quoted by Thornton.[^cite_note-gazetteer-3][^cite_note-thor-4]<sup>: 6–7 </sup>

The district forms a basin drained by the [Kurram River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurram_River), [Gambila River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambila_River) and [Tochi river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochi_river)[^cite_note-gazetteer-3]<sup>: 392 </sup> which originate in the hills of Waziristan. Extending its reach to the base of the frontier hills, the Bannu Valley unfolds as an asymmetrical oval, spanning 60 miles (97 km) from north to south and 40 miles (64 km) from east to west.[^cite_note-EB1911-5]

## History

See also: [Bannu](3769844.md)

The history of Bannu goes back to prehistoric times due to its strategic location. Notably, [Sheri Khan Tarakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheri_Khan_Tarakai) is an ancient settlement site located in the Bannu District. Here, remnants bear witness to the presence of the most ancient village settlement within the Bannu region. This site witnessed occupation from the late fifth century to the early third millennium BC.[^cite_note-6]

The sacred texts of [Zend Avesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zend_Avesta) and [Vendidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendidad) mentions Varəna, the [Avestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avesta) predecessor of the name for Bannu, as one of the sixteen most beautiful and perfect lands created by [Ahura Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahura_Mazda).[^cite_note-7] Bannu is the homeland and birthplace of [Fereydun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fereydun).[^cite_note-8]<sup>: 47–50 </sup>

[Malik Dilasa Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Dilasa_Khan?action=edit\&redlink=1) had also fought a successful battle against the Sikh Empire.[^cite_note-9] in which he killed one of the prominent Sikh Military commander Jai Singh Atariwala in the Battle of Bannu.[^cite_note-10]

### British era (1849–1947)

Bannu District was annexed by the British from its former [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) rulers after the [Second Anglo-Sikh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Sikh_War) of 1848–1849.[^cite_note-11] After the British annexation of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), then including parts of the [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province_\(1901%E2%80%9355\)) (NWFP), the valley was administered by [Herbert Edwardes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Edwardes).[^cite_note-EB1911-5] In 1857 revolt the valley was peaceful, it was subject to incursions from the [Waziri tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_\(Pashtun_tribe\)) of the [Tochi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochi_Valley) and the neighbouring hills. The primary export of the region was [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [Salt](1605200.md) and [alum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alum) were also quarried at [Kalabagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalabagh).[^cite_note-EB1911-5]

As of 1911, the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus) had no bridges within the district, but was navigable for local boats throughout its course of 76 mi (122 km).[^cite_note-EB1911-5]



Hindus and Sikhs of Bannu migrating to India during the [partition of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India).

### Jirga

#### Bannu Jirga (1947)

Main article: [Bannu Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannu_Resolution)

On 21 June 1947 in Bannu, a [jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga) was held by Pashtun leaders including [Bacha Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacha_Khan), his brother [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) [Dr Khan Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Khan_Sahib), the [Khudai Khidmatgars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khudai_Khidmatgars), members of the Provincial Assembly, [Mirzali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirzali_Khan) (Faqir of Ipi), and other tribal chiefs, just seven weeks before the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India). The jirga declared the [Bannu Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannu_Resolution), which demanded that the Pashtuns be given a choice to have an independent state of [Pashtunistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtunistan) composing all Pashtun territories of British India, instead of being made to join either India or Pakistan. However, the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) refused to comply with the demand of this resolution, in response to which the Khudai Khidmatgars boycotted the [1947 North-West Frontier Province referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_North-West_Frontier_Province_referendum) for merging the province into Pakistan.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

#### Pashtun National Jirga (2022)

Main article: [Pashtun National Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_National_Jirga)

On 11–14 March 2022, the [Pashtun National Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_National_Jirga) was held at [Mirakhel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirakhel) in Bannu District in order to defend the rights of the [Pashtun people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) in the country. The critical issues which were faced by the Pashtuns were discussed during the jirga in a bid to suggest solutions to them.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-mashaal-15]

## Universities in Bannu

District Bannu has two Universities with one i.e [University of Science & Technology, Bannu](https://ustb.edu.pk/) (USTB),[^cite_note-37] founded in 2005 by Mr. Akram Khan Durrani, the then Chief Minister, has a full degree awarding status and another one as a campus of the University of Engineering & Technology (UET), Peshawar. USTB offers a wide variety of courses in Science, Engineering and Arts subjects at Bachelors, Master and PhD level. These universities host students from District Bannu, the neighboring districts of Lakki Marwat, Karak, Waziristan as well as from all over Pakistan.
