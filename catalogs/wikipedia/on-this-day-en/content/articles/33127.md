**Wisconsin**[^cite_note-13] is a [state](18618239.md) in the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest) and [Great Lakes regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region) of the [United States](3434750.md). It borders [Minnesota](19590.md) to the west, [Iowa](26810748.md) to the southwest, [Illinois](14849.md) to the south, [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) to the east, [Michigan](18859.md) to the northeast, and [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior) to the north. With a population of about 6 million and an area of about 65,500 square miles (170,000 km<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-ReferenceA-9] Wisconsin is the [21st-largest state by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) and the [23rd-largest by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area). It is divided into [72 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Wisconsin). The state's [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Wisconsin_by_population) is [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), while its capital and second-most populous city is [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin). Other urban areas include [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin) and the [Fox Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Cities).

[Wisconsin's geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Wisconsin) is diverse, with dense forests in the north (including [Chequamegon–Nicolet National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chequamegon%E2%80%93Nicolet_National_Forest)), rugged unglaciated hills in the western [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area), and wooded plains, lowlands, and farms stretching from the interior east to Lake Michigan. Wisconsin has the third-longest [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) coastline, after [Ontario](22218.md) and Michigan. At the time of European contact, the area was inhabited by [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_peoples) and [Siouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouan) nations, and today it is home to [eleven federally recognized tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes_by_state#Wisconsin).[^cite_note-14] Wisconsin was originally part of the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory) and was [admitted as a state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) in 1848. Many European settlers entered the state during the 19th and early 20th centuries, predominantly from [Germany](11867.md) and [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Wisconsin remains a center of [German American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) and [Scandinavian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_American) culture,[^cite_note-17] particularly in [its cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Wisconsin), with foods such as [bratwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratwurst) and [kringle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kringle).

Wisconsin is one of the nation's leading [dairy producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming) and is known as "America's Dairyland"; it is particularly famous for [its cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_cheese).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The state is also recognized for [its breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_breweries_in_Wisconsin), a longstanding [beer industry centered in Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_in_Milwaukee), relatively permissive [alcohol laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Wisconsin), and a prominent [drinking culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_culture).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Its economy is dominated by manufacturing, healthcare, information technology, and agriculture—specifically dairy, [cranberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranberries), and [ginseng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginseng).[^cite_note-22] Tourism is also a major contributor to its economy.[^cite_note-23] The [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States_by_GDP) in 2020 was \$348 billion.[^cite_note-24] Wisconsin is home to one UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md), comprising [two of the most significant buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_20th-Century_Architecture_of_Frank_Lloyd_Wright) designed by Wisconsin-born architect [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright): his studio at [Taliesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliesin_\(studio\)) and his [Jacobs I House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_and_Katherine_Jacobs_First_House).[^cite_note-whs-25] Since 2016, it has been considered a [swing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state) in national and statewide elections; the [Republican Party](32070.md) was founded in Wisconsin in 1854.

## Etymology

The word *Wisconsin* originates from the name given to the [Wisconsin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_River) by one of the [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages)-speaking Native American groups living in the region at the time of [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-26] The French explorer [Jacques Marquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Marquette) was the first European to reach the Wisconsin River, arriving in 1673 and calling the river *Meskousing* (likely ᒣᔅᑯᐤᓯᣙ *meskowsin*) in his journal.[^cite_note-27] Subsequent French writers changed the spelling from *Meskousing* to *Ouisconsin*, and over time this became the name for both the Wisconsin River and the surrounding lands. English speakers [anglicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicized) the spelling from *Ouisconsin* to *Wisconsin* when they began to arrive in large numbers during the early 19th century. The legislature of [Wisconsin Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Territory) made the current spelling official in 1845.[^cite_note-28]

The Algonquian word for Wisconsin and its original meaning have both grown obscure. While interpretations vary, most implicate the river and the red sandstone that lines its banks. One leading theory holds that the name originated from the [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Illinois_language) word *Meskonsing*, meaning 'it lies red', a reference to the setting of the Wisconsin River as it flows through the reddish sandstone of the [Wisconsin Dells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dells_of_the_Wisconsin_River).[^cite_note-29] Other theories include claims that the name originated from one of a variety of [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) words meaning 'red stone place', 'where the waters gather', or 'great rock'.[^cite_note-30] More recently, [University of Wisconsin-Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Green_Bay) lecturer and [Menominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee) elder Napos Turney suggested the origin of the name may be the [Menominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee_language) word *Wēskōhsaeh*, meaning "good place".[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

## History

Main article: [History of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Wisconsin)

### Early history



[Aztalan State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztalan_State_Park) marks the site of an ancient [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) settlement that flourished during the 10th to 13th centuries.

Wisconsin has been home to a wide variety of cultures over the past 14,000 years. The first people arrived around 10,000 BCE during the [Wisconsin Glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Glaciation). These early inhabitants, called [Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians), hunted now-extinct [ice age animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene#Fauna) such as the [Boaz mastodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boaz_mastodon), a prehistoric [mastodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastodon) skeleton unearthed along with spear points in southwest Wisconsin.[^cite_note-33] After the ice age ended around 8000 BCE, people in the subsequent [Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_the_Americas) lived by hunting, fishing, and gathering food from wild plants. Agricultural societies emerged gradually over the [Woodland period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_period) between 1000 BCE to 1000 CE. Toward the end of this period, Wisconsin was the heartland of the "[Effigy Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effigy_Mound) culture", which built thousands of animal-shaped mounds across the landscape.[^cite_note-34]

Later, between 1000 and 1500 CE, the [Mississippian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) and [Oneota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneota) cultures built substantial settlements including the fortified village at [Aztalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztalan) in southeast Wisconsin.[^cite_note-35] The Oneota may be the ancestors of the modern [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_people) and [Ho-Chunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Chunk) nations who shared the Wisconsin region with the [Menominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee) at the time of European contact.[^cite_note-36] Other Native American groups living in Wisconsin when Europeans first settled included the [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe), [Sauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people), [Meskwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki), [Kickapoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickapoo), and [Potawatomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potawatomi), who migrated to Wisconsin from the east between 1500 and 1700.[^cite_note-37]

### European settlements

Main articles: [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France), [Canada (New France)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_\(New_France\)), [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), [Treaty of Paris (1763)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)), [Province of Quebec (1763–1791)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec_\(1763%E2%80%931791\)), and [Indian Reserve (1763)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Reserve_\(1763\))



&#x20;[Jean Nicolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Nicolet), depicted in a 1910 painting by Frank Rohrbeck, was probably the first European to explore Wisconsin. The mural is located in the [Brown County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_County_Courthouse_\(Wisconsin\)) in Green Bay.

The first European to visit what became Wisconsin was probably the French explorer [Jean Nicolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Nicolet). He canoed west from [Georgian Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Bay) through the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) in 1634, and it is traditionally assumed that he came ashore near [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin) at [Red Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Banks,_Brown_County,_Wisconsin).[^cite_note-38] [Pierre Radisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Radisson) and [Médard des Groseilliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9dard_des_Groseilliers) visited Green Bay again in 1654–1666 and [Chequamegon Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chequamegon_Bay) in 1659–1660, where they traded for fur with local Native Americans.[^cite_note-39] In 1673, Jacques Marquette and [Louis Jolliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Jolliet) became the first to record a journey on the [Fox-Wisconsin Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox-Wisconsin_Waterway) all the way to the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) near [Prairie du Chien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_du_Chien,_Wisconsin).[^cite_note-40] [Frenchmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frenchmen) like [Nicholas Perrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Perrot) continued to ply the [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) across Wisconsin through the 17th and 18th centuries, but the French made no permanent settlements in Wisconsin before [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) won control of the region following the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War) in 1763. Even so, French traders continued to work in the region after the war, and some, beginning with [Charles de Langlade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Langlade) in 1764, settled in Wisconsin permanently, rather than returning to British-controlled Canada.[^cite_note-Wisconsin_Historical_Society-41]

The British gradually took over Wisconsin during the French and Indian War, taking control of Green Bay in 1761 and gaining control of all of Wisconsin in 1763. Like the French, the British were interested in little but the fur trade. One notable event in the fur trading industry in Wisconsin occurred in 1791, when two free African Americans set up a fur trading post among the Menominee at present-day [Marinette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinette,_Wisconsin). The first permanent settlers, mostly [French Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadian), some Anglo-[New Englanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) and a few African American freedmen, arrived in Wisconsin while it was under British control. Charles de Langlade is generally recognized as the first settler, establishing a trading post at Green Bay in 1745, and moving there permanently in 1764.[^cite_note-Wisconsin_Historical_Society-41]

Settlement began at Prairie du Chien around 1781. French residents at the Green Bay trading post called it "La Baye" while British fur traders favored "Green Bay" for the green tints the water and shore took on each spring. The British name gradually stuck. The transition to British rule had little adverse effect on French residents, as each side needed the other's cooperation. The French had issued trading licenses sparingly, but the British, eager to maximize profits, extended them freely to both British and French traders. The fur trade reached its peak under British rule, and Wisconsin's first self-sustaining farms were established during this period. From 1763 to 1780, Green Bay was a prosperous community that grew its own food, built graceful cottages, and hosted dances and festivities.[^cite_note-42]

Joseph Roi built the [Tank Cottage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_Cottage) in [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin) in 1776. Located in [Heritage Hill State Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_Hill_State_Historical_Park), it is the [oldest standing building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_oldest_buildings_in_Wisconsin) from Wisconsin's early years and is listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).[^cite_note-NRHP-43]

### U.S. territory

Main articles: [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), [Treaty of Paris (1783)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)), [Northwest Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance), [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory), [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory), [Illinois Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Territory), [Michigan Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Territory), [Organic act § List of organic acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_act#List_of_organic_acts), and [Wisconsin Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Territory)



The [Francois Vertefeuille House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francois_Vertefeuille_House) in [Prairie du Chien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_du_Chien,_Wisconsin) was built in the 1810s by fur traders.

Wisconsin became a territorial possession of the United States in 1783 after the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). In 1787, it became part of the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory). As territorial boundaries subsequently developed, it was then part of [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory) from 1800 to 1809, [Illinois Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Territory) from 1809 to 1818, and [Michigan Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Territory) from 1818 to 1836. However, the British remained in control until after the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), the outcome of which finally established an American presence in the area.[^cite_note-44]

Under American control, the economy of the territory shifted from fur trading to lead mining. The prospect of easy mineral wealth drew immigrants from throughout the U.S. and Europe to the lead deposits at [Mineral Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_Point,_Wisconsin), [Dodgeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodgeville,_Wisconsin), and nearby areas. Some miners found shelter in the holes they had dug, and earned the nickname "badgers", leading to Wisconsin's identity as the "Badger State".[^cite_note-45] The sudden influx of white miners prompted tension with the local Native American population. The [Winnebago War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnebago_War) of 1827 and the [Black Hawk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_War) of 1832 culminated in the forced [removal of Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_removal) from most parts of the state.[^cite_note-46]

Following these conflicts, the [Wisconsin Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Territory) was created by an act of the [United States Congress](31756.md) on April 20, 1836. By fall of that year, the best prairie groves of the counties surrounding what is now Milwaukee were occupied by farmers from the [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) states.[^cite_note-47]

### Statehood

Main articles: [Admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) and [List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union)



On May 29, 1948, the U.S. Post Office issued a [commemorative stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_stamp) celebrating the 100th anniversary of Wisconsin statehood, featuring the state capitol building and map of Wisconsin.

The Great Lakes, via the [Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal), facilitated the travel of both [Yankee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee) settlers and European immigrants to Wisconsin Territory. Yankees from New England and [upstate New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_New_York) seized a dominant position in law and politics, enacting policies that marginalized the region's earlier Native American and French-Canadian residents.[^cite_note-48] Yankees also speculated in real estate, platted towns such as Racine, Beloit, Burlington, and Janesville, and established schools, civic institutions, and [Congregationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregationalist) churches.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] At the same time, many [Germans](152735.md), Irish, [Norwegians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians), and other immigrants also settled in towns and farms across the territory, establishing [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) and [Lutheran](23371382.md) institutions.

The growing population allowed Wisconsin to gain statehood on May 29, 1848, as the 30th state. Between 1840 and 1850, Wisconsin's non-Indian population had swollen from 31,000 to 305,000. More than a third of residents (110,500) were foreign born, including 38,000 Germans, 28,000 British immigrants from England, Scotland, and Wales, and 21,000 Irish. Another third (103,000) were Yankees from New England and western New York state. Only about 63,000 residents in 1850 had been born in Wisconsin.[^cite_note-52]

[Nelson Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Dewey), the first [governor of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Wisconsin), was a [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Democratic_Party_\(United_States\)). Dewey oversaw the transition from the territorial to the new state government.[^cite_note-1960bio-53] He encouraged the development of the state's infrastructure, particularly the construction of new roads, railroads, canals, and harbors, as well as the improvement of the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_River_\(Illinois_River_tributary\)) and [Wisconsin Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_River).[^cite_note-1960bio-53] During his administration, the [State Board of Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Board_of_Public_Works?action=edit\&redlink=1) was organized.[^cite_note-1960bio-53] Dewey, an [abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionist), was the first of many Wisconsin governors to advocate against the spread of [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) into new states and territories.[^cite_note-1960bio-53]

Further information: [Pioneer Women in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Women_in_Wisconsin)

### Civil War era and economic growth

Main article: [Wisconsin in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_in_the_American_Civil_War)



The [Little White Schoolhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_White_Schoolhouse) in [Ripon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon,_Wisconsin) held the nation's first meeting of the [Republican Party](32070.md).

Politics in early Wisconsin were defined by the greater national debate over slavery. A free state from its foundation, Wisconsin became a center of northern [abolitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States). The debate became especially intense in 1854 after [Joshua Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Glover), a runaway slave from [Missouri](19571.md), was captured in [Racine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racine,_Wisconsin). Glover was taken into custody under the Federal [Fugitive Slave Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugitive_Slave_Act_of_1850), but a mob of abolitionists stormed the prison where Glover was held and helped him escape to Canada. In a trial stemming from the incident, the [Wisconsin Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Supreme_Court) ultimately declared the Fugitive Slave Law unconstitutional.[^cite_note-54] The [Republican Party](32070.md), founded on March 20, 1854, by anti-slavery expansion activists in [Ripon, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon,_Wisconsin), grew to dominate state politics in the aftermath of these events.[^cite_note-55]

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), around 91,000 troops from Wisconsin fought for the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)).[^cite_note-56] A number of Wisconsin regiments were distinguished, including three that served in the celebrated "[Iron Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Brigade)"— the [2nd Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Wisconsin_Infantry_Regiment), [6th Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Wisconsin_Infantry_Regiment), and [7th Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Wisconsin_Infantry_Regiment). The [8th Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Wisconsin_Infantry_Regiment), another hard-fighting regiment, was often accompanied into battle by its mascot, [Old Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Abe), a bald eagle.



The [Daniel E. Krause Stone Barn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_E._Krause_Stone_Barn) in [Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase,_Wisconsin) was built in 1903, as dairy farming spread across the state.

Wisconsin's economy also diversified during the early years of statehood. While lead mining diminished, agriculture became a principal occupation in the southern half of the state. Railroads were built across the state to help transport grains to market, and industries like [J.I. Case & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Corporation) in Racine were founded to build agricultural equipment. Wisconsin briefly became one of the nation's leading producers of wheat during the 1860s.[^cite_note-57] Meanwhile, the lumber industry dominated in the heavily forested northern sections of Wisconsin, and sawmills sprang up in cities like [La Crosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse), [Eau Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin), and [Wausau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wausau,_Wisconsin). These economic activities had dire environmental consequences. By the close of the 19th century, intensive agriculture had devastated soil fertility, and lumbering had deforested most of the state.[^cite_note-58] These conditions forced both wheat agriculture and the lumber industry into a precipitous decline.

Beginning in the 1890s, farmers in Wisconsin shifted from wheat to dairy production to make more sustainable and profitable use of their land. Many immigrants carried cheese-making traditions that, combined with the state's suitable geography and dairy research led by [Stephen Babcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Babcock) at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), helped the state build a reputation as "America's Dairyland".[^cite_note-59] Meanwhile, conservationists including [Aldo Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Leopold) helped re-establish the state's forests during the early 20th century,[^cite_note-60] paving the way for a more renewable lumber and [paper milling](417020.md) industry as well as promoting recreational tourism in the northern woodlands. Manufacturing also boomed in Wisconsin during the early 20th century, driven by an immense immigrant workforce arriving from Europe. Industries in cities like Milwaukee ranged from brewing and food processing to heavy machine production and tool-making, leading Wisconsin to rank 8th among U.S. states in total product value by 1910.[^cite_note-61]

### Progressive era and World Wars

See also: [La Folletteism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Folletteism)



[Suffragists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage_in_Wisconsin) campaigning, 1916. Wisconsin was among the earliest states to ratify the [Nineteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution).

[^cite_note-62]

The early 20th century was notable for the emergence of [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) politics championed by [Robert M. La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette), who served as governor from 1901 to 1906 and U.S. senator from 1906 to 1925. Between 1901 and 1914, Progressive Republicans in Wisconsin created the nation's first comprehensive statewide [primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_primary) system,[^cite_note-63] the first effective [workplace injury compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_compensation) law,[^cite_note-64] and the first state [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax),[^cite_note-65] making taxation proportional to actual earnings.

During [World War I](4764461.md), due to the neutrality of Wisconsin and many of its [Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Wisconsin), [Progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States), and [Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States), including [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) and [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_and_Scandinavian_Americans) who were a significant proportion of the state's population, Wisconsin began to be accused of being the "Traitor State" by many "hyper patriots."[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

As the war raged on in Europe, La Follette led the antiwar movement in Wisconsin and steered a group of progressive senators in blocking a bill by President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) that would have armed merchant ships with guns. Many Wisconsin politicians, such as [Emanuel L. Philipp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_L._Philipp) and [Irvine Lenroot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Lenroot), were accused of having divided loyalties.[^cite_note-70] Even with outspoken opponents to the war, at the onset of the war, many Wisconsinites would abandon neutrality. Businesses, labor and farms all enjoyed prosperity from the war. With over 118,000 going into military service, Wisconsin was the first state to report for the national drafts conducted by the [U.S. military](32212.md).[^cite_note-71] As governor, Philipp was successful in combating [anti-German hysteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-German_sentiment) in the state.[^cite_note-72]

The progressive [Wisconsin Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Idea) also promoted the statewide expansion of the University of Wisconsin through the [UW–Extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Extension) system at this time.[^cite_note-73] In 1932, UW economics professors [John R. Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Commons) and Harold Groves helped Wisconsin create the United States' first [unemployment compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_compensation) program.[^cite_note-74] Other [Wisconsin Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Idea) scholars at the university generated the plan that became the New Deal's [Social Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Act) of 1935, with Wisconsin expert [Arthur J. Altmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_J._Altmeyer) playing a key role.[^cite_note-75]



Governor [Philip La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_La_Follette) announces formation of the [National Progressives of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Progressives_of_America), 1938

After La Follette died, his sons [Philip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_La_Follette) and [Robert Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette_Jr.) took over the Wisconsin Republican Party, later founding the [Wisconsin Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Progressive_Party) as a successor to [their father's Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_\(United_States,_1924%E2%80%931927\)). It gained momentum in the mid-1930s with support from President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) and progressive Democrats, winning offices statewide and in Congress. The party declined after Philip, facing scandal and accusations of authoritarianism, [lost reelection in 1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Wisconsin_gubernatorial_election) to [Julius P. Heil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_P._Heil) and left politics to serve in [World War II](32927.md). The [National Progressives of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Progressives_of_America), an organization Philip had hoped would precede a national realignment, then faltered.[^cite_note-\:4-76][^cite_note-77]

The last Progressive candidate elected governor of Wisconsin was [Orland S. Loomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orland_Steen_Loomis), in [1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1942_Wisconsin_gubernatorial_election). He died before taking office. The [Wisconsin Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Supreme_Court) ruled that Republican Lieutenant Governor [Walter S. Goodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Samuel_Goodland) would serve Loomis's term as governor.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] In 1946, the Wisconsin Progressives voted to dissolve and rejoin the Republican Party.[^cite_note-\:4-76]

In World War II's immediate aftermath, Wisconsinites were divided over issues such as the creation of the [United Nations](31769.md), support for the European recovery, and the growth of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)'s power. But when [Europe divided into Communist and capitalist camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain), and with the Progressive Party's collapse, [Robert La Follette Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette_Jr.) rejoined the Republican Party of Wisconsin to run in the 1946 Senate election. He narrowly lost to [Joseph McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy) in the [Republican primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_United_States_Senate_election_in_Wisconsin). After the [Chinese Communist Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Revolution) of 1949, public opinion began to continue move toward support for the protection of democracy and capitalism against Communist expansion.[^cite_note-\:3-80]



In the 20th century, [Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior,_Wisconsin) was a major hub for shipping iron ore across the Great Lakes via [lake freighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_freighter).

Wisconsin took part in several political extremes in the mid- to late 20th century, ranging from the [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism) crusades of Senator McCarthy in the 1950s, to the founding of [Earth Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Day) by environmental advocate [Gaylord Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaylord_Nelson) in 1970. During radical protests against the [Vietnam War](32611.md) at UW-Madison, an attack by anarchists culminated in the [Sterling Hall bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Hall_bombing) in August 1970. The state undertook [welfare reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_reform) under Republican Governor [Tommy Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Thompson) during the 1990s.[^cite_note-81] Its economy also underwent further transformations toward the end of the 20th century, as heavy industry and manufacturing declined in favor of a [service economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy) based on medicine, education, agribusiness, and tourism.

In 2011, Wisconsin became the focus of some controversy when newly elected governor [Scott Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Walker_\(politician\)) proposed and then passed and enacted [2011 Wisconsin Act 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Wisconsin_Act_10), which made large changes in the areas of collective bargaining, compensation, retirement, health insurance, and sick leave of public sector employees, among other changes.[^cite_note-82] A [series of major protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Wisconsin_protests) by union supporters took place that year in protest to the changes, and Walker survived [a recall election held the next year in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_gubernatorial_recall_election), becoming the first governor in U.S. history to do so.[^cite_note-83] Also in 2012, Congressman [Paul Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ryan) became the first Wisconsinite to appear on a major party ticket, as Republican nominee [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney)'s running mate in the [2012 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election). Ryan was elected [Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) in 2015.

Since 2023, [five University of Wisconsin branch campuses have closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closures_of_the_University_of_Wisconsin_branch_campuses), one has gone entirely online, and one has stopped using several of its buildings. Many have expressed concern about the Wisconsin Idea's future since these closures.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Wisconsin)



Wisconsin geographic regions



The [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area) is characterized by bluffs carved in [sedimentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary) rock by water from melting [Ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age) glaciers.



[Sea caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_caves) are located on the shorelines of the [Apostle Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostle_Islands) in [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior).

Wisconsin is in the [Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) and is part of both the [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region) and the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest). The state has a total area of 65,496 square miles (169,630 km<sup>2</sup>). Wisconsin is bordered by [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior) and [Michigan](18859.md) to the north; by [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan) to the east; by [Illinois](14849.md) to the south; and by [Iowa](26810748.md) to the southwest and [Minnesota](19590.md) to the northwest. A border dispute with Michigan was settled by two cases, both [Wisconsin v. Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_v._Michigan), in 1934 and 1935. The state's boundaries include the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and [St. Croix River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix_River_\(Wisconsin%E2%80%93Minnesota\)) in the west, and the [Menominee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee_River) and [Montreal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_River_\(Wisconsin%E2%80%93Michigan\)) in the northeast.[^cite_note-87]

Lying between the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) and the Mississippi River, Wisconsin has a wide variety of geographical features. The state is divided into five distinct regions. In the north, the [Lake Superior Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior_Lowland) occupies a belt of land along Lake Superior. Just to the south, the [Northern Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Highland) has massive mixed hardwood and coniferous forests including the 1,500,000-acre (610,000-hectare) [Chequamegon–Nicolet National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chequamegon%E2%80%93Nicolet_National_Forest), as well as thousands of glacial lakes, and the state's highest point, [Timms Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timms_Hill). In the middle of the state, the [Central Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Plain_\(Wisconsin\)) has some unique [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) formations like the [Dells of the Wisconsin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dells_of_the_Wisconsin_River) in addition to rich farmland. The [Eastern Ridges and Lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ridges_and_Lowlands) region in the southeast is home to many of Wisconsin's largest cities. The ridges include the [Niagara Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Escarpment) that stretches from New York, the [Black River Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_Escarpment_\(Wisconsin\)) and the [Magnesian Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesian_Escarpment).[^cite_note-Martin1965-88][^cite_note-89] In the southwest, the [Western Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Upland) is a rugged landscape with a mix of forest and farmland, including many bluffs on the Mississippi River, and the [Baraboo Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraboo_Range). This region is part of the [Driftless Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driftless_Area), which also includes parts of Iowa, Illinois, and Minnesota. Overall, 46% of Wisconsin's land area is covered by forest.

Wisconsin has geologic formations and deposits that vary in age from over three billion years to several thousand years, with most rocks being millions of years old.[^cite_note-90] The oldest geologic formations were created over 600 million years ago during the [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian), the majority below the glacial deposits. Much of the Baraboo Range consists of [Baraboo Quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraboo_Quartzite) and other Precambrian [metamorphic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock).[^cite_note-Hanson-91][^cite_note-92] This area was not covered by [glaciers](12463.md) during the most recent ice age, the [Wisconsin Glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Glaciation). [Langlade County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlade_County) has a soil rarely found outside the county called [Antigo silt loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigo_\(soil\)).[^cite_note-93]

The state has more than 12,000 named rivers and streams, totaling 84,000 miles (135,000 km) in length.[^cite_note-94] It has over 15,000 named lakes, totaling about 1 million acres (4,000 km<sup>2</sup>). [Lake Winnebago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnebago) is the largest inland lake, with over 137,700 acres (557 km<sup>2</sup>), and 88 miles (142 km) of shoreline. Along the two Great Lakes, Wisconsin has over 800 miles (1,300 km) of shoreline (over 500 miles (800 km), not counting minor islands and indentations).[^cite_note-Martin_(1916)_p._21-95][^cite_note-96] Many of the [named islands in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Wisconsin) are in the Great Lakes; many surround the [Door Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Door_Peninsula) in Lake Michigan or are part of the [Apostle Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostle_Islands) in Lake Superior.[^cite_note-97] The Mississippi River and inland lakes and rivers contain the rest of Wisconsin's islands.

Areas under the protection of the [National Park Service](161535.md) include the [Apostle Islands National Lakeshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostle_Islands_National_Lakeshore), [Ice Age National Scenic Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Age_Trail), and portions of the [Saint Croix National Scenic Riverway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Croix_National_Scenic_Riverway) and [North Country National Scenic Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Country_Trail).[^cite_note-98] There are an additional 18 [National Natural Landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Natural_Landmarks_in_Wisconsin) in the state that include dune and swales, swamps, bogs, and old-growth forests. Wisconsin has [50 state park units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_state_parks), covering more than 60,570 acres (245.1 km<sup>2</sup>) in state parks and state recreation areas maintained by the [Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Department_of_Natural_Resources). The Division of Forestry manages a further 471,329 acres (1,907.40 km<sup>2</sup>) in [Wisconsin's state forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_state_forests).[^cite_note-99]

### Climate

Further information: [Climate change in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Wisconsin)



Köppen climate types of Wisconsin

Most of Wisconsin is classified as warm-summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb*), while southern and southwestern portions are classified as hot-summer humid continental climate (Köppen *Dfa*). The highest temperature ever recorded in the state was in the Wisconsin Dells, on July 13, 1936, where it reached 114 °F (46 °C). The lowest temperature ever recorded in Wisconsin was in the village of [Couderay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couderay), where it reached −55 °F (−48 °C) on both February 2 and 4, 1996. Wisconsin also receives a large amount of regular snowfall averaging around 40 inches (100 cm) in the southern portions with up to 160 inches (410 cm) annually in the Lake Superior [snowbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowbelt) each year.[^cite_note-uwexclimate-100]

### Cities

Further information: [List of municipalities in Wisconsin by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Wisconsin_by_population) and [Administrative divisions of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Wisconsin)



Map of the [administrative divisions of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Wisconsin)

Wisconsin has three types of municipality: cities, villages, and towns. [Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_\(Wisconsin\)) and [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_\(Wisconsin\)) are incorporated urban areas. [Towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_\(Wisconsin\)) are unincorporated [minor civil divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_civil_division) of [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_subdivisions_of_Wisconsin#County) with limited self-government.

Over two-thirds of Wisconsin residents live in urban areas.[^cite_note-wi2000-102] [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), in southeastern Wisconsin, is the state's most populous city, with approximately 580,000 people. The [Milwaukee metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_metropolitan_area) accounts for 1.57 million of the state's residents. With a population of nearly 280,000, the state capital of [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin) is consistently ranked as one of the most livable cities in both the state and country and is the [fastest-growing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) city in Wisconsin.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] The [Madison metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_metropolitan_area) in southern Wisconsin has about 680,000 residents.

Medium-size cities dot the state. The largest of these is [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin) in northeastern Wisconsin, with approximately 320,000 people in the metro area. Other metropolitan cities in the state include [Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton,_Wisconsin), [Racine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racine,_Wisconsin), [Oshkosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh,_Wisconsin), [Eau Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin), [Janesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janesville,_Wisconsin), [Wausau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wausau,_Wisconsin), [La Crosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse,_Wisconsin), [Sheboygan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheboygan,_Wisconsin), and [Fond du Lac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fond_du_Lac,_Wisconsin). Furthermore, another 12 cities function as centers of [micropolitan statistical areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micropolitan_statistical_areas) which typically anchor a network of working farms surrounding them. As of 2011, there were 12 cities in Wisconsin with a population of 50,000 or more, accounting for 73% of the state's employment.[^cite_note-105]

In 2022, the five largest European ancestry groups were: [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) (36%), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) (10.2%), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_American) (7.9%), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) (6.7%), and [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_American) (6.3%).[^cite_note-122] German is the most common ancestry in every county in the state, except Menominee, Trempealeau, and Vernon.[^cite_note-123] Wisconsin has the highest percentage of residents of Polish ancestry of any state.[^cite_note-census.gov-124] According to the 2022 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), 7.6% of Wisconsin's population were of [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) origin (of any race). The largest Hispanic ancestry groups were [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American) (5.1%), [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans) (1.1%), [Central American](6121.md) (0.4%), and [Cuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_American) (0.1%), with 0.9% reporting other Hispanic or Latino origins.[^cite_note-125]

Since its founding, Wisconsin has been ethnically heterogeneous. Following the period of French fur traders, the next wave of settlers were miners, many of whom were [Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_American), who settled the southwestern area of the state. The next wave was dominated by "Yankees", migrants of [English descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) from [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) and [upstate New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_New_York); in the early years of statehood, they dominated the state's heavy industry, finance, politics, and education. Between 1850 and 1900, the immigrants were mostly [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans), [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Americans) (the largest group being [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_American)), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans), and [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans). In the 20th century, a number of African Americans and [Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) settled in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee); and after the end of the [Vietnam War](32611.md) came an influx of [Hmongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_Americans).

The various ethnic groups settled in different areas of the state. Although German immigrants settled throughout the state, the largest concentration was in Milwaukee. Norwegian immigrants settled in lumbering and farming areas in the north and west. Irish, Italian, and Polish immigrants settled primarily in urban areas.[^cite_note-126] [Menominee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee_County,_Wisconsin) is the only county in the eastern United States with a Native American majority.

African Americans came to Milwaukee, especially from 1940 on. 86% of Wisconsin's African-American population live in four cities: [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), [Racine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racine,_Wisconsin), [Beloit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloit,_Wisconsin), [Kenosha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenosha), with Milwaukee home to nearly three-fourths of the state's black Americans. In the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) region, only Detroit and [Cleveland](5951.md) have a higher percentage of African-American residents.[^cite_note-127]

About 33% of Wisconsin's Asian population is [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people), with significant communities in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), [Wausau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wausau,_Wisconsin), [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin), [Sheboygan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheboygan,_Wisconsin), [Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton,_Wisconsin), [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin), [La Crosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse), [Eau Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin), [Oshkosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh,_Wisconsin), and [Manitowoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitowoc,_Wisconsin).[^cite_note-128] 61,629 people in Wisconsin, or around 1% of the population, identify as Hmong.[^cite_note-129]

Of the residents of Wisconsin, 71.7% were born in Wisconsin, 23.0% were born in a different US state, 0.7% were born in Puerto Rico, U.S. Island areas, or born abroad to American parent(s), and 4.6% were foreign born.[^cite_note-130]

In 2018, the countries of origin for Wisconsin's immigrants came from Mexico, India, China, [Laos](17752.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-131]

### Crime

Main article: [Crime in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Wisconsin)

Statewide FBI Crime statistics for 2009 include 144 murders/non-negligent manslaughter; 1,108 rapes; 4,850 robberies; 8,431 aggravated assaults; and 147,486 property crimes.[^cite_note-146] Wisconsin also publishes its own statistics through the Bureau of Justice Information and Analysis.[^cite_note-147] The state reported 14,603 violent crimes in 2009, with a clearance rate (% solved) of 50%.[^cite_note-148] The state reported 4,633 sexual assaults in 2009, with an overall clearance rate for sexual assaults of 57%.

## Economy

See also: [Wisconsin locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_locations_by_per_capita_income) and [List of Wisconsin companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_companies)

In 2025, Wisconsin's [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) was \$473 billion and its [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income_in_the_United_States) was \$70,570.[^cite_note-149] Wisconsin's economy is driven by manufacturing, agriculture, and tourism. As of May 2025, the state's unemployment rate was 3.3%.[^cite_note-150] Since 2009, Wisconsin's [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage) has been \$7.25, the same as the federal rate.[^cite_note-151] In 2025, 99.4% of businesses in Wisconsin were [small businesses](276495.md) and they employed 48.0% of the state's workforce.[^cite_note-152]

Eight corporations based in Wisconsin are listed on the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500). In 2024, the list included [Northwestern Mutual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Mutual), [Fiserv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiserv), [ManpowerGroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ManpowerGroup), [Rockwell Automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_Automation), and [WEC Energy Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WEC_Energy_Group) (all based in Milwaukee), as well as [Kohl's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohl's) (based in [Menomonee Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menomonee_Falls,_Wisconsin)), [American Family Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Family_Insurance) (based in Madison), and [Oshkosh Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh_Corporation) (based in [Oshkosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh,_Wisconsin)).[^cite_note-153]

### Manufacturing

See also: [Manufacturing companies based in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Manufacturing_companies_based_in_Wisconsin)



The [U.S. Bank Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bank_Center_\(Milwaukee\)) in [downtown Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Milwaukee) is home to the main headquarters of [Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baird_\(investment_bank\)), [Foley & Lardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foley_&_Lardner), and [Sensient Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensient_Technologies), as well as regional headquarters for [U.S. Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bank) and [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM).

As of 2015, the number of manufacturing jobs in the state was approximately 500,000, similar to the figure in 1970. However, it declined as a share of the state's gross state product between 2000 and 2016 from about 21.5% to 18.5%, a proportion that is eighth among all states.[^cite_note-WisEconomy-154] Similarly, as a share of total employment, manufacturing declined from 28% in 1970 to 14% in 2015. The state's economic output from manufacturing was \$48.9 billion in 2008, making it the tenth largest among states in manufacturing gross domestic product.[^cite_note-155]

Major Wisconsin companies in manufacturing include the [Kohler Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohler_Company); [Mercury Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Marine); [Rockwell Automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_Automation); [Johnson Controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Controls); [John Deere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere); [Briggs & Stratton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briggs_&_Stratton); [Milwaukee Electric Tool Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Electric_Tool_Company); [Miller Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Electric); [Caterpillar Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar_Inc.); [Joy Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Global); [Oshkosh Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh_Corporation); [Harley-Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson); [Case IH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_IH); [S. C. Johnson & Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._C._Johnson_&_Son); [Ashley Furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Furniture); [Ariens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariens); and [Evinrude Outboard Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evinrude_Outboard_Motors).

Wisconsin has a significant lumber industry, being a major producer of paper and packaging. Wisconsin ranks first nationwide in the production of paper products; the lower Fox River from [Lake Winnebago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnebago) to [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_\(Lake_Michigan\)) has 24 [paper mills](417020.md) along its 39 miles (63 km) stretch.[^cite_note-156]

A large part of the state's manufacturing sector includes commercial food processing, including well-known brands such as [Oscar Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Mayer), [Tombstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombstone_\(pizza\)) frozen pizza, [Johnsonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnsonville_Foods) [brats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratwurst), and [Usinger's sausage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Usinger). [Kraft Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_Foods) alone employs more than 5,000 people in the state. Milwaukee is a major producer of beer and was formerly headquarters for [Miller Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Brewing_Company)—the nation's second-largest brewer—until it merged with Coors. Formerly, [Schlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Schlitz_Brewing_Company), [Blatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Blatz_Brewing_Company), and [Pabst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabst_Brewing_Company) were cornerstone breweries in Milwaukee.

The development and manufacture of healthcare devices and software is a growing sector of the state's economy, with key players such as [GE Healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Healthcare), [Epic Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Systems), and [TomoTherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TomoTherapy).

### Agriculture

Main article: [Agriculture in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Wisconsin)

See also: [Wisconsin dairy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_dairy_industry) and [Wisconsin cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_cheese)



Dairy cows at a [Wisconsin dairy farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_dairy_industry)

Wisconsin produces about a quarter of America's cheese, leading the nation in cheese production.[^cite_note-USDA-157] It is second in milk production, after [California](5407.md),[^cite_note-158] and third in per-capita milk production, behind California and [Vermont](32578.md).[^cite_note-159] Wisconsin is second in butter production, producing about one-quarter of the nation's butter.[^cite_note-160] Wisconsin requires cheese and butter makers to hold a license for production, being the only state in the US to require certification for either.[^cite_note-161]

The state ranks first nationally in the production of corn for [silage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silage), [cranberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranberries),[^cite_note-USDA_Cran-162] [ginseng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginseng),[^cite_note-USDA_Gin-163] and [snap beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_beans) for processing. It grows more than half the national crop of cranberries.[^cite_note-USDA_Cran-162] and 97% of the nation's ginseng.[^cite_note-USDA_Gin-163] Wisconsin is also a leading producer of [oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat), potatoes, carrots, tart [cherries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherries), [maple syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_syrup), and [sweet corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_corn) for processing.

The significance of the state's agricultural production is exemplified by the depiction of a Holstein cow, an ear of corn, and a wheel of cheese on Wisconsin's [state quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_quarter) design.[^cite_note-164] The state annually selects an "[Alice in Dairyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_Dairyland)" to promote the state's agricultural products around the world.[^cite_note-165] The prominence of the dairy industry in Wisconsin has led to Wisconsin being known as "America's Dairyland",[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-Urdang-2] which was made the official state slogan in 1940.[^cite_note-167]

### Tourism

Further information: [Economy of Door County, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Door_County,_Wisconsin)



The [Dells of the Wisconsin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dells_of_the_Wisconsin_River) form a tourism region focused on river features and nearby [theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park).

According to the Department of Tourism, tourism in Wisconsin generated \$20.9 billion in total economic impact in 2021 and is the state's third-largest sector.[^cite_note-witour-168] Festivals such as [Summerfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerfest) and the [EAA AirVenture Oshkosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAA_AirVenture_Oshkosh) draw international attention, along with hundreds of thousands of visitors annually.[^cite_note-169] Notable attractions across the state include the [Harley-Davidson Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson_Museum), [Lambeau Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambeau_Field), [Milwaukee Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Art_Museum), [Chazen Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chazen_Museum_of_Art), [Henry Vilas Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Vilas_Zoo), [National Railroad Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Railroad_Museum), and [Wisconsin State Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Capital).[^cite_note-170] Other tourist destinations include [Taliesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliesin_\(studio\)), the studio of architect [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright); [House on the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_on_the_Rock), a complex of architecturally distinct rooms; and the [Circus World Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_World_Museum), located in the [Ringling brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringling_brothers)' hometown of [Baraboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraboo,_Wisconsin).

The [Dells of the Wisconsin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dells_of_the_Wisconsin_River), a gorge noted for its rock formations in south-central Wisconsin, annually attracts more than four million visitors between water tours of the scenery and numerous [theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park) in the region.[^cite_note-witour-168] Nearby [Devil's Lake State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Lake_State_Park_\(Wisconsin\)) is one of the most visited of [Wisconsin's state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_state_parks) due to its proximity to the Dells and its own scenery.

The [Door Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Door_Peninsula), which extends off the eastern coast of the state, contains [Door County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Door_County,_Wisconsin), a popular destination for boaters due to the large number of natural harbors and boat launches on both the Green Bay and Lake Michigan sides of the peninsula. The area draws more than two million visitors yearly to its quaint villages, seasonal cherry picking, and [fish boils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_boil).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

Given the large number of lakes and rivers in the state, water recreation is popular. In the Northwoods Lake Country, what had been an industrial area focused on timber has largely been transformed into a vacation destination. Popular interest in the environment and environmentalism, added to traditional interests in hunting and fishing, has attracted a large urban audience within driving range.[^cite_note-173] [Lake Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Geneva,_Wisconsin) in southeastern Wisconsin is similarly popular for water recreation.

### Energy

See also: [Focus on Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_on_Energy), [Wind power in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Wisconsin), and [Solar power in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_in_Wisconsin)



[Wisconsin power grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Wisconsin)

Wisconsin has no production of oil, gas, or coal.[^cite_note-eia-sedb-174] Its in-state electrical generation is mostly from coal. Other important electricity sources are natural gas and nuclear.[^cite_note-eia-sedb-174]

The state has a mandate that ten percent of its electrical energy come from renewable sources by the end of 2015.[^cite_note-175] This goal has been met, but not with in-state sources. As of 2014, a third of that ten percent comes from out-of-state sources, mostly wind-generated electricity from Minnesota and Iowa. The state has agnostic policies for developing wind power in state.[^cite_note-176]

### Taxation

Main articles: [Sales taxes in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_taxes_in_the_United_States), [Property tax in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax_in_the_United_States), and [State income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_income_tax)



Wisconsin Budget (2021)

Wisconsin collects personal [income taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) which range from 4% to 7.65% based on five [income brackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_bracket). The state [sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) and [use tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_tax) rate is 5.0%. Fifty-nine counties have an additional sales/use tax of 0.5%.[^cite_note-177]

The most common property tax assessed on Wisconsin residents is the real [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax), or their residential property tax. Wisconsin does not impose a property tax on vehicles, but does levy an annual registration fee. Property taxes are the most important tax revenue source for Wisconsin's local governments, as well as major methods of funding school districts, vocational-technical colleges, special purpose districts and tax incremental finance districts. Equalized values are based on the full market value of all taxable property in the state, except for agricultural land. To provide property tax relief for farmers, the value of agricultural land is determined by its value for agricultural uses, rather than for its possible development value. Equalized values are used to distribute state aid payments to counties, municipalities, and technical colleges. Assessments prepared by local assessors are used to distribute the property tax burden within individual municipalities.

Wisconsin does not assess a tax on [intangible property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_property). Wisconsin does not collect [inheritance taxes](5824627.md). Until January 1, 2008, Wisconsin's [estate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_tax) was decoupled from the federal estate tax laws; therefore the state imposed its own estate tax on certain large estates.[^cite_note-178]

There are no [toll roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road) in Wisconsin; highway construction and maintenance are funded in part by [motor fuel tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_fuel_tax) revenues, and the remaining balance is drawn from the State General Fund. Non-highway road construction and maintenance are funded by local governments (municipalities or counties).

## Culture



[Cheesehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheesehead) hats at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics)

See also: [History of cheesemaking in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cheesemaking_in_Wisconsin)

Residents of Wisconsin are called Wisconsinites. The traditional prominence of references to [dairy farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming) and [cheesemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheesemaking) in Wisconsin's rural economy (the state's [license plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/License_plates) have read "America's Dairyland" since 1940)[^cite_note-179] have led to the nickname (sometimes used pejoratively among non-residents) of "[cheeseheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheesehead)", and to the creation of "cheesehead hats" made of yellow foam in the shape of a wedge of cheese.

Numerous [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic) festivals are held throughout Wisconsin to celebrate the heritage of its citizens. Such festivals include [Summerfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerfest), [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest_celebrations), [Polish Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Fest), [Festa Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festa_Italiana), [Irish Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Fest), Bastille Days, [Syttende Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syttende_Mai) (Norwegian Constitution Day), Brat(wurst) Days in [Sheboygan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheboygan,_Wisconsin), [Polka Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulaski,_Wisconsin#Pulaski_Polka_Days), Cheese Days in [Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_Wisconsin) and [Mequon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mequon), African World Festival, Indian Summer, Arab Fest, Wisconsin Highland Games, and many others.[^cite_note-180]

### Architecture



[Taliesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliesin_\(studio\)) was the studio of architect [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright).

With the immigration of [northern Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_and_Scandinavian_Americans) into Wisconsin and the [upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest), they brought the techniques of building [log homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_house) with them.[^cite_note-181]

The [Milwaukee Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Art_Museum) is known for its Quadracci Pavilion created by Spanish architect [Santiago Calatrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Calatrava). The Quadracci Pavilion contains a movable, wing-like Burke *[brise soleil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brise_soleil)* that opens up for a wingspan of 217 feet (66 m) during the day, folding over the tall, arched structure at night or during inclement weather.[^cite_note-182]

[Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright), an architect known as the pioneer of [Prairie School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_School) architecture and the [Usonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usonia) home concept, was raised in Wisconsin. Wright's home and studio in the 20th century was at [Taliesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliesin_\(studio\)), south of [Spring Green, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Green,_Wisconsin). Taliesin and the Usonian [Jacobs I House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_and_Katherine_Jacobs_First_House) in Madison are listed as [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) as part of "[The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_20th-Century_Architecture_of_Frank_Lloyd_Wright)".[^cite_note-whs-25] Other notable works of Wright in Wisconsin include the [Annunciation Greek Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation_Greek_Orthodox_Church) in Wauwatosa, [First Unitarian Society of Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Unitarian_Society_of_Madison), and [Johnson Wax Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Wax_Headquarters). [Monona Terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monona_Terrace) in Madison, a convention center designed by Taliesin architect Anthony Puttnam, is based on a 1930s design by Wright.[^cite_note-183]

### Alcohol



The [Miller Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Brewing_Company) in Milwaukee

Drinking alcohol has long been considered a significant part of Wisconsin culture, and the state ranks at or near the top of national measures of per-capita alcohol consumption, consumption of alcohol per state, and proportion of drinkers. Consumption per-capita per-event, however, ranks low among the nation; number of events (number of times alcohol is involved) is significantly higher or highest, but consumption at each event smaller, marking Wisconsin's consumption as frequent and moderate.[^cite_note-184] Factors such as cultural identification with the state's heritage of German immigration, the long-standing presence of major breweries in Milwaukee, and a cold climate are often associated with the prevalence of drinking in Wisconsin.

Many large breweries were founded in Wisconsin, largely in Milwaukee, which gained the epithet "Brew City" before the turn of the century. [Miller Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Brewing_Company), [Pabst Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabst_Brewing_Company), [Joseph Schlitz Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Schlitz_Brewing_Company), and [Jacob Leinenkugel Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Leinenkugel_Brewing_Company) all began as local businesses before entering national markets. Several other popular craft brews include Ale Asylum,[^cite_note-185] [Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Brewery), [Sprecher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprecher_Brewery), and [New Glarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Glarus_Brewing_Company), the latter being well known for the Spotted Cow Farmhouse Ale only sold in Wisconsin.[^cite_note-186]

In Wisconsin, the legal drinking age is 21, except when accompanied by a parent, guardian, or spouse who is at least 21 years old. Age requirements are waived for possessing alcohol when employed by a brewer, brewpub, wholesaler, or producer of alcohol fuel. The minimum legal age to purchase alcohol is 21, with no exceptions.[^cite_note-187] The Absolute Sobriety law states that any person not of legal drinking age (currently 21) may not drive after consuming alcohol.[^cite_note-188] DUI offenses were lowered to BAC 0.08 in 2003 as a result of federal government pressure.[^cite_note-189]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Cuisine of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Wisconsin)



Fried [cheese curds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_curd)

Wisconsin's cuisine is famous for [its cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_cheese) as well as other dairy products. [Colby cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby_cheese) was first created in Wisconsin in 1885 (named after [Colby, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby,_Wisconsin)), and [brick cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_cheese) was first created in the state in 1877.[^cite_note-norton2009-190][^cite_note-191] The state is the only producer of [Limburger cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburger_cheese) in the United States. [Cheese curds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_curd) are a popular variant that can be eaten separately cold as a snack, or covered in batter and fried as an appetizer, often served with [ranch dressing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranch_dressing) as a dipping sauce. [Hot and spicy cheese bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_and_spicy_cheese_bread) is a popular type of bread created and sold in Madison.

Wisconsin is the nation's top producer of [cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese), the state's official dairy product. It is also a leading producer of [cranberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranberries), the state's official fruit, and [ginseng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginseng).[^cite_note-192] [Dane County Farmers' Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_County_Farmers'_Market) in Madison is the nation's largest producers-only farmer's market.[^cite_note-BadgerHeraldReidy-193][^cite_note-WaPoWax-194]

The Friday night [fish fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_fry), often battered and fried [perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perch) or [walleye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walleye), is traditional throughout Wisconsin, while in northeast Wisconsin and [Door County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Door_County,_Wisconsin) the [fish boil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_boil) is more popular. The [supper club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supper_club) is another common phenomenon of Wisconsin culinary heritage and often a destination for fish frys; other fried food are common side dishes, along with condiments of tartar sauce and [cole slaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_slaw) or crimson slaw, a variety of cole slaw that incorporates Wisconsin cranberries.[^cite_note-195] [Beer cheese soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_soup) is usually made from a variety of beer and cheddar or Colby cheese with sausage, potatoes, and green onions.

[Booyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booyah_\(stew\)) is a [stew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew) common to Wisconsin, commonly using meat and a mirepoix of vegetables cooked together in a "booyah kettle" over low heat for several days.[^cite_note-196]

The southeastern city of [Racine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racine,_Wisconsin) is known for its [kringle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kringle), a sweet flaky pastry often served as a dessert. The recipe was brought by [Danish immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Americans) to the region in the 1800s and became the official state pastry of Wisconsin in 2013.[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] The [Wisconsin State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Fair) is known for its giant [cream puffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream_puff).[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

The [butter burger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_burger) originated in Wisconsin, most likely in [Solly's Grille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solly's_Grille) in [Glendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_Wisconsin).[^cite_note-201] [Culver's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culver's) is a midwestern [fast casual food restaurant chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_casual_restaurant) originally from [Sauk City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_City,_Wisconsin) and currently headquartered in [Prairie du Sac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_du_Sac,_Wisconsin) known for serving butter burgers, fried cheese curds, and frozen custard. [La Croix Sparkling Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Croix_Sparkling_Water) originates from [La Crosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse,_Wisconsin).

### Music

Main article: [Music of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Wisconsin)

Milwaukee hosts [Summerfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerfest), dubbed "The World's Largest Music Festival", every year[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203] The festival is held at the lakefront [Henry Maier Festival Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Maier_Festival_Park) just south of downtown, as are a summer-long array of [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic) musical festivals. The [Wisconsin Area Music Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Area_Music_Industry) hosts an annual awards show for top Wisconsin artists.[^cite_note-204] The [Mile of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_of_Music) in [Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton,_Wisconsin) is an annual all-original music festival of varying styles. It focuses on undiscovered touring musicians and bands.[^cite_note-205] Other music festivals include [Hoofbeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoofbeat_\(festival\)) country fest, Country Jam USA, the [Hodag Country Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodag_Country_Festival), [Lifest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifest), Porterfield Country Music Festival, Country Thunder USA in Twin Lakes,[^cite_note-Country_Music_Chicago-206] and Country USA.

### Recreation

See also: [List of Wisconsin amusement parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_amusement_parks)

The varied landscape of Wisconsin makes the state a popular vacation destination for outdoor recreation. Winter events include skiing, ice fishing and [snowmobile derbies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championship_Snowmobile_Derby). Wisconsin is situated on two Great Lakes and has many inland lakes of varied size; the state contains 11,188 square miles (28,980 km<sup>2</sup>) of water, more than all but three other states—[Alaska](624.md), Michigan, and Florida.[^cite_note-207] The [Wisconsin Shipwreck Coast National Marine Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Shipwreck_Coast_National_Marine_Sanctuary) was established in 2021 in the waters of Lake Michigan off Wisconsin and is the site of a large number of historically significant [shipwrecks](272778.md).[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

Outdoor activities are popular in Wisconsin, especially hunting and fishing. One of the most prevalent game animals is the [whitetail deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitetail_deer). Each year in Wisconsin, well over 600,000 deer-hunting licenses are sold.[^cite_note-wdnr2008-211] In 2008, the [Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Department_of_Natural_Resources) projected the pre-hunt deer population to be between 1.5 and 1.7 million.

### Sports

Main article: [Sports in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Wisconsin)



[Lambeau Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambeau_Field) is home to the publicly owned [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) of the [National Football League](21211.md).



[American Family Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Family_Field) is home to the [Milwaukee Brewers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Brewers), one of two professional sports teams based in Milwaukee.



[Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America), near [Elkhart Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elkhart_Lake,_Wisconsin), has hosted [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport) races since the 1950s.

Wisconsin is represented by major league teams in three sports: football, baseball, and basketball. [Lambeau Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambeau_Field), located in [Green Bay, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin), is home to the [National Football League](21211.md)'s [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers). The Packers have been part of the NFL since the league's second season in 1921 and are the smallest city franchise in the NFL, and the only one owned by shareholders statewide.[^cite_note-212] The [Milwaukee Brewers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Brewers), the state's only major league baseball team, have played in [American Family Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Family_Field) in Milwaukee since 2001. Before the Brewers, Milwaukee had two prior Major League teams. The first team, also called the Brewers, played only one season in 1901 before becoming the [St. Louis Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Browns). Milwaukee was also the home of the now-[Atlanta Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Braves) franchise when they moved from Boston from 1953 to 1965, winning the [World Series](23489161.md) in 1957.[^cite_note-213] The [Milwaukee Bucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) play home games at the [Fiserv Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiserv_Forum).[^cite_note-214]

The state also has minor league teams in hockey ([Milwaukee Admirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Admirals)) and baseball (the [Wisconsin Timber Rattlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Timber_Rattlers), based in [Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton,_Wisconsin) and the [Beloit Sky Carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloit_Sky_Carp) of the [High-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-A) minor leagues). In addition to these affiliated minor league teams, Wisconsin has two [American Association of Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Professional_Baseball) teams, being the [Milwaukee Milkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Milkmen) based in [Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin,_Milwaukee_County,_Wisconsin),[^cite_note-215] and the [Lake Country DockHounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Country_DockHounds) based in [Oconomowoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oconomowoc,_Wisconsin).[^cite_note-216] Wisconsin is also home to nine [Northwoods League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwoods_League) teams. The [Madison Mallards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Mallards), the [La Crosse Loggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse_Loggers), the [Lakeshore Chinooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeshore_Chinooks), the [Eau Claire Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire_Express), the [Fond du Lac Dock Spiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fond_du_Lac_Dock_Spiders), the [Green Bay Rockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Rockers), the [Kenosha Kingfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenosha_Kingfish), the [Wausau Woodchucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wausau_Woodchucks), and the [Wisconsin Rapids Rafters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Rapids_Rafters) all play in a collegiate all-star summer league. In addition to the Packers, Green Bay is also the home to an [indoor football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_American_football) team, the [Green Bay Blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Blizzard) of the [IFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_Football_League). The state is home to the seven-time MISL/MASL Champion [Milwaukee Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Wave).[^cite_note-217] Wisconsin is also home to [Forward Madison FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Madison_FC), which is a professional soccer team that plays in the [USL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_League_One). The Northern Elite Football League consists of many amateur semi-pro teams from Wisconsin.

Wisconsin also has many [college sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_sports) programs, including the [NCAA Division I](669931.md) [Wisconsin Badgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Badgers), [Milwaukee Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Panthers), and [Green Bay Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Phoenix). The [Marquette Golden Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquette_Golden_Eagles) of the [Big East Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_East_Conference_\(1979%E2%80%932013\)), the state's other major collegiate program, is known for its [men's basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquette_Golden_Eagles_men's_basketball). Many other schools in the University of Wisconsin system compete in the [Wisconsin Intercollegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Intercollegiate_Athletic_Conference) at the [Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_III_\(NCAA\)) level. The conference is one of the most successful in the nation, claiming 107 NCAA national championships in 15 different sports as of March 30, 2015.[^cite_note-218]

The [Milwaukee Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Mile), an oval track opened in 1903, is the oldest operating motorsports venue in the world, having hosted the [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) and [NASCAR](21114.md).[^cite_note-219] [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America) near [Elkhart Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elkhart_Lake,_Wisconsin) hosts races in the [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series), [IMSA SportsCar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSA_SportsCar_Championship), [Sports Car Club of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Car_Club_of_America) [GT World Challenge America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GT_World_Challenge_America) and [Trans-Am Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Am_Series) and the [MotoAmerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MotoAmerica) Superbike Championship. [Dirt tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirt_track_racing) are also popular, with the [World of Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Outlaws) and [High Limit Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Limit_Racing) both going to different tracks.

The [World Championship Snowmobile Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championship_Snowmobile_Derby) is held at [Eagle River, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_River,_Wisconsin). The world championship [off-road racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-road_racing) event is held at [Crandon International Off-Road Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crandon_International_Off-Road_Raceway).

Wisconsin is home to the nation's oldest operating [velodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velodrome) in [Kenosha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenosha) where races have been held every year since 1927.[^cite_note-220]

[Sheboygan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheboygan,_Wisconsin) is home to [Whistling Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistling_Straits) golf club which has hosted [PGA Championships](224616.md) in 2004, 2010 and 2015 and the [Ryder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryder_Cup) golf competition between USA and Europe in 2020.[^cite_note-221] The [Greater Milwaukee Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Milwaukee_Open), later named the [U.S. Bank Championship in Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bank_Championship_in_Milwaukee), was a PGA Tour tournament from 1968 to 2009 held annually in [Brown Deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_Deer). In 2017, [Erin Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Hills), a golf course in [Erin, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin,_Wisconsin), approximately 30 miles northwest of Milwaukee, hosted the [U.S. Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_\(golf\)).[^cite_note-ctmchges-222]

## Government and politics



The [Wisconsin State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Capitol) is located on the [isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus) between [Lake Mendota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mendota) and [Lake Monona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Monona), in the city of [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin).

The [Constitution of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Wisconsin) outlines the structure and function of state government, which is organized into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial. The [Wisconsin Blue Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Blue_Book) is the primary published reference about the government and politics of the state. Re-published every two years, copies are available from state legislators.[^cite_note-223] The law of the [Menominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee) also applies within the [Menominee Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee_Indian_Reservation).

### State government

See also: [List of Wisconsin state agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_state_agencies)

The executive branch is headed by the [Governor of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Wisconsin). The current governor, [Tony Evers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Evers), assumed office on January 7, 2019. The Wisconsin Constitution grants the governor a veto on bills passed by the state legislature as well as a [line-item veto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-item_veto) on appropriation bills. A [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Wisconsin) succeeds the governor in the event of any removal from office and performs any duties assigned by the governor. The current lieutenant governor is [Sara Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Rodriguez). The other elected constitutional offices in the executive branch are the [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Wisconsin) ([Sarah Godlewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Godlewski)), [treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Treasurer_of_Wisconsin) ([John Leiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Leiber)), [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Wisconsin) ([Josh Kaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Kaul)), and the non-partisan [superintendent of public instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Public_Instruction_of_Wisconsin) ([Jill Underly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Underly)).[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225]



The [Senate Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Senate) of the Wisconsin State Capitol

The [Wisconsin State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Legislature) is Wisconsin's legislative branch. The Legislature is a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) body consisting of the [Wisconsin State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Assembly) and the [Wisconsin State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Senate). The Assembly has 99 members, and the Senate has 33 members. All 99 members of the Wisconsin Assembly are elected in a two-year term cycle without [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits_in_the_United_States). Similarly, all 33 members of the Wisconsin Senate are elected in a four-year cycle, also without term limits.[^cite_note-legislative-226] Half of the Senate is elected every two years. Members of both houses of the Legislature vote within their ranks to select presiding officers, such as the [Speaker of the Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_\(politics\)) and the [President of the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Senate). Legislators in both the Senate and the Assembly receive an annual salary of \$55,141. Over two years, each legislator is allotted \$66,008 to cover general office expenses, printing, postage and district mailings.[^cite_note-227]

Wisconsin's court system has four levels: municipal courts, circuit courts, the Court of Appeals, and the Supreme Court. Municipal courts typically handle cases involving [local ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance) matters. The [circuit courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Circuit_Court) are Wisconsin's [trial courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_courts), they have [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) in all civil and criminal cases within the state. Challenges to circuit court rulings are heard by the [Wisconsin Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Court_of_Appeals), consisting of sixteen judges who typically sit in three-judge panels. As the state's highest appellate court, the [Wisconsin Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Supreme_Court) may hear both appeals from lower courts and original actions. In addition to deciding cases, the Supreme Court is responsible for administering the state's court system and regulating the practice of law in Wisconsin.[^cite_note-Wisconsin_Court_System-228]

### Federal representation

Main articles: [Wisconsin's congressional delegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin's_congressional_delegations) and [Wisconsin's congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin's_congressional_districts)

Following the 2020 census [reapportionment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_congressional_apportionment), Wisconsin has eight seats in the U.S. House of Representatives. As of the [119th United States Congress](70645797.md), six federal representatives are Republicans while two are Democrats. [Gwen Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Moore) is the most senior member of the Wisconsin delegation to the U.S. House of Representatives, while the least senior is [Tony Wied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Wied). Wisconsin's [senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seniority_in_the_United_States_Senate) [U.S. senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senator), [Ron Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Johnson), is a Republican, while its junior, [Tammy Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_Baldwin), is a Democrat.[^cite_note-229]

Wisconsin is under the [appellate jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_jurisdiction) of the [United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Seventh_Circuit), which includes district courts for the [Eastern District of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Wisconsin) and [Western District of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Wisconsin).

Since 2016, Wisconsin has been considered a [swing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state) at the federal level, being won by either the [Democratic](5043544.md) or [Republican](32070.md) nominees.[^cite_note-230] Republican [Donald Trump](4848272.md) won the state by 0.87% in the [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Wisconsin); it had the closest margin of any state in that election. Democrat [Joe Biden](145422.md) won the state by a narrow margin of 0.63% in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_Wisconsin);[^cite_note-231] Trump won Wisconsin in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election_in_Wisconsin) by a similarly narrow margin of 0.77%, the first time the state voted for a Republican presidential nominee since [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election). Wisconsin was part of the [blue wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_wall_\(U.S._politics\)), a group of states the Democratic Party won in each presidential election from [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election) to [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-232] Since achieving statehood in 1848, Wisconsin has been won by Republican presidential candidates 26 times, Democrats 18 times, and once by the [Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_\(United_States,_1924%E2%80%931934\)).

At the statewide level, Wisconsin is competitive, with control regularly alternating between the two parties. Following the [2014 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Wisconsin_elections), the governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general, and treasurer were all Republicans, while the secretary of state was a Democrat.[^cite_note-233] However, in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wisconsin_elections), Democrats won all constitutional statewide offices on the ballot, the first time this happened in Wisconsin since 1982.[^cite_note-234] [Among Wisconsin's 46 governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Wisconsin), 32 were Republicans, 12 were Democrats, one was a [Whig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_\(United_States\)), and two were [Wisconsin Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Progressive_Party) members.

In a 2020 study, Wisconsin was ranked as the 25th easiest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-235] Some have argued the state has experienced [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding) since 2011.[^cite_note-\:2-236][^cite_note-\:0-237][^cite_note-238] Some political scientists classify Wisconsin as a [hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime);[^cite_note-\:2-236] the state's [House of Representative](19468510.md) and [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_\(United_States\)) elections are considered to be [free but not fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_fair_election), with districts undergoing "extreme [partisan gerrymanders](12987.md)" to entrench Republicans "beyond [electoral rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_United_States)".[^cite_note-\:2-236][^cite_note-\:0-237][^cite_note-\:1-239] The Wisconsin Supreme Court overturned the legislative gerrymander in the 2023 ruling of *[Clarke v. Wisconsin Elections Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarke_v._Wisconsin_Elections_Commission).*[^cite_note-240]



The [2011 Wisconsin Act 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Wisconsin_Act_10) led to large protests around the state capitol building in Madison.

[^cite_note-241]

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Wisconsin was a [historically Republican state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Republican_Party); in fact, it is the state that gave birth to the Republican Party, although ethno-religious issues in the late 19th century caused a brief split in the coalition. The [Bennett Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Law) campaign of 1890 controversially required English as the sole medium of instruction in all schools, and Germans switched to the Democratic Party because the Republican Party supported the law.[^cite_note-242]

Wisconsin's political history is broad in scope, encompassing [Robert La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_La_Follette) and the [Progressive movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_movement) to prominent anti-communist [Joseph McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy). From the early 20th century, the [Socialist Party of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_America) had a base in Milwaukee. The phenomenon was referred to as "[sewer socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewer_socialism)" because the elected officials were more concerned with public works and reform than with revolution (although revolutionary socialism existed in the city as well). Its influence faded in the late 1950s largely because of the [red scare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Red_Scare) and racial tensions.[^cite_note-smith2003-243] The first socialist mayor of a large city in the United States was [Emil Seidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Seidel), elected mayor of Milwaukee in 1910; the city elected three more socialist mayors in [Daniel Hoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hoan), [Frank P. Zeidler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_P._Zeidler), and [Henry Maier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Maier). Socialist [newspaper editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_editor) [Victor Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Berger) was repeatedly elected as a U.S. Representative.

Through the first half of the 20th century, Wisconsin's politics were dominated by Robert La Follette and his sons, originally of the Republican Party and later of the [Wisconsin Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Progressive_Party). Since 1945, the state has maintained a close balance between Republicans and Democrats.[^cite_note-conant2006-244] Wisconsin Congressman [Paul Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ryan) was the Republican vice-presidential nominee in the [2012 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election), alongside [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney),[^cite_note-245] and later served as 54th [speaker of the House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives).[^cite_note-246]

### International relations

Wisconsin has sister-state relationships with [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) in Germany, [Chiba Prefecture](179796.md) in Japan, [Jalisco](16020434.md) in Mexico, [Heilongjiang](173816.md) in China, and [Nicaragua](21362.md).[^cite_note-iw2006-247] A [Mexican consulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_consulate) opened in Milwaukee on July 1, 2016.[^cite_note-248]

## Education

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Wisconsin), [List of high schools in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Wisconsin), and [List of school districts in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Wisconsin)



The [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison) is the flagship campus of the [University of Wisconsin System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin_System).

Wisconsin, along with Michigan and Minnesota, was among the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern) leaders in the emergent American state university movement following the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). By the start of the 20th century, education in the state advocated the "[Wisconsin Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Idea)", which emphasized service to the people of the state. The "Wisconsin Idea" exemplified the Progressive movement within colleges and universities at the time.[^cite_note-nye-249]

The [Wisconsin Department of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Department_of_Public_Instruction) is the [state education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_education_agency) and public library management agency in the state.[^cite_note-250] The department is led by the [State Superintendent of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Public_Instruction_of_Wisconsin), a non-partisan, constitutional officer elected every four years in the spring primary, six months after the previous year's presidential election.

Public [post-secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-secondary_education) in Wisconsin consists of two organizations. The [University of Wisconsin System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin_System) comprises the state's two tier 1 [research universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_universities) as classified by the [Carnegie Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Foundation_for_the_Advancement_of_Teaching): the flagship [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison), and the [University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Milwaukee).[^cite_note-251] The system has eleven other comprehensive universities and ten two-year branch campuses. It is one of the largest public higher education systems in the country, enrolling more than 160,000 [students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_student) each year and employing approximately 41,000 faculty and staff statewide.[^cite_note-252] The 16-campus [Wisconsin Technical College System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Technical_College_System) awards two-year associate degrees, one- and two-year technical diplomas, and short-term technical diplomas and certificates. It also provides training and technical assistance to Wisconsin's business and industry community.

[Carroll University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroll_University) was Wisconsin's first institution of higher education. It was chartered by the territorial legislature on January 31, 1846, and temporarily suspended operations during the Civil War.[^cite_note-253] [Beloit College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloit_College) is the state's oldest continuously operated college, chartered on February 2, 1846.[^cite_note-254] [Marquette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquette_University) is a tier 2 research university and Wisconsin's largest private university by enrollment.[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-nces-256] It became the world's first coeducational Catholic university in 1909.[^cite_note-257] Other private colleges and universities include [Alverno College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alverno_College), [Carthage College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage_College), [Concordia University Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University_Wisconsin), [Edgewood University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewood_University), [Lakeland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeland_University), [Lawrence University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_University), [Medical College of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_College_of_Wisconsin), [Milwaukee School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_School_of_Engineering), [Ripon College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon_College_\(Wisconsin\)), [St. Norbert College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Norbert_College), [Wisconsin Lutheran College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Lutheran_College), and [Viterbo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viterbo_University).

## Transportation

See also: [List of airports in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Wisconsin), [List of state trunk highways in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_trunk_highways_in_Wisconsin), [List of Wisconsin railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_railroads), and [List of lighthouses in Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lighthouses_in_Wisconsin)



[Wind Point Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_Point_Lighthouse) on Lake Michigan

Wisconsin is served by eight commercial service airports, in addition to a number of [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airports. [Milwaukee Mitchell International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Mitchell_International_Airport) is the largest airport located in Wisconsin, [categorized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAA_airport_categories) as a medium-hub primary commercial service facility.[^cite_note-NPIAS_Airports-258] [Dane County Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_County_Regional_Airport) and [Appleton International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton_International_Airport) are classified as small-hub primary commercial airports that service the Madison and Fox Cities areas.

Wisconsin is served by multiple [intercity bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus) operators, which provide service to 71 stops and 53 cities. The following carriers provide scheduled bus service: [Amtrak Thruway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway), [Badger Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badger_Bus), [Flixbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flixbus), [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), [Indian Trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Trails), [Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines), [Lamers Bus Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamers_Bus_Lines?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)), [Van Galder Bus Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Galder_Bus_Company), and [Wisconsin Coach Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Coach_Lines).[^cite_note-259]

Wisconsin is served by eight [Interstate Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Interstate_Highways_in_Wisconsin), consisting of five [primary routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System#Primary_\(one-_and_two-digit\)_routes_\(contiguous_U.S.\)) and three [auxiliary routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System#Auxiliary_\(three-digit\)_Interstates_\(contiguous_U.S.\)). The first part of this system was constructed in 1956, and its most recent expansion took place in 2015, with the addition of [I-41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_41) to the system. Wisconsin's longest Interstate Highway is [I-94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94_in_Wisconsin). There are also fourteen [United States Numbered Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Numbered_Highways) in the state of Wisconsin, which were designated beginning in 1926 and ending in the mid-1930s. There are also several [business routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_route), usually maintained by local governments. The [Wisconsin Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Department_of_Transportation) maintains [158 state trunk highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_State_Trunk_Highway_System), ranging from two-lane rural roads to limited-access freeways. These highways are paid for by the state's Transportation Fund, which is considered unique among state highway funds because it is kept entirely separate from the general fund, therefore, revenues received from transportation services are required to be used on transportation. The majority of state highway funding comes from gas taxes and vehicle registration fees.[^cite_note-260]

## State symbols

See also: [List of Wisconsin state symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wisconsin_state_symbols)



The [American badger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_badger) is the [state animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._state_mammals) of Wisconsin.

Wisconsin is traditionally known as the "Badger State" due to its early history in [lead](17747.md) [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining). Many of the state's first settlers were drawn by the prospect of mining in southwest Wisconsin, a mineral-rich region which had been contested between [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) and the U.S. Some of the miners lived burrowed within hillsides either due to lack of time or finances to build above-ground structures during the winter. Similar to the [American badger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_badger) using its claws to dig holes, the miners were nicknamed "badgers". The [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison) adopted the badger as a mascot in 1889 after the nickname; the badger was named Wisconsin's state animal in 1957.[^cite_note-263]
