**Chișinău** ([/ˌkɪʃɪˈnaʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bd/En-us-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg/En-us-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bd/En-us-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg/En-us-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*KIH-shih-NOW*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Romanian: [\[kiʃiˈnəw\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romanian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bf/Ro-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg/Ro-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bf/Ro-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg/Ro-Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; formerly known by its Russian form **Kishinev**)[^cite_note-9] is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Moldova) of [Moldova](19260.md) by area and population.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The city is Moldova's main industrial and commercial centre. It is situated in the middle of the country, on the river [Bîc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%AEc), a tributary of the [Dniester](376633.md). According to the results of the [2024 Moldovan census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Moldovan_census), the population of the city proper stood at over 567,000 inhabitants, while the population of the Municipality of Chișinău (which includes the city itself and other nearby communities) numbered over 720,000 people. Chișinău is the most economically prosperous locality in Moldova and the country's largest transport hub. Nearly a third of Moldova's population resides in the metropolitan area.

Moldova has a [history of winemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_wine) dating back to at least 3,000 BC. As the capital city, Chișinău hosts the yearly national wine festival every October.[^cite_note-\:1-12][^cite_note-\:4-13] Though the city's buildings were significantly damaged during the [Second World War](32927.md) and earthquakes, a rich architectural heritage remains. In addition, it has numerous buildings designed in the postwar [Socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism) and [Brutalist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture) styles.

The city's central railway station boasts a [Russian-Imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Russia) architectural style and maintains direct railway links to Romania. The Swiss-Italian-Russian architect [Alexander Bernardazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bernardazzi) designed many of the city's buildings, including the [Chișinău City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_City_Hall), [Church of Saint Theodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Teodora_de_la_Sihla_Church), and the Church of Saint Panteleimon. The city hosts the [National Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Fine_Arts,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u), [Moldova State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_State_University), Brancusi Gallery, and [National Museum of History of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_History_of_Moldova), with more than 236,000 exhibits.

There are bustling markets in the north of the city, including the house where [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin) once resided while in exile from [Alexander I of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia). It has now been adapted as a museum.

## Etymology

The origin of the city's name is unclear. A theory suggests that the name may be derived from the [archaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaism) Romanian word *chișla* (meaning "spring", "source of water") and *nouă* ("new"), because it was built around a small spring, at the corner of Pușkin and Albișoara streets.[^cite_note-14]

The other version, formulated by (or attributed to[^cite_note-15]) [Ștefan Ciobanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98tefan_Ciobanu) (occasionally to [Iorgu Iordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iorgu_Iordan)), a Romanian historian and academician, holds that the name was formed the same way as the name of [Chișineu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99ineu-Cri%C8%99) (alternatively spelled as *Chișinău*) in Western [Romania](25445.md), near the border with Hungary. Its Hungarian name is *Kisjenő*, from which the Romanian name originates.[^cite_note-16] Kisjenő comes from *kis* "small" and the Jenő, one of the seven [Hungarian tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_tribes) that entered the [Carpathian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Basin) in 896.

A third theory by [Kiss Lajos](https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss%20Lajos%20\(nyelv%C3%A9sz\)), linguist and [Slavist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_studies), holds (as a possible origin) that the name came from the [Cuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumans) *kešene* ("grave", [kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan)) and the [Karachayian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachays) for "[cemetery](63752.md)", and these came from the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) word *[kāšāne](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%DA%A9%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%87)* ("house").[^cite_note-\:4-13]*<https://m.nyest.hu/hirek/chisinau-is-magyar-volt?fbclid=IwY2xjawEtbRNleHRuA2FlbQIxMQABHSRuOXxjq2BsVPtX6NySq3EiNgVBjRRTZCmKRa-nkTLjva4cR3BK7LXBnQ_aem_RIM2Hyb3h7_lYQbVDnfGVA>*

Chișinău is known in Russian as *Kishinyov* (Кишинёв, pronounced [\[kʲɪʂɨˈnʲɵf\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)), while Moldova's Russian-language media call it *Kishineu* (Кишинэу, pronounced [\[kʲɪʂɨˈnɛʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)). It is written *Kişinöv* in the Latin [Gagauz alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gagauz_alphabet). It was also written as *Chișineu* in pre–20th-century Romanian[^cite_note-17] and as Кишинэу in the [Moldovan Cyrillic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_Cyrillic_alphabet). Historically, the English-language name for the city, **Kishinev**, was based on the modified Russian one because it entered the English language via Russian at the time Chișinău was part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (e.g., [Kishinev pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishinev_pogrom)). Therefore, it remains a common English name in some historical contexts. Otherwise, the Romanian-based *Chișinău* has been steadily gaining wider currency, especially in [written language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_language). The city is also historically referred to as [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language): *Kišiniovas*; [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *Kisjenő*; [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Kischinau* [\[ˌkɪʃiˈnaʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fe/De-Kischinau.ogg/De-Kischinau.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fe/De-Kischinau.ogg/De-Kischinau.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Kiszyniów* [\[kʲiˈʂɨɲuf\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/ac/Pl-Kiszyni%C3%B3w.ogg/Pl-Kiszyni%C3%B3w.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/ac/Pl-Kiszyni%C3%B3w.ogg/Pl-Kiszyni%C3%B3w.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Кишинів, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Kyshyniv*, pronounced [\[kɪʃɪˈnʲiu̯\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a7/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%88%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%96%D0%B2.wav/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%88%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%96%D0%B2.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a7/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%88%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%96%D0%B2.wav/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%88%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%96%D0%B2.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Кишинев, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Bulgarian): *Kishinev*; [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language): קעשענעװ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Yiddish): *Keshenev*; or [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Kişinev*.

### Moldavian period

Founded in 1436 as a [monastery](45856.md) village, it was part of the [Principality of Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia) (which, starting with the 16th century, became a [vassal state of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_and_tributary_states_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), but still retaining its autonomy). At the beginning of the 19th century, Chișinău was a small town of 7,000 inhabitants.

### Russian Imperial period

In 1812, in the aftermath of the [Russo-Turkish War (1806–1812)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1806%E2%80%931812\)), the eastern half of Moldavia [was ceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Bucharest_\(1812\)) by the Ottomans to the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). The newly acquired territories became known as [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia).

Under the Russian government, Chișinău became the capital of the newly annexed [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast) (later [guberniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_\(Russia\))) of [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia_Governorate). By 1834, an [imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revival_architecture) townscape with broad and long roads had emerged as a result of a generous [development plan](4565772.md), which divided Chișinău roughly into two areas: the old part of the town, with its irregular building structures, and a newer city centre and station. Between 26 May 1830 and 13 October 1836, the architect [Avraam Melnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avraam_Melnikov) established the [Catedrala Nașterea Domnului](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_Cathedral,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) with a magnificent bell tower. In 1840, the building of the [Triumphal Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumphal_Arch,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u), planned by the architect Luca Zaushkevich, was completed. Following this, the construction of numerous buildings and landmarks began.

On 28 August 1871, Chișinău was linked by [rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calea_Ferat%C4%83_din_Moldova) with [Tiraspol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiraspol), and in 1873 with [Cornești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corne%C8%99ti_\(town\),_Ungheni). Chișinău-[Ungheni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ungheni)-[Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ia%C8%99i) railway was opened on 1 June 1875 in preparation for the [Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)). The town played an important part in the war between Russia and the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), as the main [staging area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staging_area) of the Russian invasion. During the [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque), the mayor of the city was [Carol Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Schmidt), whose contribution to the modernisation of the city is still commemorated by Moldovans.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-publika-19] Its population had grown to 92,000 by 1862, and to 125,787 by 1900.[^cite_note-20]

#### Pogroms and pre-revolution

Main article: [Kishinev pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishinev_pogrom)

In the late 19th century, especially due to growing anti-Semitic sentiment in other parts of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and better economic conditions in Moldova, many Jews chose to settle in Chișinău. By the year 1897, 46% of the population of Chișinău was Jewish, over 50,000 people.[^cite_note-21]



Chișinău, 1889

As part of the pogrom wave organized in the Russian Empire, a large [anti-Semitic](1078.md) riot was organized in the town on 19–20 April 1903, which would later be known as the [Kishinev pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_pogrom_\(1903\)). The rioting continued for three days, resulting in 47 Jews dead, 92 severely wounded, and 500 suffering minor injuries. In addition, several hundred houses and many businesses were plundered and destroyed.[^cite_note-22] Some sources say 49 people were killed.[^cite_note-23] The pogroms are largely believed to have been incited by anti-Jewish propaganda in the only official newspaper of the time, *Bessarabetz* (*Бессарабецъ*). Mayor Schmidt disapproved of the incident and resigned later in 1903. The reactions to this incident included a petition to [Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar) [Nicholas II of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia) on behalf of [the American people](3434750.md) by US President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) in July 1903.[^cite_note-pogrom-24]

On 22 August 1905, another violent event occurred: the police opened fire on an estimated 3,000 demonstrating agricultural workers. Only a few months later, on 19–20 October 1905, a further protest occurred, helping to force the hand of Nicholas II in bringing about the [October Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Manifesto). However, these demonstrations suddenly turned into [another anti-Jewish pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Kishinev_pogrom), resulting in 19 deaths.[^cite_note-pogrom-24]

### Romanian period



[Sfatul Țării Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfatul_%C8%9A%C4%83rii_Palace) in c. 1920

Following the Russian [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), Bessarabia declared independence as the [Moldavian Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Democratic_Republic), before joining the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania). As of 1919, Chișinău, with an estimated population of 133,000,[^cite_note-Kaba-25] became the second largest city in Romania.

Between 1918 and 1940, the city center undertook large [renovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renovation) work. Romania granted important subsidies to its provinces and initiated large-scale investment programs in the infrastructure of the main cities in Bessarabia, expanded the railroad infrastructure, and launched an extensive program to eradicate illiteracy.

In 1927, the [Stephen the Great Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_the_Great_Monument), by the sculptor [Alexandru Plămădeală](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Pl%C4%83m%C4%83deal%C4%83), was erected. In 1933, the first higher education institution in Bessarabia was established by transferring the Agricultural Sciences Section of the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ia%C8%99i) to Chișinău, as the [Faculty of Agricultural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_State_University_of_Moldova).

### World War II



[Train of Pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_to_Victims_of_Stalinist_Repression) – the monument to the victims of communist mass deportations in Moldova

On 28 June 1940, as a direct result of the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact), Bessarabia was [annexed by the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina) from Romania, and Chișinău became the capital of the newly created [Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

Following the Soviet occupation, mass deportations, linked with atrocities, were executed by the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD) between June 1940 and June 1941. More than 400 people were [summarily executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summarily_executed) in Chișinău in July 1940 and buried in the grounds of the Metropolitan Palace, the Chișinău Theological Institute, and the backyard of the Italian Consulate, where the NKVD had established its headquarters.[^cite_note-Brez2010-26] As part of the policy of political repression of the potential opposition to the Communist power, tens of thousands of members of native families were [deported from Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_deportations_from_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina) to other regions of the USSR.

[A devastating earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Vrancea_earthquake) occurred on 10 November 1940, measuring 7.4 (or 7.7, according to other sources) on the [Richter scale](19467352.md). The [epicenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicenter) of the quake was in the [Vrancea Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrancea_Mountains), and it led to substantial destruction: 78 deaths and 2,795 damaged buildings (of which 172 were destroyed).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]



*Eternity* – a memorial complex dedicated to the soldiers who fell in World War II and the [military conflict in Transnistria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Transnistria)

In June 1941, in order to recover Bessarabia, Romania entered [World War II](32927.md) under the command of the German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht), declaring [war on the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_in_World_War_II). Chișinău was severely affected in the chaos of the Second World War. In June and July 1941, the city came under bombardment by Nazi [air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing). However, the Romanian and newly Moldovan sources assign most of the responsibility for the damage to Soviet NKVD [destruction battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_battalion), which operated in Chișinău until 17 July 1941, when it was captured by Axis forces.[^cite_note-29]

During the German and Romanian military administration, the city suffered from the [Nazi extermination policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) of its Jewish inhabitants, who were transported on trucks to the outskirts of the city and then summarily shot in partially dug pits. The number of Jews murdered during the initial occupation of the city is estimated at 10,000 people.[^cite_note-30] The deportation of the city's Jews to Transnistria reduced its Jewish population from 11,388 in the fall of 1941 to 177 in 1943; a large majority of the deportees died.[^cite_note-31] During this time, Chișinău, part of [Lăpușna County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C4%83pu%C8%99na_County_\(Romania\)), was the capital of the newly established [Bessarabia Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia_Governorate_\(Romania\)) of Romania.[^cite_note-32]

As the war drew to a conclusion, the city was once again the scene of heavy fighting as German and Romanian troops retreated. Chișinău was captured by the Red Army on 24 August 1944 as a result of the [Second Jassy–Kishinev offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Jassy%E2%80%93Kishinev_offensive).

### Soviet period



State Art Museum, during the [Cold War](325329.md) period

After the war, [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia) was fully reintegrated into the Soviet Union, with around 65 percent of its territory as the Moldavian SSR, while the remaining 35 percent was transferred to the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR).

Two other waves of deportations of Moldova's native population were carried out by the Soviets, the first one immediately after the Soviet reoccupation of Bessarabia until the end of the 1940s, and the second one in the mid-1950s.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

Between 1947 and 1949, the architect [Alexey Shchusev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Shchusev) developed a plan with the aid of a team of architects for the gradual reconstruction of the city.[^cite_note-35]



Trams in Chișinău (pictured Gothawagen ET54) were discontinued in 1961.

There was rapid population growth in the 1950s, to which the Soviet administration responded by constructing large-scale housing and palaces in the style of [Stalinist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_architecture). This process continued under [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), who called for construction under the slogan "good, cheaper, and built faster." The new [architectural style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_style) brought about dramatic change and generated the style that dominates today, with large [blocks of flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocks_of_flats) arranged in considerable settlements.  These Khrushchev-era buildings are often informally called [Khrushchyovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchyovka).

The period of the most significant [redevelopment of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) began in 1971, when the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Soviet_Union\)) of the Soviet Union adopted a decision "On the measures for further development of the city of Kishinev," which secured more than one billion [rubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_ruble) in investment from the [state budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget),[^cite_note-36] and continued until the [independence of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Moldova) in 1991. The share of dwellings built during the Soviet period (1951–1990) represents 74.3 percent of total households.[^cite_note-37]

On 4 March 1977, the city was again jolted by a devastating [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Vrancea_earthquake). Several people were killed, and panic broke out. The [Intourist Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hotel_\(Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u\)), a flagship property constructed by the [Soviet state-owned travel monopoly of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intourist), was completed in 1978.[^cite_note-38]



Prospectul Păcii in 1980

On 22 April 1993, the city inaugurated the Monument to the Victims of Jewish Ghettos, a public monument centred on a bronze statue of the [Biblical prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophet) [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses), which serves as a symbol of remembrance to the thousands of Jews who perished during the holocaust. The monument was designed by sculptor [Naum Epelbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naum_Epelbaum?action=edit\&redlink=1)m and architect [Simeon Shoihet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Shoihet?action=edit\&redlink=1). It stands on Jerusalem Street, marking the site of the main entrance to the [Chișinău Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_Ghetto), which was established in the lower part of the city in July 1941, shortly after the German and Romanian troops occupied the area.[^cite_note-39]

### After independence

Since [Moldovan independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_of_the_Republic_of_Moldova) following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), many streets of Chișinău have been [renamed after historic persons, places, or events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_names_in_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u). Independence from the Soviet Union was followed by a large-scale renaming of streets and localities from a Communist theme into a national one.[^cite_note-40]

On 5 September 2022, the country's first Christian university, Universitatea Moldo-Americană, opened its doors, supported by the Scandinavian broadcaster [Visjon Norge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visjon_Norge) and several donors in [Norway](21241.md), and run in cooperation with the [American Southeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_University_\(Florida\)) in [Florida](18933066.md), [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-\:2-41]

Following [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Moldova allowed more than 600,000 Ukrainian civilians to flee Ukraine across their border. Despite being among the [poorest states in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Moldova), Moldova has continued to host more than 100,000 [Ukrainian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_refugee_crisis_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)), many of them in Chișinău.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

On 23 November 2022, the Chișinău Court of Appeal ruled that [Chișinău International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_Eugen_Doga_International_Airport) will return to state ownership, according to [justice minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Minister_of_Moldova) [Sergiu Litvinenco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiu_Litvinenco), more than three months after an international court allowed Moldova to terminate a 49-year concession deal with airport operator Avia Invest.[^cite_note-45] In April 2023, the [Dutch government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Government) opened a new embassy in Chișinău.[^cite_note-46]

On 21 May 2023, tens of thousands of Moldovans took to the streets in a massive rally, the [European Moldova National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Moldova_National_Assembly), to support the country's [European Union membership bid](54111451.md).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Moldovan police said more than 75,000 demonstrators were present at the rally organised by Moldovan president [Maia Sandu](40796149.md).[^cite_note-49]

Later that month, Chișinău hosted a major international summit of the [European Political Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Political_Community) organised to discuss the illegal Russian invasion of Ukraine as well as cybersecurity, migration and energy security, and regional issues in [Azerbaijan](746.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), and [clashes in Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_North_Kosovo_crisis).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-\:0-52]

## Geography



"Museum of the village" (*Muzeul satului*), located on the southern outskirts of the city

Chișinău is located on the river [Bâc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A2c_River), a tributary of the [Dniester](376633.md), at [47°0′N 28°55′E  /  47.000°N 28.917°E  / 47.000; 28.917](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u\&params=47_0_N_28_55_E_), with an area of 120 km<sup>2</sup> (46 sq mi). The municipality comprises 635 km<sup>2</sup> (245 sq mi).

The city lies in central Moldova and is surrounded by a relatively level landscape with very fertile ground.

Chișinău is roughly equidistant between the borders with Romania (58 km) and Ukraine (54 km), and between the northernmost (188 km) and southernmost (179 km) points of Moldova, thus meaning that it is very close to Moldova's geographic centre.

### Climate



[Botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisinau_Botanical_Garden)

Chișinău has a [hot-summer humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfa*) characterised by hot summers and cold, windy winters. Winter minimum temperatures are often below 0 °C (32 °F), although they rarely drop below −10 °C (14 °F). In summer, the average maximum temperature is approximately 28 °C (82 °F), however, temperatures occasionally reach 35 to 40 °C (95 to 104 °F) in mid-summer in downtown. Although average [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity) during summer is relatively low, most of the annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) occurs during summer, causing infrequent yet heavy storms.

Spring and autumn temperatures vary between 16 and 24 °C (61 and 75 °F), and precipitation during this time tends to be lower than in summer but with more frequent yet milder periods of rain.

## Economy



[MallDova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malldova) shopping centre

Historically, the city was home to fourteen factories in 1919.[^cite_note-Kaba-25] Chișinău is the financial and business capital of Moldova. Its GDP comprises about 60% of the national economy[^cite_note-\:5-59] reached in 2012 the amount of 52 billion lei (US\$4 billion). Thus, the GDP per capita of Chișinău stood at 227% of the Moldova's average. Chișinău has the largest and most developed mass media sector in Moldova, and is home to several related companies ranging from leading television networks and radio stations to major newspapers. All national and international banks (15) have their headquarters located in Chișinău.

Notable sites around Chișinău include Cineplex Loteanu, the new malls [MallDova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malldova), Port Mall, and best-known retailers, such as Nr1, Linella, [Kaufland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaufland), Fourchette, and [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Brands). While many locals continue to shop at the [bazaars](556904.md), many upper class residents and tourists shop at the retail stores and at MallDova. Jumbo, an older mall in the Botanica district, and Sun City, in the centre, are more popular with locals.

Several amusement parks exist around the city. A [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-era one is located in the Botanica district, along the three lakes of a major park, which reaches the outskirts of the city centre. Another, the modern Aventura Park, is located farther from the centre. The [Chișinău State Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_State_Circus?action=edit\&redlink=1), which used to be in a grand building in the Râșcani sector, has been inactive for several years due to a poorly funded renovation project.[^cite_note-60]

Soviet-style apartment buildings in Chișinău

Romashka Tower, the tallest building in Moldova

See also: [Wooden church of Hirișeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_church_of_Hiri%C8%99eni)

Chișinău's growth plan was developed in the 19th century. In 1836, the construction of the [Kishinev Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishinev_Cathedral) and its [belfry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_tower) was finished. The belfry was demolished in Soviet times and was rebuilt in 1997. Chișinău also displays a tremendous number of Orthodox churches and 19th-century buildings around the city such as [Ciuflea Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciuflea_Monastery) or the [Transfiguration Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfiguration_Church,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u). Much of the city is made from limestone quarried from [Cricova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricova), leaving a famous [wine cellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricova_\(winery\)) there.

Many modern-style buildings have been built in the city since 1991. There are many office and shopping complexes that are modern, renovated or newly built, including Kentford, SkyTower, and [Unión Fenosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_Fenosa) headquarters. However, the old Soviet-style clusters of living blocks are still an extensive feature of the cityscape.

## Culture and education

See also: [List of public schools in Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_schools_in_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) and [List of universities in Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Moldova)

### Education

The city is home to 8 public and 4 private universities, the [Academy of Sciences of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Moldova), a number of institutions offering high school and 1–2 years of [college education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education). Among them are [Moldova State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_State_University), the [Academy of Economic Studies of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Economic_Studies_of_Moldova), [Alexandru cel Bun Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_cel_Bun_Military_Academy), [Nicolae Testemițanu State University of Medicine and Pharmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Testemi%C8%9Banu_State_University_of_Medicine_and_Pharmacy), and [Ion Creangă State Pedagogical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Creang%C4%83_State_Pedagogical_University).

On 5 September 2022, the country's first Christian university Universitatea Moldo-Americană opened its doors, supported by the Scandinavian broadcaster [Visjon Norge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visjon_Norge) and several donors in [Norway](21241.md), and run in cooperation with the [American Southeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_University_\(Florida\)) in [Florida](18933066.md), [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-\:2-41]

In Chișinău there are several museums. The three national museums are the National Museum of Ethnography and Natural History, the [National Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Fine_Arts,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u), and the [National Museum of History of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_History_of_Moldova). The National Museum of Ethnography and Natural History was founded in October 1889 by baron Alexandru Stuart, moved to its current location in 1905, and is the oldest museum in Moldova.[^cite_note-74] It houses more than 135,000 exhibit pieces, among them a life-sized reconstruction of the skeleton of a dinothere, discovered in the Rezine region in 1966.[^cite_note-75] It also includes exhibits on [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history), [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science), [archaeology](18951655.md), [paleontology](23084.md), [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology), and [ethnography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography).[^cite_note-76] The building was designed by the architect [Vladimir Tsyganko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Tsyganko) in a distinctive [Moorish architectural style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_architecture) with a signature frontal façade consisting of a triangular pediment supported by two [Doric columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doric_order).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

The [National Library of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Moldova) is also located in Chișinău.[^cite_note-79]

### Events and festivals

Chișinău, as well as Moldova as a whole, still show signs of ethnic culture. Signs that say "Patria Mea" (English: My homeland) can be found all over the capital. While few people still wear traditional Moldavian attire, large public events often draw in such original costumes.

*Moldova National Wine Day* and *Wine Festival* take place every year in the first weekend of October, in Chișinău. The events celebrate the autumn harvest and recognises the country's long history of winemaking, which dates back to at least 3,000 BCE.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Moldova has been called the wine capital of Europe and its yearly festival is a major cultural and tourist event, and every year the streets are filled with people enjoying food, wine, dance, and music taking over the streets.[^cite_note-\:1-12][^cite_note-\:3-82] Moldova's most-awarded sommelier Mihai Druta has described Moldovan wine as being about "small producers and family wineries making premium wine. And nothing costs more than 100 Euro a bottle."[^cite_note-\:3-82] *[The Daily Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Express)* in 2019 described the city as "Europe's latest hotspot" in which journalist Maisha Frost praised "its wines, monumental wineries and their epic tasting sessions."[^cite_note-83]

## Media

The majority of Moldova's [media industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) is based in Chișinău. There are almost 30 FM-radio stations and 10 TV-channels broadcasting in Chișinău. The first radio station in Chișinău, [Radio Basarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Basarabia), was launched by the [Romanian Radio Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Radio_Broadcasting_Company) on 8 October 1939, when the religious service was broadcast on air from the [Nativity Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_Cathedral,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u). The first TV station in the city, [Moldova 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_1), was launched on 30 April 1958, while [Nicolae Lupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Lupan) was serving as the redactor-in-chief of [Teleradio-Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeleRadio-Moldova).[^cite_note-84]

The state national broadcaster in the country is the [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_ownership) [Moldova 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_1), which has its head office in the city. The broadcasts of [Teleradio-Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeleRadio-Moldova) have been criticised by the [Independent Journalism Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Journalism_Center) as showing 'bias' towards the authorities.[^cite_note-85]

Other TV channels based in Chișinău are [TV8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV8_\(Moldova\)), [Jurnal TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurnal_TV), [ProTV Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_TV_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u), [Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_\(Moldovan_TV_channel\)), [Publika TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publika_TV), CTC, [DTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTV_\(Moldovan_TV_channel\)), Euro TV etc. In addition to television, most Moldovan radio and newspaper companies have their headquarters in the city. Broadcasters include the national radio [Vocea Basarabiei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocea_Basarabiei), Prime FM, BBC Moldova, [Radio Europa Libera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty), Kiss FM Chișinău, Pro FM Chișinău, Radio 21, Fresh FM, Radio Nova, [Russkoye Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russkoye_Radio), Hit FM Moldova, and many others.

The biggest broadcasters are [StarNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarNet) (IPTV), [Moldtelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldtelecom) (IPTV), Sun TV, Satellit, and Zebra TV. In 2007, Sun TV and Zebra launched [digital TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_television) cable networks.

## Transport

[Trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) on the street

### Airport

[Chișinău Eugen Doga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_Eugen_Doga_International_Airport) offers connections to major destinations in Europe and Asia.

[FlyOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlyOne) and [HiSky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiSky) airlines have their headquarters, and [Wizz Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air) has its hub on the grounds of Chișinău Eugen Doga International Airport.[^cite_note-86]

### Road

The most popular form of internal [transport in Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Moldova) is generally the bus. Although the city has just three main terminals, buses generally serve as the [means of transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_of_transport) between cities in and outside of Moldova. Popular destinations include [Tiraspol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiraspol), [Odesa](55545.md) (Ukraine), [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ia%C8%99i) and [Bucharest](36877.md) (Romania).

### Rail

The second most popular form of domestic transportation within Moldova is via railways. The total length of the network managed by [Moldovan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calea_Ferat%C4%83_din_Moldova) (as of 2009) is 1,232 kilometres (766 miles). The entire network is single track and is not electrified. The central hub of all railways is [Chișinău Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_Railway_Station). There is another smaller railway station – [Revaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revaca_railway_station) located on the city's ends.

Chișinău Railway Station has an international railway terminal with connections to [Bucharest](36877.md) and [Kyiv](585629.md).

### Public transport

#### Trolleybuses

See also: [Trolleybuses in Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u)

There is a wide [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) network operating as common public transportation within the city. From 1994, Chișinău saw the establishment of new trolleybus lines, as well as an increase in capacity of existing lines, to improve connections between the urban districts. The network comprises 22 trolleybus lines being 246 km (153 mi) in length. Trolleybuses run between 05\:00 and 23\:00. There are 320 units operating daily in Chișinău. However, the requirements are as minimum as 600 units. A trolleybus ticket costs 7 lei (ca. \$0.41). It is the cheapest method of transport within Chișinău municipality.

#### Buses

There are 29 lines of buses within Chișinău municipality. At each public transportation stops there is attached a schedule for buses and trolleybuses. There are approximately 330 public transportation stops within Chișinău municipality. There is a big lack of buses inside city limits, with only 115 buses operating within Chișinău.[^cite_note-87]

#### Minibuses

In Chișinău and its suburbs, privately operated minibuses known as "*[rutieras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutiera)*" generally follow the major bus and [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) routes and appear more frequently.[^cite_note-88]

As of October 2017, there are 1,100 units of minibuses operating within Chișinău. Minibuses services are priced the same as buses – 7 [lei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_leu) for a ticket (ca. \$0.41).[^cite_note-89]

### Traffic

The city traffic becomes more congested as each year passes. Nowadays there are about 300,000 cars in the city plus 100,000 transit transports coming to the city each day. The number of personal transports is expected to reach 550,000 (without transit) by 2025.

## Sport



[Zimbru Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbru_Stadium)

[Football](10568.md) is the most widely followed sport in Chișinău. Local clubs such as [Zimbru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Zimbru_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u), [Dacia Buiucani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_Buiucani), [Spartanii Sportul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSF_Spartanii_Sportul) and [Politehnica UTM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Politehnica_UTM_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) compete in the [Moldovan Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_Liga) (top tier), while [Real Succes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Succes_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) and [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Bardar) play in the [Liga 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_%22A%22_Division) (second tier).

[Zimbru Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbru_Stadium), which opened in May 2006 with a seating capacity of 10,104, meets all the requirements for hosting official international matches and serves as the home venue for the [Moldova national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_national_football_team).

Since 2011, [CS Femina-Sport Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CS_Femina-Sport_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) has organized women's competitions in seven sports.

[Chișinău Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u), an [indoor arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_arena) was opened in 2022.

The [FMF Beach Soccer Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FMF_Beach_Soccer_Arena), a [beach soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_soccer) stadium that opened in 2022, was the main venue for the [Euro Beach Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_Beach_Soccer_League) and the [Socca EuroCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socca_EuroCup) events held in 2022, 2023 and 2024.[^cite_note-socca1-90]

The city also hosts the annual [Chișinău International Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u_International_Marathon).
