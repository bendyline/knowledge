Beginning in early June 2026,[^cite_note-7] [armed clashes](2346678.md) erupted between the [Kurdistan Free Life Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Free_Life_Party)'s (PJAK) armed wing, the [Eastern Kurdistan Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Kurdistan_Units) (YRK), and the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) in the highlands of [western Iran](55024578.md) ([Iranian Kurdistan](61733686.md)), particularly near [Marivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marivan) and regions south of [Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad). In late June and early July 2026, clashes intensified and spread to the [Iran–Iraq border](10282111.md), the [Sardasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardasht,_West_Azerbaijan)–[Baneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baneh)–[Saqqez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqqez) triangle,[^cite_note-\:3-5] urban areas, and began to involve other [Iranian Kurdish opposition groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_Political_Forces_of_Iranian_Kurdistan), including the [Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan](3197182.md) (KDPI).

Besides the armed clashes, [ACLED](43040226.md) recorded a significant increase in [assassinations](2963.md) of IRGC affiliates and countermeasures by the [Basij](2892082.md), including the expansion of its "popular [intelligence network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_network)." The assassinations have been attributed to PJAK-linked groups such as the Patriotic Youth of Kurdistan and Kurdistan Martyrs' Revenge Teams, as well as newly emerging groups such as [Sun of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_of_Hope) (Khori Hiwa).[^cite_note-8]

## Background

During the [Iranian–Kurdish conflict](35032860.md), the [Iranian Kurdish opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_Political_Forces_of_Iranian_Kurdistan)'s last major ground confrontation with Iran occurred during the [2016–2023 clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iran_clashes_\(2016%E2%80%932023\)). As a result of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), the IRGC [targeted](82556400.md) Iranian Kurdish opposition parties in the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) of Iraq (KRI) through aerial attacks, reportedly in anticipation of a [potential U.S.-backed Kurdish offensive](82575260.md#Alleged_US_and_Israeli_involvement).[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-\:0-10] However, Kurdish groups remained relatively passive, citing the lack of long-term U.S. war objectives. Reports of alleged leaks concerning U.S. and Israeli offensive plans and weapons supplies, combined with pressure from [Turkey](11125639.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), and the [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Regional_Government) (KRG), followed by the withdrawal of U.S. support, effectively closed the window of opportunity.[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-11] Meanwhile, Iran reinforced its border regions along the KRI, pressured Iraq to expel Kurdish groups,[^cite_note-12] and reactivated elements of the [Muslim Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Peshmerga).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Although hostilities between Iran, the U.S., and Israel had largely ceased following the [Islamabad Memorandum](83441543.md) in mid-June,[^cite_note-15] Iranian strikes on Kurdish opposition groups in the KRI continued,[^cite_note-16] and fighting soon erupted between the IRGC and Kurdish armed groups. The clashes included sustained ground fighting inside Iranian Kurdistan.[^cite_note-\:122-17] *[Hengaw](72014392.md)* reported that the clashes spread to multiple cities in Iranian Kurdistan, while *[Fox News](11121.md)* indicated that the clashes could lead to the renewing of a larger Kurdish insurgency.[^cite_note-\:132-18][^cite_note-19]

PJAK stated that the renewed clashes were a consequence of the memorandum between the United States and Iran, and that Iran escalated the situation thereafter.[^cite_note-\:14-1][^cite_note-20] The killing of two prominent [Yarsani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism)-Kurdish cultural activists also sparked outrage in the region, prompting a PJAK spokesperson to state that "when the regime speaks to the Kurdish people through executions, assassinations, and massacres, the Kurds will answer with legitimate self-defense."[^cite_note-\:16-21]

## Timeline

### June

Clashes reportedly began on 7 June when pro-[PKK](69680.md) [ANF News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANF_News) claimed that the armed wing of PJAK, the Eastern Kurdistan Units (YRK), was targeted by artillery fire of the IRGC and repelled an attack in Marivan.[^cite_note-22] According to ANF News, IRGC bombardments, including drone strikes, on areas near Marivan and Baneh continued on 16 June and 25 June.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] On 20 June, Iran's [Armed Forces General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_General_Staff_\(Iran\)) (AFGS) confirmed the clashes and stated that it was closely monitoring militant activity in the provinces of [Kurdistan](448112.md), [West Azerbaijan](47567329.md), and [Kermanshah](448078.md), and that it had adopted an "active [deterrence](735661.md)" doctrine.[^cite_note-\:2-4] On 23 June, clashes in the Khawmirabad area along the Iran–Iraq border resulted in the death of an [Iranian border officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Guard_Command_\(Iran\)) at the hands of unidentified armed individuals.[^cite_note-25] On 26 June, an attack on the Saqqez–Baneh road at the entrance to Baneh, which may have been related to the clashes, killed between two and three [Iranian police officers](5187924.md) and injured between two and five others, according to differing reports.[^cite_note-\:16-21][^cite_note-\:2-4][^cite_note-26] The Sardasht–Baneh–Saqqez triangle experienced increasing Iranian military deployments and shelling of the surrounding mountain areas.[^cite_note-\:3-5] On 29 June, the YRK described ongoing clashes between its forces and the IRGC as "[military operations](666301.md)" launched by the latter, which it said had escalated in the area of Mahabad, particularly the village of Gagesh.[^cite_note-27] *The Amargi* also described the clashes as a military operation backed by significant firepower.[^cite_note-\:16-21] At the end of June, *Hengaw* reported that the clashes had resulted in the deaths of four YRK fighters and three IRGC members, as well as the injury of four Iranian security personnel and one civilian.[^cite_note-\:13-28] The deaths of the four YRK fighters were also confirmed by the group itself,[^cite_note-\:14-1] while PJAK-aligned media claimed that 16 IRGC members were killed, three Iranian drones were destroyed, and an IRGC depot was struck by a first-person-view (FPV) drone.[^cite_note-\:3-5] Iranian authorities acknowledged at least one death among its border guards.[^cite_note-29]

On 29 June, the newly formed Kurdish militant group [Sun of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_of_Hope) (Xori Hiwa) carried out an attack on the residence of a local Kurdish [Basij](2892082.md) member in [Paveh](1070269.md).[^cite_note-\:15-6] The attack reportedly resulted in the deaths of two Basij members and injury of two more.[^cite_note-\:15-6][^cite_note-30] Following the attack, Sun of Hope announced its formation and stated that its main goals were to "promote political awareness, strengthen [Kurdish national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_nationalism), and confront the policies of the Islamic Republic of Iran."[^cite_note-\:12-31] The group had also reported clashes between its fighters and the IRGC in [Piranshahr](30865712.md).[^cite_note-\:13-28] An attack in Paveh on the same day was also claimed by the PJAK-linked Kurdistan Patriotic Youth.[^cite_note-32]

### July

On 1 July and in the early hours of 2 July, heavy clashes erupted between KDPI Peshmerga and the IRGC in the village of [Qazqapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazqapan), approximately 3 kilometres west of Piranshahr, near the Iraqi border. The KDPI confirmed five deaths among its forces, while Iranian state-linked media claimed between six and eleven were killed among the group.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] On the same day, Iraq reported a [drone strike](38212231.md) on an Iranian Kurdish militant base east of [Erbil](24534207.md), [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md).[^cite_note-35]

On 7 July, *RojNews*, citing regional sources, reported that Iran had intensified troop deployments along the Iran–Iraq border following the clashes in June.[^cite_note-36] The following day, the state-affiliated [Iranian Students' News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Students'_News_Agency) reported that one IRGC member had been killed in clashes with what it described as "terrorist groups" in the [Sardasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardasht,_West_Azerbaijan) region.[^cite_note-37]

On 15 July, *[IranWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IranWire)* reported that Iran had deployed 3,000 personnel from the [Saberin Takavar Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saberin_Takavar_Brigade) to the western border region, units of the [IRGC Ground Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC_Ground_Forces) and [Aerospace Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC_Aerospace_Force), alongside tanks, artillery, [Katyusha rocket launchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rocket_launcher), and large numbers of reconnaissance and [kamikaze drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze_drones), significantly increasing its military presence in the region.[^cite_note-38]

By 19 July, Iranian authorities had closed civilian access to the Iran–Iraq border with the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md). Around the same time, *[Hengaw](72014392.md)* reported that the IRGC had held a large-scale [military meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_war) at its headquarters in the town of [Ravansar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravansar). The meeting was attended by Kourosh Asiabani, commander of the IRGC's regional headquarters in western Iran, along with other IRGC personnel, Basij forces, and armed local auxiliaries commonly known as *[jash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jash_\(term\))*.[^cite_note-39]

On July 25, 2026, Sun of Hope said it assassinated a high-ranking intelligence official in [Piranshahr](30865712.md).[^cite_note-40] *Hengaw* confirmed that the attack had occurred at the IRGC's 124th Ramadan Base and reported that another IRGC member had been seriously injured.[^cite_note-41]

On 27 July, the semi-official [Mehr News Agency](2707394.md) cited the spokesperson for the [Iranian Police Command](5187924.md) as saying that four PJAK members had been killed "in an armed clash" with Iranian forces after they had "attempted to enter the country."[^cite_note-42] *Hengaw*, however, disputed the official account, reporting that the incident was an IRGC ambush targeting Kurdish artist and cultural activist Mehdi Tavakkoli and his family while they were travelling on the [Marivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marivan)–[Baneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baneh) road. According to the human rights organization, Iranian police subsequently claimed that Tavakkoli was affiliated with PJAK and pressured his family to describe the incident as a traffic accident.[^cite_note-43]

On 30 July, an IRGC-affiliated local [auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliaries) was killed in Baneh in an attack by unidentified gunmen.[^cite_note-44]

### August

On 2 August, clashes between an unidentified armed group, presumed to be PJAK, and the [Iranian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Army)'s 328th Marivan Brigade at its base in [Sianav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sianav,_Marivan) left one Iranian soldier dead and another wounded. The attack reportedly involved [loitering munitions](53703349.md) and [RPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7) fire targeting the base.[^cite_note-45]

On 6 August, *Hengaw* reported that a veteran IRGC-affiliated local auxiliary had been killed in an attack on his family home. The attack was attributed to PJAK, although no group claimed responsibility.[^cite_note-46] The same day, the Sun of Hope organization claimed that its fighters had clashed with Iranian government forces in [West Azerbaijan province](47567329.md) over the previous 24 hours, alleging that they had inflicted casualties.[^cite_note-47]

On 8 August, two separate clashes in Gagesh, south of [Mahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabad), and [Sanandaj](29953972.md) resulted in the deaths of two IRGC members. The Sun of Hope organization was reportedly involved in both incidents.[^cite_note-48]

### September

On 8 September, an armed attack killed Eslam Kak Darvishi, who had served as an IRGC commander in Marivan, Baneh and Piranshahr in previous years, in [Piranshahr](30865712.md). On 13 September, a motorcycle [drive-by shooting](65683.md) targeted a Basij member and cleric affiliated with the IRGC in [Piranshahr county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piranshahr_County), killing him. Sun of Hope claimed responsibility for both attacks, while the [IRGC Provincial Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC_Provincial_Corps) in [Urmia](615663.md) confirmed the death of the Basij member and cleric and attributed the attack to Kurdish opposition parties.[^cite_note-49]

On 28 September, PJAK-aligned media claimed that the Kurdistan Martyrs' Revenge Teams killed Haidar Moradi, an active Basij member and Kurdish IRGC collaborator, in an assassination in the town of [Kamyaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamyaran).[^cite_note-50]

## Regional spillover

In early August, the Iranian Army claimed that the 23rd Airborne Special Forces Brigade had carried out 14 cross-border raids into the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) of [Iraq](7515928.md), killing several "anti-revolutionary elements." Neither the [Iraqi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_government) nor the [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Regional_Government) confirmed that the incursions had taken place and the reports remained unconfirmed.[^cite_note-51]

## Reactions

### Non-state actors

 [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK): In light of the clashes in late June, PKK commander [Murat Karayılan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murat_Karay%C4%B1lan) called on the Iranian government to pursue diplomacy rather than military action, stating that "a war \[...] between the Kurds and the Iranian state \[...] will not serve any side."[^cite_note-52]
