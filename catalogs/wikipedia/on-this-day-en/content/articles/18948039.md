The **Berlin International Film Festival** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Internationale Filmfestspiele Berlin*), usually called the **Berlinale** (German pronunciation: [\[bɛʁliˈnaːlə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a0/De-Berlinale2.ogg/De-Berlinale2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a0/De-Berlinale2.ogg/De-Berlinale2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), is an annual [film festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival) held in [Berlin](3354.md), Germany. Founded in 1951 and originally run in June, the festival has been held every February since 1978 and is one of Europe's "[Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival#Notable_festivals)" film festivals alongside the [Venice Film Festival](32497.md) held in Italy and the [Cannes Film Festival](101460.md) held in France. Furthermore, it is one of the "Big Five", the most prestigious film festivals in the world. The festival regularly draws tens of thousands of visitors each year.

About 400 films are shown at multiple venues across Berlin, mostly in and around [Potsdamer Platz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdamer_Platz). They are screened in nine sections across cinematic genres, with around twenty films competing for the festival's top awards in the Competition section. The major awards, called the [Golden Bear](21547166.md) and [Silver Bears](#Awards), are decided on by the international jury, chaired by an internationally recognisable cinema personality. This jury and other specialised Berlinale juries also give many other awards, and in addition there are other awards given by independent juries and organisations.

The **European Film Market** (**EFM**), a film trade fair held simultaneously to the Berlinale, is a major industry meeting for the international film circuit. The trade fair serves distributors, film buyers, producers, financiers and co-production agents. The [Berlinale Talents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlinale_Talents), a week-long series of lectures and workshops, is a gathering of young filmmakers held in partnership with the festival.

## History



Delphi Filmpalast

### First festival

During the peak of the [Cold War](325329.md) in 1950, [Oscar Martay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Martay), a film officer of the Information Service Branch of the American [High Commissioner for Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_High_Commission) stationed in [West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Berlin), proposed the idea of a [film festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival) in West Berlin.[^cite_note-germ-1][^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-Oscar_Martay-4] The proposal was put through a committee, which included members of the [Senate of West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Berlin) and people from the [West German film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_film_industry), on 9 October 1950.[^cite_note-Oscar_Martay-4] Through his efforts and influence, the American military administration was persuaded to assist and to give loans for the first years of the Berlin International Film Festival, which commenced in June 1951.[^cite_note-Oscar_Martay-4][^cite_note-germ-1][^cite_note-Britan-5] Film historian Dr. Alfred Bauer was the festival's first director, a position he would hold until 1976.[^cite_note-6]

Alfred Hitchcock's *[Rebecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_\(1940_film\))* opened the first festival at the [Titania-Palast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titania-Palast) in [Steglitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steglitz) on 6 June 1951. The festival ran from 6 to 17 June,[^cite_note-beginnings-7] with [Waldbühne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldb%C3%BChne) being another festival venue.[^cite_note-beginnings-7][^cite_note-Oscar_Martay-4]

The winners of the inaugural awards in 1951 were determined by a West German panel, and there were five winners of the [Golden Bear](21547166.md), divided by categories and genres.[^cite_note-8] *[Cinderella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_\(1950_film\))*, which won the Golden Bear for a Music Film,[^cite_note-9] also won the audience award.[^cite_note-beginnings-7]

### Early years and awards

The [FIAPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIAPF) (Fédération Internationale des Associations de Producteurs de Films) banned the awarding of jury prizes at the festival,[^cite_note-beginnings-7] so between 1952 and 1955, the winners of the Golden Bear were determined by the audience members.[^cite_note-jury-10] In 1956, FIAPF formally accredited the festival[^cite_note-beginnings-7] and since then the Golden Bear has been awarded by an international jury.[^cite_note-jury-10]

Prior to the erection of the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall) in 1961, a selection of the films were also screened in [East Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Berlin).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-germ-1]

The fifth edition in 1955 saw the first (West) German film to win the Golden Bear, [Robert Siodmak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Siodmak)'s *[Die Ratten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Ratten)*.[^cite_note-SI-12]

In 1957, the [Zoo Palast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo_Palast) became the main venue for the festival, and remained so until the move to Potsdamer Platz in 2000 (see [Venues](#Venues) below).[^cite_note-germ-1]

### 1960s



[Charlton Heston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlton_Heston) speaking to [Magda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_el-Sabahi) during the [11th Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), 1961

[^cite_note-13]

In 1963, two years after the Berlin Wall had been erected, a daily show of the Berlinale was shown on television in East Germany, with five films in competition broadcast.[^cite_note-SI-12][^cite_note-14]

Controversy arose in 1964 with the rejection by Bauer, on insistence from the church, of [Vilgot Sjöman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilgot_Sj%C3%B6man)'s second film, *[491](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/491_\(film\))*, from the competition.[^cite_note-SI-12][^cite_note-15]

[Werner Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Herzog)'s first feature film, *[Lebenszeichen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebenszeichen)*, premiered at the festival in 1968.[^cite_note-16]

### 1970s

The [20th edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) of the festival in 1970 was cut short and awards not issued following controversy over the showing of [Michael Verhoeven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Verhoeven)'s anti-war film *[o.k.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O.k._\(film\))*.[^cite_note-beginnings-7] The jury, headed by American film director [George Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stevens), decided after a 7–2 vote to remove the film from the competition, justifying their decision by citing a [FIAPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIAPF) guideline that said: "All film festivals should contribute to better understanding between nations". Stevens claimed that the film, which includes a [gang rape](1371871.md) of a Vietnamese woman by American soldiers during the [Vietnam War](32611.md), was [anti-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti_Americanism).[^cite_note-17] One jury member, [Dušan Makavejev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Makavejev), protested against this measure, stood up for the film and supported Verhoeven and producer [Rob Houwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Houwer).[^cite_note-18] Verhoeven defended his film by stating in these terms: "I have not made an anti-American film... The biggest part of the American people today is against the war in Vietnam".[^cite_note-NYT-19] Other directors taking part in the festival withdrew their films in protest, and the jury was accused of censorship and eventually disbanded, so no prizes were awarded and the competition was suspended.[^cite_note-20]

This scandal had such a big effect that it was unclear if the festival would continue to take place the next year.[^cite_note-21] The following year, the [festival was re-formed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) and a new International Forum for New Cinema was created.[^cite_note-beginnings-7]

1975 saw the first East German film to be entered into the festival, *[Jacob the Liar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_the_Liar_\(1975_film\))*.[^cite_note-SI-12]

At the premiere of *[In the Realm of the Senses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Realm_of_the_Senses)* in 1976, the film was confiscated in the projection room by West Berlin police and the "Forum" management criminally accused of the public screening a pornographic film.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-SI-12]

Bauer was succeeded by film journalist Wolf Donner in 1976,[^cite_note-23] who gave German films higher priority.[^cite_note-beginnings-7] After his first Berlinale in June 1977, Donner successfully negotiated the shift of the festival from the June to February (22 February – 5 March 1978), a change which has remained ever since.[^cite_note-24]

That festival, the [28th edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), saw the jury award the Golden Bear to Spain for its contribution to the festival rather than a specific film.[^cite_note-beginnings-7] The three Spanish films which were screened at the festival and won it were short film *[Ascensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascensor)* directed by Tomás Muñoz and feature films *[La palabras de Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Max_Said)* by [Emilio Martínez Lázaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Mart%C3%ADnez_L%C3%A1zaro) and *[Las truchas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_truchas)* by [José Luis García Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Garc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez).[^cite_note-25] The 1978 festival also saw the start of the European Film Market[^cite_note-beginnings-7] as well as a new section, "Cinema for People Six and up" (which later became "Kinderfilmfest").[^cite_note-SI-12]



Berlinale Palast (aka Theater am [Potsdamer Platz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdamer_Platz)), main venue since 2000

Just before the 1979 festival, Donner announced that he was resigning. The festival also saw further controversy that year when the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and other socialist states withdrew their films and delegates in protest at the selection of *[The Deer Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deer_Hunter)* and their solidarity with "the heroic people of Vietnam".[^cite_note-SI-12][^cite_note-26]

### 1980–2000

Donner was followed by [Moritz de Hadeln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_de_Hadeln), who held the position from 1980[^cite_note-27] until director [Dieter Kosslick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Kosslick) took over in 2001.[^cite_note-28]

In 1981, de Hadeln only nominated one (West) German film for the competition, *[Der Neger Erwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Neger_Erwin)*, and other West German producers and directors called for his resignation and proposed a boycott although no boycott took place.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-SI-12]

In 1986, [Gina Lollobrigida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Lollobrigida) was invited to head the jury at the [36th Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), which awarded the Golden Bear to [Reinhard Hauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Hauff)'s film *[Stammheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stammheim_\(film\))*. She said the majority decision was "prefabricated", and opposed it.[^cite_note-Berlinale_1986-30][^cite_note-SI-12]

### 21st century



[Tricia Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_Tuttle), Artistic Director of Berlinale (2024)

Kosslick started making some changes to the festival, moving the emphasis from [Hollywood](418334.md) in order to focus more on German and international cinema. He introduced various events to assist the development of emerging talent in German cinema.[^cite_note-germ-1] In 2010, for the [60th edition of the festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), Werner Herzog was appointed president of the jury. Also in that year, the city of Berlin unveiled its [Boulevard of the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_der_Stars), with the first of 40 stars devoted to actors and filmmakers of the German-speaking film and TV industry. First to be honoured in the Boulevard was German-American actress [Marlene Dietrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlene_Dietrich).[^cite_note-germ-1] In 2012 the 100th anniversary of the historic [Studio Babelsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_Babelsberg) was celebrated at the [62nd edition of the festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/62nd_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), with the screening of 10 classic films made at the studios.[^cite_note-germ-1]

A new Series section, devoted to longform [television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_series), was introduced in 2015.[^cite_note-specialseries-31]

In June 2018, it was announced that Mariette Rissenbeek would serve as the new executive director alongside artistic director Carlo Chatrian. They assumed their posts after Kosslick's final edition in 2019. Rissenbeek became the first woman to lead the Berlinale.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-ScreenD-18b-33]

A shortened [71st festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/71st_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) took place virtually in March 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

[73rd Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/73rd_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) held in February 2023, was the first completely in-person Berlinale since [the 70th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) in 2020.[^cite_note-36]

[Tricia Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_Tuttle) took over as the new artistic director in April 2024; the 75th Berlinale 2025 was her first festival.[^cite_note-37]

## Description and governance

The Berlinale is considered one of the five [major film festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival#Notable_festivals) in the world, alongside [Venice](32497.md), [Cannes](101460.md), [Sundance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival) and [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival),[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] and is the largest based on attendance. As of 2020, around 325,000 tickets were sold, and nearly 16,000 film industry professionals from 130 countries attended the festival.[^cite_note-germ-1] It is held in Berlin.[^cite_note-40] It attracts tens of thousands of visitors each year. For the 2022 event, still feeling the effects of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), 156,472 tickets were sold.[^cite_note-\:1-41]

About 400 films are shown in several sections across cinematic genres, with around twenty films competing for the festival's top awards, the [Golden Bear](21547166.md) and Silver Bears.[^cite_note-\:1-41]

In 2022, festival was receiving €10.3 million from the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media. There was consideration given by the federal government to help compensate for revenue lost and additional expenditure owing to the pandemic, with funds drawn from the Neustart Kultur programme.[^cite_note-\:1-41]

Since 2019, Mariette Rissenbeek has been the festival's executive director; [Carlo Chatrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Chatrian) is its [artistic director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artistic_director).[^cite_note-ScreenD-18-42] In September 2023, it was reported that artistic director Carlo Chatrian will relinquish his post after 2024 Berlinale.[^cite_note-carlo\:hr-43] On 12 December 2023 it was announced by the German culture ministry that [Tricia Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_Tuttle), formerly director of the [BFI London Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_London_Film_Festival), will be the sole director of the Berlinale from April 2024.[^cite_note-tricia\:hr-44]

## Entries

The festival is open to films of every length and genre, but there is priority given to international and European premieres, and the films need to have been completed within the year preceding the festival. Submissions open in September of the preceding year.[^cite_note-asef-45]

As of 2025 the festival is composed of eight different sections:[^cite_note-berlinale-46]

- **Competition**: feature-length films yet to be released outside their country of origin, which compete for several prizes, including the top [Golden Bear](21547166.md) for the best film and a series of Silver Bears for acting, writing and production

  [^cite_note-compberlinale-47]

- **Berlinale Special** (a diverse selection of films, events and people) & **Berlinale Series** (for television series)

  [^cite_note-specialseries-31]

- **Perspectives** a juried competition for first feature films from international filmmakers

  [^cite_note-persp-48]

- **Berlinale Shorts**, for [short films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film) since 2007 a separate section; short films were honoured with Golden and Silver Bears from 1955, with a separate jury for shorts established in 2003

  [^cite_note-49]

- **Panorama**: originally known as "Information show" screening innovative, mostly independent, films. In 1986 it was renamed "Panorama" showing films that "break with convention and tackle contemporary themes in a novel way, discover new talents or new cinematographic style."

  [^cite_note-Berlinale_1986-30]

  [^cite_note-SI-12]

  &#x20;It is currently described as "explicitly [queer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTI), explicitly [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist), explicitly political"

  [^cite_note-50]

- **Forum & Forum Expanded**: reflections on the medium of film; a selection of around 40 films, independently curated and organised by [Arsenal Institute for Film and Video Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_Institute_for_Film_and_Video_Art) as part of the Berlinale, since 1971

  [^cite_note-51]

  &#x20;Barbara Wurm, the author and curator, was appointed new head of Berlinale Forum on 7 March 2023 succeeding Cristina Nord, taking charge as of 1 August 2023.

  [^cite_note-52]

- **Generation**: comprising Generation Kplus and Generation 14plus, two competition programmes screening international cinema exploring the worlds of children and teenagers; started in 1978 with a selection "Cinema for People Six and up"; then Kinderfilmfest ("Children's Film Festival"); expanded to include the 14plus competition in 2004; renamed Generation in 2007, with the two sections

  [^cite_note-53]

- **Retrospective, Berlinale Classics & Homage**, established in 1977, curated by the [Deutsche Kinemathek – Museum für Film und Fernsehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Kinemathek_%E2%80%93_Museum_f%C3%BCr_Film_und_Fernsehen), and awarder of the Honorary Golden Bear for a lifetime's achievement in filmmaking

  [^cite_note-homage-54]

"Perspektive Deutsches Kino" (Perspectives on German Cinema) was created in 2002 by incoming director [Dieter Kosslick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Kosslick) with Alfred Holighaus.[^cite_note-55] This was dropped from 2024 Berlinale due to budget cuts.[^cite_note-carlo\:hr-43][^cite_note-56]

A section called "Culinary Cinema" had also been introduced by Kosslick in 2007, as well as a series called "NATIVe" (for indigenous filmmakers) in 2013; however, these were dropped after his departure in 2019.[^cite_note-change-57]

A section titled *Encounters* which was designed to foster "daring works" initiated in 2020, running until it was replaced by *Perspectives* in 2025.[^cite_note-change-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-persp-48]

## Awards

See also: [Golden Bear](21547166.md)



The Silver Bear statue



[Jafar Panahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafar_Panahi) with Silver Bear, [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival)&#x20;

The [Golden Bear](21547166.md) (*Goldener Bär*) is the highest prize awarded for the best film at the Berlin International Film Festival. In its first year in 1951, it was awarded to the best film in each of five categories,[^cite_note-juries-59] by an all-German jury.[^cite_note-brief-60]

From 1952 to 1955, the Golden and Silver Bears were awarded by audience voting, as the [FIAPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIAPF) had determined after the first festival that only the [Cannes](101460.md) and the [Venice](32497.md) film festivals were allowed to appoint official juries.[^cite_note-juries-59][^cite_note-61] A Silver Bear (*Silberner Bär*) and a Bronze Berlin Bear, determined by audience vote, were also awarded from 1952 to 1955.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] After the FIAPF ruled to allow it, an official international jury determined the prizes from 1956 onwards,[^cite_note-juries-59] and in the same year a second Golden Bear was added, for best [short film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film), as well as a second category of award, the Silver Bear, for individual achievements in acting or directing. In 1965, a runner-up prize to the Golden Bear was added.[^cite_note-brief-60]

The statuettes awarded as trophies are based on the *Bär* first created by sculptor [Renée Sintenis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e_Sintenis) (1888–1965) in 1932. The bear, based on the [coat of arms of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Berlin) and depicting a bear standing on its hind legs with its arms raised, became popular in the 1930s, bringing wealth to Sintenis. Since the [3rd edition of the festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) in 1953, replicas of the bear have been produced by the [Noack Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noack_Foundry).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-camera-65]

### International jury prizes

The main prizes in the festival are those awarded by the international jury since 1956, which today include the [Golden Bear](21547166.md) and various Silver Bears.[^cite_note-intjury-66] In 1956, apart from the Golden Bear, there were also Silver Bears awarded by the new international feature film jury for best director, best actress, best actor, best outstanding single achievement, outstanding artistic contribution, and a Silver Bear International Prize.[^cite_note-67]

As of 2022, the Golden Bear for Best Film is awarded to the [producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_producer) of the best [feature film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_film).[^cite_note-intjury-66]

As of 2022, the categories of Silver Bear awards are:[^cite_note-intjury-66]

- [Silver Bear Grand Jury Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_Grand_Jury_Prize)

- [Silver Bear Jury Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_Jury_Prize)

- [Silver Bear for Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Director)

- [Silver Bear for Best Leading Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Leading_Performance)

- [Silver Bear for Best Supporting Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Supporting_Performance)

- [Silver Bear for Best Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Screenplay)

- [Silver Bear for Outstanding Artistic Contribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Outstanding_Artistic_Contribution)

### Other Berlinale awards

The [Honorary Golden Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Golden_Bear) has been awarded for lifetime achievement since 1982, when it was awarded to [James Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stewart).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] It is presented to someone with an exceptional artistic career, and is given to the guest of honour of the Homage section[^cite_note-70] which has been run since 1977 by the Berlinale and the [Deutsche Kinemathek – Museum für Film und Fernsehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Kinemathek_%E2%80%93_Museum_f%C3%BCr_Film_und_Fernsehen).[^cite_note-homage-54]

Awards for [short films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film) are awarded by a separate international short film jury consisting of three filmmakers and artists. As of 2022, the short film award are:[^cite_note-71]

- [Golden Bear for Best Short Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Film_Golden_Bear)

- [Silver Bear for Best Short Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Short_Film)

- Berlin Short Film Candidate for the European Film Awards

## Related events

### European Film Market



&#x20;[Martin-Gropius-Bau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin-Gropius-Bau)

The European Film Market (EFM) is a large trade fair for marketing films, which grew from an event started in 1978.[^cite_note-beginnings-7][^cite_note-128]

Filmmesse was an event led by Aina Bellis from 1980 to 1987, being succeeded by Beki Probst in 1988. From 2014 to October 2020, [Matthijs Wouter Knol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthijs_Wouter_Knol) took over the position. In November 2020, Dennis Ruh became the director of the EFM.[^cite_note-129]

It has grown into one of three largest movie markets in the world, and is the first film market of the year;[^cite_note-profile-130] the [Marché du Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March%C3%A9_du_Film) in [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes) follows in May, and the [American Film Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Market) in November.

EFM provides exhibition space for companies presenting their current line-up, organising screenings of new films in venues around [Potsdamer Platz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdamer_Platz). In 2007, the CinemaxX and [CineStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CineStar) were used to showcase new productions. In 2010, the Astor Film Lounge showed market screenings in three dimensions using digital RealD technology.

It is a professional trade event, open to registered industry insiders, hosting up to 10,000 representatives of the international film and media industries (mostly [producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_producer), sales agents, distributors and financiers).[^cite_note-profile-130] In 2020, 971 screenings of 732 registered movies took place, with 525 films celebrating their premiere.[^cite_note-131] Taking place over eight days, the event is spread across several locations, including the [Gropius Bau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gropius_Bau), [Marriott Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriott_Hotel), modern [Berliner Freiheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berliner_Freiheit?action=edit\&redlink=1) or the historic [Zoo Palast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo_Palast).[^cite_note-profile-130]

### Berlinale Talents

Main article: [Berlinale Talents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlinale_Talents)



[Wim Wenders](32902.md) attended the Talent Campus as a lecturer.

Commencing in 2003, the Berlinale has partnered with the [Berlinale Talents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlinale_Talents) (previously Berlinale Talent Campus), which is a winter school for "up-and-coming filmmakers" that takes place at the same time as the festival. The Talent Campus accepts about 250 applicants each year; the attendees come from around the world, and represent all of the filmmaking professions.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-brief-60]

The event runs six days during the Berlinale and features lectures and panel discussions with well-known professionals addressing issues in filmmaking. Workshops, excursions, personal tutoring, coaching, and training of participants from different fields of work are part of the programme.[^cite_note-133]

The proceedings include presentations by experts,[^cite_note-134] who have included [Park Chan-wook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chan-wook), [Frances McDormand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_McDormand), [Stephen Frears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Frears), [Dennis Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Hopper), [Jia Zhangke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jia_Zhangke), [Walter Murch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Murch), [Shah Rukh Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh_Khan), [Joshua Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Oppenheimer), [Anthony Minghella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Minghella), [Charlotte Rampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Rampling), [Walter Salles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Salles), [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott), [Raoul Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Peck), [Tom Tykwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Tykwer), [Mike Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Leigh), [Tilda Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilda_Swinton), and [Wim Wenders](32902.md). Many of these presentations and lectures are archived, both as video recordings and as transcripts, on the Talents website.[^cite_note-135]

### Berlinale Co-Production Market

The Berlinale Co-Production Market is a five-day networking platform for producers and financiers, as well as broadcasting and funding representatives who are participating in international co-productions.[^cite_note-136] It was introduced by Dieter Kosslick in the 2000s.[^cite_note-change-57]

## World Cinema Fund

The World Cinema Fund (WCF) is associated with the Berlinale, and was established to provide financial support to [feature film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_film) projects in countries with a weak film industry infrastructure.[^cite_note-asef-45][^cite_note-140] It was established by [Dieter Kosslick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Kosslick)[^cite_note-change-57] in 2004 to support films "that stand out with an unconventional aesthetic approach, that tell powerful stories and transmit an authentic image of their cultural roots". It awards several projects in various stages of production with funding each year.[^cite_note-kazakh-141]

The WCF is a collaboration with the Federal Foundation for Culture, and awarded in cooperation with the [Goethe Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_Institute), the Foreign Ministry and German producers. It aims "to develop and support cinema in regions with a weak film infrastructure, while fostering cultural diversity in German cinemas", and supports films that could not be made without extra funding. It provides funding for production and distribution of feature films and feature-length documentaries, with a focus on countries in [Latin America](18524.md), [Central America](6121.md), the [Caribbean](18956035.md), Africa, the [Middle East](19323.md), [Central Asia](6742.md), [Southeast Asia](28741.md), and [the Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Caucasus), as well as Bangladesh, Nepal, [Mongolia](19271.md), and Sri Lanka.[^cite_note-wcfhome-142]
