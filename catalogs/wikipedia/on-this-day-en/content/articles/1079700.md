**Kayes** (IPA: [\[kaj\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language): ߞߊ߬ߦߋ߫, romanized: *Kàyǐ*; [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_language): *Xaayi*) is a city in western [Mali](19127.md) on the [Sénégal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9n%C3%A9gal_River) with a population of 127,368 at the 2009 census. Kayes is the capital of the [administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayes_Region) of the same name. The city is located 420 kilometres (260 mi) northwest of the capital [Bamako](56606.md).

## Toponymy

There are multiple possible etymologies of the name 'Kayes', all derived from the [Soninke language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_language). These include: the word *kharré*, which describes a low humid place that floods in rainy season; the word *kayé*, a type of grass;[^cite_note-carenews-2] *khayé*, the Soninke name for the [rhun palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_palm).[^cite_note-maliweb-3]

## History

The area around Kayes was historically a part of the [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people) states of [Diarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Diarra) and [Gajaaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajaaga). The important trading center of [Goundiourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goundiourou), known in Arabic sources as Ghiyaru, was just across the river, and was later rebuilt just south of the modern city.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Monteil-5]



Kayes in 1910



Children at the Kayes [hostage school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostage_schools), founded by [Joseph Gallieni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gallieni) (1887–1888)

Kayes itself was founded in the second half of the 19th century. Guéssé Sidy, a prince of [Khasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasso), established a base there to protect the western approaches to the kingdom's capital, [Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medine,_Mali).[^cite_note-carenews-2][^cite_note-maliweb-3]

Prior to French colonial expansion, Kayes was still a small village. In 1881 the French chose it as the site for a fort and barracks, the headquarters of the colonial presence in the central Sudan, as Medina was difficult to access by boat. By 1886 the post had been fortified, and by 1889 a market town of 6,000 people had grown up around it.[^cite_note-Bah-6]



Early 20th-century view of the railway station

From the beginning, the French began building a [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) linking Kayes, the furthest point reachable by boat on the [Senegal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River), with [Bafoulabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafoulabe) upstream. This stretch was completed in 1890, and reached to [Bamako](56606.md) in 1904. A [rail link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Niger_Railway) to [Thiès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thi%C3%A8s) was begun in 1907 which, interrupted by the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), was completed in 1923.[^cite_note-7]

In 1892, Kayes became the capital of the [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan); Bamako replaced it as the capital, first of the state of [Haut Sénégal-Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut_S%C3%A9n%C3%A9gal-Niger) on October 17, 1899, then as the capital of all of French Sudan in 1908.

During [World War II](32927.md), a portion of [Poland](22936.md)'s [gold reserve](1056205.md), evacuated during the [German-Soviet invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) in 1939, was stored for several years in Kayes before being transported to the United States in 1944, to be returned to Poland after the [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) and war ended.[^cite_note-8]

On 1 July 2025, [Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal-Muslimin](53401088.md) launched an attack on the city, as well as a few other nearby areas. The [Malian Army](19135.md) reported that they successful countered the assaults and over 80 militants were killed.[^cite_note-9]

## Economy and transport

Kayes lies on the Route Nationale 1 (RN1) highway and is 612 km (380 mi) by road from Bamako and 96 km (60 mi) from the border with [Senegal](27278.md). The town has an international airport (Kayes Airport), and lies on the [Dakar-Niger Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Niger_Railway) which offered regional passenger train service to [Bamako](56606.md) three times a week via Kati and Diamou as of 2013.[^cite_note-10] The area is rich in gold and iron.

## Climate

Kayes has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*). The climate is subject to the [West African Monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Monsoon) with all the rainfall occurring between June and October. August is the wettest month. There is almost no rainfall during the other seven months of the year. The total annual rainfall is around 650 millimetres or 26 inches.[^cite_note-wwis-11] Kayes is nicknamed the "pressure cooker of Africa" due to its extreme heat; the town is surrounded by iron-rich mountains which contribute to the temperature. The town has been described as the hottest continuously inhabited town in Africa. The average daily high temperature in the city is 36.4 °C (97.5 °F), with temperatures usually peaking in April and May at an average of nearly 42 °C (108 °F).[^cite_note-12]

On 3 April 2024, a temperature of 48.5 °C (119.3 °F) was recorded in Kayes; this is the highest temperature that has ever been reported in Mali.[^cite_note-13]

## Area

Sites found in and around Kayes include:

- [Fort du Médine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Medina_Fort)

- [Félou Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lou_Falls), 15 km (9.3 mi) upstream on the Senegal river

- [Gouina Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouina_Falls), 100 km (62 mi) to the southeast on the Senegal river

- The *[tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_\(fortification\))* of Koniakari, constructed by [El Hadj Umar Tall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hadj_Umar_Tall), 70 km (43 mi) to the northeast

- Lake Magui and Lake Doro, both watered by the [Kolimbiné River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolimbin%C3%A9_River)

- The [Manantali Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manantali_Dam)
