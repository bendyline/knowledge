The **Saskatchewan Roughriders** are a professional [Canadian football](6963.md) team based in [Regina, Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina,_Saskatchewan). The Roughriders compete in the [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) (CFL) as a member club of the league's [West Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Division_\(CFL\)). The Roughriders were founded in 1910 as the **Regina Rugby Club**. Although not the first football team in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada), the club has maintained an unbroken organizational continuity since their founding. The Roughriders are the third-oldest professional [gridiron football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridiron_football) team in existence today (only the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals) and [Toronto Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Argonauts) are older). The Roughriders are both the oldest professional sports team still in existence that continuously has been based in Western Canada and the oldest in North America to continuously have been based west of [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri). The team changed their name to the **Regina Roughriders** in 1924, and to the current moniker in 1946. The Roughriders played their home games at historic [Taylor Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_Stadium_at_Taylor_Field) from [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_in_Canadian_football) to 2016; in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), the team moved to the newly constructed [Mosaic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_Stadium) at Evraz Place.

The team draws fans from across [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan) and Canada who are affectionately known as the **Rider Nation**.[^cite_note-4] The Roughriders play in the smallest market in the CFL, and the second-smallest major-league market in North America ([Green Bay, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin) is smaller). They have finished first in what is now the West Division nine times and have won the Western championship a record 29 times. They have played for the [Grey Cup](230107.md) 20 times, winning five ([1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Grey_Cup), [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Grey_Cup), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Grey_Cup), [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Grey_Cup), [2025](73288547.md)).

The team has had 20 players inducted into the [Canadian Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_Hall_of_Fame). The Riders' biggest rival is the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers); games between the two are often sold out before the beginning of the season.[^cite_note-5] The Roughriders Football Club and the city of Regina have hosted the Grey Cup four times ([1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83rd_Grey_Cup), [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91st_Grey_Cup), [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Grey_Cup),[^cite_note-6] [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/109th_Grey_Cup)), and will host a fifth in 2027.[^cite_note-7]

## Community ownership

### Ownership

The Saskatchewan Roughrider Football Club Inc. was established in 1910 and incorporated in 1940.[^cite_note-\:1-8] The organization is registered under the Non-Profit Corporations Act of Saskatchewan.[^cite_note-\:1-8] The Roughriders are one of two CFL teams with non-profit ownership, the other one being the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers),[^cite_note-9] and are among a select group in [North American sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fan-owned_sports_teams). Prior to 2004, the club operated as a corporation without share capital (no person or entity "owned" the team or any portion of it), an ownership structure the Blue Bombers have used continuously.

### Membership shares

Since 2004, the Roughriders have offered two classes of permanent Membership Interests (referred to as "Membership Shares") similar to the [NFL's](21211.md) [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers). [Class A shares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_A_share) have voting rights and [Class B shares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_B_share) have no voting rights. As of March 2019, the Roughriders had issued 11,639 Class A shares and 435 Class B shares, though the total number of individual owners is not publicly disclosed.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

It is not possible to resell these shares, no dividend payment is possible and no person may hold more than 20 voting (Class A) shares, though they may hold an unlimited number of non-voting shares. The initial public offering of Rider Shares (Series I) commenced in 2004 at an offering price of \$250 per share[^cite_note-10] In 2006 the *[Ottawa Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Sun)* reported that the Roughriders had sold around 3,000 at \$250 each.[^cite_note-11] The Series 1 offering closed in 2008 after all 6,000 shares were sold. A second public offering, Series II, was launched in 2010 in honour of the team's 100th anniversary.[^cite_note-12] As of 2016, the Roughriders had released and subsequently sold out 1,989 shares of Series III at \$250 each. this Series "commemorates the 1989 Grey Cup championship victory" and "features the likenesses of [Kent Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Austin), [Bobby Jurasin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Jurasin) and [Don Narcisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Narcisse) with an illustration of the championship winning kick in the background."[^cite_note-13] Series IV shares began sale in 2017, with the price remaining consistent at \$250.[^cite_note-14]

When shares are purchased, the funds are placed in the team's Stabilization Fund.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

### Saskatchewan Roughrider Foundation

The Saskatchewan Roughrider Foundation was created in 2019.[^cite_note-\:2-15] It raises and distributes funds, focusing on community pillars of education, health, and amateur football.[^cite_note-\:2-15] The Foundation aims to teach youth skills such as leadership, resilience, and responsibility.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

## History

### Club origins, Regina Rugby Club (1910–1923)



1910 Regina Rugby Club

The team was founded as the *Regina Rugby Club* on Tuesday, September 13, 1910, adopting the colours of old gold and purple.[^cite_note-1910s-16] The team was also a founding member of the [Saskatchewan Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_Rugby_Football_Union) as it was organized on September 22 of that year. Regina played their first game against the Moose Jaw Tigers on October 1, 1910, at the Moose Jaw Baseball Grounds where they were defeated 16–6. After going winless in their inaugural season, Regina quickly became the strongest team in the province. For the [1911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_in_Canadian_football) season, the team changed their colours to blue and white to match the Regina Amateur Athletic Association.

Regina went 3–1 in their second season and captured their first SRFU championship by winning a [one game tiebreaker playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-game_playoff) against Moose Jaw. Meanwhile, the [Western Canada Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada_Rugby_Football_Union) had been formed within the three [prairie provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_provinces) with the intent of creating a playoff format to determine a Western champion.[^cite_note-Sask_History-17] Much to the displeasure of the SRFU, the Albertan and Manitoban unions had agreed to a format (ostensibly to minimize travel) in which the MRFU champion would host the Western semi-final and the ARFU champion would host the Western final. Regina balked at the prospect of playing up to two road games, and refused to participate in the inaugural WCRFU playoffs.

The Regina Rugby Club changed their colours again in [1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_in_Canadian_football) to red and black. Meanwhile, the WCRFU agreed to change the playoff format so that the SRFU champion was given a bye to the Western final in exchange for the Saskatchewan champion agreeing to play the championship game on the road. Regina began an era of western football dominance.[^cite_note-1910s-16] From [1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_in_Canadian_football) through [1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_in_Canadian_football), Regina not only won six consecutive WCRFU titles, but went undefeated in competitive play during that span. Due to [World War I](4764461.md), no Western playoffs were held in 1916 and in 1917 and 1918 league play was halted altogether.

In [1921](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_in_Canadian_football), the Western champion was invited to compete for the [Grey Cup](230107.md) national championship for the first time. Regina again went undefeated in the regular season, but were required to play the [Saskatoon Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatoon_Quakers_\(football\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) for the provincial championship as travel difficulties had prevented Saskatoon from playing the other southern teams. Regina defeated Saskatoon, but the result was overturned after a successful protest concerning an early touchdown which had provided the decisive points. The Quakers won the rematch to mark the only time other than their inaugural season that the Regina Rugby Club did not win the SRFU championship while it was their primary competition. Ultimately, the [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Elks) travelled east to play in the [9th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Grey_Cup).[^cite_note-1920s-18]

The team's rise to early prominence came even as it led a somewhat nomadic existence. It originally played at Dominion Park, but after the war was forced to move to the city exhibition grounds after the city sold Dominion Park. In 1921, it moved to Park Hughes on 10th Avenue in the North Central section of Regina, a rugby and soccer field built in 1910. It was the first season at an address that remained associated with the club for parts of the next nine decades.[^cite_note-LeaderPost1-19][^cite_note-20]

In [1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_in_Canadian_football), Regina returned to power as they won their eighth western championship over the [Winnipeg Victorias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Victorias_Rugby_Club) and earned the right to compete in the national playoffs. The club was given a [bye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_\(sports\)) and advanced straight to the [Grey Cup finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Grey_Cup) for the first time, but were severely outmatched, losing 54–0 to [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) at [Varsity Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_Stadium) in [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-1920s-18] This was, and still is, the most lopsided defeat in [Grey Cup history](230107.md#Team_scoring_records) as the defending champion Queen's won their third straight national championship at the expense of the Regina Rugby Club.[^cite_note-Grey_Cup_Records-21]

### Regina Roughriders (1924–1945)

Following their first [Grey Cup](230107.md) loss, the club changed their name to the *Regina Roughriders* in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_in_Canadian_football) while retaining the colours of red and black.[^cite_note-1920s-18] [Ottawa](22219.md) also had a team called the [Ottawa Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rough_Riders), but the spelling was different and the two clubs played in [different leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interprovincial_Rugby_Football_Union) then (incidentally, however, both clubs shared the same colours of red and black then). The origin of the name has multiple theories, the most credible of which describes how the [North-West Mounted Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Mounted_Police) were called Roughriders because they broke the wild horse broncos that were used by the force and the moniker was adopted from them.[^cite_note-22] Giving credence to this theory is that during this time, the team occasionally played at the RNWMP/RCMP barracks when the then-rudimentary facilities at Park Hughes were rendered unsuitable for play by inclement weather. For a long time, the playing surface at Park Hughes was little more than dirt, and heavy rain often turned the field to a muddy bog.

An alternative, discredited story states that the name was adopted from [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt)'s [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry) contingent that was known as the [Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Riders), who fought in the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War). It was believed that there were Canadian troops in the contingent who returned to Canada following the war. However, this story is more often associated with the Ottawa club. While it has been suggested that some of the troops may have eventually moved out [west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada),[^cite_note-Sask_History-17] the Roughriders did not adopt their name until 1924, by which time the Spanish–American War was (especially in Canada) barely a historical footnote compared to the then-much more recent (and much more climactic) [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). Following World War I, any attempt to deliberately name a Canadian sports team in honour of a unit that participated in an earlier American war would have been extremely unpopular.

During the first two years after their name change, the Roughriders failed to reclaim their western championship title, losing both times to clubs from [Winnipeg](100730.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The [1926 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_in_Canadian_football) marked the beginning of their next reign of dominance as the club matched their own WCRFU record with seven consecutive western championships from 1926 to 1932. With dominant players such as [Canadian Football Hall of Famer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_Hall_of_Fame) [Eddie James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_James_\(Canadian_football\)), the Roughriders were a perennial contender from the West, reaching the Grey Cup finals five consecutive years from 1928 to 1932, the second-longest streak in the championship's history (the team did not challenge for the Cup in 1926 or 1927).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Unfortunately, Regina remained winless in the national championship, being outscored 102–15 in those five Grey Cup games. The Roughriders won their last WCRFU title in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_in_Canadian_football), representing the west for the seventh time in the [22nd Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Grey_Cup), but lost to the [Sarnia Imperials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia_Imperials) in that club's first Grey Cup win.[^cite_note-27]

In 1928, Park Hughes was combined with Park de Young, a neighbouring baseball field, to create a much larger football venue. However, from 1929 to 1935, the Roughriders played most of their games at the larger exhibition grounds.

### Western Interprovincial Football Union (1936–1958)

In 1936, Regina joined the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers) and Calgary Bronks as the founding franchises of the [Western Interprovincial Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Interprovincial_Football_Union), the highest level of [Canadian football](6963.md) play in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada). Also in 1936, the first permanent grandstand was built at Park Hughes and Park de Young. The Riders moved to the facility full-time that season. The stadium, renamed [Taylor Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Field_\(Regina\)) in 1946, was the Riders' home for over eight decades. The Roughriders became the first WIFU champions after they defeated the Blue Bombers and Bronks in the West Semi-Finals and West Finals respectively. However, due to a rules dispute with the [Canadian Rugby Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Canada) over use of their five import players from the [United States](3434750.md), Regina was barred from competing for the [24th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_Grey_Cup).[^cite_note-Abandon_Final-28] Winnipeg had won the [Grey Cup](230107.md) championship [one year earlier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Grey_Cup) with seven imports and the move to prevent Regina from competing was seen as a reaction to the previous year's western win. While the Roughriders had planned on travelling east without the five ineligible players, the CRU remained steadfast in their decision to disallow the team from competition.[^cite_note-Abandon_Final-28][^cite_note-29]

The next decade in the WIFU was not as successful as the first as the team did not win another Western Final as the Regina Roughriders, nor ever finish in first place in that time span. After qualifying for the playoffs in three of their next five seasons, play from 1942 to 1944 was interrupted by [World War II](32927.md). While there was no regular season in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_in_Canadian_football), the Roughriders did play the newly named [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders) in the West Semi-Finals, but lost the series two games to none.[^cite_note-1940s-30]

With the folding of both clubs in Moose Jaw and Saskatoon, the Regina Roughriders became a provincially community-owned club (and has remained so since), and, consequently, changed their name to the *Saskatchewan Roughriders* in [1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_in_Canadian_football).[^cite_note-1940s-30] It is the first recorded instance of a major-league team in North America branding itself as a statewide or provincewide team. Prior to the [1948 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_in_Canadian_football), the Roughriders were in need of new uniforms as their red and black ones had become old and worn out. While visiting a surplus store in [Chicago](6886.md), executive member Jack Fyfe found a set of green and white uniforms and purchased them for the Roughriders. Green and white have remained as the team's primary colours to this day (although the team has also worn silver and black as accent colours since then at times).[^cite_note-Sask_History-17] The name change was made official on April 1, 1950.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-1950s-32]

After three years of first-round playoff exits, the Roughriders finally returned to prominence in [1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_in_Canadian_football), winning their first [WIFU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Interprovincial_Football_Union) regular season championship with an 8–6 record. Saskatchewan, led by quarterback [Glenn Dobbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Dobbs), defeated the [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Eskimos) in the West Final and advanced to the Grey Cup for the first time since [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Grey_Cup). In this game, they faced the [Ottawa Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rough_Riders) for the first time, marking the first Roughriders versus Rough Riders championship game in Canadian football history. Unfortunately, Saskatchewan still did not win their first championship, as they were defeated by Ottawa 21–14 in the [39th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39th_Grey_Cup).[^cite_note-33]

Saskatchewan contended on and off in the 1950s, with four consecutive winning seasons and second-place regular season finishes from 1953 to 1956. Teams from this era featured standouts such as [Frank Tripucka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Tripucka), Reggie Whitehouse, [Ken Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Carpenter_\(American_football\)), [Mike Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Cassidy_\(Canadian_football\)), player-coach [Frank Filchock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Filchock) and [Cookie Gilchrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Gilchrist) who was the first Roughrider player to rush for 1,000 yards in [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_CFL_season). Even with that talent, they could not return to the Grey Cup as clubs fielded by either the Edmonton Eskimos and Winnipeg Blue Bombers ended their season in each of these years. Their strongest season was in [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_in_Canadian_football) when the Roughriders achieved a 10–6 record and won their first playoff series since 1951, only to lose to the Eskimos in the Western Finals.

Following their 1956 campaign, tragedy struck the Roughriders franchise when four members of the team were killed in a plane crash on December 9, 1956, while returning from the Canadian Football Council (CFC) All Star Game in [Vancouver](32706.md). [Gordon Sturtridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Sturtridge_\(Canadian_football\)), [Mel Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Becket), [Ray Syrnyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Syrnyk), and [Mario DeMarco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_DeMarco) were killed when [Flight 810](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Canada_Air_Lines_Flight_810) crashed into [Slesse Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slesse_Mountain) near [Chilliwack, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilliwack,_British_Columbia). The team retired the numbers of the four players shortly after the [tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy_\(event\)).[^cite_note-34] The [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_in_Canadian_football), the Roughriders finished with seven fewer wins and a last place finish in the WIFU.

### Ken Preston Era (1958–1978)

1958 brought not only change, as the Saskatchewan Roughriders became charter members of the newly formed [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) in [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_CFL_season), but also stability with Ken Preston becoming the General Manager—a position he held for the next 20 years, arguably the most successful tenure in Roughrider history as the team only missed the playoffs five times, went to five Grey Cup final games, winning one in 1966. In the Roughriders' first season in the newly formed Canadian Football League, the team finished with a respectable 7–7–2 record and a third-place finish.[^cite_note-1950s-32] However, the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_CFL_season) proved to be the worst in [franchise history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_Roughriders_seasons), as the team finished with just one win and 15 losses under head coach Frank Tripucka, the third-worst winning percentage in [CFL history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFL_records_\(team\)#Standings).[^cite_note-team_records-35] The following years featured similar results, with various [head coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_head_coaches), as the Roughriders missed the playoffs for three consecutive seasons, their worst such streak since joining the WIFU in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_in_Canadian_football).

### Ronnie and George show (1963–1975)

Following a [1962 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_CFL_season) that saw the Roughriders return to the playoffs, the team made roster moves that defined a generation of football in Saskatchewan. In the off-season, the Roughriders signed [fullback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullback_\(American_football\)) [George Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Reed_\(Canadian_football\)) from [Washington State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Cougars_football) to replace Fred Burket, who had been traded to the [Alouettes](70601.md).[^cite_note-36] Then, prior to their season opening game of the [1963 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_CFL_season), general manager Ken Preston acquired [Ottawa Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rough_Riders) quarterback and defensive back [Ron Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Lancaster) on July 30 on a straight cash basis following three years with the Eastern Riders.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] The duo contributed to a productive season for Saskatchewan as they finished with a 7–7–2 record and won a playoff series for the first time since [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_in_Canadian_football) before losing their first playoff match-up with the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions). The Roughriders continued to make progress in the next two seasons, posting back-to-back winning records, but lost in the West Semi-Finals in both years.

### Eagle Keys Era (1965–1970)

#### 1966 Grey Cup champions

In 1965 after one year as the offensive coordinator, [Eagle Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Keys) became the head coach of the Roughriders and guided them to an 8–7–1 record and an appearance in the western semi final which they lost to the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers) 15–9. Fullback George Reed finished the season with over 1,700 yards rushing. Before the 1966 season began, the team added the final pieces to the roster by signing defensive tackle [Ed McQuarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_McQuarters) who was a recent cut by the St. Louis Cardinals, safety and backup quarterback Bruce Bennett, defensive end Don Gerhardt and running back Paul Dudley from the Stampeders. During the [1966 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) the Roughriders finally captured the [Western Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League_West_Division) regular season title with a 9–6–1 record. This was the first time they had accomplished that feat since [1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_in_Canadian_football). [Ron Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Lancaster) won the [Jeff Nicklin Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Nicklin_Memorial_Trophy) as the Western Conference's most outstanding player while [George Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Reed_\(Canadian_football\)), receiver [Hugh Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Campbell) and four other Riders were named league all-stars, the most from any team that year. Saskatchewan swept Winnipeg in the West Finals, winning two games to no losses, and qualified for the ninth [Grey Cup](230107.md) final in franchise history. In the [54th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Grey_Cup), Saskatchewan once again faced the [Ottawa Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rough_Riders) in a rematch of the [1951 championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39th_Grey_Cup). After the score was tied 14–14 at halftime, Saskatchewan scored 15 fourth-quarter points to win the franchise's first Grey Cup championship 29–14 on November 26, 1966.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Saskatchewan was the last of the original nine CFL franchises to win the Grey Cup, doing so in [Vancouver](32706.md) at [Empire Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Stadium_\(Vancouver\)). George Reed was the MVP with 31 rushes for 133 yards and one rushing touchdown.

The Roughriders began the [1967 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_CFL_season) as defending champions for the first time in franchise history. They finished in second place in the West with a franchise-best 12–4 record and advanced to [Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/55th_Grey_Cup) final once again, but lost to the [Hamilton Tiger-Cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Tiger-Cats) 24–1.[^cite_note-41] The [1968 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_CFL_season) saw the Roughriders finish with the best record in the league at 12–3–1, although they placed 1st in the west division they lost the western final series to the [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders) 2–0 by a combined score of 57–12. Besides the 1st-place finish another highlight of the season was coach Eagles Keys winning the [Annis Stukus Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annis_Stukus_Trophy) awarded to the coach of the year. The Roughriders finished in first place in [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_CFL_season) and defeated the Stampeders to qualify for another Grey Cup. In the Grey Cup against the Ottawa Rough Riders, Saskatchewan fell 29–11 in their [third Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Grey_Cup) game in four years. The Roughriders won a franchise-best 14 games in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_CFL_season), a record that stands to this day, but were upset in the West Finals by the Stampeders. Eagle Keys resigned during the following off-season, ending his career as the all-time leader in wins by a Saskatchewan Roughrider head coach with 68 wins and four first-place finishes.[^cite_note-42]

### John Payne era (1973–1976)



[George Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Reed_\(Canadian_football\)) is the club's all-time leading rusher.

[Dave Skrien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Skrien) was hired as the next head coach of the Roughriders and led them as they continued their winning ways, namely with an appearance in the [1972 Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Grey_Cup), which yielded another Saskatchewan loss to Hamilton.[^cite_note-43] For the 1973 season the Roughriders hired head coach [John Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Payne_\(American_football\)). The Riders then had three consecutive second-place finishes and West Final losses to the [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Eskimos), ending when George Reed retired after the [1975 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_CFL_season) as the all-time leading rusher in all of professional football with 16,116 rushing yards. In [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_CFL_season), the Roughriders recaptured first place in the Western Conference and defeated the Eskimos in the West Final, advancing to the Grey Cup to once again play the Ottawa Rough Riders. The Roughriders allowed a last-minute touchdown catch by Ottawa [tight end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_end) [Tony Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Gabriel) to lose their fourth [Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64th_Grey_Cup) in ten years, ending one of the most bittersweet eras in Roughrider history.[^cite_note-44] Saskatchewan had qualified for the playoffs for 15 consecutive seasons, tied for fourth-best in CFL history, and played in 11 consecutive Division Finals, which is a CFL record. While they also posted the best regular season record in all of professional football over that time period, the team only won one championship during that time.[^cite_note-45]

### Post-Ron Lancaster era (1979–1986)

After their loss in the [1976 Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64th_Grey_Cup) game to the [Ottawa Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rough_Riders), coach John Payne left the team to become head coach of the [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Lions) and Saskatchewan fell into a drought that was unheard of in the CFL. They posted an 8–8 record in [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), but finished in fourth place. It was the start of an 11-year playoff drought, the longest in CFL history. The Roughriders had several talented players during this era, including [Joey Walters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Walters) at receiver, [Vince Goldsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Goldsmith) at defensive end, offensive lineman [Roger Aldag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Aldag) from [Gull Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gull_Lake,_Saskatchewan) and [Dave Ridgway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Ridgway), who became one of the greatest kickers in CFL history. However, in an era where the West was dominated by Edmonton, Winnipeg and (by the early 1980s) the B.C. Lions, the Roughriders often found themselves in a losing battle for the third and final playoff spot in the West.

Franchise [quarterback](25277.md) [Ron Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Lancaster) retired after the [1978 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) as the [CFL's all time passing leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canadian_Football_League_records_\(individual\)#Passing) in passing yards, completions and touchdown passes.[^cite_note-ticats-46] Furthermore, he is the only Roughrider to win the [CFL's Most Outstanding Player Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFL's_Most_Outstanding_Player_Award) twice while playing with Saskatchewan. Playing without Lancaster behind centre for the first time in 16 seasons proved difficult as the team posted back-to-back 2–14 seasons in 1979 and 1980. Ironically, the head coach of those squads was none other than Lancaster himself. The Riders' only winning record during this time came in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_CFL_season) when they finished with a 9–7 record under [Joe Faragalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Faragalli), but it was only good enough for fourth place in a competitive West Division – the "crossover rule" had not yet been implemented – therefore, as in 1977, the Riders were denied a playoff spot despite the third place Eastern team having a worse record (the Montreal Alouettes finished 3–13). During the following six seasons, the Roughriders never earned more than six wins in a season, leaving them soundly out of the playoff picture. In [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_CFL_season), the Roughriders introduced a new logo as part of the 75th anniversary of the inception of the team, adding black and silver to the team's colour scheme.[^cite_note-Sask_History-17]

### Kent Austin era (1987–1993)

Before the 1987 season started, after almost a decade out of the playoffs and poor attendance saddling the team, the Roughriders felt it was necessary to conduct a telethon to keep the team afloat. Roughriders management privately knew the [Montreal Alouettes](70601.md) were on the brink of folding before the season got underway and were determined not to let the same fate befall them (as it turned out, the Alouettes shut down a day before the season opener and would not return for nearly a decade). The province-wide "Save the Roughriders" telethon was a success; the team sold enough tickets to stay in business.[^cite_note-47] Also in 1986, team executive Tom Shepherd founded the group "Friends of the Riders" to run an annual *Touchdown Lottery* to further bolster the club's finances. Originally conceived as a one-time fundraiser, it raised almost \$22.6 million during its run and operated for 33 years. By the time the lottery was discontinued with Shepherd's retirement in 2019, the club's financial situation had long been stabilized.

After a carousel of head coaches since [John Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Payne_\(American_football\))'s departure in 1977, the Roughriders hired [John Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gregory_\(American_football_coach\)) after the 1986 season. Gregory took over a team that had missed the playoffs for ten consecutive seasons. In Gregory's first season as Saskatchewan's head coach, the Roughriders finished 5–12. Gregory then led the Riders to an 11–7 record in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_CFL_season) and a playoff berth. He won the Annis Stukus Trophy as the league's coach of the year. This finally ended the franchise's 11-year playoff drought, the longest in CFL history. However, the Roughriders had to settle for second place in the West because the Eskimos also finished 11–7 and swept them in the regular season. On November 13, 1988, the Roughriders hosted a playoff game for the first time since [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), but lost to the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions) in the Western Semi-Final by a score of 42–18. Nonetheless, it was a step in the right direction as the Roughriders learned how to win and gained valuable playoff experience that they would need for next season.

#### 1989 Grey Cup champions

The Roughriders finished with a 9–9 record and a third-place finish in the [1989 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), but still qualified for the playoffs for a second consecutive season. They defeated the [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders) 33–26 in the West Semi-Finals in part because of delay run play to Brian Walling who ran 50 yards for a touchdown to make it 30–26 with 1\:38 left to play. Walling had just been picked up by the Roughriders just weeks previously from the Edmonton Eskimos practice roster. In the West Final, Saskatchewan faced the powerhouse [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Eskimos), a team that set (and still holds) a [CFL record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canadian_Football_League_records_\(team\)#Standings) with 16 regular season wins in one season since the institution of the 18-game CFL schedule in 1986. The Roughriders defeated the heavily favoured Eskimos 32–21 to advance to the [Grey Cup](230107.md) where they faced the [Hamilton Tiger-Cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Tiger-Cats) for the third time in franchise history. With a talented roster that included [Kent Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Austin) at quarterback, receivers [Ray Elgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Elgaard), [Donald Narcisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Narcisse), [Jeff Fairholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Fairholm) and James "Duke" Ellingson, and an outstanding offensive line featuring [Roger Aldag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Aldag), [Vic Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vic_Stevenson), [Dan Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Payne_\(Canadian_football\)) and [Bob Poley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Poley), the Roughriders found themselves in a game that featured extensive offensive prowess. With the score tied 40–40, [placekicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placekicker) [Dave Ridgway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Ridgway) kicked a 35-yard game-winning [field goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_\(American_and_Canadian_football\)) to win the [77th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Grey_Cup) for the Roughriders, with a play that has become simply known as "The Kick."[^cite_note-48] It was the second championship for the franchise, following a 23-year drought and is considered the greatest Grey Cup game ever played.[^cite_note-49] Saskatchewan qualified for the playoffs in four of the next five seasons, including two seasons with winning records, but lost in the West Semi-Final each time to either of the two Alberta teams, the Calgary Stampeders or Edmonton Eskimos.

### Struggles and challenges (1994–1999)

In [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_CFL_season), [Regina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina,_Saskatchewan) hosted the Grey Cup for the first time in league history, giving the Roughriders an opportunity to compete for the championship at home. Unfortunately, the Roughriders finished in sixth place in the newly named North Division, as part of the CFL's [American expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFL_USA), and did not qualify for the playoffs.[^cite_note-1990s-50]

With most CFL teams losing money after the ill-fated American expansion ended after the 1995 season, the Roughriders conducted another "Save the Roughriders" telethon in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_CFL_season) to help the team's financial health, they remain one of only two professional sports teams to organize a telethon due to a precarious financial situation. As was the case, the telethon along with the then-ongoing Touchdown Lottery helped to keep the franchise afloat again,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] although the 1997 telethon ended with intrigue after Roughriders management announced that a \$500,000 "donation" from a mysterious benefactor had helped to put the team's fundraising effort over the top. It was only after weeks of speculation that the club was able to confirm that the funds were in fact the Roughriders' share of a [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_dollar)3 million interest-free loan from none other than the [National Football League](21211.md). Roughriders management subsequently explained that loan, equal to roughly [C\$4](101846.md) million or \$500,000 for each of the then-eight CFL teams at contemporary exchange rates, had been agreed between the leagues in principle but not finalized in time for the telethon, and thus had to remain confidential until formally announced by the respective commissioners.

The Roughriders qualified again for the playoffs [that same year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) for the first time since 1994, when they did so with a losing record, which was a first for the team since [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_in_Canadian_football). The team made the most of their opportunity as they defeated both of the Alberta-based teams, the Stampeders and Eskimos in the West Semi-Final and West Final, respectively, to advance to the [85th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85th_Grey_Cup). Unfortunately, the upstart Roughriders fell to the [Doug Flutie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Flutie)-led [Toronto Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Argonauts) 47–23 in the first ever Grey Cup match-up between the two oldest franchises in the league.[^cite_note-Sask_History-17][^cite_note-53] The Roughriders closed out the 20th century with two more losing seasons, failing to qualify for the playoffs in both 1998 and 1999.

### Roy Shivers and Danny Barrett era (1999–2006)

Following the [1999 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), [Roy Shivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Shivers), the former Director of Player Personnel for the [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders), assumed the duties of [general manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_manager) of the Roughriders.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Shivers-55] Shivers then hired [Danny Barrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Barrett_\(American_football\)) as the team's [head coach](533149.md) despite the latter's limited coaching experience. The Roughriders made football history by being the first professional team with both a black general manager and head coach.

In what was described as a rebuilding process, the Roughriders began the Shivers and Barrett era with two consecutive last place finishes in 2000 and 2001, missing the playoffs in both years. In [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), progress was being made as Saskatchewan made the playoffs for the first time since their [1997 Grey Cup run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85th_Grey_Cup) with an 8–10 record and a fourth-place finish. The team played in the East Semi-Final due to the [crossover rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League#Playoffs) instituted in 1997, playing in the eastern playoffs for the first time in their 90-year history, losing to their last playoff opponent, the [Toronto Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Argonauts). The [2003 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) saw the Roughriders earn their first winning record since [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), finishing 11–7 and in third place, building optimism in a year where the franchise was hosting their second ever [Grey Cup game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90th_Grey_Cup). While the team played their longtime rival, the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers), in the playoffs for the first time since [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_CFL_season) and won, they lost the West Final to the eventual champion [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Eskimos), missing a close chance to play in the [Grey Cup](230107.md) at home.

After their strong 2003 campaign, the Roughriders were expected to build upon that success in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season). While the team regressed slightly with a 9–9 record, they won the West Semi-Final over the Eskimos and advanced to the West Final for the second consecutive year to face the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions). After Saskatchewan scored a late touchdown to take the lead, BC tied the game with a late field goal, sending the game to [overtime](633242.md). Saskatchewan [placekicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placekicker) [Paul McCallum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCallum_\(Canadian_football\)) missed an 18-yard field goal while BC kicker [Duncan O'Mahoney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_O'Mahoney) hit a 40-yarder to win the game for the Lions, adding to the frustration of the Roughrider fanbase.[^cite_note-56]

Prior to the [2005 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), quarterback [Henry Burris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Burris) signed as a [free agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agent) with Calgary, leaving the Roughriders with a smaller chance at progress. The team finished in fourth place with a 9–9 record and crossed over to the Eastern playoffs again, only to be defeated by the [Montreal Alouettes](70601.md) in the first ever post-season meeting with that team. Feeling a greater need for progress, the pressure was on the Roughriders to perform in [2006 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season). After Saskatchewan started the season with a 4–5 record, general manager Roy Shivers was fired on August 21, 2006.[^cite_note-Shivers-55] The Roughriders then hired [Eric Tillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Tillman) to take over and he elected not to renew Danny Barrett's contract at the end of the season, following a third consecutive 9–9 season and a West Final loss to the Lions.[^cite_note-57] While they did not win any championships, Shivers and Barrett restored a measure of respectability to the franchise and set the stage for things to come.

### Ken Miller and Darian Durant era (2007–2011)

#### 2007 Grey Cup champions



The Roughriders celebrate their [2007 Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Grey_Cup) victory

&#x20;After contending on and off in the early part of the 21st century, the Roughriders hired [1989 Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Grey_Cup) hero [Kent Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Austin) as [head coach](533149.md) and [Ken Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Miller_\(gridiron_football\)) as [offensive coordinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offensive_coordinator) in [the 2007 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season). Despite a rookie head coach, the team jumped out to a 7–2 start, which was their best start since 1976. They finished the season with a 12–6 record and brought along with it the Roughriders' first home playoff game since 1988, which became a 26–24 victory over the [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders). This was also their first home playoff win since 1976. The team then followed up with a 26–17 win at [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) over the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions) in the West Division final to give the Roughriders a berth in their first [Grey Cup](230107.md) final since [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85th_Grey_Cup).

On November 25, 2007, the Riders played the [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers) in the [95th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Grey_Cup). The Riders' traditional rivals had moved to the East Division the previous year following the demise of the [Ottawa Renegades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Renegades), and the 2007 championship game marked the first time that the two [Labour Day Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Day_Classic) opponents played each other in a Grey Cup game. Saskatchewan won 23–19 in a game where [James Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Johnson_\(Canadian_football\)) recorded a Grey Cup record three interceptions en route to being named [Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_Cup_Most_Valuable_Player) of the 2007 Grey Cup. Fellow Roughrider [Andy Fantuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Fantuz) was named the [Canadian MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_Cup_Most_Valuable_Canadian) in the game after recording 70 yards receiving and the game-winning touchdown.

A month and a half after capturing the 2007 Grey Cup, Austin stepped down as head coach to become the offensive co-ordinator at his alma mater [University of Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Miss_Rebels_football).[^cite_note-58] In accepting this position in the NCAA, Austin turned down a very lucrative contract that the Riders had offered. On February 6, 2008, Roughriders GM Eric Tillman announced that the new head coach would be [Ken Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Miller_\(football_coach\)).[^cite_note-59] Miller was formerly the offensive coordinator under Austin. The team also traded former [league MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFL's_Most_Outstanding_Player_Award) [Kerry Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Joseph) to the [Toronto Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Argonauts), leaving the team without their Grey Cup-winning head coach and starting quarterback.

The [2008 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) began with a 6–0 record with wins shared between three quarterbacks, including the season opening starter, [Marcus Crandell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Crandell). This was the team's best record since [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_in_Canadian_football), when they were still known as the Regina Roughriders. On August 24, 2008, the team's General Manager, [Eric Tillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Tillman), announced the acquisition of Quarterback [Michael Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bishop_\(gridiron_football\)), the Toronto Argonauts backup quarterback at the time of the trade, who went 11–1 as a starter for the Argonauts in 2007. This was the end of Marcus Crandell's run with the Roughriders, as he was released four days later. After the 6–0 start, the Riders went on to finish the 2008 CFL Regular season with the same record they finished with in 2007, at 12–6. The Roughriders finished in second place in the [CFL West Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFL_West_Division) and earned the right to host the CFL West Division Semi-Final for the second consecutive year. The Roughriders suffered a devastating 33–12 loss to the BC Lions in the western semi-final game, leading to Bishop's release shortly after the loss.

In [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), the Roughriders were led by quarterback [Darian Durant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darian_Durant), who had seen his first significant playing time in 2008 and was named the opening day starter. Durant started all 18 games for Saskatchewan and led the team to a 10–7–1 record and their first West Division regular season title since [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season).[^cite_note-60] After defeating the Calgary Stampeders in the West Final, the team advanced to the [97th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/97th_Grey_Cup) to face the [Montreal Alouettes](70601.md). After the Roughriders were leading 27–11 in the fourth quarter, Montreal stormed back to make the score 27–25 late in the fourth. Montreal kicker [Damon Duval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Duval) attempted a 43-yard field goal and missed, but Saskatchewan had been called for a too-many-men penalty, advancing the placement 10 yards. Duval did not miss a second time, scoring the three points to win the game 28–27 for the Alouettes and adding to the Roughriders' championship woes.[^cite_note-61]

The Roughriders celebrated their 100-year anniversary as a football club during the [2010 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season), wearing retro-themed red and black uniforms based on the ones worn by the Regina Roughriders. The Roughriders finished second in the West with a 10–8 record and defeated the BC Lions in double overtime in the West Division Semi-Final.[^cite_note-62] After defeating the Stampeders in the West Final for the second year in a row, the Roughriders faced the Alouettes in the [98th Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/98th_Grey_Cup) once again. Despite leading 11–8 at the half, the Roughriders faced a ten-point deficit in the fourth quarter. The lead proved insurmountable, as Saskatchewan lost the game to Montreal for the second consecutive year by a score 21–18.[^cite_note-63]

Following the Grey Cup loss, head coach Ken Miller resigned and became Vice President of Football operations. The club hired [Greg Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Marshall_\(defensive_lineman\)) as his replacement, but the [2011 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) was one to forget, as the Roughriders finished last in the West with a 5–13 record and missed the playoffs. The Roughriders fired Marshall after a 1–7 start and had Miller step in as his replacement. The season was plagued by errors and mishaps, most of them self-inflicted as the team could not dig itself out of their early season hole. 2011 proved to be Ken Miller's last season with the Roughriders, as he retired shortly after the 2011 season.[^cite_note-64]

### Corey Chamblin era (2012–2015)

#### 2013 Grey Cup champions



Roughrider Cheerleaders, 2012.

On December 16, 2011 [Corey Chamblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Chamblin), who had been the [defensive coordinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_coordinator) for the [Hamilton Tiger-Cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Tiger-Cats), was named the new head coach of the Roughriders.[^cite_note-65] The Roughriders signed two of the top Canadian players available in free agency in non-import offensive linemen [Brendon LaBatte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendon_LaBatte) and [Dominic Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Picard).[^cite_note-66] However, they were not so fortunate with all-star linebacker [Jerrell Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerrell_Freeman), who led the league in tackles in 2011 and departed for the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL). The Roughriders also lost outstanding Canadian slotback [Andy Fantuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Fantuz), who led the league in receiving yards in 2010, as he signed as a free agent with Hamilton. Nonetheless, the team qualified for the playoffs after missing out during the previous season. In 2012, Saskatchewan lost the Western Division Semi-Final game to the [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders) in a close game, 36–30.

On January 24, 2013, the Riders traded [Justin Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Harper_\(American_football\)) and a 4th round 2014 pick to the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions) for six-time all-star [Geroy Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geroy_Simon). Simon holds the record for most career receiving yards. Simon played for the Lions from 2001 to 2012. The 2013 season started off spectacularly for the Roughriders, mainly for [Darian Durant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darian_Durant) and [Kory Sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kory_Sheets). The Roughriders went 8–1 in the first nine games and set a record for the best start in franchise history (their previous best was 7–1 during the 1970 season). Running back Kory Sheets had the best start for a running back in CFL history and Darian Durant had thrown only one interception while throwing 21 touchdowns.

The [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Saskatchewan_Roughriders_season) season ended with an 11–7 record, for 2nd in the West Division, behind Calgary. The team hosted its first playoff game since 2010 on November 10, the West Semi Finals against the BC Lions. The Roughriders won the game, 29–25, the first playoff win of Corey Chamblin's CFL head coaching career and the first since 2010 for the Roughriders, when quarterback Darian Durant put the team on his shoulders and rushed for 41 yards.[^cite_note-67] On Sunday, November 17, 2013, the Roughriders successfully defeated the Calgary Stampeders in the Western Finals, the score being 35–13. This allowed the Saskatchewan Roughriders to advance to the [101st Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Grey_Cup). It was the first time in Saskatchewan Roughriders history that they were part of the Grey Cup in their own hometown. In defiance of the individual player introduction format the league had planned for the Grey Cup game, the Roughriders took to the field as a team as the Saskatchewan crowd chanted "bring 'em out," their usual home game entrance song.[^cite_note-68] The Roughriders defeated the Hamilton Tiger-Cats, 45–23, with RB Kory Sheets winning the [Grey Cup Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_Cup_Most_Valuable_Player) Award after rushing for a franchise and [Grey Cup](230107.md) record 197 yards, to be the third consecutive team to win the championship at their home field. The following offseason was a difficult one for the Roughriders, as they lost top players Kory Sheets and Weston Dressler to the NFL and key leaders Mike McCullough, Renauld Williams and Geroy Simon to retirement. The expansion draft saw them give up Canadian players in Keith Shologan and Zach Evans.

In February 2014, the Roughriders traded backup quarterback Drew Willy to the Winnipeg Blue Bombers. The Roughriders gave head coach Corey Chamblin an extension through 2017 after Chamblin won Coach of The Year in the CFL. 2014 training camp found starters in Anthony Allen and Brett Swain as the Roughriders went out to attempt the first repeat championship season in franchise history. They began the 2014 season with a record of 9–3, similar to that of the 2013 season start. The Roughriders defensive line led by John Chick, Ricky Foley and Terrious George led the league in sacks throughout the season, and Brian Peters led a defence poised to make a run at another championship. On September 7, 2014, Darian Durant sustained an elbow injury during the Banjo Bowl that required surgery, ultimately ending his 2014 campaign. The Roughriders then gave backup Tino Sunseri a shot at starting quarterback. With Tino Sunseri leading the team, the Roughriders went 1–4 and [Seth Doege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Doege) got a shot as well. The Roughriders then brought 2007 Most Outstanding Player [Kerry Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Joseph) out of retirement to help the struggling team. Joseph, alongside new running back [Jerome Messam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Messam) and receiver [Korey Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korey_Williams) won one game before losing to the Edmonton Eskimos in the Western Semi Final.

The 2015 offseason saw the Roughriders lose top defensive players in [Brian Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Peters_\(gridiron_football\)) and [Ricky Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Foley). Peters was signed by the Minnesota Vikings of the NFL and Foley was traded to Toronto for Canadian linebacker [Shea Emry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Emry) on January 24, 2015. Soon after, Weston Dressler was signed to a 4-year contract with the Roughriders and Tyron Brackenridge also signed a contract to stay in Saskatchewan. Free agency saw the Roughriders bring back [Kevin Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Glenn) as their backup quarterback. They also brought back veterans Alex Hall, Keenan MacDougall and Jamel Richardson. The 2015 CFL Draft saw them pick receiver Nic Demski from the University of Manitoba Bisons. To begin the year, the Roughriders found a linebacker, Jeff Knox Jr, in training camp and he became an immediate starter next to Shea Emry and Weldon Brown. The first game of the 2015 season was devastating, with the Roughriders losing quarterback Darian Durant once again to a season-ending injury, along with injuries to Shea Emry and Keenan MacDougall early in the season. This led to players such as Jake Doughty getting a starting gig with the team.

With the injury to Durant, Kevin Glenn became the starting quarterback, fulfilling the purpose for which he was signed by Saskatchewan. Days later, Chris Milo was released by the Roughriders, and they brought in veteran Paul McCallum as starting kicker. McCallum first game back with the Green and White was on July 5, 2015, a 42–40 overtime loss to the Toronto Argonauts. Weeks later, the Roughriders still did not have a win in the 2015 season, raising concern on whether head coach Corey Chamblin would eventually be fired. The injury bug around Saskatchewan got worse as quarterback Kevin Glenn, receiver Chris Getzlaf, linebacker Weldon Brown, and receiver Taj Smith were lost to injury. This eventually led to rookie quarterback Brett Smith getting a shot as the starter. During the August 30, 2015 game against the Ottawa Redblacks, head coach Corey Chamblin made the decision to bench Smith in favour of Tino Sunseri following an interception, which disgusted many Roughrider fans. Following another disappointing loss to Ottawa and a record of 0–9 for the first half of the season, head coach Corey Chamblin and general manager Brendan Taman were fired on September 1, 2015. Special teams coordinator [Bob Dyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dyce) was appointed interim head coach and assistant general manager Jeremy O'Day was appointed interim general manager.

### Chris Jones era (2016–2018)

On December 7, 2015, a mere week after winning the [103rd Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/103rd_Grey_Cup) with the [Edmonton Eskimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Eskimos) as their head coach, it was announced that [Chris Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Jones_\(Canadian_football_coach\)) would be the new head coach, general manager and vice-president of football operations for the Saskatchewan Roughriders. Jones has won four Grey Cups rings with four teams: Montreal 2002, Calgary 2008, Toronto 2012, Edmonton 2015. On December 16, 2015, eight assistant coaches under Jones in Edmonton joined the Roughrider coaching staff. This is includes Stephen McAdoo as Offensive co-ordinator and assistant head coach, Craig Dickenson as Special teams co-ordinator, Jarious Jackson as Quarterbacks coach and passing game co-ordinator, Mike Scheper as Offensive line coach, Phillip Lolley as Linebackers coach, Jason Shivers as Defensive backs coach, Ed Philion as Defensive line coach and Craig Davoren as Running backs coach and special teams assistant.

Jones' first major moves as head coach and general manager of the Roughriders occurred on December 15, when Jones cut 19 players from the roster including [Tyron Brackenridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyron_Brackenridge), [Anthony Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Allen_\(running_back\)) and [Terrell Maze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_Maze). On January 14, 2016, Jones continued the rebuild by cutting fan favourites and long-time Roughriders [John Chick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Chick) and [Weston Dressler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston_Dressler). Chick went on to sign with the [Hamilton Tiger Cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Tiger_Cats), while Dressler went on to sign with rival [Winnipeg Blue Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Blue_Bombers). However, the Roughriders suddenly signed defensive end [Shawn Lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Lemon) to replace Chick soon after. Later that month, the Roughriders re-signed longtime receiver [Rob Bagg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Bagg). Jones departed after the 2018 season, being succeeded by [Craig Dickenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Dickenson) on January 25, 2019.[^cite_note-69]

### Craig Dickenson's Riders (2019–2023)

The Green Riders clinched their first first-place West Division finish since 2009 with a 13–5 record, in Craig Dickenson's first year at the coaching helm. However, the team lost in a hard-fought heartbreaker in the West Final game over the arch-rival (and eventual Grey Cup champion) Blue Bombers, 20–13. After the [2020 CFL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_CFL_season) was cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the Riders returned to the playoffs in 2021, finishing in second place at 9–5 in the shortened season, defeating the Calgary Stampeders in the West Semi-Final, but losing to the Winnipeg Blue Bombers again (who would win their second straight Grey Cup championship) in the West Final for the second straight season.

In 2022, the Riders missed the playoffs for the first time in six years, posting a 6–12 mark, good for fourth place in the West. The season was much more successful off the field as the franchise again became profitable. This, combined with the team's share of revenues from the [2022 Grey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Grey_Cup) (held at Mosaic Stadium as a replacement for the cancelled 2020 championship game) allowed the club to replenish its stabilization fund back to pre-pandemic levels.

Following the season, the Riders replaced both offensive coordinator [Jason Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Maas) and quarterback [Cody Fajardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Fajardo), but the result was the same in the 2023 season as the year previous. The green and white posted the exact same record as the previous year at 6–12, missing the playoffs after ending the season on a seven-game losing streak. The team's replacement for Fajardo, veteran CFL quarterback [Trevor Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Harris), was injured early in the season, creating a quarterback controversy where both [Mason Fine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_Fine) and [Jake Dolegala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Dolegala) took turns as the starter.

On October 23, 2023, the Riders announced the organization would not renew its contract with Dickenson, ending his time as head coach.[^cite_note-70] The team did sign Vice President of Football Operations and General Manager [Jeremy O'Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_O'Day) to a three-year extension.

### Corey Mace takes over (since 2023)

The Riders announced [Corey Mace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Mace) would become the team's 48th head coach on November 30, 2023.[^cite_note-71] Mace left the Toronto Argonauts organization after two seasons as defensive coordinator to take on the role, his first as a head coach.

Among Mace's first moves as head coach was hiring Regina product [Marc Mueller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Mueller)[^cite_note-72] as offensive coordinator, someone Mace had previously coached with in Calgary. It was also announced that Kent Maugeri would stay on as special teams coordinator for his ninth overall season with the team.[^cite_note-73]

In early 2024, the Riders announced Mace would also serve as the team's defensive coordinator in addition to his head coaching duties.[^cite_note-74]

During Mace's first season at the helm in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CFL_season), the Riders opened the season on a hot start before falling into a summer slump that included the club's first tie game since [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_CFL_season).[^cite_note-75] The team was able to rally later in the season, finishing the regular season with a record of 9–8–1. It was enough to make the playoffs for the first time since 2021, although the team fell short of a Grey Cup appearance.[^cite_note-76]

After defeating the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions) 24–21 to win the West Division, the Riders won their fifth Grey Cup in [2025](73288547.md), 25–17 over the [Montreal Alouettes](70601.md).

Mace would hand over defensive coordinator duties to [Josh Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Bell_\(gridiron_football\)) ahead of the [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CFL_season) season.[^cite_note-77]

## Popularity

### Fan support



Now-defunct [Mosaic Stadium at Taylor Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_Stadium_at_Taylor_Field) prior to a CFL match.

In 2009, the team was described as having the largest fan base in the CFL.[^cite_note-78] The team is third behind the Montreal Canadiens and [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) for merchandise sales of Canadian sports teams,[^cite_note-79] According to a survey (July 2015), the Riders were considered the third strongest sports Brand in Canada.[^cite_note-80] When the Roughriders play on the road, there is always a strong contingent of fans due to fans travelling and also because of people having relocated from Saskatchewan to other parts of Canada. The Roughriders home attendance record came in 1995 in a win over the [Calgary Stampeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Stampeders) as 55,438 (more than 25% of Regina's population) fans watched on.[^cite_note-81] This crowd was attainable that year because of the increased capacity of [Taylor Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_Stadium_at_Taylor_Field) in preparation of hosting the [Grey Cup](230107.md). Rider fans are also known for dressing up in unique and often bizarre Rider-themed costumes, the most popular being the watermelon helmet. They are the only CFL football team with a fan-based analytic website, dedicated to fan-based opinion and perception. Beginning with the 2011 season, Insightrix Research Inc. has conducted a poll after each Roughrider game, surveying a random selection of fans from the SaskWatch Research online community. Each week, fans weigh in on the Riders' game performance and predictions for the rest of the season. These predictions are compared against other Regions in Saskatchewan, titled "Battle of the Regions."[^cite_note-82]

### Fundraisers

Fans supported the team through the Friends of the Riders Touchdown Lottery. From 1986 to 2018, the Lottery raised over \$23 million for the Saskatchewan Roughriders, while giving out more than \$33 million in prizes.[^cite_note-83] During difficult financial times, lottery revenues were crucial to the team's survival. In 1987 and 1997,[^cite_note-84] fans responded when the Roughriders held season-ticket telethons to try and raise money to help the team avoid bankruptcy.

### Section 28

Section 28 (formerly known as the University Section) was a notorious section in the East Side bleachers at [Taylor Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_Stadium_at_Taylor_Field). They were known for their strict allegiance to the Riders, standing through the entire game, and often being merciless to opposing fans who sit in the section and cheer for the road team. The University Section got its nickname from the section's main purpose in the 1980s and 1990s and was a discount section offered to university students in [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan). The section later ceased to be a university section but remained a section with a rowdy reputation until Taylor Field's closure in 2016.[^cite_note-85]

## Fight and theme songs

The official Rider songs are played regularly at the stadium, and include "Rider Pride", and "Paint the Whole World Green."[^cite_note-86] Among several 'borrowed songs' is the team's touchdown song and victory march, "Green Is the Colour" (adapted from the original "[Blue Is the Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Is_the_Colour_\(song\))" written by D. Boone and R. McQueen for the [Chelsea Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Football_Club)) and "On Roughriders" (adapted from "[On Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Wisconsin)", the fight song for the [Wisconsin Badgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Badgers)). In addition, during every fourth quarter intermission, the P.A. system plays the cult hit "[The Last Saskatchewan Pirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Saskatchewan_Pirate)" by [The Arrogant Worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arrogant_Worms), and the Riders Cheer Team leads the crowd in a fourth quarter stretch. There are many other songs that have been created over the years to tribute the team as well. Many of these songs have proved so popular in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) that they have become [popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_culture) phenomena.[^cite_note-87] The music selection at Mosaic Stadium mostly consists of mainstream popular music. "Green Is the Colour" is played after every rider touchdown, followed by Gainer the Gopher being driven around the stadium's track and giving high fives to those in the first row.

## Mascots



Gainer the Gopher

The current official Rider mascot is [Gainer the Gopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainer_the_Gopher), who made his first appearance in 1977,[^cite_note-88] and was updated in 2019.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] *Gainer* is an [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) of *Regina*, and the gopher, or more precisely [Richardson's ground squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richardson's_ground_squirrel), is a common animal on the [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies).

## Radio

Roughriders radio broadcasts are broadcast throughout the province via *[The Co-operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Co-operators) Roughrider Football Network*, fronted by [flagship station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship_station) [CKRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKRM) in Regina. Affiliates include [CFWD-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFWD-FM) in [Saskatoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatoon), [CJGX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJGX) in [Yorkton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkton), [CKBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKBI_\(AM\)) in Prince Albert, [CJNB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJNB) in North Battleford and [CJNS-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJNS-FM) in Meadow Lake. Additionally, CKRM's 10,000-watt signal brings Rider games to much of the province during the day.

Notable broadcasters for the Roughriders include [Dave Dryburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Dryburgh) from 1940 to 1947,[^cite_note-92] and [John Badham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Badham_\(sportscaster\)) from 1959 to 1969, on [CKCK/620](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKCK-FM).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Rod Peterson was the Roughriders' voice on CKRM from 1999 to 2019. He was replaced in 2019 by former [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) anchor Derek Taylor. Prior to the 2022 season, Taylor moved to the Blue Bombers to replace the retiring [Bob Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Irving_\(sportscaster\)).[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-\:0-96] For the 2022 season, Michael "Ballsy" Ball—who hosted CKRM's pre-game and post-game shows, was play-by-play announcer for the [Regina Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Rams), and co-hosted sister station [CFWF-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFWF-FM)'s morning show—became the new voice of the Roughriders.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-\:0-96] In 2024, he was replaced by Dave Thomas, a former broadcaster for the [Saskatoon Blades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatoon_Blades) ([CJWW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJWW)) and [Weyburn Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyburn_Red_Wings) ([CFSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFSL)), and previously a pre-game host and sideline reporter for the Riders.[^cite_note-98]
