The **United Arab Emirates Air Force** (**UAEAF**) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القوات الجوية والدفاع الجوي الاماراتي, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Quwwāt al-Jawiyah wa al-Defa' al-Jawiy al-ʾImārāty*) is the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (UAE), part of the [United Arab Emirates Armed Forces](31841.md). The airforce was established in 1968 as a small air wing of the [Trucial Oman Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_Oman_Scouts). The air wing was later named Air Wing of the Abu Dhabi Defense Forces before later being incorporated into the [Union Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Defence_Force_\(UAE\)) of the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-2]

Since then, the airforce has undergone a continual reorganisation and expansion in terms of both capability and the number of aircraft. Currently, the UAEAF has around 4,000 personnel and operates approximately 592 aircraft.

The UAE Air Force history began in May 1968, with the formation of an Air Wing of the [Abu Dhabi Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_Army_Air_Force?action=edit\&redlink=1) (ADDF) under [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_the_British_Empire). Its key roles were to provide both a transport service and a ground attack support capability for ADDF land forces.[^cite_note-\:0-3] Major investment in the early 1970s assured an expansion in terms of capabilities, quality, and quantity of aircraft.[^cite_note-AFM60-4] It also led to the renaming of the Air Wing to the ADDF Air Force in 1972. Training and instruction were provided by the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) in the early years. During the [1973 Arab-Israel War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) (6-25 October 1973), the ADDF Air Force's [Caribous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_DHC-4_Caribou) served as air ambulances in Jordan.[^cite_note-5]

The [Emirate of Dubai](3509285.md) maintained its own air component, the Dubai Defence Force Air Wing, until 1999, when the two were effectively merged to become what is now the United Arab Emirates Air Force. Although the integration of the two independent forces was completed, a small degree of autonomy exists at the operational command level, with the Western Air Command being headquartered in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) and the Central Air Command in [Dubai](211583.md).[^cite_note-AFM60-4] Since the 1980s, a combination of regional instability and high oil prices has resulted in an ambitious modernisation of the UAEAF to attain a level of capability matching the [NATO](21133.md) standards.[^cite_note-AFM60-4] In the 1991 Gulf War, the UAE helped other countries by carrying out airstrikes against Iraqi forces.

In 2014, the UAE Air Force, along with the [Egyptian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Air_Force), carried out airstrikes in Libya against Islamist factions in [Tripoli](31232.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In September 2014, UAE Air Force aircraft joined in US-led air strikes against terrorist targets in Syria and Iraq that later became known as [Operation Inherent Resolve](44120613.md). These operations were suspended after a Jordanian pilot was captured by Islamic State militants in late December 2014, pending improvements in US search and rescue capabilities in the region.

In 2015, the UAE Air Force dropped bombs on ISIS targets in Syria. One of them was Major Mariyam Al Mansouri, the first female UAE Air Force pilot.[^cite_note-9] The UAE military was also part of the [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) from 2015 to 2025 when the UAE withdrew.

## Personnel and training



UAEAF crew chief communicating during an engine test at [Nellis Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellis_Air_Force_Base) during [Red Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Red_Flag) 11–2 on February 2, 2011.

During the formative years of the United Arab Emirates Armed Forces, foreign officers were recruited to train Emirati personnel, with British, Jordanian and Pakistani officers among those recruited during the 1960s and 1970s.[^cite_note-10]

In 2002, the UAE Air Force consisted of about 4,000 personnel.[^cite_note-DOS-11]

UAEAF candidates apply to the [Khalifa bin Zayed Air College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_bin_Zayed_Air_College). Military pilot training is conducted at the college in [Al Ain International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ain_International_Airport) in [Al Ain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ain). The college trains both male and female military pilot officers and other aviation personnel in fields including fixed-wing aviation, helicopters, unmanned aerial vehicles and air defence. Women have served as combat pilots in the UAE Air Force and Air Defence.[^cite_note-12] [Mariam Al Mansouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariam_Al_Mansouri) is the UAE's first female fighter pilot and served as an F-16 pilot.[^cite_note-13]

The United States has provided training associated with the UAE's F-16E/F Block 60 fleet. UAE F-16 pilot training began at [Tucson Air National Guard Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson_Air_National_Guard_Base) in Arizona in September 2004, and the first group of Emirati pilots completed the training program in April 2005.[^cite_note-14]

In 2009, the UAE acquired [Pilatus PC-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-21) to modernize it's pilot-training fleet. The UAE ordered 25 PC-21 aircraft in 2009 for military pilot training, and delivery of the fleet was completed in January 2012.[^cite_note-15]

## Overview



A UAEAF [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) fighter.



&#x20;A UAEAF [Lockheed Martin F-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-16) Block 60 developed specifically for the UAEAF. It is also called the F-16 Desert Falcon.

2007 marked the culmination of the largest procurement programmes ever undertaken by the UAE Air Force, with the final deliveries of the 80 [F-16E/F Block 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcon_variants#F-16E/F_Block_60) "Desert Falcons" and approximately 60 upgraded [Mirage 2000-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000#Mirage_2000-9), giving the air force a considerable [multirole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft) capability.[^cite_note-AFP-16] These two investments represented a total expenditure of around \$10 billion, with additional money spent on infrastructure and logistics.[^cite_note-AFM60-4] A \$6.4 billion contract with [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) for the supply and support of the 80 F-16s was signed in March 2000, while a \$3.4 billion deal for the purchase of 30 new Mirage 2000-9 and retrofitting of the 33 older UAE Mirage 2000s was signed earlier in 1998.[^cite_note-AFM61-17] Missiles were also purchased: 160 [AGM-88 HARMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-88_HARM), 1,000 or more [AGM-65 Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick), about 500 [AIM-120 AMRAAMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM), 270 [AIM-9 Sidewinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) and 52 [AGM-84 Harpoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84_Harpoon).[^cite_note-AFM61-17] In November 2017, the United Arab Emirates Armed Forces announced their intention to sign a contract with [Dassault Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Aviation) for the upgrade of its Mirage 2000-9 aircraft. French newspaper *La Tribune* reported the modernization would cost roughly €300 million.[^cite_note-MirageUpgrade-18]

After a competition between the BAE Hawk, [KAI T-50 Golden Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle) and [Alenia Aermacchi M-346 Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_Aermacchi_M-346_Master), the UAEAF announced the acquisition of 48 trainer and light attack aircraft, with the first deliveries to take place in 2012.[^cite_note-DID-19] The other training types that are thought to be near replacement are the 30 Pilatus PC-7s and five Aermacchi MB-339s serving with the Air Academy at Al Ain.[^cite_note-AFM62-20] The MB-339 is also in use with the UAEAF flight display team, [Al Fursan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Fursan).[^cite_note-21]

The UAEAF has operated 20 [IAR 330 Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_330) helicopters since the late 1970s. These have been recently upgraded to the IAR-330SM standard by [IAR Ghimbav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industria_Aeronautic%C4%83_Rom%C3%A2n%C4%83) in [Romania](25445.md) in cooperation with [Eurocopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter).[^cite_note-Janes-22] These aircraft, supplemented by a further ten ex-[South African Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Air_Force) reworked [SA-330s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA-330), are expected to remain in service for at least 15 years.[^cite_note-AFM63-23] Although no replacement for the Puma fleet is required in the immediate future, the force will be supplemented by 26 [Sikorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Aircraft) [UH-60M *Battlehawks*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Blackhawk), with 390 [AGM-114N Hellfire II](38151.md) missiles.[^cite_note-DID2-24] 30 [AH-64A Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-64_Apache) helicopters were modernised as well, to the AH-64D Longbow standard, and a dozen [Eurocopter Fennecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Fennec) were recently acquired for [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) use.[^cite_note-AFM62-20]

The most important facility of the UAEAF is the [Al Dhafra Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Dhafra_Air_Base), with almost the entire [fighter aircraft](10929.md) fleet located there. However, to prevent all of the air defence and strike assets being located at a single base, a \$1 billion, completely new facility has been constructed deep in the Abu Dhabi desert,[^cite_note-AFM60-4] near the border corner with Saudi Arabia and Oman, near [Al Gharbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gharbia,_Abu_Dhabi), housing at least one Mirage 2000 unit. Al-Safran is believed to have opened around 2008.[^cite_note-aw2015-04-02-25] It is 3,000 m long and has aircraft parking nearly the same size as in [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md), Qatar. A 4,000 m runway at [Al-Safran Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Safran_Air_Base) was built around 2008.[^cite_note-aw2015-04-02-25]

### Retired

Previous aircraft operated by the Air Force were the [Dassault Mirage 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_5), [Boeing 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707), [Aeritalia G.222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeritalia_G.222), [CASA C-212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_C-212), [SF.260T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAI-Marchetti_SF.260), [Hawker Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hunter), [Alouette III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_III), [SA 342 Gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Gazelle), [Bölkow Bo 105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6lkow_Bo_105), [Bell 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_206) & [Bell 214](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_214) helicopter.[^cite_note-38]

### Future equipment

As of 2019, multiple investigations for a UAE Next-Generation Fighter were initiated, request for proposals were sent to [Boeing](18933266.md) [F/A-18 Super Hornet](845012.md), [Dassault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault) [Rafale](33731380.md), [Eurofighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_Gmbh) [Typhoon](167667.md), [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) [F-35A Lightning II](11812.md) and [Sukhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi) [Su-57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-57).[^cite_note-39]

On 3 December 2021, it was announced that the UAE had signed an order for 80 [Rafale F4s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafale_F4), with the first aircraft slated to be received in January 2025.[^cite_note-40] In April 2026 the UAE withdrew from co-financing the Rafale F5 upgrade program (estimated at €3.5–5 billion)[^cite_note-41] and the Rafale order for 80 aircraft became less certain.
