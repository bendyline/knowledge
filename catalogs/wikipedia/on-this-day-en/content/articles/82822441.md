Since Israeli independence in 1948, most [Haredi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_Jews) in Israel have been [exempt from military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exemption_from_military_service_in_Israel) under an arrangement based on the principle of *[Torato Umanuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torato_Umanuto)*, whereby young men who declare themselves full time [yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) students and devote their lives to [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study) serve a shortened service or are exempted entirely. This arrangement lasted until 2014, when legislation was passed with a timeline for eventual conscription. In 2024, widespread enforcement of conscription began amidst the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-1]

In response to conscription efforts, the Haredi community has staged large scale protests to maintain their exemption. The most widespread protests occurred in 2014 and 2024, after legislation on Haredi conscription.[^cite_note-2]

Overall, the issue of Haredi conscription in Israel has remained highly contentious.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-Haaretz20240626-4]

## Status

As the Israeli population has grown over the years, the number of Haredi men eligible for exemption has risen from 400 to tens of thousands in 2024.[^cite_note-5] In 2022, an estimated 18.4% of Jewish men refused to serve, with the majority citing religious belief as the reason.[^cite_note-6] As of 2026, the number of Haredi soldiers in the IDF remains low.[^cite_note-7]

Israelis who belong to the [Religious Zionist community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) are conscripted, often under the yeshiva system of the [Hesder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesder) program, which combines Torah study with military service.[^cite_note-8]

## History

### *Torato Umanuto* exemption arrangement



"Tehillim neged Tilim" ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): תהילים נגד טילים, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) '[Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms) against missiles'), a slogan initially coined during the [Iraqi missile campaign against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_rocket_attacks_on_Israel) in 1991. It has since gained widespread popularity among the [Religious Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) and the [Haredi community](53835.md).

During the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), Israeli prime minister [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) reached a special arrangement with Haredi representative [Yitzhak-Meir Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak-Meir_Levin), who led the Haredi party [Agudat Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agudat_Yisrael) in Israel. The arrangement was based on the Jewish principle of *[Torato Umanuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torato_Umanuto)*, so that those in the Haredi community eligible for conscription would be exempt from service in the IDF as long as [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study) is their sole profession, which for many is a religious commandment that is devoted a majority of the day.&#x20;

The Haredi community maintains that studying [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah) is crucial for defending the [Israeli people](181296.md) from threats.[^cite_note-9]

The Israeli government seeks to preserve the [secular–religious status quo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_\(Israel\)) with the aim of maintaining national and political stability.[^cite_note-\:2-10] This arrangement based on the principle of *Torato Umanuto* effectively became a regulation under the jurisdiction of the [Ministry of Defense](3226446.md).

Following the [Six Day war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Day_war), defense minister [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan) headed a committee to examine the issue of Haredi conscription based on a request from chief of staff [Haim Bar-Lev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_Bar-Lev), but decided on keeping the existing arrangement with 800 exemptions per year.[^cite_note-KnessetTal-11][^cite_note-12]

In 1970, a petition was filed with the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md) regarding the exemptions, with the court upholding the existing arrangements.[^cite_note-13]

In 1977, after the [legislative election that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Israeli_legislative_election) introduced [the nation's first right-wing government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighteenth_government_of_Israel), the quotas on Haredi exemptions were lifted completely following an agreement between [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) and [Agudat Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agudat_Yisrael).[^cite_note-KnessetTal-11]

In 1981, the governing coalition expanded exemptions to include those besides students involved in Torah study including teachers, rabbis, and others.[^cite_note-KnessetTal-11][^cite_note-IDIChallenges-14]

In 1986, a petition to the [Supreme Court](1666510.md) was again filed on Haredi exemptions, with the court again upholding the arrangement. However, the court also determined that the petitioner had a right of [standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_\(law\)), and that the number of exemptions is an important factor on this matter.[^cite_note-15]

Also in 1986, the [Knesset](74656.md) formed the Cohen Committee to examine Haredi exemption policy, and decided on an arrangement with more limitations on exemption. The recommendation was for the exemption arrangement to only apply to select schools, and that only 200 outstanding yeshiva students per year would receive full exemption, with the others conscripted for a shortened service of about a year.[^cite_note-IDIChallenges-14]

### Tal Law

Main article: [Tal Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal_Committee)

In 1998, a petition was filed to the Supreme Court regarding the exemptions. The court ruled that due to the scale of exemptions, new legislation from the [Knesset](74656.md) was needed to regulate this matter.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In 1999, in accordance with the judicial ruling, Prime Minister [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) set up a public committee, which was headed by Justice Zvi Tal and therefore known as the [Tal Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal_Committee). In April 2000, the committee reported its recommendations, which were approved by the [Knesset](74656.md) in July 2002. This law became known as the "Tal Law", and provided for a continuation of the existing arrangement based on the principle of *[Torato Umanuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torato_Umanuto)*, but laid down several specific conditions into the law, in the hopes that exemptions would decrease over time.

Under this legislation, [yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) students who dedicate to [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study) full time can delay army service until the age of 23, and afterwards can either serve 16 months of military service (shortened from three years) or may also serve for a year of [Sherut Leumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherut_Leumi). Afterwards, they may then choose to join the workforce.[^cite_note-toi2012-18]

Following the [2003 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Israeli_legislative_election), the Haredi parties lost their place in the government to the secular anti-religious [Shinui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinui) party. In 2005, Shinui left the government and [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon) brought the Haredi [United Torah Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Torah_Judaism) party into his ruling coalition. Shinui advocates stopping extra funding to mostly Haredi schools and resistance to [Tal Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal_Law), which gives legal status exemptions from Haredi conscription. Nevertheless, in recent years as many as 1,000 Haredi Jews have chosen to volunteer to serve in the IDF, in a Haredi Jewish unit, the [Netzah Yehuda Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netzah_Yehuda_Battalion), also known as Nahal Haredi. The vast majority of Haredi men, however, continue to receive deferments from military service.[^cite_note-19]

In 2005, Justice Minister [Tzipi Livni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzipi_Livni) stated that the Tal Law, which by then had yet to be fully implemented, did not provide an adequate solution to Haredi conscription. The legislation originally intended for Haredi students to leave the yeshivas with shortened military service and then join the workforce, but in reality most chose to continue [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study) in order to avoid military service.[^cite_note-toi2012-18] At the time, only 1,115 of the 41,450 yeshiva students covered by the arrangement had taken the "decision year" provided by the law, and of these only 31 had later enlisted in the IDF.[^cite_note-20] In 2007, the Tal Law was extended until August 2012.

In January 2012, Defense Minister [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) said his ministry was preparing an alternative to the Tal Law. Dozens of IDF reserve soldiers had put up what they called "the suckers' camp" near the [Tel Aviv Savidor Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Savidor_Central_railway_station), to protest the possible extension of the Tal Law. Several politicians, public figures, disabled IDF veterans and high school and university students visited the protest encampment.[^cite_note-21]

### Conscription amendments

In February 2012, the [Supreme Court](1666510.md) ruled that the Tal Law in its current form was [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconstitutional) and could not be extended beyond August.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] After the law expired, [military conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_Israel) into the IDF became mandatory for all members of the Haredi community, with a penalty of imprisonment for up to five years for those who refuse to enlist.

In response, prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) said that the government would formulate a new bill that would guarantee a more equal sharing of the burden by all parts of Israeli society.[^cite_note-24] The issue also caused division within the [2nd Netanyahu government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-second_government_of_Israel) and almost led to a government collapse leading into the [2013 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Israeli_legislative_election).

Following unsuccessful attempts to draft a new law, including the [Plesner Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesner_Committee), a new committee was formed to decide on a new Haredi conscription policy.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] This committee was called the Special Committee for the Equal Sharing of the Burden Bill, and informally known as the "Shaked Committee" after its chairwoman MK [Ayelet Shaked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayelet_Shaked) of [Bayit Yehudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayit_Yehudi).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Large crowd of young men, dressed in black

Anti-conscription demonstration in March 2014

In March 2014, the committee proposed a bill for increasing enlistment among those eligible in the Haredi community, leading to widespread protests.[^cite_note-29]

On 12 March 2014, the bill passed the [Knesset](74656.md) as an amendment to the [Israeli Defense Service Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defense_Service_Law).[^cite_note-30] Under the new law, the government would establish annual enlistment targets for the Haredim that would increase gradually until 2017. If the targets are met by 2017, then enlistment will remain voluntary. But if they are not met, then conscription will begin for most Haredi students, with the only exemptions being for a total of 1,800 standout students, who would be allowed to continue their yeshiva studies.[^cite_note-31] This decision led to massive protests among the Haredim against conscription.[^cite_note-toi#1-32]

In November 2015, the Knesset under the [34th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_government_of_Israel) passed an amendment that provided extensions for the enlistment plan passed in March 2014. Under the new amendment, the deadline for enlistment was pushed back from 2017 to 2020, and criminal sanctions for those not enlisted were also pushed back until 2023.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

On 12 September 2017, the [Supreme Court](1666510.md) struck down both the 2014 and 2015 amendments, ruling that blanket military service exemptions for Haredi students were illegal and discriminatory. The court decided to terminate the amendments in a year to allow time for the Knesset to pass new legislation.[^cite_note-\:3-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

In February 2018, defense minister [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman) led a committee to draft a new conscription bill after the previous attempt was struck down. The bill passed first reading in July,[^cite_note-38] but could not continue after [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) withdrew support for it. The coalition reached a deadlock over the issue, with the Knesset dissolving itself and leading to early elections.[^cite_note-39] This would ultimately lead to the [2018–2022 Israeli political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932022_Israeli_political_crisis), with Haredi conscription remaining a cause for political deadlock.

On 20 January 2019, due to [continued political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932022_Israeli_political_crisis), the Supreme Court decided to extend the deadline for passing a conscription bill until 28 July 2019.[^cite_note-40] On 29 May 2019, coalition talks failed and the Knesset dissolved itself, leading to [a new election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election).[^cite_note-41]

In January 2022, the Knesset under the [36th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel) voted on the first reading of a new draft law, but it did not pass due to opposition from MK [Ghaida Rinawie Zoabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaida_Rinawie_Zoabi) of [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz).[^cite_note-42]

On 12 July 2022, the Supreme Court again gave an extension for passing a conscription bill due to ongoing political instability, with the deadline now set for July 2023.[^cite_note-43]

On 25 June 2023, as the court extension for passing a conscription bill was set to expire, the [cabinet](5006115.md) passed a resolution that extended conscription exemptions for the Haredim by 10 months until April 2024.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] By July, a petition was filed to the Supreme Court for conscripting Haredi students, with the court ruling to wait and provide some time for a government response.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-TOI20240625-47]

### Enforcement since 2024

On 26 February 2024, the Supreme Court issued an interim order requiring a government explanation for the non-enlistment of yeshiva students by 31 March.[^cite_note-48]

In March 2024, a draft was prepared for legislating Haredi conscription. This draft allowed continued exemption for those engaged in [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study), but everyone else would be required to serve in either the IDF or in non-military roles such as in [United Hatzalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Hatzalah) or the [Israel Police](745595.md). For the first time, a Haredi conscription draft was recognized by the Haredi rabbis.[^cite_note-49] However, chief rabbi [Yitzhak Yosef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Yosef) remained opposed to this conscription draft.[^cite_note-50]

Also in March 2024, Attorney General [Gali Baharav-Miara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gali_Baharav-Miara) instructed both the Education Ministry and the Defense Ministry to begin the drafting process for Haredi men.[^cite_note-\:3-35]

On 1 April 2024, the Supreme Court decided that the Haredim would no longer receive an exemption from military service and that yeshivas could no longer receive the associated government subsidies.[^cite_note-Guardian20240404-51][^cite_note-Shtetl20240410-52][^cite_note-53] In response, the coalition government clarified that it had not agreed to an extension of the exemption, which had thus expired.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

*The Times of Israel* reported that per government figures, 1,257 yeshivas would lose subsidies for 49,485 students receiving the exemption.[^cite_note-Shtetl20240410-52] Haredi lawmakers, members of the political parties [United Torah Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Torah_Judaism) and [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas), and supporters of the coalition government have stated their intention to walk out if the exemption removal was enforced; [Anshel Pfeffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshel_Pfeffer), a journalist for the newspaper [Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz), argued that these threats were hollow. Haredi youth in the ultra-Orthodox neighbourhood of [Mea Shearim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mea_Shearim) in Jerusalem burned Israeli flags and military uniforms in protest.[^cite_note-Guardian20240404-51][^cite_note-54]

On May 15, 2024, prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) revived a conscription bill from the [previous government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel) and presented it to committee in the Knesset.[^cite_note-55] On 10 June 2024, the bill passed first reading in the Knesset by a vote of 63 to 57 and was transferred to the [Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_and_Defense_Committee).[^cite_note-56]

On 2 June 2024, the Supreme Court reconvened with an expanded nine-judge panel, to hear a case on Haredi conscription.[^cite_note-\:3-35] On 25 June, the court agreed by unanimous decision that there is no longer a legal framework for blanket exemptions from conscription, and that the government has an obligation to begin conscripting Haredi men into military service.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-TOI20240625-47]

In response, officials from the ultra-Orthodox [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas) party urged potential conscripts to ignore any call-ups from the IDF and to protest. During at least one protest, protestors attacked both law enforcement and the vehicle of Minister [Yitzhak Goldknopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Goldknopf).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

In July 2024, the army issued draft notices for 3,000 Haredi men,[^cite_note-TOI20240718-60] but less than 10% showed up at recruitment centers. In November, 7,000 additional draft orders for Haredi men were approved,[^cite_note-61] and military officials also issued 1,126 arrest warrants for Haredi conscripts who [did not respond to drafting orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusal_to_serve_in_the_Israel_Defense_Forces).[^cite_note-62]

On 14 January 2025, defense minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) presented a policy outline regarding Haredi conscription into the IDF, which included annual conscription targets that gradually increased to 50% by 2032. Failure to meet these targets would result in financial sanctions on educational institutions such as [yeshivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva). The plan preserves exemption for elite students in [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study).[^cite_note-63]

On 4 August 2025, outgoing [Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_and_Defense_Committee) chair [Yuli Edelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuli_Edelstein) publicized his proposed version of the Haredi conscription bill, which included full conscription for anyone not engaged in full time [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study), along with implementing various penalties for draft evaders, including cancellation of drivers licenses and banning of international travel.[^cite_note-64]

On 27 November 2025, the new [Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_and_Defense_Committee) chairman [Boaz Bismuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boaz_Bismuth) laid out a revised bill on Haredi conscription, which would conscript 50% of Haredi men who are not engaged in full time [Torah study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah_study). This plan would allow for the conscription of an estimated 23,000 Haredim, out of a total of 80,000 Haredim currently eligible but exempt from military service.[^cite_note-65]

In response to demands for conscription, Haredi Israelis eligible for military service, mainly yeshiva students, staged several protests to express their desire to continue exemption from military service. Large anti-conscription protests have been held in Israel and worldwide. Three of these protests had more than 200,000 participants and were significant in the ultra-Orthodox community:[^cite_note-66]

- The ultra-Orthodox demonstration against the Supreme Court ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): הפגנת החצי מיליון), 1999.

- The ultra-Orthodox demonstration against conscription of yeshiva students ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): עצרת המיליון), 2014

- The Haredis demonstration against conscription of yeshiva students ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): עצרת המיליון), 2025, in response to arrests of yeshiva students refusing conscription.

### 2012

Smaller protests against Haredi conscription began around 2012 with the meeting of the [Plesner committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesner_Committee).[^cite_note-70] The first of such protests was held in [Kikar HaShabbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikar_HaShabbat) in [Jerusalem](16043.md), with thousands in attendance.[^cite_note-71]

### 2014

On February 24, 2014, the leaders of [Agudat Yisrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agudat_Yisrael), [Degel HaTorah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degel_HaTorah), and [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas), including Rav Aharon Leib Shteinman, Rav Shmuel Auerbach, the Vizhnitzer Rebbe, and Rav Shalom Cohen, gathered for a conference in [Bnei Brak](314671.md) and decided on a demonstration a week after the conference.[^cite_note-72] All Haredi boys and men over age nine were summoned to attend. Rav Shteinman publicly encouraged attendance at the protest. He said that in the IDF, there is [gilui arayot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilui_arayot) (sexual immorality), shefichut damim (bloodshed), and [avodah zarah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avodah_zarah) (idolatry), but greater than these three cardinal sins is the [chillul hashem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chillul_hashem) that a country calling itself the Jewish State should put quotas on Torah learning.[^cite_note-73] Hundreds of thousands of protesters lined the streets surrounding the area, with Jaffa Road designated for women, despite unfavorable weather. Many leaders of the Haredi community, including the rabbis of [Gur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ger_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)), [Belz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belz_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)) and [Vizhnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizhnitz_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)), Lithuanian rabbis [Aharon Leib Shteinman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aharon_Yehuda_Leib_Shteinman), [Chaim Kanievsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Kanievsky) and [Shmuel Auerbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Auerbach), Sephardic rabbis [Shalom Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalom_Cohen_\(rabbi\)) and Shimon Desserts and other members of the Great Council of Torah and the [Council of Torah Sages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moetzes_Gedolei_HaTorah) attended the rally. Members of the orthodox rabbinical community (including Yitzhak Tuvia Weiss, chief Rabbis David Lowe and Isaac Joseph, and Hasidic leaders, rabbis and public figures) were also in attendance. Small groups and religious Zionist rabbis, including Shmuel Eliyahu and Yaakov Shapira, were present.

Leading rabbis from the conservative wing of the [national religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) community (including [Shmuel Eliyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Eliyahu), Mordechai Sternberg, Micha Halevi, and [Shlomo Aviner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Aviner)) supported the rally,[^cite_note-Jpost,_massive-74] and a group of nationalist Haredi rabbis issued a proclamation calling on the public to participate in the religious, Zionist rally.[^cite_note-75] Other groups, such as the [Tzohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzohar_\(organization\)) and Beit Hillel rabbinical associations,[^cite_note-Jpost,_massive-74] and rabbis from the [religious Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) community (including [Haim Druckman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_Druckman))[^cite_note-Jpost,_massive-74][^cite_note-76] opposed the protest. After harsh commentary by a Haredi newspaper about Religious Zionist leader Haim Druckman, Yehoshua Shapira (rabbi of the Ramat Gan yeshiva) and the Association of Community Rabbis (led by Chief Rabbi of Tzfat Shmuel Eliyahu) canceled plans to attend the "million-man march".[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Roads in the capital around the protest area were blocked in the early afternoon, and Route 1, the main highway between the capital and the coast, was closed to private vehicles from 2\:00 to 7\:00 p.m.[^cite_note-79]

The organizers, who called for a "million-man protest"[^cite_note-toi#1-32] by men and boys aged nine and older,[^cite_note-80] estimated attendance at 500,000; police estimated a crowd at 300,000.[^cite_note-toi#1-32] Some believed that 600,000 were present, which led to a public recitation of the *Chacham HaRazim* blessing.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] All three major Jewish streams (Lithuanian, Hasidic and Sephardic) were represented.[^cite_note-83] The protest drew hundreds of thousands and was one of the largest in Israel's history, although it remained peaceful.[^cite_note-84] There was loudspeaker noise heard across Jerusalem as a result. It was secured by about 3,500 police and other security personnel.[^cite_note-toi#1-32] No speeches were made at the rally, but at its end statements received by the Council of Torah Sages were read opposing the conscription of [yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) and [kolel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolel) students.[^cite_note-85]

### 2015–2019

On 2 February 2015, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, hundreds of ultra-Orthodox Jews blocked roads in Israel, in the cities of [Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdod), [Hadera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadera), [Bnei Brak](314671.md), [Elad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elad) and more, About 50 were arrested, a policeman was slightly injured.[^cite_note-86] On 21 August 2016, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, riots broke out in Jerusalem and Beit Shemesh. stones were thrown at Israeli policemen. Three were arrested.[^cite_note-87]

On 8 February 2017, ultra-Orthodox Jews from Jerusalem Faction blocked many places in Israel. Three policeman were injured and 50 protesters were arrested.[^cite_note-88] On 29 March, ultra-Orthodox Jews from Edah HaChareidis and Jerusalem Faction were in a huge rally against the conscription and arrest of refusers. 30 were arrested.[^cite_note-89] On 11 June, a rally was held at Barclays Center in Brooklyn. Close to 20,000 Haredim attended. The speakers included Rabbi [Aaron Schechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Schechter), [Yeshiva Rabbi Chaim Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_Rabbi_Chaim_Berlin), Rabbi Leibish Leiser of [Pshevorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pshevorsk_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)), known as The Pshevorsker Rebbe, one of the most prominent leaders of the Haredi community of Antwerp, Belgium, and Rabbi Yaakov Shapiro, author of *The Empty Wagon: Zionism's Journey from Identity Crisis to Identity Theft*. A letter was read from Rabbi [Aharon Feldma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aharon_Feldma?action=edit\&redlink=1), the rosh yeshiva of [Yeshivas Ner Yisroel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshivas_Ner_Yisroel) in Baltimore, who was unable to attend in person. Rabbi Schechter lambasted the attempt to draft Orthodox Jews as an assault on the essential characteristics of religious Jews. On 26 October 2017, hundreds of ultra-Orthodox Jews held a protest at [Kikar HaShabbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikar_HaShabbat) in Jerusalem. 5 were arrested.[^cite_note-90] On 26 November, ultra-Orthodox Jews blocked [Chords Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chords_Bridge). 36 were arrested.[^cite_note-91]

On 8 March 2018, ultra-Orthodox Jews from the Jerusalem Faction protested in different places in Jerusalem.[^cite_note-92] On 22 March, ultra-Orthodox Jews from the Jerusalem Faction blocked entrance to Jerusalem. 28 were arrested.[^cite_note-93] On 5 July, [Edah HaChareidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edah_HaChareidis) held a protest at Kikar HaShabbat in Jerusalem, chanting "We'll die and not conscript".[^cite_note-94] On 6 August, 500 ultra-Orthodox Jews from the Jerusalem Faction protested in Bnei Brak. 29 were arrested.[^cite_note-95]

On 7 March 2019, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, more than 500 from the Jerusalem Faction blocked main roads in Jerusalem. they changed "We'll die and not conscript". 30 were arrested.[^cite_note-96] On 2 July, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, more than 2,000 from the Jerusalem Faction blocked main roads in Jerusalem. 22 were arrested.[^cite_note-97]

### 2020s



Ultra-Orthodox Jews hold a sign saying "Jewish military only by the [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah)", 2025

On 3 February 2020, hundreds of ultra-Orthodox Jews from the Jerusalem Faction blocked Israeli Highway 4 in [Bnei Brak](314671.md) in solidarity with "conscription refusers".[^cite_note-maariv.co.il-98][^cite_note-99] 3 policeman were injured and 13 were arrested.[^cite_note-maariv.co.il-98] On 22 December, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew (Shachna Rotenberg) was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, 2,000 from the Jerusalem Faction blocked [Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_1_\(Israel%E2%80%93Palestine\)). one policeman and one protester got injured. 3 were arrested[^cite_note-100] On 27 December, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew (Shachna Rotenberg) was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, more than 1,000 from the Jerusalem Faction blocked Highway 4. 13 protesters were arrested.[^cite_note-101]

On 15 November 2021, ultra-Orthodox Jews from the Jerusalem Faction blocked Israeli Highway 4 in [Bar-Ilan Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-Ilan_Interchange?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-102] 2 were arrested.[^cite_note-103]

On 6 March 2022, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, the Jerusalem Faction protested in Chords Bridge. Israeli politician [Yoel Razvozov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoel_Razvozov) was stuck in his car because of protesters. 7 were arrested.[^cite_note-104] On 8 March, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, the Jerusalem Faction blocked Israeli Highway 4. 7 were arrested.[^cite_note-105]

On 13 September 2023, following an incident where an ultra-Orthodox Jew was arrested for refusing to join the IDF, thousands from the Jerusalem Faction blocked the [Tel Aviv Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail), the entrance to Jerusalem, and Israeli Highway 4 in protests.[^cite_note-106] 3 were arrested.[^cite_note-107]

### Solidarity protests abroad

On June 9, 2013, a rally was held in [Foley Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foley_Square) in [Manhattan](45470.md) attended by 20,000–30,000 Haredim.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] Among the speakers was Rabbi Elya Ber Wachtfogel,[^cite_note-126] the [rosh yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_yeshiva) of [Yeshiva Gedolah Zichron Moshe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_Gedolah_Zichron_Moshe) in [South Fallsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Fallsburg,_New_York), N.Y.

On June 27, 2013, Haredim protested in front of the EU headquarters in [Brussels](3708.md), Belgium against Israel attempting to draft Orthodox *[yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva)* students.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] The protest was attended by Rabbi [Ephraim Padwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephraim_Padwa), head of The [Union of Orthodox Hebrew Congregations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Orthodox_Hebrew_Congregations) in London, [Rabbi Elyakim Schlesinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elyakim_Schlesinger), a prominent English *[rosh yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_yeshiva)* and internationally recognized *[halachic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halakha)* authority, and Rabbi Leibish Leiser of [Pshevorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pshevorsk_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)) from Antwerp, Belgium.[^cite_note-129]

A protest in London on March 3, 2014 drew 4,000 demonstrators[^cite_note-jewishnews.co.uk-130]

Close to 20,000 Haredim attended.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] The speakers included Rabbi [Aaron Schechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Schechter), [Yeshiva Rabbi Chaim Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_Rabbi_Chaim_Berlin), Rabbi Leibish Leiser of [Pshevorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pshevorsk_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)), known as The Pshevorsker Rebbe, one of the most prominent leaders of the Haredi community of Antwerp, Belgium, and Rabbi Yaakov Shapiro, author of *The Empty Wagon: Zionism's Journey from Identity Crisis to Identity Theft*.
