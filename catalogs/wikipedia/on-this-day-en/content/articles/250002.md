The **Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Ministère de l'Europe et des Affaires étrangères* [\[ministɛʁ də løʁɔp e dez‿afɛʁ(z‿)etʁɑ̃ʒɛʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French), **MEAE**) is the [ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)) of the [Government of France](3398091.md) that handles France's [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations). Since 1855, its headquarters have been located at 37 [Quai d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quai_d'Orsay), close to the [National Assembly](299616.md). The term **Quai d'Orsay** is often used as a [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymy) for the ministry. Its cabinet minister, the **minister for Europe and foreign affairs** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Ministre de l'Europe et des Affaires étrangères*) is responsible for the [foreign relations of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_France). The current officeholder, [Jean-Noël Barrot](54388162.md), was appointed in September 2024. For a brief period from 1984 to 1986, the office was titled **minister for external relations**.

In 1547, royal secretaries became specialised, writing correspondence to foreign governments and negotiating peace treaties. The four French [secretaries of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_state) where foreign relations were divided by region, in 1589, became centralised with one becoming first secretary responsible for international relations. The [Ancien Régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime) position of [Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_\(France\)) became Foreign Minister around 1723;[^cite_note-1] it was renamed Minister of Foreign Affairs in 1791 in the aftermath of early stages of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution). All ministerial positions were abolished in 1794 by the [National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Convention) and reestablished with the [Directory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Directory).

## Central administration



Foreign Affairs Ministry building on the [Quai d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quai_d'Orsay)

There are multiple services under its authority, along with that of some other ministers. Under the authority of the Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Development, that of Cooperation and European Affairs, and that of Foreign and European Affairs, there are numerous services directly related to the ministers. Here is a list of those services.

- The ministers' cabinet

- The office of cabinets, which gathers a personnel in charge of the administrative and logistics aspects of the three ministers' cabinets

- The budget control service (*CBCM*)

- General inspection of foreign affairs (*IGAE*)

- The prospective office (*DP*)

- The Protocole, upon which the President's protocol cell relies

- The Crisis management Department (*CDC*)
