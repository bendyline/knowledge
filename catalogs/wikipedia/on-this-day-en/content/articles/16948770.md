The [seventh major outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholera_outbreaks_and_pandemics) of [cholera](7591.md) began in 1961 and is ongoing.[^cite_note-epi-1] Cholera has become [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(epidemiology\)) in many countries. In 2017, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) announced a global strategy aiming to end the pandemic by 2030.[^cite_note-WHO-2]

This [pandemic](24255.md) is based on the strain called [El Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Tor); it started in [Indonesia](14579.md) in 1961 and spread to [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) (now [Bangladesh](3454.md)), by 1963.[^cite_note-Ahiarah_2008-3] It went to [India](14533.md) in 1964, and into the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) by 1966. In July 1970, there was an outbreak in [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa) (now Ukraine) and in 1972 there were reports of outbreaks in [Baku](4566.md), but the Soviet Union suppressed this information.[^cite_note-Ahiarah_2008-3] Cholera reached [Italy](14532.md) in 1973 from [North Africa](21714.md). [Japan](15573.md) and the [South Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia) saw a few outbreaks by the late 1970s.[^cite_note-Ahiarah_2008-3] In 1971, the number of cases reported worldwide was 155,000. But in 1991, it reached 570,000.[^cite_note-Hays_2005-4] The spread of the disease was helped by modern transportation and mass migrations. Mortality rates, however, dropped markedly as governments began modern curative and preventive measures. The usual mortality rate of 50% dropped to 10% by the 1980s and less than 3% by the 1990s.[^cite_note-Hays_2005-4]

In 1991, the strain made a comeback in [Latin America](18524.md). It began in Peru, where it killed roughly 10,000 people.[^cite_note-CBC_2015-5] Research has traced the origin of the strain to the seventh cholera pandemic.[^cite_note-WHO_2015-6] Researchers initially suspected the strain came to Latin America through Asia from contaminated water, but samples from Latin America and samples from Africa were found to be identical.[^cite_note-Lam_2010-7]



Bay of Bengal

This rapid transmission of the pathogen around the globe in the 20th century can be attributed chiefly to the major hub, the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md), from where the disease spread.

In the 2020s, global cholera cases rose sharply after decades of decline, driven by persistent poverty, conflict, and worsening climate change. By 2024, outbreaks had spread to sixty countries—mostly in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia—straining international response efforts and depleting vaccine supplies.[^cite_note-choups-8] As of 31 August 2025, 462,890 cases and 5,869 deaths had been reported from 32 countries across five WHO regions.[^cite_note-9]

Cholera is caused by eating food or drinking water that is contaminated with the bacteria *[V. cholerae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibrio_cholerae)*. It affects both children and adults, causing severe watery [diarrhea](53951.md) with [dehydration](155056.md). But, as noted, the El Tor strain has persisted for decades to the present, causing repeated epidemics in varied locations, with 570,000 cases in 1991 alone. [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) and other authorities believe that the seventh pandemic continues.

## Introduction

[Cholera](7591.md) is an acute diarrhoeal infection caused by the ingestion of food or water contaminated with the bacterium *[Vibrio cholerae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibrio_cholerae)*. Most commonly the contamination of food or water occurs via [faecal matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feces), and the infection is spread through the [faecal-oral route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecal%E2%80%93oral_route). Cholera has also been found to be caused by eating raw [shellfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellfish). Symptoms of the disease appear between 12 hours and 5 days of infection; however, only 10% of infected people show severe symptoms of watery diarrhoea, vomiting and leg cramps.[^cite_note-10] Cholera is diagnosed through a stool test or rectal swab, and today treatment takes the form of an [oral rehydration solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_Rehydration_Solution) (ORS). The ORS uses equimolar concentrations of [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium) and [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose) to maximise sodium uptake in the [small intestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_intestine), and carefully replaces fluid losses.[^cite_note-11] In severe cases, the rapid loss of bodily fluids leads to dehydration and patients are at risk of [shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_\(circulatory\)). This requires administration of [intravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous_therapy) fluids and [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic).

The transmission of cholera is closely linked to inadequate access to clean water and sanitation facilities and hence, people at risk largely live in [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum) and poor communities.[^cite_note-12]

## Epidemiology

The 7th pandemic is traced to early 1961 and is ongoing.[^cite_note-epi-1] The evolution of the classical cholera strain known from the first six pandemics was revealed through [genetic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_analysis). The first observation of the new lineage came from a laboratory in El Tor, [Egypt](8087628.md), in 1897. By this time, the 'El Tor' strain differed from its relatives by 30%.[^cite_note-Shultz_2016-13] It originated in [South-Asia](21566765.md), then transitioned to its [non-pathogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpathogenic_organisms) form in the [Middle East](19323.md) in 1900. Sometime between 1903 and 1908, the El Tor strain picked up [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) that triggered its ability to cause disease in humans.[^cite_note-Shultz_2016-13] Hence, it had mutated into the El Tor pandemic strain.[^cite_note-14] [Makassar, South Sulawesi](389464.md) was the source of a 1960 outbreak of the El Tor strain, where it gained new [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene) that likely increased transmissibility.[^cite_note-Shultz_2016-13] Cholera spread overseas in 1961, indicating a pandemic strain.

Many studies point to Indonesia as the source of the seventh cholera pandemic; however, research has indicated that outbreaks in [China](5405.md) between 1960 and 1990 were associated with the same sub-lineages. These strains spread globally from the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) on multiple occasions.[^cite_note-didelot-15] China has been classified as both a sink and source during the pandemic spread of cholera throughout the 1960s and 1970s. The suggestion that the pandemic spread of cholera may have been augmented by Chinese cases, in addition to China being identified as an origin for bordering countries, contrasts with the view that the pandemic began in Indonesia.[^cite_note-didelot-15]

During the 2020s, after decades of decline, cholera cases began to rise again, including in countries that had not recorded outbreaks for many years. The traditional drivers of cholera, such as poverty, inadequate sanitation, and conflict, persisted, while climate change and ongoing wars further worsened conditions.[^cite_note-choups-8] Extreme weather events such as floods, cyclones, and droughts reduced access to safe water and created favourable conditions for the disease to spread. By 2024, 60 countries had reported cholera cases, up from 45 in 2023. Most infections occurred in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia, which together accounted for 98% of global cases. These trends continued into 2025, placing heavy pressure on international response efforts and causing shortages of essential supplies, including vaccines.[^cite_note-choups-8] As of 31 August 2025, 462,890 cases and 5,869 deaths had been reported from 32 countries across five WHO regions.[^cite_note-16]

## Spread and mortality

The El Tor cholera outbreak was first reported in [Java](69336.md), a seaside community near [Kendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal,_Kendal) which was visited by travellers from [Makassar](389464.md) in May 1961.[^cite_note-17] Shortly after, [Semarang](1134311.md) and [Djakarta](16275.md) became infected in June.

The disease was carried into [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching), Sarawak when boats from Celebes participated in a [regatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regatta) in Kuching; the first cholera cases appeared on 1 July. This outbreak lasted two weeks, infecting 582 persons with 79 deaths (17% mortality). By August, the outbreak had reached [Kalimantan](165470.md) and [Macau](19068.md) (13 patients and six deaths). The first case in [Hong Kong](13404.md) appeared on 15 August in a community near Kwangtung, a fishing area. The second case was from a boat-dwelling population located between Kwangtung and Hong Kong. Hong Kong had 72 cases with 15 deaths (20.8% mortality).[^cite_note-Felsenfeld_1963-18]

By February 1, 1962, 4,107 people were infected with cholera, with 897 deaths (21.8% mortality). By September, despite a massive vaccination campaign, cholera had rapidly moved through the [Philippines](23440.md), where the number of infected people reached 15,000 by March 1962, with 2,005 deaths. In the Philippines alone, mortality reached 1,682 in 1962.[^cite_note-Kotar_2014-19] It was reintroduced into [British Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Borneo), supposedly by an [asymptomatic](602790.md) traveller from [Jolo Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolo). Outbreaks subsequently occurred in [Cambodia](334751.md), [Thailand](30128.md), [Singapore](27318.md) and [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-Felsenfeld_1963-18]

In 1963, [WHO](33583.md) declared that cholera remained the number-one killer in diseases subject to international [quarantine](25237.md), having been reported in [Taiwan](25734.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), Burma, [Cambodia](334751.md), [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Nepal](171166.md), [Thailand](30128.md), [Uzbekistan](31853.md) and [Hong Kong](13404.md).[^cite_note-Kotar_2014-19]

The mid-60s saw cholera infiltrate Southeast Asia, with outbreaks in [Chittagong](380451.md), Bangladesh, Cambodia, Thailand and Malaysia, and India in 1964. The El Tor strain moved further westward, invading South Asia in 1965, including [Pakistan](23235.md), [Nepal](171166.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), [Iran](14653.md) and part of [Uzbek SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_SSR). Iran, which had been free from cholera since 1939, reported 2,704 cases by mid-October.[^cite_note-Kotar_2014-19] When these outbreaks occurred in Iran, the [Pasteur Institute of Iran](41907959.md) produced 9.5 million cholera vaccines to protect the population of the eastern regions of Iran.[^cite_note-20] In 1966 Iraq reported its first case.

The cholera strain reached the Middle East and Africa in 1970 and spread rapidly. It is thought that a traveler returning from Asia or the Middle East introduced the disease into Africa.[^cite_note-Swerdlow_1994-21] The Arabian Peninsula, Syria and Jordan became infected, followed by Guinea in August 1970. Cholera was first thought to have spread along [waterways](174851.md) along the coast and into the interior along the [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River).

Coinciding with the commencement of Ramadan in November 1970, infected individuals seemed to travel by modernised rapid transport. This allowed cholera to extend 1,000 km, when it appeared in [Mopti, Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mopti). Subsequently, customary large gatherings of people facilitated the outward radiation of cholera.[^cite_note-Swerdlow_1994-21] From 1970 to 1971, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria and southern Cameroon, experienced outbreaks. The west-African outbreak of cholera during 1970–1971 infected more than 400,000 persons.[^cite_note-22] Africa had a high cholera [fatality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_fatality_rate) of 16% by 1962. 25 countries were infected by the end of 1971 and, between 1972 and 1991, cholera spread throughout much of the remainder of Africa.[^cite_note-Swerdlow_1994-21]

## Research

An international campaign began in 1970, including the research laboratory in [Dhaka](56656.md), Bangladesh; the [Southeast Asia Treaty Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia_Treaty_Organization) (SEATO), the United Kingdom, Australia, and various American agencies. Human volunteers took part in an [NIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health)-sponsored series of tests to develop an effective cholera vaccine. At this time, new outbreaks of cholera were occurring in Egypt, South Korea, and the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-Kotar_2014-19]

The 1964 invention of use of oral rehydration salts (ORS) to treat cholera was endorsed by [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) in the 1980s. This practice of replacing fluids and electrolytes is estimated to have saved the lives of 40 million individuals infected with cholera. As a result of the success of ORS treatment, the past 30 years have seen changes to cholera response that centred on treating affected individuals in the short term, and attempting to provide safe water and improved hygiene in the long term.[^cite_note-Ryan_2011-23]

The cholera pandemic beginning in 1962 is identified through the 'El Tor' biotype, and considerable research has been undertaken into this specific strain of cholera. The El Tor biotype has been demonstrated to have increased resistance to the environment.[^cite_note-Ryan_2011-23] This has heightened the risk of unknowing transmission from asymptomatic carriage in humans, as opposed to the classical biotype that caused the first six cholera pandemics.

Previously, health workers who were against the administration of the [cholera vaccine](21053458.md) based their opinion on the view that always limited resources should be directed at immediate rehydration and improved practices, and longer-term investment to provide safe water and improved sanitation. Such practices as appropriately cooking food before consumption, using sterilised water, following general personal hygiene, and sanitising environments, decrease the spread of cholera.[^cite_note-24] In the 21st century, cholera control activities have typically still been focused on emergency responses to outbreaks, with limited attention to the underlying causes that can prevent recurrence.[^cite_note-Somboonwit_2017-25]

But, the development of new and improved [cholera vaccines](21053458.md) has allowed this practice of emergency responses to be revised.[^cite_note-Ryan_2011-23] In addition, recent research has advanced understanding of cholera, its transmission and the [immune response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_response). Subsequently, these advances have resulted in the development of experimental cholera vaccines derived from non-living and [attenuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attenuated_vaccine) live strains.[^cite_note-26] By 2017 the [FDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDA) had approved a single-dose, live, oral cholera vaccine called [Vaxchora](21053458.md) for adults aged 18–64 who are travelling to an area of active cholera transmission.[^cite_note-27] A study in [Haiti](13373.md) has shown lasting protection from a two-dose cholera vaccine. During the [2010–2017 cholera outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010s_Haiti_cholera_outbreak) in Haiti, those who received the two doses of the vaccine were 76% less likely to become sick. This protection lasted 4 years.

The clinical severity of the El Tor biotype causing pandemic cholera in 1962 also resulted in modern research assessing administration of [antimicrobials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimicrobial) in the initial phase of an outbreak. This was tested in the 1970s with tetracycline but was found not to be useful due to [resistance against this antibiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimicrobial_resistance).[^cite_note-28] Questions have been raised alluding to newer drugs, and whether the administration of these will be more useful than such previous attempts.[^cite_note-Ryan_2011-23]

The ongoing seventh pandemic has affirmed that cholera is still prevalent in society and can cause high mortality. In 1992 the Global Task Force on Cholera Control (GTFCC) was organized to coordinate activities and support countries after a severe cholera outbreak in Peru. Today it consists of more than 30 collaborating institutions, including NGOs, academic institutions, and UN agencies supporting affected countries.[^cite_note-gtfcc-29] In 2017 they convened a high-level meeting with officials from cholera-affected countries, donors, and technical partners to announce their strategy "The Global Roadmap to 2030", an initiative to end cholera as a threat to public health by 2030. The three components of the strategy are: "early detection and quick response to contain the outbreaks; a multi-sectorial {sic} approach to prevent cholera recurrence, and, coordination of technical support and advocacy, resource mobilisation and partnership at the global level.[^cite_note-Somboonwit_2017-25]
