**Omar El Akkad** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عمر العقاد, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ʿUmar al-ʿAqqād*; born 1982) is an [Egyptian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Egyptian_Americans)-Canadian novelist and journalist.[^cite_note-NBFBio-1] A former staff reporter for *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail)*, he is the author of the novels *[American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_War_\(novel\))* (2017) and *[What Strange Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Strange_Paradise)* (2021), and of the nonfiction book *[One Day, Everyone Will Have Always Been Against This](81659794.md)* (2025).[^cite_note-WritersTrustBio-2] *What Strange Paradise* won the 2021 Scotiabank Giller Prize, and *One Day, Everyone Will Have Always Been Against This* won the 2025 [National Book Award for Nonfiction](33774285.md).[^cite_note-GillerWin-3][^cite_note-NBAWin-4]

## Early life and education

El Akkad was born in [Cairo](6293.md) and grew up in, [Doha](26214389.md), Qatar.[^cite_note-NBFBio-1] He moved to Montreal[^cite_note-\:0-5] as a teenager with his family.[^cite_note-NBFBio-1] He studied computer science at [Queen's University at Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston), where he worked at the student newspaper *[The Queen's Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_Journal)*.[^cite_note-QueensAlumni-6]

## Career

El Akkad worked for about a decade as a staff reporter for *The Globe and Mail,*[^cite_note-WritersTrustBio-2] after first completing a summer internship at their Toronto office.[^cite_note-\:0-5] His reporting included the [war in Afghanistan](19666611.md), military proceedings at [Guantanamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp), and the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) in Egypt.[^cite_note-WritersTrustBio-2] He later covered the [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) movement in [Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson,_Missouri), Missouri.[^cite_note-OPBAmericanWar-7] His journalism received a [National Newspaper Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Newspaper_Awards) for investigative reporting and the Goff Penny Memorial Prize for young Canadian journalists.[^cite_note-OPB2021Awards-8]

His debut novel, *[American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_War_\(novel\))*, was published in 2017.[^cite_note-NYTReview-9] The novel won the Rakuten Kobo Emerging Writer Prize and the [Oregon Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Book_Awards) for fiction, and it was shortlisted for the 2017 [Atwood Gibson Writers' Trust Fiction Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atwood_Gibson_Writers'_Trust_Fiction_Prize).[^cite_note-KoboPrize-10][^cite_note-OPB2021Awards-8][^cite_note-WTShortlist-11] In 2019, the [BBC](19344654.md) included *American War* on its list of 100 novels that shaped the world.[^cite_note-BBC100-12]

His second novel, *[What Strange Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Strange_Paradise)*, was published in 2021.[^cite_note-NPRWSP-13] It won the 2021 [Giller Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giller_Prize), the 2022 Oregon Book Award for fiction, and a 2022 [Pacific Northwest Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest_Booksellers_Association_Award).[^cite_note-GillerWin-3][^cite_note-OBA2022-14][^cite_note-PNBA2022-15]

In 2025, El Akkad published his first nonfiction book, *[One Day, Everyone Will Have Always Been Against This](81659794.md)*.[^cite_note-GuardianOD-16] The book won the 2025 [National Book Award for Nonfiction](33774285.md).[^cite_note-NBAWin-4]

El Akkad's books have been translated into 13 languages.[^cite_note-NBFBio-1]

## Personal life

In a 2017 essay for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, El Akkad wrote about being Arab and Muslim in the U.S.[^cite_note-GuardianArabBrown-17] He lives in [Portland](23503.md), Oregon.[^cite_note-18]
