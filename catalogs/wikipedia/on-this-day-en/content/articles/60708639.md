**André Claro Amaral Ventura** (Portuguese pronunciation: [\[ɐ̃ˈdɾɛ vẽˈtuɾɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese); born 15 January 1983) is a Portuguese politician and founder of the [far-right](221220.md) political party [Chega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chega_\(political_party\)).[^cite_note-1] He is the [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Portugal\)) since [Portugal's 2025 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portuguese_legislative_election).

Ventura was affiliated with the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) (PSD) until 2018, having run for Mayor of [Loures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loures) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Portuguese_local_elections) as the PSD candidate. He founded the political party [Chega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chega_\(political_party\)) in April 2019 and six months later was elected to the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md) in the [October 2019 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Portuguese_legislative_election). In 2021, he ran for [President of Portugal](516093.md), coming third in the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_presidential_election) with 11.9% of the votes. In the [2024 Portuguese legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Portuguese_legislative_election), Chega, under his leadership, received 18.1% of the vote, more than quadrupling its seat count to a final total of 50.[^cite_note-2] A year later in the [2025 Portuguese legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portuguese_legislative_election), Chega's vote total rose to 22.8% of the vote and the party won 60 seats, overtaking the [Socialist Party](403237.md) for second place.

## Early life and education

Ventura is the son of the owner of a small local bicycle shop,[^cite_note-3] and an office worker. He is a native of [Algueirão–Mem Martins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algueir%C3%A3o%E2%80%93Mem_Martins), [Sintra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintra), a suburban locality in the [Lisbon metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_metropolitan_area). Unlike his peers, he was not raised in a religion because his parents wanted him to choose his own.[^cite_note-sol-4] At 14, he became an enthusiastic Catholic, was baptised, and made his [first communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Communion) and [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation). He wanted to be a priest and attended the Penafirme Seminary, the [minor seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_seminary) of the [Patriarchate of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Lisbon), but said he did not continue his ecclesiastical formation because he fell in love.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-sol-4] He graduated in law from the Law Faculty of [NOVA University Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOVA_University_Lisbon), with a [grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grading_in_education) of 19 out of 20.[^cite_note-6] As a law student he took part in the [ERASMUS Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Programme) in [Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salamanca), Spain.[^cite_note-\:3-7]

He published two novels, *Montenegro* in 2008, and *A Última Madrugada do Islão* ("The Last Dawn of Islam") in 2009, both with significant elements of [female submission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_submission) and [homoeroticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoeroticism).[^cite_note-Alves2019-8][^cite_note-Alves2020-9] Notably, in *Montenegro*, the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) word for uprising ([intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intifada)) is used four times: three times as a metaphor for strength and courage, and once when describing the act of [sexual penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_penetration).[^cite_note-Alves2019-8] The publication of *A Última Madrugada do Islão*, a novel about the death of [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat), was suspended by the publishers, Chiado Editora, for its "incendiary potential", for its gratuitous references to [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) and the leaders of the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization).[^cite_note-10]

In 2013, he finished his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) in [public law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_law) from the Faculty of Law, [University College Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Cork), [Ireland](147575.md),[^cite_note-11] with a [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) from the Portuguese national science foundation, the [Foundation for Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funda%C3%A7%C3%A3o_para_a_Ci%C3%AAncia_e_Tecnologia).[^cite_note-12] In the thesis, he criticised "criminal populism" and "stigmatisation of minorities", and revealed concern about the "expansion of police powers".[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In a 2019 interview to *[Diário de Notícias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di%C3%A1rio_de_Not%C3%ADcias)* addressing the apparent contradictions between the issues raised in his PhD thesis and his later political views, Ventura said he has "always made a distinction between science and opinion" and called his thesis "scientific analysis, not ideological postulate".[^cite_note-15]

## Career

He taught at the [Autonomous University of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidade_Aut%C3%B3noma_de_Lisboa), from 2013 to 2019, and at NOVA, from 2016 to 2018.[^cite_note-parlamento-16] He worked at Caiado Guerreiro, a prominent Portuguese [law firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_firm), from 2018 to 2019,[^cite_note-\:1-17] and was a consultant at Finpartner, a [tax advising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_advisor) firm,[^cite_note-\:0-18] for 9 months until 2020. He also had a [column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_\(periodical\)) in the newspaper *[Correio da Manhã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correio_da_Manh%C3%A3_\(Portugal\))*, the most widely read daily newspaper in the country,[^cite_note-19] and, from 2014 to 2020, was a football commentator representing [S.L. Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica)'s point of view[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:2-21] on the TV channel [CMTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMTV).[^cite_note-\:1-17] He is also a jurist-consultant of the Tax Authority (AT) where he was employed from 2011 to 2014. As a Portuguese [civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service), he was granted leave[^cite_note-22] from AT without pay since 2014 to teach and ultimately offer tax consultancy and advisory services in the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector), and had also previously enjoyed the right to be absent from his workplace in the [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) due to his student-worker status as an international doctoral student until 2013.[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-\:1-17][^cite_note-23]

In an interview in July 2017, in response to Ventura's statements about [Portuguese Romani people](28870464.md), [José Pinto Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Pinto_Coelho) (leader of far-right and previously named [National Renewal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergue-te)) wrote that "unfortunately, it seems, some of 'my people' are still in the parties of the system". In another, Ventura said that he "vehemently repudiates the support of the far-right".[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In the course of the same campaign, Ventura made several controversial statements about the Romani community in the municipality of Loures,[^cite_note-26] having become the target of a criminal complaint presented by the opposing candidate from the [Left Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Bloc_\(Portugal\)), [Fabian Figueiredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Figueiredo).[^cite_note-27] He was accused by Pinto Coelho of stealing the speech from the National Renewal Party.[^cite_note-28] In October 2017, Ventura stated that he was ready to dispute the leadership of the PSD, in case nobody else advanced against [Rui Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_Rio).[^cite_note-29]

On 9 April 2019, he founded the political party [Chega](60708659.md), and three days later he joined the [Basta!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basta!_\(coalition\)) coalition for the [2019 European Parliamentary Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_Portugal). Failing to elect any MEP, the coalition was dissolved on 30 July 2019. He ran in the [2019 Portuguese legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Portuguese_legislative_election) as the main candidate of Chega's [electoral list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_list) for the [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_\(Assembly_of_the_Republic_constituency\)) constituency; he was the party's first and single member to be elected to Parliament. He claims to have positions that are "economically liberal, culturally nationalist and conservative in matters of customs".[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-sol-4]

## Member of the Assembly of the Republic



Ventura in the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md)

André Ventura was elected a member of the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md) for the [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_\(Assembly_of_the_Republic_constituency\)) constituency in the [2019 Portuguese legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Portuguese_legislative_election). He claims to be "the voice of common people" and an "anti-system politician". In September 2020, he presented a proposal to decrease the number of deputies from 230 to 100, which was ruled unconstitutional by the [Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee) on Constitutional Affairs, Rights, Freedoms and Guarantees.[^cite_note-31] In November, he renounces these proposals to accompany the PSD.[^cite_note-32] In January 2020, he proposed a 5 to 7.5% decrease in Members' salaries.[^cite_note-33]

He provoked an outcry in Parliament in January 2020 by proposing that [Joacine Katar Moreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joacine_Katar_Moreira), an Assembly member born in Guinea Bissau who said that museum items from Portugal's former colonies be returned, be similarly "returned to her country of origin".

Ventura was present at a Zero Movement protest in front of the Assembly. The Zero Movement is an unofficial police union that has been accused of political links to Ventura's party. The only politician to speak, he did so allegedly without an invitation from union leaders. He received a shower of applause.[^cite_note-34]

In November 2020, he was fined more than €400 for discrimination against Romani communities. In December of the same year, he was ordered to pay €3,370 for ethnic discrimination in the form of harassment. Ventura, later in a press conference at the Assembly of the Republic, stated that he would not pay the fine: "to limit the freedom of expression of a citizen, a deputy of the nation and a political leader".[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Ventura criticised the Minister of Justice, [Francisca Van Dunem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisca_Van_Dunem), for the release of prisoners to ease COVID-19 transmissions, saying that the measure was an "infamy".[^cite_note-38]

&#x20;Ventura criticised the state of emergency decree of 17 December 2020, saying:&#x20;

> "This is not really a state of emergency. It is a state of chaos over a state of chaos, which is destroying the lives of the Portuguese people without planning, that the only thing they have to give to the country is a Director of Health who says to have breakfast at Christmas and \[this] will solve your problems."[^cite_note-39]

On 22 December 2020, Ventura requested the temporary suspension of his term in the Assembly to run in the 2021 Portuguese presidential election[^cite_note-40] but this suspension was prevented by the Parliament on 29 December.[^cite_note-41] On 31 December, after the decision of the majority of the parliamentary groups not to authorise the suspension of mandate, Ventura advanced with a subpoena against the Assembly of the Republic and [Ferro Rodrigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Ferro_Rodrigues), the [Speaker of the Assembly of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Assembly_of_the_Republic_\(Portugal\)), in the [Supreme Administrative Court of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Administrative_Court_\(Portugal\)).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Presidential campaigns



Ventura's speech during election night of the [2021 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_presidential_election)

### 2021

On 8 February 2020, in [Portalegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portalegre,_Portugal), Ventura announced his candidacy for the office of [President of the Republic](516093.md) in the [2021 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_presidential_election).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

Ventura invited actress [Maria Vieira](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria%20Vieira) to be his [campaign chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_manager) (*mandatária*) for the Portuguese communities abroad[^cite_note-47] and chose Patrícia Sousa Uva (ex-member of Chega) to be the national director. Subsequently, with the resignation of the latter, Ventura invited Rui Paulo Sousa, 7th member of the national board, to become the national campaign director.[^cite_note-48]

On 18 December 2020, Ventura handed 10,250 signatures of proponents to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_\(Portugal\)), as legally required to formalise his candidacy for [Belém Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_Palace).[^cite_note-49] On 30 December, his candidacy was formally accepted by the Constitutional Court.[^cite_note-50]

At the beginning of the electoral campaign, the president of the French [National Rally](67119.md) party, [Marine Le Pen](572897.md), confirmed that she would go to Lisbon to support Ventura's presidential candidacy.[^cite_note-51]

In a televised debate against incumbent [Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa](1652059.md), Ventura showed a photograph of the president in the Bairro da Jamaica, a poor and largely black neighborhood in [Amora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amora_\(Seixal\)), [Setúbal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set%C3%BAbal_District), where there had been tensions with the police. He accused the president of not being truly right-wing, and called the black residents in the photograph "bandits".[^cite_note-52] In September 2021, a Portuguese court convicted Ventura for [social segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_segregation#Social_segregation) in the aftermath of these events related to Bairro da Jamaica.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

Ventura came third with 11.9% of the vote, behind Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa (60.7%) and the [Socialist Party](403237.md)’s [Ana Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Gomes) (12.9%).[^cite_note-55]

### 2026

Ventura placed second in the first round [2026 Portuguese presidential election](68734153.md) on 18 January with 23.5% of the vote and was defeated by [António José Seguro](35591042.md) from the [Socialist Party](403237.md) in a runoff on 8 February.[^cite_note-56]

## Political views



Spanish [Vox](41928488.md)’s [Santiago Abascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Abascal) and [Chega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chega_\(political_party\)) leader André Ventura in Lisbon, 2021

Ventura has expressed controversial views in the past, whilst the majority of international media refer to his ideology as being far-right.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Ventura categorizes himself as an "[economic liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism), [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) and [conservative](6675.md)". Many of his statements on social issues are said to be [anti-Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Romani_sentiment), [Islamophobic](162173.md), [xenophobic](34141.md) and [sexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexism) by the [news media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media).[^cite_note-61] In a TV debate in 2024, [Luís Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Montenegro) called Ventura "xenophobic, racist and demagogic".[^cite_note-62]

About [António de Oliveira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Oliveira_Salazar), Portuguese dictator during the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)) regime, André Ventura said: "The Republic led by Dr. António de Oliveira Salazar, for most of the time, also didn't solve \[the country's problems] and set us back a long way in various aspects. It didn't allow us to have the [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development) that we could have had, especially in the post-World War II framework. Portugal could have developed extraordinarily well and we fell behind, just like the [Spaniards](963152.md)".[^cite_note-63]

Under Ventura's leadership, the [Chega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chega_\(political_party\)) party has been the target of critics who underline the party's extreme views on various subjects, some of which include the negative comments regarding immigration and minorities, namely the [Romani](28870464.md) and some [black communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Portuguese_people)[^cite_note-64] subjected to [ghettoization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghetto)[^cite_note-65] living in problematic neighborhoods associated with high[^cite_note-66] [crime rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_statistics) that Ventura described[^cite_note-67] at one time as bandits and for which he was convicted by the courts of justice.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Ventura's comments on Romani people are often described as racist and xenophobic.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

On being compared negatively to [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md), Ventura responded "I am very accustomed to that and it doesn't worry me. These are the ideas that I believe in. In addition to [life imprisonment](148681.md) and [chemical castration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_castration), I also want a reduction in Islamic migration, especially from countries known for terrorism".[^cite_note-sol-4] Ventura attended [Trump's January 2025 inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) and has embraced the support of Bolsonaro.[^cite_note-France_24,_May_2025-74]

Ventura supports some rights for gay people, but he believes that same-sex couples should be in [civil partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_partnerships) and not marriages.[^cite_note-sol-4] He is personally opposed to [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Portugal), but does not want the procedure to be criminalised for women.[^cite_note-sol-4] He supports the [legalisation and regularisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalization_of_prostitution) of [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution_in_Portugal) as a way to protect and integrate sex workers. However, he believes that [legalisation of recreational drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_policy_of_Portugal) increases drug traffic.[^cite_note-sol-4] He is personally opposed to [bullfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_bullfighting), but opposes its sudden abolition due to the economic role it plays in some towns.[^cite_note-sol-4]

In May 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Portugal), Ventura proposed a specific containment plan for the Romani community.[^cite_note-75] He was lambasted for this proposal by professional footballer [Ricardo Quaresma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Quaresma), of Romani descent.[^cite_note-76] In June 2020, Ventura organised a counter-protest the day after anti-racist concentrations were announced in honour of actor Bruno Candé, victim of a premeditated homicide. This counter-protest was made under the motto "Portugal is not racist", denying the allegation of racism in Portugal and condemning the "[politically correct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_correctness)" associations and affirming that the counter-protest "is a manifestation of everything but [white supremacy](33178.md)".[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

He has spoken in admiration of [Mariano Rajoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Rajoy), conservative former [prime minister of Spain](203556.md).[^cite_note-sol-4] Ventura signed the *[Madrid Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Charter)*, a document drafted by the Spanish party [Vox](41928488.md) that describes [far left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics) groups as enemies of [Ibero-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibero-America) involved in a "criminal project" that are "under the umbrella of the Cuban regime".[^cite_note-\:42-79] On 24 February 2022, Twitter permanently suspended Ventura's account for violating the rules of the social network regarding the "conduct of propagating hate".[^cite_note-\:43-80] Shortly after, the suspension of his account was lifted.[^cite_note-81]

Changing and evolving the focus of his speech, Ventura has expressed views in which he says that he does not want immigrants unable to integrate and dedicated to a life of marginalization and economic deprivation for themselves and their children, perpetuating cycles of poverty and crime, and constraining the Portuguese welfare state, the [national health service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servi%C3%A7o_Nacional_de_Sa%C3%BAde), the security forces, wages and housing in the country.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

André Ventura is married to Dina Marques Nunes Ventura,[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-\:1-17] a children's [physiotherapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiotherapist) at a hospital in Lisbon, who met André Ventura after he had left law school at the Universidade Nova de Lisboa.[^cite_note-86] They are [childless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childlessness)[^cite_note-87] but he wants to be a father someday.[^cite_note-88]

Mário Rui Leal Pedras, a priest of the [Lisbon Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Lisbon) in the churches of São Nicolau and Santa Maria Madalena, located in Lisbon's [Baixa Pombalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Baixa), who celebrated the [Catholic wedding](395673.md) of Ventura, is his [confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessor) and [spiritual director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_direction) since when Ventura was still a university student.[^cite_note-89] In 2025, Ventura said his path had been guided by a "divine mission".[^cite_note-France_24,_May_2025-74]

He is a [supporter of S.L. Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporters_of_S.L._Benfica).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-\:2-21]

Ventura spoke in 2024 of [Matteo Salvini](1767165.md) being "a very good friend" and of having "a great relation" with [Geert Wilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geert_Wilders).[^cite_note-FT,_2024-92]
