**Sicily** ([Italian](14708.md) and [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language): *Sicilia*)[^cite_note-7], officially the **Sicilian Region**,[^cite_note-8] is an island in the central [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and one of the twenty [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of [Italy](14532.md), situated south of the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula) in [continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe). With over 4.7 million inhabitants, including 1.2 million in and around the capital city of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo),[^cite_note-population-3] it is both the largest and most populous island in the Mediterranean Sea.

Sicily's namesake is the [Sicels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicels), who inhabited the eastern part of the island during the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). Sicily has a rich and unique culture in [arts](#Art_and_architecture), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Sicily), [literature](#Literature), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_cuisine), and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Baroque). Its most prominent landmark is [Mount Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna), the tallest active [volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) in Europe, and one of the most active in the world, currently 3,403 m (11,165 ft) high. The island has a typical [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate). It is separated from [Calabria](44772.md) by the [Strait of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina). It is one of the five [Italian autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy#Autonomous_regions_with_special_statute) and is considered part of [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy).

The earliest [archaeological record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_record) of human activity on the island dates to around 14,000 BC. By around 750 BC, Sicily had three [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) and around twelve [Greek colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Italy) along its coasts, becoming one of the centers of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia). The [Sicilian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Wars) of 580–265 BC were fought between the Carthaginians and Greeks, and the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) of 264–146 BC were fought between Rome and Carthage. The Roman province of [Sicilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilia_\(Roman_province\)) ended with the fall of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the 5th century AD. Sicily was ruled during the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages) by the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals), the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), and the [Emirate of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily).

The [Norman conquest of southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_southern_Italy) led to the creation of the [County of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Sicily) in 1071, which was succeeded by the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily) in 1130.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Following the [Sicilian Vespers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Vespers) in 1282, Sicily was ruled by the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) and then the [Monarchy of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain), either in [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) with the crown or by a [cadet branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet_branch), except for a brief period of [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy) and then [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) rule in 1713–1735. In 1816, the kingdom unified with the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples) to form the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies). Following the [Expedition of the Thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_the_Thousand), an invasion led by [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi), and a subsequent [plebiscite](51582.md), the island became part of the [newly unified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy) [Italy](14532.md) in 1860. Sicily was given special status as an [autonomous administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) on 15 May 1946, 18 days before the [1946 Italian institutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Italian_institutional_referendum).

## Etymology

The name *[Sicilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilia_\(Roman_province\))* was given to the [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) in 241 BC. It is named after the [Sicels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicels), who inhabited the eastern part of the island during the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). The ancient name of the island is *Trinacria* (Greek [Τρινακρία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%A4%CF%81%CE%B9%CE%BD%CE%B1%CE%BA%CF%81%CE%AF%CE%B1) "having three headlands") for its triangular shape, likely a re-interpretation of earlier ([Homeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeric_Greek)) *[Thrinacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrinacia)*. The Greek name was rendered as *Trīnācrĭa* in [classical Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Latin) ([Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil), [Ovid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## History

Main article: [History of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sicily)

### Prehistory



Megaliths of [Argimusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argimusco), [Montalbano Elicona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montalbano_Elicona)

Humans first colonized Sicily towards the end of the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene), around 16,000 years ago, by people associated with the [Epigravettian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigravettian) culture.[^cite_note-13]

Discoveries of [dolmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmen) on the island (dating to the second half of the third millennium BC) seem to offer new insights into the culture of primitive Sicily.[^cite_note-Piccolo-14] The impact of at least two influences is clear: the European one coming from the Northwest, and the Mediterranean influence of an eastern heritage.[^cite_note-Piccolo-14]<sup>: 31 </sup>

### Antiquity

Main articles: [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia), [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), and [Sicilia (Roman province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilia_\(Roman_province\))



Ruins of the ancient Phoenician city of [Motya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motya)



Ruins of the ancient Carthaginian city of [Soluntum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluntum)



[Temple of Concordia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Concordia,_Agrigento), [Valle dei Templi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_dei_Templi), an [archaeological site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) in [Agrigento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigento). It is one of the most outstanding examples of ancient Greek art and architecture of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia).

[^cite_note-15]



[Villa Romana del Casale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Romana_del_Casale), a large and elaborate [Roman villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_villa) or palace located about 3 km from the town of [Piazza Armerina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Armerina)

The original classical-era inhabitants of Sicily comprised three defined groups of the [ancient peoples of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ancient_peoples_of_Italy): the [Sicani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicani), the [Elymians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elymians) and the [Sicels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicels). The most prominent and by far the earliest of these were the Sicani, who ([Thucydides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thucydides) writes) arrived from the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) (perhaps [Catalonia](6822.md)).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Some modern scholars, however, suggest classifying the Sicani as possibly an [Illyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians) tribe.[^cite_note-18] Important historical evidence has been discovered in the form of cave drawings by the Sicani, dated from the end of the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) epoch around 8000 BC.[^cite_note-sicanian-19] The Elymians, thought to have come from the area of the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea), became the next tribe to join the Sicanians on Sicily.[^cite_note-20]

No evidence survives of warring between tribes, but the Sicanians moved eastwards when the Elymians settled in the northwest corner of the island. The [Sicels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicels) are thought[^cite_note-sicanian-19] to have originated in [Liguria](71864.md); they arrived from mainland Italy in 1200 BC and forced the Sicanians to move back across Sicily and to settle in the middle of the island.[^cite_note-sicanian-19] Other minor [Italic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_peoples_of_Italy) who settled in Sicily included the [Ausones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ausones) ([Aeolian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_Islands), [Milazzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milazzo)) and the [Morgetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgetes) of [Morgantina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgantina).

The [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) settlements in the western part of the island predate the arrival of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_people) colonists.[^cite_note-catholi-21] From about 750 BC, the Greeks began to live in Sicily ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Σικελία – *Sikelia*), establishing many significant settlements. The most important colony was in [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily); others grew up at [Akragas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigentum), [Selinunte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selinunte), [Gela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gela), [Himera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himera) and [Zancle](59705.md).[^cite_note-22] The native Sicani and Sicel peoples became [absorbed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation) into the [Hellenic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) with relative ease, and the area became part of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia) along with the coasts of the [south of the Italian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy), which the Greeks had also colonised. Sicily had fertile soils, and the successful introduction of [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) and [grape vines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape_vine) fostered profitable trading.[^cite_note-knowital-23] [Greek culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Greece) significantly centered around [Greek religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_ancient_Greece), and the settlers built many [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_temple) throughout Sicily, including several in the *Valley of the Temples* at [Agrigento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigento).[^cite_note-24]

Politics on the island became intertwined with those of Greece; [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) became desired by the [Athenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian) who set out on the [Sicilian Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Expedition) (415–413 BC) during the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War). Syracuse gained [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta) and [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth) as allies and, as a result, defeated the Athenian expedition. The victors destroyed the Athenian army and their ships, selling most of the survivors into [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_ancient_Greece).[^cite_note-25]

The Greek kingdom of Syracuse controlled most of eastern Sicily while [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) controlled the western side.[^cite_note-26] The two cultures began to clash, leading to the [Greek-Punic wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek-Punic_wars) (between 580 and 265 BC). The Greek states had begun to make peace with the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) in 205 BC,[^cite_note-archeologiaviva-27] before the Romans sought to [annex](18950961.md) Sicily as their republic's first [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province). Rome attacked Carthage's holdings in Sicily in the [First Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Punic_War) (264 to 241 BC) and won, making Sicily–with the exception of Syracuse–the first Roman province outside of the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula) by 242 BC.[^cite_note-28]

In the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War) (218 to 201 BC), the Carthaginians attempted to recapture Sicily. Some of the Greek cities on the island who were loyal to Rome switched sides to help the Carthaginians, prompting a Roman military response. [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes), who lived in Syracuse, helped defend his city from Roman invasion; Roman troops killed him after they captured Syracuse in 212 BC.[^cite_note-29] The Carthaginian attempt failed, and Rome became more unrelenting in its annihilation of the invaders; [Roman consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_consul) [M. Valerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Valerius_Laevinus) told the [Roman Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate) in 210 BC that "no Carthaginian remains in Sicily".[^cite_note-30]



A 10th century castle built by the [Muslims in Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Sicily) on the mountain top of the mountain in Agira in central Sicily.

As the Roman Republic's [granary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granary), Sicily ranked as an important province, divided into two [quaestorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaestor): Syracuse to the east and [Lilybaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilybaeum) to the west.[^cite_note-catholi-21] Roman rule introduced the [Latin language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) to the island, which underwent a slow process of [latinisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinisation_of_Greek) but Sicilian culture remained largely Greek and the Greek language did not become extinct on the island, facilitating its [re-hellenisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization) much later under the Byzantines.[^cite_note-catholi-21] The prosperity of the island went into sharp decline during the governorship of [Verres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verres) (73 to 71 BC). In 70 BC, the noted statesman [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero) condemned the misgovernment of Verres in his oration *[In Verrem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Verrem)*.[^cite_note-31]

Various groups used the island as a power base at different times: slave insurgents occupied it during the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Servile_War) (135−132 BC) and [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Servile_War) (104−100 BC) [Servile Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servile_Wars). [Sextus Pompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextus_Pompey) had his headquarters there during the [Sicilian revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_between_Sextus_Pompey_and_the_Second_Triumvirate) of 44 to 36 BC. Christianity first appeared in Sicily during the years following AD 200; between this time and AD 313, when Emperor [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I) lifted the prohibition on Christianity, a significant number of [Sicilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilians) had become [martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr), including [Agatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_of_Sicily), [Christina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Christina_of_Bolsena), [Lucy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lucy), and [Euplius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euplius).[^cite_note-earlymediev-32] Sicily remained a Roman province for around 700 years.[^cite_note-earlymediev-32]

### Roman and Germanic viceregal rule (469–535)



[Belisarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisarius) in [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily)

The [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) began falling apart after the invasion of [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals), Alans, and Sueves [across the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_of_the_Rhine) on the last day of 406. Eventually the Vandals, after roaming about western and southern [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) (present-day [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia)) for 20 years, moved to North Africa in 429 and occupied Carthage in 439. The Franks moved south from present-day Belgium. The Visigoths moved west and eventually settled in Aquitaine in 418; the Burgundians were settled in present-day Savoy in 443.

The Vandals found themselves in a position to threaten Sicily – only 100 miles away from their North African bases.[^cite_note-jpriv-33] After taking Carthage, the Vandals, personally led by King [Gaiseric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaiseric), laid siege to Palermo in 440 as the opening act in an attempt to wrest the island from Roman rule.[^cite_note-34] The Vandals made another attempt to take the island one year after the 455 sack of Rome, at Agrigento, but were defeated decisively by [Ricimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricimer) in a [naval victory off Corsica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Corsica) in 456.[^cite_note-35] The island remained under Roman rule until 469. The Vandals lost possession of the island 7 years later in 476 to the [East Germanic tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) of the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), who then controlled Italy and Dalmatia.[^cite_note-jpriv-33] The island was granted by the Vandal king [Geiseric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaiseric) to [Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer), the ruler of Italy, in exchange for the payment of tribute. The Vandals kept a small part of the island, probably at the western end and centred on [Lilybaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilybaeum).[^cite_note-36] They lost this in 491 after making one last attempt to conquer the island from this port.[^cite_note-37] The Ostrogothic conquest of Sicily (and of Italy as a whole) under [Theodoric the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great) began in 488. The Byzantine Emperor [Zeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno_\(emperor\)) had appointed Theodoric as a military commander in Italy. The Goths were Germanic, but Theodoric fostered Roman culture and government and allowed freedom of religion.[^cite_note-38] In 461, from the age of seven or eight until 17 or 18, Theodoric had become a Byzantine hostage; he resided in the great palace of Constantinople, was favoured by Emperor [Leo I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_I_\(emperor\)) (r. 457–474) and learned to read, write and do arithmetic.[^cite_note-39]

### Byzantine period (535–827)

Further information: [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire)



Image depicting the [Byzantine army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_army) landing in Sicily commanded by [George Maniaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Maniaces)



The [Saracen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracen) conquest of the Byzantine stronghold Syracuse, [Siege of Syracuse (877–878)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Syracuse_\(877%E2%80%93878\))

After taking areas occupied by the Vandals in North Africa, [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) retook Italy as an ambitious attempt to recover the lost provinces in the West. The re-conquests marked an end to over 150 years of accommodating policies with tribal invaders. His first target was Sicily, leading to the [Gothic War (535–554)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_\(535%E2%80%93554\)) between the Ostrogoths and the Eastern Roman Empire, also known as the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). Justinian's general [Belisarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisarius) was assigned to the military task.[^cite_note-40] Sicily was used as a base for the Byzantines to conquer the rest of Italy, including [Naples](55880.md), Rome, and [Milan](36511.md). It took five years before the Ostrogoth capital [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna) fell in 540.[^cite_note-hisnet-41] However, the new Ostrogoth king [Totila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totila) counterattacked, moving down the Italian peninsula, plundering and conquering Sicily in 550. Totila was defeated and killed in the [Battle of Taginae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taginae) by Byzantine general [Narses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narses) in 552 but Italy was in ruins.[^cite_note-hisnet-41]

At the time of the reconquest Greek was still the predominant language spoken on the island. Sicily was invaded by the [Arab forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_army) of [Caliph Uthman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman_Ibn_Affan) in 652, but the Arabs failed to make permanent gains. They returned to Syria with their booty.[^cite_note-42] Raids seeking loot continued until the mid-8th century.[^cite_note-43]

The Eastern Roman Emperor [Constans II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constans_II_\(Byzantine_Empire\)) moved from [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) to [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) in 660. The following year he launched an assault from Sicily against the [Lombard](43807.md) [Duchy of Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento), which occupied most of southern Italy.[^cite_note-travsyrac-44] Rumors that the capital of the empire was to be moved to Syracuse probably cost Constans his life, as he was assassinated in 668.[^cite_note-travsyrac-44] His son [Constantine IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_IV) succeeded him. A brief usurpation in Sicily by [Mezezius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezezius) was quickly suppressed by this emperor. Contemporary accounts report that the Greek language was widely spoken on the island during this period.[^cite_note-45] In 740 Emperor [Leo III the Isaurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_III_the_Isaurian) transferred Sicily from the jurisdiction of the church of Rome to that of Constantinople, placing the island within the eastern branch of the Church.[^cite_note-46]

In 826 [Euphemius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemius_\(Sicily\)), the Byzantine commander in Sicily, having apparently killed his wife, forced a nun to marry him. Emperor [Michael II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_II) caught wind of the matter and ordered general Constantine to end the marriage and cut off Euphemius' head. Euphemius rose up, killed Constantine, and then occupied Syracuse; he, in turn, was defeated and driven out to North Africa.[^cite_note-stan-47] He offered the rule of Sicily to [Ziyadat Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyadat_Allah_I_of_Aghlabids), the [Aghlabid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghlabid) Emir of [Tunisia](30188.md), in return for a position as a general and a place of safety. A [Muslim army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) was then sent to the island consisting of [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab), [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people), [Cretans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretans), and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people).[^cite_note-stan-47]

The [Muslim conquest of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Sicily) was a see-saw affair and met with fierce resistance. It took over a century for Byzantine Sicily to be conquered; the largest city, Syracuse, held out until 878 and the Greek city of [Taormina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taormina) fell in 902. It was not until 965 that all of Sicily was conquered by the Arabs.[^cite_note-stan-47] In the 11th-century Byzantine armies carried out a partial reconquest of the island under [George Maniakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Maniakes), but it was their [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Norman) mercenaries who would eventually complete the island's reconquest at the end of the century.

### Arab period (827–1091)

Main article: [Emirate of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily)



[Arabesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabesque) on a wall in the [Cuba Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba,_Palermo) in Palermo



Trilingual sign from the [Palazzo dei Normanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_dei_Normanni) in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), written in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), [Byzantine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Greek) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic)

The language spoken in Sicily under Arab rule was [Siculo-Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siculo-Arabic) and [Arabic influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influence_of_Arabic_on_other_languages) is present in some Sicilian words today. Although long extinct in Sicily, the language has developed into what is now the [Maltese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_language) on the islands of [Malta](19137.md) today.[^cite_note-48]

A description of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) was given by [Ibn Hawqal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hawqal), an [Arab merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Islamic_economics) who visited Sicily in 950. A walled suburb, called the Al-Kasr (the palace), is the centre of Palermo to this day, with the great Friday mosque on the site of the later Roman cathedral. The suburb of al-Khalisa (modern [Kalsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalsa)) contained the [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan)'s palace, baths, a mosque, government offices, and a private prison. Ibn Hawqal estimated there were 7,000 butchers trading in 150 shops. The Muslim rule introduced lemons, oranges, pistachios,[^cite_note-49] and sugar cane, as well as cotton and mulberries for sericulture, and introduced the [Qanat](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanat%20di%20Palermo) to improve irrigation systems for agriculture.[^cite_note-Metcalfe2014-50] Around 1050, the western half of Sicily was ethnically and culturally distinct from central and eastern Sicily. During this time, there was also a small Jewish presence in Sicily, evidence seen in the catacombs discovered on the island.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

Palermo was initially ruled by the [Aghlabids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghlabids); later it was the centre of the Emirate of Sicily, which was under the nominal suzerainty of the [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate). Muslim sovereignty was never absolute across the island, and the creation of three subdivisions served to distinguish different approaches to government. Under the Arab rule the island was divided in [three administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_valli_of_Sicily), or "vals", roughly corresponding to the three "points" of Sicily: [Val di Mazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_di_Mazara) in the west; [Val Demone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Demone) in the northeast; and [Val di Noto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_di_Noto) in the southeast. Western Sicily was more [Islamized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) and heavily populated by Arabs, allowing for full and direct administration; by contrast, the northeast region of Val Demone remained majority Christian and often resistant to Muslim rule, prompting a focus on tax collection and maintaining public order, as a result, revolts by Byzantine Sicilians continuously occurred in the east where Greek-speaking Christians predominated. Parts of the island were re-occupied before revolts were quashed. By the 11th century, the [Emirate of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily) began to fragment as intra-dynastic quarrelling fractured the Muslim government.[^cite_note-stan-47]

### Norman Sicily (1038–1198)

See also: [Norman conquest of southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_southern_Italy)



The Norman [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily) (in green) in 1154, upon the death of [Roger II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_II)



[Cappella Palatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappella_Palatina), [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo)

In 1038, seventy years after losing their last cities in Sicily, the Byzantines under the Greek general [George Maniakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Maniakes) invaded the island together with their [Varangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangian_guard) and [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) mercenaries. Maniakes was killed in a Byzantine civil war in 1043 before completing a reconquest and the Byzantines withdrew. Later the Normans invaded in 1061 and after taking [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) and [Calabria](44772.md), [Roger I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_I_of_Sicily) occupied [Messina](59705.md) with an army of 700 knights.[^cite_note-stan-47][^cite_note-Boise_State_University_Sicily_under_the_ormans-53] In 1068, Roger I was victorious at [Misilmeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Misilmeri). Most crucial was the siege of Palermo, whose fall in 1071 eventually resulted in all Sicily coming under Norman control.[^cite_note-initalymag-54] The conquest was completed in 1091 when they captured [Noto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noto) the last Arab stronghold. Palermo continued to be the capital under the Normans. The Normans formed a small but violent ruling class. They destroyed many of the Arab towns in Sicily, and very few physical remains survive from the Arab era.[^cite_note-stan-47]

The Norman [Hauteville family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauteville_family) appreciated and admired the rich and layered culture in which they now found themselves. They also introduced into Sicily their own culture, customs, and politics from [Normandy](21724.md). Many Normans in Sicily adopted the habits and comportment of Muslim rulers and their Byzantine subjects in dress, language, literature, even to the extent of having palace [eunuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunuch) and, according to some accounts, a harem.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

While Roger I died in 1101, his wife [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_del_Vasto) ruled until 1112 when their son [Roger II of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily) came of age.[^cite_note-Boise_State_University_Sicily_under_the_ormans-53] Having succeeded his brother [Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_of_Sicily) as Count of Sicily, Roger II was ultimately able to raise the status of the island to a kingdom in 1130, along with his other holdings, which included the [Maltese Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_Islands) and the Duchies of [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Apulia) and [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Calabria).[^cite_note-initalymag-54][^cite_note-57] Roger II appointed the powerful Greek [George of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_of_Antioch) to be his "emir of emirs" and continued the syncretism of his father. During this period, the Kingdom of Sicily was prosperous and politically powerful, becoming one of the wealthiest states in all of Europe—even wealthier than the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England).[^cite_note-58]

The court of Roger II became the most luminous centre of culture in the Mediterranean, both from Europe and the Middle East, like the multi-ethnic [Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba), then only just eclipsed. This attracted scholars, scientists, poets, artists, and artisans of all kinds. Laws were issued in the language of the community to whom they were addressed in Norman Sicily, at the time when the culture was still heavily Arab and Greek.[^cite_note-Advanced_Studies_in_Cultural_History-59][^cite_note-Loud,_G._A._2007_494-60] Governance was by rule of law which promoted justice. Muslims, Jews, [Byzantine Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greeks), Lombards, and Normans worked together fairly amicably. During this time many extraordinary buildings were constructed.[^cite_note-Advanced_Studies_in_Cultural_History-59]

However this situation changed as the Normans imported immigrants from [Normandy](21724.md), [England](9316.md), Lombardy, Piedmont, Provence and [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania) to secure the island. Linguistically, the island shifted from being one-third Greek- and two-thirds Arabic-speaking at the time of the Norman conquest to becoming fully [Latinised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_Latinisation).[^cite_note-Loud,_G._A._2007_494-60] In terms of religion the island became completely Roman Catholic (bearing in mind that until 1054 the Churches owing allegiance to the Pope and the Patriarch of Constantinople belonged to one Church); Sicily before the Norman conquest was under the Eastern Orthodox Patriarch.[^cite_note-61] After Pope Innocent III made him Papal Legate in 1098, Roger I created several Catholic bishoprics while still allowing the construction of 12 Greek-speaking monasteries (the Greek language, monasteries, and 1500 parishes continued to exist until the adherents of the Greek Rite were forced in 1585 to convert to Catholicism or leave; a small pocket of Greek-speakers still live in Messina).

### Kingdom of Sicily (1198–1860)

Main articles: [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily) and [List of monarchs of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Sicily)

After a century, the Norman [Hauteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauteville_family) dynasty died out; the last direct descendant and heir of Roger II, [Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_I_of_Sicily), married [Emperor Henry VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).[^cite_note-dieli-62] This eventually led to the crown of Sicily being passed to the [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen) dynasty, who were Germans from [Swabia](616237.md). The last of the Hohenstaufens, [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), the only son of Constance, was one of the greatest and most cultured men of the Middle Ages. His mother's will had asked [Pope Innocent III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III) to undertake the guardianship of her son. Frederick was four when at [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), he was crowned [King of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Sicily) in 1198. Frederick received no systematic education and was allowed to run free in the streets of Palermo. There he picked up the many languages he heard spoken, such as Arabic and Greek, and learned some of the lore of the Jewish community. At age twelve, he dismissed Innocent's deputy regent and took over the government; at fifteen he married [Constance of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_of_Aragon,_Holy_Roman_Empress), and began his reclamation of the imperial crown. Subsequently, due to Muslim rebellions, Frederick II destroyed the remaining Muslim presence in Sicily, estimated at 60,000 people, moving all to the city of Lucera in Apulia between 1221 and 1226.[^cite_note-63]

Conflict between the Hohenstaufen house and the [Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papacy) led, in 1266, to [Pope Innocent IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV) crowning the [French prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou) [Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Naples), [count of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Anjou) and [Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Provence), as the king of both Sicily and Naples.[^cite_note-dieli-62]

Strong opposition to French officialdom due to mistreatment and taxation saw the local peoples of Sicily rise up, leading in 1282 to an [insurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrection) known as the [War of the Sicilian Vespers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sicilian_Vespers), which eventually saw almost the entire French population on the island killed.[^cite_note-dieli-62] During the war, the Sicilians turned to [Peter III of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_III_of_Aragon), son-in-law of the last Hohenstaufen king, for support after being rejected by the Pope. Peter gained control of Sicily from the French, who, however, retained control of the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples). A crusade was launched in August 1283 against Peter III and the Crown of Aragon by [Pope Martin IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Martin_IV) (a pope from [Île-de-France](1716427.md)), but it failed. The wars continued until the [peace of Caltabellotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Caltabellotta) in 1302, which saw Peter's son [Frederick III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III_of_Sicily) recognized as the king of the Isle of Sicily, while [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_Naples) was recognized as the king of Naples by [Pope Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII).[^cite_note-dieli-62] Sicily was ruled as an independent kingdom by relatives of the kings of Aragon until 1409 and then as part of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon).[^cite_note-knowital-23]

In October 1347, in Messina, Sicily, the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) first arrived in Europe.[^cite_note-64]

Between the 15th and 18th centuries, waves of [Greeks](42056.md) from the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) (such as the [Maniots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maniots)) and [Arvanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanites) migrated to Sicily in large numbers to escape persecution after the [Ottoman conquest of the Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_conquest_of_the_Balkans). They brought with them [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) as well as the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and [Arvanitika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanitika) languages, once again adding onto the extensive [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine)/[Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Culture) influence.

The onset of the [Spanish Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Inquisition) in 1492 led to [Ferdinand II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon) decreeing the expulsion of all Jews from Sicily.[^cite_note-dieli-62] The eastern part of the island was hit by destructive earthquakes in 1542 and 1693. Just a few years before the latter earthquake, the island was struck by a [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_\(disease\)).[^cite_note-dieli-62] The [earthquake in 1693](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1693_Sicily_earthquake) took an estimated 60,000 lives.[^cite_note-65] There were revolts during the 17th century, but these were quelled with force, especially the revolts of Palermo and Messina.[^cite_note-knowital-23] [North African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) [slave raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade) discouraged settlement along the coast until the 19th century.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht) in 1713 saw Sicily assigned to the [House of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy); however, this period of rule lasted only seven years, as it was exchanged for the island of [Sardinia](29376.md) with [Emperor Charles VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of the Austrian [Habsburg Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg).[^cite_note-68]

While the Austrians were concerned with the [War of the Polish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Polish_Succession), a [Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon) prince, [Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain) from Spain was able to conquer Sicily and Naples.[^cite_note-69] At first Sicily was able to remain as an independent kingdom under [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union), while the Bourbons ruled over both from Naples. However, the advent of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I)'s [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) saw Naples taken at the [Battle of Campo Tenese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Campo_Tenese) and Bonapartist [King of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Naples) was installed. [Ferdinand III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies), the Bourbon, was forced to retreat to Sicily which he was still in control of with the help of [British naval](26061.md) protection.[^cite_note-70]

Following this, Sicily joined the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), and subsequently the British under [Lord William Bentinck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_William_Bentinck) established a military and diplomatic presence on the island to protect against a French invasion. Sicilian volunteers joined the British military to form the [Royal Sicilian Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Sicilian_Regiment), which saw action at the [Battle of Maida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maida) and then transferred to [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War). After the wars were won, Sicily and Naples formally merged as the [Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Sicilies) under the Bourbons. Major [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary) movements occurred in 1820 and 1848 against the Bourbon government with Sicily seeking independence; the second of which, the [1848 revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_revolution_of_independence_of_1848) resulted in a short period of independence for Sicily. However, in 1849 the Bourbons retook control of the island and dominated it until 1860.[^cite_note-71]

### Italian unification

See also: [Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento)



[Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi) lands in [Marsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala) in 1860, during the [Expedition of the Thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_the_Thousand), as part of the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification)

The [Expedition of the Thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_the_Thousand) led by [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi) captured Sicily in 1860, as part of the *[Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification)*.[^cite_note-modern-72] The conquest started at [Marsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala), and native Sicilians joined him in the capture of the southern Italian peninsula. Garibaldi's march was completed with the [siege of Gaeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Gaeta_\(1861\)), where the final [Bourbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbons) were expelled and Garibaldi announced his dictatorship in the name of [Victor Emmanuel II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emanuel_II_of_Italy) of [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_\(1720%E2%80%931861\)).[^cite_note-73] Sicily became part of the Kingdom of Sardinia after a referendum in which more than 75% of Sicily voted in favour of the annexation on 21 October 1860 (although not everyone was allowed to vote). As a result of the [proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy), Sicily became part of the kingdom on 17 March 1861.

The Sicilian economy (and the wider *[mezzogiorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezzogiorno)* economy) remained relatively underdeveloped after the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification), in spite of the strong investments made by the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) in terms of modern infrastructure, and this caused an unprecedented [wave of emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_diaspora).[^cite_note-modern-72] In 1894, organisations of workers and peasants known as the *[Fasci Siciliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasci_Siciliani)* protested against the bad social and economic conditions of the island, but they were suppressed in a few days.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

This period was also characterized by the first contact between the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) (the crime syndicate also known as Cosa Nostra) and the Italian government. The battle against the Mafia made by the Kingdom of Italy was controversial and ambiguous. The [Carabinieri](45173.md) (the military police of Italy) and sometimes the [Royal Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Italian_Army) were often involved in fights against the mafia members, but their efforts were frequently useless because of the weakness of the Italian judicial system and cooperation between the mafia and local governments.[^cite_note-76]

### 20th and 21st centuries



Ruins of [Messina](59705.md) after the [1908 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Messina_earthquake) and tsunami. Taken at the northern section of the Palazzata in front of the harbour.

The [Messina earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Messina_earthquake) of 28 December 1908 killed more than 80,000 people.[^cite_note-77]

In the 1920s, the [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism) regime began taking stronger military action, led by [Cesare Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Mori) (nicknamed the "Iron [Prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect)" for his iron-fisted campaigns), against the Sicilian Mafia, the first that ended with considerable success.[^cite_note-modern-72] An [Allied invasion of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Sicily) during World War II began on 10 July 1943. In preparation for the invasion, the Allies [revitalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaborations_between_the_United_States_government_and_Italian_Mafia) the Mafia to aid them. The invasion of Sicily contributed to the [25 July crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy); in general, the Allied victors were warmly embraced by Sicilians.[^cite_note-78]

In the aftermath of World War II, Italy [became a republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Italian_institutional_referendum) in 1946. Under the [Constitution of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy), Sicily is one of five [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) with [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division).[^cite_note-79] Both the partial Italian [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform) and special funding from the Italian government's *[Cassa per il Mezzogiorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassa_per_il_Mezzogiorno)* (*Fund for the South*) from 1950 to 1984 helped the Sicilian economy. During this period, the economic and social condition of the island was generally improved because of investments in infrastructure (such as [motorways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorways) and [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport)) and the creation of industrial and commercial areas.[^cite_note-80] In the 1980s, the Mafia was weakened by another campaign led by magistrates [Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone) and [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino).[^cite_note-81] Between 1990 and 2005, the [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) fell from about 23% to 11%.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

The Cosa Nostra has traditionally been the most powerful group in Sicily, especially around Palermo.[^cite_note-84] A police investigation in the summer of 2019 confirmed strong links between the Palermo area [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) and American organized crime, particularly the [Gambino crime family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambino_crime_family).[^cite_note-85] According to *[La Repubblica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Repubblica)*, "Off they go, through the streets of Passo di Rigano, Boccadifalco, Torretta and at the same time, Brooklyn, Staten Island, \[and] New Jersey. Because from Sicily to the US, the old mafia has returned."[^cite_note-86]

## Geography

See also: [Geology of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Sicily)



Relief map of Sicily



Detailed map of Sicily



[Madonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonie) mountains in the [Madonie Regional Natural Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonie_Regional_Natural_Park).



Cala Rossa, [island of Favignana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favignana#Island), [Aegadian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegadian_Islands)

Sicily has a roughly triangular shape, earning it the name *Trinacria*.

To the north-east, it is separated from [Calabria](44772.md) and the rest of the Italian mainland by the [Strait of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina), about 3 km (1.9 mi) wide in the north, and about 16 km (9.9 mi) wide in the southern part.[^cite_note-87] The northern and southern coasts are each about 280 km (170 mi) long measured as a straight line, while the eastern coast measures around 180 km (110 mi); total coast length is [estimated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_of_coast) at 1,484 km (922 mi). The total area of the island is 25,711 km<sup>2</sup> (9,927 sq mi),[^cite_note-eb-88] while the [Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) of Sicily (which includes the smaller surrounding islands of [Lipari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipari), [Egadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egadi), [Ustica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustica), and [Pantelleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantelleria)) has an area of 27,708 km<sup>2</sup> (10,698 sq mi).[^cite_note-89]

The terrain of inland Sicily is mostly hilly and is intensively cultivated wherever possible. Along the northern coast, the [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) of [Madonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonie), 2,000 m (6,600 ft), [Nebrodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebrodi), 1,800 m (5,900 ft), and [Peloritani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloritani), 1,300 m (4,300 ft), are an extension of the mainland [Apennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennines). The cone of [Mount Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna) dominates the eastern coast. In the southeast lie the lower [Hyblaean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyblaean_Mountains), 1,000 m (3,300 ft).[^cite_note-frommers-90] The mines of the [Enna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enna) and [Caltanissetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltanissetta) districts were part of a leading [sulphur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur)-producing area throughout the 19th century, but have declined since the 1950s.

Sicily and its surrounding small islands have some highly active volcanoes. This is due to Sicily being geographically on the northern edge of the [African Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Plate).[^cite_note-91] Mount Etna is the largest active volcano in Europe and casts black ash over the island with its recurrent eruptions. It stands 3,403 metres (11,165 ft) high as of September 2024.[^cite_note-92] It is the highest mountain in Italy south of the [Alps](981.md). Etna covers an area of 1,190 km<sup>2</sup> (459 sq mi) with a basal circumference of 140 km (87 mi). This makes it the largest of the three [active volcanoes in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism_in_Italy), being about two and a half times the height of the next largest, [Mount Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius). In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), the deadly monster [Typhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhon) was trapped under the mountain by [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus), the god of the sky.[^cite_note-93] Mount Etna is widely regarded as a cultural symbol and icon of Sicily.

The [Aeolian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_Islands) in the [Tyrrhenian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea), to the northeast of mainland Sicily form a volcanic complex. The three volcanoes of [Vulcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcano), [Stromboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromboli) and [Lipari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipari) are also active, although the last is usually dormant. Off the southern coast of Sicily, the underwater volcano of [Ferdinandea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinandea), which is part of the larger [Empedocles volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empedocles_\(volcano\)), last erupted in 1831. It is located between the coast of [Agrigento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigento) and the island of [Pantelleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantelleria) (which itself is a dormant volcano).

The [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) also includes several neighbouring islands: the [Aegadian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegadian_Islands), the Aeolian Islands, Pantelleria and [Lampedusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampedusa). From a geographical perspective, also forming a part of Sicily is the Maltese Archipelago, the islands constitute the [republic of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Malta).

### Climate



Inner Sicily

Sicily has for the most part a typical [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) with mild and wet winters and hot, dry summers with changeable intermediate seasons. On the coasts, especially in the southwest, the climate is affected by the African currents and summers can be hot.

Snow falls above 900 metres, but it can fall in the hills. The interior mountains, especially [Nebrodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebrodi), [Madonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonie), and [Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna), enjoy a mountain climate, with heavy snowfalls during winter. The summit of Mount Etna is usually snow-capped from October to May.

In the summer, the [sirocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirocco) – the wind from the Sahara – can be felt. Rainfall is scarce, and in some provinces a water crisis can occur.

According to the Regional Agency for Waste and Water, on 10 August 1999, the weather station of [Catenanuova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenanuova) (EN) recorded an unofficial maximum temperature of 48.5 °C (119 °F).[^cite_note-96] On 11 August 2021, a new [highest temperature record](4506267.md) for [Europe](9239.md) with a reading of 48.8 °C (119.8 °F) was set near the city of [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily).[^cite_note-97] Total precipitation is highly variable, generally increasing with elevation. In general, the southern and southeast coast receives the least rainfall (less than 50 cm (20 in)), and the northern and northeastern highlands the most (over 100 cm (39 in)).

### Flora and fauna

See also: [List of endemic plants of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_endemic_plants_of_Sicily)



The [Sicilian wall lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_wall_lizard) endemic to Sicily and the [Aegadian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegadian_Islands)



The [Sicilian wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_wolf) reportedly went extinct due to human persecution in the 1920s, though there were several possible sightings up to the 1970s.

Sicily is an often-quoted example of man-made [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), which has occurred since Roman times when the island was turned into an agricultural region.[^cite_note-frommers-90] This gradually dried the climate, leading to a decline in rainfall and the drying of rivers. The central and southwest provinces are practically devoid of forest.[^cite_note-98] In Northern Sicily, there are three important forests; near Mount Etna, in the [Nebrodi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebrodi) and in the [Bosco della Ficuzza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosco_della_Ficuzza?action=edit\&redlink=1) Natural Reserve near [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo). The Nebrodi Mountains Regional Park, established on 4 August 1993 and covering 86,000 hectares (210,000 acres), is the largest protected natural area of Sicily; it contains the largest forest in Sicily, the [Caronia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caronia). The [Hundred Horse Chestnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Horse_Chestnut) (*Castagno dei Cento Cavalli*), in [Sant'Alfio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Alfio), on the eastern slopes of Mount Etna, is the largest and oldest known [chestnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut) tree in the world at 2,000–4,000 years old.[^cite_note-99]

Sicily has a wide variety of fauna. Species include the [European wildcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wildcat), [red fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_fox), [least weasel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_weasel), [pine marten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_marten), [fallow deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallow_deer),[^cite_note-100] [wild boar](52255.md), [crested porcupine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crested_porcupine), [European hedgehog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_hedgehog), [common toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_toad), *[Vipera aspis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vipera_aspis)*, [golden eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_eagle), [peregrine falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon), [Eurasian hoopoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_hoopoe) and [black-winged stilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-winged_stilt).[^cite_note-101] [Roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer) were driven to extinction on the island.[^cite_note-102] The [Sicilian wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_wolf) (*Canis lupus cristaldii*) was an [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) wolf [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies_of_Canis_lupus) that was driven to extinction in the 20th century. During the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene), a species of [dwarf elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_elephant), [Palaeoloxodon mnaidriensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeoloxodon_mnaidriensis) inhabited the island, with its latest records on Sicily dating to around 20,000 years ago.[^cite_note-103]

The [Riserva naturale dello Zingaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riserva_naturale_dello_Zingaro) (Zingaro Natural Reserve) is one of the best examples of unspoiled coastal wilderness in Sicily.[^cite_note-104]

[Marine Life of the Straits of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Life_of_the_Straits_of_Messina) includes varieties of birds and marine life, including larger species such as [greater flamingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_flamingo) and [fin whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin_whale).

### Emigration

See also: [Sicilian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Americans)



The Sicilian [triskelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion) in [Ybor City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ybor_City), a district of [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida), home to many [Sicilian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Americans)

Sicilian emigration started shortly after [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification) and has continued into the present day. The aforementioned factors, along with a failed land reform, resulted in an unprecedented wave of Sicilians emigrating, [first to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Americans) between the 1880s and the 1920s and later to northern Italy; since the 1960s, other population destinations include [Belgium](3343.md), [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Switzerland](26748.md), as well as [Australia](4689264.md) and [South America](26769.md). An estimated 10 million people of Sicilian origin live around the world.

The trend of emigration, particularly among young Sicilians leaving the island in search of employment elsewhere in Italy and abroad, continues in the early 21st century.[^cite_note-109] Sicily remains the Italian region with the highest number of [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate): as of 2017, over 750,000 Sicilians, 14.4% of the island's population, lived abroad.[^cite_note-110]

### Religion

See also: [Italo-Albanian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Albanian_Catholic_Church), [History of the Jews in Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Sicily), and [History of Islam in southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Islam_in_southern_Italy)

As in most Italian regions, [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is the predominant religious denomination in Sicily, and the church still plays an important role in the lives of most people. There is also a notable small minority of Eastern-rite [Byzantine Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Catholics) which has a mixed congregation of ethnic [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians); it is operated by the [Italo-Albanian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Albanian_Catholic_Church). Most people still attend church weekly or at least for religious festivals, and many people get married in churches. There was a wide presence of Jews in Sicily for at least 1,400 years and possibly for more than 2,000 years. Some scholars believe that the Sicilian Jewry are partial ancestors of the [Ashkenazi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews).[^cite_note-113] However, much of the Jewish community faded away when they were [expelled from the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Jews_from_Sicily) in 1492. [Islam](6037917.md) was present during the [Emirate of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily), although Muslims were also expelled. Today, mostly due to immigration to the island, there are also several religious minorities, such as Jehovah's Witnesses, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), Islam, Judaism, and [Sikhism](27964.md). There are also some [Evangelical Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) who live on the island. As of 2020, there are approximately 4,989,921 Roman Catholics in Sicily constituting about 99.2% of the island's population. Additionally there are also about 23,120 members of the Italo-Albanian Catholic Church also living in Sicily constituting roughly 0.5% of the population.[^cite_note-114]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Sicily)



[Palazzo dei Normanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_dei_Normanni), the seat of the [Sicilian Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Regional_Assembly). It was a 9th-century Arab palace, converted by the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_southern_Italy) into their governing castle.

The politics of Sicily takes place in a framework of a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), whereby the President of Regional Government is the [head of government](37570.md), and of a pluriform [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the Regional Government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the government and the [Sicilian Regional Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Regional_Assembly). The capital of Sicily is [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo).

Traditionally, Sicily votes for centre-right parties during elections.[^cite_note-115] From 1943 to 1951, there was also a [separatist](286527.md) political party called [Sicilian Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Independence_Movement) (*Movimento Indipendentista Siciliano*, MIS). Their most successful result was at the [1946 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_general_election,_1946), when MIS obtained 0.7% of national votes (8.8% of votes in Sicily), and four seats. However, the movement lost all their seats following the [1948 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_general_election,_1948) and the 1951 regional election. Even though it has never been formally disbanded, today the movement is no longer part of the [politics of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Sicily). After [World War II](32927.md), Sicily became a stronghold of the [Christian Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democracy_\(Italy\)). Sicily is now governed by a centre-right coalition. [Renato Schifani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Schifani) is the current President and has served since 2022.

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Italy)



Olive groves

Thanks to regular growth, Sicily is the eighth largest regional economy of Italy in terms of total GDP (see [List of Italian regions by GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_regions_by_GDP_\(PPP\))). A series of reforms and investments in agriculture, such as the introduction of modern irrigation systems, has made this important industry competitive.[^cite_note-116] In the 1970s, some factories were opened, resulting in growth in the [industrial sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector).[^cite_note-117] In recent years the [service industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_industry) has become more important due to the opening of several shopping malls and some modest growth in financial and telecommunication activities.[^cite_note-118] Tourism is an important source of income for the island, which attracts visitors due to its rich natural and historical heritage. Today Sicily is investing a large amount of money into the development of its [hospitality industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality_industry), to attract even more tourism.[^cite_note-119] However, Sicily continues to have a GDP per capita below the Italian average, and higher unemployment than the rest of Italy.[^cite_note-120]

### Agriculture



A sample of [Marsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala_wine), a [DOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominazione_di_origine_controllata) wine produced in the city of [Marsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala)

Sicily has long been noted for its fertile soil, which is the result of past volcanic eruptions. The local agriculture is also helped by the pleasant climate of the island. The main agricultural products are wheat, [citrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamante_citron), oranges *([Arancia Rossa di Sicilia IGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_orange))*, lemons, tomatoes *([Pomodoro di Pachino IGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomodoro_di_Pachino))*, [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil), [artichokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artichoke), [prickly pear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opuntia_ficus-indica) *(Fico d'India dell'[Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna) DOP)*, [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), [pistachios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistachio) *(Pistacchio di [Bronte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronte,_Sicily) DOP)* and wine. Cattle and sheep are raised. The production of cheese is particularly important thanks to the [Ragusano DOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragusano_cheese) and the [Pecorino Siciliano DOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Siciliano). [Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragusa,_Italy) is noted for its [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) (*Miele Ibleo*) and chocolate (*[Cioccolato di Modica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cioccolato_di_Modica) IGP*) products.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-insicilia-124][^cite_note-125]

Sicily is the third largest wine producer in Italy, after [Veneto](43780.md) and [Emilia Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia_Romagna) (and Italy is the world's largest wine producer).[^cite_note-126] The region is known mainly for fortified [Marsala wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala_wine). In recent decades the wine industry has improved, new winemakers are experimenting with less-well-known native varieties, and Sicilian wines have become better known.[^cite_note-127] The best known local variety is [Nero d'Avola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero_d'Avola) named after [Avola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avola), a town not far from [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily). Other important native varieties are: [Nerello Mascalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerello), used to make the [Etna Rosso DOC wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etna_DOC); [Frappato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frappato), a component of the Cerasuolo di Vittoria DOCG wine; [Moscato di Pantelleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_of_Alexandria) (also known as *Zibibbo*), which is used to make different [Pantelleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantelleria) wines; [Malvasia di Lipari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvasia#Italian_varieties), used for the Malvasia di Lipari DOC wine; and [Catarratto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catarratto), mostly used to make a white wine, [Alcamo DOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcamo_wine). Furthermore, in Sicily, high-quality wines are also produced using non-native varieties like [Syrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrah), [Chardonnay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chardonnay) and [Merlot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlot).[^cite_note-128]

Sicily is also known for its liqueurs, such as [Amaro Averna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaro_Averna), produced in [Caltanissetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltanissetta), and the local [limoncello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limoncello).

Fishing is another fundamental resource for Sicily. It has important [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna), [sardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardine), [swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish) and [European anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_anchovy) fisheries. [Mazara del Vallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazara_del_Vallo) is the largest fishing centre in Sicily and one of the most important in Italy.[^cite_note-esploriamo.com-129]

### Railways

Main article: [Railway network of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_network_of_Sicily)



[Palermo Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo_Centrale_railway_station)



Two trains inside [Punta Raisi railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Raisi_railway_station) within [Palermo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo_International_Airport)



[Catania Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania_Metro)

The first railway in Sicily was opened in 1863 (Palermo-Bagheria) and today all of the Sicilian provinces are served by a network of railway services, linking to most major cities and towns; this service is operated by [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia). Of the 1,378 km (856 mi) of railway tracks in use, over 60% has been [electrified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification_system) whilst the remaining 583 km (362 mi) are serviced by [diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieselisation) engines. 88% of the lines (1.209 km) are single-track and only 169 km (105 mi) are double-track serving the two main routes, Messina-Palermo ([Tyrrhenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea)) and Messina-Catania-Syracuse ([Ionian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Sea)), which are the main lines of this region. Of the [narrow-gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railway) the [Ferrovia Circumetnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovia_Circumetnea) is the only one that still operates, going round [Mount Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna). From the major cities of Sicily, there are services to [Naples](55880.md), [Rome](25458.md) and [Milan](36511.md); this is achieved by the trains being loaded onto [ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferries) which cross the Strait.[^cite_note-143]

In [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania) there is an [underground railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) service ([metropolitana di Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitana_di_Catania)); in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) the national railway operator [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia) operates a [commuter rail](6286.md) ([Palermo metropolitan railway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo_metropolitan_railway_service)), the Sicilian Capital is also served by 4 AMAT (Comunal Public Transport Operator) tramlines; [Messina](59705.md) is served by a [tramline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Messina).

### Airports

Sicily has several airports that serve numerous Italian and European destinations and some extra-European.

- [Pantelleria Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantelleria_Airport).

### Ports



[Port of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Messina)

By sea, Sicily is served by several ferry routes and cargo ports, and in all major cities, cruise ships dock on a regular basis.

- Mainland Italy: Ports connecting to the mainland are [Messina](59705.md) (route to [Villa San Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_San_Giovanni) and [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno)), the busiest passenger port in Italy, [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) (routes to [Genoa](47332321.md), [Civitavecchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitavecchia) and [Naples](55880.md)) and [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania) (route to [Naples](55880.md)).

From Palermo there is a service to the island of [Ustica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustica) and to [Sardinia](29376.md).

- International connections: From Palermo and Trapani there are weekly services to [Tunisia](30188.md) and there is also a daily service between [Malta](19137.md) and [Pozzallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Pozzallo).

### Strait of Messina Bridge



Cross-sectional diagram of the [Strait of Messina Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina_Bridge)

The [Strait of Messina Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina_Bridge) is a proposed 3.6 km (2.2 mi) [suspension bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_bridge) across the [Strait of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina), connecting [Torre Faro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Faro) in Sicily with [Villa San Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_San_Giovanni) on the Italian peninsula.[^cite_note-146]

While a bridge across the Strait of Messina had been proposed since ancient times, the first detailed plan was made in the 1990s for a suspension bridge. The project was cancelled in 2006 under prime minister [Romano Prodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Prodi).[^cite_note-plans-dropped-bbc-147] On 6 March 2009, as part of a massive new public works programme, prime minister [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi)'s government announced that construction of the Messina Bridge would indeed go ahead, pledging €1.3 billion as a contribution to the total cost, estimated at €6.1 billion.[^cite_note-148] The project was cancelled again on 26 February 2013, by prime minister [Mario Monti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Monti)'s government, due to budget constraints.[^cite_note-sinks-149] A decade later, the project was revived again with a decree by [Giorgia Meloni](29228976.md)'s government, on 16 March 2023,[^cite_note-approves-150] which received presidential approval on 31 March 2023.[^cite_note-Stretto-151][^cite_note-152]

The bridge was fully approved by the Meloni government in August 2025, with construction set to begin in the autumn of 2025.[^cite_note-153] It will be the [longest suspension bridge in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_suspension_bridges) when it opens in 2032. The bridge would also be part of the [Berlin–Palermo railway axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin%E2%80%93Palermo_railway_axis) (Line 1) of the [Trans-European Transport Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Transport_Networks) (TEN-T).[^cite_note-ansa-154]

### Coastal towers

The coastal towers in Sicily (*Torri costiere della Sicilia*) are 218 old [watchtowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchtowers) along the coast. In Sicily, the first coastal towers date back to the late Norman period. From 1360 the threat came from the south, from [North Africa](21714.md) to [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), mainly to [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) and corsairs of [Barbary Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Coast). In 1516, the Turks settled in [Algiers](1644.md), and from 1520, the corsair [Hayreddin Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Barbarossa) under the command of [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) operated from that harbour.

Most existing towers were built on architectural designs of the Florentine architect [Camillo Camilliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Camilliani) from 1583 to 1584 and involved the coastal periple of Sicily. The typology changed completely in '800, because of the new higher fire volumes of cannon vessels, the towers were built on the type of [Martello towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martello_towers) that the British built in the UK and elsewhere in the British Empire. The decline of Mediterranean piracy caused by the [Second Barbary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Barbary_War) led to a smaller number of coastal towers built during the 19th century.[^cite_note-167]

## Culture

> To have seen Italy without having seen Sicily is to not have seen Italy at all, for Sicily is the clue to everything.

— [Goethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_von_Goethe)[^cite_note-172]

Sicily has long been associated with [the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts); many poets, writers, [philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), intellectuals, architects and painters have roots on the island. Among the earliest illuminaries there are [Gorgias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgias) and [Empedocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empedocles), two highly noted Sicilian-Greek philosophers, while the Syracusan-Greek [Epicharmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicharmus_of_Kos) is held to be the inventor of comedy.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] One of the most famous intellectuals in Greek antiquity was [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes), a [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) native who is recognized as one of the greatest mathematicians of all time.[^cite_note-175]

### Art

Among the Sicilian artists who succeeded in bringing to the international stage a language of strong civic engagement while at the same time narrating the history of the island, [Mario Bardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Bardi), a representative of [Existential realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_realism?action=edit\&redlink=1), stands out. His painting, characterized by dramatic tones and a constant attention to [labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_\(human_activity\)) and [social tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Question), offers a collective and powerful portrayal of Sicily.

### Architecture

Baglio are traditional living structures in Western Sicily.

#### Ceramics

[Terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta) [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramics_\(art\)) from the island are well known, the art of ceramics on Sicily goes back to the original ancient peoples named the [Sicanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicani), it was then perfected during the period of Greek colonisation and is still prominent and distinct to this day.[^cite_note-176] Nowadays, [Caltagirone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltagirone) is one of the most important centres in Sicily for the artistic production of ceramics and terra-cotta sculptures. Famous painters include [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) artist [Antonello da Messina](254609.md), [Pietro Novelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Novelli), [Bruno Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Caruso), [Renato Guttuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Guttuso) and Greek born [Giorgio de Chirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_de_Chirico) who is commonly dubbed the "father of [Surrealist art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealist_art)" and founder of the [metaphysical art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysical_art) movement.[^cite_note-177] The most noted architects are [Filippo Juvarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Juvarra) (one of the most important figures of the Italian [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque)) and [Ernesto Basile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Basile).

#### Sicilian Baroque

Main article: [Sicilian Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Baroque)

The [Sicilian Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Baroque) has a unique architectural identity. [Noto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noto), [Caltagirone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltagirone), [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), [Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragusa,_Italy), [Modica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modica), [Scicli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scicli) and particularly [Acireale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acireale) contain some of Italy's best examples of [Baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture), carved in the local red [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone). Noto provides one of the best examples of the Baroque architecture brought to Sicily. The Baroque style in Sicily was largely confined to buildings erected by the church, and [palazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo) built as private residences for the Sicilian aristocracy.[^cite_note-178] The earliest examples of this style in Sicily lacked individuality and were typically heavy-handed pastiches of buildings seen by Sicilian visitors to Rome, [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), and [Naples](55880.md). However, even at this early stage, provincial architects had begun to incorporate certain vernacular features of Sicily's older architecture. By the middle of the 18th century, when Sicily's Baroque architecture was noticeably different from that of the mainland, it has a unique freedom of design that is difficult to characterize in words.

### Literature



[Giacomo da Lentini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_da_Lentini) (detail of a 14th century [miniature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_\(illuminated_manuscript\)), [National Central Library of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Central_Library_of_Florence)), a senior poet of the [Sicilian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_School). Giacomo da Lentini is credited with the invention of the [sonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnet).

[^cite_note-Giacomo-180]



[Luigi Pirandello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Pirandello) ([Nobel laureate, 1934](23385442.md))

The [Sicilian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_School) was a small community of [Sicilian](27619.md) and mainland Italian poets gathered around [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), most of them belonging to his imperial court in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), which was highly influential on [Italian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_literature). Headed by [Giacomo da Lentini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_da_Lentini), they produced more than 300 poems of [courtly love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtly_love) between 1230 and 1266, the experiment being continued after Frederick's death by his son, [Manfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_of_Sicily). Giacomo da Lentini is credited with the invention of the [sonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnet).[^cite_note-Giacomo-180]

These poets drew inspiration from the [troubadour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubadour) poetry of [Occitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitania) written in [langue d'oc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langue_d'oc), which applied the feudal [code of honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_system) to the relation between a man (acting as the vassal) and a woman (acting as king or superior). This is a reversal of the traditional role of women, traditionally dependent on men, and marks a new awareness in [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) society: the decadence of [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) with the increasing power of the [middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class), causes a shift in the reading public, the [epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry) (traditionally devoted to great military pursuits) gradually giving way to the [lyric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyric_poetry) (generally focused on love). In the lower Middle Ages more and more women were reading books than ever before and poetry tried to adapt to their point of view and their newly acquired role in society.

This features Occitan poetry, then very influential in Italy. What distinguishes the Sicilian School from the troubadours, however, is the introduction of a kinder, gentler type of woman than that found in their Occitan models; one who was nearer to [Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri)'s madonnas and [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch)'s Laura, though much less characterised psychologically. The poems of the Sicilians hardly portray real women or situations (Frederick's song cannot be read as autobiographical), but the style and language are remarkable, since the Sicilians (as Dante called them) created the first literary standard in Italy by enriching the existing vernacular base, probably inspired by popular love songs, with new words of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) and [Provençal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) origin.

Some of the most noted figures among modern writers and poets are [Luigi Pirandello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Pirandello) ([Nobel laureate, 1934](23385442.md)), [Salvatore Quasimodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Quasimodo) (Nobel laureate, 1959), [Giovanni Verga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Verga) (the father of the *Italian [Verismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verismo_\(literature\))*), [Domenico Tempio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Tempio), [Giovanni Meli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Meli), [Luigi Capuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Capuana), [Mario Rapisardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Rapisardi), [Federico de Roberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_de_Roberto), [Leonardo Sciascia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Sciascia), [Vitaliano Brancati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliano_Brancati), [Giuseppe Tomasi di Lampedusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Tomasi_di_Lampedusa), [Elio Vittorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_Vittorini), [Vincenzo Consolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Consolo) and [Andrea Camilleri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Camilleri) (noted for his novels and short stories with the fictional character [Inspector Salvo Montalbano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvo_Montalbano) as protagonist). On the political side notable philosophers include [Gaetano Mosca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Mosca) and [Giovanni Gentile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Gentile) who wrote *[The Doctrine of Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Doctrine_of_Fascism)*.

### Languages

Main articles: [Sicilian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language), [Regional Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Italian), [Gallo-Italic of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_of_Sicily), and [Arbëresh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arb%C3%ABresh_language)



The languages spoken in Sicily (excluding Italian)

Sicily is mostly [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism), with most people speaking both Italian and [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language). Sicilian is a [Romance language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages), closely related to Italian but distinct from it, with vocabulary influenced by [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language), [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_language), French, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language), Spanish and other languages.[^cite_note-181] Today, however, the use of Sicilian is limited to informal and family contexts, and is often replaced by a regional variety of Italian (known as *[regional Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Italian) of Sicily*).[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] [Varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(linguistics\)) related to Sicilian are also spoken in [Calabria](44772.md) and [Salento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salento).[^cite_note-184] Sicilian had a significant influence on the [Maltese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_language).[^cite_note-185]

Beyond Sicilian and Italian, other languages are spoken across the island. Within the [province of Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Palermo), four towns are home to speakers of [Arbëresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arb%C3%ABresh_language),[^cite_note-186] which is the name given to varieties of [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) spoken in Italy. The [Arbëreshë people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arb%C3%ABresh%C3%AB_people) originally migrated from the [Balkans](4829.md) to escape the invasion of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in the 15th and 16th centuries and settled in isolated areas of southern Italy.[^cite_note-187]

In the eastern part of the island, [Gallo-Italic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_languages) varieties, collectively known as [Gallo-Italic of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_of_Sicily), are spoken.[^cite_note-\:0-188][^cite_note-\:1-189] These are related to the other Gallo-Italic languages spoken in most of northern Italy and in other isolated pockets of southern Italy. Gallo-Italic speakers originally migrated to Sicily from southern [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) and [Liguria](71864.md) to meet the Norman monarchy's need to repopulate strategic centres and control Arab presence in the 11th–13th centuries.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-\:0-188][^cite_note-\:1-189]

### Science



[Stanislao Cannizzaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislao_Cannizzaro), known for the [Cannizzaro reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannizzaro_reaction) and for his influential role in the atomic-weight deliberations of the [Karlsruhe Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Congress)

[Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania) has one of the four laboratories of the [Istituto Nazionale di Fisica Nucleare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Fisica_Nucleare) (National Institute for Nuclear Physics) in which there is a [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron) that uses [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton) both for [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics) experiments and for particle therapy to treat cancer ([proton therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_therapy)).[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] [Noto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noto) has one of the largest [radio telescopes](46656.md) in Italy that performs geodetic and astronomical observations.[^cite_note-193] There are [observatories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatories) in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) and Catania, managed by the *[Istituto Nazionale di Astrofisica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Astrofisica)* (National Institute for Astrophysics). In the *Observatory of Palermo* the astronomer [Giuseppe Piazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Piazzi) discovered the first and the largest [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) to be identified [Ceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceres_\(dwarf_planet\)) (today considered a [dwarf planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_planet)) on 1 January 1801;[^cite_note-194] Catania has two observatories, one of which is situated on [Mount Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna) at 1,800 metres (5,900 [feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_\(unit\))).[^cite_note-195]

[Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) is also an experimental centre for solar technologies through the creation of the project [Archimede solar power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimede_solar_power_plant) that is the first [concentrated solar power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power) to use [molten salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molten_salt) for heat transfer and storage which is integrated with a [combined-cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined-cycle) gas facility. All the plant is owned and operated by [Enel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enel).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] The touristic town of [Erice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erice) is also an important science place thanks to the [Ettore Majorana Foundation and Centre for Scientific Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Majorana_Foundation_and_Centre_for_Scientific_Culture) which embraces 123 schools from all over the world, covering all branches of science, offering courses, seminars, workshops, and annual meetings. It was founded by the physicist [Antonino Zichichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonino_Zichichi) in honour of another scientist of the island, [Ettore Majorana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Majorana) known for the [Majorana equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorana_equation) and [Majorana fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorana_fermion).[^cite_note-198] Sicily's famous scientists also include [Stanislao Cannizzaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislao_Cannizzaro) (chemist), [Giovanni Battista Hodierna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Hodierna) and [Niccolò Cacciatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Cacciatore) (astronomers).

Sicily has four universities:

[Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania) hosts also the *[Scuola Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_superiore_di_Catania)*, an [academic institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_institution) linked to the University of Catania, aiming for excellence in education.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Sicilian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_cuisine)



*[Pasta alla Norma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasta_alla_Norma)* is amongst Sicily's most historic and iconic dishes.



[Cannoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannoli) with pistachio, candied fruit, and chocolate chips



[Arancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arancini), rice balls fried in breadcrumbs



[Sicilian pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_pizza)

[Sicilian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_cuisine) is the style of cooking on the island of Sicily. It shows traces of all cultures that have existed on the island of Sicily over the last two millennia.[^cite_note-200] Although its cuisine has much in common with [Italian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine), Sicilian food also has [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cuisine), [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_cuisine), [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_cuisine), [Maghrebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebi_cuisine), and [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_cuisine) influences.[^cite_note-201] The Sicilian cook [Mithaecus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithaecus), born during the 5th century BC, is credited with having brought knowledge of Sicilian gastronomy to [Greece](12108.md):[^cite_note-202] his cookbook was the first in Greek, therefore he was the earliest cookbook author in any language whose name is known.

Much of the island was initially [settled by Greek colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia), who left a preference for [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_as_food), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitis_vinifera), [broad beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicia_faba), [chickpeas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chickpea), [lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentil), [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond), [pistachios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistachio), and fresh vegetables. [Arab](2185.md) influences on Sicilian cuisine trace to the [Arab domination of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily) in the 10th and early 11th centuries,[^cite_note-Piras,_423-203] and include the use of [sugar](27712.md), [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [raisins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin), [pine nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_nut) and spices such as [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron), [nutmeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutmeg), and [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon). [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) influences are also found, such as in the fondness for [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) dishes.[^cite_note-204] The [Jewish](25955086.md) community, who lived in the island, also left their mark on the Sicilian cuisine, they were responsible for introducing garlic fried in olive oil into the sauce.[^cite_note-205] Later, the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_cuisine) introduced [numerous items from the New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange), including [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_pepper), [zucchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchini), [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), and [tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), along with other [produce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Produce).[^cite_note-Piras,_423-203] Much of the island's cuisine encourages the use of fresh vegetables such as [eggplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eggplant), [artichoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artichoke), and tomatoes, and fish such as [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna_as_food), [sea bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparidae), [sea bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_seabass), [cuttlefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuttlefish), and [swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish). In [Trapani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapani) in the extreme western corner of the island, [North African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebi_cuisine) influences are clear in the use of [couscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couscous).

The island has a long history of producing a variety of noted cuisines and wines, to the extent that Sicily is sometimes nicknamed *God's Kitchen* because of this.[^cite_note-206] Every part of Sicily has its speciality (e.g. Cassata is typical of Palermo although available everywhere in Sicily, as is Granita). The ingredients are typically rich in taste while remaining affordable to the general public.[^cite_note-207] The savoury dishes of Sicily are viewed to be [healthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthy_diet), using fresh vegetables and fruits, such as tomatoes, [artichokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artichoke), [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) (including [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil)), [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus), [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot), [aubergines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubergines), onions, [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean), [raisins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin) commonly coupled with seafood, freshly caught from the surrounding coastlines, including [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna), [sea bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_bream), [sea bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_seabass), [cuttlefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuttlefish), [swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish), [sardines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardine), and others.[^cite_note-208]

The most well-known part of Sicilian cuisine is the rich sweet dishes including [ice creams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream) and [pastries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastry). [Cannoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannoli) (singular: *cannolo*), a tube-shaped shell of fried pastry dough filled with a sweet filling usually containing [ricotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta), is strongly associated with Sicily worldwide.[^cite_note-authentic-209] Biancomangiare, biscotti ennesi (cookies native to [Enna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enna)), braccilatte (a Sicilian version of [doughnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doughnut)), [buccellato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccellato), [ciarduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciarduna), [pignoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pignolo_\(macaroon\)), [Biscotti Regina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscotti_Regina), [giurgiulena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giurgiulena), [frutta martorana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frutta_martorana), [cassata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassata), [pignolata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pignolata), [granita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granita), [cuccidati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuccidati) (a variety of fig cookie; also known as buccellati) and [cuccìa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucc%C3%ACa) are some notable sweet dishes.[^cite_note-authentic-209]

Like the cuisine of the rest of southern Italy, pasta plays an important part in Sicilian cuisine, as does rice; for example with [arancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arancini).[^cite_note-210] As well as using some other cheeses, Sicily has spawned some of its own, using both cow's and sheep's milk, such as [pecorino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Siciliano) and [caciocavallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caciocavallo).[^cite_note-211] Spices used include [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron), [nutmeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutmeg), [clove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clove), [pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_pepper), and [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon), which were introduced by the Arabs. [Parsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsley) is used abundantly in many dishes. Although Sicilian cuisine is commonly associated with sea food, meat dishes, including [goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose), [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_sheep), goat, rabbit, and [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_meat), are also found in Sicily. It was the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) and [Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen) who first introduced a fondness for meat dishes to the island.[^cite_note-212] Some varieties of wine are produced from vines that are relatively unique to the island, such as the [Nero d'Avola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero_d'Avola) made near the baroque town of [Noto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noto).[^cite_note-213]

### Sports



[Giuseppe Gibilisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Gibilisco), [pole vaulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_vaulting) from [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily), [2003 World Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Championships_in_Athletics) and bronze [Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_pole_vault) medalist

The most popular sport in Sicily is [football](10568.md), which came to the fore in the late 19th century under the influence of the English. Some of the oldest football clubs in Italy are from Sicily: the three most successful are [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Citt%C3%A0_di_Palermo), [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcio_Catania), and [Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C.R._Messina), which have played 29, 17 and 5 seasons in the [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) respectively. No club from Sicily has ever won Serie A, but football is still deeply embedded in local culture and all over Sicily most towns have a representative team.[^cite_note-derbysicil-214]

Palermo and Catania have a heated rivalry and compete in the [Sicilian derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_di_Sicilia) together. Palermo is the only team in Sicily to have played on the European stage, in the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup). In the island, the most noted footballer is [Salvatore Schillaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Schillaci), who won the [Golden Boot](3482503.md) at the [1990 FIFA World Cup](157232.md) with [Italy](362466.md).[^cite_note-derbysicil-214] Other noted players include [Giuseppe Furino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Furino), [Pietro Anastasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Anastasi), [Francesco Coco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Coco), [Christian Riganò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Rigan%C3%B2), and [Roberto Galia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Galia).[^cite_note-derbysicil-214] There have also been some noted managers from the island, such as [Carmelo Di Bella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelo_Di_Bella) and [Franco Scoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Scoglio).

Although football is the most popular sport in Sicily, the island also has participants in other fields. [Amatori Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amatori_Catania) have competed in the top Italian national [rugby union](25405.md) league called [National Championship of Excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top12). They have even participated at the European level in the [European Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Challenge_Cup). Competing in the basketball variation of [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A_\(basketball\)) is [Orlandina Basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlandina_Basket) from [Capo d'Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capo_d'Orlando) in the [province of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Messina), where the sport has a reasonable following. Various other sports that are played to some extent include volleyball, [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball), and [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo). Previously, in [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport), Sicily held the prominent [Targa Florio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targa_Florio) sports car race that took place in the Madonie Mountains, with the start-finish line in [Cerda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerda).[^cite_note-targa-215] The event was started in 1906 by Sicilian industrialist and automobile enthusiast [Vincenzo Florio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Florio), and ran until it was cancelled due to safety concerns in 1977.[^cite_note-targa-215]

From 28 September to 9 October 2005 [Trapani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapani) was the location of Acts 8 and 9 of the [Louis Vuitton Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Vuitton_Cup). This sailing race featured, among other entrants, all boats that took part in the 2007 [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup).

**Clockwise from top**:&#x20;

Each town and city has its own patron saint, and the [feast days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_day) are marked by colourful processions through the streets with marching bands and displays of fireworks.

Sicilian religious festivals also include the *presepe vivente* (living [nativity scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_scene)), which takes place at Christmas time. Deftly combining religion and folklore, it is a constructed mock 19th-century Sicilian village, complete with a nativity scene, and has people of all ages dressed in the costumes of the period, some impersonating the Holy Family, and others working as artisans of their particular assigned trade. It is normally concluded on [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_\(holiday\)), often highlighted by the arrival of the [magi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magi) on horseback.

Oral tradition plays a large role in Sicilian folklore. Many stories passed down from generation to generation involve a character named "[Giufà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuf%C3%A0)". Anecdotes from this character's life preserve Sicilian culture as well as convey moral messages.

Sicilians also enjoy outdoor festivals, held in the local square or *piazza* where live music and dancing are performed on stage, and food fairs or *sagre* are set up in booths lining the square. These offer various local specialties, as well as typical Sicilian food. Normally these events are concluded with fireworks. A noted *sagra* is the *Sagra del Carciofo* or *[Artichoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artichoke) Festival*, which is held annually in [Ramacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramacca) in April. The most important traditional event in Sicily is the [carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival). Famous carnivals are in [Acireale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acireale), [Misterbianco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misterbianco), [Regalbuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regalbuto), [Paternò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patern%C3%B2), [Sciacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciacca), [Termini Imerese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termini_Imerese).

The **[Opera dei Pupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_dei_Pupi)** (Opera of the Puppets; [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language): Òpira dî pupi) is a [marionette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marionette) theatrical representation of Frankish romantic poems such as the [Song of Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_Roland) or *[Orlando furioso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_furioso)* that is one of the characteristic cultural traditions of Sicily. The sides of donkey carts are decorated with intricate, painted scenes; these same tales are enacted in traditional [puppet theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_theatre) featuring hand-made marionettes of wood. The opera of the puppets and the Sicilian tradition of *cantastorî* (singers of tales) are rooted in the Provençal [troubadour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubadour) tradition in Sicily during the reign of [Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), in the first half of the 13th century. A great place to see this marionette art is the puppet theatres of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo). The Sicilian marionette theatre Opera dei Pupi was proclaimed in 2001 and inscribed in 2008 in the [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists).[^cite_note-216]

Today, there are only a few troupes that maintain the tradition. They often perform for tourists. However, there are no longer the great historical families of marionettists, such as the Greco of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo); the [Canino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspare_Canino) of [Partinico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partinico) and [Alcamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcamo); Crimi, Trombetta and Napoli of [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), Pennisi and Macri of [Acireale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acireale), Profeta of [Licata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licata), Gargano and Grasso of [Agrigento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigento). One can, however, admire the richest collection of marionettes at the Museo Internazionale delle Marionette Antonio Pasqualino and at the [Museo Etnografico Siciliano Giuseppe Pitrè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Etnografico_Siciliano_Giuseppe_Pitr%C3%A8) in Palermo. Other elaborate marionettes are on display at the Museo Civico Vagliasindi in [Randazzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randazzo).

The [Sicilian cart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_cart) is an ornate, colourful style of a horse- or donkey-drawn cart native to Sicily. Sicilian woodcarver [George Petralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Petralia?action=edit\&redlink=1) states that horses were mostly used in the city and flat plains, while donkeys or mules were more often used in rough terrain for hauling heavy loads.[^cite_note-217] The cart has two wheels and is primarily handmade out of wood with iron components.

The Sicilian [coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppola_\(cap\)) is a traditional kind of [flat cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_cap) typically worn by men in Sicily. First used by English nobles during the late 18th century, the *tascu* began being used in Sicily in the early 20th century as a driving cap, usually worn by car drivers. The *coppola* is usually made in [tweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_\(cloth\)). Today it is widely regarded as a definitive symbol of Sicilian heritage.[^cite_note-218]

### &#x20; Flag and emblem

Main articles: [Triskelion § Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion#Sicily), and [Flag of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Sicily)







**Clockwise from top**:&#x20;

- The [Triskelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion) and [Gorgoneion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgoneion) symbol of Sicily

- Red-figure [Lekythos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekythos) from a tomb near [Licata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licata), ca. 470 BCE, depicting a warrior with a triskelion on the shield

- [Triskelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion) painted on [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_art) of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia) vase, [Agrigento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigento)

The [Flag of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Sicily), regarded as a regional icon, was first adopted in 1282, after the [Sicilian Vespers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Vespers) of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo).[^cite_note-219] It is characterised by the presence of the [triskeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion) in the middle, depicting the head of [Medusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusa) and three wheat ears representing the extreme fertility of the land of Sicily. In early mythology, when Medusa was slain and beheaded by Perseus, the Medusa head was placed in the centre of Athena's shield.[^cite_note-220]

Palermo and Corleone were the first two cities to found a confederation against the [Angevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou) rule. The triskeles symbol came to be on the Sicilian flag in 1943 during [World War II](32927.md) when [Andrea Finocchiaro Aprile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Finocchiaro_Aprile) led an independence movement, in collaboration with the allies. Their plan was to help Sicily become independent and form a free republic. The colours, likewise introduced in the 1940s, respectively represent the cities of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) and [Corleone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corleone). The separatist behind the movement used a yellow and red flag with the Trinacria in the centre of it. When World War II ended, Sicily was recognized as an autonomous region in the Italian Republic.

The flag became the official public flag of the *Regione Siciliana* in January 2000, after the passing of an apposite regional law, which advocates its use on public buildings, schools and city halls along with the national [Italian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy) and the [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_EU) one.

Familiar as an ancient symbol of the region, the [Triskelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion) is also featured on Greek coins of [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily), such as coins of [Agathocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathocles) (317–289 BC).The symbol dates back to when Sicily was part of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia), the colonial extension of [Greece](12108.md) beyond the [Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea).[^cite_note-221] The triskelion was revived, as a [neoclassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) – and non-[Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon) – emblem for the new Napoleonic Kingdom of the [Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Sicilies), by [Joachim Murat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Murat) in 1808. In the case of Sicily, the triskelion symbol is said to represent the three [capes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_\(geography\)) ([headlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headland) or [promontories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promontory) of the island of Sicily, namely: [Pelorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_del_Faro) (Peloro, Tip of Faro, Messina: North-East); [Pachynus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capo_Passero) (Passero, Syracuse: South); and [Lilybæum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala) (Lilibeo, Cape Boeo, Marsala: West), which form three points of a triangle.[^cite_note-222]
