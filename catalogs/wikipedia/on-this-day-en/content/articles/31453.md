**Tel Aviv**,[^cite_note-8] officially **Tel Aviv-Yafo**,[^cite_note-9] and also known as **Tel Aviv-Jaffa**, is the most populous city in the [Gush Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Dan) metropolitan area of [Israel](9282173.md). Located on the [Israeli Mediterranean coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_coastal_plain) and with a population of 494,900, it is the [economic](14690.md) and [technological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_of_Israel) center of the country and a global [high-tech hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Wadi). If [East Jerusalem](343508.md) is [considered part of Israel](4024423.md), Tel Aviv is the country's second-most-populous city, after [Jerusalem](16043.md); if not, Tel Aviv is the most populous city, ahead of [West Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Jerusalem).[^cite_note-11]

Tel Aviv is governed by the [Tel Aviv-Yafo Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv-Yafo_Municipality), headed by Mayor [Ron Huldai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Huldai), and is home to most of Israel's [foreign embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_Israel).[^cite_note-14] It is a [beta+ world city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) and is ranked 53rd in the 2022 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index). Tel Aviv has the [third- or fourth-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP) and the largest economy [per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita) in the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Brookings-17] Tel Aviv is ranked the 4th top global startup ecosystem hub.[^cite_note-Wrobel-2024-18][^cite_note-Genome-19] The city currently has the highest cost of living in the world.[^cite_note-BBCcol-20][^cite_note-21] Tel Aviv receives over 2.5 million international visitors annually.[^cite_note-Goldman-2012-22][^cite_note-Sapty-2011-23] Tel Aviv is home to [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University), the largest university in the country with more than 30,000 students.

The city was founded in 1909 by the [Yishuv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishuv) ([Jewish](25955086.md) residents) and initially given the Hebrew name *Ahuzat Bayit* ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): אחוזת בית, lit. 'House Estate' or 'Homestead'),[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] [namesake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namesake) of the Jewish association which established the neighbourhood as a modern housing estate on the outskirts of the ancient [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) (*Yafo* in Hebrew), then part of the [Mutasarrifate of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarrifate_of_Jerusalem) within the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). Its name was changed the following year to Tel Aviv, after the biblical name [Tel Abib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Abib) (lit. "[Tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_\(archaeology\)) of Spring") adopted by [Nahum Sokolow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahum_Sokolow) as the title for his Hebrew translation of [Theodor Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl)'s 1902 novel [*Altneuland*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_New_Land) ("Old New Land"). Other Jewish suburbs of Jaffa had been established before Tel Aviv, the oldest among them being [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek). Over the following decades, Tel Aviv started rapidly developing and expanding, under the leadership of [Meir Dizengoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Dizengoff).[^cite_note-Elkayam-1990-26] Tel Aviv was given township status within the Jaffa Municipality in 1921, and eventually became independent from Jaffa in 1934.[^cite_note-Goren-27][^cite_note-Gorion-28] Accelerating Jewish immigration, [largely from Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Aliyah), caused the growth of Tel Aviv to soon outpace that of Jaffa, which had a majority [Arab](23267.md) population at the time.[^cite_note-29] In 1948, the [Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) was proclaimed in the city, and government functions were carried out there until they moved to Jerusalem over the following few years.[^cite_note-30] After the [1947–1949 Palestine war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947%E2%80%931949_Palestine_war), Tel Aviv began the municipal annexation of parts of Jaffa, being fully reunified with Jaffa under the name Tel Aviv in April 1950, before a renaming to Tel Aviv-Yafo in August 1950.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31]

Tel Aviv's [White City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City_\(Tel_Aviv\)), designated a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2003, comprises the world's largest concentration of [International Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Style_\(architecture\)) buildings, including [Bauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus) and other related [modernist architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture) styles.[^cite_note-UNESCO-32][^cite_note-times-33] Popular attractions include [Old Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Jaffa), the [Eretz Israel Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretz_Israel_Museum), the [Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Museum_of_Art), [Yarkon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkon_Park), and the [city's promenade and beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Promenade).

## Etymology and origins

See also: [Tel Abib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Abib)



The city's name, chosen in 1910, comes from the title of the first Hebrew edition of [Theodor Herzl's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl) 1902 novel *[Altneuland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_New_Land)*, translated from German by [Nahum Sokolow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahum_Sokolow).

*Tel Aviv* is the Hebrew title of [Theodor Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl)'s 1902 novel *[Altneuland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_New_Land)* ("Old New Land"), as translated from German by [Nahum Sokolow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahum_Sokolow). Sokolow had adopted the name of a [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) site near the city of Babylon mentioned in [Ezekiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Ezekiel): "Then I came to them of the captivity at Tel Abib \[Tel Aviv], that lived by the [river Chebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Chebar), and to where they lived; and I sat there overwhelmed among them seven days."[^cite_note-Ezekiel_3-1] The name was chosen in 1910 from several suggestions, including "[Herzliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya)". It was found fitting as it embraced the idea of a renaissance in the ancient Jewish homeland. *Aviv* (אביב, or *Abib*) is a Hebrew word that can be translated as "spring", symbolizing renewal, and *[tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_\(archaeology\))* (or *tel*) is an artificial mound created over centuries through the accumulation of successive layers of civilization built one over the other and symbolizing the ancient.

Although founded in 1909 as a small settlement on the sand dunes north of Jaffa, Tel Aviv was envisaged as a future city from the start. Its founders hoped that in contrast to what they perceived as the squalid and unsanitary conditions of neighbouring Arab towns, Tel Aviv was to be a clean and modern city, inspired by the European cities of [Warsaw](32908.md) and [Odesa](55545.md).[^cite_note-Shavit_2012-34] The marketing pamphlets advocating for its establishment stated:[^cite_note-Shavit_2012-34]

> In this city we will build the streets so they have roads and sidewalks and electric lights. Every house will have water from wells that will flow through pipes as in every modern European city, and also sewerage pipes will be installed for the health of the city and its residents.&#x20;

— Akiva Arieh Weiss, 1906

### Jaffa

Main article: [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa)



Ancient port of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) where, according to the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible), [Jonah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah) set sail into the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) before being swallowed by a fish

[^cite_note-35]

The [walled city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_city) of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) is modern-day Tel Aviv-Yafo's only urban centre that existed in early modern times. Jaffa was an important port city in the region for millennia. Archaeological evidence shows signs of human settlement there starting in roughly 7,500 BC.[^cite_note-36] The city was established around 1,800 BC at the latest.[^cite_note-37] Its natural harbour has been used since the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age). By the time Tel Aviv was founded as a separate city during [Ottoman](22278.md) rule of the region, Jaffa had been ruled by the [Canaanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan), [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), [Philistines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philistines), [Israelites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire), [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia), [Ptolemies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemies), [Seleucids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire), [Hasmoneans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_dynasty), [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [the early Islamic caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate), [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades), [Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty), and [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) before coming under Ottoman rule in 1515. It had been fought over numerous times. The city is mentioned in ancient Egyptian documents, as well as the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible).

Other ancient sites in Tel Aviv include: [Tell Qasile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Qasile), [Tel Gerisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Gerisa), [Abattoir Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abattoir_Hill), [Tel Hashash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Hashash), and [Tell Qudadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Qudadi).

During the [First Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Aliyah) in the 1880s, when Jewish immigrants began arriving in the region in significant numbers, new Jewish neighborhoods were founded outside Jaffa on the current territory of Tel Aviv. The first was [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek), founded in 1887 by [Mizrahi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews) due to overcrowding in Jaffa and built on lands owned by [Aharon Chelouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aharon_Chelouche).[^cite_note-Elkayam-1990-26] Other neighborhoods were [Neve Shalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Shalom_\(neighborhood\)) (1890), [Yafa Nof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yafa_Nof_\(neighborhood\)) (1896), [Achva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achva_\(neighborhood\)) (1899), [Ohel Moshe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_Moshe_\(neighborhood\)) (1904), [Kerem HaTeimanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerem_HaTeimanim) (1906), and others. Those neighborhoods later joined Tel Aviv, becoming separated from Jaffa.

The [Second Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Aliyah) led to further expansion. In 1906, a group of Jews, among them residents of Jaffa, followed the initiative of [Akiva Aryeh Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Aryeh_Weiss) and banded together to form the *Ahuzat Bayit* (lit. 'homestead') society. One of the society's goals was to form a "Hebrew urban centre in a healthy environment, planned according to the rules of aesthetics and modern hygiene".[^cite_note-38] The urban planning for the new city was influenced by the [garden city movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_city_movement).[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] The first 60 plots were purchased in Kerem Djebali (Karm al-Jabali) near Jaffa by [Jacobus Kann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Kann), a Dutch citizen, who registered them in his name to circumvent the Turkish prohibition on Jewish land acquisition.[^cite_note-40] [Meir Dizengoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Dizengoff), later Tel Aviv's first [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Tel_Aviv), also joined the Ahuzat Bayit society.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

On 11 April 1909, 66 Jewish families gathered on a desolate sand dune to parcel out the land by lottery using seashells. This gathering is considered the official date of the establishment of Tel Aviv. The lottery was organised by [Akiva Aryeh Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Aryeh_Weiss), president of the building society.[^cite_note-AzaryahuTroen2012-43][^cite_note-44] Weiss collected 120 sea shells on the beach, half of them white and half of them grey. The members' names were written on the white shells and the plot numbers on the grey shells. A boy drew names from one box of shells and a girl drew plot numbers from the second box. A photographer, Abraham Soskin (b. 1881 in Russia, made *[aliyah](351513.md)* 1906[^cite_note-45]), documented the event. The first water well was later dug at this site, located on what is today [Rothschild Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Boulevard), across from Dizengoff House.[^cite_note-46] Within a year, [Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl), [Ahad Ha'am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahad_Ha'am), [Yehuda Halevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_Halevi), [Lilienblum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Leib_Lilienblum), and Rothschild streets were built; a water system was installed; and 66 houses (including some on six subdivided plots) were completed.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] At the end of Herzl Street, a plot was allocated for a new building for the [Herzliya Hebrew High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Herzliya_Hebrew_Gymnasium), founded in Jaffa in 1906.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] The cornerstone for the building was laid on 28 July 1909. The town was originally named Ahuzat Bayit ("Homestead" in Hebrew). On 21 May 1910, the name Tel Aviv was adopted.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] The flag and city arms of Tel Aviv (see above) contain under the red Star of David 2 words from the biblical book of Jeremiah: "I (God) will build You up again and you will be rebuilt." (Jer 31\:4) Tel Aviv was planned as an independent Hebrew city with wide streets and boulevards, running water for each house, and street lights.[^cite_note-brit-47]

By 1914, Tel Aviv had grown to more than 1 km<sup>2</sup> (247 acres) and had 182 houses.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39][^cite_note-shemerisrael-48] In 1915 a census of Tel Aviv was conducted, recording a population 2,679.[^cite_note-49] However, growth halted during [World War I](4764461.md). In 1917, the [Ottoman](22278.md) authorities [expelled the residents of Jaffa and Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_and_Jaffa_deportation) as a wartime measure.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] A report published in *The New York Times* by United States Consul Garrels in [Alexandria, Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) described the Jaffa deportation of early April 1917. The orders of evacuation were aimed chiefly at the Jewish population.[^cite_note-Turkish-50] Jews were free to return to their homes in Tel Aviv at the end of the following year when, with the end of World War I and the defeat of the Ottomans, the British took control of Palestine. In 1919, Tel Aviv 182 houses, the same number that it had in 1914.[^cite_note-shemerisrael-48]

The town had rapidly become an attraction to immigrants, with a local activist writing:[^cite_note-51]

> The immigrants were attracted to Tel Aviv because they found in it all the comforts they were used to in Europe: electric light, water, a little cleanliness, cinema, opera, theatre, and also more or less advanced schools... busy streets, full restaurants, cafes open until 2 a.m., singing, music, and dancing.

### British administration (1917–1934)

Tel Aviv, along with the rest of the Jaffa municipality, was conquered by the [British imperial army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire#First_World_War) in late 1917 during the [Sinai and Palestine Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_and_Palestine_Campaign) of [World War I](4764461.md) and became part of British-administered [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) until 1948.

Tel Aviv, established as suburb of Jaffa, received "township" or local council status within the Jaffa Municipality in 1921.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-Goren-27][^cite_note-Gorion-28] According to a [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted in 1922 by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Tel Aviv had a population of 15,185 (15,065 Jews, 78 Muslims and 42 Christians).[^cite_note-Census1922-53] The population of Tel Aviv had increased to around 34,000 by 1925.[^cite_note-UNESCO-32][^cite_note-autogenerated2-54] The [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) recorded Tel Aviv as having a population of 46,101 (45,564 Jews, 288 with no religion, 143 Christians, and 106 Muslims) in 12,545 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-55]

With increasing Jewish immigration during the [British administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), friction between Arabs and Jews in Palestine increased. On 1 May 1921, the [Jaffa riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_riots_\(May_1921\)) resulted in the deaths of 48 Arabs and 47 Jews and injuries to 146 Jews and 73 Arabs.[^cite_note-56] In the wake of this violence, many Jews left Jaffa for Tel Aviv.

Tel Aviv began to develop as a commercial center.[^cite_note-57] In 1923, Tel Aviv was the first town to be wired to electricity in Palestine, followed by Jaffa later in the same year. The opening ceremony of the Jaffa Electric Company powerhouse, on 10 June 1923, celebrated the lighting of the two main streets of Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-58]

In 1925, the Scottish biologist, sociologist, philanthropist and pioneering town planner [Patrick Geddes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Geddes) drew up the [Geddes Plan for Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geddes_Plan_for_Tel_Aviv), a [master plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) for Tel Aviv which was adopted by the city council led by [Meir Dizengoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Dizengoff). Geddes's plan for developing the northern part of the township was based on [Ebenezer Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer_Howard)'s [garden city movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_city_movement).[^cite_note-Levine-59] The plan consisted of four main features: a hierarchical system of streets laid out in a grid, large blocks consisting of small-scale domestic dwellings, the organization of these blocks around central open spaces, and the concentration of cultural institutions to form a civic center.[^cite_note-Welter_2009_Master_Plan-60] While most of the northern area of Tel Aviv was built according to this plan, the [influx of European refugees in the 1930s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Aliyah) necessitated the construction of taller apartment buildings on a larger footprint in the city.[^cite_note-Welter_2009_Master_Plan-60]

[Ben Gurion House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_House) was built in 1930–31, part of a new workers' housing development. At the same time, Jewish cultural life was given a boost by the establishment of the Ohel Theatre and the decision of [Habima Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habima_Theatre) to make Tel Aviv its permanent base in 1931.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39]

### 1934 municipal independence from Jaffa



Shadal Street in 1926



[Rothschild Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Boulevard) in the late 1930s



Tel Aviv, Allenby Street, 1940

Tel Aviv was granted the status of an independent municipality separate from Jaffa in 1934.[^cite_note-Goren-27][^cite_note-Gorion-28] The Jewish population rose dramatically during the [Fifth Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Aliyah) after the Nazis came to power in Germany.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] Many new Jewish immigrants to Palestine disembarked in Jaffa, and remained in Tel Aviv, turning the city into a center of urban life. Friction during the [1936–39 Arab revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine) led to the opening of a local Jewish port, [Tel Aviv Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Port), independent of Jaffa, in 1938. It closed on 25 October 1965. [Lydda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_International_Airport) (later Ben Gurion Airport) and [Sde Dov Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sde_Dov_Airport) opened between 1937 and 1938.[^cite_note-61] According to the [Jewish Virtual Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Virtual_Library), the Jewish population of Tel Aviv had risen to 150,000 by 1937, compared to Jaffa's mainly Arab 69,000 residents, and by 1939 rose to 160,000, which was over a third of Palestine's total Jewish population.[^cite_note-JewishVL-39] The village statistics of 1938 listed Tel Aviv's population as 140,000, all Jews.[^cite_note-62]

Many [German Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Jews) architects trained at the [Bauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus), the [Modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) school of architecture in Germany, and left Germany during the 1930s. Some, like [Arieh Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arieh_Sharon), came to Palestine and adapted the architectural outlook of the Bauhaus and similar schools to the local conditions there, creating what is recognized as the largest concentration of buildings in the International Style in the world.[^cite_note-UNESCO-32]

Tel Aviv's [White City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City_\(Tel_Aviv\)) emerged in the 1930s, and became a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2003.[^cite_note-63] During World War II, Tel Aviv was [hit by Italian airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Palestine_in_World_War_II) on 9 September 1940, which killed 137 people in the city.[^cite_note-64]

The [village statistics of 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) listed Tel Aviv's population as 166,660, consisting of 166,000 Jews, 300 "other", 230 Christians, and 130 Muslims.[^cite_note-65]

During the [Jewish insurgency in Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_insurgency_in_Mandatory_Palestine), Jewish [Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun) and [Lehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehi_\(group\)) guerrillas launched repeated attacks against British military, police, and government targets in the city. In 1946, following the [King David Hotel bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_David_Hotel_bombing), the British carried out [Operation Shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shark), in which the entire city was searched for Jewish militants and most of the residents questioned, during which the entire city was placed under curfew. During the [March 1947 martial law in Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_1947_martial_law_in_Mandatory_Palestine), Tel Aviv was placed under martial law by the British authorities for 15 days, with the residents kept under curfew for all but three hours a day as British forces scoured the city for militants. In spite of this, Jewish guerrilla attacks continued in Tel Aviv and other areas under martial law in Palestine.

According to the [1947 UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) for dividing Palestine into Jewish and Arab states, Tel Aviv was to be included in the proposed [Jewish state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people). Jaffa with, as of 1945, a population of 101,580 people—53,930 Muslims, 30,820 Jews and 16,800 Christians—was designated as part of the Arab state. [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947-1948_Civil_War_in_Mandatory_Palestine) broke out in the country and in particular between the neighbouring cities of Tel Aviv and Jaffa, which had been assigned to the Jewish and Arab states respectively. After several months of fighting, on 13 May 1948, Jaffa came under Israeli control. Most of the Arab population left during or after the fighting, amid a wider regional conflict following Arab rejection of the UN Partition Plan.

### Israel

#### 1940s and 1950s



Crowd outside Dizengoff House (now [Independence Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Hall_\(Israel\))) to witness the proclamation and signing of Israel's Declaration of Independence in 1948

After Israel [declared Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) on 14 May 1948, Tel Aviv was the temporary government center of the State of Israel. The city was repeatedly bombed by Egyptian warplanes and shelled by Egyptian warships during the [Israeli War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab-Israeli_War), killing around 150 people. The most significant attack was the [bombing of the central bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Tel_Aviv_bus_station_bombing), in which 42 people were killed.[^cite_note-66] On 3 June 1948, the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) scored its first aerial victory over Tel Aviv when Israeli fighter pilot [Modi Alon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modi_Alon) shot down two Egyptian bombers during a raid. The city was also the scene of fighting between the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) and [Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun) during the [Altalena Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altalena_Affair), in which the IDF stopped an Irgun attempt to import arms for its own use.

In December 1949, the Israeli government relocated to [Jerusalem](16043.md). Due to the international dispute over the [status of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positions_on_Jerusalem), most embassies remained in or near Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-VTLV-67] The boundaries of Tel Aviv and Jaffa became a matter of contention between the Tel Aviv municipality and the Israeli government in 1948.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] The former wished to incorporate only the northern Jewish suburbs of Jaffa, while the latter wanted a more complete unification.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] The issue also had international sensitivity, since the main part of Jaffa was in the Arab portion of the [United Nations Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine), whereas Tel Aviv was not, and no armistice agreements had yet been signed.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] On 10 December 1948, the government announced the annexation to Tel Aviv of Jaffa's Jewish suburbs, the [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) neighborhood of [Abu Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Kabir), the Arab village of [Salama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salama,_Jaffa) and some of its agricultural land, and the Jewish [Hatikva Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatikva_Quarter).[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] On 25 February 1949, the former Arab village of [al-Shaykh Muwannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shaykh_Muwannis) was also incorporated into Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] On 18 May 1949, [Manshiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manshiya) and part of Jaffa's central zone were added, for the first time including land that had been in the Arab portion of the UN partition plan.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] The government voted on the unification of Tel Aviv and Jaffa on 4 October 1949, but the decision was not implemented until 24 April 1950 due to the opposition of Tel Aviv mayor [Israel Rokach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Rokach).[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] The name of the unified city was Tel Aviv until 19 August 1950, when it was renamed Tel Aviv-Yafo in order to preserve the historical name Jaffa.[^cite_note-Golan1995-31] Tel Aviv thus grew to 42 km<sup>2</sup> (16.2 sq mi). In 1949, a memorial to the 60 founders of Tel Aviv was constructed.[^cite_note-68]



Tel Aviv in 1961

#### 1960s and 1970s

In the 1960s, some of the older buildings were demolished, making way for the country's first high-rises. The historic [Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Hebrew_Gymnasium) was controversially demolished, to make way for the [Shalom Meir Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalom_Meir_Tower), which was completed in 1965, and remained [Israel's tallest building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Israel) until 1999. Tel Aviv's population peaked in the early 1960s at 390,000, representing 16 percent of the country's total.[^cite_note-profile-69] By the early 1970s, the population of Tel Aviv began to decline, followed by a period of [urban decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_decay). By 1981, Tel Aviv had entered not just natural population decline, but an absolute population decline as well.[^cite_note-Interregional_Migration_2012_page_164-70] In the late 1980s the city had an aging population of 317,000.[^cite_note-profile-69] Construction activity had moved away from the inner ring of Tel Aviv, and had moved to its outer perimeter and adjoining cities. A mass out-migration of residents from Tel Aviv, to adjoining cities like [Petah Tikva](202411.md) and [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot), where better housing conditions were available, was underway by the beginning of the 1970s, and only accelerated by the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War).[^cite_note-Interregional_Migration_2012_page_164-70] Cramped housing conditions and high property prices pushed families out of Tel Aviv and deterred young people from moving in.[^cite_note-profile-69] From the beginning of 1970s, the common image of Tel Aviv became that of a decaying city,[^cite_note-Tel_Aviv_2007_page_132-71] as Tel Aviv's population fell 20%.[^cite_note-StrategicPlan26-72]



Tel Aviv in 1970

In the 1970s, the apparent sense of Tel Aviv's urban decline became a theme in the work of novelists such as [Yaakov Shabtai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaakov_Shabtai), in works describing the city such as *Sof Davar* (*The End of Things*) and *Zikhron Devarim* (*The Memory of Things*).[^cite_note-Tel_Aviv_2007_page_132-71] A symptomatic article of 1980 asked "Is Tel Aviv Dying?" and portrayed what it saw as the city's existential problems: "Residents leaving the city, businesses penetrating into residential areas, economic and social gaps, deteriorating neighbourhoods, contaminated air – Is the First Hebrew City destined for a slow death? Will it become a ghost town?".[^cite_note-Tel_Aviv_2007_page_132-71] However, others saw this as a transitional period. In the late 1970s, mayor [Shlomo Lahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Lahat) partnered with an urban planning firm to make changes to the city's urban fabric. Offices which had emerged throughout the city's neighborhoods, turning them into corporate spaces, were removed. Tel Aviv's main business center became the area along the [Ayalon Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_20_\(Israel\)), enabling neighborhoods in the rest of the city to be revitalized.[^cite_note-73] By the late 1980s, attitudes to the city's future had become markedly more optimistic. It had also become a center of nightlife and discotheques for Israelis who lived in the suburbs and adjoining cities. By 1989, Tel Aviv had acquired the nickname "Nonstop City", as a reflection of the growing recognition of its nightlife and 24/7 culture, and "Nonstop City" had to some extent replaced the former moniker of "First Hebrew City".[^cite_note-74] The largest project built in this era was the [Dizengoff Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Center), Israel's first shopping mall, which was completed in 1983. Other notable projects included the construction of [Marganit Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marganit_Tower) in 1987, the opening of the [Suzanne Dellal Center for Dance and Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Dellal_Center_for_Dance_and_Theater) in 1989, and the [Tel Aviv Cinematheque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Cinematheque) (opened in 1973 and located to the current building in 1989).



A poster mourning the [assassination of Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Yitzhak_Rabin) hangs in the [Carmel Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel_Market) in Tel Aviv, 1995

#### 1980s and 1990s

In the early 1980s, 13 embassies in Jerusalem moved to Tel Aviv as part of the [UN's measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_478) responding to Israel's 1980 [Jerusalem Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Law).[^cite_note-75] Today, most national embassies are located in Tel Aviv or environs.[^cite_note-76] In the 1990s, the decline in Tel Aviv's population began to be reversed and stabilized, at first temporarily due to a wave of immigrants from the [former Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states).[^cite_note-profile-69] Tel Aviv absorbed 42,000 immigrants from the FSU, many educated in scientific, technological, medical and mathematical fields.[^cite_note-StrategicPlan26-72] In this period, the number of engineers in the city doubled.[^cite_note-77] Tel Aviv soon began to emerge as a global high-tech center.[^cite_note-Economist-78] The construction of many [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_structures_in_Israel) and high-tech office buildings followed. In 1993, Tel Aviv was categorized as a [world city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city).[^cite_note-79] However, the city's municipality struggled to cope with an influx of new immigrants. Tel Aviv's tax base had been shrinking for many years, as a result of its preceding long term population decline, and this meant there was little money available at the time to invest in the city's deteriorating infrastructure and housing. In 1998, Tel Aviv was on the "verge of bankruptcy".[^cite_note-80] Economic difficulties would then be compounded by a wave of Palestinian suicide bombings in the city from the mid-1990s, to the end of the Second Intifada, as well as the [dot-com bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble), which affected the city's rapidly growing hi-tech sector. On 4 November 1995, Israel's prime minister, [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin), [was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Yitzhak_Rabin) at a rally in Tel Aviv in support of the Oslo peace accord. The outdoor plaza where this occurred, formerly known as Kikar Malchei Yisrael, was renamed [Rabin Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabin_Square).[^cite_note-81]



[Patriot missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_missiles) being launched to intercept an Iraqi [Scud missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile) during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in 1991

In the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in 1991, Tel Aviv was attacked by [Scud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud) missiles from Iraq. Iraq hoped to provoke an Israeli military response, which could have destroyed the US–Arab alliance. The [United States](3434750.md) pressured Israel not to retaliate, and after Israel acquiesced, the US and [Netherlands](21148.md) rushed [Patriot missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_missile) to defend against the attacks, but they proved largely ineffective. Tel Aviv and other Israeli cities continued to be hit by Scuds throughout the war, and every city in the Tel Aviv area except for [Bnei Brak](314671.md) was hit. A total of 74 Israelis died as a result of the Iraqi attacks, mostly from suffocation and heart attacks,[^cite_note-82] while approximately 230 Israelis were injured.[^cite_note-83] Extensive property damage was also caused, and some 4,000 Israelis were left homeless. It was feared that Iraq would fire missiles filled with [nerve agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_agent) or [sarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarin). As a result, the Israeli government issued [gas masks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_gas_mask) to its citizens. When the first Iraqi missiles hit Israel, some people injected themselves with an antidote for nerve gas. The inhabitants of the southeastern suburb of Hatikva erected an angel-monument as a sign of their gratitude that "it was through a great miracle, that many people were preserved from being killed by a direct hit of a Scud rocket."[^cite_note-84]



The [Dizengoff Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Center) after the [bombing of 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Center_suicide_bombing)

Since the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), Tel Aviv has suffered from [Palestinian political violence](2646043.md). The first [suicide attack](18835454.md) in Tel Aviv occurred on 19 October 1994, on the [Line 5 bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Street_bus_bombing), when a bomber killed 22 civilians and injured 50 as part of a [Hamas](13913.md) suicide campaign.[^cite_note-85] On 6 March 1996, another Hamas suicide bomber killed 13 people (12 civilians and 1 soldier), many of them children, in the [Dizengoff Center suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Center_suicide_bombing).[^cite_note-victims-86][^cite_note-nyt-87] Three women were killed by a Hamas terrorist in the [Café Apropo bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_Apropo_bombing) on 27 March 1997.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]



Tel Aviv Dolphinarium, demolished in 2018, site of the 2001 [Dolphinarium discotheque suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphinarium_discotheque_suicide_bombing), in which 21 Israelis, mostly teenagers, were killed

#### 2000s and 2010s

The demographic profile of the city changed in the 2000s, as it began to attract a higher proportion of young residents. By 2012, 28 percent of the city's population was aged between 20 and 34 years old. Between 2007 and 2012, the city's population growth averaged 6.29 percent. As a result of its population recovery and industrial transition, the city's finances were transformed, and by 2012 it was running a budget surplus and maintained a credit rating of AAA+.[^cite_note-91] In the 2000s and early 2010s, Tel Aviv received tens of thousands of illegal immigrants, primarily from [Sudan](27421.md) and [Eritrea](17238590.md),[^cite_note-autogenerated10-92] changing the demographic profile of areas of the city. In 2009, Tel Aviv celebrated its official centennial.[^cite_note-centennial-93] In addition to city- and country-wide celebrations, digital collections of historical materials were assembled. These include the History section of the official Tel Aviv-Yafo Centennial Year website;[^cite_note-centennial-93] the Ahuzat Bayit collection, which focuses on the founding families of Tel Aviv, and includes photographs and biographies;[^cite_note-94] and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University)'s Eliasaf Robinson Tel Aviv Collection,[^cite_note-95] documenting the history of the city. Today, the city is regarded as a strong candidate for [global city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_world_city).[^cite_note-GAWC-96] Over the past 60 years, Tel Aviv had developed into a [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularity), liberal-minded center with a vibrant nightlife and café culture.[^cite_note-Economist-78]

One of the deadliest attacks occurred on 1 June 2001, during the [Second Intifada](71717.md), when a suicide bomber exploded at the entrance to the [Dolphinarium discothèque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphinarium_discotheque_suicide_bombing), killing 21, mostly teenagers, and injuring 132.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Another Hamas suicide bomber killed six civilians and injured 70 in the [Allenby Street bus bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allenby_Street_bus_bombing).[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-bbc-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Twenty-three civilians were killed and over 100 injured in the [Tel Aviv central bus station massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_central_bus_station_massacre).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] [Al-Aqsa Martyrs Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Martyrs_Brigades) claimed responsibility for the attack. In the [Mike's Place suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike's_Place_suicide_bombing), an attack on a bar by a [British Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Muslim) suicide bomber resulted in the deaths of three civilians and wounded over 50.[^cite_note-jewishsf.com-108] Hamas and Al Aqsa Martyrs Brigades claimed joint responsibility. An Islamic Jihad bomber killed five and wounded over 50 on 25 February 2005 [Stage Club bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_Club_bombing).[^cite_note-USA_Today-2005-109] The most recent suicide attack in the city occurred on 17 April 2006, when 11 people were killed and at least 70 wounded in a [suicide bombing near the old central bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Rosh_Ha'ir_restaurant_bombing).[^cite_note-110] Another attack took place on 29 August 2011 in which a Palestinian attacker stole an Israeli taxi cab and rammed it into a police checkpoint guarding the popular [Haoman 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haoman_17) [nightclub](19321330.md) in Tel Aviv which was filled with 2,000[^cite_note-autogenerated5-111] [Israeli](181296.md) teenagers. After crashing, the assailant went on a stabbing spree, injuring eight people.[^cite_note-USA_Today-2005-109] Due to an [Israel Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Border_Police) roadblock at the entrance and immediate response of the Border Police team during the subsequent stabbings, a much larger and fatal mass-casualty incident was avoided.[^cite_note-112]



[Israeli Air Force](177619.md) [F-16I Sufas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-16_Fighting_Falcon_variants#F-16I_Sufa) over Tel Aviv

On 21 November 2012, during [Operation Pillar of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pillar_of_Defense), the Tel Aviv area was targeted by rockets, and air raid sirens were sounded in the city for the first time since the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). All of the rockets either missed populated areas or were shot down by an [Iron Dome](18342427.md) rocket defense battery stationed near the city. During the operation, a bomb blast on a bus wounded at least 28 civilians, three seriously.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] This was described as a terrorist attack by Israel, Russia, and the United States and was condemned by the United Nations, United States, United Kingdom, France and Russia, whilst Hamas spokesman [Sami Abu Zuhri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_Abu_Zuhri) declared that the organisation "blesses" the attack.[^cite_note-BBC-Nov21-117] More than 300 rockets were fired towards the Tel Aviv Metropolitan area in the [2021 Israel–Palestine crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_crisis).[^cite_note-118]

Short video about Tel Aviv from the [Israeli News Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_News_Company)

New laws were introduced to protect Modernist buildings, and efforts to preserve them were aided by [UNESCO](21786641.md) recognition of Tel Aviv's White City as a world heritage site in 2003. In the early 2000s, Tel Aviv municipality focused on attracting more young residents to the city. It made significant investment in major boulevards, to create attractive pedestrian corridors. Former industrial areas like the city's previously derelict Northern [Tel Aviv Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Port) and the [Jaffa railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_railway_station), were upgraded and transformed into leisure areas. A process of gentrification began in some of the poor neighborhoods of southern Tel Aviv and many older buildings began to be renovated.[^cite_note-Economist-78]

## Geography



Tel Aviv seen from space in 2016

Tel Aviv is located around [32°5′N 34°48′E  /  32.083°N 34.800°E  / 32.083; 34.800](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Tel_Aviv\&params=32_5_N_34_48_E_) on the [Israeli Mediterranean coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_coastal_plain), in central Israel, the [historic land bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Maris) between Europe, Asia and Africa. Immediately north of the ancient port of Jaffa, Tel Aviv lies on land that used to be sand dunes and as such has relatively poor [soil fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_\(soil\)). The land has been flattened and has no important gradients; its most notable geographical features are bluffs above the Mediterranean coastline and the [Yarkon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkon_River) mouth.[^cite_note-119] Because of the expansion of Tel Aviv and the Gush Dan region, absolute borders between Tel Aviv and Jaffa and between the city's neighborhoods do not exist.

The city is located 60 km (37 mi) northwest of Jerusalem and 90 km (56 mi) south of the city of [Haifa](55606.md).[^cite_note-120] Neighboring cities and towns include [Herzliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya) to the north, [Ramat HaSharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_HaSharon) to the northeast, [Petah Tikva](202411.md), [Bnei Brak](314671.md), [Ramat Gan](312761.md) and [Giv'atayim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giv'atayim) to the east, [Holon](231871.md) to the southeast, and [Bat Yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat_Yam) to the south.[^cite_note-121] The city is economically stratified between the north and south. Southern Tel Aviv is considered less affluent than northern Tel Aviv with the exception of [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek) and northern and north-western [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa). Central Tel Aviv is home to [Azrieli Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrieli_Center) and the important financial and commerce district along [Ayalon Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_20_\(Israel\)). The northern side of Tel Aviv is home to [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University), [Yarkon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkon_Park), and upscale residential neighborhoods such as [Ramat Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Aviv) and [Afeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afeka).[^cite_note-yarkoni-122]

### Environment

[^cite_note-123]

Tel Aviv is ranked as the [greenest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_city) in Israel.[^cite_note-124] Since 2008, city lights are turned off annually in support of [Earth Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Hour).[^cite_note-125] In February 2009, the municipality launched a water saving campaign, including competition granting free parking for a year to the household that is found to have consumed the least water per person.[^cite_note-senyorwater-126]

In the early 21st century, Tel Aviv's municipality transformed a derelict [power station](212141.md) into a public park, now named "Gan HaHashmal" ("Electricity Park"), paving the way for [eco-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) and environmentally conscious designs.[^cite_note-FT-127] In October 2008, Martin Weyl turned an old garbage dump near [Ben Gurion International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_International_Airport), called [Hiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiriya), into an attraction by building an arc of plastic bottles.[^cite_note-NYTPlastic-128] The site, which was renamed [Ariel Sharon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon_Park) to honor Israel's former prime minister, will serve as the centerpiece in what is to become a 2,000-acre (8.1 km<sup>2</sup>) [urban wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_wilderness) on the outskirts of Tel Aviv, designed by German [landscape architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_architect), [Peter Latz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Latz_\(landscape_architect\)).[^cite_note-NYTPlastic-128]

At the end of the 20th century, the city began restoring historical neighborhoods such as [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek) and many buildings from the 1920s and 1930s. Since 2007, the city hosts its well-known, annual [Open House Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_House_Tel_Aviv) weekend, which offers the general public free entrance to the city's famous landmarks, private houses and public buildings. In 2010, the design of the renovated Tel Aviv Port (*Nemal Tel Aviv*) won the award for outstanding landscape architecture at the European Biennial for Landscape Architecture in [Barcelona](4443.md).[^cite_note-129]

In 2014, the Sarona Market Complex opened, following an 8-year renovation project of [Sarona colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarona_\(colony\)).[^cite_note-130]

### Climate



[Rainstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainstorm) in Tel Aviv

Tel Aviv has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): Csa),[^cite_note-131] and enjoys plenty of sunshine throughout the year. Most precipitation falls in the form of rain between the months of October and April, with intervening dry summers, and there is almost no rainfall from June to September. The average annual temperature is 20.9 °C (69.6 °F), and the average sea temperature is 18–20 °C (64–68 °F) during the winter, and 24–29 °C (75–84 °F) during the summer. The city averages 528 mm (20.8 in) of precipitation annually.

Summers in Tel Aviv last about five months, from June to October. August, the warmest month, averages a high of 30.6 °C (87.1 °F), and a low of 25 °C (77 °F). The high relative humidity due to the location of the city by the Mediterranean Sea, in a combination with the high temperatures, creates a [thermal discomfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_comfort) during the summer. Summer low temperatures in Tel Aviv seldom drop below 20 °C (68 °F).

Winters are mild and wet, with most of the annual precipitation falling within the months of December, January and February as intense rainfall and thunderstorms. In January, the coolest month, the average maximum temperature is 17.6 °C (63.7 °F), the minimum temperature averages 10.2 °C (50.4 °F). During the coldest days of winter, temperatures may vary between 8 °C (46 °F) and 12 °C (54 °F). Both freezing temperatures and snowfall are extremely rare in the city.

Autumns and springs are characterized by sharp temperature changes, with heat waves that might be created due to hot and dry air masses that arrive from the nearby deserts. During heatwaves in autumn and springs, temperatures usually climb up to 35 °C (95 °F) and even up to 40 °C (104 °F), accompanied with exceptionally low humidity. An average day during autumn and spring has a high of 23 °C (73 °F) to 25 °C (77 °F), and a low of 15 °C (59 °F) to 18 °C (64 °F).

The highest recorded temperature in Tel Aviv was 46.5 °C (115.7 °F) on 17 May 1916, and the lowest is −1.9 °C (28.6 °F) on 7 February 1950, during a cold wave that brought the [only recorded snowfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_in_Israel#History) in Tel Aviv.

## Government

See also: [Mayor of Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Tel_Aviv) and [Mayoral elections in Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoral_elections_in_Tel_Aviv)



[Tel Aviv City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_City_Hall) and [Rabin Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabin_Square)

Tel Aviv is governed by a 31-member city council elected for a five-year term by in direct proportional elections,[^cite_note-govt-140] and a mayor elected for the same term by direct elections under a [two-round system](25932.md). Like all other mayors in Israel, no [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits) exist for the Mayor of Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-141] All Israeli citizens over the age of 17 with at least one year of residence in Tel Aviv are eligible to vote in municipal elections. The municipality is responsible for social services, community programs, public infrastructure, urban planning, tourism and other local affairs.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] The Tel Aviv City Hall is located at [Rabin Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabin_Square). [Ron Huldai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Huldai) has been mayor of Tel Aviv since 1998.[^cite_note-govt-140] Huldai was reelected to a sixth term in the [2024 municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tel_Aviv_municipal_election), defeating former [Minister of Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_\(Israel\)) [Orna Barbivai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orna_Barbivai).[^cite_note-145] Huldai has become city's longest-serving mayor, surpassing [Shlomo Lahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Lahat)'s 19-year term.[^cite_note-ToIWootliff-146] The shortest-serving mayor was [David Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bloch-Blumenfeld), who served a two year term between 1925 and 1927. In June 2026, City Council member Amir Badran was appointed deputy mayor. He became the second Arab citizen of Israel to hold the post in Tel Aviv-Yafo, first one being [Rifaat Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifaat_Turk) in 2003.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

Politically, Tel Aviv is known to be a stronghold for the left, in both local and national issues. The left wing vote is especially prevalent in the city's mostly affluent central and northern neighborhoods, though not the case for its working-class southeastern neighborhoods which tend to vote for right wing parties in national elections.[^cite_note-149] Outside the [kibbutzim](106200.md), [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz) receives more votes in Tel Aviv than in any other locality in Israel.[^cite_note-150]

### Voting Patterns

In the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election), 67 percent of voters in Tel Aviv-Yafo voted for the parties of the center-left coalition led by [Yair Lapid](2448072.md), while 27 percent voted for the parties of the right-wing coalition led by [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and 4 percent voted for the Arab parties.[^cite_note-151]

## Demographics



Tel Aviv population pyramid in 2021

Tel Aviv has a population of 494,900 spread over a land area of 52,000 dunams (52 km<sup>2</sup>; 20 sq mi). According to the [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (CBS), as of 2009 Tel Aviv's population was growing at an annual rate of 0.5 percent. Jews of all backgrounds formed 91.8 percent of the population, Muslims and [Arab Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christians) made up 4.2 percent, and the remainder belonged to other groups (including various Christian and Asian communities).[^cite_note-152] As Tel Aviv is a multicultural city, many languages are spoken in addition to [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language). According to some estimates, about 50,000 unregistered African and Asian [foreign workers](384142.md) live in the city.[^cite_note-153] Compared with Westernised cities, crime in Tel Aviv is relatively low.[^cite_note-154]



Aerial view of Tel Aviv

According to Tel Aviv-Yafo Municipality, the average income in the city, which had an [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) rate of 4.6% in 2014,[^cite_note-155] is 20% above the national average.[^cite_note-TA_Stats-156] The city's education standards are above the national average: of its 12th-grade students, 64.4 percent are eligible for [matriculation certificates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagrut).[^cite_note-TA_Stats-156] The age profile is relatively even, with 22.2 percent aged under 20, 18.5 percent aged 20–29, 24 percent aged 30–44, 16.2 percent aged between 45 and 59, and 19.1 percent older than 60.[^cite_note-157]

Tel Aviv's population reached a peak in the early 1960s at around 390,000, falling to 317,000 in the late 1980s as high property prices forced families out and deterred young couples from moving in.[^cite_note-profile-69] Since the 1990s, population has steadily grown.[^cite_note-profile-69] Today, the city's population is young and growing.[^cite_note-young-158] In 2006, 22,000 people moved to the city, while only 18,500 left,[^cite_note-young-158] and many of the new families had young children. Meanwhile, the average age of residents fell from 35.8 in 1983 to 34 in 2008.[^cite_note-young-158] The population over age 65 stands at 14.6 percent compared with 19% in 1983.[^cite_note-young-158] The population is expected to reach 535,000 in 2030.[^cite_note-159] Under a master plan for the city approved by the Tel Aviv District Planning and Building Committee in 2025, the city's population will grow to 600,000 by 2035.[^cite_note-master-plan-160]

### Religion



The [Great Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Synagogue_\(Tel_Aviv\)) is the largest synagogue in the center of Tel Aviv

Tel Aviv has 544 active synagogues,[^cite_note-LauINN-161] including historic buildings such as the [Great Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Synagogue_\(Tel_Aviv\)), established in the 1930s.[^cite_note-162] In 2008, a center for secular [Jewish studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_studies) and a [secular yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BINA_Center_for_Jewish_Identity_and_Hebrew_Culture) opened in the city.[^cite_note-arfa-163] Tensions between religious and [secular Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_culture) before the 2006 gay pride parade ended in vandalism of a synagogue.[^cite_note-cohen-164] The number of churches has grown to accommodate the religious needs of diplomats and foreign workers.[^cite_note-165] In 2019, the population was 89.9% Jewish, and 4.5% Arab; among Arabs, 82.8% were Muslim, 16.4% were Christian, and 0.8% were [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze).[^cite_note-166] The remaining 5 percent were not classified by religion. [Israel Meir Lau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Meir_Lau) is [Chief Rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Rabbi) of the city.[^cite_note-lau-167]

Tel Aviv is an ethnically diverse city. The Jewish population, which forms the majority group in Tel Aviv, consists of the descendants of immigrants from all parts of the world, including [Ashkenazi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) from Europe, North America, South America, Australia and South Africa, as well as [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews) and [Mizrahi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews) from Southern Europe, North Africa, India, Central Asia, West Asia, and the Arabian Peninsula. There are also a sizable number of [Ethiopian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Jews) and their descendants living in Tel Aviv. In addition to Muslim and [Arab Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christian) minorities in the city, several hundred [Armenian](387816.md) Christians reside in the city, concentrated mainly in [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa). There are also some Christians from the former Soviet Union who immigrated to Israel with Jewish spouses and relatives. In recent years, Tel Aviv has received many non-Jewish migrants from Asia and Africa, students, foreign workers (documented and undocumented) and refugees. There are many economic migrants and refugees from African countries, primarily [Eritrea](17238590.md) and [Sudan](27421.md), located in the southern part of the city.[^cite_note-168]

### Neighborhoods

Further information: [Neighborhoods of Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Tel_Aviv)

Tel Aviv is divided into nine boroughs that have formed naturally over the city's short history. The oldest of these is Jaffa, the ancient [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) out of which Tel Aviv grew. This area is traditionally made up demographically of a greater percentage of Arabs, but recent [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) is replacing them with a young professional and artist population. Similar processes are occurring in nearby [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek), the original Jewish neighborhood outside of Jaffa. [Ramat Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Aviv), a district in the northern part of the city that is largely made up of luxury apartments and includes [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University), as of 2007 is undergoing extensive expansion and is set to absorb the beachfront property of Sde Dov Airport after its decommissioning.[^cite_note-169] The area known as [HaKirya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaKirya) is the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) headquarters and a large [military base](843566.md).[^cite_note-yarkoni-122] Moreover, in the past few years, [Rothschild Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Boulevard) which is beginning in Neve Tzedek has become an attraction for tourists, businesses and startups. It features a wide, tree-lined central strip with pedestrian and bike lanes. Historically, there was a demographic split between the [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) northern side of the city, including the district of Ramat Aviv, and the southern, more [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) and [Mizrahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews) neighborhoods including [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek) and [Florentin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentin,_Tel_Aviv).[^cite_note-Economist-78]

Since the 1980s, major restoration and gentrification projects have been implemented in southern Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-Economist-78] Baruch Yoscovitz, city planner for Tel Aviv beginning in 2001, reworked old British plans for the Florentin neighborhood from the 1920s, adding green areas, pedestrian malls, and housing. The municipality invested two million shekels in the project. The goal was to make Florentin the [Soho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soho) of Tel Aviv, and attract artists and young professionals to the neighborhood. Street artists, such as [Dede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dede_\(artist\)), installation artists such as [Sigalit Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigalit_Landau), and many others made the upbeat neighborhood their home base.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Florentin is now known as a hip, "cool" place to be in Tel Aviv with coffeehouses, markets, bars, galleries and parties.[^cite_note-172]

### Health



[Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Sourasky_Medical_Center)

Tel Aviv is home to [Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Sourasky_Medical_Center), the third-largest hospital complex in Israel.[^cite_note-173] It contains Ichilov Hospital, the Ida Sourasky Rehabilitation Center, Lis Maternity and Women's Hospital, and Dana-Dwek Children's Hospital. The city also contains [Assuta Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assuta_Medical_Center), a private hospital which offers surgical and diagnostic services in all fields of medicine and has an [IVF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IVF) clinic.

### Education



[Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University)

In 2006, 51,359 children attended school in Tel Aviv, of whom 8,977 were in municipal kindergartens, 23,573 in municipal elementary schools, and 18,809 in high schools.[^cite_note-TA_Stats-156] Sixty-four percent of students in the city are entitled to matriculation, more than 5 percent higher than the national average.[^cite_note-TA_Stats-156] About 4,000 children are in first grade at schools in the city, and population growth is expected to raise this number to 6,000.[^cite_note-young-158] As a result, 20 additional kindergarten classes were opened in 2008–09 in the city. A new elementary school is planned north of Sde Dov as well as a new high school in northern Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-young-158]

The first Hebrew high school, called [Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Hebrew_Gymnasium), was established in Jaffa in 1905 and moved to Tel Aviv after its founding in 1909, where a new campus on Herzl Street was constructed for it.

[Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University), the largest university in Israel, is known internationally for its [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) and [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) departments. Together with [Bar-Ilan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-Ilan_University) in neighboring [Ramat Gan](312761.md), the student population numbers over 50,000, including a sizeable [international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_student).[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] Its campus is located in the neighborhood of [Ramat Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Aviv).[^cite_note-176] Tel Aviv also has several colleges.[^cite_note-colleges-177] The [Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Hebrew_Gymnasium) moved from Jaffa to old Tel Aviv in 1909 and moved to [Jabotinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabotinsky) Street in the early 1960s.[^cite_note-178] Other notable schools in Tel Aviv include [Shevah Mofet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shevah_Mofet), the second [Hebrew school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_school) in the city, Ironi Alef High School for Arts and [Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Israelite_Universelle).

## Economy



[Azrieli Sarona Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrieli_Sarona_Tower), the [third tallest building in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Israel)

Tel Aviv has been ranked as the twenty-fifth most important financial center in the world.[^cite_note-longfinance1-179] In 1926, the country's first shopping arcade, Passage Pensak, was built there.[^cite_note-180] By 1936, as tens of thousands of middle class [immigrants](351513.md) arrived from Europe, Tel Aviv was already the largest city in Palestine. A small port was built at the Yarkon estuary, and many cafes, clubs and cinemas opened. Herzl Street became a commercial thoroughfare at this time.[^cite_note-181]

Economic activities account for 17 percent of the GDP.[^cite_note-profile-69] In 2011, Tel Aviv had an unemployment rate of 4.4 percent.[^cite_note-182] The city has been described as a "flourishing technological center" by *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)* and a "miniature Los Angeles" by *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-183][^cite_note-Newsweek-184] In 1998, the city was described by Newsweek as one of the 10 most technologically influential cities in the world. Since then, high-tech industry in the Tel Aviv area has continued to develop.[^cite_note-Newsweek-184] The Tel Aviv metropolitan area (including [satellite cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_town) such as [Herzliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya) and [Petah Tikva](202411.md)) is Israel's center of high-tech, sometimes referred to as [Silicon Wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Wadi).[^cite_note-Newsweek-184][^cite_note-mercer-185]

In 2016, the [Globalization and World Cities Study Group and Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Study_Group_and_Network) (GaWC) at [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University) reissued an inventory of [world cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) based on their level of advanced producer services. Tel Aviv was ranked as an [alpha- world city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city).[^cite_note-lboro.ac.uk-186]

The [Kiryat Atidim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiryat_Atidim) [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech) zone opened in 1972 and the city has become a major world high tech hub. In December 2012, the city was ranked second on a list of top places to found a high tech [startup company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company), just behind [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley).[^cite_note-187] In 2013, Tel Aviv had more than 700 startup companies and research and development centers, and was ranked the second-most innovative city in the world, behind [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn) and ahead of [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-188]



Towers on [Rothschild Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Boulevard)

According to [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes), nine of its fifteen Israeli-born billionaires live in Israel; four live in Tel Aviv and its suburbs.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] The [cost of living](522934.md) in Israel is high, with Tel Aviv being its most expensive city to live in. In 2021, Tel Aviv became the world's most expensive city to live in, according to the [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md).[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-BBCcol-20]

Shopping malls in Tel Aviv include [Dizengoff Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Center), [Ramat Aviv Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Aviv_Mall) and [Azrieli Shopping Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrieli_Center) and markets such as Carmel Market, Ha'Tikva Market, and Bezalel Market.

Tel Aviv is home to the [Tel Aviv Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Stock_Exchange) (TASE), Israel's only [stock exchange](28168.md), which has reached record heights since the 1990s.[^cite_note-192] The Tel Aviv Stock exchange has also gained attention for its resilience and ability to recover from war and disasters. For example, the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange was higher on the last day of both the 2006 Lebanon war and the 2009 Operation in Gaza than on the first day of fighting.[^cite_note-193] Many international [venture-capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) firms, [scientific research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method) institutes and high-tech companies are headquartered in the city. Industries in Tel Aviv include chemical processing, textile plants and food manufacturers.[^cite_note-Economist-78]

Tel Aviv is ranked the 4th top global startup ecosystem hub according to the startup genome.[^cite_note-Wrobel-2024-18][^cite_note-Genome-19]

### Tourism and recreation

Tel Aviv receives about 2.5 million international visitors annually, the fifth-most-visited city in the Middle East & Africa.[^cite_note-Goldman-2012-22][^cite_note-Sapty-2011-23] In 2010, *[Knight Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Frank)*'s [world city survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#Rankings) ranked it 34th globally.[^cite_note-194] Tel Aviv has been named the third "hottest city for 2011" (behind only New York City and Tangier) by *[Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet)*, third-best in the Middle East and Africa by [*Travel + Leisure magazine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_+_Leisure) (behind only Cape Town and Jerusalem), and the ninth-best [beach city](293990.md) in the world by *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_\(magazine\))*.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196][^cite_note-Top_10_Beach_Cities-197] Tel Aviv is consistently ranked as one of the top [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) destinations in the world.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] The city has also been ranked as one of the top 10 oceanfront cities.[^cite_note-200]

Tel Aviv is known as "the city that never sleeps" and a "party capital" due to its thriving [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife), young atmosphere and famous [24-hour culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24/7).[^cite_note-Top_10_party_towns-201][^cite_note-lonelyplanet.com-202][^cite_note-203] Tel Aviv has branches of some of the world's leading hotels, including the [Crowne Plaza](1166288.md), [Sheraton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraton_Hotels_and_Resorts), [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Hotels), [Isrotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isrotel_Tower) and [Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Hotels). It is home to many museums, architectural and cultural sites, with city tours available in different languages.[^cite_note-204] Apart from bus tours, architectural tours, [Segway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segway_PT) tours, and walking tours are also popular.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] Tel Aviv has 44 hotels with more than 6,500 rooms.[^cite_note-TA_Stats-156]

The [beaches of Tel Aviv](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A8%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%AA%20%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A3%20%D7%AA%D7%9C%20%D7%90%D7%91%D7%99%D7%91-%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%95) and the city's [promenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Promenade) play a major role in the city's cultural and touristic scene, often ranked as some of the best beaches in the world.[^cite_note-Top_10_Beach_Cities-197] [Yarkon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkon_Park) is the most visited [urban park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park) in Israel, with 16 million visitors annually. Other parks within city limits include [Charles Clore Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Clore_Park), [Independence Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Park_\(Tel_Aviv\)), [Meir Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Park,_Tel_Aviv) and [Dubnow Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubnow_Garden). About 19% of the city land consists of green spaces.[^cite_note-208]

### Architecture

See also: [White City (Tel Aviv)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City_\(Tel_Aviv\)) and [List of tallest buildings in Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Tel_Aviv)



1930s [Bauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus) (left) and 1920s [Eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism) (right) architectural styles

Tel Aviv is home to different [architectural styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_style) that represent influential periods in its history. The early architecture of Tel Aviv consisted largely of European-style single-storey houses with red-tiled roofs.[^cite_note-arch-209] [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek), the first neighbourhood to be built outside of Jaffa, is characterised by two-storey sandstone buildings.[^cite_note-UNESCO-32] By the 1920s, a new [eclectic Orientalist style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism_in_art) came into vogue, combining European architecture with Eastern features such as arches, domes and ornamental tiles.[^cite_note-arch-209] [Pagoda House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagoda_House) (*Beit HaPagoda*), designed by Alexander Levy and built in 1924, is an example of this style.[^cite_note-TA-210] Municipal construction followed the "[garden city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_city_movement)" [master plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy) drawn up by [Patrick Geddes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Geddes). Two- and three-storey buildings were interspersed with boulevards and public parks.[^cite_note-arch-209] Various architectural styles, such as [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco), classical and modernist also exist in Tel Aviv.



[Bauhaus Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus_Museum_Tel_Aviv) displaying Bauhaus furnishings

Bauhaus architecture was introduced in the 1920s and 1930s by German Jewish architects who settled in Palestine after the rise of the Nazis. Tel Aviv's [White City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City_\(Tel_Aviv\)), around the city center, contains more than 5,000 Modernist-style buildings inspired by the [Bauhaus school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus_school) and [Le Corbusier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier).[^cite_note-UNESCO-32][^cite_note-times-33] Construction of these buildings, later declared protected landmarks and, collectively, a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md), continued until the 1950s in the area around [Rothschild Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Boulevard).[^cite_note-times-33][^cite_note-211] Some 3,000 buildings were created in this style between 1931 and 1939 alone.[^cite_note-arch-209] In the 1960s, this architectural style gave way to office towers and a chain of waterfront hotels and commercial skyscrapers.[^cite_note-Economist-78] Some of the city's Modernist buildings were neglected to the point of ruin. Before legislation to preserve this landmark architecture, many of the old buildings were demolished. Efforts are under way to refurbish Bauhaus buildings and restore them to their original condition.[^cite_note-212]



The [Azrieli Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrieli_Center) complex contains some of the tallest skyscrapers in Tel Aviv.

The [Shalom Meir Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalom_Meir_Tower), Israel's first skyscraper, was built in Tel Aviv in 1965 and remained the country's tallest building until 1999. At the time of its construction, the building rivaled Europe's tallest buildings in height, and was the tallest in the Middle East.

In the mid-1990s, the construction of skyscrapers began throughout the entire city, altering its skyline. Before that, Tel Aviv had had a generally low-rise skyline.[^cite_note-213] In 2010, the Tel Aviv Municipality's Planning and Construction Committee launched a new master plan for the city for 2025. It decided not to allow the construction of any additional skyscrapers in the city center, while at the same time greatly increasing the construction of skyscrapers in the east. The ban extends to an area between the coast and [Ibn Gabirol Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Gabirol_Street), and also between the [Yarkon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkon_River) and Eilat Street. It did not extend to towers already under construction or approved. One final proposed skyscraper project was approved, while dozens of others had to be scrapped. Any new buildings there will usually not be allowed to rise above six and a half stories. However, hotel towers along almost the entire beachfront will be allowed to rise up to 25 stories. According to the plan, large numbers of skyscrapers and high-rise buildings at least 18 stories tall would be built in the entire area between Ibn Gabirol Street and the eastern city limits, as part of the master plan's goal of doubling the city's office space to cement Tel Aviv as the business capital of Israel. Under the plan, "forests" of corporate skyscrapers will line both sides of the [Ayalon Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_20_\(Israel\)). Further south, skyscrapers rising up to 40 stories will be built along the old Ottoman railway between [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek) and [Florentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florentin,_Tel_Aviv), with the first such tower there being the [Neve Tzedek Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek_Tower). Along nearby Shlavim Street, passing between Jaffa and south Tel Aviv, office buildings up to 25 stories will line both sides of the street, which will be widened to accommodate traffic from the city's southern entrance to the center.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215]

### Arts and museums

See also: [Visual arts in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts_in_Israel)



[Tel Aviv Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Museum_of_Art), the Herta and Paul Amir Building

In the 1920s Tel-Aviv gradually became the center of art in Israel. In 1919, several prominent Olim from [Odessa](55545.md) arrived in the [Ruslan ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruslan_\(ship\)).[^cite_note-Museum_of_the_Jewish_People-216] In 1920 some of these set up the [HaTomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ha-Tomer) art cooperative as well as opened the first modern art exhibition in Israel.[^cite_note-Museum_of_the_Jewish_People-216][^cite_note-\:032-217] In the 1925 following the return of [Isaac Frenkel Frenel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Frenkel) from [Paris](22989.md) and his opening of the [Histadrut art studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histadrut_Art_Studio), and the introduction of [École de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Paris) influence; Tel Aviv grew to supplement [Jerusalem](16043.md) in its cultural importance in the visual arts; especially in respect to modern art.[^cite_note-Hecht_Museum-2013-218][^cite_note-\:1-219][^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] In the late 1920s to 1940s Tel Aviv painters were heavily influenced by the [École de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Paris), painting Tel Aviv's urban landscape, people and cafes in a manner influenced by [Soutine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha%C3%AFm_Soutine), [Pascin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Pascin), [Frenel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Frenkel), [Chagall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Chagall) and others from the [School of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Paris).[^cite_note-fr.timesofisrael.com-223][^cite_note-224][^cite_note-Hecht_Museum-2013-218] Tel Aviv's bohemian culture was characterized by cafes such as [Kassit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_Kassit) which attracted numerous writers and painters.[^cite_note-fr.timesofisrael.com-223][^cite_note-225] Numerous exhibitions were held in the [Ohel theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_Theater) and the [Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Hebrew_Gymnasium) prior to the opening of museums. [Reuben Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rubin) and [Nahum Gutman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachum_Gutman) also worked and painted in the city, painting in the naive style.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227] Tel Aviv hosts the [Tel Aviv museum of art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Museum_of_Art), established in 1932 in [Meir Dizengoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Dizengoff)'s house, since having moved to a new larger location in 1971, as well as numerous galleries.[^cite_note-228]

Israel has the highest number of museums per capita of any country, with three of the largest located in Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230] Among these are the [Eretz Israel Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretz_Israel_Museum), known for its collection of archaeology and history exhibits dealing with the [Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel), and the [Tel Aviv Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Museum_of_Art). In 2023, the Tel Aviv Museum of Art was ranked 48th on *[The Art Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_Newspaper)*'s list of the 100 most popular museums in the world. Housed on the campus of [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) is [ANU - Museum of the Jewish People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANU_-_Museum_of_the_Jewish_People), a museum of the international Jewish diaspora that tells the story of Jewish prosperity and persecution throughout the [centuries of exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora). Batey Haosef Museum specializes in [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) [military history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history). The [Palmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmach) Museum near Tel Aviv University offers a multimedia experience of the history of the Palmach. Right next to [Charles Clore Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Clore_Park) is a museum of the [Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun). The [Israel Trade Fairs & Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Trade_Fairs_&_Convention_Center), located in the northern part of the city, hosts more than 60 major events annually. Many offbeat museums and galleries operate in the southern areas, including the Tel Aviv Raw Art [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art) gallery.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232]

### Entertainment and performing arts



[Tel Aviv Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Performing_Arts_Center)

Tel Aviv is a major center of culture and entertainment.[^cite_note-233] Eighteen of Israel's 35 major centers for the performing arts are located in the city, including five of the country's nine large theatres, where 55% of all performances in the country and 75 percent of all attendance occurs.[^cite_note-profile-69][^cite_note-culture-234] The [Tel Aviv Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Performing_Arts_Center) is home of the [Israeli Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Opera), where [Plácido Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pl%C3%A1cido_Domingo) was house tenor between 1962 and 1965, and the [Cameri Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameri_Theater).[^cite_note-235] With 2,482 seats, the [Heichal HaTarbut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heichal_HaTarbut) is the city's largest theatre and home to the [Israel Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Philharmonic_Orchestra).[^cite_note-236]



[Heichal HaTarbut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heichal_HaTarbut) theatre, home to the [Israel Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Philharmonic_Orchestra)

[Habima Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habima_Theatre), Israel's national theatre, was closed down for renovations in early 2008, and reopened in November 2011 after major remodeling. Enav Cultural Center is one of the newer additions to the cultural scene.[^cite_note-culture-234] Other theatres in Tel Aviv are the Gesher Theatre and [Beit Lessin Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Lessin_Theater); Tzavta and [Tmuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tmuna) are smaller theatres that host [musical performances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance) and [fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_theatre) productions. In Jaffa, the Simta and Notzar theatres specialize in fringe as well. Tel Aviv is home to the [Batsheva Dance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batsheva_Dance_Company), a world-famous [contemporary dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_dance) troupe. The Israeli Ballet is also based in Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-culture-234] Tel Aviv's center for modern and classical dance is the [Suzanne Dellal Center for Dance and Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Dellal_Center_for_Dance_and_Theater) in [Neve Tzedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Tzedek).[^cite_note-iexplore-237]



[Suzanne Dellal Centre for Dance and Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Dellal_Centre_for_Dance_and_Theatre)

The city often hosts international musicians at venues such as [Yarkon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkon_Park), [Expo Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_Tel_Aviv), the Barby Club, the Zappa Club and Live Park Rishon Lezion just south of Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-Reuters-238][^cite_note-Haaretz-239][^cite_note-Ynet-240] The [Eurovision Song Contest 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019) was held at Expo Tel Aviv (the first Israeli-hosted Eurovision held outside of Jerusalem), following Israel's win the year prior.[^cite_note-241] Opera and classical music performances are held daily in Tel Aviv, with many of the world's leading [classical conductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conducting) and [soloists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_\(music\)) performing on Tel Aviv stages over the years.[^cite_note-culture-234]

The [Tel Aviv Cinematheque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Cinematheque) screens art movies, premieres of short and full-length Israeli films, and hosts a variety of film festivals, among them the Festival of Animation, Comics and Caricatures, "Icon" Science Fiction and Fantasy Festival, the Student Film Festival, the Jazz, Film and Videotape Festival and Salute to Israeli Cinema. The city has several [multiplex cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplex_\(movie_theater\)).[^cite_note-culture-234]

Tel Aviv is an international hub of highly active and diverse nightlife with bars, dance bars and [nightclubs](19321330.md) staying open well past midnight. The largest area for nightclubs is the Tel Aviv port, where the city's large, commercial clubs and bars draw big crowds of young clubbers from both Tel Aviv and neighboring cities. The South of Tel Aviv is known for the popular [Haoman 17 club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haoman_17), as well as for being the city's main hub of alternative clubbing, with underground venues including established clubs like the Block Club, Comfort 13 and Paradise Garage, as well as various warehouse and loft party venues. The Allenby/Rothschild area is another popular nightlife hub, featuring such clubs as the Pasaz, Radio EPGB and the Penguin. In 2013, [Absolut Vodka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolut_Vodka) introduced a specially designed bottle dedicated to Tel Aviv as part of its international cities series.[^cite_note-242]

### LGBT culture



[Tel Aviv Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Pride) is the largest annual [pride parade](58168.md) in the Middle East.

Named "the best gay city in the world" by [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines), Tel Aviv is one of the most popular destinations for [LGBT tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_tourism) internationally, with a large [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) community.[^cite_note-Was_Arafat_Gay?-243][^cite_note-Burden-244] Approximately 25% of Tel Aviv's population identify as [gay](5171128.md).[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246] American journalist [David Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kaufman_\(journalist\)) has described the city as a place "packed with the kind of 'we're here, we're queer', vibe more typically found in Sydney and San Francisco". The city hosts its well-known [pride parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Pride), the biggest in Asia, attracting over 200,000 people yearly.[^cite_note-247] In January 2008, Tel Aviv's municipality established the city's [LGBT Community center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Municipal_LGBT_Community_Center), providing all of the municipal and cultural services to the LGBT community under one roof. In December 2008, Tel Aviv began putting together a team of gay athletes for the [2009 World Outgames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Outgames) in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-zeitun-248] In addition, Tel Aviv hosts an annual LGBT [film festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_festival), known as [TLVFest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TLVFest).

Tel Aviv's LGBT community is the subject of [Eytan Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eytan_Fox)'s 2006 film *[The Bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bubble_\(2006_film\))*.

### Fashion

Tel Aviv has become an international center of fashion and design.[^cite_note-T+L1-249] It has been called the "next hot destination" for fashion. Israeli designers, such as swimwear company [Gottex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottex) show their collections at leading fashion shows, including New York's [Bryant Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryant_Park) fashion show. In 2011, Tel Aviv hosted its first [fashion week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_week) since the 1980s, with Italian designer [Roberto Cavalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Cavalli) as a guest of honor.[^cite_note-250]

### Media



[Reshet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reshet) studio building in Tel Aviv

The two major Israeli television networks, [Keshet Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshet_Media_Group) and [Reshet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reshet), are based in the city, as well as two of the most popular radio stations in Israel: [Galatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Army_Radio) and [Galgalatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galgalatz), which are both based in [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa). ### Cuisine

Tel Aviv is famous for its wide variety of world-class restaurants, offering traditional Israeli dishes as well as international fare.[^cite_note-253] More than 100 [sushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushi) restaurants, the third highest concentration in the world, do business in the city.[^cite_note-254] In Tel Aviv there are some dessert specialties, the most known is the [Halvah ice cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halvah_ice_cream) traditionally topped with date syrup and pistachios.

### Sports



[Bloomfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield_Stadium), the largest of Tel Aviv

The city has a number of football stadiums, the largest of which is [Bloomfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield_Stadium), which contains 29,400 seats used by [Hapoel Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_Tel_Aviv_F.C.), [Maccabi Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabi_Tel_Aviv_F.C.) and [Bnei Yehuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bnei_Yehuda_Tel_Aviv_F.C.). Another stadium in the city is the [Hatikva Neighborhood Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatikva_Neighborhood_Stadium). [Menora Mivtachim Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menora_Mivtachim_Arena) is a large multi-purpose sports [indoor arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_arena), The arena is home to the [Maccabi Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabi_Tel_Aviv_B.C.), and the [Drive in Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive_in_Arena), a multi-purpose hall that serves as the home ground of the [Hapoel Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_Tel_Aviv_B.C.). ## Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Tel_Aviv)

Tel Aviv is a major transportation hub, served by a comprehensive public transport network, with many major routes of the national transportation network running through the city. As of 2023, 56% of the residents are going to work without using cars and the plan is to expand it to 70% by the end of the decade.[^cite_note-262]

### Bus and taxi



[Tel Aviv central bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_central_bus_station)

As with the rest of Israel, bus transport is the most common form of public transport and is very widely used. The [Tel Aviv central bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_central_bus_station) is located in the southern part of the city. The main bus network in Tel Aviv metropolitan area operated by [Dan Bus Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bus_Company), [Metropoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropoline), and [Kavim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavim). the [Egged Bus Cooperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egged_\(company\)), Israels's largest bus company, provides intercity transportation.[^cite_note-263]

The city is also served by local and inter-city [share taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxis). Many local and inter-city bus routes also have sherut taxis that follow the same route and display the same route number in their window. Fares are standardised within the region and are comparable to or less expensive than bus fares. Unlike other forms of public transport, these taxis also operate on Fridays and Saturdays (the Jewish sabbath "Shabbat"). Private taxis are white with a yellow sign on top. Fares are standardised and metered, but may be negotiated ahead of time with the driver.[^cite_note-264]

### Rail

Main articles: [Tel Aviv Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail) and [Tel Aviv Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Metro)



[Tel Aviv Savidor Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Savidor_Central_railway_station)

The [Tel Aviv Savidor Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Savidor_Central_railway_station) is the main railway station of the city, and the second-busiest station in Israel. The city has five additional [railway stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Railways) along the Ayalon Highway: three of them, [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University_railway_station), [HaShalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_HaShalom_railway_station) (the busiest station in Israel, adjacent to [Azrieli Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrieli_Center)) and [HaHagana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_HaHagana_railway_station) (near the [Tel Aviv central bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_central_bus_station)), serve Tel Aviv directly, while the remaining two, [Holon Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holon_Junction_railway_station) and [Holon–Wolfson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holon%E2%80%93Wolfson_railway_station), are within Tel Aviv's municipal boundaries but serve the southern suburb of [Holon](231871.md). It is estimated that over a million passengers travel by rail to Tel Aviv monthly. The trains do not run on Saturday and the principal Jewish festivals (Rosh Hashana (2 days), Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Simkhat Torah, Pessach (Passover) first and fifth days and Shavuot (Pentecost)). [Jaffa railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_railway_station) was the first railway station in the Levant. It served as the terminus for the [Jaffa–Jerusalem railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa%E2%80%93Jerusalem_railway). The station opened in 1891 and closed in 1948. In 2005–2009, the station was restored and converted into an entertainment and leisure venue marketed as "HaTachana", Hebrew for "the station" (see homepage here:[^cite_note-265]). The Jaffa–Jerusalem railway also included the Tel Aviv Beit Hadar railway station, which was opened in 1920 and replaced in 1970, and the [Tel Aviv South railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_South_railway_station), which was opened in 1970 to replace Beit Hadar and itself closed in 1993. The [Bnei Brak railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bnei_Brak%E2%80%93Ramat_HaHayal_railway_station), while located in Bnei Brak's municipal borders, is closer to the Tel Aviv neighborhood of [Ramat HaHayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_HaHayal) than to Bnei Brak's city center and was originally called Tel Aviv North.



[Tel Aviv Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail)

[Tel Aviv Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail) is a [mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit) system for the [Tel Aviv metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_metropolitan_area). As of 2023, two LRT lines are under construction. Work on the [Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_\(Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail\)), the first in the project, started on September 21, 2011, following years of preparatory works,[^cite_note-266] and was expected to be completed and opened in late 2022 after numerous delays,[^cite_note-maariv920-267][^cite_note-268] and was finally opened on August 18, 2023, after the opening day was postponed numerous times.[^cite_note-269] Construction of the [Purple Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Line_\(Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail\)) started in December 2018;[^cite_note-globes-purple-270] work on the [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail\)) began in 2021 and is scheduled for completion in 2028.[^cite_note-271] Tel Aviv Metro is a proposed subway system for the [Tel Aviv Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Metropolitan_Area). It will augment the [Tel Aviv Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail) and [Israel Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Railways) suburban lines and 3 underground metro lines to form a rapid transit transportation solution for the city. Construction is expected to start in 2025, with the first public opening in 2032.[^cite_note-ynet-2021-update-272]

### Roads



[Begin Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begin_Road) as seen from [Azrieli Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrieli_Center)

The main highway leading to and within the city is the [Ayalon Highway (Highway 20)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_20_\(Israel\)), which runs in the eastern side of the city from north to south along the Ayalon River riverbed. Driving south on Ayalon gives access to [Highway 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_4_\(Israel\)) leading to [Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdod), [Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_1_\(Israel/Palestine\)), leading to [Ben Gurion International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_International_Airport) and [Jerusalem](16043.md) and [Highway 431](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_431_\(Israel\)) leading to Jerusalem, [Modiin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modiin), [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot) and the [Highway 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_6_\(Israel\)) Trans-Israel Highway. Driving north on Ayalon gives access to the [Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_2_\(Israel\)) coastal road leading to [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya), [Hadera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadera) and [Haifa](55606.md). Within the city, main routes include [Kaplan Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaplan_Street), [Allenby Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allenby_Street), [Ibn Gabirol Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Gabirol_Street), [Dizengoff Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Street), [Rothschild Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Boulevard), and in Jaffa the main route is Jerusalem Boulevard. Namir Road connects the city to [Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_2_\(Israel\)), Israel's main north–south highway, and Begin/Jabotinsky Road, which provides access from the east through Ramat Gan, [Bnei Brak](314671.md) and Petah Tikva. Tel Aviv, accommodating about 500,000 commuter cars daily, suffers from increasing congestion. In 2007, the Sadan Report recommended the introduction of a [congestion charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_pricing) similar to that of London in Tel Aviv as well as other Israeli cities. Under this plan, road users traveling into the city would pay a fixed fee.[^cite_note-273]

### Air



[Ben Gurion International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_International_Airport)

The main airport serving Greater Tel Aviv is [Ben Gurion International Airport](113149.md). Located in an [unincorporated area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) between the neighbouring cities of [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod) and [Or Yehuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or_Yehuda), it handled over 20 million passengers in 2017. Ben Gurion is the main hub of [El Al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Al), [Arkia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkia), [Israir Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israir) and [Sun d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_d'Or). The airport is 15 kilometres (9.3 mi; 8.1 nmi) southeast of Tel Aviv, on [Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_1_\(Israel\)) between Tel Aviv and Jerusalem. [Sde Dov Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sde_Dov_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): SDV), in northwestern Tel Aviv, is a domestic airport and was closed in 2019 in favor of real-estate development.[^cite_note-274] All services to Sde Dov will be transferred to Ben Gurion Airport.

### Cycling



[Tel-O-Fun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel-O-Fun) bicycle rental system

The Tel Aviv Municipality encourages the use of bicycles in the city. Plans called for expansion of the paths to 100 km (62.1 mi) by 2009.[^cite_note-275] By 2020, the city had 140 kilometres of bicycle paths with plans to reach 300 km by 2025.[^cite_note-276] The city is at the center of the [Ofnidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofnidan), a network of bicycle paths throughout the Gush Dan metropolitan area.

In April 2011, the Tel Aviv municipality launched [Tel-O-Fun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel-O-Fun), a [bicycle sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_sharing_system), in which 150 stations of bicycles for rent were installed within the city limits.[^cite_note-277]
