**Alois, Hereditary Prince of Liechtenstein, Count of Rietberg** ([né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9) **Alois Philipp Maria Liechtenstein**; born 11 June 1968) is the eldest son of [Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Adam_II,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein), and [Countess Marie Kinsky von Wchinitz und Tettau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie,_Princess_of_Liechtenstein), and the [heir apparent](3599687.md) to the throne of [Liechtenstein](17810.md). Alois has been [regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent) of the country since 15 August 2004, while his father remains the head of state.[^cite_note-vl-1]

He also holds the title of [Count of Rietberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Rietberg). He is married to [Duchess Sophie in Bavaria](1802647.md), who is a member of the [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach), and second in line for the [Jacobite succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_succession).[^cite_note-bio-2][^cite_note-times-3]

## Early life

Alois attended the [Liechtensteinisches Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtensteinisches_Gymnasium) in [Vaduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaduz) and then the [Royal Military Academy Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md). He was a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) serving in the [Coldstream Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldstream_Guards) in [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [London](17867.md) for six months, resigning his commission in the Coldstream Guards on 8 August 1988.[^cite_note-4] He entered the [University of Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Salzburg), from which he earned a [master's degree](170346.md) in [jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence) in 1993. Until 1996, Alois worked at a firm of chartered accountants in London. In May of that year, he returned to Vaduz and became active in managing the princely family's finances.[^cite_note-vl-1]

## Hereditary prince

In the [2003 Liechtenstein constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Liechtenstein_constitutional_referendum), Alois and his father Hans-Adam II pushed for constitutional amendments that granted the prince sweeping powers (the right to [veto](74661.md) laws and elect judges).[^cite_note-vl-1][^cite_note-wp-5] Hans-Adam had threatened to abdicate the throne and leave the country if it was not successful. However, it was accepted by voters.[^cite_note-wp-5] The next year, on 15 August 2004, Hans-Adam made Alois his regent and turned over the powers of prince to him, though Hans-Adam remains the official head of state, much like his father [Franz Joseph II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_II,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein) had done for him in 1984.[^cite_note-vl-1][^cite_note-6]

On 27 November 2005, Liechtenstein voters [rejected an initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_abortion_referendum,_2005) that would prohibit [abortion](765.md) and birth control in the country. The initiative was supported by [Roman Catholic](606848.md) Archbishop [Wolfgang Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Haas). Alois was initially sympathetic to the proposal, but he became neutral during the run-up to the vote. Instead, a government-sponsored counter-proposal was ratified.[^cite_note-7] In 2011, Alois announced he would veto any relaxing of the ban on abortion in Liechtenstein, which was to be the subject of a referendum later that year.[^cite_note-8] Such a veto was not necessary, however, as the voters rejected the proposal.[^cite_note-9]

Following the prince's threat, an initiative called "*_Damit deine Stimme zählt_*" ("So that your voice counts") was launched to change the [constitution of Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Liechtenstein) to prevent the prince from vetoing legislation approved in referendums. [The referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Liechtenstein_constitutional_referendum) was held on 1 July 2012, and 76% of voters upheld the prince's power to veto referendum results.[^cite_note-bbc-10]

On 11 August 2022, Prince Alois said that [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Liechtenstein) is "not a major problem".[^cite_note-11] In a speech made in August 2024, he supported Liechtenstein's accession to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md).[^cite_note-12]

## 2026 Princely House Law

On 15 August 2026, as part of his speech on [Liechtenstein National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_National_Day),[^cite_note-iz-13] Hereditary Prince Alois announced that the house law would be amended to move from [agnatic primogeniture](136535.md) to [absolute primogeniture](136535.md), meaning that in the future women will be eligible to inherit the throne.[^cite_note-vl-1][^cite_note-14] The changes were approved on 12 August by the required two-thirds of the princely family members with voting rights.[^cite_note-pt-15]

As per the new house rules, all female descendants of the princely house will be granted the same participation rights in matters governed by the house as male descendants.[^cite_note-iz-13] Alois specified that the new succession rules apply to future births in the princely family, and would not affect the current line of succession.[^cite_note-pt-15][^cite_note-16]

## Personal wealth

Alois is set to inherit an extensive art collection, much of which is displayed for the public at the [Gartenpalais Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gartenpalais_Liechtenstein) in [Vienna](55866.md). As of July 2022, his father's net worth was estimated by *[Bloomberg Billionaires Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index)* around US\$6.20 billion, making him the 380th richest person on earth.[^cite_note-HansA2_net-17] However, he placed these assets in a family foundation, the [Prince of Liechtenstein Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Liechtenstein_Foundation), from which each family member receives an equal annual allowance and which maintains the family's castles, cultural assets, collections and museums and last but not least, the costs of the princely court including even some salaries for princes who are ambassadors abroad, which are not a burden on the taxpayer.
