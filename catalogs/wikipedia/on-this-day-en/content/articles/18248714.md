# List of people indicted in the International Criminal Court

&#x20;The [International Criminal Court](14880.md) (ICC) has issued [indictments](14559.md) and [arrest warrants](146746.md) for individuals on any counts of [genocide](12441.md), [crimes against humanity](61099.md), [war crimes](48626.md), [aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_of_aggression), or [contempt of court](7201.md) pursuant to the [Rome Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court). An individual is indicted when a [Pre-Trial Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judges_of_the_International_Criminal_Court#Judicial_divisions) issues either an [arrest warrant](146746.md) or a [summons](175322.md) after it finds that "there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person has committed a crime within the jurisdiction of the Court".[^cite_note-Article_58-1] An arrest warrant is issued where it appears necessary "to ensure the person's appearance at trial, to ensure that the person does not obstruct or endanger the investigation or the court proceedings, or, where applicable, to prevent the person from continuing with the commission of that crime or a related crime which is within the jurisdiction of the Court and which arises out of the same circumstances".[^cite_note-Article_58-1] The Pre-Trial Chamber issues a summons if it is satisfied that a summons is sufficient to ensure the person's appearance.[^cite_note-Article_58-1]

## Overview

The ICC has publicly indicted 73 people. Proceedings against 38 are ongoing: 32 are at large as fugitives, two cases are in the pre-trial stage, one person is on trial, and three cases are in the appeals stage. Proceedings against 36 have been completed: three are serving sentences, seven have finished sentences, four have been acquitted, eight have had the charges against them dismissed, five have had the charges against them withdrawn, and nine have died before the conclusion of the proceedings against them.

The list below details the counts against each individual indicted in the Court and his or her current status. Respectively, the column titled **G** lists the number of counts (if any) of the [crime of genocide](12441.md) with which an individual has been charged; **H** list the number of counts of [crimes against humanity](61099.md); **W** the number of counts of [war crimes](48626.md); **A** the number of counts of the [crime of aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_of_aggression); and **C** lists the number of counts of [contempt of the court](7201.md) and other offenses against the administration of justice. Note that these are the counts with which an individual was indicted, not convicted. The column titled **Ind.** provides a link to an indictment, arrest warrant, summons, or other document listing the final charges against the individual before the beginning of the confirmation of charges hearing.

Shown in the table below under the column titled **S** are the Court's investigations into [17 situations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigations): (1) the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo); (2) [Uganda](32447500.md); (3) [Darfur, Sudan](33596946.md); (4) the Central African Republic I; (5) [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Kenya); (6) [Libya](60488397.md); (7) Côte d'Ivoire; (8) [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Mali); (9) the Central African Republic II; (10) [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Georgia); (11) Burundi; (12) [Bangladesh/Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Bangladesh/Myanmar); (13) [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Afghanistan); (14) [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Palestine); (15) the [Philippines](68742643.md); (16) [Venezuela I](62526848.md); (17) [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Ukraine); and (18) Lithuania/Belarus.[^cite_note-2]

## List of indictees

### Bahr Abu Garda

Main article: [Bahr Idriss Abu Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahr_Idriss_Abu_Garda)

Bahr Abu Garda was indicted on 7 May 2009 on three counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Darfur, Sudan. Abu Garda was alleged to have been a commander of a splinter group of the [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) (JEM), a rebel group that fought in the [Darfur conflict](5043637.md) against the Sudanese government. He was accused of leading JEM forces under his command (in conjunction with other rebel forces) in a [raid on the Haskanita base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskanita_raids) of the [African Union Mission in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_in_Sudan) (AMIS) on 29 September 2007, in which 12 AMIS peacekeepers were killed and eight were seriously injured; the base was also extensively damaged.[^cite_note-Abu_Garda_summons-44] Abu Garda was accused of being criminally responsible for murder, pillaging, and "intentionally directing attacks against personnel, installations, materials, units and vehicles involved in a peacekeeping mission".[^cite_note-Abu_Garda_summons-44] Abu Garda was summoned to appear before the Court on 18 May 2009 and the confirmation of charges hearing was held from 19 October 2009 to 30 October 2009. On 8 February 2010 Pre-Trial Chamber I ruled that the charges against him would not be confirmed.[^cite_note-Abu_Garda_confirmation_of_charges-45] On 23 April 2010 Pre-Trial Chamber I rejected the Prosecutor's application to appeal its decision, thus ending the proceedings in the case.[^cite_note-155]

### Mohammed Hussein Ali

Main article: [Mohammed Hussein Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Hussein_Ali)

Mohammed Ali was indicted on 8 March 2011 on five counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Republic of Kenya. Ali, who at the time was the Commissioner of the [Kenya Police](5610443.md), was alleged to have conspired with [Francis Muthaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Muthaura), an adviser of Kenyan [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_Kenya) [Mwai Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki), to order the police forces that he commanded not to intervene in stopping violence perpetrated by [Mungiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mungiki) forces loyal to President Kibaki during [post-election violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Kenyan_crisis) from 27 December 2007 to 29 February 2008.[^cite_note-Ali,_Muthaura,_Kenyatta_summons-57] Ali was alleged to be criminally responsible for murders, deportations, rapes and other forms of sexual violence, persecutions, and other inhumane acts perpetrated by Mungiki against civilians who were perceived to be loyal to the [Orange Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Democratic_Movement) (the political party of President Kibaki's rival) in the towns of [Kibera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibera), [Kisumu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu), [Naivasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naivasha), and [Nakuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakuru).[^cite_note-Ali,_Muthaura,_Kenyatta_summons-57] Ali was summoned to appear before the Court on 8 April 2011 and the confirmation of charges hearing was held from 21 September 2011 to 5 October 2011, in conjunction with the cases against Muthaura and [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta). On 23 January 2012, Pre-Trial Chamber II decided not to confirm the charges against Ali, thus ending the proceedings against him.[^cite_note-Ali,_Muthaura,_Kenyatta_confirmation_of_charges-58]

### Abdallah Banda

Main article: [Abdallah Banda](32321707.md)

Abdallah Banda was indicted on 27 August 2009 on three counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Darfur, Sudan. Banda is alleged to have been a commander of a splinter group of the [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) (JEM), a rebel group that fought in the [Darfur conflict](5043637.md) against the Sudanese government. He is accused of leading JEM forces under his command (in conjunction with other rebel forces) in a [raid on the Haskanita base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskanita_raids) of the [African Union Mission in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_in_Sudan) (AMIS) on 29 September 2007, in which 12 AMIS peacekeepers were killed and eight were seriously injured; the base was also extensively damaged.[^cite_note-Banda_summons-47] Banda is accused of ordering murders, pillaging, and "intentionally directing attacks against personnel, installations, materials, units and vehicles involved in a peacekeeping mission".[^cite_note-Banda_summons-47] Banda was summoned to appear before the Court on 17 June 2010 and the confirmation of charges hearing was held on 8 December 2010, in conjunction with the case against [Saleh Jerbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleh_Jerbo). On 7 March 2011 Pre-Trial Chamber I confirmed all the charges against him.[^cite_note-Banda/Jerbo_confirmation_of_charges-48] On 11 September 2014, Trial Chamber IV replaced the summons to appear with an arrest warrant and suspended the case until Banda appears in court.[^cite_note-Banda_arrest_warrant-49]

### Omar al-Bashir

Main article: [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md)



Sudanese President [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) and Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in November 2017

Omar al-Bashir was indicted on 4 March 2009 on five counts of crimes against humanity and two counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Darfur, Sudan.[^cite_note-al-Bashir_arrest_warrant_1-43] On 12 July 2010 he was additionally charged with three counts of genocide.[^cite_note-al-Bashir_arrest_warrant_2-41] During the [Darfur conflict](5043637.md) (specifically from April 2003 to 14 July 2008), al-Bashir, from his position as [President](450116.md) of Sudan, is accused of implementing a government policy that used the state apparatus (the military, police, security, and [Janjaweed](683270.md) forces) to attack [Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_people), [Masalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masalit_people), and [Zaghawa](1467480.md) populations that were perceived to be sympathetic to rebel groups. Al-Bashir is accused of ordering the rape, murder, extermination, forcible transfer, and torture of civilians, as well as the pillaging of numerous villages and camps.[^cite_note-al-Bashir_arrest_warrant_1-43] Additionally, he is accused of intending to partially destroy the Fur, Masalit, and Zaghawa ethnic groups by killings, "causing serious bodily or mental harm", and "deliberately inflicting conditions of life calculated to bring about physical destruction" of the ethnic groups.[^cite_note-al-Bashir_arrest_warrant_2-41] The Court has issued two arrest warrants for al-Bashir but he is currently living openly in Sudan, where he served as President until 11 April 2019. Sudanese state policy has been not to cooperate with the Court. Since the warrants have been issued, al-Bashir has traveled to several other countries and has not been arrested. Among the countries he traveled to include Chad, Djibouti, Kenya, Malawi, and South Africa which are [states parties to the Rome Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_parties_to_the_Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court), and were therefore obligated to arrest him.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] On 26 March 2013, Pre-Trial Chamber II made a finding that Chad had failed to cooperate with the Court and therefore referred the non-compliance to the Security Council.[^cite_note-160] On 5 September 2013, however, Pre-Trial Chamber II found that a similar visit to Nigeria did not constitute non-compliance, but it requested Nigeria "to immediately arrest Omar Al Bashir and surrender him to the Court should a similar situation arise in the future".[^cite_note-161]

### Jean-Pierre Bemba

Main article: [Jean-Pierre Bemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Bemba)



Jean-Pierre Bemba in December 2006

Jean-Pierre Bemba was indicted on 23 May 2008 on two counts of crimes against humanity and four counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in the Central African Republic (CAR).[^cite_note-162] On 10 June 2008, the arrest warrant was amended and the charges changed to three counts of crimes against humanity and five counts of war crimes.[^cite_note-Bemba_arrest_warrant-163] Bemba is alleged to have led the [Movement for the Liberation of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Congo) (MLC), a Congolese rebel group, into the CAR after Central African [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_the_Central_African_Republic_and_Central_African_Empire) [Ange-Félix Patassé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ange-F%C3%A9lix_Patass%C3%A9) sought Bemba's assistance in suppressing a rebellion led by [François Bozizé](213625.md). Bemba was accused of being criminally responsible for acts of rape, torture, "outrages upon personal dignity", murder, and pillage that occurred in the towns and cities of [Bangui](56617.md), [Bossangoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossangoa), [Bossembélé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossemb%C3%A9l%C3%A9), [Damara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damara,_Central_African_Republic), and [Mongoumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoumba) from 25 October 2002 to 15 March 2003.[^cite_note-Bemba_arrest_warrant-163] Bemba was arrested in Belgium on 24 May 2008, transferred to the Court's custody on 3 July 2008, and first brought before the Court the next day. The final charges filed by the Prosecutor were three counts of crimes against humanity and five counts of war crimes.[^cite_note-Bemba_document_containing_the_charges-40] The confirmation of charges hearing was held from 12 to 15 January 2009, and on 15 June 2009 Pre-Trial Chamber II partially confirmed the charges against Bemba, finding that he would stand trial for two counts of crimes against humanity and three counts of war crimes. Specifically, Pre-Trial Chamber II declined to confirm the charges of torture or outrages upon personal dignity.[^cite_note-164] The trial against Bemba began on 22 November 2010.[^cite_note-165] On 21 March 2016, Trial Chamber III delivered its judgment in which it found Bemba guilty of all five counts.[^cite_note-166] The Trial Chamber sentenced Bemba to 18 years' imprisonment on 21 June 2016.[^cite_note-Bemba_sentenced-167]

On 28 September 2016, Bemba served the ICC appeals chamber with an appeal against his 18-year conviction citing numerous procedural and legal errors in the judgment, and alleging a mistrial.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] This conviction was overturned on 8 June 2018[^cite_note-Bemba-overturned-170] by Judge [Christine Van den Wyngaert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Van_den_Wyngaert). She said he cannot be held responsible for the actions of his men, and that the lower court "ignored significant testimonial evidence that Bemba's ability to investigate and punish crimes in the CAR was limited".[^cite_note-BBC-171]

### Rodrigo Duterte



Rodrigo Duterte

Main article: [Arrest of Rodrigo Duterte](79430656.md)

[Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) was indicted on 7 March 2025 with 43 counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Philippines. Duterte was known for initiating a deadly [war on drugs in the Philippines](51032591.md) during his tenure as president. This involves the allegations of the [Davao Death Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Death_Squad) killing of drug dealers and thieves in [Davao City](489401.md) and the usage of law enforcement to kill suspected criminals nationwide.[^cite_note-172] Duterte was arrested at [Ninoy Aquino International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport) in Metro Manila on 11 March 2025 upon his arrival from [Hong Kong](13404.md), and extradited to [The Hague](30269.md) later that day.[^cite_note-173]

Duterte's arrest drew mixed reactions from Filipinos. [^cite_note-174] Diehard supporters expressed anger and staged protests.[^cite_note-175] Meanwhile, many other Filipinos, including human rights groups,[^cite_note-176] Filipino bishops,[^cite_note-177] and victims' families [^cite_note-178] expressed relief and celebrated the indictment and arrest, hopeful that the ICC would administer long-awaited justice.

### Charles Blé Goudé

Main article: [Charles Blé Goudé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bl%C3%A9_Goud%C3%A9)

Charles Blé Goudé was indicted on 21 December 2011 with four counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Republic of Côte d'Ivoire. As the leader of the [Congrès Panafricain des Jeunes et des Patriotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congr%C3%A8s_Panafricain_des_Jeunes_et_des_Patriotes), the youth organization that supported Ivorian [President](384272.md) [Laurent Gbagbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Gbagbo), Blé Goudé is alleged to have been "an indirect co-perpetrator" in Gbagbo's organized plan of systematic attacks against civilians in and around [Abidjan](21371767.md), including in the vicinity of the Golf Hotel, during [post-election violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Ivorian_Civil_War) that began on 28 November 2010. Fighters under the command of Gbagbo are alleged to have murdered, raped, persecuted, and inhumanly treated civilians who were perceived to be supporters of [Alassane Ouattara](1173624.md), Gbagbo's opponent in the [2010 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Ivorian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-Blé_Goudé_arrest_warrant-76] Blé Goudé was arrested on 17 January 2013 in Ghana, and extradited to Côte d'Ivoire the next day.[^cite_note-179] On 22 March 2014, the Ivorian government transferred Blé Goudé to the Court's custody.[^cite_note-180] He arrived at the Court's detention center in The Hague on 23 March.[^cite_note-181] The confirmation of charges hearing was held from 29 September to 2 October 2014, and on 11 December 2014, the Pre-Trial Chamber confirmed all the charges against Blé Goudé, thereby committing him to trial.[^cite_note-182] On 11 March 2015, the Trial Chamber joined the cases against Blé Goudé and Laurent Gbagbo.[^cite_note-Blé_Goudé_and_Laurent_Gbagbo_joinder-183] The trial in the case began on 28 January 2016.[^cite_note-Gbagbo/Blé_Goudé_trial_start-184]

Gbagbo and Goudé were acquitted on 15 January 2019 and their release was ordered.[^cite_note-Gbagbo-acquitted-185]

and [Tripoli](31232.md).[^cite_note-Muammar_Gaddafi_arrest_warrant-67] Gaddafi was [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Muammar_Gaddafi) in the Libyan city of ### Saif al-Islam Gaddafi

Saif al-Islam Gaddafi was indicted on 27 June 2011 on two counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in Libya. Although not an official member of the Libyan government, Saif al-Islam Gaddafi is alleged to have been the *de facto* prime minister and the "unspoken successor and the most influential person" to Muammar Gaddafi, the *de facto* head of state.[^cite_note-Saif_al-Islam_Gaddafi_arrest_warrant-69] Saif al-Islam Gaddafi, in conjunction with Muammar Gaddafi and his inner circle of advisers, allegedly planned a policy of violent oppression in response to the 2011 [Tunisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_revolution) and [Egyptian revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_revolution) that was implemented in the early weeks of the [Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2011\)). They allegedly murdered hundreds of civilians and committed "inhuman acts that severely deprived the civilian population of its fundamental rights" in the cities of [Ajdabiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajdabiya), [Bayda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayda,_Libya), [Benghazi](204114.md), [Derna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna,_Libya), [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata), [Tobruk](405871.md), and [Tripoli](31232.md).[^cite_note-Saif_al-Islam_Gaddafi_arrest_warrant-69] Saif al-Islam Gaddafi was detained by members of a revolutionary militia on 19 November 2011 near the Libyan town of [Ubari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubari) and taken to the city of [Zintan](26063692.md).[^cite_note-186] In 2012, Libyan authorities stated their intention to try Gaddafi in Libya.[^cite_note-187] However, on 10 December 2014 the Pre-Trial Chamber found Libya in non-compliance with several of its orders, including an order to transfer Gaddafi to its custody, and accordingly it referred Libya to the Security Council.[^cite_note-188] On 9 June 2017, the militia that had been holding Gaddafi since 2011 freed him from custody.[^cite_note-189] On 3 February 2026, Gaddafi was shot and [killed](82317452.md) by unidentified gunmen in [Zintan](26063692.md).[^cite_note-190]

Laurent Gbagbo in October 2007

### Ahmed Haroun

Ahmed Haroun was indicted on 27 April 2007 on 20 counts of crimes against humanity and 22 counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Darfur, Sudan. He is alleged to have coordinated the operations of Sudanese military, police, security, and [Janjaweed](683270.md) forces in the Darfur region while he was Minister of State for the Interior from April 2003 to September 2005 during the [Darfur conflict](5043637.md). These forces were allegedly aided and encouraged by Haroun to attack [Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_people) civilian populations, specifically those in the towns of [Arawala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawala), Bindisi, Kodoom, Mukjar, and the surrounding areas. He served as Sudan's Minister of State for Humanitarian Affairs until May 2009 when he was appointed Governor of [South Kordofan](767653.md).[^cite_note-200]

### Abdel Rahim Hussein

Abdel Rahim Hussein in July 2011

### Saleh Jerbo

Main article: [Saleh Jerbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleh_Jerbo)

Saleh Jerbo was indicted on 27 August 2009 on three counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Darfur, Sudan. Jerbo is alleged to have been a commander of a splinter group of the [Sudan Liberation Movement/Army](2031543.md) (SLM/A), a rebel group that fought in the [Darfur conflict](5043637.md) against the Sudanese government. He is accused of leading SLM/A forces under his command (in conjunction with other rebel forces) in a [raid on the Haskanita base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskanita_raids) of the [African Union Mission in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_in_Sudan) (AMIS) on 29 September 2007, in which 12 AMIS peacekeepers were killed and eight were seriously injured; the base was also extensively damaged.[^cite_note-Jerbo_summons-52] Jerbo is accused of ordering murders, pillaging, and "intentionally directing attacks against personnel, installations, materials, units and vehicles involved in a peacekeeping mission".[^cite_note-Jerbo_summons-52] Jerbo was summoned to appear before the Court on 17 June 2010 and the confirmation of charges hearing was held on 8 December 2010, in conjunction with the case against [Abdallah Banda](32321707.md). On 7 March 2011 Pre-Trial Chamber I confirmed all the charges against him.[^cite_note-Banda/Jerbo_confirmation_of_charges-48] On 22 April 2013 [Radio Dabanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Dabanga) reported that Jerbo had been killed on 19 April in a battle between the [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) and a splinter group in which Jerbo was a commander.[^cite_note-Jerbo_death-201] On 4 October 2013, the Court terminated the proceedings against Jerbo "without prejudice to resume such proceedings should information become available that he is alive".[^cite_note-Jerbo_termination_of_proceedings-53]

### Germain Katanga

Main article: [Germain Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germain_Katanga)



Germain Katanga in May 2016

Germain Katanga was indicted on 2 July 2007 on three counts of crimes against humanity and six counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC).[^cite_note-Katanga_arrest_warrant-29] On 26 June 2008, the charges were revised to four counts of crimes against humanity and nine counts of war crimes.[^cite_note-Katanga/Ngudjolo_Chui_charges-31] He was alleged to have been the leader of the [Front for Patriotic Resistance in Ituri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_for_Patriotic_Resistance_in_Ituri) (FRPI), an armed group composed mostly of members of the Ngiti ethnicity that was active during the [Ituri conflict](1585788.md). On and around 24 February 2003, he is alleged to have ordered his forces to [attack the village of Bogoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogoro_attack) in a military operation coordinated with the [Nationalist and Integrationist Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_and_Integrationist_Front) (FNI), an allied armed group composed mostly of members of the Lendu ethnicity. The target of the attack was alleged to have been both the village's predominantly [Hema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hema_people) civilian population and the base of the Hema armed group, the [Union of Congolese Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Congolese_Patriots) (UPC), located in the center of the village. Katanga is alleged to be responsible for the resulting crimes committed by FRPI and FNI fighters, including the intentional attack on the civilian population of Bogoro, the destruction and pillaging of Bogoro, the killing of at least 200 civilians, the use of child soldiers during the attack, rape, outrages upon personal dignity, "inhumane acts of intentionally inflicting serious injuries upon civilian residents", and "cruel treatment of civilian residents of, or persons present at Bogoro village \[...] by detaining them, menacing them with weapons, and imprisoning them in a room filled with corpses".[^cite_note-Katanga/Ngudjolo_Chui_charges-31]

Katanga was arrested by Congolese authorities on 1 March 2005 in connection with an attack that killed nine United Nations peacekeepers.[^cite_note-202] After the Court issued a warrant for his arrest, Katanga was transferred to the Court on 17 October 2007. His trial began on 24 November 2009. The Trial Chamber delivered the judgment in the case on 7 March 2014, finding Katanga guilty of four counts of war crimes and one count of crime against humanity.[^cite_note-203] On 23 May 2014, Katanga was sentenced to 12 years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-204] Although both the prosecution and the defense appealed the judgment and the sentence, both parties discontinued their appeals on 25 June 2014, thus ending the proceedings.[^cite_note-205] On 13 November 2015, a panel of judges from the Appeals Chamber reduced Katanga's sentence of 12 years' imprisonment by 3 years and 8 months.[^cite_note-Katanga_sentence_reduction-30] The Court transferred Katanga to the Democratic Republic of the Congo on 19 December 2015 to serve his sentence.[^cite_note-Lubanga_Dyilo/Katanga_sentence_enforcement-206] Katanga completed his sentence on 18 January 2016.[^cite_note-Katanga_sentence_reduction-30]

### Uhuru Kenyatta

Main article: [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta)



Uhuru Kenyatta in July 2015

Uhuru Kenyatta is the former [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_Kenya) of the Republic of Kenya. He was indicted on 8 March 2011 on five counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Republic of Kenya. Kenyatta, as a supporter of Kenyan [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_Kenya) [Mwai Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki), is alleged to have planned, financed, and coordinated the violence perpetrated against the perceived supporters of the [Orange Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Democratic_Movement), the political party of the President's rival, during [post-election violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Kenyan_crisis) from 27 December 2007 to 29 February 2008.[^cite_note-Ali,_Muthaura,_Kenyatta_summons-57] Kenyatta is alleged to have "had control over the [Mungiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mungiki) organization" and directed it to conduct murders, deportations, rapes and other forms of sexual violence, persecutions, and other inhumane acts against civilians in the towns of [Kibera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibera), [Kisumu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu), [Naivasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naivasha), and [Nakuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakuru).[^cite_note-Ali,_Muthaura,_Kenyatta_summons-57] Kenyatta was summoned to appear before the Court on 8 April 2011 and the confirmation of charges hearing was held from 21 September 2011 to 5 October 2011, in conjunction with the cases against [Mohammed Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Hussein_Ali) and [Francis Muthaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Muthaura). All the charges against Kenyatta were confirmed by Pre-Trial Chamber II on 23 January 2012.[^cite_note-Ali,_Muthaura,_Kenyatta_confirmation_of_charges-58] However, before the trial began, the Prosecutor announced on 3 December 2014 that she was withdrawing all of the charges.[^cite_note-207] The Trial Chamber terminated the proceedings against Mr. Kenyatta on 13 March 2015.[^cite_note-Kenyatta_withdrawal_of_charges-60]

### Tohami Khaled

Main article: [Tohami Khaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohami_Khaled)

Tohami Khaled was indicted on 13 April 2013 on four counts of crimes against humanity and three counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Libya. Khaled served as the head of the Internal Security Agency ("ISA") of Libya from the beginning of the [Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)) in February 2011 through August 2011. During that period, members of the ISA arrested persons who were perceived by the Libyan government to be opposed to the rule of the country's leader, [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md). Persons arrested by the ISA "were subjected to various forms of mistreatment, including severe beatings, electrocution, acts of sexual violence and rape, solitary confinement, deprivation of food and water, inhumane conditions of detention, mock executions, threats of killing and rape".[^cite_note-Khaled_arrest_warrant-84] The ISA conducted these activities throughout Libya, including in the cities of [Benghazi](204114.md), [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata), [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte), [Tajura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajura), [Tawergha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawergha), [Tripoli](31232.md), and [Zawiya](3105551.md).[^cite_note-Khaled_arrest_warrant-84] Khaled is accused of being responsible for crimes against humanity and war crimes both as a participant and as the commander of the ISA.[^cite_note-Khaled_arrest_warrant-84] Specifically, the Prosecutor alleges that Khaled is responsible for the crimes against humanity of imprisonment, torture, other inhumane acts, and persecution and the war crimes of torture, cruel treatment, and outrages upon personal dignity.[^cite_note-Khaled_arrest_warrant-84] Khaled was believed to have fled to Egypt during the defeat of Gaddafi's forces and is currently still at large.[^cite_note-Khaled_arrest_warrant-84]

### Joseph Kony

Joseph Kony was indicted on 8 July 2005 on 12 counts of crimes against humanity and 21 counts of war crimes with regard to the situation in Uganda. ### Henry Kosgey

Henry Kosgey was indicted on 8 March 2011 on four counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Republic of Kenya. Kosgey, as a top leader in the *ad hoc* Kalenjin organization, directed Kalenjin youths to target civilians of the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people), [Kamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamba_people), and [Kisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisii_people) ethnic groups, which were perceived to be supporters of the [Party of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_National_Unity_\(Kenya\)), the political party of Odinga's opponent during the election.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] Kosgey is alleged to be criminally responsible for the murder, deportation, torture, and persecution of civilians in the towns of [Kapsabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapsabet), [Nandi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_Hills,_Kenya), Turbo, and the greater [Eldoret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldoret) area.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] Kosgey first appeared before the Court, voluntarily, on 7 April 2011 and through the confirmation of charges hearing, which was held in conjunction with the cases against [William Ruto](2577967.md) and [Joshua Sang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Sang).[^cite_note-Kosgey_et_al._initial_appearance-210] On 23 January 2012, Pre-Trial Chamber II decided not to confirm the charges against Kosgey therefore ending his proceedings before the Court.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_confirmation_of_charges-209]

On 13 July 2012 he was additionally charged with three counts of crimes against humanity and four counts of war crimes. He is alleged to be a third highest-ranking official in the Patriotic Force for the Liberation of the Congo (FPLC), the armed wing of the [Union of Congolese Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Congolese_Patriots) (UCP), a rebel movement in the northeast part of the DRC, that fought in the [Ituri conflict](1585788.md). From July 2002 to December 2003, Ntaganda is alleged to have ordered FPLC troops to conscript and enlist children to the FPLC and UCP, and of using them "to participate actively in hostilities".[^cite_note-Ntaganda_arrest_warrant-20] He is also alleged to have ordered attacks on Lendu and other non-[Hema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hema_people) civilian populations in the town of Mongbwalu and the villages of Bambu, Kobu, Lipri, and Sayo, and the surrounding areas, from 1 September 2002 until 30 September 2003.[^cite_note-Ntaganda_arrest_warrant_2-231] In the course of the attacks, murders, rapes, and other forms of sexual violence were allegedly committed and homes were pillaged, resulting in the death of approximately 800 civilians and the displacement of 60,000.[^cite_note-Ntaganda_arrest_warrant_2-231] The finalized charges against Ntaganda were filed on 10 January 2014, and listed a total of 18 charges: five crimes against humanity and 13 war crimes.[^cite_note-Ntaganda_confirmation_of_charges-232]

The Congolese government refused to arrest him and in 2009 Ntaganda became a general in the armed forces in the city of [Goma](1210483.md) in [North Kivu](446958.md) province.[^cite_note-233] In April 2012 he and his troops defected from the military and left Goma to form the [March 23 Movement](35878650.md), an armed group that began attacking villages and towns around Goma.[^cite_note-234] Ntaganda was an alleged leader of the group and since his defection the Congolese government announced its intentions to arrest him.[^cite_note-235] On 18 March 2013, following reports of clashes between factions of the March 23 Movement, Ntaganda fled to Rwanda and entered the Embassy of the United States in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali). He requested that the United States facilitate his surrender the Court.[^cite_note-236] On 22 March, the ICC took custody of Ntaganda and transferred him to The Hague.[^cite_note-237] His first appearance before the Court took place on 26 March 2013.[^cite_note-238] The confirmation of charges hearing occurred from 10 to 14 February 2014 and on 9 June 2014 all the charges against Ntaganda were confirmed by the pre-trial chamber.[^cite_note-Ntaganda_confirmation_of_charges-232] The trial in the case began on 2 September 2015.[^cite_note-Ntaganda_trial_start-239] Ntaganda was found guilty on all counts of Crimes against humanity and War Crimes on 8 July 2019.

### Okot Odhiambo

Main article: [Okot Odhiambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okot_Odhiambo)

Okot Odhiambo was indicted on 8 July 2005 on three counts of crimes against humanity and seven counts of war crimes in regard to the situation in Uganda. He is alleged to be an integral member of the policy-making leadership of the [Lord's Resistance Army](17873743.md) (LRA), an armed group which has been waging a [guerrilla campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord's_Resistance_Army_insurgency) since 1987 against the Ugandan government. Additionally, he was also a military commander and sometime after 1 July 2002 (the date the Rome Statute entered into force) he allegedly issued "standing orders to attack and brutalise civilian populations".[^cite_note-Odhiambo_arrest_warrant-9] These attacks on civilian populations allegedly resulted in murder, pillaging of camps, enslavement, and the forcible conscription of children.[^cite_note-Odhiambo_arrest_warrant-9] In 2014, an LRA defector claimed that the Ugandan military wounded Odhiambo in October 2013 during an ambush in the [Central African Republic](5478.md) and that he later died from his injuries.[^cite_note-240] In February 2015, Ugandan officials announced that they had exhumed a grave believed to contain Odhiambo's remains and were conducting DNA testing on the remains.[^cite_note-241] Ugandan authorities confirmed that the remains were Odhiambo's and issued a death certificate stating that he died on 27 October 2013 near [Djemah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djemah), [Central African Republic](5478.md).[^cite_note-Odhiambo_termination_of_proceedings-8] Accordingly, the Court terminated proceedings against Odhiambo on 10 September 2015.[^cite_note-Odhiambo_termination_of_proceedings-8]

### Dominic Ongwen

Main article: [Dominic Ongwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Ongwen)

Dominic Ongwen was indicted on 8 July 2005 on three counts of crimes against humanity and four counts of war crimes in regard to the situation in Uganda.[^cite_note-Ongwen_arrest_warrant-10] He was allegedly a military commander and a member of the leadership of the [Lord's Resistance Army](17873743.md) (LRA), an armed group which has been waging a [guerrilla campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord's_Resistance_Army_insurgency) since 1987 against the Ugandan government.[^cite_note-Ongwen_charges-13] On 22 December 2015, the charges were expanded to 34 counts of crimes against humanity and 36 counts of war crimes.[^cite_note-Ongwen_charges-13] The Prosecutor alleges that from before July 2002 to at least December 2005 Ongwen commanded a brigade, and later a battalion, of LRA soldiers during "a widespread or systematic attack directed against the civilian population of northern Uganda."[^cite_note-Ongwen_charges-13] The campaign included attacks against four [internally displaced person](427408.md) camps: Pajule on 10 October 2003, Odek on 29 April 2004, Lukodi on 20 May 2004, and Abok on 8 June 2004.[^cite_note-Ongwen_charges-13] During such attacks, troops under Ongwen's command pillaged the camps and murdered, tortured, enslaved, and inflicted cruel treatment and inhumane acts on civilians.[^cite_note-Ongwen_charges-13] During the overall campaign directed against the civilian population, troops under Ongwen's command also persecuted the civilian population, forced woman into "marriages" and sexual slavery, committed rape, and conscripted and used child soldiers.[^cite_note-Ongwen_charges-13] Ongwen surrendered to United States military advisors assisting Ugandan forces on 6 January 2014 in the [Central African Republic](5478.md).[^cite_note-Ongwen_surrender-242] The Court took custody of Ongwen on 17 January 2015[^cite_note-243] and he was transferred to the Court's detention center on 21 January 2015.[^cite_note-244] He made his first appearance before the Court on 26 January 2015.[^cite_note-245] The confirmation of charges hearing took place from 21 to 27 January 2016 before Pre-Trial Chamber II, which confirmed all the charges on 23 March 2016.[^cite_note-246] The trial in the case began on 6 December 2016.[^cite_note-Ongwen_trial_start_date-247]

### Vincent Otti

Main article: [Vincent Otti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Otti)

Vincent Otti was indicted on 8 July 2005 on 11 counts of crimes against humanity and 21 counts of war crimes in regard to the situation in Uganda. He was allegedly a military commander and the second-in-command of the [Lord's Resistance Army](17873743.md) (LRA), an armed group which has been waging a [guerrilla campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord's_Resistance_Army_insurgency) since 1987 against the Ugandan government. Sometime after 1 July 2002 (the date the Rome Statute entered into force) he allegedly issued orders to attack civilian populations. These attacks allegedly resulted in sexual enslavement, rape, forcible conscription of children into the LRA, enslavement, cruel treatment, murders, pillaging of camps, and other inhumane acts.[^cite_note-Otti_arrest_warrant-15] In December 2007, [BBC News](1139893.md) reported that on 2 October 2007 Otti had been executed on orders from Joseph Kony, the commander-in-chief of the LRA.[^cite_note-Otti_death-14] Kony later confirmed that Otti was dead to [Riek Machar](2407248.md), a mediator between the Ugandan government and the LRA.[^cite_note-248] The ICC terminated all proceedings against Otti on November 17th, 2023 after an investigation confirmed his death.[^cite_note-249]

### William Ruto

Main article: [William Ruto](2577967.md)



William Ruto in July 2014

William Ruto is the current [President of Kenya](392265.md). He was indicted on 8 March 2011 on four counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Republic of Kenya. He is alleged to be the leader of an *ad hoc* organization created by members of the [Kalenjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalenjin_people) ethnic group which was created to perpetrate violence on behalf of the [Orange Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Democratic_Movement) (ODM), the political party of presidential candidate [Raila Odinga](2917410.md), during [post-election violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Kenyan_crisis) in December 2007 and January 2008.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] On 1 August 2011, the charges were reduced to three counts.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_confirmation_of_charges-209] Ruto, as the a top leader in the *ad hoc* Kalenjin organization, directed Kalenjin youths to target civilians of the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people), [Kamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamba_people), and [Kisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisii_people) ethnic groups, which were perceived to be supporters of the [Party of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_National_Unity_\(Kenya\)), the political party of Odinga's opponent during the election.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] Ruto is alleged to be criminally responsible for the murder, deportation, torture, and persecution of civilians in the towns of [Kapsabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapsabet), [Nandi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_Hills,_Kenya), Turbo, the greater [Eldoret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldoret) area.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] Ruto first appeared before the Court, voluntarily, on 7 April 2011 and through the confirmation of charges hearing, which was held in conjunction with the cases against [Henry Kosgey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kosgey) and [Joshua Sang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Sang).[^cite_note-Kosgey_et_al._initial_appearance-210] All the charges against Ruto were confirmed by Pre-Trial Chamber II on 23 January 2012[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_confirmation_of_charges-209] Ruto's trial began on 10 September 2013, in conjunction with the case against Joshua Sang, but the Trial Chamber vacated the charges against both accused on 5 April 2016, ending the proceedings in the case.[^cite_note-Ruto/Sang_dismissal-65]

### Joshua Sang

Main article: [Joshua Sang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Sang)

Joshua Sang was indicted on 8 March 2011 on four counts of crimes against humanity with regard to the situation in the Republic of Kenya. He is alleged to a top leader of an *ad hoc* organization created by members of the [Kalenjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalenjin_people) ethnic group which was created to perpetrate violence on behalf of the [Orange Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Democratic_Movement) (ODM), the political party of presidential candidate [Raila Odinga](2917410.md), during [post-election violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Kenyan_crisis) in December 2007 and January 2008.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] On 1 August 2011, the charges were reduced to three counts.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_confirmation_of_charges-209] As a broadcaster for the Kass FM radio station, Sang incited Kalenjin youths to target civilians of the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people), [Kamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamba_people), and [Kisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisii_people) ethnic groups, which were perceived to be supporters of the [Party of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_National_Unity_\(Kenya\)), the political party of Odinga's opponent during the election.[^cite_note-Kosgey,_Ruto,_Sang_summons-61] He is alleged to be indirectly responsible for the murder, deportation, torture, and persecution of civilians in the towns of [Kapsabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapsabet), [Nandi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_Hills,_Kenya), Turbo, the greater [Eldoret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldoret) Ruto, but the Trial Chamber vacated the charges against both accused on 5 April 2016, ending the proceedings in the case.[^cite_note-Ruto/Sang_dismissal-65]

### Abdullah Senussi

Main article: [Abdullah Senussi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Senussi)

Abdullah Senussi was indicted on 27 June 2011 on two counts of crimes against humanity in regard to the situation in Libya. As the head of military intelligence in Libya he is alleged to have planned, in conjunction with Muammar Gaddafi, the head of state, and his inner circle, formulated a plan in response to the 2011 [Tunisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_revolution) and [Egyptian revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_revolution) whereby Libyan state security forces under his authority were ordered to use all means necessarily to quell public protests against his regime. The plan was later implemented in the early weeks of the [2011 Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)).[^cite_note-Senussi_arrest_warrant-71] From 15 February 2011 until at least 28 February 2011 forces from government-organized militias, the national police, the [Libyan Armed Forces](35021184.md), the [Revolutionary Guard Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Guard_Corps_\(Libyan_paramilitary_unit\)), and other security services allegedly murdered hundreds of civilians and committed "inhuman acts that severely deprived the civilian population of its fundamental rights" in the cities of [Ajdabiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajdabiya), [Bayda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayda,_Libya), [Benghazi](204114.md), [Derna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna,_Libya), [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata), [Tobruk](405871.md), and [Tripoli](31232.md).[^cite_note-Senussi_arrest_warrant-71] Furthermore, Senussi commanded forces in and around Benghazi and "directly instructed the troops to attack civilians demonstrating in the city".[^cite_note-Senussi_arrest_warrant-71] Senussi was arrested on 17 March 2012 at [Nouakchott International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouakchott_International_Airport) in [Mauritania](18778516.md) after he arrived on a flight from [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Morocco](19291.md) with a fake [Malian](19127.md) passport.[^cite_note-250] On 5 September 2012 he was extradited to Libya.[^cite_note-251] On 11 October 2013, Pre-Trial Chamber I ruled that the case against Senussi was inadmissible before the ICC because of ongoing proceedings against him in Libya. On 24 July 2014, the Appeals Chamber confirmed the decision.[^cite_note-Senussi_referral-70]

### Mahmoud al-Werfalli

Main article: [Mahmoud al-Werfalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_al-Werfalli)

Mahmoud al-Werfalli was indicted on 15 August 2017 on one count of a war crime in regard to the situation in Libya. As a commander of [al-Saiqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Saiqa_\(Libya\)), an elite unit of the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md), he is alleged to have personally carried out or ordered the execution of 33 prisoners during the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War).[^cite_note-al-Werfalli_arrest_warrant-107] Following the [2011 Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)), in which rebels overthrew the regime of Muammar Gaddafi, the victorious armed forces splintered into various groups and began battling each other for control of Libya in 2014. [Protracted hostilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Benghazi_\(2014%E2%80%932017\)) took place in the city of [Benghazi](204114.md) from October 2014 through 2017 between the Libyan National Army (LNA) and the [Shura Council of Benghazi Revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shura_Council_of_Benghazi_Revolutionaries). During this battle, from 3 June 2016 to 17 July 2017, al-Werfalli is alleged to have been responsible for the war crime of murder for the execution of 33 prisoners.[^cite_note-al-Werfalli_arrest_warrant-107] The Court notes that seven incidents were recorded on video and circulated by al-Saiqa's social media accounts and depicted the "exceptionally cruel, dehumanising and degrading" killing of prisoners, who were shot at close range as they were either hooded, kneeling, or have their hands restrained.[^cite_note-al-Werfalli_arrest_warrant-107] On 17 August 2017, the LNA arrested al-Werfalli and said that it was conducting an investigation of him.[^cite_note-252] However, on 13 September 2017, the Office of the Prosecutor of the ICC stated that there were contradictory reports on whether he was arrested or not.[^cite_note-al-Werfalli_OTP_statement-253] He was shot dead on 24 March 2021 in Benghazi.[^cite_note-254]

### Osama Almasri Najim

On 19 January 2025, the [Libyan](17633.md) general Osama Almasri Najim, the head of Libya's judicial police, was arrested after the Juventus–Milan match near the [Juventus Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_Stadium), in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), Italy, and transported to the Vallette prison.[^cite_note-255] Almasri was indicted by the ICC for war crimes and crimes against humanity allegedly committed at the Mitiga Detention Centre, which Almasri oversaw from 2015 onwards.[^cite_note-Njeem_status-145] Subsequently, the [court of appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_appeal) of Rome ordered his release on January 21 due to the irregularity of the arrest, which resulted from the lack of approval from the Ministry of Justice. After his release, the Libyan military officer was expelled and repatriated to Libya aboard a [Falcon 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_900) aircraft of the Italian intelligence services.[^cite_note-256]

On 5 November 2025, Almasri was arrested by the Libyan government for torture and rights violations.[^cite_note-257]
