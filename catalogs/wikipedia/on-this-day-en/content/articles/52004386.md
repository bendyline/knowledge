**Achraf Hakimi Mouh**[^cite_note-5] (born 4 November 1998) is a professional [footballer](10669.md) who plays as a [right-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-back) for [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) and [captains](633462.md) the [Morocco national team](1024865.md). Known for his attacking prowess and strong defensive contribution, he is widely regarded as one of the best right-backs in the world, and is the African player with the most European titles.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-\:3-8][^cite_note-\:4-9]

At club level, Hakimi began playing for [Real Madrid Castilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_Castilla) in 2016 and was promoted to the [first team](26413.md) in 2017, where he won the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) and the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup). He was sent on a two-year loan deal to [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) side [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), winning the [DFL-Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFL-Supercup) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_DFL-Supercup). He then signed with [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) side [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan), helping the club win the [2020–21 Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Serie_A) title, their first league title in 11 years. Paris Saint-Germain then signed him in 2021 for a reported fee of €68 million.[^cite_note-\:5-10] With PSG, he won five [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) titles and two [Coupes de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France), and was integral to the club winning their first [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) titles back-to-back in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League) and [2026](74127997.md), with the former being part of a [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_treble).

Born in [Spain](26667.md), Hakimi was [capped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_\(sport\)) by Morocco at the [under-20](15604185.md) level, before making his senior international debut in 2016 at 17 years old. He was chosen in Morocco's squads for three [FIFA World Cups](11370.md) (in [2018](13327177.md), [2022](17742072.md), and [2026](36581929.md)), four [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) (in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and [2025](57061770.md)), and was one of three [overage players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_overage_players_in_Olympic_football) selected for the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament) team. At the 2022 World Cup, he and Morocco made history by becoming the first African nation to reach the semi-finals.[^cite_note-11] He later won with the team its second Africa Cup of Nations title in 2025.

Hakimi ranked 6th in the [2025 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ballon_d'Or), the highest position ever achieved by a Moroccan player.[^cite_note-12] He was also named the 2025 [African Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Player_of_the_Year), becoming the fifth Moroccan recipient of the award and the first since [Mustapha Hadji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustapha_Hadji) in 1998.[^cite_note-13]

## Early life

Hakimi was born on 4 November 1998, in Madrid, Spain, to Moroccan parents of [Arab](2185.md) descent.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] His father Mohamed, from [Oued Zem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oued_Zem), was a street vendor, and his mother Saida, from [Ksar el-Kebir](644641.md), worked as a housewife.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Hakimi grew up in [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe) belonging to the [Community of Madrid](69802.md), where his family faced economic challenges.[^cite_note-18] At the age of 8, Hakimi joined [CD Colonia Ofigevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Colonia_Ofigevi), a local football club, where he began his football journey. His talent was quickly noticed, and in 2006, he joined [Real Madrid's youth academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_Castilla).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] He spent several years developing his skills in the academy, initially playing as a [winger](548981.md) before transitioning to his more familiar role as a [right-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_back). Hakimi has stated that although he was raised in Spain, he grew up in a household shaped by Arab and Muslim culture, which later influenced his decision to represent Morocco at international level.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Club career

### Real Madrid



Hakimi after winning the [2017–18 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_UEFA_Champions_League) with [Real Madrid](26413.md)

Hakimi made his debut for Real Madrid in the first match of the [2016 International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_International_Champions_Cup), a 3–1 loss against [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.).[^cite_note-23] He subsequently returned to the B team, making his senior debut on 20 August 2016 by starting in a 3–2 [Segunda División B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B) home win against [Real Sociedad B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Sociedad_B).[^cite_note-24]

Hakimi scored his first senior goal on 25 September 2016, netting the equalizer in a 1–1 draw at [Fuenlabrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Fuenlabrada).[^cite_note-25]

On 19 August 2017, Hakimi was promoted to the main squad as a backup to [Dani Carvajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Carvajal) and [Nacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacho_\(footballer,_born_1990\)), and was assigned the number 19 jersey.[^cite_note-26] He made his first team – and [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) – debut on 1 October, starting in a 2–0 home win over [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCD_Espanyol).[^cite_note-27] He scored his first La Liga goal on 9 December 2017 in a 5–0 win against [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC).[^cite_note-28] On 12 May 2018, he scored his second goal against [Celta Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Celta_de_Vigo) in a 6–0 win.[^cite_note-29] In the [2017–18 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_UEFA_Champions_League), he made two appearances under the age of 18 as Madrid won the title, their third consecutive and 13th overall.[^cite_note-30] He thus became the first Moroccan to win the [European Cup/UEFA Champions League](44220.md) and the third Moroccan player to reach the final, after [Redouane Hajry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajry_Redouane) and [Mehdi Benatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Benatia).

#### Loan to Borussia Dortmund

On 11 July 2018, Hakimi signed for [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) on a two-year [loan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_\(association_football\)) deal.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] He scored his first goal for the club in a 7–0 victory over [1. FC Nürnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_N%C3%BCrnberg) on 27 September.[^cite_note-33] He provided three assists in a single match for the first time in his career against [Atlético Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid), in his first Champions League appearance for Dortmund.[^cite_note-34] Hakimi scored a brace against [Slavia Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Slavia_Prague) in the group stage of the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Champions_League) on 2 October 2019, his first goals in the competition.[^cite_note-35] On 5 November 2019, Hakimi scored another brace in the second half to turn a 0–2 deficit against [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan) to a 3–2 win at the [Westfalenstadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfalenstadion).[^cite_note-36]

In February 2020, Hakimi set a Bundesliga speed record when he was clocked at 36.48 km/h (22.67 mph) in a match against the [Union Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_Union_Berlin), beating the old league record which he had set against [RB Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RB_Leipzig) three months prior at 36.2 km/h (22.5 mph).[^cite_note-37] On 31 May, after scoring in the club's 6–1 away win over [SC Paderborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Paderborn_07), he removed his shirt to reveal a shirt with the message "Justice for [George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd)".[^cite_note-38] His teammate, [Jadon Sancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadon_Sancho), revealed a similar shirt after scoring as well.[^cite_note-39]

### Inter Milan

On 2 July 2020, Hakimi signed for [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) club Inter Milan on a five-year contract,[^cite_note-40] with a reported fee of around €40 million.[^cite_note-41] He made his debut on 26 September and provided an assist in a 4–3 win against [Fiorentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACF_Fiorentina) in the Serie A.[^cite_note-42] He scored his first goal for the club in the subsequent league game against [Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benevento_Calcio), which Inter won 5–2.[^cite_note-43]

### Paris Saint-Germain



Hakimi in a 2024 [Champions League](44220.md) match with [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md)

Hakimi signed for [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) (PSG) on 6 July 2021 on a five-year contract.[^cite_note-44] The transfer fee paid by PSG was reported by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* to be an initial €60 million, potentially rising by €11 million in add-ons.[^cite_note-45] Hakimi made his Ligue 1 debut on 7 August, playing the entire ninety minutes and scoring his first goal for the club against [Troyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ES_Troyes_AC).[^cite_note-46] He received his first red card in a 0–0 draw against [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) on 24 August.[^cite_note-47] On 22 September, Hakimi scored twice in a 2–1 victory against [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metz).[^cite_note-48] In his first season at PSG, he won a Ligue 1 title, his second league title in a row.[^cite_note-\:8-49]

On 14 February 2023, Hakimi was nominated for the 2022 [FIFA FIFPRO World 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFPro#FIFA_FIFPRO_Men's_World_11).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] On 19 September 2023, Hakimi scored a goal in a 2–0 victory against his former club Dortmund in the [2023–24 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Champions_League), thus earning a spot on the UEFA's Team of Week.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In October 2023, together with PSG players [Randal Kolo Muani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randal_Kolo_Muani), [Ousmane Dembélé](48621084.md) and [Layvin Kurzawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layvin_Kurzawa), Hakimi was handed a one-match suspension for participating in offensive chants following a victory against rivals Marseille.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] On 1 November 2023, Hakimi was nominated for the 2023 [African Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Footballer_of_the_Year) by [CAF](715265.md).[^cite_note-56] On 24 October 2024, Hakimi along with his international teammate [Soufiane Rahimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soufiane_Rahimi), were nominated for the 2024 [African Footballer of the Year award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Footballer_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

On 8 February 2025, Hakimi extended his contract with Paris Saint-Germain until 2029.[^cite_note-59] On 7 May 2025, Hakimi scored PSG's second goal in a 2–1 win over [Arsenal](2174.md) in the second leg of the [Champions League semi-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League), securing their place in the final for the second time in the competition's history.[^cite_note-60] On 24 May, he netted a goal in a 3–0 win over [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_Reims) in the [Coupe de France final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Coupe_de_France_final).[^cite_note-61] On 31 May, he opened the scoring in the [Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Champions_League_final), contributing to a dominant 5–0 victory over his former club Inter Milan, becoming PSG’s first Champions League final goalscorer.[^cite_note-62] He became the second Moroccan to play a [European Cup/Champions League](44220.md) final (after [Hajry Redouane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajry_Redouane) in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_European_Cup_final) with [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica)), the first Moroccan (and seventh African) to score in a [European Cup/Champions League](44220.md) final,[^cite_note-63] and the second Moroccan ever (after [Ayoub El Kaabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayoub_El_Kaabi) in the [2024 Europa Conference League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_Europa_Conference_League_final)) to score in an [UEFA club competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competitions) final.[^cite_note-64] Hakimi's performances across the season led to him emerging as a contender for the [2025 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ballon_d'Or).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

In the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), Hakimi won both the [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Ligue_1) title and the [UEFA Champions League](74127997.md) after a penalty shootout victory over [Arsenal](2174.md) in the [final](76987191.md).[^cite_note-68] The latter marked his third Champions League title, equaling [Samuel Eto'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Eto'o)'s record for the most titles won by an African player.[^cite_note-69] It was also the 19th major trophy of his career, surpassing the totals of both Eto'o and [Yaya Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaya_Tour%C3%A9) to become the most decorated African footballer in history.[^cite_note-70]

## International career



Hakimi (right) vs Portugal's [Bernardo Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Silva) (left) at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md)

After representing Morocco at [under-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_national_under-17_football_team) and [under-20](15604185.md) levels,[^cite_note-71] Hakimi made his debut for [the under-23s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_national_under-23_football_team) on 5 June 2016, in a 1–0 [friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_\(association_football\)) win over [Cameroon U23s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_under-23_football_team).[^cite_note-72] He made his full international debut on 11 October 2016, coming on as a [substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_\(association_football\)) for [Fouad Chafik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouad_Chafik) in a 4–0 win against [Canada](723688.md).[^cite_note-73] He scored his first international goal on 1 September 2017, netting the fourth in a 6–0 home routing of [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team).[^cite_note-firstintlgoal-74]

In May 2018, he was named in [Morocco](1024865.md)'s preliminary [squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_squads) for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) and on 4 June he was named in the final 23-man squad for the tournament.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

Hakimi was also called up for the [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in Cameroon.[^cite_note-77] He started all of his matches in the group stages.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] He scored from a free kick in a 2–2 draw against [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon_national_football_team).[^cite_note-80] He started in the round of 16 against [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team), scoring a free kick in the 70th minute to earn his team a 2–1 victory.[^cite_note-81]



Hakimi with Morocco in 2026

On 10 November 2022, Hakimi was named in Morocco's 26-man squad for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] He scored the winning penalty with a [panenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panenka_\(penalty_kick\)) style shot in a [shoot-out](511302.md) against [Spain](609825.md) (his birth country) in the round of 16, securing a place for his country in the quarter-finals and ultimately paving the way for his team to reach the semi-finals of the competition as the first African nation to do so in history.[^cite_note-84] Morocco was also the first Arab country to reach the semi-finals, and the second Muslim one after [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup).

On 28 December 2023, Hakimi was amongst the 27 players selected by coach [Walid Regragui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walid_Regragui) to represent Morocco in the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) in Ivory Coast.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] He scored a goal in a 1–1 group stage draw against [DR Congo](1030243.md).[^cite_note-87] In the round of 16, he missed a penalty in a 2–0 loss to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team).[^cite_note-88]

On 4 July 2024, Hakimi was included in the [Moroccan Olympic football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_national_under-23_football_team) for the [2024 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Olympics) in France as one of their three [overage players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_overage_players_in_Olympic_football) and was selected as captain for the team.[^cite_note-89] He recorded an assist and a goal in victories against [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_national_under-23_football_team) and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_under-23_soccer_team) respectively.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] He scored again in Morocco's 6–0 win over [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_national_under-23_football_team) in the bronze medal match.[^cite_note-\:7-92] In May 2025, Achraf Hakimi was the favorite for the RFI "Marc-Vivien Foé" prize, which he ended up winning.[^cite_note-93]



Hakimi with [Morocco](1024865.md) in 2025

In October 2025, Hakimi's Morocco broke the [world record for the longest winning streak in international football](66634086.md), surpassing [Spain](609825.md)'s previous mark of 15 consecutive victories. With a 1–0 win over [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team) in [Rabat](57400.md), they extended their unbeaten run to 16 in all competitions, including World Cup qualifiers and friendlies.[^cite_note-94]

Despite carrying an injury, he was named in Morocco's squad for the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md), which was hosted on home soil.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] On 4 January 2026, he provided the assist for the only goal in a 1–0 victory over [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_national_football_team) in the round of 16.[^cite_note-97] Hakimi made international news on 18 January 2026 when he was penalized by the [CAF](715265.md) with a two-match suspension for unsporting conduct during the final.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] Hakimi and teammate [Ismael Saibari](65163271.md) had blocked attempts to give Senegalese goalkeeper [Édouard Mendy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Mendy) a towel for his gloves, while also helping ball boys steal towels from behind the goal during the rainy [AFCON final](81129500.md) against [Senegal](1017436.md).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] One of the match sanctions was suspended for one year from date of the decision.[^cite_note-103]

On 26 May 2026, Hakimi was selected in the 26-man squad for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-104] He made his eleventh World Cup appearance in a 1–1 opening draw against [Brazil](149286.md), becoming the Moroccan player with the most appearances in the competition, breaking the tie with [Hakim Ziyech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakim_Ziyech).[^cite_note-105] He went on to make his twelfth appearance in a 1–0 victory over [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team), becoming the African player with the most appearances in World Cup history, surpassing the previous record held by [François Omam-Biyik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Omam-Biyik) and [Asamoah Gyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asamoah_Gyan).[^cite_note-106] On 24 June, he netted his first World Cup goal, provided an assist and was named Man of the Match in a 4–2 victory over [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti_national_football_team).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] On 29 June, he earned his 100th international cap for Morocco in their Round of 32 victory over the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), winning 3–2 on penalties after a 1–1 draw, becoming only the second Moroccan player to reach the milestone after [Noureddine Naybet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noureddine_Naybet).[^cite_note-109] On July 4, he set up the provided an assist in the World Cup match against Canada, that ended in a 3–0 victory over [Canada](723688.md), helping the team qualify for the quarter-finals for the second consecutive time a first for an African national team.[^cite_note-110]

## Player profile

Upon signing for Borussia Dortmund, Hakimi was described as a right-sided attacking [full-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-back_\(association_football\)) or wing-back who had previously trained as a [winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winger_\(association_football\)) and could also play as a defender. His playing style included speed, physical strength, tactical awareness, technical ability, and the capacity to play long passes from defense.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Some sources have referred to him as one of the best right-backs in the world.[^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-\:3-8][^cite_note-\:4-9]

## Personal life

Hakimi was married to Spanish actress of Tunisian descent [Hiba Abouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiba_Abouk) from 2020 until 2023. The couple have two sons, born in 2020 and 2022.[^cite_note-\:0-113][^cite_note-114] On 27 March 2023, Abouk released a statement on her [Instagram](31591547.md) account confirming that the couple had previously separated, and that they were awaiting divorce proceedings.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] It was reported that Abouk requested more than half of Hakimi's assets and fortune, although the assets are allegedly held in his mother's name.[^cite_note-117] The claim was later disproven as [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news).[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

Following Hakimi's performances at the 2022 FIFA World Cup in Qatar, a football stadium in Ksar el-Kebir was named after him.[^cite_note-121] He was named Arab Sportsman of the Year for 2022 at the Joy Awards in [Riyadh](51554.md).[^cite_note-122] On 12 October 2023, Hakimi, [Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba), [Mikel John Obi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_John_Obi) and [Sadio Mané](34758489.md) were selected as draw assistants for the final draw of the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

### Legal

On 3 March 2023, Hakimi was indicted by an investigating judge in Paris over an allegation of rape of a 24-year-old woman, and placed under [judicial supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_supervision_\(France\)).[^cite_note-125] He was banned from contacting his alleged victim but allowed to leave French territory.[^cite_note-126] Hakimi's lawyer, Fanny Colin, has stated that his client strongly denies these allegations.[^cite_note-127] On 19 June 2026, an appeals court in France announced that Hakimi would face charges for rape.[^cite_note-128] France's highest court in criminal matters rejected Hakimi's appeal on 23 September 2026, upholding the decision that the footballer will stand trial for rape.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]
