The **German Navy** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): ***Deutsche Marine***, pronounced [\[ˈdɔʏtʃə maˈʁiːnə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7e/De-Deutsche_Marine.ogg/De-Deutsche_Marine.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7e/De-Deutsche_Marine.ogg/De-Deutsche_Marine.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is part of the unified *[Bundeswehr](288188.md)* (Federal Defense), the German Armed Forces. The German Navy was originally known as the *Bundesmarine* (Federal Navy) from 1956 to 1995, when *Deutsche Marine* (German Navy) became the official name with respect to the 1990 incorporation of the East German *[Volksmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksmarine)* (People's Navy). It is deeply integrated into the [NATO](21133.md) alliance. Its primary mission is protection of Germany's territorial waters and maritime infrastructure as well as [sea lines of communication](1365004.md). Apart from this, the German Navy participates in peacekeeping operations, and renders humanitarian assistance and disaster relief. It also participates in anti-piracy operations.[^cite_note-2]

## History

Further information: [Anglo-German naval arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-German_naval_arms_race) and [Naval history of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_World_War_II)

German Naval history has its roots in the naval history of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), to which the [Dutch Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_the_Netherlands) and even the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy) once belonged. Proper German language early maritime history is represented by the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) and the [Brandenburg Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Navy), later [Prussian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Navy).

The modern German Navy traces its roots back to the *[Reichsflotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsflotte)* (Imperial Fleet) of the [revolutionary era of 1848–52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848). The *Reichsflotte* was the first German navy to sail under the [black-red-gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Germany) flag. Founded on 14 June 1848 by the orders of the democratically elected [Frankfurt Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament), the *Reichsflotte*'s brief existence ended with the failure of the revolution and it was disbanded on 2 April 1852; thus, the modern day navy celebrates its birthday on 14 June.

The small [Prussian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Navy) evolved in 1867 into the [North German Federal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_German_Federal_Navy), which was mainly for coast defence. It became the [Imperial German Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Navy) in 1871. From 1919 to 1935, only a small *[Reichsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsmarine)* was permitted. Under [Nazi Germany](21212.md), it was transformed into the *[Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)*, which was disbanded in 1945 after the [end of World War II in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Europe).



A sailor of the West German navy during the 1970s

Between May 1945 and 1956, the [German Mine Sweeping Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Mine_Sweeping_Administration) and its successor organizations, made up of former *Kriegsmarine* members, became something of a transition stage for the German navy, allowing the future *Marine* to draw on recently experienced personnel upon its formation. Also, from 1949 to 1952 the [United States Navy](20518076.md) maintained the [Naval Historical Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Historical_Team) in Bremerhaven. This group of former *Kriegsmarine* officers acting as historical and tactical consultants to the Americans, was significant in establishing a German element in the NATO senior naval staff. In 1956, with [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany)'s accession to NATO, the *Bundesmarine* (Federal Navy), as the navy was known colloquially, was formally established. In the same year the East German *Volkspolizei See* (literally People's Police Sea) became the *Volksmarine* (People's Navy). During the Cold War all of the German Navy's combat vessels were assigned to [NATO](21133.md)'s [Allied Forces Baltic Approaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Forces_Baltic_Approaches)'s naval command [NAVBALTAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Forces_Baltic_Approaches#Commander.2C_Allied_Naval_Forces_Baltic_Approaches).

With the accession of former [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany)'s states to the Federal Republic of Germany in 1990 the *[Volksmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksmarine)* along with the whole [National People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Army) became part of the *Bundeswehr*. Since 1995 the name *German Navy* is used in international context, while the official name since 1956 remains *Marine* without any additions. As of August 2024, the strength of the navy is 15,531 men and women.[^cite_note-bundeswehr-1]

A number of naval forces have operated in different periods. See

- *[Preußische Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preu%C3%9Fische_Marine)* (Prussian Navy), 1701–1867

- *[Reichsflotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsflotte)* (Fleet of the Realm), 1848–52

- [North German Federal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_German_Federal_Navy), 1867–71

- [Imperial German Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Navy) (*Kaiserliche Marine*), 1871–1919

- *[Reichsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsmarine)*, 1919–35

- *[Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)*, 1935–45

- [German Mine Sweeping Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Mine_Sweeping_Administration), 1945–48

- *[Volksmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksmarine)*, the navy of [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) (GDR) 1956–90

- *Marine*, 1956–present (*Bundesmarine*, colloquially)

## Current operations

German warships permanently participate in all four NATO Maritime Groups. The German Navy is also engaged in operations against international [terrorism](30636.md) such as [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) and NATO [Operation Active Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Active_Endeavour).

Presently the largest operation the German Navy is participating in is [UNIFIL](787766.md) off the coast of [Lebanon](17771.md). The German contribution to this operation is two frigates, four [fast attack craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Attack_Craft), and two auxiliary vessels. The naval component of UNIFIL has been under German command.[^cite_note-3]

The navy operates a number of development and testing installations as part of an inter-service and international network. Among these is the [Centre of Excellence for Operations in Confined and Shallow Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_of_Excellence_for_Operations_in_Confined_and_Shallow_Waters) (COE CSW), an affiliated centre of [Allied Command Transformation](2789125.md). The COE CSW was established in April 2007 and officially accredited by NATO on 26 May 2009.[^cite_note-4] It is co-located with the staff of the German Flotilla 1 in [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel) whose Commander is double-hatted as Director, COE CSW.

### Ships and submarines

Main article: [List of active German Navy ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_German_Navy_ships)



A *[Baden-Württemberg-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg-class_frigate)*[class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg-class_frigate) in [Wilhelmshaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmshaven) in April 2017

In total, there are about [65 commissioned ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_German_Navy_ships) in the German Navy, including; 11 [frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigates), 5 [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvettes), 2 [minesweepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minesweeper_\(ship\)), 10 [minehunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minehunter), 6 [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarines), 11 replenishment ships and 20 miscellaneous [auxiliary vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_ship). The displacement of the navy is 220,000 [tonnes](31185.md).

Ships of the German Navy include:

- 4 [*Baden-Württemberg*-class frigates F125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg-class_frigate)

- 3 [*Sachsen*-class frigates F124](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsen-class_frigate)

- 4 [*Brandenburg*-class frigates F123](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-class_frigate)

- 5 [*K130 Braunschweig* class corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig-class_corvette) (5 additional units in production, planned commissioning from 2025)

  [^cite_note-5]

- 6 [Type 212 submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_212_submarine) and additional 212 CD class submarines

  [^cite_note-6]

In addition, the German Navy and the [Royal Netherlands Navy](198478.md) are in cooperation in the "Ark Project". This agreement made the Ark Project responsible for the strategic sealift of German armed forces where the full-time charter of three [roll-on-roll-off](1457484.md) cargo and troop ships are ready for deployments. In addition, these ships are also kept available for the use of the other European NATO countries. The three vessels have a combined displacement of 60,000 tonnes.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Including these ships, the total ships' displacement available to the *Deutsche Marine* is 280,000 tonnes.

Procurement of [joint support ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_support_ship) (either two JSS800 for an amphibious group of 800 soldiers, or three smaller JSS400), was planned during the 1995–2010 period but the programme appears now to have been abandoned, not having been mentioned in two recent defence reviews. The larger ships would have been tasked for strategic troop transport and amphibious operations, and were to displace 27,000 to 30,000 tons for 800 soldiers.[^cite_note-9]

### Aircraft

Main article: [Marineflieger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marineflieger)

The [naval air arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aviation) of the German Navy is called the *Marinefliegerkommando*. The *Marinefliegerkommando* operates 56 aircraft, in May 2021 it was announced that the German Navy intended to replace the P-3C aircraft with [Boeing P-8 Poseidon](731169.md) MPA aircraft through a FMS agreement from 2025 onwards.[^cite_note-10]

## Future developments

### Planned investments

As of August 2025, Germany plans to invest €350 billion in new equipment through 2041. These investments come on top of the special fund of €100 billion. This includes the following estimated budgets:[^cite_note-13]

- German Navy:&#x20;

  - Naval vessels and other equipment: €36.6 billion

- [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army):&#x20;

  - Communications equipment: €15.9 billion

  - Vehicles and accessories: €20.8 billion

  - Combat vehicles: €52.5 billion

  - Munitions: €70.3 billion

  - Field and logistics material: €20.9 billion

- [German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force):&#x20;

  - Aircraft and missiles: €34.2 billion

  - Satellite communications: €13.3 billion

### Equipment on order

- Three Type 424 Electronic reconnaissance ships ordered in July 2023 to replace the Type 423 *[Oste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oste-class_fleet_service_ship)*. It will be delivered from 2029 to 2031. The cost of this project is €3.26 billion, and the financing comes from the regular defence budget.

  [^cite_note-14]

- The German government has announced the selection in January 2020 and contracting in June 2020 of [Damen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damen_Group) as the main contractor, together with partners [Blohm+Voss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blohm+Voss) and [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group), for supplying four Multi-Purpose Combat Ship [F126 frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F126_frigate) to the German Navy with an additional Order for 2 ships in 2024. The ships will be built at Blohm + Voss shipyard in Hamburg and at other shipyard locations of the North German Lürssen Group.

  [^cite_note-15]

  &#x20;The project was cancelled on 24 June 2026 and replaced with the procurement of eight [MEKO A-200 DEU-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEKO_A-200_DEU-class_frigate) (F128) instead.

  [^cite_note-16]

- Two further-developed [Type 212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_212) submarines with significant advancements (Known as [Type 212CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_212CD_submarine)) will be designed & procured with the [Royal Norwegian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Navy) in the next decade.

  [^cite_note-17]

  &#x20;The contract was signed in July 2021,

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;where according to the official statement the "NDMA and its German counterparts in the Bundesamt für Ausrüstung, Informationstechnik und Nutzung der Bundeswehr (BAAINBw) will acquire six new submarines – four Norwegian (in June 2024 increased to six) and two German – as well as [Naval Strike Missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Strike_Missile) for use on both German and Norwegian naval vessels." According to [ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ThyssenKrupp_Marine_Systems) the delivery of the two boats for the German Navy is scheduled for 2032 and 2034.

  [^cite_note-19]

- Five additional [*Braunschweig*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig-class_corvette) [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) are ordered and will be delivered 2020–2023.

  [^cite_note-20]

- [NH90 NFH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NH90_NFH) 'Sea Tiger' Helicopters ordered to replace [Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Lynx) in ASW/AsuW role, originally ordered by the German Army as NH90 TTH variant with deliveries planned from 2025 onwards. Up to 31 could be ordered.

- Integration of the German Navy Marines ([Seebataillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seebataillon)) in the [Netherlands Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Marine_Corps) and use of the Amphibious ships of the Royal Netherlands Navy such as the joint support ship [HNLMS *Karel Doorman* (A833)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Karel_Doorman_\(A833\)) as of 2016.
