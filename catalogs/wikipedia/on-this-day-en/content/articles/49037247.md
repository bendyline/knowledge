**Lev Markovich Shlosberg** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Лев Маркович Шлосберг; born 30 July 1963) is a Russian [opposition](38212364.md) politician, human-rights activist, journalist, historian,[^cite_note-1] [political prisoner](37307.md) according to [Memorial society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_society),[^cite_note-memopzk_shlosberg-2] former chairman of the [Pskov Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast) branch of [Yabloko](429579.md), and a member of the Yabloko Party's federal political committee.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Background

Shlosberg is of [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) descent.

## Career

He joined [Yabloko](429579.md) in 1994, becoming head of the Pskov branch of the party in 1996. From 2011 to 2015 he was a member of the [Pskov Oblast Assembly of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast_Assembly_of_Deputies). He is [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_secularism).[^cite_note-5]

In August 2014, the newspaper «Pskovskaya guberniya», of which Shlosberg was the director, published information about the funerals of several soldiers serving in the [76th Guards Air Assault Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Guards_Air_Assault_Division), who had reportedly died in the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas). Later that month, Shlosberg was assaulted, resulting in injuries to the head and a partial loss of memory. Yabloko party leader [Sergey Mitrokhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Mitrokhin) wrote that the attack was probably linked to Shlosberg's investigation on these unexplained deaths.[^cite_note-6]

In 2015, Shlosberg was deprived of authority by a court and by vote of fellow deputies. Shlosberg claims this was politically motivated.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] That year, he ran for the post of chairman of the democratic party [Yabloko](429579.md). He was defeated by [Emilia Slabunova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia_Slabunova) in the second round.[^cite_note-9]

On 4 July 2016 he announced he was running for [Parliament](1010187.md), in that year's [State Duma Election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Russian_legislative_election), but lost the election in his constituency, coming in fifth with around 6% of the vote.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Nonetheless, that same election, [Yabloko](429579.md) won one seat in the [Pskov Oblast Assembly of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast_Assembly_of_Deputies), and Shlosberg returned to the legislature.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In 2021, Shlosberg decided to run in the [2021 State Duma Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_legislative_election), again as a candidate of [Yabloko](429579.md). However this time he is running in the [Khovrino constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khovrino_constituency) in [Moscow](19004.md), instead of his home district of [Pskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov).[^cite_note-14]

He also ran in the simultaneous Pskov Oblast Assembly elections. On 3 August 2021 his registration as a candidate was declined, due to alleged links with banned [Anti-Corruption Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Corruption_Foundation). However, on the next day he was registered, due to the fact that the court decision to ban Anti-Corruption Foundation was not yet final. Regarding State Duma elections, however, on 9 August 2021 Moscow city court annulled his registration as a candidate, also due to alleged links with Anti-Corruption Foundation. Then he was also excluded from the federal Yabloko party list.[^cite_note-15]

In April 2022, following the increased repression after the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Russian police filed charges against him and his wife for having "discredited" the Russian military.[^cite_note-16]

In January 2025, Shlosberg was charged under the [Russian foreign agent law](39572380.md) and prohibited from leaving [Pskov Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast).[^cite_note-17] As of March 2025 Shlosberg has been indicted and trial is upcoming.[^cite_note-18]

On 10 June 2025 he was indicted on the ["discreditation of the Russian military charges"](70646883.md) (part 1 of the Article 280.3 of the [Russian Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_of_Russia)) and was placed under [house arrest](247643.md).[^cite_note-19]

On 3 December 2025 he was indicted for the third time under different war censorship statute - "spreading false information about the Russian army (part 2 of the Article 207.3 of the Russian Criminal Code) - for a [Telegram](41501305.md) post made in February 2022 (even though the actual war censorship laws were signed into law in March of that year). On 14 August 2026 the prosecution asked for a sentence of 12 years and 1 month on charges of "discrediting" the Russian military and [spreading "fake news"](70307905.md) about it.[^cite_note-20] Three days later he was sentenced to 11 years and 1 month in prison by the Pskov City Court.[^cite_note-21]
