**Félix Moloua** (born 1 June 1957[^cite_note-eiti-1]) is a [Central African](5478.md) politician and mathematician who has served as the [prime minister of the Central African Republic](955268.md) since 7 February 2022.[^cite_note-2] He was the previous minister of planning, economy and cooperation of the Central African Republic.[^cite_note-3] Moloua has a background as a [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician), [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and [demographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demography).

## Background, education and research

Moloua was born 1 June 1957 in [Bangui](56617.md), Central African Republic.[^cite_note-\:3-4] He received a teaching certificate in Mathematics and Physics from the [University of Bangui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bangui) in 1981,[^cite_note-\:0-5] continuing into a University Diploma in Scientific Studies in 1985.[^cite_note-\:1-6] From 1993 to 1995 he completed a [diplôme d'études supérieures spécialisées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipl%C3%B4me_d'%C3%A9tudes_sup%C3%A9rieures_sp%C3%A9cialis%C3%A9es) in Demography at the Institute of Training and Demographic Research of the [University of Yaoundé II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Yaound%C3%A9_II) in [Cameroon](5447.md).[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-7]

Moloua worked as a mathematics teacher at the [lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_France) Marie-Jeanne Caron in Bangui from 1981 to 1986.[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-\:4-8] Between 1986 and 2003, he taught at the [University of Bangui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bangui), serving as a professor of physics and statistics/demography.[^cite_note-\:1-6] He has authored three books on children and [adolescents](83859.md) in the CAR.[^cite_note-eiti-1][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Political career

Félix Moloua was an early activist of the [United Hearts Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Hearts_Movement) (MCU), a political party founded by president [Faustin-Archange Touadéra](15370659.md) in November 2018. Within the Executive Office of the MCU, he serves as a political adviser in charge of relations with other political parties and non-governmental organizations.

During the [2015-2016 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Central_African_general_election), Moloua was deputy national director of Touadéra's campaign. Following the election of Touadéra, on 11 April 2016, Moloua was appointed Minister of Economy, Planning and Cooperation under prime minister [Simplice Sarandji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplice_Sarandji).[^cite_note-11] After Touadéra's re-election in 2020, Moloua was made Minister of State of Economy, Planning, and International Cooperation on June 23, 2021, under prime minister [Henri-Marie Dondra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri-Marie_Dondra).[^cite_note-eiti-1][^cite_note-\:3-4][^cite_note-\:0-5]

On February 7, 2022, Touadéra removed Dondra from the role of prime minister, in part due to diplomatic tensions with [France](5843419.md), replacing him with Moloua.[^cite_note-\:2-12] After replacing the more pro-French Dondra, he was described by international media as a "[technocrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy)" and a "Touadéra loyalist"; an anonymous Central African diplomat stated that he had "no political profile".[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-13] On 10 February, Moloua formed his first government, re-appointing all 32 ministers who were in Dondra's cabinet, including himself, meaning he retained his position economic minister while serving as PM.[^cite_note-14]

Moloua was re-appointed as both prime minister and Minister of State in Economy, Planning, and International Cooperation by Touadera in 2024.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

Moloua was re-appointed again as prime minister in May 2026.

## Other work

Between 1987 and 1992, Moloua served as head of department and director of the National Center for Scientific and Technical Information at the Secretariat of State.[^cite_note-\:3-4] Moloua was the director of the [Bank of Central African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Central_African_States) (BEAC) from 2010 to 2013.[^cite_note-\:4-8] He is currently an [ex officio member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member) on the Board of Governors of the [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank) (AfDB).[^cite_note-17]
