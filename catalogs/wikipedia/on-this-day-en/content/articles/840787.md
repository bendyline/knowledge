The **president of Ecuador** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Presidente del Ecuador*), officially the **Constitutional President of the Republic of Ecuador** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Presidente Constitucional de la República del Ecuador*),[^cite_note-4] serves as the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md) of [Ecuador](9334.md). It is the highest political office in the country as the head of the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) of government. Per the [current constitution](18864296.md) adopted in 2008, the president can serve two four-year terms. Prior to that, the president could only serve one four-year term.&#x20;

The current president of Ecuador is [Daniel Noboa](73980059.md), who was first elected in [2023](70348458.md) and re-elected in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ecuadorian_general_election). Having been sworn in at age 35, Noboa is the second youngest president in Ecuadorian history, after [Juan José Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jose_Flores).[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

The presidency of Ecuador has been marked by periods of instability, causing the office to change hands frequently throughout the [history of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ecuador). At least five times, presidential duties have been assumed by a [provisional government](598010.md) or a [military junta](216815.md). Often, the office has been left to an interim or [acting president](352134.md), many of whom would go on to become president. The president who has had the most terms in office is [José María Velasco Ibarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Velasco_Ibarra), who served five.&#x20;

In May 2017 [Rafael Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa) became the first President in over two decades to complete his terms in office since [Sixto Durán Ballén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixto_Dur%C3%A1n_Ball%C3%A9n), who served from 1992 to 1996. Before Correa, a period of deep political instability from 1997 to 2007 also saw a [grave economic crisis in 1998–2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%931999_Ecuador_economic_crisis). During this time, Durán Ballén's three elected successors, [Abdalá Bucaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdal%C3%A1_Bucaram), [Jamil Mahuad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamil_Mahuad) and [Lucio Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucio_Guti%C3%A9rrez), were deposed in popular revolts, followed by military or legislative *[coups d'état](25914154.md)*, in 1997, [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Ecuadorian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat), and [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_Revolution_of_2005), respectively. Since Correa, [Lenín Moreno](8187373.md) had also completed a full, four-year term (2017–2021), despite a large [2019 popular revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ecuadorian_protests) that nearly toppled his government.&#x20;
