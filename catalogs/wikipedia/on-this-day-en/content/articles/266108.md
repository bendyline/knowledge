**Naruhito**[^cite_note-1] (born 23 February 1960) is [Emperor of Japan](10110.md). He acceded to the [Chrysanthemum Throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysanthemum_Throne) on 1 May 2019 following [the abdication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japanese_imperial_transition) of his father, [Akihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihito).[^cite_note-BBC_2019-05-01-2] He is the 126th monarch, according to [the traditional order of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_emperors_of_Japan). His reign is known as the [Reiwa era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiwa_era).

Naruhito is the elder son of Emperor Emeritus Akihito and [Empress Emerita Michiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Michiko). He was born during the reign of his paternal grandfather, [Hirohito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirohito). He became [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent) following his father's accession in 1989, and was formally invested as Crown Prince in 1991. He attended [Gakushūin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakush%C5%ABin) schools in Tokyo and later studied history at [Gakushuin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakushuin_University) and English at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford). He married the diplomat [Masako Owada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Masako) in 1993. They have one daughter: [Aiko, Princess Toshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiko,_Princess_Toshi).&#x20;

Continuing his grandfather's and father's boycott over the [enshrinement of convicted war criminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversies_surrounding_Yasukuni_Shrine), he has never visited [Yasukuni Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasukuni_Shrine).[^cite_note-3] He is an advocate in the fields of [water policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_politics) and [water conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conservation). He was an honorary president of the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) and [Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Paralympics) and is a supporter of the [World Organization of the Scout Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Organization_of_the_Scout_Movement).[^cite_note-IHA-Activities-4]

## Name

Before his accession, he was generally referred in the Japanese press by his princely title *Kōtaishi* (Crown Prince, 皇太子; lit. 'Great Imperial Son'). Upon succeeding to the throne he is referred to as "His Majesty the Emperor" (天皇陛下, *Tennō Heika*), which may be shortened to "His Majesty" (陛下, *Heika*).[^cite_note-\:0-5] In writing, the emperor is also referred to formally as "**The Reigning Emperor**" (今上天皇, *Kinjō Tennō*). The [era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_era_name) of his reign bears the name "[Reiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiwa)" (令和) pronounced [\[ɾeːwa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a9/Ja-reiwa.ogg/Ja-reiwa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a9/Ja-reiwa.ogg/Ja-reiwa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, and according to custom he will be referred to as **Emperor Reiwa** (令和天皇, *Reiwa Tennō*; see "[posthumous name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumous_name)") by order of the [Cabinet](573784.md) after his death.

The name of the next era under his successor will be established after his death or before his [abdication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication).[^cite_note-\:1-6]

## Early life



Naruhito in February 1961

Naruhito was born on 23 February 1960 at 4\:15 p.m. in the Imperial Household Agency Hospital in [Tokyo Imperial Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Imperial_Palace).[^cite_note-\:2-7] As a prince, he later quipped, "I was born in a barn inside the moat".[^cite_note-8] His parents, [Emperor Akihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihito) and [Empress Michiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Michiko), were then crown prince and crown princess of Japan, while his paternal grandfather, Hirohito, reigned as emperor.[^cite_note-9]

His childhood was reported to be happy, and he enjoyed activities such as [mountain climbing](20341.md), [horseback riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseback_riding), and learning the violin. He played with the children of the royal [chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamberlain_\(office\)), and he was a fan of the [Yomiuri Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Giants) in the [Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League), his favorite player being No. 3, later team manager, [Shigeo Nagashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Nagashima). One day, Naruhito found the remains of an ancient [roadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadway) on the palace grounds, sparking a lifelong fascination with the history of [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation), which would provide the subject of his bachelor's and master's degrees in history.[^cite_note-10] He later said, "I have had a keen interest in roads since childhood. On roads, you can go to the unknown world. Since I have been leading a life where I have few chances to go out freely, roads are a precious bridge to the unknown world, so to speak."[^cite_note-11]

In August 1974, when the prince was 14, he was sent to [Melbourne](17306237.md), Australia, for a homestay. His father, then the crown prince, had a positive experience there on a trip the year before and encouraged his son to go as well.[^cite_note-12] He stayed with the family of a businessman named Colin Harper.[^cite_note-13] He got along with his host brothers, riding around [Point Lonsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Lonsdale), playing the violin and tennis, and climbing [Uluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru) together.[^cite_note-14] Once, he played the violin for [dignitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignitaries) at a state dinner at [Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House,_Canberra) hosted by Governor-General [Sir John Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerr_\(governor-general\)).[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._60-15]

## Education



Prince Naruhito, aged 9, with his parents and siblings, 1969

When the Prince was four years old he was enrolled in the prestigious [Gakushūin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakush%C5%ABin) school system, where many of Japan's elite families and *narikin* ([nouveaux riches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouveau_riche)) send their children.[^cite_note-16] In senior high, Naruhito joined the geography club.[^cite_note-17]

He graduated from [Gakushuin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakushuin_University) in March 1982 with a [Bachelor of Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Letters) degree in history.[^cite_note-18] In July 1983, he undertook a three-month intensive English course before entering [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College) of the [University of Oxford](31797.md) in the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-19] where he studied until 1986. He did not, however, submit his thesis *A Study of Navigation and Traffic on the Upper Thames in the 18th Century* until 1989.[^cite_note-20] He later revisited these years in his book, *[The Thames and I – a Memoir of Two Years at Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thames_and_I_%E2%80%93_a_Memoir_of_Two_Years_at_Oxford).* He visited some 21 historic pubs, including [The Trout Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trout_Inn,_Lower_Wolvercote).[^cite_note-21] He joined the Japan Society and the drama society, and became the honorary president of the [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) and [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) clubs.[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._150-22] He played inter-college tennis, seeded number three out of six on the Merton team,[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._150-22] and took golf lessons from a pro.[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._150-22] In his three years at Merton he also climbed the highest peaks in three of the constituent countries of the United Kingdom: Scotland's [Ben Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Nevis), Wales's [Snowdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon) and [Scafell Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scafell_Pike) in England.[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._151-23]

While at Oxford, he also was able to go sightseeing across Europe and meet much of its royalty, including the [British royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family).[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._151-23] The relatively relaxed manners of the United Kingdom's royals amazed him: "Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II), he noted with surprise, poured her own tea and served the sandwiches."[^cite_note-24] He also went skiing with [Liechtenstein](17810.md)'s [Prince Hans-Adam II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Adam_II,_Prince_of_Liechtenstein), holidayed in [Mallorca](59310.md) in the [Mediterranean](19006.md) with Spain's King [Juan Carlos I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_I), and sailed with Norway's [Crown Prince Harald](66069.md) and [Crown Princess Sonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Sonja_of_Norway) and Queen [Beatrix of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrix_of_the_Netherlands).[^cite_note-25]

Upon his return to Japan, he enrolled once more in Gakushūin University to earn a [Master of Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Humanities) degree in history, successfully earning his degree in 1988.[^cite_note-kunaicho-26]

## Personal life

### Marriage and family



The newly married crown prince and crown princess in traditional court dress, with the prince wearing a *[sokutai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokutai)*, the princess a *[jūnihitoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%ABnihitoe)* (1993)



A 500-yen coin issued to commemorate the Imperial Wedding

Naruhito first met Masako Owada, a staff member working at the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](868429.md), at a tea gathering for [Infanta Elena of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanta_Elena,_Duchess_of_Lugo) in November 1986,[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._150-22] during her studies at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). The prince was immediately captivated by her,[^cite_note-28] and arranged for them to meet several times over the next few weeks.[^cite_note-29] Because of this, they were pursued relentlessly by the press throughout 1987.[^cite_note-30]

Despite the [Imperial Household Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Household_Agency)'s disapproval of her, and her attending [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford), for the next two years, he remained interested in her. He proposed to her three times before the Imperial Palace announced their engagement on 19 January 1993. The wedding took place on 9 June the same year at the Imperial Shinto Hall in Tokyo before 800 invited guests, including many of Europe's heads of state and royalty.[^cite_note-31]

By the time of their marriage, his father had ascended the throne, so the prince had been invested as the crown prince with the title **Prince Hiro** (浩宮, *Hiro-no-miya*) on 23 February 1991.[^cite_note-kunaicho-26]

Her first pregnancy was announced in December 1999, but she [miscarried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage).[^cite_note-32] They finally had one daughter, [Aiko, Princess Toshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiko,_Princess_Toshi) (敬宮愛子内親王, *Toshi-no-miya Aiko Naishinnō*), born 1 December 2001 at the Imperial Household Agency Hospital at Tokyo Imperial Palace.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Hobbies and interests

He is interested in water policy and water conservation. In March 2003, in his capacity as honorary president of the Third [World Water Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Water_Forum), he delivered a speech at the forum's opening ceremony titled "Waterways Connecting [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) and Local Regions". Visiting Mexico in March 2006, he gave the keynote address at the opening ceremony for the Fourth World Water Forum, "[Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo) and Water Transport". And in December 2007, he gave a commemorative talk at the opening ceremony for the First Asia-Pacific Water Summit, "Humans and Water: From Japan to the Asia-Pacific Region".[^cite_note-kunaicho-26]

He plays the viola, having switched from the violin because he thought the latter "too much of a leader, too prominent" to suit his musical and personal tastes.[^cite_note-35] He enjoys jogging, hiking, and mountaineering in his spare time.[^cite_note-Hills,_Ben_p._60-15]

Naruhito at his Ceremony for Proclamation of Crown Prince (*Rikkōshi-Senmei-no-gi*) in 1991

As crown prince, he was a patron of the [1998 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Winter_Olympics) and [1998 Winter Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Winter_Paralympics). He is also a supporter of the [World Organization of the Scout Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Organization_of_the_Scout_Movement) and in 2006 attended the 14th [Nippon Jamboree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Jamboree), the Japanese national jamboree organized by the [Scout Association of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_Association_of_Japan). The crown prince had also been an honorary vice-president of the [Japanese Red Cross Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Red_Cross) since 1994.[^cite_note-kunaicho-26] In 2001, the crown prince visited the United Kingdom; he met Queen Elizabeth II and [Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh) at [Windsor Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsor_Castle).[^cite_note-36]

For two weeks in 2012, the crown prince temporarily took charge of [his father's duties](10110.md#Role) while Emperor Akihito underwent and recovered from heart bypass surgery.[^cite_note-37]

## Reign

Further information: [2019 Japanese imperial transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japanese_imperial_transition) and [Reiwa era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiwa_era)



The Emperor and Empress with their daughter, Princess Aiko, in [Nasu, Tochigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasu,_Tochigi), 2019

On 1 December 2017, Prime Minister [Shinzo Abe](2634434.md) announced that Naruhito's father, Emperor Akihito, would [abdicate on 30 April 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japanese_imperial_transition), and that Naruhito would become the 126th emperor of Japan as of 1 May 2019.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-Japan_Times_2017-12-01-39] Following an abdication ceremony on the afternoon of 30 April, Akihito's reign and the [Heisei era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisei_era) continued until the end of the day. Naruhito then succeeded him as emperor at the beginning of the day on 1 May, ushering in the [Reiwa era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiwa_era). The transition took place at midnight, and Naruhito formally began his reign in a ceremony later that morning. In his first statement as emperor, he pledged to reflect deeply on the course followed by his father, and fulfill his constitutional responsibility "as the symbol of the state and of the unity of the people of Japan".[^cite_note-BBC_2019-05-01-2]

Under Article 4 of the [Japanese constitution](62699.md), the emperor's role is defined as entirely ceremonial and representative. Unlike most other constitutional monarchs, the emperor lacks even nominal powers related to government;[^cite_note-40] he is barred from making political statements.[^cite_note-BBC_2019-05-01-2] His role is limited to performing ceremonial duties as delineated by the constitution, and even then he is constrained by the requirements of the constitution and the binding advice of the cabinet. For instance, while he formally appoints the Prime Minister, he is constitutionally required to appoint the person designated by the [National Diet](163847.md).[^cite_note-41]

The [enthronement ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enthronement_of_the_Japanese_emperor) took place on 22 October 2019,[^cite_note-42] where he was duly enthroned in an ancient-style proclamation ceremony. On 23 July 2021, the new emperor opened the 2020 Summer Olympics (originally scheduled to be played in 2020, postponed by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Japan)) hosted in Tokyo, just as his grandfather, Emperor Shōwa, had done in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics).



Emperor Naruhito (left) poses for a photo while visiting the [Borobudur Temple Compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borobudur_Temple_Compounds) in [Magelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magelang), Indonesia, June 2023

The imperial couple's first trip abroad as emperor and empress took place in September 2022, to the United Kingdom to attend the [state funeral of Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] They visited Indonesia in June 2023, their first state visit.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In February 2024, the emperor marked his 64th birthday with a message mourning the victims of the [Noto earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Noto_earthquake), and expressed desire to visit the affected areas.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] He had previously received condolences for the victims from King Charles III of the United Kingdom in early January.[^cite_note-49] The emperor and empress visited [Wajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wajima,_Ishikawa) and [Suzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzu,_Ishikawa), two earthquake-stricken cities in the [Noto Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noto_Peninsula), on 22 March. The couple later visited an evacuation center in [Anamizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamizu,_Ishikawa) on 12 April.[^cite_note-50]

In April 2024, the Imperial Household Agency launched an [Instagram](31591547.md) account for the imperial family, which received 300,000 followers by the end of its debut on the platform. The account was reportedly launched to "reach out" to Japan's younger generations.[^cite_note-51]

The Emperor and Empress embarked on a three-day state visit to the United Kingdom in late June 2024, at the invitation of King Charles III.[^cite_note-\:6-52] The imperial couple had originally planned to visit in 2020 as guests of Queen Elizabeth II, but the state visit was cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-53] The rescheduled visit went ahead despite concerns of postponement due to the [British general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election) campaign that began in late May.[^cite_note-54] It was the first state visit in modern times to take place during an active election campaign.[^cite_note-55]

On 7 April 2025, the imperial couple visited [Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwo_Jima) to commemorate the 80th anniversary of the [Battle of Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima), in the first visit to the island by a Japanese monarch since 1994.[^cite_note-56]

The Imperial Couple traveled to Mongolia for one week as state guests beginning on 6 July 2025; the timing was chosen based on the 80th anniversary of the end of World War II.[^cite_note-57] Former Foreign Minister [Taro Kono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro_Kono) was named as the couple's chief attendant for the trip,[^cite_note-58] which marked the first official visit to Mongolia by a Japanese Emperor.[^cite_note-59] On the visit, the emperor and empress visited a memorial to [Japanese prisoners of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_prisoners_of_war_in_the_Soviet_Union) who had been held in modern-day Mongolia, as well as attending Mongolia's annual [Naadam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naadam) festival.[^cite_note-60]
