The **Senate** is the [upper chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) of [Nigeria](21383.md)'s bicameral [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), the [National Assembly](1898990.md).[^cite_note-2] The National Assembly (popularly referred to as NASS) is the nation's legislature and has the power to make laws, as summarized in chapter one, section four of the 1999 [Constitution of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nigeria).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The [lower chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) is the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Nigeria\)).

The [President of the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Nigerian_Senate) is the presiding officer of the Senate, whose chief function is to guide and regulate the proceedings in the Senate. The Senate President is second in the [Nigerian presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_presidential_line_of_succession). He is assisted by the Deputy President of the Senate. The current President is Sen. [Godswill Akpabio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godswill_Akpabio) while the current [Deputy President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_President_of_the_Nigerian_Senate) is Sen. [Barau Jibrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barau_Jibrin), both members of the [APC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Progressive_Congress_\(Nigeria\)).[^cite_note-5] The Senate President and his deputy are assisted by principal officers including the Majority Leader, Deputy Majority Leader, Minority Leader, Deputy Minority Leader, Chief Whip, Deputy Chief Whip, Minority Whip, and Deputy Minority Whip. In addition, there are 63 Standing Committees in the Senate chaired by Committee Chairmen.[^cite_note-6]

## Composition

The Senate consists of 109 [senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator). The [36 states of Nigeria](226734.md) are each divided into 3 senatorial districts, with each district electing one senator using the [first-past-the-post electoral system](261709.md). The [Federal Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Capital_Territory,_Nigeria) elects only one senator *[at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large)*, also using first-past-the-post.

Senators serve a term of four years. There are no [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits)[^cite_note-7] and senators can remain in the chamber for as long as they are re-elected in general elections.

The [majority party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_party) is the party that has a majority of seats, either alone or as the main party of a coalition or [caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus). If two or more parties are tied, the Senate President's affiliation determines which party becomes the majority party. The second largest party is the [minority party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_party).

## Functions of the Senate

### Legislation

[Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) may be introduced in either chamber of the [National Assembly](1898990.md).[^cite_note-8]

### Checks and balances

The [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nigeria) provides several unique functions for the Senate that form its ability to "check and balance" other elements of the [Federal Government of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_Nigeria).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] These include the requirement that the Senate may advise and must consent to some of the President's government appointments; also the Senate must consent to all treaties with foreign governments and it tries all impeachments.
