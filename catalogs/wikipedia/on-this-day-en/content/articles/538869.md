The **Nigerian Air Force** (**NAF**) is the air branch of the [Nigerian Armed Forces](21391.md). It is the youngest branch of the Nigerian military, established four years after the nation became independent. As at 2021, the air force is one of the largest in Africa, consisting of over 18,000 personnel.[^cite_note-3] Some of its popular aircraft include the [Chengdu F-7s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_F-7), [Dassault-Dornier Alpha Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault-Dornier_Alpha_Jet), [JF-17 Thunder](1928578.md), [T129 Atak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI/AgustaWestland_T129_ATAK), [Agusta Westland 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW109), [Eurocopter EC135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC135) and [Embraer EMB 314 Super Tucano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_314_Super_Tucano).

## Historical events

Although an air force was originally proposed in 1958, many lawmakers preferred to rely on the United Kingdom for air defense. But during [peacekeeping operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operation) in [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) and [Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_\(1961%E2%80%931964\)), the [Nigerian Army](14943749.md) had no [air transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_transport) of its own, and so in 1962, the government began to recruit [cadets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet) for pilot training in various foreign countries, with the first ten being taught by the [Egyptian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Air_Force).[^cite_note-4]

### 1960s

The Nigerian Air Force was formally established on 18 April 1964 with the passage of the Air Force Act 1964. The Act stated that the "Nigerian Air Force shall be charged with the defense of the Federal Republic by air, and to give effect thereto, the personnel shall be trained in such duties as in the air as well as on the ground." The NAF was formed with technical assistance from [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) (now [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany)). The air force started life as a transport unit with the first air crew trained with the [Ethiopian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Air_Force). The second set of cadets underwent training in February 1963 with the Royal [Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Air_Force) while some cadets were sent to train with the [Indian Air Force](403355.md).[^cite_note-5]

The head of the German Air Force Assistance Group (GAFAG) was Colonel [Gerhard Kahtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Kahtz), and he became the first commander of the NAF. The nucleus of the NAF was thus established with the formation of the Nigerian Air Force headquarters at the Ministry of Defense.

The NAF did not acquire combat capability until several [Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-17) aircraft were presented by the Soviet Union in support of Nigeria's war effort during the Nigerian Civil War. On 13 August 1967, following several damaging attacks by Biafran aircraft, the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) started delivering the first MiG-17s from Egypt to Kano IAP, simultaneously sending a large shipment aboard a Polish merchant ship. Initially two [MiG-15UTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-15UTI) (NAF601 and NAF 602), and eight [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17) (NAF603 to NAF610) were supplied to Nigeria. Later six [Il-28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-28) bombers, initially flown upon deployment by Egyptian and Czech pilots, were delivered from Egypt and stationed at Calabar and Port Harcourt.

### 1970s

In July 1971, the International Institute for Strategic Studies estimated that NAF had 7,000 personnel and 32 combat aircraft: six [Ilyushin Il-28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-28) medium bombers, eight [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17), eight [Aero L-29 Delfín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-29_Delf%C3%ADn) jet trainers, and 10 [P-149D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_P.149) trainers. Other aircraft included six [C-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-47), 20 [Do-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_Do_27)/[28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_Do_28), and eight [Westland Whirlwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Whirlwind_\(helicopter\)) and [Alouette II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alouette_II) helicopters.

During the 1970s, Nigeria bought [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md) from the United States. Six were acquired at a total cost of \$45 million. 25 [Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-21MFs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21_variants) and six MiG-21UM were delivered in 1975 upon the advent of the Murtala-Obasanjo administration that replaced the regime of General [Yakubu Gowon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakubu_Gowon). Most of these aircraft were deployed, making the NAF one of the most formidable air forces in Africa during this period.

Jimi Peters wrote: "...the 1975-1980 NAF development plan restructured NAF formations" into group (air force) level units that reported to air force headquarters. That structure, he went on, was found too cumbersome, and thus two intermediate commands (military formations) were formed in 1978: NAF Tactical Air Command and NAF Training Command.

### 1980s

From 1984, 18 [SEPECAT Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPECAT_Jaguar) fighters (13 Jaguar SNs and 5 Jaguar BNs) were delivered and operated from Makurdi. They retired in 1991. Nigeria purchased 24 [Aero L-39 Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39_Albatros) armed jet trainers in 1986–87, having retired its fleet of [L-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-29) that were donated to the Republic of Ghana Air Force at the inception of the West African Monitoring Group (ECOMOG) operations in Liberia. A subsequent attempt to expand the fleet by acquiring 27 more in 1991 was not executed.

In 2005, under the administration of President Olusegun Obasanjo, the Nigerian Parliament appropriated US\$251 million to purchase 15 [Chengdu F-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) fighter aircraft from China. The deal included 12 [F-7NI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chengdu_J-7_variants) (NI-Nigeria) single-seat fighter variant, and 3 FT-7NI dual-seat trainer aircraft. The \$251 million package included \$220 million for 15 aircraft, plus \$32 million for armaments: live PL-9C AAM, training [PL-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-9) rounds, unguided rockets, and 250/500 kg bombs. The pioneer NAF pilots on the aircraft trained in China in 2008, while delivery of the aircraft began in 2009. Nigeria had previously considered a \$160 million deal to refurbish its fleet of [MiG-21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) by Aerostar/Elbit Systems, IAI, and RSK MiG. However, it was considered more cost-effective to opt for acquiring the F-7s which were brand new. Nigeria also initiated modification of its variant of the F7, including installing some western equipment and avionics and hence its official designation as "F7-Ni" to reflect that its variant differs in some respects from a typical Chinese F-7. With this acquisition, the fleet of MiG 21s was subsequently retired. The Federal Government of Nigeria under the same dispensation acquired some [ATR Maritime Patrol](346378.md) aircraft, built by EADS and Finmeccania / Alenia Aeronautica, boosting the capacity of the service to carry out extensive Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance (ISR) missions on land and far into the sea.

From September 2009, Nigeria began refurbishing some of its [C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130) aircraft beginning with the NAF 917 which it brought back to life with the support of U.S. Air Forces Africa and 118th Airlift Wing. The NAF has subsequently further improved its domestic capacity with the increase in the serviceability of a good number of its transport aircraft.

### 2010s

The NAF designed and built its first indigenous UAV, the "Gulma," which was unveiled by former president [Goodluck Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Jonathan) in Kaduna, who said that the "Gulma" would be useful in aerial imaging/mapping, telecommunications, and weather monitoring. According to him, the UAV was rapidly becoming an important tool in news coverage, [environmental monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_monitoring), and oil and gas exploration.[^cite_note-6]

On 24 March 2011, the new Air Officer Commanding of NAF Mobility Command, Air Vice Marshal John Aprekuma, explained the rationale behind the establishment of the headquarters of the newly established Air Force Mobility Command in Yenagoa, Bayelsa State as being part of the Federal Government's strategy to protect its socio-economic interest in the Niger Delta, affirming that the presence of the command's headquarters would bring about security and calm to the people of the State.

On 9 December 2011, the Nigerian Air Force commissioned its first female pilot, [Blessing Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessing_Liman), following a directive to the NAF [hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy) by former President Goodluck Jonathan, for the service to start offering flying opportunities to qualified Nigerian female citizens, especially since women had long been flying civil aviation aircraft in the country but did not get a chance to fly in the military.[^cite_note-7]

In March 2014, the Nigerian Government approached Pakistan for the purchase of joint Chinese-Pakistani made [CAC/PAC JF-17 Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAC/PAC_JF-17), Block II multi-role fighter aircraft. In December 2015, the Government of President Muhammadu Buhari presented a budget to the National Assembly that included N5bn for three JF-17 aircraft. On 28 March 2018, 'The Diplomat' reported Pakistan as confirming the sale of three JF-17s to Nigeria. In March 2020, NAF Chief of Air Staff announced the delivery schedule of three JF-17 to be affected in November 2020. It is understood that the Buhari administration will expand the fleet of JF-17 fighter aircraft upon an expression of satisfaction by NAF Generals, with the performance of the initial batch procured.

In December 2017, NAF formally announced that the United States of America had agreed to sell the [A-29 Super Tucano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_314_Super_Tucano) attack aircraft to Nigeria after the deal had previously stalled.[^cite_note-8]

In November 2018, Sierra Nevada was officially awarded the contract for the 12 Super Tucano aircraft for the NAF, with an estimated completion date by 2024.

On 2 January 2019, one [Mi-35M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mil_Mi-24_variants) attack helicopter from the Nigerian Air Force helicopter squadron crashed in Damasak, Borno State while providing close-air support for troops of the 145 Battalion combating [Boko Haram](27868253.md) insurgents, killing all on board. The Mi-35 in the fleet of the NAF is the top-of-the-range model acquired in the life of the administration of President Muhammadu Buhari that had also ordered several [Mi-171](1493374.md) and [Agusta 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW109) helicopters from both Mil Moscow Helicopters of Russia and Italy's Leonardo Aerospace for the service.

On 15 October 2019, the NAF winged its first female combat fighter jet pilot, Flight Lieutenant Kafayat Sanni, and its first female combat helicopter pilot Lieutenant [Tolulope Arotile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolulope_Arotile). They were amongst thirteen other pilots also winged on the same day.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### 2020s

In April 2020, Embraer reported the completion of the first set of Super [Tucanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucano_people) out of the 12 on order, with an expected full delivery in 2021, bolstering the capacity of the Air Force to engage in counter insurgency interdiction operations (COIN).&#x20;

On December 7, 2025, at the request of the Beninese government and President [Patrice Talon](49884967.md), two A-29 Super Tucano aircraft scrambled from air bases in [Lagos](85232.md) and [Abuja](56823.md), flew over Cotonou to suppress a military coup aimed at toppling the government. The jets patrolled the Beninese skies for five hours, executing airstrikes on suspected sites in the capital harboring the coup plotters while ensuring air supremacy. This represented an active effort from an international community such as [ECOWAS](36848.md) in guaranteeing peace and stability of its neighbors for strategic purposes[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Structure

The Air Force includes a service headquarters, 6 principal staff branches, 4 direct reporting units, and 4 operational commands.[^cite_note-13]

### Administrative organization

The [Chief of the Air Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Air_Staff_\(Nigeria\)) (CAS) is the principal or lead adviser to the President and also the Minister of Defense and the Chief of Defense Staff, on air-related defense matters. The Nigerian Air Force headquarters is responsible for establishing long and short-term mission objectives and articulating policies, carrying out plans and procedures for the attainment of peace and stability. Also, HQ Nigerian Air Force liaises with the [Nigerian Army](14943749.md) and [Nigerian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Navy) on joint operational policies and plans. The headquarters Nigerian Air Force consists of the office of the [Chief of the Air Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Air_Staff_\(Nigeria\)) and 8 staff or branches namely; Policy and Plans Branch, Operations Branch, Air Engineering Branch, Logistics Branch, Administration Branch, Accounts and Budget Branch, Inspections Branch and Air Secretary Branch respectively. Each of these branches is headed by an [Air Vice Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Vice_Marshal) as branch chief.

### Air Force structure and organization

In 2017, Key Publishing/Air Forces Monthly wrote that NAF commands included:

> ..two new training commands derived from the now defunct Kaduna-based Training Command – the Air Training Command (ATC) with its HQ at Kaduna and the Ground Training Command (GTC) with its HQ at Enugu; and the Lagos-based Logistics Command. Every command has several subordinate groups, each of which has at least one wing – and each wing includes one or more squadrons. Wings are not always co-located with their parent unit, and while the numerical designations of some flying wings are known, information on current flying squadrons is not openly available.[^cite_note-14]

#### Presidential Air Fleet

Further information: [Nigerian Presidential Transport Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Presidential_Transport_Fleet?action=edit\&redlink=1)

The Nigerian Air Force also maintains and is the primary custodian of the air fleet in which the president flies on, as well as other state and foreign dignitaries. The fleet accounts for 10 various fixed wings and rotary aircraft.[^cite_note-20] According to the government, there are 10 aircraft in the fleet. They include two each of [AgustaWestland AW189](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW189), and [Falcon 7X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_7X), as well as one each of [Bombardier Challenger 605](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Challenger_605), [Boeing Business Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Business_Jet), [Falcon 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_900), [Hawker 4000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_4000), [Gulfstream G500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G500), and [Gulfstream G550](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G550). The fleet is situated at the Presidential Wing of the [Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nnamdi_Azikiwe_International_Airport).

In 2024, an Airbus was acquired for presidential travel. Reported to have cost N150 billion, the 15-year-old plane is said to have an elaborate configuration for VIPs and replaces the president's former plane, a 19-year-old Boeing BBJ 737-700.[^cite_note-21]

## Incidents and Accidents

### Crashes

- On 26 September 1992, a NAF [Lockheed C-130H Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_C-130H_Hercules) serial number *911* crashed three minutes after take-off from [Lagos](85232.md), [Nigeria](21383.md), when three engines failed, possibly due to high take-off weight. All 158 people on board were killed, including eight foreign nationals.

  [^cite_note-30]

- On 25 January 2015, a photo appeared online at Beegeagle's Blog, appearing to show a [CASC Rainbow CH-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASC_Rainbow#CH-3) UCAV which crashed upside down near Dumge village in the Mafa District of [Borno State](2227628.md). The two anti-tank missiles on the CH-3's wings appear to be intact. Borno is the area where much of the [Boko Haram](23769768.md) violence, including the massacre of 2,000 civilians, occurred in 2015. The Nigerian military is fighting to hold onto the city of Maiduguri against a Boko Haram onslaught, so it appears likely that the CH-3 in question was flying reconnaissance and fire support missions for the military when it crashed. The use of armed drones by Nigerian forces in combat makes Nigeria one of the first five countries to do that in combat history.

  [^cite_note-31]

- On 28 September 2018, a fatal air collision involving two [F-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) aircraft occurred during a formation flying exercise involving an [Aeritalia G.222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeritalia_G.222) and three [Alpha Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Jets) as they practiced flight maneuvers for the 58th [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Nigeria\)) celebrations in the capital, [Abuja](56823.md). As the F-7 jets turned to the formation flying, their wings clipped each other's side.

  [^cite_note-32]

  &#x20;Both planes lost [stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_dynamics) due to the collision and it resulted to the spiral loss of both jets and they both crashed at the Katampe district of [Abuja](56823.md). Three pilots ejected out of the crippled jets. The two pilots who were on the F-7Ni ejected and landed with minor G-force injuries, and the third pilot on the F-7 ejected and sustained head injuries due to the problems from the [parachute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute) as it deployed. The pilot died on the way to the hospital as emergency services rushed to the scene of the crash. The Nigerian Air Force was notified and responded with search and rescue for all three pilots, while witnesses helped in evacuating the pilots from their stricken planes.

  [^cite_note-33]

  [^cite_note-34]

- On 2 January 2019, one Mi-35M attack helicopter from the Nigerian Air Force helicopter squadron crashed in Damasak, Borno State while providing close-air support for troops of the 145 Battalion combating Boko Haram insurgents, killing all on board.

- On 31 March 2021, an [Alpha Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Jet) crashed near Borno State in North Eastern Nigeria, after it was conducting an [air interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction) on Boko Haram. At about 5\:08 p.m., the jet was reported to have stopped pinging on radar and presumed to have crashed. The wreckage was reported missing and found nineteen months later by Nigerian Army troops.

  [^cite_note-35]

  [^cite_note-36]

- On 18 July 2021, while returning from an air interdiction mission in the North Western region of Nigeria along the Kaduna-Zamfara state boundaries, an [Alpha Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Jet) piloted by [Flight Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_Lieutenant) Abayomi Dairo was shot down by "armed bandits" in [Zamfara State](2227664.md). Flight Lieutenant Dairo successfully ejected from the plane, evaded capture and made his way to a Nigerian Armed Forces base in the area with minor injuries. He was given a "hero's return" by the Chief of Defense Staff and other top officers, Armed Forces of Nigeria, General Lucky Irabor, alongside other military top brass.

  [^cite_note-37]

  [^cite_note-38]

- On 14 July 2023, while on a routine training exercise in Markudi, [Benue State](1556364.md), a [Chengdu FT-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_F-7) crashed at 4\:15 pm [WAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa_Time) local time. Both pilots ejected out of the jet and were rescued by the Air Force personnel assigned to search and rescue.

  [^cite_note-Document-39]

- On 14 August 2023, a NAF [MI-171](1493374.md) Helicopter, which was involved in on "casualty evacuation mission", encountered a crash at approximately 1\:00 pm. The helicopter's initial point of departure was Zungeru Primary School, with its destination set for [Kaduna state](2227637.md). However, it was later determined that the helicopter had suffered a crash in the proximity of Chukuba Village, situated within the administrative jurisdiction of the Shiroro Local Government Area in [Niger State](1723431.md). The number of passengers or crew members could not be ascertained.

  [^cite_note-40]

  [^cite_note-41]

  &#x20;Meanwhile, Islamic Jihadists claimed responsibility for the incident.

  [^cite_note-42]

### Errant Missions

The Nigerian military has made several mistakes while conducting air strikes in recent times. One particularly fatal error happened in December 2023, when the Nigerian forces [accidentally hit a village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudun_Biri_drone_strike) with an armed UAV in [northwestern Nigeria](2227637.md) killing 85 civilians[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] celebrating a Muslim festival. The army said they thought the people were rebels.[^cite_note-45]

In 2024, a [Christmas Day airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gidan_Sama_and_Rumtuwa_airstrike) in [Sokoto state](2227660.md) killed at least 10 people and injured many others.[^cite_note-46] The military targeted the terror group [Lakurawa](78327630.md) in the villages of Gidan Sama and Rumtuwa, but civilians were caught in the crossfire. The incident raised concerns among human rights groups, including [Amnesty International](18947898.md), which called on the Nigerian military to review its procedures and avoid such deadly accidents in the future.

Other reported and noteworthy incidents include:

- January 2017: The air force bombed a camp of internally displaced people in Rann, killing more than 100 refugees and aid workers

- 25 April 2021: A fighter jet mistakenly bombed a Nigerian Army truck, killing over 20 officers

- September 2021: The air force bombed a fish market in Daban Masara, killing at least 60 people

- 18 December 2022: An air strike killed 64 people in Mutumji village

- 24 January 2023: An air strike killed more than 40 herders in Doma region

- 5 December 2023: A drone attack killed more than 85 civilians who were villagers that the remote pilots reportedly mistook for terrorists.

- 27 December 2024: At least 10 people were killed and several wounded when military jets mistakenly bombed civilians in the northwestern state of Sokoto.

- On 13 January 2025, it was reported that at least 16 civilians in Nigeria's north-western Zamfara State were killed in a military air strike, apparently after being mistaken for criminal gangs.

  [^cite_note-47]

According to SBM Intelligence, a Lagos-based research firm, more than 400 Nigerian civilians have died during these types of incidents between 2017 and 2025.[^cite_note-48] Unfortunately, no significant lessons or operational changes have resulted from these mishaps as they seem to regularly recur.
