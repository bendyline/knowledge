# Guinea–Sierra Leone border



Map of the Guinea-Sierra Leone border

The **Guinea–Sierra Leone border** is 794 km or 493 mi in length and runs from the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) in the west to the tripoint with [Liberia](17791.md) in the east.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the west at the Atlantic coast, and then proceeds overland in a north-easterly direction via various irregular and some straight lines, as well as the [Great Scarcies River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Scarcies_River), before reaching the [10th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_parallel_north).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] The boundary then follows this parallel eastwards for circa 75 km/47 mi, before proceeding in a south-westerly direction, cutting across the [Loma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loma_Mountains) and [Tingi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingi_Hills_Forest_Reserve), down to the Meli river. The border follows the Meli down to the confluence with the [Moa/Makona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moa_River), and then follows the Makona eastwards to the Liberian tripoint.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]

## History

[Sierra Leone](27308.md) was founded by the British in the 1780s as haven for rescued and freed slaves; the area around [Freetown](56661.md) was made a crown colony in 1808 and British rule gradually extended over the interior over the following decades.[^cite_note-IBS136-3] [France](5843419.md) had also taken an interest in the West African coast, settling in the region of modern [Senegal](27278.md) in the 17th century and later annexing the coast of what is now Guinea in the late 19th century as the [Rivières du Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivi%C3%A8res_du_Sud) colony.[^cite_note-4] The area was renamed [French Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guinea) 1893, and was later included within the [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) colony.[^cite_note-IBS136-3]

The 1880s saw an intense competition between the European powers for territories in Africa, a process known as the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa). The process culminated in the [Berlin Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference) of 1884, in which the European nations concerned agreed upon their respective territorial claims and the rules of engagements going forward. As a result, France and Britain signed a treaty on 28 June 1882 delimiting a boundary between Sierra Leone and Guinea, terminating inland at an undetermined point; another treaty of 10 August 1889 extended the boundary further to the east.[^cite_note-IBS136-3] This boundary was extended again by a treaty of 21 January 1895 down to the vicinity of Timbekundu, and was then demarcated on the ground from December 1895-May 1896; this demarcation was approved by an exchange of notes in June 1898.[^cite_note-IBS136-3][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] Meanwhile, the [French Guinea–Liberia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea%E2%80%93Liberia_border) was modified in September 1907–11, as was the [Liberia–Sierra Leone border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia%E2%80%93Sierra_Leone_border) in January 1911, thereby extending the French Guinea-Sierra Leone boundary further south.[^cite_note-IBS136-3] Britain and France confirmed the new French-Guinea-Sierra Leone boundary line in June 1911 and signed a treaty to this effect on 4 September 1913.[^cite_note-IBS136-3]

French Guinea gained independence in 1958, followed by Sierra Leone in 1961, and the boundary then became one between two sovereign states. During the [Sierra Leone Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War) (1991-2001) some trouble occurred at the border town of [Yenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenga), prompting Guinea to cross the border and occupy the town.[^cite_note-Konneh-5] The dispute was settled in 2013 and the town returned to Sierra Leonean control.
