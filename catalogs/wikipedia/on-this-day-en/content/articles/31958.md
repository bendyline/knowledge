The **United Nations Economic and Social Council** (**ECOSOC**) is one of [six principal organs](216034.md#Six_principal_organs) of the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN), responsible for coordinating the economic and social fields of the organization, specifically in regards to the [fifteen specialized agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_specialized_agencies_of_the_United_Nations), the eight functional commissions, and the five regional commissions under its jurisdiction.

ECOSOC serves as the central forum for discussing international economic and social issues, and formulating policy recommendations addressed to member states and the United Nations System.[^cite_note-2] It has a rotating membership of 54 countries,[^cite_note-3] and over 1,600 nongovernmental organizations have [consultative status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consultative_status) with the Council to participate in the work of the United Nations.[^cite_note-4]

ECOSOC holds one four-week session each year in July, and since 1998 has also held an annual meeting in April with finance ministers of heading key committees of the [World Bank](36752.md) and the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF). Additionally, the [High-level Political Forum on Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-level_Political_Forum_on_Sustainable_Development) (HLPF), which reviews the implementation of the [2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Agenda_for_Sustainable_Development), is convened under the auspices of the Council every July.[^cite_note-5]

The members of ECOSOC are responsible for electing the executive board of [UNICEF](19698110.md), the council of the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme), the committee of [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR), various commissions and other bodies under its jurisdiction, and one-half of the executive board of the [World Food Programme](33582.md). ECOSOC is also responsible for tasks such as reviewing and updating the UN list of [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## President

Main article: [President of the United Nations Economic and Social Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Council)

The president of the Council is elected for a one-year term and chosen from the small or medium sized states represented on the Council at the beginning of each new session.[^cite_note-9] The presidency rotates among the [United Nations Regional Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Regional_Groups) to ensure equal representation.[^cite_note-10]

His Excellency Lok Bahadur Thapa was elected the President of the Economic and Social Council for the 2025-26 Session on 31 July 2025. His Excellency [Bob Rae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Rae) was elected the President of the Economic and Social Council for the 2024-25 Session on 25 July 2024. [Paula Narváez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Narv%C3%A1ez), Representative of Chile, was elected as the seventy-ninth president of the Council on 27 July 2023. She succeeded [Lachezara Stoeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachezara_Stoeva), who was elected as the seventy-eighth president of the Council on 25 July 2022,[^cite_note-11] succeeding [Collen Vixen Kelapile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collen_Vixen_Kelapile) of Botswana.[^cite_note-president-12]

## Members

See also: [List of members of the United Nations Economic and Social Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Council)

The Council consists of 54 Member States, which are elected yearly by the [General Assembly](31957.md) for overlapping three-year terms. Seats on the Council are allocated ensuring equitable geographic rotation among the United Nations regional groups.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Outgoing members are eligible for immediate re-election, and some seats are held by *de facto* permanent members.

### History

In 1945 when the [United Nations Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Charter) was originally signed, the Economic and Social Council consisted of 18 seats. The formal concept of the [United Nations Regional Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Regional_Groups) did not yet exist, and unlike the [Security Council](31956.md), there was no "gentlemen's agreement" between the [superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower) to assign ECOSOC seats. Regardless, with 4 exceptions out of 102 elections ([see list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Council#Non-permanent)), a relatively stable pattern emerged and held until 1960:[^cite_note-ecosoc1963-15][^cite_note-19]

- 5 seats to the [Permanent Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Five) of the UNSC

- 4 seats to [Latin America](18524.md)

- 2 seats to [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe)

- 1 seat to the [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth)

- 3 seats to the "[Near](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) and [Far East](11672.md)"

  [^cite_note-20]

- 3 seats to [Western Europe](33800.md)

As the number of United Nations members grew with [decolonization](302084.md), the pattern began to break down starting in 1961, with nations in [Africa](5334607.md) winning elections to seats formerly held by Western Europe and the [Republic of China (Taiwan)](25734.md).[^cite_note-ecosoc1963-15][^cite_note-21]

In 1965, the Charter was amended to increase the size of ECOSOC to 27 seats, and the Regional Groups were formally introduced. The seat distribution became:[^cite_note-ecosoclist-22]

- 5 seats to the [Asian Group](60084519.md)

- 3 seats to the [Eastern European Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_European_Group)

- 5 seats to the [Latin American and Caribbean Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Group)

- 7 seats to the [Western European and Others Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_and_Others_Group)

In 1973, the Charter was amended again to increase the size of ECOSOC to 54 seats. The seat distribution became:[^cite_note-ecosoclist-22]

- 14 seats to the [African Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Group)

- 11 seats to the [Asia-Pacific Group](60084519.md) (renamed from the Asian Group in 2011)

- 6 seats to the [Eastern European Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_European_Group)

- 10 seats to the [Latin American and Caribbean Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Group)

- 13 seats to the [Western European and Others Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_and_Others_Group)

### Regional commissions

The following are the active regional commissions of the Council:[^cite_note-Handbook-29]

- Europe: [United Nations Economic Commission for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_Commission_for_Europe) (ECE)

- Africa: [United Nations Economic Commission for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_Commission_for_Africa) (ECA)

- Latin America and the Caribbean: [United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_Commission_for_Latin_America_and_the_Caribbean) (ECLAC)

- Asia and the Pacific: [United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Commission_for_Asia_and_the_Pacific) (ESCAP)

- Western Asia: [United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Commission_for_Western_Asia) (ESCWA)

The key goal of the regional commissions is to "raise the \[regional] level of economic activity"; none of the regional commissions has in its founding mandate a reference to [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) or to the [environmental dimension of development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development).[^cite_note-\:1-34]

All UN regional commissions have expanded their activities to work toward achieving the [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) (SDGs). They increasingly include a concern for the environmental and social dimensions of development, along with their traditional economic focus. They have set standards for SDG implementation by providing (financial) support through reporting guidelines, performance indicators, and other managerial tools. However, their limited resources force them to prioritize; diverse sets of priorities in the region only partially overlap with the SDGs.[^cite_note-\:1-34]

The regional commissions seek to link the global ambitions of the SDGs with regional actors, contexts, and priorities. In practice, however, when it comes to agenda setting, the regional commissions mostly seek to balance the new global agenda with their regional priorities and prior agendas.[^cite_note-\:1-34]

## Specialized agencies

See also: [List of specialized agencies of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_specialized_agencies_of_the_United_Nations)

The specialized agencies of the United Nations are autonomous organizations working within the United Nations System, meaning that while they report their activities to the Economic and Social Council, they are mostly free to their own devices. Some were created before the United Nations existed and were integrated into the system, others were created by the League of Nations and were integrated by its successor, while others were created by the United Nations itself to meet emerging needs.[^cite_note-35] Each agency must negotiate with the Council as to what their relationship will look and work like. This leads to a system where different organizations maintain different types of relationships with the Council.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] For example, in the 1950s the members of ECOSOC were responsible for granting a state admission to [UNESCO](21786641.md),[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] but now this is done by the members of UNESCO themselves.

## *World Economic & Social Survey 2011*: Great Green Technological Transform

In a report issued in early July 2011, the UN called for spending nearly US\$2  trillion on [green technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_technologies) to prevent what it termed "a major planetary catastrophe", warning that "It is rapidly expanding energy use, mainly driven by [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels), that explains why humanity is on the verge of breaching planetary [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) boundaries through [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming), [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss), and disturbance of the [nitrogen-cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen-cycle) balance and other measures of the sustainability of the [earth](9228.md)'s [ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem)".[^cite_note-41]

[UN Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Secretary-General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) added: "Rather than viewing growth and sustainability as competing goals on a collision course, we must see them as complementary and mutually supportive imperatives". The report concluded that "Business as usual is not an option".[^cite_note-42]

## Reforms

The governance of the multilateral system has historically been complex and fragmented. This has limited the capacity of ECOSOC to influence international policies in trade, finance, and investment. Reform proposals aim to enhance the relevance and contribution of the council. A major reform was approved by the [2005 World Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_World_Summit) based on proposals submitted by secretary-general [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan).[^cite_note-43] The Summit aimed to establish ECOSOC as a quality platform for high-level engagement among member states and with international financial institutions, the private sector, and civil society on global trends, policies, and action. It resolved to hold biennial high-level Development Cooperation Forums at the national-leadership level, transforming the high-level segment of the Council to review trends in international development cooperation and promote greater coherence in development activities. At the Summit it was also decided to hold annual ministerial-level substantive reviews to assess progress in achieving internationally agreed on development goals (particularly the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals)). These "Annual Ministerial Reviews" will be replaced by the High-Level Political Forum from 2016 onwards after the new post-MDG/post-2015 [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) are agreed upon.[^cite_note-44]

Subsequent proposals by the High-Level Panel Report on System-Wide Coherence in November 2006 aimed to establish a forum within the ECOSOC as a counter-model to the exclusive clubs of the [G8](59755.md) and [G20](2206608.md). The Forum was to comprise 27 heads of state (L27, corresponding to half of ECOSOC's membership) to meet annually and provide international leadership in the development area. This proposal however, was not approved by the General Assembly.[^cite_note-45]

## Society and culture

### Chamber design

The Economic and Social Council Chamber in the [United Nations Conference Building](188955.md) was a gift from [Sweden](5058739.md). It was conceived by Swedish architect [Sven Markelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_Markelius), one of the 11 architects in the international team that designed the UN headquarters. Wood from Swedish pine trees was used in the delegates' area for the railings and doors.[^cite_note-\:0-46]<sup>: 6 </sup> The pipes and ducts in the ceiling above the public gallery were deliberately left exposed; the architect believed that anything useful could be left uncovered. The "unfinished" ceiling is a symbolic reminder that the economic and social work of the United Nations is never finished; there will always be something more that can be done to improve living conditions for the world's people.[^cite_note-\:0-46]<sup>: 7 </sup>
