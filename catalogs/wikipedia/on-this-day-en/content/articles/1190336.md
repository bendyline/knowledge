**Novorossiysk** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Новоросси́йск, IPA: [\[nəvərɐˈsʲijsk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b2/Ru-%D0%9D%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%BA.ogg/Ru-%D0%9D%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%BA.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b2/Ru-%D0%9D%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%BA.ogg/Ru-%D0%9D%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%BA.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Adyghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adyghe_language): ЦӀэмэз, romanized: *C̣ămăz*) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) in [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md), Russia. The [port of Novorossiysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Novorossiysk) is one of the largest ports on the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] It is one of the cities designated as a [Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)) by the Soviet Union. The population is 262,293 ([2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2021Census-11]&#x20;

## History

In antiquity, the shores of the [Tsemes Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsemes_Bay) were the site of Bata ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Βατά), an ancient Greek colony that specialized in the [grain trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_trade). It is mentioned in the works of [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo)[^cite_note-12] and [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy), among others.

Following brief periods of [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Khazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars) control, from the 9th century onwards, the area was part of the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) θέμα Χερσῶνος [*Thema Khersonos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherson_\(theme\)) (Province of Cherson).

During the 11th century, the area was overrun and controlled by nomads from the Eurasian steppe, led by the [Cumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumans). Later that century, the Byzantine emperor Ἀλέξιος Κομνηνός [Alexios I Komnenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexios_I_Komnenos) ([r.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign) 1081–1118) was approached by [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) refugees, who had left England following the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest). Alexios offered land to these refugees in *Thema Khersonos* if they could recapture it from the nomads and there is contemporaneous evidence that a [Byzantine-English colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_\(medieval\)) was subsequently founded. For example, [medieval nautical charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portolan) mention place names on the Kuban coast with possible English origins, including a port (located within or near the future site of Novorossiysk) known as Susaco (or *Susacho*) – a name that may have been derived from [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex).[^cite_note-CG-NE-13] (The same maps also show, north-west of *Susaco*, a river *Londina*, which may derive its name from [London](17867.md).)[^cite_note-CG-NE-13]

In the 13th century, [Genoese](47332321.md) merchants from the [Ghisolfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghisolfi) family maintained a trade outpost in the area. A 2007 archaeological investigation of related sites discovered some interesting items.[^cite_note-14]



General view from the early 20th century

During the rest of the 19th century, Novorossiysk developed rapidly. It was granted city status in 1866 and became the capital of the [Novorossiysk Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Okrug) and [Black Sea Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Governorate), the smallest in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), in 1896. In December 1905, the city was the seat of the short-lived [Novorossiysk Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Republic). From August 26, 1918, until March 27, 1920, the city was used as the principal center of [Denikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Denikin)'s [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) during the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War). Denikin's [South Russian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Russian_Government) was moved to [Crimea](163045.md) and many Whites escaped from Novorossiysk to [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) during the [Evacuation of Novorossiysk (1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_of_Novorossiysk_\(1920\)), with the help of [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_\(World_War_I\)) warships.[^cite_note-15]

According to the 1926 census, the population was 52.8% Russian, 22.8% Ukrainian, 6.1% Armenian, 3.4% Belarusian, 2.8% Jewish, 2.2% Greek, 1.4% Polish, and 0.9% Czech.[^cite_note-cen1926-16]

During [World War II](32927.md), most of the city was occupied by the German and Romanian Armies on September 10, 1942.[^cite_note-17] A small unit of Soviet sailors defended one part of the city, known as [Malaya Zemlya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaya_Zemlya), for 225 days beginning on February 4, 1943, until the town was liberated by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) on September 16, 1943. The defense of the port by the sailors allowed the Soviets to retain possession of the city's bay, which prevented the Axis from using the port for supply shipments. Novorossiysk was awarded the title of [Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)) in 1973.

In 1960, the town was commemorated in [Dmitri Shostakovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Shostakovich)'s work [*Novorossiysk Chimes, the Flame of Eternal Glory*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Chimes) (Opus 111b).

In 2003, [President](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) signed a [decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree_of_the_President_of_Russia) setting up a naval base for the Black Sea Fleet in Novorossiysk. Russia allocated 12.3 billion [rubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruble) (about \$480 million) for the construction of the new base between 2007 and 2012. The construction of other facilities and infrastructure at the base, including units for coastal troops, aviation and logistics, continued after 2012.[^cite_note-18] Russia planned to move the Black Sea Fleet with 80 warships and its headquarters from [Sevastopol](51587.md) to the Novorossiysk base in 2020.[^cite_note-19]

The Russian lease on port facilities in [Sevastopol](51587.md), which, though the main base of Russia's [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet), was part of [Ukraine](31750.md), was set to expire in 2017.[^cite_note-Iht2009-01-09-20] Ukraine was reported to be planning not to renew the lease; however, in April 2010 the Russian and Ukrainian presidents signed [an agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Ukrainian%E2%80%93Russian_Naval_Base_for_Natural_Gas_treaty) to extend the lease by twenty-five years, with an option of further extension of five years after the new term expires.[^cite_note-21] However, in 2014, Crimea was militarily occupied by the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md) during the [2014 Crimean crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_crisis) in February. Crimea was [annexed by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) in March 2014, and as such the question of the lease became moot.

## Administrative and municipal status

Within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), it is, together with twenty-four [rural localities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia), incorporated as the [City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) of Novorossiysk—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Krasnodar_Krai).[^cite_note-Ref1481-1] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the City of Novorossiysk is incorporated as Novorossiysk Urban Okrug.[^cite_note-Ref752-6]

### Coat of arms of Novorossiysk

The coat of arms of Novorossiysk was officially approved by Emperor Nicholas II on October 15, 1914. The description of the coat of arms was as follows: in a golden field above a black wavy tip, a black double-headed eagle under a crown, in the paws of an eagle a scepter and a power, on the chest a scarlet shield in which a golden Orthodox cross above a silver inverted crescent.[^cite_note-22] In 1994, the Soviet coat of arms was changed to a new one. In 2006, the coat of arms of 1914 was re-adopted with minor changes (a crown was added). The description of the modern coat of arms sounds like this in official sources: In a gold shield with a black wavy tip is a black double-headed eagle crowned with an Imperial crown, with a scepter and a power in its paws, on the wings of which is a shield, in the scarlet field of which is a golden Orthodox eight-pointed cross mounted on a silver inverted crescent. The shield is crowned with a golden five-pronged tower crown.[^cite_note-23]

## Geography



"A Map of The Caucasian Isthmus". Designed and drawn by J. Grassl, 1856.

The city is located on the [Black Sea](3386.md). It is not a resort town, but [Anapa](1194038.md) to the north and [Gelendzhik](1462700.md) to the south are. There are several urban settlements under the jurisdiction of Novorossiysk. The most famous is [Abrau-Dyurso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrau-Dyurso), which consists of a townlet on the shore of [Lake Abrau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Abrau) and a village on the coast of the Black Sea, connected by a winding mountain road.

The area of Novorossiysk is one of Russia's main wine-growing regions. The [wineries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wineries) of [Abrau-Dyurso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrau-Dyurso), established by [Tsar Alexander III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_Alexander_III) in 1870, produce table and sparkling wines for domestic consumption.

### Climate

Novorossiysk has a borderline [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) (*Cfa*) and [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa*) in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). Since the driest month has 36 mm (1.4 inches) and may or may not happen consistently in the summer, the city cannot be classified as solely humid subtropical or Mediterranean.

## Economy

The city sprawls along the shore of the [non-freezing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-water_port) [Tsemess Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsemess_Bay), which has been recognized since antiquity as one of the superior bays of the [Black Sea](3386.md). The [Novorossiysk Commercial Sea Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Commercial_Sea_Port)–with the market capitalization of \$1.11 billion and shares listed at [Moscow Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Exchange) and [London Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stock_Exchange)–serves Russian sea trade with regions of Asia, Middle East, Africa, Mediterranean, and South America. It is the busiest oil port in the Black Sea and the terminus of the pipeline from the [Tengiz Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengiz_Field) in [Kazakhstan](16642.md), developed by the [Caspian Pipeline Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Pipeline_Consortium).[^cite_note-davy20-25]

Novorossiysk is also an industrial city, dependent on [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel), [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), and the production of metal goods and other manufactures. Extensive [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) [quarries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry) supply important [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement) factories in and around the city. The town is home to the Maritime State Academy[^cite_note-26] and Novorossiysk Polytechnic Institute.[^cite_note-27]

## Transportation

Novorossiysk is the biggest Russian seaport. In 2019, cargo turnover amounted to 142.5m tons.[^cite_note-28] In 2021, cargo turnover amounted to 105,2m tons.[^cite_note-29]



A panoramic view of the port

Novorossiysk is connected by rail and highways to the main industrial and population centres of Russia, Transcaucasia, and Central Asia. It is served by the [Novorossiysk railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_railway_station).

The closest airports ([Gelendzhik Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelendzhik_Airport), [Anapa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapa_Airport) and [Krasnodar Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnodar_Airport), situated 33 km (21 mi), 53 km (33 mi) and 172 km (107 mi) away from the city, respectively, offer flights to many cities in Russia.
