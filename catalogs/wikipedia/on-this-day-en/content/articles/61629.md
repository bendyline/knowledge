The **1966 FIFA World Cup** was the eighth [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the quadrennial [football](10568.md) tournament for senior national teams. It was played in [England](9316.md) from 11 to 30 July 1966. England defeated West Germany 4–2 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final) to win their first and only World Cup title. The final was level at 2–2 after 90 minutes and went into [extra time](633242.md#Association_football), during which [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) scored two goals to complete his [hat-trick](411541.md), the first to be scored in a World Cup final. Portuguese striker [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio) was the tournament's top scorer with nine goals, earning him the [golden boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Golden_Boot) award with three goals more than second placed [Helmut Haller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Haller). It was the first World Cup held in the [English-speaking world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_world).

England were the fifth nation to win the event, and the third host nation to win after [Uruguay](679782.md) in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) and [Italy](362466.md) in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup). Reigning world champions [Brazil](149286.md) failed to get past the group stages as they were defeated by Hungary and Portugal. It was the second time that defending champions were eliminated in the group stages after Italy in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup). This would not occur again until [36 years later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup#Group_A). The two debut teams performed well at the competition – [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) beat Italy 1–0 on the way to reaching the quarter-finals, where they lost to [Portugal](576328.md) 5–3 after leading 3–0. Portugal themselves finished third, beating the Soviet Union 2–1 in the bronze-match.

During the qualifying games, FIFA, citing logistic and competitive reasons, announced that there would be no direct qualifying place for an African team: all 15 African nations who had entered immediately [boycotted](18591996.md) the competition in protest.

Matches were played at eight stadiums across England, foremost [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)), which had a capacity of 98,600. It was at Wembley that England played all its matches; that the tournament was opened by [Queen](47063.md) [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II);[^cite_note-1] and where the final was played.

Prior to the tournament, the [Jules Rimet trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy#Jules_Rimet_Trophy) was [stolen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_theft_of_the_Jules_Rimet_Trophy), but was recovered by a dog named [Pickles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickles_\(dog\)) four months before the tournament began. It was the first World Cup to have selected matches broadcast via [satellite](45207.md) to countries on other continents.[^cite_note-2] The final, which was broadcast locally by the [BBC](19344654.md), was the last to be shown entirely in [black and white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_white). This was also the only World Cup in history that did not have matches on Sundays, owing to [religious laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) at the time, which were repealed ten years later in 1976.

## Background

Main article: [FIFA World Cup hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts)

In Rome, Italy on 22 August 1960, England was chosen as host nation for the 1966 World Cup over rival bids from West Germany and Spain. It was the first tournament to be held in a country that was affected directly by World War II, as the four previous tournaments were held either in countries out of war theatres or in neutral countries.[^cite_note-mirr_9sur-3][^cite_note-curr_196-4]

### Qualification

Main article: [1966 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification)

Despite the Africans' absence, there was another new record number of entries for the qualifying tournament, with 70 nations taking part. FIFA determined that ten teams from Europe would qualify, along with four from South America, one from Asia and one from North and Central America.[^cite_note-\:1-5]

Portugal and North Korea qualified for the first time.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-6]

### Mascot and match ball



Official match ball for the 1966 FIFA World Cup produced by [Slazenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slazenger)

The [mascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascot) for the 1966 competition was "[World Cup Willie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cup_Willie)", a cartoon lion wearing a [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack) jersey emblazoned with the words "WORLD CUP". This was the first World Cup mascot, and one of the first mascots to be associated with a major sporting competition. Willie was designed by freelance children's book illustrator Reg Hoye.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The [official match ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_official_match_balls) was produced by [Slazenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slazenger) for the tournament.[^cite_note-9]

### Controversies

#### African boycott

All fifteen entered African nations boycotted the tournament to protest against a 1964 FIFA ruling that required the three second-round winners from the African zone to enter a play-off round against the winners of the Asian zone in order to qualify for the World Cup: they considered winning their zone was enough in itself to merit qualification.

The [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF) considered that the lack of direct representation of African nations in the World Cup was unfair, and demanded that FIFA guarantee at least one African nation a place in the finals of the following tournament. They also protested against the readmission of [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) to FIFA in 1963, despite their expulsion from CAF because of the country's [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) regime in 1958.[^cite_note-BBCBoycott-10][^cite_note-pund_This-11]

As a result of this boycott, FIFA fined CAF 5,000 Swiss francs. Yidnekatchew Tessema, then president of the CAF, responded to this punishment by saying, "FIFA has adopted a relentless attitude against the African Associations and its decisions resemble methods of intimidation and repression designed to discourage any further impulses of a similar nature. In our opinion, the African National Associations ... really deserved a gesture of respect rather than a fine."[^cite_note-12]

South Africa was subsequently assigned to the Asia and Oceania qualifying group, before being disqualified after being suspended again due to pressure from other African nations in October 1964.[^cite_note-twoh_Worl-13] Despite this, FIFA refused to change the qualifying format, citing competitive and logistical issues, and the African teams withdrew in protest.

CAF subsequently informed FIFA that they would refuse to participate in World Cup qualifying for the [1970](157227.md) tournament unless at least one African team had an automatic place assured in the World Cup: this was put in place for the 1970 FIFA World Cup, and all subsequent World Cup finals.[^cite_note-BBCBoycott-10] Players from the [Portuguese African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) colonies of [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola) and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Mozambique) participated for Portugal.[^cite_note-BBCBoycott-10][^cite_note-twoh_Worl-13]

#### North Korea–United Kingdom relations

The United Kingdom, which had fought for South Korea in the [Korean War](16772.md), was concerned over the entry of North Korea in the tournament. The [UK did not recognise North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations) and feared that its presence would strain relations with South Korea and the United States. FIFA told England's Football Association that the tournament would be moved if any qualified team were to be refused entry.[^cite_note-nk1-14][^cite_note-nk2-15]

On the suggestion of the British [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office), the playing of national anthems, and meetings between players and state figures such as Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II), would take place in only two games: the opening game and the final. North Korea was not scheduled for the former, and was considered unlikely to reach the latter. A Foreign Office suggestion for flags outside stadiums to be removed after each team's elimination, in the expectation of an early North Korean exit, was vetoed by the [Department of Education and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Education).[^cite_note-nk1-14][^cite_note-nk2-15]

#### Trophy incident

The 1966 World Cup had a rather unusual hero off the field, a dog called [Pickles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickles_\(dog\)).[^cite_note-16] In the build-up to the tournament, the [Jules Rimet trophy was stolen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_theft_of_the_Jules_Rimet_Trophy) from an exhibition display. A nationwide hunt for the icon ensued. It was discovered wrapped in newspaper as the dog sniffed under bushes in a London garden.[^cite_note-17] The FA commissioned a replica cup in case the original cup was not found in time. This replica, as well as Pickles' collar, is held at the [National Football Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Museum) in Manchester, where it is on display.[^cite_note-18]

#### Doping

[West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) encouraged and covered up a culture of doping across many sports for decades.[^cite_note-19] A 2013 report by researchers at Berlin's Humboldt University on behalf of the German Olympic Sports Confederation, titled "Doping in Germany from 1950 to today", linked the West Germany national team of 1966, which reached the World Cup final, with doping.[^cite_note-20]

#### Officiating issues and subsequent reforms

While refereeing controversies are common in World Cup history, the 1966 tournament stands out because of the structural reforms it provoked. A series of violent matches and contentious dismissals led FIFA to introduce major changes to the Laws of the Game in the following tournament.[^cite_note-CardsInspiration-21]

The tournament was marked by persistently rough play and inconsistent officiating. In their opening match, Brazilian star [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) was repeatedly fouled and left the pitch injured after a strong tackle to the knee, forcing him to miss the subsequent match against Hungary.[^cite_note-PeleInjury1-22] Upon returning for the final group stage clash with Portugal, Pelé suffered two heavy challenges from Portuguese defenders, most notably [João Morais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Morais), that ended his tournament. Pelé departed on a stretcher, and Brazil exited the competition 3–1.[^cite_note-PeleInjury1-22][^cite_note-PeleInjury3-23] Morais was allowed to remain on the pitch. No players were sent off in any of those games.[^cite_note-WorldCupsCards-24]

Further tension unfolded in the quarter-final between England and Argentina, when captain [Antonio Rattín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Ratt%C3%ADn) was controversially sent off by referee [Rudolf Kreitlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Kreitlein) for alleged "violence of the tongue." Rattín's refusal to immediately exit the field prompted a delay and police intervention, fuelling accusations of arbitrary officiating.[^cite_note-GuardianRattin-25] In another quarter-final between West Germany and Uruguay, shortly after halftime, Uruguayan players [Horacio Troche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Troche) and [Héctor Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Silva_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)) were both dismissed by English referee [Jim Finney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Finney). Uruguay conceded 3 goals when reduced to nine men[^cite_note-GermanyUruguay-26] and South American media decried a pattern of European referee bias.[^cite_note-Newyorker-27]

In light of these high-profile incidents, FIFA recognised the need for clearer disciplinary procedures and greater consistency in officiating. Ahead of the 1970 World Cup, the yellow and red card system was introduced, alongside the allowance of two substitutions per team and new efforts to standardise referee training and interpretations of the Laws of the Game.[^cite_note-Alkass-28][^cite_note-PremierFootballUK-29]

#### Later accusations of matchfixing

In 2008, Brazilian FIFA president from 1974 until 1998, [João Havelange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Havelange), accused this competition, along with the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) in West Germany for being fixed to benefit the host country. According to him Brazil deserved to win in both cases.[^cite_note-30]

## Format

The format of the 1966 competition remained the same as in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup): 16 qualified teams were divided into four groups of four. Each group played a [round-robin](939742.md) format.[^cite_note-draw-31] Two points were awarded for a win and one point for a draw, with [goal average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_average) and eventually drawing lots used to separate teams equal on points.[^cite_note-draw-31] The top two teams in each group advanced to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-draw-31]

In the knockout games, if the teams were tied after 90 minutes, 30 minutes of extra time were played. For any match other than the final, if the teams were still tied after extra time, lots would be drawn to determine the winner. The final would have been replayed if tied after extra time; but if still tied after the replay, the champion would have been decided by drawing lots. In the event, no replays or drawing of lots were necessary.

The draw for the final tournament took place on 6 January 1966 at the [Royal Garden Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Garden_Hotel) in London, and was the first to be televised. The seeded teams for the draw were England, West Germany, Brazil, and Italy.[^cite_note-32]

## Venues

Eight venues were used for this World Cup. The newest and biggest venue used was [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)) in north west London, which was 43 years old in 1966. As was often the case in the World Cup, group matches were played in two venues close to each other. Group 1 matches (which included the hosts) were all played in London: five at Wembley, which was England's national stadium and was considered to be the most important football venue in the world; and one at [White City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City_Stadium) in west London, which was used as a temporary replacement for nearby Wembley. The group stage match between Uruguay and France played at White City Stadium (originally built for the [1908 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics)) was scheduled for a Friday, the same day as regularly scheduled [greyhound racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_racing) at Wembley. Because Wembley's owner refused to cancel this, the game had to be moved to the alternative venue in London. Group 2's matches were played at [Hillsborough Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_Stadium) in Sheffield and [Villa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Park) in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham); Group 3's matches were played at [Old Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford) in [Manchester](20206.md) and [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park) in [Liverpool](18081.md); and Group 4's matches were played at [Ayresome Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayresome_Park) in Middlesbrough and [Roker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roker_Park) in [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland).[^cite_note-33] The stadium construction cost are estimated to be today's equivalent of £9.2 million, additional to £36.22 million for tournament organisation.[^cite_note-34]

The most used venue was Wembley, which was used for nine matches, including all six featuring England, the final and the match for third place. Goodison Park was used for five matches, Roker Park and Hillsborough both hosted four, while Old Trafford, Villa Park and Ayresome Park each hosted three matches and did not host any knockout round matches.[^cite_note-35]

## Tournament summary

The opening match took place on Monday, 11 July, which made it the second World Cup after [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) not to begin in May or June. Before the tournament began, eventual winners England were 9/2 second favourites with bookmakers behind Brazil (9/4), while beaten finalists West Germany were 25/1 outsiders.[^cite_note-36] The final took place on Saturday, 30 July 1966, the 36th anniversary of the first final. Until 2022, this was the latest date that any tournament had concluded. The reason for the unusually late scheduling of the tournament appears to lie with the outside broadcast commitments of the BBC, which also had to cover the Wimbledon tennis tournament (which ran between 20 June and 2 July) and the Open Golf Championship (6 to 9 July).

### Group stage



[Wolfgang Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Weber) (left) and [Luis Artime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Artime) during the match between West Germany and Argentina in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Park)

1966 was a World Cup with few goals as the teams began to play much more tactically and defensively. This was exemplified by Alf Ramsey's [England](9904.md) as they finished top of Group 1 with only four goals, but having none scored against them. They also became the first World Cup winning team not to win its first game in the tournament. [Uruguay](679782.md) were the other team to qualify from that group at the expense of both [Mexico](808402.md) and [France](250197.md). All the group's matches were played at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)) apart from the match between Uruguay and France which took place at [White City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_City_Stadium).

In Group 2, [West Germany](250204.md) and [Argentina](454699.md) qualified with ease as they each finished the group with 5 points, [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team) managed 2, while [Switzerland](681862.md) left the competition after losing all three group matches. FIFA cautioned Argentina for its violent style in the group games, particularly in the scoreless draw with West Germany, which saw Argentinean [Rafael Albrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Albrecht) get sent off and suspended for the next match.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In the northwest of England, [Old Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford) and [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park) played host to Group 3 which saw the two-time defending champions [Brazil](149286.md) finish in third place behind [Portugal](576328.md) and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team), and be eliminated along with [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team). Brazil were defeated 3–1 by Hungary in a classic encounter before falling by the same scoreline to Portugal in a controversial game. Portugal appeared in the finals for the first time, and made quite an impact. They won all three of their games in the group stage, with a lot of help from their outstanding striker [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio), whose nine goals made him the tournament's top scorer.

Group 4, however, provided the biggest upset when [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) beat [Italy](362466.md) 1–0 at [Ayresome Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayresome_Park), [Middlesbrough](334775.md) and finished above them, thus earning qualification to the next round along with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team). This was the first time that a nation from outside Europe or the Americas had progressed from the first stage of a World Cup: the next would be Morocco in 1986.

### Knock-out stages

The quarter-finals provided a controversial victory for West Germany as they cruised past Uruguay 4–0; the South Americans claimed that this occurred only after the referee (who was [Jim Finney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Finney) from England) had not recognised a handball by Schnellinger on the goal line and then had sent off two players from Uruguay: [Horacio Troche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Troche) and [Héctor Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Silva_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)).[^cite_note-39] It appeared as if the surprise package North Korea would claim another major upset in their match against Portugal at Goodison Park: they led 3–0 after 22 minutes. It fell to one of the greatest stars of the tournament, [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio), to change that. He scored four goals in the game and [José Augusto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Augusto_\(footballer\)) added a fifth goal in the 78th minute to earn Portugal a 5–3 victory.

Meanwhile, in the other two games, [Ferenc Bene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Bene)'s late goal for Hungary against the Soviet Union, who were led by [Lev Yashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Yashin)'s stellar goalkeeping, proved little more than a consolation as they crashed out 2–1, and the only goal between Argentina and England came courtesy of England's [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst). During that controversial game (for more details see [Argentina and England football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_and_England_football_rivalry)), Argentina's [Antonio Rattín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Ratt%C3%ADn) became the first player to be sent off in a senior international football match at Wembley.[^cite_note-40] Rattín at first refused to leave the field and eventually had to be escorted by several policemen. Scoreless when Rattin was dismissed, the game was decided by Hurst's headed goal twelve minutes from the end of normal time. This game is called *el robo del siglo* (lit. 'the robbery of the century') in Argentina.[^cite_note-41]

All semi-finalists were from Europe. [Bobby Charlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Charlton) scored both goals in England's win, with Portugal's goal coming from a penalty in the 82nd minute after a handball by Jack Charlton on the goal line.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The other semi-final also finished 2–1: [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) scoring the winning goal with a left foot shot from the edge of the area for West Germany as they beat the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-44]

Portugal went on to beat the Soviet Union 2–1 to take third place. Portugal's third place was the best finish by a team making its World Cup debut since 1934. It was equalled by Croatia in 1998.

### Final

Main article: [1966 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final)

London's Wembley Stadium was the venue for the final, and 98,000 people attended. After 12 minutes 32 seconds [Helmut Haller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Haller) put West Germany ahead, but the score was levelled by [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) four minutes later. [Martin Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Peters) put England in the lead in the 78th minute; England looked set to claim the title when the referee awarded a free kick to West Germany with one minute left. The ball was launched goalward and [Wolfgang Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Weber) scored, with England appealing in vain for handball as the ball came through the crowded penalty area.[^cite_note-mcilvanney-45]

With the score level at 2–2 at the end of 90 minutes, the game went to [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtime_\(association_football\)). In the 98th minute, Hurst found himself on the scoresheet again; his shot hit the crossbar, bounced down onto the goal line, and was awarded as a goal. Debate has long raged over whether the ball crossed the line, with the goal becoming part of World Cup history.[^cite_note-46] England's final goal was scored by Hurst again, as a celebratory pitch invasion began. This made Geoff Hurst the first player to have scored three times in a single World Cup final.[^cite_note-mcilvanney-45] [BBC](19344654.md) commentator [Kenneth Wolstenholme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Wolstenholme)'s description of the match's closing moments has gone down in history: "Some people are on the pitch. [They think it's all over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_think_it's_all_over) ... *\[Hurst scores]* It is now!"[^cite_note-47]

England's total of eleven goals scored in six games set a new record low for average goals per game scored by a World Cup winning team. The record stood until 1982, when it was surpassed by Italy's 12 goals in seven games; in 2010 this record was lowered again by Spain, winning the Cup with eight goals in seven games. England's total of three goals conceded also constituted a record low for average goals per game conceded by a World Cup winning team. That record stood until 1994, when it was surpassed by Brazil's three goals in seven games. France again lowered the record to two goals in seven during the [1998 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), a record that has since been equalled by Italy at the 2006 tournament and by Spain's two goals conceded during the [2010 tournament](168079.md). Spain then lowered the record to one in eight in [2026](36581929.md).

England received the recovered Jules Rimet trophy from [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) and were crowned World Cup winners for the first time.[^cite_note-mcilvanney-45]

In this World Cup, the national anthems were played only in the final. They were not played in the earlier matches because the organisers (FIFA and the FA) feared that North Korea's presence – a socialist country that was not recognised by the United Kingdom – in the World Cup would cause problems with South Korea. A memo from the Foreign Office months before the finals began stated that the solution would be "denying the visas to North Korean players".[^cite_note-nk1-14] The final, held at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)), was the last whose television broadcast was in black and white.[^cite_note-48]
