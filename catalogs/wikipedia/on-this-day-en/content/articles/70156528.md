# Special military operation

This article is about the term used by the Russian government. For special military activities in general, see [Special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations).

"СВО" redirects here. Not to be confused with the non-Cyrillic [CBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBO).



A banner of the [Russian Ministry of Defence](7875884.md) with the phrase "special military operation"

"**Special military operation**"[^cite_note-1] (also "**special operation**", and abbreviated as "**SMO**" or "**SVO**", or [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): спецопера́ция, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *spetsoperatsiya*, [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): спецопера́ція) is the official term used by the [Russian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_government) to describe the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) that began in February 2022 following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] It is widely considered a [euphemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemism) intended to minimize the invasion and obfuscate the war's original objective of annexing all [Russophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_distribution_of_Russian_speakers) regions of Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:2-6][^cite_note-\:4-7][^cite_note-\:7-8][^cite_note-9]

Russia has banned the use of the terms "war" or "invasion" in referring to its invasion of Ukraine,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] as well as discussion of the censorship itself.[^cite_note-12] The expression appears prominently in the [public address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin_speech_on_invading_Ukraine) by Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), released on 24 February 2022. The term "special military operation" has been used in [Ukrainian media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Ukraine) in specific contexts, generally in [scare quotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scare_quotes), to mock or criticize the Russian invasion.[^cite_note-\:2-6]

## Usage

According to some observers, such as Russian journalist [Ksenia Turkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksenia_Turkova?action=edit\&redlink=1), the purpose of this terminology is mainly to create a perception that war is more benign than it actually is, by softening the wording in official reports and in the media.[^cite_note-carnegie2-18][^cite_note-Очень_странные-28]

### Applications in Russian media

Further information: [Russian fake news laws](70307905.md), [Russian 2022 war censorship laws](70646883.md), and [Vladimir Putin speech on invading Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin_speech_on_invading_Ukraine)

See also: [Russian information war against Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_information_war_against_Ukraine)

In [Russian propaganda](45060755.md), the term "special military operation" is the main designation for aggression against Ukraine and is used to replace the definition of "war", which the Russian authorities and state media carefully avoided.[^cite_note-opiniojuris-23][^cite_note-\:3-29] On 24 February 2022, 6 hours after the start of the invasion of Russian troops into Ukraine, the Russian government tightened censorship by officially requiring the media to use only materials provided by Russian government sources. Subsequently, under pressure from the authorities, many organizations left the country or were closed. The Russian authorities blocked access to a number of Internet resources that refused to comply with the requirements.[^cite_note-\:0-30]

In early November 2022, the "rule" was first violated by TV presenter [Vladimir Solovyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Solovyov_\(TV_presenter\)) during a radio broadcast; later on, the events in Ukraine were publicly called "war" by [Vladimir Putin](32817.md),[^cite_note-\:4-7] [Sergey Lavrov](541346.md) and [Margarita Simonyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Simonyan).[^cite_note-31] In general, Russian authorities and media have tried to avoid the term "war" in the context of Ukraine, instead using it in terms like "[gas war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Ukraine_gas_disputes)" or "[information war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_war)".[^cite_note-\:0-30] In March 2024, Kremlin spokesperson [Dmitry Peskov](9214683.md) said that the invasion was a "*[de jure](43594.md)* a special military operation" which, after foreign countries [began delivering military aid to Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_aid_to_Ukraine_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War), devolved into a "*[de facto](43594.md)* war" against the "[collective West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_West)".[^cite_note-32] In July 2026, Peskov again claimed that what had started as a "special military operation" was now a "real war" due to Western support for Ukraine.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Applications in other media

Certain internationally distributed news agencies, such as [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) (AJE), have also used the term in instances with quotation marks applied and details provided about the war's conduct. For example, a March 2022 report by AJE stated that the terminology of having a "'special military operation' should be used to describe Moscow's assault on Ukraine" according to Russian officials because "the Kremlin has been working hard to promote its version of events in the face of widespread indignation and an anti-war movement at home".[^cite_note-AJE-35]

On 20 September 2023, the [Permanent Representative of Russia to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_Russia_to_the_United_Nations), [Vasily Nebenzya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Nebenzya) accused the [Prime Minister of Albania](259136.md) [Edi Rama](277805.md), who was serving as the [council](31956.md) president, of turning a council meeting into "a one-man stand-up show" by allowing an appearance by [President of Ukraine](20688479.md) [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md), to which Rama replied "this is not a special operation by the Albanian presidency", adding: "There is a solution for this. If you agree, you stop the war and President Zelenskyy will not take the floor."[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-cbc_2265350211731-37][^cite_note-38]

### In colloquial speech

[Social anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_anthropology) [Aleksandra Arkhipova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandra_Arkhipova?action=edit\&redlink=1) noted that as of September 2022, the abbreviation "SVO" was more commonly used instead of "special operation". According to Arkhipova, this is due to the length of the full term, which is inconvenient in speech, and the concealment of the essence of the term in the abbreviation.[^cite_note-автоссылка2-39]

## Meaning and interpretation

Further information: [Special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations)

Strictly speaking, a "[special operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations)" (as defined by organizations like [NATO](21133.md)) is a [military term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_term) for a task that requires specially trained units for an unconventional operation.[^cite_note-NATO_doctrine-40][^cite_note-carnegie2-18] However, the meaning of the Russian "Special military operation" in Ukraine is different.[^cite_note-\:6-41] This ambiguity was used to hide and distort the true meaning of what an "SVO" would entail,[^cite_note-\:5-42] and has been described as an example of "[doublespeak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doublespeak)" and "[doublethink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doublethink)".[^cite_note-opiniojuris-23][^cite_note-\:1-43]

Konstantin Gorobets, an associate professor at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen), argued that, unlike "war", the term "special military operation" positioned Ukraine as a [colony of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_imperialism), denies it equal standing as a sovereign state, and uses the "language of policing". Gorobets says that the implication of the term is imperialistic, "because it assumes that Russia is using force within its own domain, of which Ukraine \[in their view] is but a part."[^cite_note-opiniojuris-23]

## In popular culture

Due to its nature as an overt euphemism, the term has become an Internet meme that satirizes Russian propaganda.[^cite_note-44] At the end of 2022, the euphemism won in the nomination "Expression of the Year" of Russia's "[Word of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_of_the_year_\(Russia\))" competition (with the Russian word for [war](33158.md) (война) being the overall winner).[^cite_note-carnegie2-18][^cite_note-\:3-29] It was also selected as the 2022 [Euphemism of the Year by the American Dialect Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_American_Dialect_Society's_Words_of_the_Year).

The semi-official symbol of the term is the [Latin letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_letter) "[Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_\(military_symbol\))".[^cite_note-45]
