On April 23, 2026, two passenger trains transporting a total of 37 people collided head-on near [Hillerød](815846.md) in the [Capital Region](2521189.md) of [Denmark](76972.md), injuring at least 18 people, including five critically. Claus Pedersson, safety director at [Lokaltog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokaltog), described the collision as "one of the worst we can imagine in the railway industry".[^cite_note-BBC-2]

## Collision

The collision occurred at a [level crossing](193595.md) at Isterødvejen near [Hillerød](815846.md), a town north of [Copenhagen](5166.md). The details surrounding the collision are unclear, but police said the trains were travelling fast. Each train was operated by an [Alstom Coradia LINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom_Coradia_LINT).[^cite_note-3] Two yellow and grey trains with visible damage to the front were pictured in a wooded area.[^cite_note-BBC-2] Glass from the windshields and windows were scattered.[^cite_note-4] A large number of ambulances and police cars were dispatched and all the passengers were evacuated and the injured transported to hospitals.[^cite_note-5]

## Victims

Eighteen people were injured, including five critically. The critically injured were flown to the [National Hospital in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigshospitalet). Among the injured were several [Lokaltog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokaltog) staff members.[^cite_note-BBC-2] People who were not physically hurt were offered assistance.[^cite_note-6]

## Investigation

The [Accident Investigation Board Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accident_Investigation_Board_Denmark) arrived at the scene and Inspector Morten Pedersen of [North Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Zealand) police said they would be working together to find out what had happened. The board's railway unit manager, Klaus Jensen, told broadcaster [TV 2 News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_2_News) that investigators are looking at "all hypotheses", including "a failure in the signalling system, or whether there may have been a failure due to human factors".[^cite_note-BBC-2]
