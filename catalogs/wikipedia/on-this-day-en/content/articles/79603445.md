# Tropical Forest Forever Facility

A world map showing the distribution of tropical and sub-tropical moist broadleaf forests in green

Tropical and sub-tropical moist broadleaf forests, the biome to be conserved through the TFFF

**The Tropical Forest Forever Facility** (**TFFF**) is a proposed blended-finance mechanism to prevent the [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and [degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_degradation) of [moist broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests). The goal is to turn forests into a protected assets and provide forested countries with a financial incentive protect them.[^cite_note-1]

The fund would use profits from capital market investments to provide results-based payments to rainforest countries.[^cite_note-\:0-2] The mechanism was proposed by the [Brazilian government](33367309.md)[^cite_note-3] with the aim of launching the facility at [COP30](75227876.md) in [Belem](409573.md), Brazil in November 2025.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## History

A version of this fund concept was originally proposed by [World Bank](36752.md) treasurer Kenneth Lay in the 2000s.[^cite_note-\:0-2] The mechanism, then called the Tropical Forest *Finance* Facility received broader attention through an article Lay co-wrote for [The Center for Global Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Center_for_Global_Development).[^cite_note-5] The current iteration was announced at [COP28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) in [Dubai](211583.md), [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) in 2023 by Brazil’s Environment Minister [Marina Silva](17484359.md) and Finance Minister [Fernando Haddad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Haddad).[^cite_note-6]

## Government endorsements

The governments of [France](5843419.md),[^cite_note-7] [Germany](11867.md),[^cite_note-\:2-8] [Colombia](5222.md),[^cite_note-\:2-8] the [UAE](69328.md),[^cite_note-\:2-8] [Malaysia](3607937.md),[^cite_note-\:2-8] [Singapore](27318.md)[^cite_note-\:4-9] and [Norway](21241.md)[^cite_note-\:2-8] have endorsed the facility or expressed interest in investing. Germany and Norway are amongst the largest donors for tropical forest conservation, along with the [UK](31717.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The UK declined to fund the Tropical Forests Forever Facility at the COP30 summit.[^cite_note-12]

During a visit to [Brazil](3383.md) in December 2024, then U.S. President [Biden](145422.md) endorsed the TFFF.[^cite_note-13]

## Technical advisors

The [World Bank](36752.md), the [U.N. Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme), the [Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development](33853117.md), as well as the [Wildlife Conservation Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Conservation_Society), [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International), and Campaign for Nature are technical advisors to the TFFF.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## Proposed structure

The program would consist of two primary institutions, the TFFF and the Tropical Forest Investment Facility (TFIF).[^cite_note-\:1-4]

The TFIF is to be a \$125 billion fund,[^cite_note-\:4-9] constituted through a mix of public and private investments hosted at a [Multilateral Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_financial_institutions),[^cite_note-14] possibly the [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-\:4-9] These funds are to be invested in predominantly global south [sovereign bonds](93768.md).[^cite_note-\:3-15] The profits from the fund, after interest payments to the investors, would be dispersed to the TFFF.

The TFFF uses the funds to reward countries for protecting those forests.&#x20;

To be eligible to receive funds, a country must:

- Have [tropical moist broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests)

  [^cite_note-\:3-15]

- A deforestation rate below 0.5%

  [^cite_note-\:0-2]

- Not be a high-income country (as classified by [UNCTAD](36587.md))

  [^cite_note-\:3-15]

### Proposed disbursements

Rainforest countries would receive US\$4 for every [hectare](24919513.md) of intact forest as budget contributions.[^cite_note-16] For each hectare deforested that year, a deduction of US\$400 is made from that sum.[^cite_note-\:3-15] For each hectare degraded, US\$100 are deducted.[^cite_note-\:3-15] If deductions exceed the payout, countries are not required to pay a penalty. Previously deforested or degraded ecosystems that are fully restored become eligible for payouts again.&#x20;

20% of total disbursements are to be passed along by the receiving countries to their "indigenous peoples and local communities".[^cite_note-\:1-4]
