**Wicknell Munodaani Chivayo** (22 November 1980 – 30 September 2026), popularly known as **Sir Wicknell**, was a Zimbabwean businessman and the founder and managing director of Intratrek Zimbabwe (Private) Limited.[^cite_note-zimprofiles-1] He was known for his involvement in government tenders, his close public association with [ZANU-PF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZANU%E2%80%93PF), and his associations with Presidents of [Zimbabwe](34399.md)[^cite_note-2], [Kenya](188171.md)[^cite_note-3], [Uganda](31816.md), [Tanzania](30118.md)[^cite_note-4] and [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland), among others.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

Chivayo was reported to have been awarded state contracts in Zimbabwe through companies associated with him, with the combined value of contracts reportedly estimated at approximately US\$1 billion over the course of his business career. The reported figure refers to the stated value of contracts awarded to his companies.[^cite_note-7]

He was also associated with a series of legal proceedings relating to [fraud](58790.md) and [money laundering](19390.md) allegations, although some of the fraud proceedings resulted in his acquittal or the dismissal of charges. He was also known for public gifts to celebrities, churches, and footballers, including cash, vehicles and other items.[^cite_note-8]

Chivayo sometimes attracted controversy by being referred to as *Mukuru Wemazuva*, meaning *ancient of days* in the [Shona language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shona_language), a moniker he acquired due to public gifts to congregants of various churches within Zimbabwe.[^cite_note-9] His public gifting included donations and vehicles given to religious leaders and church communities, including substantial donations to churches and their congregants.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Early life and education

Chivayo was born on 22 November 1982 and grew up in the Gandami area of [Chivhu](3482364.md), [Mashonaland East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashonaland_East). His father, Isaac Mathias Chivayo, died in a car accident when Chivayo was approximately ten years old, after which he was raised by his mother.

He attended Dudley Hall Primary School and subsequently Churchill Boys High School for his secondary education.[^cite_note-zimhuchi-12] He left school in 1996 and worked as a wages clerk at a local bus company before becoming involved in illegal foreign currency exchange at the Union Avenue Flea Market (now Kwame Nkrumah Avenue) in [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare).[^cite_note-zimprofiles-1]

## Career

### Intratrek Zimbabwe

Chivayo was the founder and managing director of Intratrek Zimbabwe (Private) Limited, a company that has secured government contracts in the energy and infrastructure sectors. He has also been associated with IMC Communications, a telecommunications company.[^cite_note-zimprofiles-1]

### Gwanda Solar Project

In 2015, Intratrek Zimbabwe was awarded a contract — valued at approximately US\$172 million — to construct a 100-megawatt solar power plant in [Gwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanda), [Matabeleland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matabeleland_South), under a contract with the [Zimbabwe Electricity Supply Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Electricity_Supply_Authority) (ZESA) and the Zimbabwe Power Company (ZPC). Intratrek received an advance payment of US\$5 million from ZPC, but did not carry out substantive construction work on the project.[^cite_note-nehanda-gwanda-13]

Parliamentary committee reviews found that advance payment certificates had been issued without corresponding progress, and that the advance had been paid without a required Advance Payment Guarantee.[^cite_note-zimmorning-14]Chivayo faced multiple rounds of criminal prosecution over the advance payment, and was ultimately acquitted in 2024.

### ZEC election materials contract

In 2024, leaked audio recordings linked Chivayo, along with associates Mike Chimombe and Moses Mpofu, to a US\$100 million contract awarded to supply election materials to the [Zimbabwe Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Electoral_Commission) (ZEC) ahead of the [2023 Zimbabwean general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Zimbabwean_general_election). The ZACC investigated and announced in December 2025 that no evidence had been found directly implicating Chivayo in the transaction.[^cite_note-15]

## Legal issues

### 2004 criminal conviction

In 2004, Chivayo was convicted of theft by false pretences involving approximately R837,000, arising from a foreign currency transaction in which he received money but did not complete his side of the exchange. He was sentenced to three years' imprisonment at [Chikurubi Maximum Security Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikurubi_Maximum_Security_Prison?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-zimprofiles-1]

### 2011 money laundering proceedings

In 2011, Chivayo was arrested on eight counts of fraud and money laundering, and five of his vehicles were confiscated by the state. He was acquitted of all eight counts following trial.[^cite_note-zimhuchi-12]

### 2018 Gwanda Solar Project fraud proceedings

In August 2018, Chivayo was arrested and charged with fraud, money laundering, and contravening the Exchange Control Act in connection with the advance payment regarding the Gwanda Solar Project. In March 2019, High Court judge Justice Ephraim Tagu upheld Chivayo's application for exception, ruling that the facts as stated did not disclose an offence, and the acquittal was subsequently formalised at the magistrate court level.[^cite_note-lower-court-16]

The Prosecutor General appealed the acquittal. In June 2020, the [Supreme Court of Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Zimbabwe) reversed the High Court ruling and ordered that Chivayo stand trial.[^cite_note-supreme-17] Following subsequent proceedings at the magistrate court, Chivayo was again acquitted in 2024, with the court ruling that the state had failed to establish a prima facie case. A separate charge of bribing former ZPC board chairman Stanley Kazhanje with US\$10,000 to influence the tender award was also dismissed.[^cite_note-nehanda-acquit-18] In March 2025, Energy Minister [July Moyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Moyo) announced that the government was reviewing the Gwanda contract with Intratrek with a view to reviving the project under revised terms and updated technical specifications.[^cite_note-nehanda-gwanda-13]

### 2018 triangle scam proceedings

In the month of August in the year 2018, Chivayo was arrested for allegations of R1.58 million mining-equipment fraud involving Genius Kadungure that involved the supply, sourcing and delivery of mining equipment across entities between [Zimbabwe](34399.md) and [South Africa](17416221.md), with some of the entities noted to be non-existent and only existing for the execution of the triangle-like scam.[^cite_note-19] Chivayo was separately acquitted of fraud charges he faced jointly with socialite [Genius Kadungure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius_Kadungure?action=edit\&redlink=1), relating to approximately R1.5 million allegedly defrauded from Kadoma miners and ZANU-PF MP [Dexter Nduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dexter_Nduna?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### 2018 Zimbabwe Power Company bribery arrest

In November 2018, Chivayo was arrested for a separate matter relating to alleged bribery involving [Zimbabwe Power Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Electricity_Supply_Authority)[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Political associations

Chivayo was a publicly declared supporter of [ZANU-PF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZANU-PF) and stated his backing for President [Emmerson Mnangagwa](6390734.md), including publicly endorsing calls for Mnangagwa to serve beyond the then constitutional term limits.[^cite_note-nigeria-pulse-22]

In January 2025, Chivayo publicly criticised Acting President [Constantino Chiwenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantino_Chiwenga), describing him as a "failed politician" following Chiwenga's public remarks on corruption within ZANU-PF.[^cite_note-newsday-chiwenga-23]

In February 2025, Chivayo was reported to have developed close ties with Kenyan President [William Ruto](2577967.md), with media reports describing him as a frequent presence within the president's circle and noting his growing connections to the Kenyan presidency.[^cite_note-citizen-digital-24]

In June 2026, Chivayo's company, IMC Construction Kenya, secured a stake in the proposed US\$2.9 billion expansion of [Jomo Kenyatta International Airport](142547.md) (JKIA) after being included as a joint-venture partner by a Chinese consortium led by [China Communications Construction Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Communications_Construction_Company).[^cite_note-standard-jkia-25]

## Public gifts and spending

Chivayo was publicly known for high-value gifts made to musicians, journalists, sports organisations, and security forces. An investigation by Zimbabwean media reported that he spent over US\$9.3 million on luxury cars and gifts within a twelve-month period.[^cite_note-myzim-26]

Reported gifts include a 2025 Toyota Land Cruiser VXR and US\$100,000 in cash to broadcaster [Reuben Barwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuben_Barwe?action=edit\&redlink=1) in May 2025;[^cite_note-barwe-27] a 2025 Range Rover Autobiography and US\$150,000 in cash to musician [Jah Prayzah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jah_Prayzah) in May 2025;[^cite_note-jahprayzah-28] vehicles totalling R7.2 million to [Zimbabwe Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Football_Association) president [Nqobile Magwizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nqobile_Magwizi) across two occasions in 2025 and 2026;[^cite_note-zifa-gifts-29] R10.4 million and a bus to [Highlanders F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlanders_F.C.) ahead of the 2026 season;[^cite_note-highlanders-30] and 20 luxury vehicles and US\$2 million to the Zimbabwe Defence Forces, Zimbabwe Republic Police, and Zimbabwe Prisons Service in December 2025.

In January 2025, Chivayo was accused of attempting to influence [Zimbabwe Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Football_Association) (ZIFA) elections by promising vehicles and financial incentives to voters. He denied the allegations.[^cite_note-zifa-vote-31]

He purportedly sponsored [Scottland F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottland_F.C.), winners of the [2025 Zimbabwe Premier Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Zimbabwe_Premier_Soccer_League), to the amount of US\$1 million.[^cite_note-32]

## Personal life

Chivayo paid lobola for Sonja Madzikanda in July 2017 and held a formal wedding ceremony in December 2017, with the bride price reported as US\$50,000.[^cite_note-married-33] The couple divorced in 2024. Chivayo stated that the primary reason was irreconcilable tensions between Madzikanda and his family, and that he continues to financially support his former wife and their two children.[^cite_note-divorce-34]

In March 2025, Chivayo married Lucy "Lulu" Muteke. The couple held traditional and religious wedding ceremonies, including a celebration within the Johane Masowe eChishanu congregation.[^cite_note-lulu-35]

### Death

On 30 September 2026, Chivayo and his wife, Lucy, were among the five people killed in a helicopter crash in [Marondera](2272458.md), Zimbabwe. He was 43. The aircraft, carrying two pilots and three passengers, went down at approximately 17\:09 CAT.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]
