**Qalandia** or **Qalandiya** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): قلنديا), also known as **Kalandia** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): קלנדיה), is a [Palestinian](23267.md) village located in the [West Bank](33209.md), between [Jerusalem](16043.md) and [Ramallah](44132.md), just west of the [West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_barrier). The village had a population of 572 residents in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] Qalandia is also the name of a [refugee camp](400347.md), established by [UNRWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Relief_and_Works_Agency_for_Palestine_Refugees_in_the_Near_East) in 1949. It is located just east of Jerusalem municipality. [Qalandia refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalandia_refugee_camp) was built for [Palestinian refugees](74681.md) who fled from [Lydda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydda), [Ramla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramla), and Jerusalem during the [1948 Palestinian expulsion and flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight).[^cite_note-3]

## History

A large Jewish farmhouse from the late [Second Temple period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple_period) has been discovered in Qalandiya.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6] Occupied from the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) until its destruction during the [First Jewish–Roman War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War), the site includes two large structures and rock-cut industrial facilities, including wine and oil presses.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6] The presence of two [miq'vaot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikveh) and typical [stone vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_vessels_in_ancient_Judaea) indicates the site's Jewish identity.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Excavations yielded two complete [amphoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphora), several amphora fragments,[^cite_note-\:0-4] hundreds of coins, potsherds, chalk vessels, metal objects, jewelry, and various tools. Nearby exploration revealed burial caves, winepresses, cisterns, and quarries.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6]

Ancient tombs have been found at Qalandia.[^cite_note-SWP11-7] A [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) bath has been excavated, and pottery from the same period has also been located there.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

During the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states) period, it was noted that Qalandia was one of 21 villages given by King [Godfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_of_Bouillon) as a [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) to the [canons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(priest\)) of the [Holy Sepulchre](7810.md).[^cite_note-SWP11-7][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] In 1151 the [Abbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbot) leased the use of the vineyards and orchards of Qalandia to a Nemes the Syrian and his brother Anthony and their children. In return the convent was given a part of the yearly production from these fields.[^cite_note-13] In 1152 Queen [Melisende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melisende,_Queen_of_Jerusalem) exchanged villagers whom she owned for shops and two moneychanger counters in Jerusalem. All the names of the Qalandia villagers were Christian, which indicate that Qalandia was a Christian village at the time.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Ottoman era

Qalandia, like the rest of Palestine, was incorporated into the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1517. In the Ottoman [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daftar) of 1596, the village, called Qalandiya, was a part of the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* ("subdistrict") of [Al-Quds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds) which was under the administration of the *[liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* ("district") of Al-Quds. The village had a population of 15 households, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), and paid a fixed tax rate of 33.3% on wheat, barley, olives, beehives and/or goats, in addition to occasional revenues; a total of 3,900 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-16]

In 1838, it was noted as a Muslim village in the Jerusalem District.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

In 1863, the French explorer [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited the village, which he described as small [hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)) consisting of a few houses with [fig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus) plantations around them,[^cite_note-19] while an Ottoman village list of about 1870 showed 16 houses and a population of 50, though the population count included only the men.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

In 1883, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* described the village as a "small village on a swell, surrounded by olives, with [quarries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarries) to the west."[^cite_note-22]

In 1896 the population of *Kalandije* was estimated to be about 150 persons.[^cite_note-23]

### British Mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine), *Qalandieh (Qalandia)* had a population of 144, of which 122 Muslims and 22 [Jews](25955086.md).[^cite_note-Census1922-24] This had decreased in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) when *Qalandiya* had an all-Muslim population of 120, in 25 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-25]

In the [1945 survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945), Qalandia had a population of 190 Muslims,[^cite_note-1945p25-26] and a land area of 3,940 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam).[^cite_note-Hadawi58-27] 427 dunams were designated for plantations and irrigable land, 2,202 for cereals,[^cite_note-28] while six dunams were built-up.[^cite_note-29]

#### Qalandia airport

Main article: [Atarot Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarot_Airport)

An airstrip to the east of Qalandia was built by the British army in 1925. It was located a few kilometers north of Jerusalem at a site that offered flat terrain in a largely hilly region. In 1936 it was renovated by the Jewish entrepreneur [Pinchas Rutenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinchas_Rutenberg) and began to be used commercially on a limited basis by Rutenberg’s airline, Palestine Airways, and the British carrier, Imperial Airways.[^cite_note-30]

Until 1927, it was the only airport in [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), although there were several military airfields. Qalandia was used for prominent guests bound for Jerusalem.[^cite_note-31] It opened for regular flights in 1936.[^cite_note-32]



Jerusalem airport, 1961

After the [Six-Day War](29328.md), it was renamed [Atarot Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarot_Airport) by Israel, but closed down due to disturbances related to the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md), and because international companies refused to land there.[^cite_note-33] Israel confiscated 639 dunums from Qalandia village in order to establish a military base at the former airport.[^cite_note-ARIJ16-34]

### 1947–1949

During the [1947–1948 Civil War in Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947%E2%80%931948_Civil_War_in_Mandatory_Palestine), in early January 1948, the residents of Qalandia evacuated the village and moved to [Ramallah](44132.md), leaving a few young men to protect the property and make sure mines were not planted on the way leading to the village and the nearby mine.[^cite_note-35] The villagers returned to the village, and after the news of the [Deir Yassin massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Yassin_massacre) arrived, the women, the children, and most of the men were evacuated again and the village became a post of the [Arab Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Liberation_Army)[^cite_note-36] In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Qalandia came under [Jordanian](7515964.md) rule. It was [annexed by Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) in 1950.

### Qalandia refugee camp

Main article: [Qalandia Camp](56340341.md)

The Qalandia [refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_refugee_camps) was established in 1949 by the [Red Cross](15487.md)[^cite_note-37] on land leased from [Jordan](7515964.md). It covers 353 dunums (0.353 km<sup>2</sup>; 35.3 ha) as of 2006,[^cite_note-ochaoptPDF-38] and has a population of 10,024,[^cite_note-39] with 935 structures divided into 8 blocks.[^cite_note-ochaoptPDF-38] Israeli authorities consider it part of [Greater Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Jerusalem), and it remains under their control.[^cite_note-unrwa-40]

### 1967–present

Since the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Qalandia has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md).

After the [1995 accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord), 2% of Qalandiya’s land was classified as [Area B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves), while the remaining 98% is [Area C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_C_\(West_Bank\)).[^cite_note-ARIJ16-34] Israel has confiscated 574 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land from Qalandiya in order to construct the Israeli industrial settlement [Atarot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarot), and 639 dunams for the Israeli [Qalandiya military base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarot_Airport).[^cite_note-ARIJ16-34] 1,940 dunums of the village–59.3% of the village’s total area–is isolated behind the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier).[^cite_note-ARIJ17-41] In 2006, 1,154 people were living in the village according to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics).[^cite_note-42]

The [Qalandia checkpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalandia_checkpoint) is the main checkpoint between the northern West Bank and Jerusalem, and is known for frequent demonstrations against the occupation.[^cite_note-43]

The Israeli [2013 Qalandia raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Qalandia_raid) led to clashes with local residents, leaving three of Qalandia's inhabitants dead and several critically wounded.[^cite_note-44]
