***The Bear*** is an American [comedy-drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy-drama) television series created by [Christopher Storer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Storer) for [FX on Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_on_Hulu). [Jeremy Allen White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Allen_White) stars as [Carmy Berzatto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmy_Berzatto), an award-winning chef who returns to his hometown of [Chicago](6886.md) to manage the chaotic kitchen at his recently deceased brother's [Italian beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_beef) sandwich shop. The regular cast includes [Ebon Moss-Bachrach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebon_Moss-Bachrach), [Ayo Edebiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayo_Edebiri), [Lionel Boyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Boyce), [Liza Colón-Zayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liza_Col%C3%B3n-Zayas), [Abby Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abby_Elliott), [Matty Matheson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matty_Matheson), and [Edwin Lee Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Lee_Gibson).[^cite_note-1]

The [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_season_1) was released in its entirety on FX on Hulu on June 23, 2022. The [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_season_2), [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_season_3) and [fourth seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_season_4) were released in June 2023, June 2024, and June 2025, respectively. A standalone [special episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_\(The_Bear_episode\)) was released on May 5, 2026, starring Moss-Bachrach and [Jon Bernthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Bernthal), and focused solely on their characters. The [fifth and final season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_season_5) was released on June 25, 2026.

The series has received critical acclaim for its performances, writing, directing, visual style, soundtrack, and production values. Also, many real-life culinary professionals have praised the show for its sense of urgency and realistic depiction of the physical, emotional, and financial demands of restaurant life.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] *The Bear* has received many accolades, including 22 [Primetime Emmy Awards](6579025.md) (including [Outstanding Comedy Series](4719642.md)) and five [Golden Globe Awards](142724.md) (including [Best Television Series – Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Television_Series_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy)).

## Premise

Talented [haute cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute_cuisine) chef Carmy Berzatto inherits [his family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_on_The_Bear_\(TV_series\))'s Italian beef sandwich shop after his older brother [Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikey_Berzatto)'s suicide. He returns home to Chicago to run it, leaving behind his world of working in [Michelin-starred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin-starred) restaurants. He is left to deal with his brother's unresolved debts, a rundown kitchen, and an unruly staff, while dealing with his own pain and family trauma.

## Production

### Development

[FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_\(TV_channel\)) ordered a [pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_pilot) for *The Bear* in March 2021, to be written and directed by series creator [Christopher Storer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Storer).[^cite_note-12] Storer had initially conceived *The Bear* as a [feature film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_film) before being convinced the story needed time to unreel.[^cite_note-13] The pilot was filmed in July 2021.[^cite_note-14] FX then ordered the series in October 2021 for a 2022 premiere.[^cite_note-15] The sandwich shop interior is copied from the Chicago shop [Mr. Beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Beef) on Orleans Street, in [River North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_North). Storer was a frequent patron and is a friend of [Christopher Zucchero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Zucchero), the shop's current owner who inherited it from his father.[^cite_note-16]

In July 2022, the series was renewed for a 10-episode second season.[^cite_note-17] In November 2023, it was renewed for a third season.[^cite_note-18] In March 2024, the series was renewed for a fourth season.[^cite_note-19] Parts of the fourth season were filmed during filming for the third season, with production set to restart in 2025.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] In July 2025, it was renewed for a fifth and final season of eight episodes.[^cite_note-S5Renewal-22][^cite_note-23]

### Writing

The Bear of the title refers to an actual [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear) that appears in a [dream sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_sequence) in the [pilot episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_episode), Carmy's nickname "Bear" (derived from his surname), the [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bears) and the [Chicago Cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cubs), maybe the whole city [metonymically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymic), the name of the new restaurant, Carmy's girlfriend's nickname, the [collective noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_noun) for the extended family, and can be interpreted as a metaphor for trauma.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENikolova202546-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESinger2026127-26] The show's themes include masculinity and father figures,[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] team sports and collaboration,[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] menswear and good taste,[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] filmmaking and creativity,[^cite_note-33] addiction and intergenerational transfer of maladaptive behaviors,[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] the importance of a good wine list,[^cite_note-36] the importance of strong institutions,[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] the importance of regulatory easing for small business owners,[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] gentrification and class warfare,[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Catholic mysticism,[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] scars and ghosts and grief,[^cite_note-46] women without men,[^cite_note-47] mothers and women laboring,[^cite_note-48] women in the workplace,[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] dreams, and love.[^cite_note-51]

*Creative Screenwriting* magazine wrote in 2025, "...the dialogue in *The Bear* unfurls as poetry—a meditation, a stream of consciousness, a series of fragmented thoughts. It's messy, natural speech."[^cite_note-52] The dialogue is rich with profanity—one reviewer described it as a "symphony of swearing."[^cite_note-53]

### Filming

The show films in Chicago, including on location in neighborhoods such as River North, [Ukrainian Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Village), and [Wicker Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicker_Park,_Chicago), and at real Chicago restaurants, including Mr. Beef, [Ever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ever_\(restaurant\)#The_Bear), and [Kasama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasama_\(restaurant\)).[^cite_note-54] Recurring visual and thematic motifs include time and trains.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] They typically shoot for "about 39 days" or an average of about four days per episode.[^cite_note-57]

### Cinematography

The show's primary [cinematographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematographer) is [Andrew Wehde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wehde).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] A-camera operator Gary Malouf and B-camera operator Chris Dame had both worked with Storer and Wehde on their previous projects.[^cite_note-60] The show is shot with digital [Alexa Mini LF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Mini_LF) cameras and [Panavision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavision) lenses.[^cite_note-61] Two flashback episodes, "Fishes" and "Gary", were shot using film.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] The show frequently uses handheld cameras.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-Panavision-S3-65]

## Release

*The Bear* premiered on [FX on Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_on_Hulu) in the United States on June 23, 2022,[^cite_note-102] and became available internationally in the [Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_\(Disney+\)) hub on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+).[^cite_note-103] The 10-episode second season was released on June 22, 2023.[^cite_note-S2Premiere-104] Along with other [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu) content, *The Bear* became available to stream on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+) in the U.S. via the Hulu hub on December 6, 2023.[^cite_note-105] The third season was released on June 26, 2024.[^cite_note-S3Premiere-106] The fourth season was released on June 25, 2025.[^cite_note-107] A surprise special flashback episode, "Gary", was released on May 5, 2026, on Hulu.[^cite_note-108] The fifth season was released on June 25, 2026, on FX on Hulu, with a weekly broadcast on [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_\(TV_channel\)) beginning the same day,[^cite_note-S5FX-109] making it the first returning FX on Hulu series to receive a simultaneous broadcast on FX following a change in the latter's programming release strategy.[^cite_note-110]

*The Bear* has received critical acclaim.[^cite_note-129] On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), the overall series holds a 93% rating, with an average of rated reviews of 8.3/10.[^cite_note-RT-130] On [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a weighted average, the overall series has received a score of 83 out of 100.[^cite_note-MC-131]

#### Season 1

For the first season, Rotten Tomatoes reported an approval rating of 100% based on 82 critic reviews, with an average of rated review of 8.7/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "Like an expertly confected sandwich, *The Bear* assembles a perfect melange of ingredients and stacks them for optimal satisfaction—and thankfully keeps the crust-iness for extra flavor."[^cite_note-RTS1-111] Metacritic gave it a weighted average score of 88 out of 100 based on 24 critic reviews.[^cite_note-MCS1-112]

The [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute) (AFI) named it [one of the ten best television programs of the year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute_Awards#2022).[^cite_note-AFI2022-132] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* named it number one of the best 100 TV shows of 2022 and described it as "the best workplace drama since *[Mad Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_Men)*".[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] *The Bear* appeared in the top 10 on numerous publications' "Best of 2022" lists, including first for *[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club)*, *[BBC](19344654.md)*, *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\))*, and *[TVLine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVLine)*, among others.[^cite_note-135]

#### Season 2

On Rotten Tomatoes, 99% of 112 critic reviews are positive, with an average of rated review of 9.3/10 for the second season. The site's critical consensus reads, "Instead of reinventing the menu, *The Bear*'s second season wisely opts to toss its lovable characters into another frying pan of adversity, lets 'em cook, and serves up yet another supremely satisfying dish."[^cite_note-RTS2-113] Metacritic assigned it a weighted average score of 92 out of 100 based on 43 critic reviews.[^cite_note-MCS2-114]

For the second year in a row, the AFI named *The Bear* one of the ten best television programs of the year.[^cite_note-136] *The Bear* appeared on many publications' lists of the best TV shows of 2023, including *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*, *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*, and *People*, among others.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

#### Season 3

On Rotten Tomatoes, 89% of 103 critics gave the third season a positive review, with an average of rated review of 7.9/10. The website's critics consensus states, "Having set an exceedingly high standard of excellence for itself, *The Bear* spends its third season simmering, stewing, and giving off an aroma that whets the appetite."[^cite_note-RTS3-115] The website also reported that the season was "as audacious as ever" and it "still seems to continue delivering compelling and often intense television with performances that are so dependably brilliant that they're not even mentioned in most of the reviews. Instead, at this point in a show's life, it's easier to find faults in its sameness or changes, or in its attempts to repeat or outdo itself."[^cite_note-RTES3-121] Metacritic assigned it a weighted average score of 80 out of 100 based on 45 critic reviews.[^cite_note-MCS3-116]

#### Season 4

On Rotten Tomatoes, 84% of 86 critics gave the fourth season a positive review, with an average of rated review of 7.3/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "After simmering for too long, *The Bear*'s fourth season finally turns the heat back up with a renewed sense of urgency, serving a rich meal despite tiresome wait times between courses."[^cite_note-RTS4-117] Metacritic assigned it a weighted average score of 72 out of 100 based on 40 critic reviews.[^cite_note-MCS4-118]

#### Season 5

On Rotten Tomatoes, 97% of 77 critics gave the fifth season a positive review, with an average of rated reviews of 8.2/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "*The Bear* closes out its final season with the character-driven finesse that it started with, ensuring its story and fans are served the finest of televisual meals without diminished returns."[^cite_note-RTS5-119] Metacritic assigned it a weighted average score of 82 out of 100 based on 28 reviews.[^cite_note-MCS5-120]

#### Genre classification

The classification of *The Bear* as a comedy in certain award ceremonies, specifically the Primetime Emmys, has attracted criticism, given its dramatic style and focus on heavy topics including [suicide](16831059.md), [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism), [familial trauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgenerational_trauma), and workplace dysfunction.[^cite_note-231] Proponents of the series' categorization as a "comedy" argue the show unfolds as a [dark comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_comedy) marked by ridiculous situations[^cite_note-\:5-232] and comedic timing,[^cite_note-\:6-233] relies on the comedic effect inherent to chaos,[^cite_note-\:5-232] is a comedy of errors,[^cite_note-\:7-234] and is heavily characterized by back-and-forth [banter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banter)[^cite_note-\:6-233] or [snark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcasm).[^cite_note-\:7-234]

In 2024, the series became the most-nominated series in comedy categories at the [Emmys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmys) with 23 nominations, surpassing *[30 Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rock)* which had 22 nominations in 2009.[^cite_note-235] However, it went on to lose the award for [Outstanding Comedy Series](4719642.md), which it had been heavily favored to win, to *[Hacks](67541962.md)*, with *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* suggesting there was an insult among voters over its continued classification as a comedy.[^cite_note-236] In June 2025, *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_\(website\))* published an article claiming that "an undercurrent of industry resentment" over the show's lack of comedic content led to its 2024 Comedy Series Emmy loss.[^cite_note-237]

### Viewership

#### Season 1

The streaming aggregator Reelgood, which monitors real-time data from 5 million users in the U.S. for original and acquired streaming programs and movies across subscription video-on-demand (SVOD) and ad-supported video-on-demand (AVOD) services, reported that *The Bear* was the second-most-streamed program during the week of July 13.[^cite_note-\:8-238] It later rose to become the most-streamed program for the week of July 22, before ranking as the seventh most-streamed during the week of July 27.[^cite_note-\:8-238][^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] [JustWatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JustWatch), a guide to streaming content with access to data from more than 20 million users around the world, estimated that *The Bear* was the second most-streamed series in the U.S. during the week ending July 3.[^cite_note-242] It maintained the same position during the week ending July 17.[^cite_note-243] FX announced that the first season of *The Bear* was the most-watched comedy series in the network's history.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246]

#### Season 2

FX reported that the second season of *The Bear* was the most-watched season premiere in the network's history.[^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] Reelgood calculated that *The Bear* was the second most-streamed program in the U.S. during the week of June 22.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] It later rose to first place during the week of June 29.[^cite_note-252] JustWatch reported that *The Bear* was the most-streamed series in the U.S. during the week ending June 25.[^cite_note-253] [Whip Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_Media), which tracks viewership data for the more than 25 million worldwide users of its [TV Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Time) app, announced that *The Bear* was the eighth most-watched streaming original television series of 2023.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255]

#### Season 3

The season 3 premiere of *The Bear* garnered 5.4 million views in its first four days of streaming, marking the best performance for an FX premiere on Hulu and the most-watched season premiere for any scripted series on the platform. This viewership represents a 24% increase over the season 2 premiere and accounts for streaming on Hulu, Hulu on Disney+ in the U.S., and Disney+ in available international territories. *The Bear* achieved the third-largest season premiere for Hulu overall and the biggest Hulu on Disney+ premiere since the bundle's launch on March 27.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257] [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), which records streaming viewership on U.S. television screens, calculated that *The Bear* was watched for 1,233 million minutes from June 24–30.[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259] It surpassed its previous peak of 1.01 billion minutes recorded shortly after the premiere of its second season the previous year.[^cite_note-260] JustWatch reported that *The Bear* was the third most-streamed show in the U.S. in 2024, while in Canada, it ranked as the second most-streamed show of the year.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262]

#### Season 4

The premiere of *The Bear*'s fourth season was the seventh most-streamed title in the U.S. according to Nielsen, it hit 917 million minutes watched from June 23–29, marking a drop of about 24% from the 1.2 billion minutes reached during the third season's premiere the year before.[^cite_note-263]
