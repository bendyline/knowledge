The **Royal Malaysian Air Force** (**RMAF**, [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Tentera Udara Diraja Malaysia; TUDM*; [Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_alphabet): تنترا اودارا دراج مليسيا) was formed on 2 June 1958 as the **Royal Federation of Malaya Air Force** (*Tentera Udara Diraja Persekutuan Tanah Melayu*; تنترا اودارا دراج ڤرسكوتون تانه ملايو). However, its roots can be traced back to the Malayan Auxiliary Air Force formations of the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) in then-colonial [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya). The Royal Malaysian Air Force operates a mix of modern [American](3434750.md), [European](9317.md) and [Russian](25391.md)-made aircraft.

## History

### Early years



[Labuan Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Labuan_airbase) with [CAC Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAC_Sabre) in 1965, today became the main airfield for [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia).

The Malaysian air forces trace their lineage to the Malayan Auxiliary Air Force formations of the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) formed in 1934. They later transformed into the [Straits Settlements Volunteer Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_Settlements_Volunteer_Air_Force) (SSVAF) and the Malayan Volunteers Air Force (MVAF) formed in 1940 and dissolved in 1942 during the height of the [Japanese advance over Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Malaya). The latter was re-established in 1950 in time for the [Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency) and contributed very much to the war effort.

On 2 June 1958 the MVAF finally became the Royal Federation of Malaya Air Force (RFMAF), this date is celebrated as RMAF Day yearly, the Royal title granted by Parliament in honor of its contributions to national defence during the Emergency and the transition to independent status.

On 25 October 1962, after the end of the Malayan Emergency, the RAF handed over their first airfields in Malaya to the RFMAF, at [Simpang Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpang_Airport); it was opened on 1 June 1941, in [Sungai Besi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungai_Besi), [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) which was formerly part of [Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selangor) and the national capital city. The first aircraft for the fledgling air force was a [Scottish Aviation Twin Pioneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Aviation_Twin_Pioneer) named "*Lang Rajawali*" by the then Prime Minister [Tunku Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Abdul_Rahman). Several Malayans serving with the Royal Air Force transferred to the Royal Federation of Malaya Air Force. The role played by RMAF was limited initially to communications and the support of ground operations against Communist insurgents during the [Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency). RMAF received its first combat aircraft with the delivery of 20 [Canadair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair) [CL41G Tebuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair_CL-41_Tutor) (an armed version of the Canadair Tutor trainer). RMAF also received [Aérospatiale Alouette III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_III) helicopters, to be used in the liaison role.

With the formation of [Malaysia](3607937.md) on 16 September 1963, the name of the air force was changed to "Tentera Udara Diraja Malaysia" or "Royal Malaysian Air Force". New types introduced into service included the [Handley Page Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handley_Page_Herald) transport and the [De Havilland Canada DHC-4 Caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_DHC-4_Caribou). RMAF received [Sikorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Aircraft_Corporation) [S-61A-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-3_Sea_King) helicopters in the late 1960s and early 1970s which were used in the transport role.&#x20;

RMAF gained an air defence capability when the [Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia) donated 10 ex-[Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) (RAAF) [CAC Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAC_Sabre) fighters. These were based at the [Butterworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterworth,_Penang) Air Base. After the withdrawal of British military forces from Malaysia and Singapore at the end of 1971, a [Five Power Defence Arrangements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Power_Defence_Arrangements) (FPDA) agreement between Malaysia, [Singapore](27318.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Australia](4689264.md), and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) was concluded to ensure defence against external aggression. The RAAF maintained two [Mirage IIIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_III) squadrons at RAF/RAAF Station Butterworth, [Butterworth Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterworth_Air_Base) as part of its commitment to the FPDA. These squadrons were withdrawn in 1986, although occasional deployments of RAAF aircraft continue.

### Modernisation



RMAF [Sukhoi Su-30MKM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKM), [F/A-18 Hornet](45998.md), [MiG-29N](185653.md) and [BAE Hawk 208](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_Hawk_200) with [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Air_Force) [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) and [F-15C Eagle](11715.md) participating in Cope Taufan 2014.

With the withdrawal of British military forces, RMAF underwent gradual modernisation from the 1970s to the 1990s. The [Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAC_Sabre) were replaced by 16 [Northrop F-5E Tiger-IIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5). A reconnaissance capability was acquired with the purchase of two RF-5E Tigereye aircraft. RMAF also purchased 88 ex-[US Navy](20518076.md) [Douglas A-4C Skyhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk), of which 40 of the airframes were converted/refurbished by [Grumman Aircraft Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman) at [Bethpage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethpage,_New_York) into the A-4PTM ('Peculiar To Malaysia'), configuration (similar to A-4M standard). RMAF has traditionally looked to the West for its purchases, primarily to the United States. However, limitations imposed by the US on "new technology" to the region, such as the [AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) fire-and-forget air-to-air missile, has made RMAF consider purchases from [Russia](25391.md) and other non-traditional sources. The 1990s saw the arrival of first the [BAE Hawk Mk108/208](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Hawk) which replaced the T/A-4PTMs, followed by the [MiG-29N/NUB](185653.md) in 1995 in the air superiority role and delivery of the [F/A-18D Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18_Hornet) in 1997 to provide an all weather interdiction capability. In 2003 a contract was signed for 18 [Su-30MKMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKM) for delivery in 2007 to fulfill a requirement for an initial order of Multi-Role Combat Aircraft (MRCA). A requirement for a further 18 MRCAs remains unfulfilled. RMAF is also looking for an [AWACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) aircraft, although no firm orders have been placed.

On 8 December 2005, four [Airbus Military A400M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A400M) aircraft were ordered to enhance the airlift capability. By March 2017, all Malaysian A400Ms were delivered.[^cite_note-3] In late 2006, the Government signed a contract to purchase eight [Aermacchi MB-339CMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aermacchi_MB-339) to add to the eight MB-339AMs already in service.

In March 2007, then-[Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Malaysia) and Defence Minister [Najib Razak](523641.md) notified the public that the [MiG-29](185653.md) would continue in service until 2010. Later that year, Najib announced the [Sikorsky S-61A4 Nuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_SH-3_Sea_King#Variants) helicopter, in service since 1968 with 89 crew members killed in 15 accidents, would be phased out by 2012 and replaced by the [Eurocopter EC725](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC725).[^cite_note-eurocopter-4] Deputy RMAF Chief Lieutenant General Bashir Abu Bakar told the media after opening Heli-Asia 2007, that tender assessment for the replacement of the Sikorsky S-61A-4 Nuri would occur in early 2008.[^cite_note-BernamaNuri-5] At the 12th Defence Services Asia (DSA) exhibition 2010,[^cite_note-LargestDefenceExpo-6] a Letter of Agreement (LOA) was signed for 12 EC725 helicopters to be supplied to the RMAF.[^cite_note-EuroLikely-7]

The RMAF has multiple [ongoing procurement programs for combat, utility and maritime patrol aircraft](#Present_development).

## Ground and special forces

In addition to personnel assigned to aviation, airspace management, engineering, and logistics branches, the Royal Malaysian Air Force (RMAF) maintains dedicated [ground and special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force_ground_forces_and_special_forces) to support its mission. The largest of these formations is the [RMAF Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Regiment), which consists of at least three distinct units. There is also one ground unit that operates independently of the RMAF Regiment. Most personnel within these forces are recruited from the RMAF Administration branch trade ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *ketukangan cawangan tadbir*).[^cite_note-9]

### RMAF Regiment

Main article: [RMAF Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Regiment)

Established in 1980, the RMAF Regiment is the primary ground formation of the air force. Its headquarters is currently located at RMAF Jugra. The regiment is commanded by a [one-star general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-star_general) holding the appointment of Commander of the RMAF Regiment ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Panglima Rejimen TUDM*). It currently comprises three distinct units, with personnel stationed across various airbases throughout Malaysia.[^cite_note-10]

Historically, the formation was known as the **RMAF Security Regiment** ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Rejimen Keselamatan TUDM*), and its headquarters was designated as the RMAF Security Regiment Headquarters ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Markas Rejimen Keselamatan TUDM*), or **MAREJ**. The units under its command are detailed below:[^cite_note-\:0-11]

#### RMAF Special Forces



PASKAU detachments with dressed in the No.4 Digital Camouflage, tactical gear and ghillie suits parading during the 60th [National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Malaysia\)) Parade of Malaysia at Sultan Abdul Samad Street, Kuala Lumpur.

Main article: [RMAF Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Special_Forces)

Commonly known as **PASKAU** (an acronym for *Pasukan Khas TUDM*), this is the [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) of the RMAF. Formerly known as the *Pasukan Khas Udara* (lit. '**RMAF Special Air Service**'), it was formally established in 1996 by the merger of two [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations) squadrons from the RMAF Air and Ground Defence Force. The unit adopted its current name in 2002.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

PASKAU is tasked with [combat search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_search_and_rescue), responding to [aircraft hijacking](2075.md) incidents, and protecting vital RMAF airbases. Other roles include [air-to-ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-ground_weaponry) target designation, sabotage of enemy assets, and RMAF aircraft defence. The unit is also trained for [counter-terrorism](83392.md), [urban warfare](319027.md), and [close-quarters combat](356000.md). PASKAU's structure and doctrines are influenced by the [U.S. Air Force Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Special_Operations_Command), particularly units such as [Combat Controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Combat_Control_Team), [Pararescue Jumpers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Pararescue), and [Tactical Air Control Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Tactical_Air_Control_Party) (TACP).[^cite_note-12]

#### RMAF Ground Defence Force

Main article: [RMAF Ground Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Ground_Defence_Force)

The RMAF Ground Defence Force, originally the **RMAF Air and Ground Defence Force**, is the progenitor of all ground units within the RMAF. Its modern roles include [light infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_infantry), [security operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_forces), and [airmobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airmobile) (air assault/fly-away security) infantry.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-\:1-13]

The unit was established in 1980 following a [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Communist_Party) [mortar](324499.md) attack on [RMAF Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Kuala_Lumpur_Air_Base) in 1974, with the aim of strengthening airbase security. In 1983, it was renamed the **RMAF Provost** after assuming [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police) duties from the [Malaysian Army](1966311.md)’s [Royal Military Police Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Police_Corps_\(Malaysia\)). At that time, its responsibilities spanned security, special operations, provost duties, and air defence. The unit was decommissioned in 1999, with its personnel divided amongst the Provost, Special Air Service, and Ground Base Air Defence.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

Under the *Capability Development 55* (CAP55) plan, the unit was reactivated in 2020 as the **RMAF Ground Defence Force**. Its air defence responsibilities were delegated to the RMAF Ground Base Air Defence. The force is influenced by the [U.S. Air Force Security Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Security_Forces) and the British [RAF Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Regiment).[^cite_note-\:1-13]

#### RMAF Provost

The [RMAF Provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Provost?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police) unit responsible for maintaining discipline and [military justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_justice) at RMAF bases. It has a direct lineage to the RMAF Air and Ground Defence Force. Following the reinstatement of the Ground Defence Force, the RMAF Provost now focuses primarily on [provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_\(military_police\)) duties, the management of [detention depots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detention_\(confinement\)), and legal investigations. Its personnel include military investigators and military police officers, many of whom are drawn from the RMAF Ground Defence Force.[^cite_note-14]

### RMAF Ground Base Air Defence

The [RMAF Ground Base Air Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Ground_Base_Air_Defence?action=edit\&redlink=1) is an [artillery](2508.md) formation that uses [anti-aircraft weapons](146640.md) to protect air bases, [radar](25676.md) installations, and other vital assets. Established in 1995, it also traces its lineage to the original Air and Ground Defence Force. Unlike the units mentioned above, it is not part of the RMAF Regiment; instead, these units fall under the command of the specific RMAF bases where they are stationed.[^cite_note-15]

## Aerobatic team

Main article: [Kris Sakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Sakti)

The Kris Sakti (English: Magic Dagger) is the aerobatic display team of the Royal Malaysian Air Force. It made its debut on [Langkawi International Maritime and Aerospace Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Maritime_and_Aerospace_Exhibition) in December 2011. They operated four [Extra 300L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_EA-300) aircraft.[^cite_note-16]

Main article: [Smokey Bandits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smokey_Bandits?action=edit\&redlink=1)

The Smokey Bandits is the aircraft aerobatic display team under the Royal Malaysian Air Force. This team operates five [Mikoyan MiG-29](185653.md) fighter aircraft and based in [RMAF Kuantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Kuantan_Air_Base). The team's name is taken from the smoke emissions from two Russian-made fighter aircraft engines when operating at full thrust. The team was first established in 2001.[^cite_note-17]

## Present development

While the RMAF has traditionally looked to the West for its purchases, primarily the United States and Europe, it has also recently looked to Russia and other non-traditional sources for its modernisation programs.[^cite_note-19]

### Multi-role combat aircraft



RMAF [Sukhoi Su-30MKMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKM) seen from top and bottom.

Faced with aging [MiG-29](185653.md) and [F-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5) aircraft, the RMAF began its Multi-Role Combat Aircraft (MRCA) acquisition program in 2011, with a planned entry of service date between 2015 and 2016. While it was initially reported that the RMAF was seeking to procure 18 aircraft,[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-restore-21] this number rose to 24 to 40 new aircraft in 2014 with a budget reported between \$1.8 and \$2.4 billion.[^cite_note-22] According to then minister of defence [Ahmad Zahid Hamidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Zahid_Hamidi), only Western manufactured aircraft were being considered.[^cite_note-restore-21] Four companies were shortlisted; [Boeing](18933266.md), [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems), [Dassault Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Group), [Saab AB](26968.md), each offering their respective [F/A-18 Super Hornet](845012.md), [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md), [Dassault Rafale](33731380.md), [JAS 39 Gripen](87577.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-23]

The program was put on hold indefinitely in 2014 as a result of budget restrictions and the forthcoming [Eleventh Malaysia Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Malaysia_Plan), which covers government spending from 2016 to 2020. In response, Dassault offered a financial package consisting of a loan with a ten-year repayment plan guaranteed by the French government.[^cite_note-24] This was countered by Saab, which offered a leasing deal,[^cite_note-25] as did BAE Systems,[^cite_note-26] which also offered to set up a local facility for maintenance and repair with possible financing by the British government.[^cite_note-27] The Russian defence export corporation, [Rosoboronexport](3094695.md), which previously supplied the Royal Malaysian Air Force with the [Sukhoi Su-30MKM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKM) expressed its readiness to discuss the prospect of establishing joint licensed production facilities in Malaysia in 2016.[^cite_note-28]

By 2016, the choice was narrowed down to either Dassault Rafale or Eurofighter Typhoon, with the former reportedly favoured by officials within the defence ministry. However, as a result of economic uncertainty as well as the rise of Islamist militancy in the region (see the [Siege of Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Marawi) in the neighbouring [Philippines](23440.md)), immediate priority shifted from procuring new combat aircraft to increasing surveillance capability and the MRCA program was put on hold again with the a final decision only expected as late as 2020.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

With economic difficulties hindering the completion of MRCA program, then deputy defence minister [Ikmal Hisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikmal_Hisham_Abdul_Aziz) announced that the government would seek to purchase the [Kuwait Air Force](4641117.md)'s F/A-18C/D Hornet fleet, which it was phasing out in favour of newer aircraft in 2021.[^cite_note-32] Pending US government approval, the defence ministry intends on acquiring as many as 30 aircraft.[^cite_note-33] In June 2025, Air Force Chief-of-Staff [General Asghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohd_Asghar_Khan_Goriman_Khan) announced that the US had finally approved the sale of Kuwait's fleet of F/A-18C/D Hornet's to Malaysia, with deliveries expected to be completed by 2027 after the Kuwait Air Force has first brought their replacement fleet of [Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet](845012.md) up to full operational capability.[^cite_note-34]

It was reported in August 2025 that the deal to acquire Kuwaiti Hornets was cancelled as Kuwait itself faced delays in procuring the Super Hornet, a deal which had been pending since 2017 and had been delayed by multiple factors, including the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and [US Navy](20518076.md)'s requirement of new Super Hornets taking priority over export orders. Furthermore, upon initial assessment of the Kuwaiti Hornets by RMAF personnel, it was realized that those aircraft had older software and radars than Malaysia's existing Hornets and had to be upgraded before being inducted into the Malaysian fleet which would cost US\$4 million per aircraft and at least a year of work to bring them up to standard. Defence analysts in Malaysia pointed out that even when integrated into the Air Force, the Kuwaiti fleet would only have a few years before having to being retired with no real return on investment for upgrading the aircraft. Malaysia instead is now focusing on acquiring fifth generation combat aircraft as a long term solution to supplement its defense needs.[^cite_note-35] The next day, RMAF chief General [Muhamad Norazlan Aris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhamad_Norazlan_Aris) dismissed the report and said that RMAF is still considering acquiring the Kuwaiti Hornets.[^cite_note-36] A team of eight RMAF personnel was sent to the [Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base](5306887.md) on 11–26 November 2025 to evaluate the Kuwaiti aircraft. General Norazlan also personally inspected some of the Hornets.[^cite_note-37] Malaysian government formally cancelled the Kuwaiti Hornets procurement on 6 February 2026. The plan was cancelled due to delays of the aircraft projected delivery and operational timeline, and uncertainty of their maintenance and support arrangements in the future.[^cite_note-38]

### Light combat aircraft

In 2018, the Light Combat Aircraft (LCA) program began with the aim of replacing the RMAF's [Aermacchi MB-339](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aermacchi_MB-339) and [BAE Systems Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Hawk) fleet with 36 new aircraft as fighter-lead in trainers as well as light combat aircraft.[^cite_note-39] Eight designs were submitted during the initial phase of the program, with the major contenders being the [Alenia Aermacchi M-346 Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_Aermacchi_M-346_Master), [HAL Tejas](749967.md) and [KAI T-50 Golden Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle), while the [Aero L-39NG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39NG), [Boeing T-7 Red Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_T-7_Red_Hawk), [Hongdu L-15 Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_JL-10), [Yakovlev Yak-130](1513240.md) and [CAC/PAC JF-17 Thunder](1928578.md) were considered unlikely to be selected owing to their relatively new designs as well as the possibility of [United States Office of Foreign Assets Control](231045.md) sanctions.[^cite_note-40] The [Saab Gripen](87577.md) was later added onto the RMAF's list of aircraft being considered, replacing the Boeing T-7 Red Hawk.[^cite_note-hogging-41]

The program in its initial form was approved in 2019 with funding slated for 2021.[^cite_note-hogging-41] It was renamed the Fighter-Lead In Trainer/Light Combat Aircraft (FLIT-LCA) program in the government's defence white paper in 2020 and launched as an open tender, a first for military aircraft procurement. Six designs were submitted, Alenia Aermacchi M-346 Master, HAL Tejas, KAI T-50 Golden Eagle, Hondu L-15 Falcon, [Mikoyan MiG-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_MiG-35) and [TAI Hürjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_H%C3%BCrjet).[^cite_note-42] The tender was eventually won by the KAI T-50 Golden Eagle in 2022 but challenged and reported to the [Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission](25431607.md), as the decision to award the tender was made by a caretaker government.[^cite_note-43] The subsequent Malaysian government eventually placed an order for 18 'Block 20' variants, which feature an [AESA radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_Electronically_Scanned_Array), a [Link 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_16) tactical data link, the capability for [inflight refueling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflight_refueling) and a [Sniper Advanced Targeting Pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper_Advanced_Targeting_Pod) in 2023 with a deal worth \$920 million and the deliveries set to begin by October 2026 and lasting until June 2027.[^cite_note-44] It is likely that another 18 KAI T-50 Golden Eagles will be ordered in 2027, which would bring the total up to 36 aircraft.[^cite_note-45]

In March 2024, it was reported that six Royal Malaysian Air Force pilots would begin training in early 2026 on the FA-50 at a [Republic of Korea Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea_Air_Force) airbase in [Gwangju](323128.md).[^cite_note-46]

### Maritime patrol aircraft

The RMAF expressed a need for increasing its maritime patrol capability with new aircraft as early as 2011, but budgetary constraints meant approval for new acquisitions wasn't given until 2017.[^cite_note-47] Four designs were shortlisted to replace the RMAF's aging fleet of [Beechcraft Super King Air](9023483.md), namely the [Airbus](26220236.md) [C-295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA_C-295), [Boeing](18933266.md) [P-8 Poseidon](731169.md), [ATR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)) [72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72) and [CASA/IPTN CN-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA/IPTN_CN-235), which can be acquired from [CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)) (an Airbus subsidiary) or [Indonesian Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Aerospace), with procurement to begin in 2021.[^cite_note-48] It was reported as late as 2019 that the Japanese government had offered its surplus [P-3C Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion) aircraft but that it was unlikely the Malaysian government would accept.[^cite_note-49]

In 2018, the Malaysian government signed a contract with Indonesian Aerospace to convert three of the RMAF's existing CN-235 transport planes into maritime patrol aircraft.[^cite_note-50] In 2020, funding was secured from the United States' Maritime Security Initiative (MSI) and conversions began the same year,[^cite_note-51] and all three aircraft were formally handed over in 2024.[^cite_note-52]

In 2022, it was reported that the Malaysian government had finalised a tender[^cite_note-53] and a contract worth \$172 million for two ATR 72 MP was awarded to [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_\(company\)), one of ATR's parent companies in 2023.[^cite_note-54] The deal, described by the Malaysian defence minister as the first phase, will see deliveries begin in 2026.[^cite_note-55]

### Airborne early warning and control aircraft

In 2018, the RMAF announced its Capability Plan 2025 (CAP55), which details its plans up to 2025. It intends to operate a squadron of [airborne early warning and control aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEW\&C), which it currently does not possess. Saab had previously offered two [Saab 340 AEW\&C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_340_AEW\&C) aircraft in a package deal with its Gripen fighters as part of the MRCA program in 2014.[^cite_note-56] Saab has also previously signed [Memorandums of Understanding](1018279.md) (MoU) concerning airborne early warning and control systems with Malaysian company [DRB-HICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DRB-HICOM), which owns [DefTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DefTech), a local defence contractor.[^cite_note-57]

### Medium-altitude long-endurance unmanned aerial vehicle

As a part of the modernization program, Malaysia also intends to acquire six [MALE UAV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-altitude_long-endurance_unmanned_aerial_vehicle).[^cite_note-58] Malaysian government has issues a MALE UAV tender in 2020 and it is expected the procurement will take place in 2021.[^cite_note-59] The major contenders of this project would be the [General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md), [TAI Anka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka), [Bayraktar TB2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_TB2), [Safran Patroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safran_Patroller), [Thales Watchkeeper WK450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Watchkeeper_WK450), [Kronshtadt Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronshtadt_Orion), [CAIG Wing Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAIG_Wing_Loong) and [CASC Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASC_Rainbow).[^cite_note-60]

As of March 2022, it is reported the program is currently undergoing physical evaluation stage which has been shortlisted by the Procurement Board of the Ministry of Defence, and this process is still ongoing.[^cite_note-61]

In October 2022, Malaysia's Defence Minister, [Hishammudin Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hishammuddin_Hussein) announced that the [TAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Aerospace_Industries) [Anka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka) had been selected for the procurement of three MALE-UAS units.[^cite_note-62] According to Minister of Defence [Mohamed Khaled Nordin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Khaled_Nordin), the Anka-S UAV is set to be received in February 2026.[^cite_note-63] They would be based at [RMAF Labuan Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Labuan_Air_Base) on the northern part of [Borneo](4518.md) to provide maritime surveillance over the [South China Sea](74209.md) in general and [Malaysia’s Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Malaysia) specifically.[^cite_note-64]

### Utility helicopter

In 2007, [Najib Razak](523641.md) announced that the [Sikorsky S-61A4 Nuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_SH-3_Sea_King#Variants) helicopter in service since 1968 would be phased out by 2012 and replaced by the [Eurocopter EC725](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC725).[^cite_note-eurocopter-4] Deputy RMAF Chief Lieutenant General Bashir Abu Bakar told the media after opening Heli-Asia 2007 that tender assessment for the replacement of the Sikorsky S-61A-4 Nuri would occur in early 2008.[^cite_note-BernamaNuri-5] At the 12th Defence Services Asia (DSA) exhibition 2010,[^cite_note-LargestDefenceExpo-6] a Letter of Agreement (LoA) was signed for 12 EC725 helicopters to be supplied to the RMAF.[^cite_note-EuroLikely-7] With that, [EADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS), (the European Aeronautical Defence and Space Company), pledged 100 million Euros to set up a comprehensive helicopter centre in Subang for an aeronautical academy, training, simulation and a maintenance, repair and overhaul facility for the Eurocopter EC725 military version and the Eurocopter EC225 civilian model.[^cite_note-65]

Although there was a plan to replace all the Sikorsky S-61A-4 Nuri, due to budget constraints the government only managed to buy 12 Eurocopter EC725. For this reason, Nuri helicopters were still active in service until 2019. In LIMA 2019, Malaysia also expressed interest to buy [Mil Mi-171](1493374.md) (modified armed version for Mil Mi-17) from Russia. It was reported on 7 January 2020 that the RMAF grounded its Nuri helicopters and RMAF General Ackbal Abdul Samad remarked that there was an evaluation of a new utility helicopter to replace all the remaining Nuri.[^cite_note-66]

In 2021, four AgustaWestland AW139 were leased from a Malaysian aviation company, [Weststar Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weststar_Aviation), as temporary replacement before the eventual procurement of new 24 helicopters to replace the S-61A4 Nuri.[^cite_note-67] Another four units were leased on 2025 bring the total of eight aircraft.[^cite_note-68]

### Ground-based radar

In 2019, it is confirmed that RMAF sought for three new ground-based radar. The major contenders of this project would be the [Thales Ground Master 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Master_400), [Selex RAT-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selex_RAT-31DL), [Giraffe radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraffe_radar) and [Lockheed Martin TPS-77 MMR](3579319.md).[^cite_note-69]

In 2021, it is reported the [US Department of Defense](7279897.md) has 'gifted' an export version of [Lockheed Martin TPS-77 MMR](3579319.md) to Malaysia in order to provide secure interoperable C3I (Command Control Communications and Intelligence) and sensor systems supporting U.S. Joint Air Operations to US Government FMS (Foreign Military Sales) customers.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Through [LIMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Maritime_and_Aerospace_Exhibition) 2023, Malaysia has awarded [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group) for the procurement of one unit [Ground Master 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Master_400) radar for the RMAF.[^cite_note-72] In LIMA 2025, Malaysia has awarded Thales for another two Ground Master 400 radar.[^cite_note-73]

### Medium-range air defence

In 2021, it was confirmed that RMAF sought for a regiment of medium-range air defence system to improve the national air defence capabilities.[^cite_note-74] The contenders for the program are still unknown.

In 2014, a Malaysian private company signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with a Chinese defence firm to acquire Chinese [LY-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-16) medium range air defence missile, but the government of Malaysia did not endorse it.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

## Incidents and accidents

- Since 1989, around 95 armed forces personnel (most of those are the RMAF) have been killed in 18 crashes involving the ageing type American-made [Sikorsky S-61A4 Nuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_SH-3_Sea_King#Variants) helicopter.

  [^cite_note-77]

  &#x20;This led the RMAF to purchase the French-made [EC725](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC_725) helicopter to replace it. But with the nation having ordered only 12 of a planned 28 EC725 helicopters as replacements, the RMAF was forced to prolong the life of its Sikorskys.

  [^cite_note-nuri_replacement_and_upgrade-78]

  &#x20;On October 21, 2016, it was reported that a Canadian helicopter company Heli-One will upgrade all Malaysia's *Nuri* helicopter.

  [^cite_note-79]

- On 2 September 1998, a [Mikoyan MiG-29](185653.md) suffered a hydraulic failure during a flight and a battery failure when it was returning to base. The aircraft crashed into a forested area and the pilot survived. Subsequent checks on all MiG-29 engines showed that they did not meet manufacturer specifications.

  [^cite_note-80]

- On 9 November 2004, a Mikoyan MiG-29 crashed into an oil palm plantation in [Kemaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemaman), [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu). The pilot survived the crash.

  [^cite_note-Kronologi_nahas_pesawat_TUDM_sejak_1991-81]

- In May 2008, two J85-GE-21 engines that power the [Northrop F-5E Tiger II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5) fighter jets belonging to the Royal Malaysian Air Force were reported missing, as of sometime in 2007, from an RMAF warehouse in Kuala Lumpur during Najib's tenure as [Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Malaysia\)) in [Abdullah Ahmad Badawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Ahmad_Badawi)'s cabinet. The jet engines belonged to the 12th Squadron (Scorpion) based in Butterworth. The issue became a matter of political dispute

  [^cite_note-82]

  &#x20;and it was reported a brigadier-general together with 40 other armed forces personnel had been sacked over the incident.

  [^cite_note-83]

  &#x20;Further investigation led to the arrest of two RMAF personnel and a civilian contractor were charged in connection with the theft and disposal of both engines on 6 January 2010.

  [^cite_note-84]

  &#x20;On February 5, 2010, [Attorney-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Malaysia) [Abdul Gani Patail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Gani_Patail) revealed that the two missing engines had been found in [Uruguay](31843.md) with the help of the [Government of Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uruguay) and the [Malaysian government](9660028.md) is proceeding with the necessary measures to secure their return. Investigations showed that the engines were taken out of the RMAF base between 20 December 2007 to 1 January 2008 before being sent to a warehouse in [Subang Jaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subang_Jaya) to be shipped out of Malaysia to [South America](26769.md).

  [^cite_note-85]

  [^cite_note-86]

- On 25 March 2010, a [Pilatus PC-7 Mark II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-7) from the Air Force College, RMAF Kepala Batas crashed into a forest reserve near the sports complex in [Universiti Utara Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universiti_Utara_Malaysia), Sintok approximately during 10\:15 a.m. The pilot, Major Muhammed Zulrihan Jusofin was killed in the crash during a [flypast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flypast) in conjunction of the 23rd Convocation Festival held in Universiti Utara Malaysia.

  [^cite_note-87]

- On 26 February 2016, an engine failure caused a [CN-235-220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPTN_CN-235) to make an [emergency landing](1914622.md) in a [mangrove swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) near [Kuala Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Selangor).

  [^cite_note-swamp_crash-88]

  &#x20;The co-pilot sustained a broken left arm during the incident while the rest of the crew managed to escape without any major injuries. A fisherman who were trying to help the crews out of the aircraft were reported drowned after his foot got stuck in the mud. The [black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box) from the aircraft was eventually found on the next day and was sent to [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung), Indonesia for it to be analyzed by the Indonesian manufacturer over the cause of the accident.

  [^cite_note-89]

- On 17 May 2016, an [Aermacchi MB-339](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aermacchi_MB-339) crashed into a paddy field near Nenasi, Pahang during a training mission. Both crew members managed to eject from the aircraft, with one suffering a broken leg.

  [^cite_note-90]

- On 21 December 2016, a [Beechcraft King Air 200T](9023483.md) crashed into [Butterworth airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Butterworth) during a training mission, killing one pilot and leaving three others injured.

  [^cite_note-base_crash-91]

- On 14 June 2017, two pilots were killed after a [BAE Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Hawk) 108 crashed at the [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang) - [Terengganu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu) border.

  [^cite_note-pilots_killed-92]

  &#x20;Previously, several other Hawks had crashed during training missions.

  [^cite_note-93]

- On 16 November 2021, at around 10\:07pm, a British-made [BAE Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Hawk) 108 (M40-08) crashed on the runway of Butterworth airbase,

  [^cite_note-INSIDEN_KEJADIAN_KEMALANGAN_PESAWAT_HAWK_TUDM-94]

  [^cite_note-95]

  &#x20;killing one of the pilots.

  [^cite_note-Pesawat_TUDM_terhempas:_Hishammuddin_zahir_ucapan_takziah-96]

  [^cite_note-97]

- On 6 May 2025, the front landing gear of a BAE Hawk 208 (M40-26) collapsed as it touched down on the runway, landing on its nose in Alor Setar, Kedah. The aircraft had been participating in a training exercise ahead of the opening ceremony for the [Langkawi International Maritime and Aerospace Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Maritime_and_Aerospace_Exhibition) 2025.

  [^cite_note-98]

- On 23 June 2025, a CN-235-220's nose landing gear collapsed on the runway at Kuching International Airport around 11\:30 am. The airport was closed from 11\:00 am to 3\:00 pm.

- On 21 August 2025, the engines of an F/A-18D (M45-07) caught fire soon after taking off from the runway of the [RMAF Kuantan Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMAF_Kuantan_Air_Base), resulting in the crash of the aircraft. The incident occurred at 9\:05 p.m. local time. Both the pilot and weapons system officer ejected safely with minor injuries. An investigation was conducted and the crash was caused by a [bird strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike).
