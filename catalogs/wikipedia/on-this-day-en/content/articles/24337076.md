&#x20;The **Catholic sexual abuse scandal in Europe** has affected several dioceses in European nations. This article summarises reported cases of sexual abuse perpetrated by clergy and representatives of the Catholic Church by country and diocese.

Italy is an exceptional case as the 1929 [Lateran Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateran_Treaty) gave the Vatican legal autonomy from Italy, giving the clergy recourse to Vatican rather than Italian law.

It has been reported that, ever since Pope Francis was elected pope, the Vatican gradually increased its efforts to deal with clerical sexual abuse.[^cite_note-lowen-1] For example, in 2019, it discontinued the application of "[pontifical secrecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_secrecy)" to sex abuse legal proceedings; this measure affects all countries.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Austria

### Archdiocese of Vienna

In 1995, [Cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\)) [Hans Hermann Groër](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Hermann_Gro%C3%ABr) stepped down as head of the Roman Catholic Church in Austria following accusations of sexual misconduct. In 1998, he left the country. He remained a cardinal.[^cite_note-4]

#### Diocese of Sankt Pölten

Bishop [Kurt Krenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Krenn) resigned from his post in 2004 after there was a scandal concerning [child pornography](25211885.md) allegedly being downloaded by a student at the seminary.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Up to 40,000 photos and an undisclosed number of films, including child pornography, were found on the computer of one of the seminarians, but Krenn earlier angered many by calling the images a "childish prank".[^cite_note-7]

##### Kremsmuenster Abbey

In March 2010, several monks were suspended at Kremsmunster Abbey, located in the Upper Austria city of Kremsmunster, for severe allegations of sexual abuse and physical violence. The reported incidents ranged over a period from the 1970s until the late 1990s and had been subject to police investigation.[^cite_note-8] In July 2013 an Austrian court found Kremsmuenster Abbey director Alfons Mandorfer guilty in 24 documented cases of child abuse and sexual violence.[^cite_note-usatodayaustria-9] The now laicized priest, who was accused of committing "sexual acts of differing intensity" on the pupils between 1973 and 1993, was sentenced to twelve years in prison.[^cite_note-usatodayaustria-9] By 2013, the school had paid approximately €700,000 in compensation.[^cite_note-10]

## Belgium

Abuse affairs have affected several Belgian dioceses, which were hurt by allegations of abuse similar to those found in other Western countries. In response to this, an **independent commission** was established by the [Belgian Episcopal Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roman_Catholic_dioceses_\(structured_view\)#Episcopal_Conference_of_Belgium) in 2000 under the presidency of [Godelieve Halsberghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godelieve_Halsberghe?action=edit\&redlink=1), a retired [magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate). A total of more than 300 complaints were made to the commission. The commission ultimately dealt with 33 formal complaints. 32 complaints were upheld by the commission, 1 was ruled false. Of these, only 1 case came before a court, because the facts of the other 31 cases were concerned with statutes of limitation. In about half of the 32 cases, the alleged abusers, clerics and religious, refused to appear before the commission, due to a lack of cooperation from the Belgian episcopate in forcing hierarchical obeyance to do so. The president and numerous commission members resigned from the commission as a result.

A second Independent Commission was established in 2009 under the presidency of [psychiatrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatrist) [Peter Adriaenssens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Adriaenssens?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-De_Standaard,_17–18_April_2010-11] He resigned his commission in the aftermath of the large-scale police raid on 24 June 2010. Judicial police searched the archbishopric palace in [Mechelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechelen) whilst the Belgian episcopal conference was officially meeting. Further searches were conducted at the archbishopric cathedral of Mechelen, the private residence of former archbishop [Godfried Danneels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfried_Danneels) in Mechelen and the offices of the independent commission in [Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven). 450 internal dossiers were confiscated.[^cite_note-12]

## France

Abuse affairs have also affected the Church in France.[^cite_note-42] On 3 June 2019, the French Catholic Church activated a sex abuse commission—made up of 22 legal professionals, doctors, historians, sociologists and theologians—which will obtain witness statements and deliver its conclusions by the end of 2020.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] In July 2019, the Holy See waived the diplomatic immunity of its [nuncio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuncio) to France [Luigi Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Ventura), who has been under investigation for sex abuse and will likely face criminal charges in Paris.[^cite_note-45] Despite an ongoing criminal investigation in Paris, it was revealed on 30 September 2019 that Ventura no longer resides in France and now lives in [Rome, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome,_Italy).[^cite_note-46]

On 9 November 2019, the large majority of the 120 members of the [Conference of French Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops'_Conference_of_France) (CEF) approved a resolution agreeing that every French Bishop will pay compensation for sex abuse committed in their Diocese.[^cite_note-francecompensation-47][^cite_note-48] The size of the compensations was determined in April 2020.[^cite_note-francecompensation-47] In June 2020, the Independent Commission on Sexual Abuse in the Church (CIASE), which was set up in June 2019, concluded that 3,000 children in France were sexually abused by Catholic clergy and officials since 1950 and that there was an average of 40 victims per year.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] On 11 November 2020, Jean-Marc Sauve, the head of the independent commission set up by the Catholic Church in France to investigate claims of sex abuse, acknowledged the commission's sex abuse hotline, which closed on 31 October 2020, received 6,500 calls reporting sex abuse in a period 17 months.[^cite_note-51] On 2 March 2021 the head of a commission examining sexual abuse in France's Catholic Church put the possible number of child victims at more than 10,000.[^cite_note-52]

On 23 July 2020, the Paris Prosecutor's Office announced that Ventura had been criminally charged with sexually assaulting four men in 2018 and 2019 and will stand trial in Paris starting 10 November 2020.[^cite_note-venturatrial-53][^cite_note-54] Ventura's lawyer stated that during the trial, Ventura would return to Paris and make in-person court appearances.[^cite_note-55] However, Ventura's trial began in Paris on the scheduled date without his presence.[^cite_note-notpledge-56][^cite_note-trialbegins-57] Despite Ventura's absence, which was justified by a doctor's note pointing out the dangers of traveling to France due to the country's surging number of COVID-19 cases, a motion filed by Ventura's lawyers to postpone the trial was denied and the trial proceeded with several men accusing Ventura of groping them, including a Catholic seminarian, testifying in court.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-trialgoeson-59] On 15 November 2020, it was reported that as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, Ventura, who remained in Rome throughout the duration of his Paris trial, "could not return to his trial".[^cite_note-60] A verdict was announced on 16 December 2020,[^cite_note-61] and saw Ventura convicted and handed an eight-month suspended prison sentence.[^cite_note-62]

On 3 October 2021, a commission released a report stating that since the 1950s, there have been about 3,000 pedophiles among the ranks of Catholic clergy in France. The report found the estimation among more than 115,000 priests and religious officials.[^cite_note-63] according to this report About 216,000 children are estimated to have been sexually abused by thousands of French Catholic priests, rising to 330,000 if abuse by lay members is included.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Pope Francis said that he was shamed by the church's failure to deal with paedophile priests in France.[^cite_note-francshame-66]

Archbishop [Éric de Moulins-Beaufort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_de_Moulins-Beaufort) of [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims), president of the [Bishops' Conference of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops'_Conference_of_France), expressed "shame and horror" at the report, but later caused outrage by rejecting the commission's recommendation to require priests to inform police of any child abuse cases if the information had been given under the [seal of confession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_confession), saying that the secrecy of confession was in a way above the laws of the Republic, "a free space for speaking before God". This statement was in line with 2020 Vatican guidance on clerical child abuse cases, which state that any crime discovered during confession is subject to "the strictest bond of the sacramental seal".[^cite_note-chrisafis-67] However, [Interior minister](871703.md) [Gérald Darmanin](54070416.md) told de Moulins-Beaufort that professional secrecy did not apply to such cases: "there is no law that is superior to the laws of the National Assembly and the Senate ... The French Republic respects all religions from the moment they respect the Republic and the laws of the Republic." De Moulins-Beaufort then issued an apology, and the Bishops Conference said that the church should review its practises given the scale of the abuse, and that work was needed "to reconcile the nature of confession with the need to protect children".[^cite_note-chrisafis-67]

### Archdiocese of Lyon

What he's trying to do, obviously, is protect the pope."[^cite_note-Irish_Times-117]

Referring both to this case and also to reports of sex abuse cases linked to the Regensburger Domspatzen choir, directed for 30 years by the pope's brother Mgr [Georg Ratzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Ratzinger), Holy See senior spokesman Fr [Federico Lombardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Lombardi) argued at the weekend that the pope had been the victim of a media witch-hunt. ## Poland

During 2013, reports of a succession of child sex abuse scandals within the church, some of which reached the courts, and the poor response by the church, became a matter of public concern. Responding to criticism of the church President of the [Polish Episcopal Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Episcopal_Conference), Archbishop [Jozef Michalik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozef_Michalik), said "Often that inappropriate approach or abuse is released when the child is looking for love. It clings, it seeks. It loses itself and also draws in that second person." This comment was heavily criticised, and Michalik apologised for it. The church resisted demands to pay compensation to victims.[^cite_note-ft-20131011-204][^cite_note-telegraph-20140711-205]

On 27 September 2018, however, Bishop [Romuald Kamiński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romuald_Kami%C5%84ski) of the Diocese of Warsaw-Praga stated that Polish church leaders were working on a document, to be published later, on priestly sexual abuse of minors in Poland, and ways to prevent it. Cases were being evaluated by Warsaw courts, and the priests involved were banned from working with minors; three were suspended from all pastoral work.[^cite_note-polandsexabuse-206] According to [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Gniezno) [Wojciech Polak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Polak), who serves as the [head of Poland's Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_archbishops_of_Gniezno_and_primates_of_Poland), the document will include data on the scale of priestly sex abuse in Poland.[^cite_note-polandsexabuse-206]

On 8 October 2018, a victims group mapped out 255 cases of alleged sex abuse in Poland.[^cite_note-207]

Statistics were released on 14 April 2019, commissioned by the [Episcopal Conference of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Conference_of_Poland) and with data from over 10,000 local parishes. It was found that from 1990 to mid-2018, abuse reports about 382 priests were made to the Church, with 625 children, mostly under 16, sexually abused by members of the Catholic clergy. There were opinions that the figures underestimated the extent of the problem, and failed to answer questions church officials had avoided for years.[^cite_note-nyt190314-208] Marek Lisinski, the co-founder of *Don't Be Afraid*, which represents victims of clerical abuse, said "Tell us how \[the priests] hurt those children and how many times they were transferred to different parishes before you paid notice". The data were released a few weeks after [Pope Francis](1687680.md) had called for "an all-out battle against the abuse of minors". After pressure from the Pope, in the preceding years Poland's church had publicly apologized to abuse victims, and accepted the need to report those accused of such crimes. In earlier times clergy to whom sexual abuse of minors was reported were not required by their superiors to notify the police, but to investigate themselves, and if necessary inform the Vatican.[^cite_note-nyt190314-208]

On 11 May 2019, Polak issued an apology on behalf of the entire Catholic Church in Poland.[^cite_note-abcnews19-209][^cite_note-210] The same day, *[Tell No One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_No_One_\(2019_film\))*, a documentary detailing accounts of sex abuse by Catholic church workers in Poland, went viral, obtaining 8.1 million viewers on [YouTube](3524766.md) by 13 May.[^cite_note-may13-211] Among other things, the film alleges that Rev. Dariusz Olejniczak, a priest who was sentenced for molesting seven-year-old girls, was allowed to continue working with young people despite his conviction.[^cite_note-abcnews19-209] The film also accused former Polish leader Lech Wałęsa's personal priest Franciszek Cybula, who is now deceased, of sexual abuse and noted that he transferred between parishes.[^cite_note-abcnews19-209] On 14 May 2019, Poland's ruling Law and Justice (PiS) party, which has long had an alliance with the nation's Catholic Bishops,[^cite_note-may13-211] agreed to increase penalties for child sex abuse by raising the maximum prison sentence from 12 years to 30 years and raising the age of consent from 15 to 16.[^cite_note-212] Prosecutor and PiS lawmaker Stanislaw Piotrowicz, who heads the Polish Parliament's Justice Commission, has also been criticized for playing down the actions of a priest who was convicted for inappropriately touching and kissing young girls.[^cite_note-213]

On 16 May 2020, Polak asked the Vatican to investigate sex abuse claims involving brothers Marek and Tomasz Sekielski.[^cite_note-2020brothers-214] The two brothers released a popular YouTube documentary titled *Hide and Seek*, which detailed their allegations that they were molested by a Polish Catholic priest.[^cite_note-2020brothers-214] Polak expressed support the allegations, stating "The film... shows that protection standards for children and adolescents in the Church were not respected."[^cite_note-2020brothers-214]

In 2009, Dom David Pearce, a monk of [Ealing Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ealing_Abbey) and former headmaster of the junior department of its associated school, [St Benedict's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Benedict's_School,_Ealing), was sentenced to eight years in prison for sexually abusing boys.[^cite_note-269] In April 2006, civil damages were awarded jointly against Pearce in relation to an alleged assault by Pearce on a pupil while teaching at the school in the 1990s, although [criminal charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_charges) were dropped.[^cite_note-270]

In October 2017, Andrew Soper (known as Father Laurence), former abbot of Ealing Abbey, was found guilty on 19 sexual offences against pupils of St Benedict's school in the 1970s and 1980s.[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272]

##### Downside School

In 2004, a Benedictine monk was jailed for 18 months after taking indecent images of schoolboys and possessing child pornography when he was a teacher at [Downside School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downside_School).[^cite_note-273] In January 2012, Father Richard White, a monk who formerly taught at the school, was jailed for five years for gross indecency and indecent assault against a pupil in the late 1980s. White, 66, who was known to pupils as Father Nick, had been allowed to continue teaching after he was first caught abusing a child in 1987 and was able to go on to groom and assault another pupil in the junior school. He was placed on a restricted ministry after the second incident, but was not arrested until 2010. Two other Downside monks, also former teachers, received police cautions during an 18-month criminal trial.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275]

In May 2020, it was revealed that a 2018 Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse (IICSA) report regarding child sex abuse at Downside School later resulted major financial problems for the school due to spiralling legal costs.[^cite_note-downsideburden-276] In order to raise money, Downside was forced to sell some of its Renaissance-era paintings.[^cite_note-downsideburden-276]

#### Case of Francis McDermott

In March 2019, Father Francis McDermott was sentenced to 9+1⁄2 years in prison for molesting six children in London, Norwich and High Wycombe between 1971 and 1978.[^cite_note-277]

### Scotland

Further information: [Scottish Child Abuse Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Child_Abuse_Inquiry)

Child sex abuse has affected many different Scottish diocese and the credibility of the Church has been damaged. Some Catholics lost faith due to the scandal.[^cite_note-Reid-278]

One notable case was an unnamed woman many times locked in a darkened room by a sexually abusive nun who was her carer. Aged 8 she told a priest about the abuse during [Confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrament_of_Penance_\(Catholic_Church\)). After that the priest and the nun raped her together. There are allegations that at [Fort Augustus Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Augustus_Abbey#Allegations_of_abuse) there were physical beating, verbal humiliation and sexual abuse. Carlkemp [prep school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_school_\(United_Kingdom\)), a feeder school preparing younger pupils for Fort Augustus is also implicated. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* and the *[BBC](19344654.md)* both reported complaints that the Scottish Church hierarchy did not cooperate fully over investigations of child sex abuse.[^cite_note-279] Alan Draper of [Dundee University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee_University) accused the Scottish Catholic Church of reluctance to expose priests leading double lives including those accused of sex abuse. Draper revealed bishops knew of 20 cases from 1985 to 1995 but refused to bring in experts. Draper wants relevant files given to a judicial enquiry.[^cite_note-280] Public offers of support from the Church for abuse victims are met with private lack of support and an adversarial attitude when legal action is involved. Draper alleges this contrasts with protection, therapy and financial help traditionally provided for abusers. Draper commented, "The latest statement makes no mention of assessing what support has been provided to survivors. It is window dressing yet again. They have learned nothing."[^cite_note-281]

Victims describe The [McLellan Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLellan_Report) into child sex abuse as a [whitewash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewash_\(censorship\)).[^cite_note-282] The McLellan Report fails to state which bishops and priests were responsible over decades of child sex abuse and in Scotland, which members of the hierarchy knew about abuse without acting, and ordered victims not to be supported. Some guilty priests will be given the job of introducing safeguards in their parishes while it is feared [denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denial) and [corruption](3608404.md) will continue in the Church.[^cite_note-283] Flaws in the procedures for addressing sexual abuse highlighted in the report include different rules and standards in different dioceses and lack of central guidance on sanctions, abuse victims being left out when central policies were drafted and disregarding [United Nations](31769.md) definitions of abuse.[^cite_note-284] There was a culture of [cover-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover-up) where words were not met with actions.[^cite_note-Reid-278][^cite_note-285] However, it has been acknowledged in at least one case that former Diocese of Galloway Bishop Maurice Taylor had received a confession of abuse from local [Ayrshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrshire) priest Paul Moore in 1996 and choose to send Moore to a treatment centre in Toronto and to Fort Augustus Abbey in the Highlands instead of turning him into the authorities.[^cite_note-bbcconfess-286]

#### Archdiocese of St Andrews and Edinburgh

Complaints were made that [Cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\)) [Keith O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_O'Brien) [was guilty of predatory sexual acts against various junior clerics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_O'Brien#Sexual_misconduct_and_consequences). O'Brien admitted unspecified sexual misconduct.

At the time of his arrest, David Murphy was also living in Edinburgh.[^cite_note-edinburgaccount-259]

#### Diocese of Galloway

In 1998, former teacher at [St. Columba's College, Largs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Columba's_College,_Largs), Norman Bulloch (Brother Norman) made a successful not-guilty plea with respect to the abuse of a pupil at the school between September 1971 and June 1972.[^cite_note-287] Despite the not-guilty plea regarding the St Columba's abuse, Bulloch was jailed for eight years for the sexual assault of two boys at St Joseph's, Dumfries between 1972 and 1976.[^cite_note-288]

In 2014 the Marist Brothers offered a former full-board pupil of St. Columba's College, Largs compensation following allegations that David Germanus (Brother Germanus) had sexually and physically abused him between 1962 and 1964 when he was aged between 7 and 9. This offer was turned down and the case went to the [Court of Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Session).[^cite_note-289] Judge [Lady Wolffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_P._L._Wolffe,_Lady_Wolffe) ruled that the case be time-barred due to a "long negative prescription".[^cite_note-290] In January 2017, [Lord Justice Clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Justice_Clerk) [Lady Dorrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeona_Dorrian,_Lady_Dorrian) dismissed an appeal in the case.[^cite_note-291]

In June 2017, the Marist Brothers admitted to systemic failures to protect pupils from sexual abuse[^cite_note-292] at the [Scottish Child Abuse Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Child_Abuse_Inquiry).

In February 2018, former teacher at St. Columba's, Peter Toner (Brother Peter) was convicted of sexual and physical abuse of six pupils between 1980 and 1982 when the pupils were aged between 8 and 11.[^cite_note-293] This prompted calls from his victims for a wider review of possible abuse he may have carried out elsewhere.[^cite_note-294] Toner was jailed for 10 years for the St Columba's offences in March 2019 with the judge stating, "Your predatory sex offending was a dreadful breach of trust. You wrecked the lives of these young boys. You present a danger of causing serious sexual harm to children". At the time of sentencing Toner was already behind bars for attacking two boys at a different school.[^cite_note-295]

In September 2018 [St. Columba's College, Largs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Columba's_College,_Largs) and [St Joseph's College, Dumfries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Joseph's_College,_Dumfries) was announced as being added to the Scottish Child Abuse Inquiry's investigations[^cite_note-296] with particular focus given to the schools in phase 4 of the investigation when hearings looked at residential child care establishments run by male religious orders.[^cite_note-297] In October 2019 the Inquiry heard that one pupil experienced physical and sexual abuse from David Germanus and sexual abuse from an unnamed member of teaching staff between 1958 and 1962 when the pupil was aged between 8 and 12.[^cite_note-298]

In 2018, Fr. Paul Moore was convicted of sexually abusing three children between 1977 and 1981 and a student priest in 1995.[^cite_note-bbcconfess-286][^cite_note-299][^cite_note-300]

#### Archdiocese of Glasgow

In July 2015, the Archdiocese of Glasgow issued an apology for the victims of Fr. Colman Mcgrath, who was convicted of sexually abusing a boy at his parish in Langside, Glasgow.[^cite_note-mcgrathdone-301]

On 6 May 2021, Fr. John Sweeney was cleared of false allegations made against him.[^cite_note-302]

In December 2018, it was announced that the Archdiocese of Glasgow was being sued by a former altar boy who stated that Fr. John Gowens, who died in 1999, repeatedly sexually abused him over a two-year period in the 1970s at the St, Patrick's church in Dumbarton.[^cite_note-303]

In June 2020, Fr. Neil McGarrity, who long led St. Thomas Parish in Riddrie, Glasgow, was arrested on numerous sex abuse charges.[^cite_note-304]

#### Diocese of Motherwell

In 2016, Fr. John Farrell, Retired priest of the Diocese of Motherwell, the last head teacher at St Ninian's Orphanage, Falkland, Fife, was sentenced to five years imprisonment. His colleague Paul Kelly, a retired teacher from Portsmouth, was given ten years, both were convicted of the physical and sexual abuse of boys between the years 1979 and 1983.[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306] More than 100 charges involving 35 boys were made.[^cite_note-307] Farrell and Kelly were members of the Irish Christian Brothers when the crimes were committed at the orphanage which closed in 1983. According to The Times, it is believed this was the largest historical abuse case ever tried in Scotland.[^cite_note-308]

#### Diocese of Aberdeen

In addition to his Glasgow conviction, Fr. Colman McGrath was convicted for sexually abusing two boys who were training to join the priesthood at Blairs College in Aberdeen. His crimes in Glasgow and Abedeen occurred between 1972 and 1982.[^cite_note-mcgrathdone-301] For all of these convictions, McGrath received a sentence of 200 hours of unpaid community service, three years of supervised release, and will be placed on the sex offenders' register throughout the remainder of this life.[^cite_note-mcgrathdone-301]

##### Fort Augustus Abbey

In 2013 *[The Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Observer)* newspaper reported that Scottish police were investigating allegations that pupils had been subject to physical and [sexual abuse](158934.md) while at the abbey school.[^cite_note-309] A [BBC Scotland Investigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Scotland_Investigates) programme, entitled *Sins of Our Fathers*,[^cite_note-310] reported allegations that Fort Augustus Abbey was used as a "dumping ground" for clergy previously accused of abuse elsewhere.[^cite_note-311] Some 50 former pupils spoke of their experiences. Many former pupils reported only good memories, but there were accounts of violence and sexual assault including rape by monks. The programme contains evidence against seven Fort Augustus monks; two headmasters have also been accused of covering-up the abuse. The head of the Benedictines, Dom Richard Yeo, apologised to any victims. In particular, five men were raped or sexually abused by Father Aidan Duggan, an Australian monk who taught at Carlekemp Priory School in [North Berwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Berwick) and Fort Augustus Abbey between 1953 and 1974.[^cite_note-guard130729-312] Fort Augustus Abbey closed as a school in 1993[^cite_note-313] and ceased to be a Catholic facility in 1998.[^cite_note-314]

In 2013, an apology was issued by the former headmaster to victims of Fr. Denis Chrysostom Alexander.[^cite_note-guard130729-312] In 2017, Alexander was arrested in Sydney, Australia and faces an extradition for sexual and physical abuse he reportedly committed at the former Fort Augustus Abbey in the 1970s.[^cite_note-315] In April 2019, the Australian government ruled that he could be extradited, though this has yet to receive final approval from the Federal Court of Australia.[^cite_note-ministryofjustice-316]

In March 2019, Scottish priest Fr. Robert MacKenzie was arrested in Canada and faces an extradition for sexually abusing children at the now closed [Fort Augustus Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Augustus_Abbey) between the 1950s and 1980s.[^cite_note-317][^cite_note-318] Canada's Minister of Justice approved the extradition,[^cite_note-ministryofjustice-316] though an appeal is pending.[^cite_note-ministryofjustice-316]

## Vatican

### Holy See

On 23 June 2018, a Vatican tribunal convicted former diplomat Monsignor Carlo Capella for possessing child pornography while in the Vatican's U.S. nunciature and handed him a five-year prison sentence.[^cite_note-322] Capella, who had also been appointed as a Chaplain of His Holiness by Pope Benedict XVI in 2008,[^cite_note-implicated-323][^cite_note-324] was implicated by 2017.[^cite_note-implicated-323]

On 9 December 2019, lawyers brought a sexual abuse lawsuit against the Holy See, regarding an alleged cover up of abuse committed by former Cardinal [Theodore McCarrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_McCarrick).[^cite_note-325]

On 19 November 2020, four people who accused McCarrick of sexually abusing them filed a lawsuit against the [Holy See](13393.md) in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, saying it had failed in its oversight of McCarrick over whom it exercised complete control as his employer. The Holy See says priests are not its employees and that its status as a foreign sovereign is a defense from such a suit.[^cite_note-overallvaticanlawsuit22-326][^cite_note-327]

### Vatican City

On 14 October 2020, the first ever criminal trial held within the Vatican City for sex abuse began, and involves a priest accused of sexually abusing a former St. Pius X youth seminary student between 2007 and 2012 and another for aiding and abetting the abuse.[^cite_note-328][^cite_note-329][^cite_note-twodefendants-330] The accused abuser, Rev. Gabriele Martinelli, 28, was a seminarian and has since become a priest.[^cite_note-twodefendants-330] The other defendant is the seminary's 72-year-old former rector Rev. Enrico Radice, who is charged with aiding and abetting the alleged abuse.[^cite_note-twodefendants-330]
