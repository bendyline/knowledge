# Prisoner exchange



North Korean prisoners of war [being returned to North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Big_Switch) by US authorities during the [Korean War](16772.md)

A **prisoner exchange** or **prisoner swap** is a deal between opposing sides in a conflict to release prisoners: [prisoners of war](25008.md), [spies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy), [hostages](146794.md), etc. Sometimes, [dead bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaver) are involved in an exchange.[^cite_note-1]

## History

Prisoners exchanged occurred throughout history; a number of large exchanges took places, for example, during the 8th century or so in the Middle East region (see [Arab–Byzantine prisoner exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Byzantine_prisoner_exchanges)).[^cite_note-2]

### Modern era

#### Geneva Conventions

Under the [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions), prisoners who *cannot* contribute to the [war effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_effort) because of [illness or disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wounded_in_action) are entitled to be repatriated to their home country. That is regardless of number of prisoners so affected; the detaining power cannot refuse a genuine request.[^cite_note-3]

Under the [Geneva Convention (1929)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Convention_on_Prisoners_of_War_\(1929\)), this is covered by Articles 68 to 74, and the annex. One of the largest exchange programmes was run for [prisoners of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II) by the [International Red Cross](5023832.md) during [World War II](32927.md) under these terms.[^cite_note-4] Under the [Third Geneva Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Geneva_Convention) of 1949, that is covered by Articles 109 to 117.

The [Second World War in Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia) saw a brutal struggle between the armed forces of the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich) and the [communist-led Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans). Despite that, the two sides negotiated prisoner exchanges virtually from the beginning of the war. Under extraordinary circumstances, these early contacts evolved into a formal exchange agreement, centered on the creation of a neutral zone, possibly the only such in occupied Europe, where prisoners were regularly swapped until late April 1945, saving several thousand lives.[^cite_note-5]
