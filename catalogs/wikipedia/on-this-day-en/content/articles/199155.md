**Ürümqi**[^cite_note-7] is the capital and largest city of the [Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) in [Northwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_China).[^cite_note-8] With a census population of 4,054,000 in 2020, Ürümqi is the second-largest city in China's northwestern interior after [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an). Ürümqi has seen significant economic development since the 1990s and currently serves as a regional transport hub and a cultural, political, and commercial center.

## Etymology

According to Herold J Wiens, the name "Ürümqi" (Oirat: [\[yrəm t͡ʃə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)) comes from the [Oirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirat_language) words *ürü* and *mchi*, together meaning "beautiful pasture".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWiens1963447-1]

The [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) took Ürümqi in 1755, during [its conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar%E2%80%93Qing_Wars) of the [Dzungar Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_Khanate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward2007\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid8FVsWq31MtMCpgPA99_99\]-9] Qing settlers expanded the town into a walled city from 1763 to 1767,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10] and upon completing the expansion renamed the city **Dihua** (Chinese: 迪化; [previously romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_postal_romanization) as **Tihwa**), meaning "to enlighten and civilize".[^cite_note-Holdstock-11][^cite_note-Zhao-12] The [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) restored the name "Ürümqi" on 1 February 1954, believing Dihua to be a belittling and [ethnically chauvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_chauvinism) name.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWiens1963447-1]

### Early period

During prehistory, the site of the future Ürümqi was occupied by a nomadic people known in Chinese accounts as the [Jushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jushi_Kingdom), who lived mainly on the northern slopes of the surrounding [Tianshan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian_Shan). The Jushi are often regarded as likely precursors of the [Tocharian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocharian_people), who later established city states in the [Tarim Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarim_Basin), south of the present site of Ürümqi.

The oldest known settlement, a town called Urabo, was located about 10 kilometres (6.2 miles) from the southern suburbs of the present-day Ürümqi.

[Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) states, located to the east, exerted increasing control of the Tarim Basin. Under the name **Luntai**, the city was founded by the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) government, in 648 CE, the 22nd year of [Emperor Taizong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong_of_Tang)'s reign, as part of the [Protectorate General to Pacify the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_General_to_Pacify_the_West).[^cite_note-chinatoday-13] It was a seat of local government and collected taxes from the [caravans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_\(travellers\)) along the northern route of the Silk Road.[^cite_note-chinatoday-13]

After the Tang retreated from the [Western Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Regions), the region came under the control of the [Uyghurs](31783.md) of the [Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_Khaganate) and [Gaochang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaochang) / [Qocho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qocho). There is little information about the Ürümqi area during the time between the Tang and Qing dynasties, and researchers generally believe that there were no permanent settlements there for most of this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10] The Mongols referred to the wider area as [Bishbalik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beshbalik), meaning five cities, a reference to the five towns that surrounded the present-day Ürümqi area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10]

### Dzungar period

The [Oirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirat_language)-speaking [Dzungar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_people) tribes that formed the [Dzungar Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_Khanate) were the last major power to control Ürümqi before the Manchus gained control of Xinjiang. During the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), there was a record of a place at Jiujiawan 5 kilometres (3.1 miles) to the west of present Ürümqi, which may have been the Dzungar town that was later destroyed during the Qing conquest. The Mongolians also used the area as herding ground in this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10] Steppe peoples had used the location, the pass between the [Bogda Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogda_Shan) to the east and the [Tian Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian_Shan) to the west, connecting the [Dzungar Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungaria) to the north and the [Turpan Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turpan_Depression) to the south.

Ürümqi remained a small town of lesser importance than the [oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis) and Silk Road trade center [Turpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turpan) 200 km (120 mi) to the southeast. Fighting for the control of Dzungaria led to the [Khoshuuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoshut) (now classified as Mongols) leaving Ürümqi for [Qinghai](173818.md) and [Tibet](31516.md) in the 1620s and 1630s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward2007\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid8FVsWq31MtMCpgPA89_89\]-14]

### Qing rule

In the 18th century, the Qing went to war against the [Dzungar Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_Khanate). Ürümqi was taken by the Qing in 1755, and the Dzungars of the region were eliminated in the [Dzungar genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_genocide). One writer, [Wei Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Yuan), described the resulting desolation in what became northern Xinjiang as "an empty plain for a thousand *[li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_\(unit\))*, with no trace of man". A fort was built (either in 1755 or 1758 depending on sources), and the Qing then established garrisons of [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people) and Mongol [bannermen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Banners) and Han Chinese troops at Ürümqi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward2007\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid8FVsWq31MtMCpgPA99_99\]-9] After 1759, the Qing government established state farms in the under-populated areas around Ürümqi, where there was fertile, well-watered land.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward200795,_104-15] Manchu soldiers also constructed a temple with red walls dedicated to [Guandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guan_Yu) on Pingding mountain overlooking Ürümqi, which gave Ürümqi the nickname "Red Temple".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10]

The Manchus began to construct a walled city in 1763 to the south of the first fort, and it was completed in 1767.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10] The [Qianlong Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianlong_Emperor) named the new settlement "Dihua" (Chinese: 迪化; pinyin: *Díhuà*; [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_language): *Wen de dahabure fu*), meaning "to enlighten and civilize".[^cite_note-Holdstock-11][^cite_note-Zhao-12] In 1771, another city named Gongning Cheng (鞏寧城) was built nearby to the northwest to house Manchu bannermen, and this would become the seat of government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199670–72-10] The bannermen settlement to the west was commonly referred to as "Mancheng" (滿城; 'Manchu City'), while Dihua to the east became a Han Chinese town commonly called "Hancheng" (漢城; 'Han City').[^cite_note-jeong-16] The Ürümqi of the early period was therefore a [twin-city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_cities), with Gongning Cheng forming the administrative center while Dihua grew into Xinjiang's commercial and financial center.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward1998131–134-17]

Han Chinese from all over China moved into Dihua, as did Chinese [Hui Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) from the areas of [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) and [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward1998\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid7ir2CAAAQBAJpgPA133_133\]-18] The origin of Hui in Ürümqi is often indicated by the names of their mosques.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward1998\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidMC6sAAAAIAAJpgPA169_169\]-19] By 1762, more than 500 shops had already been opened by Chinese migrants to the area of modern-day Ürümqi. Those Qing literati who visited Dihua were impressed by its cultural sophistication and similarity to eastern China. The writer [Ji Yun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji_Yun) compared Dihua to Beijing, in that both had numerous wine shops which offered daily performances of Chinese music and dance.[^cite_note-20]

In 1870, the [Battle of Ürümqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_\(1870\)) took place between the Turkic Muslim forces of [Yaqub Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakub_Beg_of_Yettishar) against the [Dungan Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) forces of Tuo Ming (Daud Khalifa). With the help of Xu Xuegong's Han Chinese militia, Yaqub Beg's forces defeated the Dungans.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward2007\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid8FVsWq31MtMCpgPA119_119–120\]-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Gongning Cheng was captured, its Qing administrator killed, and the city burnt to the ground and abandoned.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199672-25] The Qing later regained control of Ürümqi. In 1884, the [Guangxu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxu_Emperor) established [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) as a province, with Dihua as its capital.[^cite_note-26]

### Republican era

After the collapse of the Qing dynasty, Xinjiang was ruled from Ürümqi by a succession of warlords: [Yang Zengxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Zengxin) (1911–1928), [Jin Shuren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Shuren) (1928–1933), [Sheng Shicai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheng_Shicai) (1933–1942), and [Zhang Zhizhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Zhizhong) (1942–1949) as governor of Xinjiang. Of these, Yang and Sheng were considered capable rulers.[^cite_note-27]

During the [Kumul Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumul_Rebellion), the [First Battle of Ürümqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_\(1933\)) and the [Second Battle of Ürümqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_\(1933%E2%80%9334\)) took place between the forces of [Ma Zhongying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Zhongying)'s [36th Division (National Revolutionary Army)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_Division_\(National_Revolutionary_Army\)) and [Jin Shuren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Shuren) and [Sheng Shicai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheng_Shicai)'s provincial forces. At the second battle, Ma was assisted by the Han Chinese General [Zhang Peiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Peiyuan).

### People's Republic era

On 1 February 1954, following the [founding of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), the city's name was officially changed back to Ürümqi. The ruling [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) believed that the name "Dihua", which literally means "to enlighten and civilize", was belittling and [ethnically chauvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_chauvinism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWiens1963447-1]

Ürümqi became the *de facto* political and economic capital of Xinjiang in 1962, following protests against Chinese rule in the previous capital [Yining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yining) (Ghulja). In what came to be known as the [Yi–Ta incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi%E2%80%93Ta_incident), 60,000 Chinese citizens left Yining and [Tacheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacheng) for the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), prompting the Chinese government to move its administrative buildings and industrial focus from Yining to Ürümqi. The Chinese government also began construction on a railway connecting Ürümqi to [China proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_proper) in the east. As a result, Xinjiang was culturally and economically reoriented away from Central Asia and toward China proper.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMao2018155–156-28]

In the late 1970s, [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) relaxed China's tight control over Xinjiang, and Ürümqi benefited from the development of the oil and gas industry in Xinjiang.[^cite_note-29]

New mosques were built in Ürümqi with financial assistance from the Chinese government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinley2013\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidLQBBAQAAQBAJpgPA238_238\]-30] While the Chinese government implemented strict rules on religion in southern Xinjiang, the treatment of the Uyghurs and their religion in Ürümqi were more lax and permissive.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinley2013\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidLQBBAQAAQBAJpgPA240_240\]-31]

In May 1989, [unrest in Ürümqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_unrest) resulted in 150 injuries. In February 1997, bombings in Ürümqi following the [Ghulja incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghulja_incident) resulted in 20 deaths and scores of injuries.[^cite_note-32]

#### July 2009 riots and subsequent unrest

See also: [July 2009 Ürümqi riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2009_%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_riots) and [September 2009 Xinjiang unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2009_Xinjiang_unrest)

In the largest eruption of ethnic violence in China in decades, there were riots in July 2009 between ethnic Han Chinese and Uyghurs. The *New York Times* reporter covering the riot described the violence as "clashes with riot police and Uyghurs rampaging through the city and killing Han civilians. Then, for at least three days, bands of Han vigilantes roamed Ürümqi, attacking and killing Uyghurs."[^cite_note-nyt-33] Before the riot broke out, young Uyghurs had marched through the city "to protest a case of judicial discrimination".[^cite_note-nyt-33] According to official figures, most of the 197 killed in the riot were Han, a statement which *New York Times* reporter [Edward Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Wong) says is disputed by Uyghurs.[^cite_note-nyt-33][^cite_note-deathtooll-34]

## Geography

The largest city in [western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China), Ürümqi has earned a place in the *[Guinness Book of Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_Book_of_Records)* as the most remote city from any sea in the world. It is about 2,500 kilometres (1,600 mi) from the nearest coastline as Ürümqi is the closest major city to the Eurasian [pole of inaccessibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_of_inaccessibility), although [Karamay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamay) and [Altay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altay_City), both in Xinjiang, are closer.[^cite_note-35] The city has an administrative area of 10,989 square kilometres (4,243 sq mi) and has an average elevation of 800 metres (2,600 ft).

The location [43°40′52″N 87°19′52″E  /  43.68111°N 87.33111°E  / 43.68111; 87.33111](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi\&params=43_40_52_N_87_19_52_E_) in the southwestern suburbs of Ürümqi ([Ürümqi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_County)) was designated by local geography experts as the "center point of Asia" in 1992,[^cite_note-36] and a [monument to this effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Center_of_Asian_Continent) was erected there in the 1990s. The site is a local tourist attraction.[^cite_note-37]

### Water supply

Although surrounded by deserts (the [Gurbantünggüt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurbant%C3%BCngg%C3%BCt_Desert) in the north and the [Taklamakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taklamakan_Desert) in the south), the Ürümqi area is naturally watered by a number of small rivers flowing from the snow-capped Tian Shan mountains: the main range of the Tian Shan in south of the city (Ürümqi County), and the [Bogda Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogda_Shan) east of the city ([Dabancheng District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabancheng_District)).[^cite_note-\:0-38]

There are 20,000 glaciers in Xinjiang – nearly half of all the glaciers in China. Since the 1950s, Xinjiang's glaciers have retreated by between 21 percent to 27 percent due to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming). [Tianshan Glacier No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urumqi_Glacier_No.1) (一号冰川), origin of Ürümqi River, is the largest glacier near a major city in China, but has already split into two smaller glaciers.[^cite_note-\:0-38]

As the Ürümqi region's population and economy are growing, the water demand exceeds the natural supply. To alleviate water shortages, the [Irtysh–Ürümqi Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irtysh%E2%80%93Karamay%E2%80%93%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_Canal) was constructed in the first decade of the 21st century. The canal's main trunk terminates in the so-called "Reservoir 500" ("500"水库; [44°12′00″N 87°49′00″E  /  44.20000°N 87.81667°E  / 44.20000; 87.81667](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi\&params=44_12_00_N_87_49_00_E_type\:landmark_region\:CN-65)) in the far north-eastern suburbs of the city (on the border of Ürümqi's suburban [Midong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midong_District) and [Fukang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukang) City). A new industrial area, called Ganquanbao Industrial Park (甘泉堡工业园), or Industrial New City 500 (500工业新城) was being developed in 2009, west of the reservoir, relying on it for water supply.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] From the reservoir area water is further distributed over a network of canals throughout the lower Midong District.

### Climate

Ürümqi lies on the boundary between a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon-influenced_hot-summer_humid_continental_climate#Hot/warm_summer_subtype) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfa*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dcac*) and a [cool semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cool_semi-arid_climate) (Köppen: *BSk*, Trewartha: *BSac*). It sees vast differences between summer and winter temperatures, featuring very warm to hot summers, with a July daily average of 24.2 °C (75.6 °F), and frigid winters, with a January daily average of −12.2 °C (10.0 °F). The annual average temperature is 7.8 °C (46.0 °F). Although the cityʼs summers are slightly wetter than its winters, sunny weather is much more likely in the hotter months, and relative humidity is the lowest during summer. With monthly percent possible sunshine ranging from 33 percent in December to 75 percent in September, the city receives 2,643 hours of bright sunshine annually. Its annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) is about 365 millimetres (14.4 in).[^cite_note-cma_new-41] Extremes since 1951 have ranged from −41.5 °C (−42.7 °F) on 27 February 1951 to 42.1 °C (107.8 °F) on 1 August 1973.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### Air quality and pollution

[Blacksmith Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacksmith_Institute) mentioned Ürümqi in *2007 World's Worst Polluted Places* caused by [air pollution](10934212.md) along with [Linfen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linfen) and [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou), all in China.[^cite_note-47] In 2008, *[Toronto Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Star)* listed Ürümqi as one of the top ten worst places to live in the world due to sulphurous pollution.[^cite_note-48] Heavy haze is extremely common in winter, which frequently affects air traffic. Officials believed that severe winter air pollution in Ürümqi is mainly caused by energy-heavy industries and the outdated coal-firing winter heating system.[^cite_note-49]

According to a report by Department of Environmental Science and Engineering of [Fudan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fudan_University), the average PM<sub>2.5</sub> and TSP concentrations in the winter of 2007 were 12 times higher than USA standard for PM<sub>2.5</sub> and 3 times the National Ambient Air Quality Standard of China for TSP.[^cite_note-fudan-50] The [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) from industrial emissions mixed with the local anthropogenic aerosol with the transported soil dust from outside the city were the main sources of the high concentration of sulfate, one of the main factors causing the heavy air pollution over Ürümqi.[^cite_note-fudan-50]

### Timing of sunrise

Because of the use of a [single time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_China) for all of China, in Ürümqi which is at a far western location in China, the sun is 2 hours and 10 minutes behind [China Standard Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Standard_Time) (CST = [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC)+8). During early January the sun does not rise until 09\:45 and it sets between 18\:45 and 19\:10 local time. In September and March, the sun rises around 08\:00, and sets around 19\:45. However, in June the sun rises at about 06\:25 and does not set until 21\:45.

## Demographics



The [Grand Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Bazaar_\(%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi\))

Ürümqi has been a multiethnic city from the time of the Qing conquest; in the early years, the Manchus lived in Gongning Cheng, Han Chinese in Dihua and various other ethnic groups such as the Hui, Uyghurs and others in the suburban districts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199673–75-52] Muslims settled to the south of the walled-city of Dihua, and although the walls have since been demolished, the Muslims are still concentrated there.[^cite_note-53]

A census in 1787 showed that there were 114,348 Hui and Han in the prefectures of Zhenxi (which included Barköl and [Hami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hami)) and Ürümqi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward2007\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid8FVsWq31MtMCpgPA306_306–307\]-54] In Ürümqi city itself, there were 39,000 people in the 1880s and by the early 20th century, 50,000 people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaubatz199673–75-52] Ürümqi in 1908 was said to have been inhabited by Uyghurs (then called Turkis), Chinese, Manchus and a few Mongols, with the Uyghurs estimated to comprise a quarter of the population.[^cite_note-55]

In the People's Republic of China era, an active program to resettle Han population in Xinjiang was initiated.[^cite_note-56] In 1960, there were 76,496 Uyghurs and 477,321 Han Chinese in Ürümqi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillward2007\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid8FVsWq31MtMCpgPA260_260\]-57]

According to the 2000 census, Ürümqi had 2,081,834 inhabitants, with a population density of 174.53 inhabitants / km<sup>2</sup> (452.3 inhabitants / sq. mi.).[^cite_note-2000_census-58]

In 2021, Ürümqi was estimated to have a population of 4.544 million people.[^cite_note-59]

Outer Ring Road viaducts in Ürümqi at night

Ürümqi is a major industrial center within Xinjiang. Ürümqi, together with [Karamay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamay) and [Korla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korla), account for 64.5 percent of the total industrial output of Xinjiang. Ürümqi is also the largest consumer center in the region, recording [¥](66958.md)41.9 billion retail sales of consumer goods in 2008, an increase of 26 percent from 2007. As of 2025, the city's GDP was [CN¥](66958.md)465.8 billion (US\$65.2 billion), while the GDP per capita was around CN¥112,485 (US\$15,748).[^cite_note-data2022-4] Ürümqi has been a central developmental target for the [China Western Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Western_Development) project that the Central Government is pursuing.

The [Urumqi Foreign Economic Relations and Trade Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urumqi_Foreign_Economic_Relations_and_Trade_Fair) has been held annually since 1991 and has been upgraded into the first [China-Eurasia Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China-Eurasia_Expo) in 2011. Its purpose is to promote domestic and foreign markets. The 17th Fair has attracted participants from the [Ministry of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) and the [China Council for Promotion of International Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Council_for_the_Promotion_of_International_Trade).



Buildings in Ürümqi CBDs near [People's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Square_\(%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi\))&#x20;

[Xinjiang Guanghui Industry Investment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Guanghui_Industry_Investment_Group) is the largest [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) enterprise and most powerful [privately owned company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_held_company) in Xinjiang and is currently engaged in energy and automobiles. [China CITIC Bank Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhong_Tian_Plaza), headquarters of Guanghui, located in one of the CBDs in North Xinhua Road, is the tallest building in Ürümqi and Xinjiang; with a height of 229 metres (751 feet), it is also the tallest in [Northwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_China) and [Central Asia](6742.md). Zhongshan Road (Sun Yat-sen Road; Chinese: 中山路) has been one of the ten most famous commercial streets in China since 2005. Zhongshan Road has always been the hub of [consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronics) in the city, with the largest computer, [mobile phone](19644137.md) and [consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronics) market in Xinjiang, including Baihuacun, Cyber Digital Plaza and Fountain Plaza.

As the economic center in Xinjiang, Ürümqi has expanded its urban area since the 1990s. The [CBDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Business_District) in the city increased rapidly all around the major districts. Despite the old city areas being primarily in the south, the development in the north part began since the late 1980s. The completion of the new office tower for Ürümqi Municipal Government in 2003 at [Nanhu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanhu_Square?action=edit\&redlink=1) (南湖广场) in Nanhu Road marked a shift of the city center to the north. Lacking a subway, the city commenced the construction of [viaducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viaduct) for Outer Ring Road (外环路) since 2003, which considerably facilitates transport. Youhao Road (友好路) and surrounding neighborhood, is the commercial center for business, shopping and amusement. Youhao Group (友好集团), the namesake local enterprise, owns a major market share of retails. Maison Mode Ürümqi (乌鲁木齐美美百货), open since 2008, became one of the few notable department stores for [luxury merchandise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_good) in the city.[^cite_note-62] The Ürümqi Economic and Technological Development Zone (UETD) located in the northern [Toutunhe District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toutunhe_District), has been a leading base for steel, machinery manufacturing, biochemistry and other industrial innovations.

## Transportation



Ürümqi Tianshan International Airport



Ürümqi South Railway Station

### Air

Ürümqi is served by the [Ürümqi Tianshan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_Tianshan_International_Airport). It is a hub for [China Southern Airlines](382884.md). Ürümqi Tianshan International Airport is the largest airport in Xinjiang, and the only airport in China to serve flights from [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Tajikistan](30108.md).

### Bus rapid transit

The [Ürümqi Bus Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_BRT) (Ürümqi BRT) bus service was launched in August 2011 after an investment of 930 million yuan in an effort to improve urban traffic.[^cite_note-69] There are currently 9 routes operated, BRT1, BRT2, BRT3, BRT4, BRT5, BRT 6, its branch BRT 61, BRT 7, and its branch BRT 71.[^cite_note-70]

### Metro

The [Ürümqi Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_Metro) opened on 25 October 2018, when the northern section of Line 1 was opened to the public. The southern section of Line 1 opened on 28 June 2019.[^cite_note-Line1a-71] Line 1 runs between [Ürümqi Tianshan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_Tianshan_International_Airport) and Santunbei in downtown Ürümqi, with a total length of 27.615 km (17.2 mi) and 21 stations.[^cite_note-Line1a-71][^cite_note-Line1Length-72] It is fully underground.[^cite_note-U1length-73]

The planned system consists of 7 lines being 211 km (131 mi) in length. The first two lines, Line 1 and Line 2 will be constructed with an estimated cost of 31.24 billion yuan.[^cite_note-74]

### Rail

Ürümqi is Xinjiang's main rail hub with two primary railway stations, the older [Ürümqi South railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_South_railway_station) (formerly the Ürümqi railway station) and [Ürümqi railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_railway_station) opened in July 2016. The [Lanzhou-Xinjiang High Speed Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanxin_High-Speed_Railway) stops at both stations, running from Ürümqi to [Lanzhou railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou_railway_station), and has been in operation since the end of 2014.

The city is served by several conventional rail lines. Ürümqi is the western terminus of the [Lanzhou–Xinjiang (Lanxin)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou%E2%80%93Xinjiang_railway) and [Ürümqi–Dzungaria (Wuzhun)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi%E2%80%93Dzungaria_railway) Railway, and the eastern terminus of the [Northern Xinjiang (Beijiang)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Xinjiang_railway) and the [Second Ürümqi–Jinghe railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi%E2%80%93Jinghe_railway). The Beijiang and the Lanxin Lines form part of the [Trans-Eurasian Continental Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Land_Bridge), which runs from [Rotterdam](26049.md) through the [Alataw Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungarian_Gate) on the Kazakhstan border to Ürümqi and on to Lanzhou and [Lianyungang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang).

## Media

The [Xinjiang Networking Transmission Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Networking_Transmission_Limited) operates the [Ürümqi People's Broadcasting Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urumqi_People's_Broadcasting_Station) and the [Xinjiang People Broadcasting Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_People_Broadcasting_Station), broadcasting in the [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese), [Uyghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_language), [Kazakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_language), [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language), Russian and the [Kyrgyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_language) languages.

The [Xinjiang Television Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Television_Station) (XJTV), located in Ürümqi, is the major TV broadcasting station in the [Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Uyghur_Autonomous_Region). The local television station for Ürümqi city is [Ürümqi Television Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cr%C3%BCmqi_Television_Station) (UTV).

The Ürümqi Multilingual Public Service Platform is a mobile application and website that provides government services and information in multiple languages, including Mandarin, Uyghur, Kazakh, and Kyrgyz. It facilitates access to administrative procedures, public announcements, and emergency alerts for the city's diverse ethnic communities, promoting digital inclusion.[^cite_note-75]

- [Xinjiang Flying Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Flying_Tigers) is a basketball team that is part of the [Chinese Basketball Association](939720.md), based in Ürümqi, Xinjiang. Its corporate sponsor is Xinjiang Guanghui Group.

- [Xinjiang Tianshan Leopard F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_Tianshan_Leopard_F.C.) is a local football team in the [China League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_League_One).

China started a [bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy) development programme by organizing educational days in Ürümqi in June 2009.[^cite_note-76]

In 2015, an indoor [speed skating](28028.md) arena was opened.[^cite_note-77]
