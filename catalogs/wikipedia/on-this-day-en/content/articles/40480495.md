The practice of [mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance) in the United States, which entails the pervasive surveillance of virtually all Americans' communications, dates back to [wartime monitoring and censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_the_United_States#Wartime_censorship) of international communications from, to, or which passed through the United States.&#x20;

After the [First](4764461.md) and [Second World Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), mass surveillance continued throughout the [Cold War](325329.md) period, via programs such as the [Black Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Chamber) and [Project SHAMROCK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_SHAMROCK). President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) established the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA) in 1952 for the purposes of collecting, processing, and monitoring intelligence data.[^cite_note-1] The formation of the international UKUSA surveillance agreement of 1946 evolved into the [ECHELON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHELON) collaboration by 1955[^cite_note-2] of five English-speaking nations, also known as the [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes), and focused on interception of electronic communications, with substantial increases in domestic surveillance capabilities.[^cite_note-3]

The formation and growth of federal [law-enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency) and [intelligence agencies](81014.md) such as the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI), [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA), and [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) institutionalized surveillance used to also silence political dissent, as evidenced by [COINTELPRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COINTELPRO) projects which targeted various organizations and individuals. During the [Civil Rights Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Movement) era, many individuals put under surveillance orders were first labelled as integrationists, then deemed subversive, and sometimes suspected to be supportive of the [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) model of the United States' rival at the time, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Other targeted individuals and groups included Native American activists, African American and Chicano liberation movement activists, and anti-war protesters.

When the [Citizens' Commission to Investigate the FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Commission_to_Investigate_the_FBI) published stolen FBI documents revealing abuse of intelligence programs in 1971, Senator [Frank Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Church) began an investigation into the programs that become known as the [Church Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Committee). The committee sought to investigate intelligence abuses throughout the 1970s. Following a report provided by the committee outlining egregious abuse, in 1976 Congress established the [Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence). It would later be joined by the [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Court) in 1978.[^cite_note-reutershistory-4] The institutions worked to limit the power of the agencies, ensuring that surveillance activities remained within the rule of law.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

Following the attacks of [September 11, 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001), domestic and international mass surveillance capabilities grew immensely. [Congress](31756.md) passed the [Patriot Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_Act) to strengthen security and intelligence efforts. The act granted the President broad powers on the [war against terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_against_Terror), including the power to bypass the FISA Court for surveillance orders in cases of national security. Additionally, mass surveillance activities were conducted alongside various other surveillance programs under the head of [President's Surveillance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Surveillance_Program).[^cite_note-6] Contemporary mass surveillance relies upon annual presidential executive orders declaring a continued State of National Emergency, first signed by George W. Bush on September 14, 2001 and then continued on an annual basis during the presidencies of Barack Obama, Joe Biden, and Donald Trump.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The [FISA Amendments Act of 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FISA_of_1978_Amendments_Act_of_2008) authorized the [PRISM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRISM_\(surveillance_program\)) surveillance program. Political commentators have criticized these acts, orders, and resulting database network of [fusion centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_center) as forming an American [police state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_state) that institutionalized the illegal [COINTELPRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COINTELPRO) tactics used to assassinate dissenters and leaders from the 1950s onwards.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Under pressure from the public, the warrantless wiretapping program was allegedly ended in January 2007.[^cite_note-12]

Additional surveillance agencies, such as the DHS and the position of Director of National Intelligence, have greatly escalated mass surveillance since 2001. A series of [media reports in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_mass_surveillance_disclosures) revealed more recent programs and techniques employed by the [US intelligence community](501244.md).[^cite_note-guardian6-13][^cite_note-14] Many details about the surveillance activities conducted in the United States were revealed in the disclosure by [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) in June 2013.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-guardian-2013-16] Regarded as one of the biggest media leaks in the United States, it presented extensive details about the surveillance programs of the NSA, that involved interception of Internet data and telephonic calls from over a billion users, across various countries.[^cite_note-\:0-17][^cite_note-guardian-2013-16] Advances in computer and information technology allow the creation of huge [national databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_databases_of_United_States_persons) that facilitate mass surveillance in the United States[^cite_note-guardian6-13] by [DHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHS) managed fusion centers, the CIA's Terrorist Threat Integration Center (TTIC) program, and the FBI's [Terrorist Screening Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_Screening_Database) (TSDB).

## History

### Post World War I

After [World War I](4764461.md), the US Army and State Department established the [Black Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Chamber), also known as the Cipher Bureau, which began operations in 1919.[^cite_note-18] The Black Chamber was headed by [Herbert O. Yardley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Yardley), who had been a leader in the Army's Military Intelligence program. Regarded as a precursor to the National Security Agency, it conducted peacetime decryption of material including diplomatic communications until 1929.[^cite_note-reutershistory-4][^cite_note-19]

In the advent of [World War II](32927.md), the [Office of Censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Censorship) was established. The wartime agency monitored "communications by mail, cable, radio, or other means of transmission passing between the United States and any foreign country".[^cite_note-Fiset2001-20] This included the 350,000 overseas cables and [telegrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegrams) and 25,000 international telephone calls made each week.[^cite_note-kennett1985-21]<sup>: 144 </sup> "Every letter that crossed international or U.S. territorial borders from December 1941 to August 1945 was subject to being opened and scoured for details."[^cite_note-Fiset2001-20]

### Post World War II

With the end of [World War II](32927.md), [Project SHAMROCK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_SHAMROCK) was established in 1945. The organization was created to accumulate telegraphic data entering and exiting from the United States.[^cite_note-reutershistory-4][^cite_note-22] Major communication companies such as [Western Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Union), [RCA Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Corporation) and [ITT World Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITT_Corporation) actively aided the project, allowing American intelligence officials to gain access to international message traffic.[^cite_note-pbs-23] Under the project, and many subsequent programs, no precedent had been established for judicial authorization, and no warrants were issued for surveillance activities. The project was terminated in 1975.[^cite_note-reutershistory-4]

### Establishment of the FBI

Institutional domestic surveillance was founded in 1896 with the [National Bureau of Criminal Identification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Criminal_Identification), which evolved by 1908 into the Bureau of Investigation, operated under the authority of the Department of Justice. In 1935, the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) had grown into an independent agency under the direction of J. Edgar Hoover whose staff, through the use of wire taps, cable taps, mail tampering, garbage filtering and infiltrators, prepared secret [FBI Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Index) Lists on more than 10 million people by 1939.[^cite_note-s546-24]

### Establishment of the NSA

In 1947, the [National Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) was signed by President Truman, establishing a [National Security Council](44026.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] In 1949, the Armed Forces Security Agency was established to coordinate signal operations between military branches.[^cite_note-27] In 1952, the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA) was officially established by President Truman by way of a National Security Council Intelligence Directive 9, dated Oct. 24, while the NSA officially came into existence days later on Nov. 4.[^cite_note-reutershistory-4] The existence of NSA was not known to people as the memorandum by President Truman was classified.[^cite_note-28] According to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, the NSA was created in "absolute secrecy" by President Truman,[^cite_note-29] whose surveillance-minded administration ordered, only six weeks after President Truman took office, [wiretaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_tapping) on the telephones of [Thomas Gardiner Corcoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gardiner_Corcoran), a close advisor of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt).[^cite_note-30] The recorded conversations are kept at the [Harry S. Truman Presidential Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman_Presidential_Library_and_Museum), along with other documents considered sensitive.[^cite_note-31]

### McCarthyism and COINTELPRO

Purported to be chasing 'communists' and other alleged subversives, the FBI used public and private pressure to destroy the lives of those it targeted during [McCarthyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism), including those lives of the Hollywood 10 with the [Hollywood blacklist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_blacklist). The FBI's surveillance and investigation roles expanded in the 1950s while using the collected information to facilitate political assassinations, including the murders of [Fred Hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hampton) and Mark Clark in 1969. FBI is also directly connected to the bombings, assassinations, and deaths of other people including Malcolm X in 1963, Viola Liuzzo in 1965, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. in 1968, Anna Mae Pictou Aquash in 1976, and Judi Bari in 1990.[^cite_note-32]

### Church committee review



At the request of the [U.S. Army](32087.md), those who protested against the [Vietnam War](32611.md) were put on the NSA's "watch list".

[^cite_note-pbs-23]

In 1975, the [Church Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Committee) of the [United States Senate](24909346.md) was set up to investigate widespread intelligence abuses by the NSA, [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) and [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI).[^cite_note-reutershistory-4] Domestic surveillance, authorized by the highest [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) branch of the federal government, spanned from the [FDR Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt_Administration) to the [Presidency of Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Richard_Nixon). The following examples were reported by the Church Committee:

- [President Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt) asked the FBI to put in its files the names of citizens sending telegrams to the White House opposing his "national defense" policy and supporting Col. [Charles Lindbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lindbergh).

  [^cite_note-churchsenate-50]

- [President Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S_Truman) received inside information on a former Roosevelt aide's efforts to influence his appointments, labor union negotiating plans, and the publishing plans of journalists.

  [^cite_note-churchsenate-50]

- [President Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) received reports on purely political and social contacts with foreign officials by [Bernard Baruch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Baruch), [Eleanor Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Roosevelt), and Supreme Court Justice [William O. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_O._Douglas).

  [^cite_note-churchsenate-50]

- The [Kennedy administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) ordered the FBI to wiretap a congressional staff member, three executive officials, a lobbyist, and a Washington law firm. [US Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Attorney_General) [Robert F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy) received data from an FBI wire tap on [Martin Luther King Jr.](20076.md) and an electronic listening device targeting a congressman, both of which yielded information of a political nature.

### ECHELON

Main article: [ECHELON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHELON)

In 1988 an article titled "Somebody's listening" by [Duncan Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Campbell_\(journalist,_born_1952\)) in the *[New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman)* described the signals-intelligence gathering activities of a program code-named "[ECHELON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHELON)".[^cite_note-56] The program was engaged by English-speaking World War II [Allied countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) – Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States (collectively known as [AUSCANNZUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUSCANNZUKUS)). It was created by the five countries to monitor the military and diplomatic communications of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and of its [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) allies during the [Cold War](325329.md) in the early 1960s.[^cite_note-EP-57]

By the 1990s the ECHELON system could intercept satellite transmissions, [public switched telephone network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_switched_telephone_network) (PSTN) communications (including most Internet traffic), and transmissions carried by microwave. The New Zealand journalist [Nicky Hager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicky_Hager) provided a detailed description of ECHELON in his 1996 book *Secret Power*.[^cite_note-58] While some member governments denied the existence of ECHELON, a report by a committee of the [European Parliament](9581.md) in 2001 confirmed the program's use and warned Europeans about its reach and effects.[^cite_note-online.wsj.com-59] The European Parliament stated in its report that the term "ECHELON" occurred in a number of contexts, but that the evidence presented indicated it was a signals-intelligence collection system capable of interception and content-inspection of telephone calls, fax, e-mail and other data-traffic globally.[^cite_note-EP-57]

[James Bamford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bamford) further described the capabilities of ECHELON in *Body of Secrets* (2002) about the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency).[^cite_note-Bamford-60] Intelligence monitoring of citizens, and their communications, in the area covered by the AUSCANNZUKUS security agreement have, over the years, caused considerable public concern.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

### Escalation following September 11, 2001 attacks

Further information: [NSA warrantless surveillance (2001–07)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_warrantless_surveillance_\(2001%E2%80%9307\))

> We will come together to strengthen our [intelligence](146678.md) capabilities to know the plans of [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) before they act and to find them before they strike.

— [President Bush](3414021.md) speaking in Congress on September 20, 2001[^cite_note-63]



The [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) on the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)) and [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) led to major reforms of U.S. intelligence agencies, and paved the way for the establishment of the [Director of National Intelligence](456082.md) position.



On 1 January 2006, days after *[The New York Times](30680.md)* wrote that "*Bush Lets U.S. Spy on Callers Without Courts*,

[^cite_note-nytimes123-64]

&#x20;the President emphasized that "*This is a limited program designed to prevent attacks on the United States of America. And I repeat, limited.*"

[^cite_note-65]

In the aftermath of the September 2001 attacks on the World Trade Center and the Pentagon, bulk domestic spying in the United States increased dramatically. The desire to prevent future attacks of this scale led to the passage of the [Patriot Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_Act). Later acts include the [Protect America Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protect_America_Act) (which removes the warrant requirement for government surveillance of foreign targets)[^cite_note-Judiciary_Testimony-66] and the [FISA Amendments Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FISA_Amendments_Act) (which relaxed some of the original FISA court requirements).

Follow-on legislation have amended and reauthorized the post-9/11 surveillance authorities. The USA FREEDOM Act of 2015 limited certain bulk collection authorized under the USA PATRIOT Act. Subsequent legislation reauthorized Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, which allows for warrantless surveillance of non-U.S. persons located outside the United States and will sunset in April 2026 if not reauthorized by Congress[^cite_note-PCLOB215-67].

In 2002, "[Total Information Awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Information_Awareness)" was established by the U.S. government in order to "revolutionize the ability of the United States to detect, classify and identify foreign terrorists".[^cite_note-68]

In 2005, a report about President Bush's [President's Surveillance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Surveillance_Program) appeared in *The New York Times*. According to reporters [James Risen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Risen) and [Eric Lichtblau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lichtblau), the actual publication of their report was delayed for a year because "The [White House](33057.md) asked *The New York Times* not to publish this article".[^cite_note-nytimes123-64]

Also in 2005, the existence of [STELLARWIND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STELLARWIND) was revealed by [Thomas Tamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tamm).[^cite_note-69] In 2006, [Mark Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Klein) revealed the existence of [Room 641A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_641A) that he had wired back in 2003.[^cite_note-WiredEvidence-70] In 2008, Babak Pasdar, a computer security expert, and CEO of Bat Blue publicly revealed the existence of the "Quantico circuit", that he and his team found in 2003. He described it as a [backdoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backdoor_\(computing\)) to the federal government in the systems of an unnamed wireless provider; the company was later independently identified as [Verizon](18619278.md).[^cite_note-71]

The NSA's database of American's phone calls was made public in 2006 by *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)* journalist Leslie Cauley in an article titled, "NSA has massive database of Americans' phone calls."[^cite_note-usatoday2006-72] The article cites anonymous sources that described the program's reach on American citizens:

> ... it means that the government has detailed records of calls they made — across town or across the country — to family members, co-workers, business contacts and others. The three telecommunications companies are working under contract with the NSA, which launched the program in 2001 shortly after the Sept. 11 terrorist attacks.[^cite_note-usatoday2006-72]

In 2009, *The New York Times* cited several anonymous intelligence officials alleging that "the N.S.A. made Americans targets in eavesdropping operations based on insufficient evidence tying them to terrorism" and "the N.S.A. tried to wiretap a member of Congress without a warrant".[^cite_note-nytimes2-73]

On 6 June 2013, Britain's *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* newspaper began publishing a series of revelations by an unnamed American whistleblower, revealed several days later to be former CIA and NSA-contracted systems analyst [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden). Snowden gave a cache of internal documents in support of his claims to two journalists: [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald) and [Laura Poitras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Poitras), Greenwald later estimated that the cache contains 15,000–20,000 documents, some very large and very detailed, and some very small.[^cite_note-cepr1-76][^cite_note-Senado1-77] This was one of the largest [news leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_leak) in the modern [history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-\:0-17] In over two months of publications, it became clear that the NSA operates a complex web of spying programs which allow it to intercept internet and telephone conversations from over a billion users from dozens of countries around the world. Specific revelations have been made about China, the European Union, Latin America, Iran and Pakistan, and Australia and New Zealand, however the published documentation reveals that many of the programs indiscriminately collect bulk information directly from central servers and internet backbones, which almost invariably carry and reroute information from distant countries.

Due to this central server and backbone monitoring, many of the programs overlap and interrelate among one another. These programs are often done with the assistance of US entities such as the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) and the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI),[^cite_note-microsoft-78] are sanctioned by US laws such as the [FISA Amendments Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FISA_Amendments_Act), and the necessary court orders for them are signed by the secret [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Court). In addition to this, many of the NSA's programs are directly aided by national and foreign intelligence services, Britain's [GCHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCHQ) and Australia's [DSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Signals_Directorate), as well as by large private telecommunications and Internet corporations, such as [Verizon](18619278.md), [Telstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telstra),[^cite_note-79] [Google](1092923.md) and Facebook.[^cite_note-80]

On 9 June 2013, [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) told *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*:

> They (the NSA) can use the system to go back in time and scrutinize every decision you've ever made, every friend you've ever discussed something with, and attack you on that basis to sort of derive suspicion from an innocent life and paint anyone in the context of a wrongdoer.

— Edward Snowden[^cite_note-81]

The US government has aggressively sought to dismiss and challenge [Fourth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) cases raised: *[Hepting v. AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepting_v._AT\&T)*, *[Jewel v. NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewel_v._NSA)*, *[Clapper v. Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clapper_v._Amnesty_International)*, *Al-Haramain Islamic Foundation v. Obama*, and *[Center for Constitutional Rights v. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Constitutional_Rights_v._Bush)*. The government has also granted retroactive immunity to ISPs and telecoms participating in domestic surveillance.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

The US district court judge for the District of Columbia, Richard Leon, declared[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] on December 16, 2013 that the mass collection of metadata of Americans' telephone records by the National Security Agency probably violates the Fourth Amendment prohibition of unreasonable searches and seizures.[^cite_note-WP20131216-90]

> Given the limited record before me at this point in the litigation – most notably, the utter lack of evidence that a terrorist attack has ever been prevented because searching the NSA database was faster than other investigative tactics – I have serious doubts about the efficacy of the metadata collection program as a means of conducting time-sensitive investigations in cases involving imminent threats of terrorism.[^cite_note-Guardian20131216-91]

"Plaintiffs have a substantial likelihood of showing that their privacy interests outweigh the government's interest in collecting and analysing bulk telephony metadata and therefore the NSA's bulk collection program is indeed an unreasonable search under the fourth amendment," he wrote.[^cite_note-Guardian20131216-91]

"The Fourth Amendment typically requires 'a neutral and detached authority be interposed between the police and the public,' and it is offended by 'general warrants' and laws that allow searches to be conducted 'indiscriminately and without regard to their connections with a crime under investigation,'" he wrote.[^cite_note-FoxNews20131217-92] He added:

> I cannot imagine a more 'indiscriminate' and 'arbitrary invasion' than this systematic and high-tech collection and retention of personal data on virtually every single citizen for purposes of querying and analyzing it without prior judicial approval. Surely such a program infringes on 'that degree of privacy' that the founders enshrined in the Fourth Amendment. Indeed I have little doubt that the author of our Constitution, [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison), who cautioned us to beware 'the abridgement of freedom of the people by gradual and silent encroachments by those in power,' would be aghast.[^cite_note-FoxNews20131217-92]

Leon granted the request for a preliminary injunction that blocks the collection of phone data for two private plaintiffs (Larry Klayman, a conservative lawyer, and Charles Strange, father of a cryptologist killed in Afghanistan when his helicopter was shot down in 2011)[^cite_note-Guardian20131216-91] and ordered the government to destroy any of their records that have been gathered. But the judge stayed action on his ruling pending a government appeal, recognizing in his 68-page opinion the "significant national security interests at stake in this case and the novelty of the constitutional issues."[^cite_note-WP20131216-90]

### Legislation in 2014 and 2015

On 20 May 2014, [U.S. Representative](19468510.md) for [Michigan's 8th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan's_8th_congressional_district) Republican congressman [Mike Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Rogers_\(Michigan_politician\)) introduced the [Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Years 2014 and 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Years_2014_and_2015) with the goal of authorizing appropriations for fiscal years 2014 and 2015 for intelligence and intelligence-related activities of the United States Government, the Community Management Account, and the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA) Retirement and Disability System, and for other purposes.[^cite_note-93] Versions of the bill were passed into law for FY2014 and FY2015.[^cite_note-94]

The [USA Freedom Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Freedom_Act) was signed into law on June 2, 2015, the day after certain provisions of the Patriot Act had expired. It mandated an end to bulk collection of phone call metadata by the NSA within 180 days, but allowed continued mandatory retention of metadata by phone companies with access by the government with case-by-case approval from the [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Court).[^cite_note-95] Later audits and a management decision led to a suspension and cancellation of the Call Detail Records program[^cite_note-96], with the government oversight bodies determining it had a low yield of unique information.

Section 215 of the USA PATRIOT Act had previously been used to justify the bulk collection of domestic telephone metadata by the National Security Agency. Two later oversight reviews of the use of this authority examined its operations and its value, the Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board found that the Section 215 bulk metadata program had "yielded limited unique counterterrorism value" and "often duplicated information more efficiently obtained by targeted investigations.[^cite_note-97]

### 2020s

The 2020s are seeing even more surveillance on Americans by utilizing a loophole left in the [Fourth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution)[^cite_note-98] in tandem with increased [artificial intelligence](1164.md) or AI capabilities. The increasing utilization of AI in surveillance applications is best illustrated by reports such as [Anthropic](6206236.md)'s AI model [Claude](75879512.md) being used in the [raid on Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States-Venezuela_conflict).[^cite_note-99] Shortly after the raid, CEO of Anthropic [Dario Amodei](74329563.md) warned their technologies could be used on American citizens as permitted by the Fourth Amendment's loophole to produce a comprehensive profile on Americans " automatically and at massive scale."[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Additionally, organizations such as the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation), a digital rights group, have already spoken out against companies such as [Flock Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flock_Safety), a surveillance company, for their utilization of these same AI surveillance technologies.[^cite_note-\:2-102] The organization notably takes issue with how Flock's policing tools are often utilized, such as when their tracing tools were deployed against protestors during the [No Kings Protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Kings_protests).[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-\:2-102]

### Logging postal mail

Main article: [Mail Isolation Control and Tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail_Isolation_Control_and_Tracking)

Under the Mail Isolation Control and Tracking program, the U.S. Postal Service photographs the exterior of every piece of paper mail that is processed in the United States — about 160 billion pieces in 2012. The U.S. Postmaster General stated that the system is primarily used for mail sorting, but the images are available for possible use by law enforcement agencies.[^cite_note-APInterview-August2013-112] Created in 2001 following the anthrax attacks that killed five people, it is a sweeping expansion of a 100-year-old program called "[mail cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail_cover)" which targets people suspected of crimes. Together, the two programs show that postal mail is subject to the same kind of scrutiny that the National Security Agency gives to telephone calls, e-mail, and other forms of electronic communication.[^cite_note-USMailLogging-NYT-July2013-113]

Mail cover surveillance requests are granted for about 30 days, and can be extended for up to 120 days. Images captured under the Mail Isolation Control and Tracking program are retained for a week to 30 days and then destroyed.[^cite_note-APInterview-August2013-112] There are two kinds of mail covers: those related to criminal activity and those requested to protect national security. Criminal activity requests average 15,000 to 20,000 per year, while the number of requests for national security mail covers has not been made public. Neither the Mail Isolation Control and Tracking program nor the mail cover program require prior approval by a judge. For both programs the information gathered is metadata from the outside of the envelope or package for which courts have said there is no [expectation of privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectation_of_privacy). Opening the mail to view its contents would require a [warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_\(law\)) approved by a judge.[^cite_note-USMailLogging-NYT-July2013-113]

### Wiretapping

Billions of dollars per year are spent, by agencies such as the [Information Awareness Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Awareness_Office), [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency), and the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md), to develop, purchase, implement, and operate systems such as [Carnivore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivore_\(FBI\)), [ECHELON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echelon_\(signals_intelligence\)), and [NarusInsight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NarusInsight) to intercept and analyze the immense amount of data that traverses the Internet and telephone system every day.[^cite_note-zdnet-fbi-114]

The [Total Information Awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Information_Awareness) program, of the [Information Awareness Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Awareness_Office), was formed in 2002 by the Pentagon and led by former rear admiral John Poindexter.[^cite_note-115] The program designed numerous technologies to be used to perform mass surveillance. Examples include advanced [speech-to-text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech-to-text) programs (so that phone conversations can be monitored en-masse by a computer, instead of requiring human operators to listen to them), [social network analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network_analysis) software to monitor groups of people and their interactions with each other, and "Human identification at a distance" software which allows computers to identify people on surveillance cameras by their facial features and gait (the way they walk). The program was later renamed "[Terrorism Information Awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Information_Awareness)", after a negative public reaction.

#### Legal foundations

The [Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Assistance_for_Law_Enforcement_Act) (CALEA), passed in 1994, requires that all U.S. telecommunications companies modify their equipment to allow easy [wiretapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_tapping) of telephone, VoIP, and broadband Internet traffic.[^cite_note-eff-calea-archive-116][^cite_note-eff-calea-summary-117][^cite_note-eff-calea-faq-118]

In 1999 two models of [mandatory data retention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_data_retention) were suggested for the US. The first model would record the [IP address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address) assigned to a customer at a specific time. In the second model, "which is closer to what Europe adopted", telephone numbers dialed, contents of Web pages visited, and recipients of e-mail messages must be retained by the [ISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISP) for an unspecified amount of time.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] In 2006 the [International Association of Chiefs of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Chiefs_of_Police) adopted a resolution calling for a "uniform data retention mandate" for "customer subscriber information and source and destination information."[^cite_note-121] The [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) announced in 2011 that criminal investigations "are being frustrated" because no law currently exists to force Internet providers to keep track of what their customers are doing.[^cite_note-122]

The [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation) was involved in a lawsuit ([Hepting v. AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepting_v._AT\&T)) against the telecom giant [AT\&T Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Inc.) for its assistance to the U.S. government in monitoring the communications of millions of American citizens. Recently the documents, which were exposed by a whistleblower who had previously worked for AT\&T, and showed schematics of the massive data mining system, were made public.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

#### Internet communications

The FBI developed the computer programs "[Magic Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Lantern_\(spyware\))" and [CIPAV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIPAV), which it can remotely install on a computer system, in order to monitor a person's computer activity.[^cite_note-teen-busted-in-wired-125]

The NSA has been gathering information on financial records, Internet surfing habits, and monitoring e-mails. It has also performed extensive surveillance on [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network_analysis) such as Facebook.[^cite_note-126] Recently, Facebook has revealed that, in the last six months of 2012, they handed over the private data of between 18,000 and 19,000 users to law enforcement of all types—including local police and federal agencies, such as the FBI, Federal Marshals and the NSA.[^cite_note-127] One form of wiretapping utilized by the NSA is RADON, a bi-directional host tap that can inject Ethernet packets onto the same target. It allows bi-directional exploitation of Denied networks using standard on-net tools. The one limitation of RADON is that it is a USB device that requires a physical connection to a laptop or PC to work. RADON was created by a Massachusetts firm called Netragard. Their founder, Adriel Desautels, said about RADON, "it is our 'safe' malware. RADON is designed to enable us to infect customer systems in a safe and controllable manner. Safe means that every strand is built with an expiration date that, when reached, results in RADON performing an automatic and clean self-removal."[^cite_note-128]

The NSA is also known to have splitter sites in the United States. Splitter sites are places where a copy of every packet is directed to a secret room where it is analyzed by the Narus STA 6400, a deep packet inspection device.[^cite_note-129] Although the only known location is at 611 Folsom Street, San Francisco, California, expert analysis of Internet traffic suggests that there are likely several locations throughout the United States.

#### Advertising data

In September 2022 the EFF and AP revealed their investigation into the use of [advertising IDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_IDs) to develop the [Fog Reveal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fog_Reveal) database.[^cite_note-2022-09-01-EFF-130][^cite_note-2022-09-01-AP-News-131] Fog Reveal aggregates location data from mobile applications, which is then supplied as a service to United States law enforcement agencies.

#### Intelligence apparatus to monitor Americans

Infographic showing the extent of U.S. mass surveillance after 9/11

&#x20;Infographic showing the extent of U.S. mass surveillance after 9/11

Since the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), a vast domestic intelligence apparatus has been built to collect information using FBI, local police, state homeland security offices and military criminal investigators. The intelligence apparatus collects, analyzes and stores information about millions of (if not all) American citizens, most of whom have not been accused of any wrongdoing. Every state and local law enforcement agency is to feed information to federal authorities to support the work of the FBI.[^cite_note-132]

The [PRISM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRISM_\(surveillance_program\)) special source operation system was enabled by the [Protect America Act of 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protect_America_Act_of_2007) under President Bush and the [FISA Amendments Act of 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FISA_Amendments_Act_of_2008), which legally immunized private companies that cooperated voluntarily with US intelligence collection and was renewed by Congress under President Obama in 2012 for five years until December 2017. According to *The Register*, the FISA Amendments Act of 2008 "specifically authorizes intelligence agencies to monitor the phone, email, and other communications of U.S. citizens for up to a week without obtaining a warrant"  when one of the parties is outside the U.S.

PRISM was first publicly revealed on 6 June 2013, after classified documents about the program were leaked to *The Washington Post* and *The Guardian* by [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden).

#### Telephones

In early 2006, *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)* reported that several major telephone companies were cooperating illegally with the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) to monitor the phone records of U.S. citizens, and storing them in a large database known as the [NSA call database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_call_database). This report came on the heels of allegations that the [U.S. government](195149.md) had been conducting electronic surveillance of domestic telephone calls without warrants.[^cite_note-133]

Law enforcement and intelligence services in the United States possess technology to remotely activate the microphones in cell phones in order to listen to conversations that take place nearby the person who holds the phone.[^cite_note-FBI-Flame-134][^cite_note-roving-bugs-135][^cite_note-CluesInMalware-136]

U.S. federal agents regularly use mobile phones to collect location data. The geographical [location of a mobile phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_tracking) (and thus the person carrying it) can be determined easily (whether it is being used or not), using a technique known [multilateration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateration) to calculate the differences in time for a signal to travel from the cell phone to each of several [cell towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_towers) near the owner of the phone.[^cite_note-bbc-phone-locate-137][^cite_note-foxnews-phone-locate-138]

In 2013, the existence of the [Hemisphere Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemisphere_Project), through which AT\&T provides [call detail records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_detail_record) to government agencies, became publicly known.

#### Infiltration of smartphones

As worldwide sales of [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) began exceeding those of [feature phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_phone), the NSA decided to take advantage of the smartphone boom. This is particularly advantageous because the smartphone combines a [myriad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myriad) of data that would interest an intelligence agency, such as social contacts, user behavior, interests, location, photos and [credit card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card) numbers and passwords.[^cite_note-spiegel201399-139]

An internal NSA report from 2010 stated that the spread of the smartphone has been occurring "extremely rapidly"—developments that "certainly complicate traditional target analysis."[^cite_note-spiegel201399-139] According to the document, the NSA has set up [task forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_force) assigned to several smartphone manufacturers and [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_systems), including [Apple Inc.](856.md)'s [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone) and [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) operating system, as well as [Google](1092923.md)'s [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) mobile operating system.[^cite_note-spiegel201399-139] Similarly, Britain's [GCHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCHQ) assigned a team to study and crack the [BlackBerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackBerry).[^cite_note-spiegel201399-139]

Under the heading "iPhone capability", the document notes that there are smaller NSA programs, known as "scripts", that can perform surveillance on 38 different features of the [iPhone 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_3G) and [iPhone 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_4) operating systems. These include the [mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map) feature, [voicemail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voicemail) and photos, as well as [Google Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Earth), [Facebook](7529378.md) and [Yahoo! Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Messenger).[^cite_note-spiegel201399-139]

##### Stingray devices

This section is an excerpt from [Stingray use in United States law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingray_use_in_United_States_law_enforcement).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Stingray_use_in_United_States_law_enforcement\&action=edit)]

The [use of stingrays by United States law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingray_use_in_United_States_law_enforcement) is an investigative technique used by both federal and local law enforcement in the United States to obtain information from cell phones by mimicking a cell phone tower. ### Surveillance cameras

[Wide-area motion imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-area_motion_imagery) (WAMI), also known as wide-area persistent surveillance, is a form of airborne surveillance system that collects pattern-of-life data by recording motion images of an area larger than a city – in sub-meter resolution. This video allows for anyone within the field of regard to be tracked – both live and retroactively, for forensic analysis. The use of sophisticated tracking algorithms applied to the WAMI dataset also enables mass automated geo-location tracking of every vehicle and pedestrian.[^cite_note-youtube.com-142] WAMI sensors are typically mounted on crewed airplanes, drones, blimps and aerostats. WAMI is currently in use on the southern border of the US and in the past has been deployed in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore),[^cite_note-143] [Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio), and [Compton, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California) in 2012.[^cite_note-q125-144][^cite_note-u751-145][^cite_note-Jennings_Winton_Rainey_2014-146] An aerial motion imagery surveillance system was deployed more narrowly in [Lancaster, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_California) starting in 2012.[^cite_note-u751-145][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-x170-149] WAMI systems such as ARGUS WAMI are capable of live viewing and recording a 68 square mile area with enough detail to view pedestrians and vehicles and generate chronographs. These WAMI cameras, such as Gorgon Stare, Angelfire, Hiper Stare, Hawkeye and ARGUS,[^cite_note-150] create airborne video so detailed that pedestrians can be followed across the city through forensic analysis. This allows investigators to rewind and playback the movements of anyone within this 68 square mile area for hours, days or even months at a time depending on the airframe the WAMI sensors are mounted on. JLENS, a surveillance aerostat scheduled for deployment over the east coast of the US, is a form of WAMI that uses sophisticated radar imaging along with electro-optical WAMI sensors to enable mass geo-location tracking of ground vehicles.

There has been some resistance to the domestic deployment of WAMI. Its use in Compton, California was intentionally hidden from the public, including from the mayor, because the sheriff's department believed many people would object to the program.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-Jennings_Winton_Rainey_2014-146][^cite_note-q570-152][^cite_note-x170-149] The Compton program was revealed and publicized by the American Civil Liberties Union,[^cite_note-153] Teame Zazzu,[^cite_note-youtube.com-142] and [The Center for Investigative Reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Center_for_Investigative_Reporting).[^cite_note-Jennings_Winton_Rainey_2014-146][^cite_note-u751-145][^cite_note-Schulz_Pike_2014-154]

After becoming aware of the deployment of WAMI system in her jurisdiction, Compton Mayor Aja Brown proposed requiring that the public be notified before authorities implement monitoring equipment.[^cite_note-Jennings_Winton_Rainey_2014-146]

PeSEAS[^cite_note-155] and PerMIATE[^cite_note-156] software automate and record the movement observed in the WAMI video.[^cite_note-157] This technology uses software to track and record the movements of pedestrians and vehicles using automatic object recognition software across the entire frame, generating "tracklets" or chronographs of every car and pedestrian movements. 24/7 deployment of this technology has been suggested by the DHS on spy blimps such as the recently killed Blue Devil Airship.[^cite_note-158]

[Traffic cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road-rule_enforcement_camera), which were meant to help enforce traffic laws at intersections, have also sparked some controversy, due to their use by law enforcement agencies for purposes unrelated to traffic violations.[^cite_note-159] These cameras also work as transit choke-points that allow individuals inside the vehicle to be positively identified and license plate data to be collected and time stamped for cross reference with airborne WAMI such as ARGUS and HAWKEYE used by police and Law Enforcement.[^cite_note-160]

The [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) is funding networks of surveillance cameras in cities and towns as part of its efforts to combat terrorism.[^cite_note-161] In February 2009, [Cambridge, MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) rejected the cameras due to privacy concerns.[^cite_note-162]

In July 2020, the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) reported that the San Francisco Police Department (SFPD) used a camera network in the city's Business Improvement District amid protests against police violence.[^cite_note-\:1-163] The report claims that the SFPD's usage of the camera network went beyond investigating footage, likening the department's access to real-time video feeds as "indiscriminate surveillance of protestors."[^cite_note-\:1-163]

### Surveillance drones

Three police surveillance drones overhead at the [2026 Delaney Hall protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaney_Hall_hunger_strike_and_protests) shortly before [riot police](1148542.md) were deployed

Earlier in 2012, Congress passed a US\$63 billion bill that will grant four years of additional funding to the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA). In February 2013, a spokesman for the [Los Angeles Police Department](218985.md) explained that these drones would initially be deployed in large public gatherings, including major protests. The FBI and [Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) have used drones for surveillance of protests by the [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) movement.[^cite_note-167]

## Uses of intercepted data

Most of the [NSA's collected data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surveillance_disclosures_\(2013%E2%80%93present\)) which was seen by human eyes (i.e., used by NSA operatives) was used in accordance with the stated objective of combating terrorism.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

In addition to combatting terrorism, these surveillance programs have been employed to assess the foreign policy and economic stability of other countries.[^cite_note-spiegellaurapoitras-197]

According to reports by Brazil's O Globo newspaper, the collected data was also used to target "commercial secrets".[^cite_note-198] In a statement addressed to the [National Congress of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil), journalist [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald) testified that the U.S. government uses [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) as a "pretext" for clandestine surveillance in order to compete with other countries in the "business, industrial and economic fields".[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

In an interview with *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* published on 12 August 2013, former NSA Director [Michael Hayden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hayden_\(general\)) admitted that "We \[the NSA] steal secrets. We're number one in it". Hayden also added that "We steal stuff to make you safe, not to make you rich".[^cite_note-spiegellaurapoitras-197]

According to documents seen by the news agency [Reuters](18998750.md), information obtained in this way is subsequently funnelled to authorities across the nation to help them launch criminal investigations of Americans.[^cite_note-reuters3-201] Federal agents are then instructed to "recreate" the investigative trail in order to "cover up" where the information originated,[^cite_note-reuters3-201] known as [parallel construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_construction). (Were the true origins known, the evidence and resulting case might be invalidated as "[fruit of the poisonous tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_of_the_poisonous_tree)", a legal doctrine designed to deter [abuse of power](4308572.md) that prevents [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence) or subsequent events being used in a case if they resulted from a search or other process that does not conform to legal requirements.)

According to NSA Chief Compliance Officer John DeLong, most violations of the NSA's rules were self-reported, and most often involved spying on personal [love interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_interest) using surveillance technology of the agency.[^cite_note-202]

Most [agricultural surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_surveillance_\(disambiguation\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) is not covert and is carried out by government agencies such as [APHIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APHIS) (USDA's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service).[^cite_note-GovInfo-203] DHS has lamented the limited surveillance coverage provided by these inspections and works to augment this protection with their own resources.[^cite_note-GovInfo-203]
