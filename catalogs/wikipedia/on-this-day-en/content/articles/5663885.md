The **Slovenian Parliament** ([Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language): *Slovenski parlament*) is the informal designation of the general [representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) body of the [Slovenian](27338.md) nation and the [legislative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of the Republic of Slovenia.

According to the [Constitution of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Slovenia), the general representative body of the Slovenian nation is the [National Assembly](1025128.md). The general public in Slovenia often refer to the National Assembly alone as the Slovenian Parliament.[^cite_note-1] However, the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Slovenia\)), the representative body of basic social groups, also performs a further, if minor, part of the legislative function.[^cite_note-Celje-2]

The opinions of experts and of the general Slovenian public on whether the Slovenian Parliament is [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) or [unicameral](204304.md) differ, although most consider it to be incompletely bicameral.[^cite_note-Lakota-3][^cite_note-4] In 2008, the [Constitutional Court of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Slovenia) recognized the Slovenian Parliament as incompletely bicameral.[^cite_note-US2008-5]

## History

The first Slovenian legislature was the *[Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag)* or Diet of the Duchy of Carniola, the most predominantly Slovene-populated (95% in 1910) region of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire). Established as part of the reforms that attended the [Dual Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Compromise_of_1867) of 1867, the devolved legislature sat until the end of the empire in 1918. It consisted of a single chamber of thirty-seven delegates, whose competencies extended solely to local matters.&#x20;

The interwar [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) was a [centralist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidovdan_Constitution), with a national parliament but no regional assemblies. The legal continuity of the present Slovenian Parliament can therefore be traced to the Constituent Assembly of the [People's Republic of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Slovenia), a unicameral body with 120 delegates elected on 27 October 1946.&#x20;

The Assembly had been convened to draft a constitution; having done so, it began functioning as a legislature, and was renamed to the People's Assembly of the People's Republic of Slovenia on 17 January 1947.&#x20;

In 1953, the Assembly was made bicameral, encompassing two houses ("*zbor*", usually also translated as "assembly".) The generally-representative Assembly of the Republic was supplemented with an Assembly of Producers, intended for representatives of state-owned industries.

In 1963, a new constitution renamed the polity the Socialist Republic of Slovenia. The new Assembly of the Socialist Republic of Slovenia was made a rare quintocameral legislature, comprising an:

- Assembly of the Republic

- Economic Assembly

- Educational and Cultural Assembly

- Socio-Medical Assembly

- Organisational and Political Assembly

This was found to be unwieldy, and a 1968 amendment combined the middle three *zbors* into a single Assembly of Labor Associations, and renamed the final one to the Socio-Political Assembly.&#x20;

A more radical reform followed soon afterward, when the devolutionary [1974 Yugoslav Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Yugoslav_Constitution) abolished the Assembly of the Republic altogether and transferred its competencies to the Socio-Political Assembly. Unlike the Assembly of the Republic - which had been de-facto dominated by the [League of Communists of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Communists_of_Slovenia) (ZKS), but was nominally a representative legislature elected by universal suffrage - the Socio-Political Assembly was legally a single-party body. Its fifty seats were formally allocated (ten each) to the ZKS and four of its subsidiary organizations:&#x20;

- the League of Socialist Youth of Slovenia (ZSMS)

- the Socialist League of the Working People of Slovenia (SZDLS)

- the League of Trade Unions, and

- the Association of Veterans of the Slovenian National Liberation War

Delegates were elected indirectly, in [Soviet-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_Soviet_Union) single-candidate approval votes held by similar socio-political assemblies established at the municipal level.&#x20;

Despite this fundamental change, the 1974 Constitution retained the tricameral structure established in 1968. The Socio-Political Assembly was supplemented with a new Municipalities' Assembly (150 delegates) and an Assembly of United Labor (60-65 delegates). The latter *zbor* was a continuation of the 1968 Assembly of Labor Associations, and was (like it) geared toward the representation of economic and occupational interests (though it also contained representatives from state agencies, and a single delegate from the [federal armed forces](536643.md).)&#x20;

[Legislative reforms in 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989) saw the word "Socialist" dropped from the republic's name and the ZKS surrendering its privileged status. In the multi-party elections of the same year, the three *zbors* were retained, though their number of deputies was equalized at 80 each, for a total of 240. The Socio-Political Assembly was converted into a general representative body, while the other two *zbors* retained their specialized representation, but now on a contested basis. The basic structure of the 1974 constitution therefore endured until the first elections under the [1991 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Slovenia) could be held in December of 1992.&#x20;

The new constitution abolished all three *zbors*. The Socio-Political Assembly was replaced by the [National Assembly](1025128.md), a democratically-elected general representative body with ninety delegates. The functions of the Municipalities' Assembly and Assembly of United Labor were informally combined in the new 40-seat [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Slovenia\)), with 18 seats allotted to local interests and the remaining 22 to basic social groups such as employers, students, and workers.&#x20;

## Seat

Main article: [National Assembly Building of the Republic of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_Building_of_the_Republic_of_Slovenia)

The National Assembly and the National Council convene in a [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist) palace known as the "Slovenian Parliament" and sited in [Republic Square, Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Square,_Ljubljana). It was built between 1954 and 1959 by the architect [Vinko Glanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinko_Glanz?action=edit\&redlink=1). An [unrealized project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ple%C4%8Dnik_Parliament) for a Slovenian Parliament building, designed by the architect [Jože Plečnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C5%BEe_Ple%C4%8Dnik) in the late 1940s, features on the [Slovenian euro coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenian_euro_coins).
