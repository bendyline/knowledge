On 23 September 2026, the [Ethiopian Peoples' Forces Coalition for Survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Peoples'_Forces_Coalition_for_Survival), a coalition of various Ethiopian rebel and insurgent groups, launched a large-scale military offensive against the [Federal Government of Ethiopia](77016.md) in [Tigray](357350.md), [Afar](2483633.md) and [Amhara](1746709.md).[^cite_note-martinplaut-10]

The [Tigray People's Liberation Front](1276759.md) (TPLF) claimed that it launched a "[defensive war](1079530.md)" against the [federal government of Ethiopia](77016.md).[^cite_note-reuters12-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-BBC_Tigray_forces_seize-13] The offensive marks the largest escalation in fighting since the [Tigray war](65760352.md) (2020–2022), which [killed an estimated 600,000 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualties_of_the_Tigray_war).[^cite_note-Staff-14]

## Background

### Tigray war and Pretoria agreement

Further information: [Tigrayan peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayan_peace_process), [Ethiopia–Tigray peace agreement](72171064.md), and [Reinstatement of the Tigray Regional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinstatement_of_the_Tigray_Regional_Council)

The TPLF was effectively the ruling party of Ethiopia, dominating the [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF), from 1991–2018. In 2018, [Abiy Ahmed](56624029.md) [was elected](56624029.md#EPRDF_leadership_election) leader of the EPDRF and thus became prime minister. The [Tigray war](65760352.md) took place under Abiy's leadership from November 2020 to November 2022, when Ethiopia and the TPLF signed the [Pretoria agreement](72171064.md). The situation remained unstable, with officials from the [Amhara Region](1746709.md) continuing to occupy [Western Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Tigray) and the government launching a series of drone strikes in 2025 while the TPLF amassed troops at the border.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In May 2025, the TPLF declared the restoration of the pre-war administration and installed the TPLF's chairman [Debretsion Gebremichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debretsion_Gebremichael)[^cite_note-BBC_Tigray_forces_seize-13] as Tigray's regional president, creating a rival government to that of the pro-government [Interim Regional Administration of Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Regional_Administration_of_Tigray).[^cite_note-Rosser-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Outbreak of fighting and escalation

During July 2026 the TPLF declared the [Pretoria agreement](72171064.md) "effectively dead".[^cite_note-aljazeera-21] On 1 August 2026 a battle lasting several hours broke out between the Ethiopian army and the TPLF near the Sudan border. The incident represented the most serious escalation since the end of the Tigray War in 2022.[^cite_note-aljazeera-21][^cite_note-22]

In the weeks following the fighting, the ENDF carried out numerous airstrikes in Tigray.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The regional capital of [Mekelle](1221559.md) was hit by a series of drone strikes in late August, including an attack on 20 August against a civilian compound with no obvious military targets that wounded 13 civilians.[^cite_note-HRW_indiscriminate_Tigray-25]

## Offensive

### Rebel coalition and Alamata school strikes

On 20 September 2026, seven Ethiopian rebel groups from Tigray and the [Somali Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Region) announced the formation of the [Ethiopian People's Forces Coalition for Survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Forces_Coalition_for_Survival) (EPFAS),[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-\:2-27] a coalition comprising the [Amhara Fano National Movement](82166505.md) (AFNM), the TPLF, the [Oromo Liberation Army](41461749.md), the [Ogaden National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_National_Liberation_Front),[^cite_note-29] the [Afar Revolutionary Democratic Unity Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_Revolutionary_Democratic_Unity_Front) (per *[Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English)*)[^cite_note-Rosser-18] or the [Afar National Unity Revolutionary Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_National_Unity_Revolutionary_Front) (per *[Addis Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Standard)*),[^cite_note-AddisStandard_seven_announce-30] the [Benishangul People's Liberation Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benishangul_People's_Liberation_Movement), and the [Gumuz People's Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumuz_People's_Democratic_Movement).[^cite_note-Rosser-18] The coalition, consisting of some members which fought against each other during the 2020–2022 Tigray war,[^cite_note-\:2-27] pledged to overthrow the [Abiy Ahmed](56624029.md)-led government towards establishing a [transitional government](598010.md).[^cite_note-Rosser-18] Eritrean officials denied involvement in the formation of the coalition.[^cite_note-\:3-31] Furthermore, despite concluding that EPFAS would indeed concern the government and would likely not agree to [de-escalation](1285569.md) measures, experts questioned the group's collaborative capabilities.[^cite_note-\:2-27][^cite_note-\:3-31]

On 22 September 2026, Ethiopia launched at least five drones and carried out more than 21 airstrikes; two of the drone strikes hit two schools in [Alamata](11602398.md), and left at least 14 civilians dead and injured 22 others.[^cite_note-BBC_Tigray_forces_seize-13][^cite_note-Rosser-18] After the strikes, the TPLF accused the government of launching an "open invasion" and declared the start of a "defensive war" in response.[^cite_note-BBC_Tigray_forces_seize-13]

### Timeline

#### 23 September

On the morning of 23 September 2026, the TDF launched an offensive and seized the airports of [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum), [Shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire,_Ethiopia), and the [Alula Aba Nega Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alula_Aba_Nega_Airport) in [Mekelle](1221559.md) in Tigray,[^cite_note-martinplaut-10][^cite_note-Rosser-18][^cite_note-32] causing [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) to cancel all flights to the region. The TDF also launched offensives on government positions in [the nearby regions](690337.md) of [Afar](2483633.md) and [Amhara](1746709.md), including [Zata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zata), Dinqa, and [Soqota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqota) on the Tigray–Amhara border, and [Taunsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunsa) near the Tigray–Afar border.[^cite_note-martinplaut-10][^cite_note-BBC_Tigray_forces_seize-13] Fighting broke out along the [Kobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobo,_Ethiopia)–Tumuga front between Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF) troops and a joint force of the TDF and the AFNM.[^cite_note-martinplaut-10]

The regional government of Afar said that [Afari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) forces would defend themselves from the TDF-led invasion. In neighboring Amhara, government forces were launching strikes using heavy weapons in [Lalibela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalibela). The firing targeted [Fano](65998148.md) militants, as they conducted an offensive.[^cite_note-reuters12-11] According to *[Washington Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Examiner)*, EPFAS alleged that all of its factions carried out attacks in their respective regions, including the OLA.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-washingtonexaminer-34]

#### 24 September

On 24 September, fighting continued along the Tigray–Afar border and in parts of the Amhara region. In Amhara, clashes were reported in Gazgibela [woreda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woreda) in the Wag Hemra zone, as well as [Gidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gidan), [Lasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasta), and Raya Kobo woredas in the North Wello zone. The fighting involved the ENDF, the [Tigray Peace Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_Peace_Force), [Amhara](1746709.md) [Riot Police](1148542.md), and local militias on one side, and the AFNM and TDF on the other.[^cite_note-acleddata-35] A new front also opened near the [Tekezé River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekez%C3%A9_River) in western Tigray.[^cite_note-acleddata-35] On the same day, Fano insurgents captured Melese Ashagirea, a [Colonel](199023.md) for the [Ethiopian Army](68039791.md) in Amhara.[^cite_note-36]

Fighting also occurred near Lalibela, southwest of [Alamata](11602398.md), has caused the ENDF to close the Lalibela airport.[^cite_note-\:1-8] The rebel forces attempted to advance to Sekota, west of Alamata, but the ENDF reportedly repelled the offensive.[^cite_note-\:1-8] [Armed Conflict Location and Event Data](43040226.md) (ACLED) reported that the ENDF and TDF clashed near Tselimoy, southwest of [Shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire,_Ethiopia). The ENDF also conducted airstrikes in the area.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

#### 25 September

On 25 September, Fano insurgents claimed to have captured two ENDF colonels, Meles Ashagrie and deputy commander Edmealem Amare, during fighting in North Wollo, Amhara. According to Fano, the officers and their remaining troops were surrounded while retreating after suffering a defeat. Fano also claimed to have seized 10 [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Automotive_Plant) military trucks and weapons, including artillery ammunition, while destroying two additional trucks.[^cite_note-37] In Afar, clashes were reported in [Yalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalo_\(Ethiopian_District\)), while a drone strike hit Hayelom Tabia, [Sheraro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraro), Tigray.[^cite_note-38] At the orders of the TPLF, [Safaricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaricom) and [Ethio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethio_Telecom) [Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethio_Telecom) cut off access ⁠to phone and internet networks in Tigray.[^cite_note-Staff-14]

ACLED reported that the composition and level of military involvement of EPFAS remained contested. The [Gumuz People's Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumuz_People's_Democratic_Movement) denied membership in the alliance and stated that it did not have an armed wing, while one faction of the [Ogaden National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_National_Liberation_Front) denied membership and another confirmed its participation. ACLED also reported that the [Oromo Liberation Army](41461749.md) had indicated that its primary interest in the alliance was removing the federal government rather than conducting a coordinated military operation.[^cite_note-39]

Fighting continued in northern Ethiopia, including around [Abala](14726138.md) in the Afar Region. Reports differed over control of the town, while claims concerning territorial gains and battlefield casualties remained difficult to independently verify.[^cite_note-40]

On 27 September, the ENDF reportedly recaptured the strategic town of [Sekota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekota) from Tigrayan forces.[^cite_note-41] Fighting continued around Sama, Soqota and [Raya Alamata](11602398.md), with government forces reportedly advancing towards Alamata. In the Afar Region, fighting also continued around Abala.[^cite_note-42]

In Afar, a humanitarian source told [Agence France-Presse](471981.md) (AFP) that [Erebti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erebti) had been captured by Tigrayan forces following heavy fighting involving artillery and that the forces were subsequently advancing towards [Afdera](7354673.md). On the same day, a drone struck the headquarters of Tigrai TV in [Mekelle](1221559.md) at approximately 09\:00 local time (06\:00 GMT), damaging the building and forcing the station off air. No casualties were immediately reported.[^cite_note-AFP27-43] Later that day, a drone, suspected to have been launched from rebel forces, struck the Debre Zeit Air Force Base while Abiy Ahmed was at the compound celebrating a holiday. Three casualties have reportedly been recorded.[^cite_note-44]

#### 28 September

On 28 September, the [Tigray Peace Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_Peace_Force) announced the capture of the city of [Alamata](11602398.md), in Southern [Tigray](357350.md).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] During the clashes in [Alamata](11602398.md), at least 52 civilians were killed.[^cite_note-47]

#### 29 September

The governor of the Afar region, [Awol Arba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awol_Arba?action=edit\&redlink=1), accused the [Eritrean Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Defence_Forces) of infiltrating the region and stated that they were allegedly attempting to cut a road linking Djibouti and Ethiopia. Eritrea did not respond to the immediate accusations.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] the governor of Afar Region told Al Jazeera that 2/3 of the region is under Eritrean control.[^cite_note-52] The Eritrean opposition group [Brigade N’hamedu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_Nhamedu) responded to the reports stating their condemnation in the alleged Eritrean direct involvement in the offensive.[^cite_note-53] Despite these statements, independent mapping and conflict data from [Armed Conflict Location and Event Data](43040226.md) did not indicate the presence of a large Eritrean offensive,[^cite_note-54] contrasting with the claims.

**30 September**

About 20 Israeli tourists stranded in [Lalibela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalibela) amid the fighting were evacuated by helicopter to a safe area, following three days of efforts by the [Israeli Foreign Ministry](18133296.md) and embassy in [Addis Ababa](52593.md). The tourists couldn't leave because flights from the area were suspended due to the fighting.[^cite_note-55]

#### 1 October

In Addis Ababa, explosions were heard after drone strikes were conducted by rebel forces in the region. The [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Ethiopia\)) then ordered the closure of the Eritrean embassy in [Addis Ababa](52593.md) after accusing them of "direct and indirect hostility" and that caused them to break ties.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] same day, pro-government forces regained control of [Abala](14726138.md) and [Erebti](7354773.md) towns in the Afar region.[^cite_note-58] The [OSINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSINT) research group Hamer Intel reported the existence of an "Eritrean incursion" in Northern Afar region,[^cite_note-59] without confirming its size.&#x20;

#### 2 October

During a [BBC](19344654.md) interview, an unnamed pro-government militia commander fighting in Tigray stated that there were Eritrean troops fighting in the area alongside the rebels. The [Eritrean Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ministers_of_Eritrea) of Information [Yemane Gebremeskel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemane_Gebremeskel?action=edit\&redlink=1) denied the presence of Eritrean troops in Tigray Region.[^cite_note-60]

#### 3 October

According to [Reuters](18998750.md), the ENDF recaptured Mekelle's [Alula Aba Nega Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alula_Aba_Nega_Airport), with the city being encircled by ENDF and pro-government militias.[^cite_note-61] The Ethiopian federal forces entered Mekelle after TPLF militants withdrew and abandoned the city.[^cite_note-Mekelle-3][^cite_note-62]

## Peace process

See also: [Ethiopia–Tigray peace agreement](72171064.md) and [Tigrayan peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayan_peace_process)

The takeover of the three Tigray Region airports by Tigrayan forces was a violation of the [Pretoria Agreement](72171064.md) according to the [African Union Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Commission) (AUC) [chairperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson_of_the_African_Union_Commission), [Mahamoud Ali Youssouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamoud_Ali_Youssouf). On 23 September 2026, Youssouf requested [Olusegun Obasanjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olusegun_Obasanjo), African Union High Representative for the Horn of Africa, to carry out negotiations to preserve the peace process.[^cite_note-AU_AUC_chair_calls_deescalation-63]

## Consequences

### Withdrawal from Somalia

Further information: [Ethiopian–Somali conflict § AMISOM/ATMIS/AUSSOM and non-AU Ethiopian forces (2014 – present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian%E2%80%93Somali_conflict#AMISOM/ATMIS/AUSSOM_and_non-AU_Ethiopian_forces_\(2014_%E2%80%93_present\))

The Ethiopian army announced plans to withdraw 3,000 soldiers from Somalia, allegedly without informing the Somali government, as a direct consequence of the renewed hostilities and the need of manpower.[^cite_note-\:4-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] This came despite the fact that Ethiopia was described by the *Somali Guardian* as one of the most significant forces to counter [al-Shabaab](8726463.md), this will give the Islamic Group more freedoms to advance in [Somalia](27358.md), there will be less resistance between the 2 sides.[^cite_note-\:4-64] An analysis by *Horn Review* on 2 October 2026 noted how this could present an opportunity to Al-Shabaab's "opportunity-driven model" to strike against Ethiopia, akin to its [offensive in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shabaab_invasion_of_Ethiopia), in a coalition with the [Ogaden National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_National_Liberation_Front).[^cite_note-67]

### Diplomatic crisis with Eritrea

Main article: [Eritrea–Ethiopia relations](57858711.md)

On 1 October, 2026, the [Ethiopian Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Ethiopia\)) announced it was shutting its embassy in [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) due to the alleged Eritrean involvement in the conflict and expelled 10 diplomats ordering them to leave the country within 48 hours and declaring them as [persona non grata](521241.md).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-\:5-69] In response, Eritrea announced the severance of all diplomatic ties with it's [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](34015067.md) saying on [X](9988187.md) that it "had no choice but to reciprocate".[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-\:5-69] Eritrea stated that one of its fears was that Ethiopia would utilize the conflict to initiate an offensive on the port of [Assab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assab),[^cite_note-71] and added that Ethiopia's latest escalations signified a "publicly pronounced [declaration of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_war)" and an attempt at [regime change](154074.md).[^cite_note-72]

### Communication disruption

Due to the intense fighting in Northern Ethiopia, the internet connection in the area was disrupted, alongside telephone lines, which prevented news agencies and families from contacting external regions and individuals within the region itself.[^cite_note-73]

## War crimes

See also: [War crimes in the Tigray war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crimes_in_the_Tigray_war)

According to [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) (HRW), the ENDF 20 August strike against the Daero area of [Mekelle](1221559.md), during the leadup to the September conflict, was "unlawfully indiscriminate" since it had "no evident military targets". HRW's evidence included interviews with three survivors, two medical workers, and two residents, and analysis of photographic, video, and satellite evidence. The 20 August attack was the dropping of a bomb by an aircraft on a residential compound for teachers around 11\:00 local time. A 4 m (13 ft) diameter crater resulted from the bombing.[^cite_note-HRW_indiscriminate_Tigray-25]

HRW recommended investigation into the 20 August Daero attack as a possible [war crime](48626.md).[^cite_note-HRW_indiscriminate_Tigray-25]
