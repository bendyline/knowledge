# Western Europe

Not to be confused with [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union).

&#x20;  **Western Europe** is the western region of [Europe](9239.md). The region's extent varies depending on context. The concept of "the West" appeared in Europe in juxtaposition to "the East" and originally applied to the Western half of the ancient [Mediterranean world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_world), the [Latin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_West) of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), and "[Western Christendom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christendom)". Beginning with the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) and the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery), roughly from the [15th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_century), the concept of *Europe* as "the [West](21208200.md)" slowly became distinguished from and eventually replaced the dominant use of "Christendom" as the preferred [endonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym) within the area.[^cite_note-1] By the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) and the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), the concepts of "[Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe)" and "Western Europe" were more regularly used.[^cite_note-2] The distinctiveness of Western Europe became most apparent during the [Cold War](325329.md), when Europe was divided for 46 years by the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain) (1945–1991) into the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc) by the [US](3434750.md)-led [NATO](21133.md) and the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) by the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-led [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) between the [two global superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union%E2%80%93United_States_relations), each characterised by distinct political and economical systems.[^cite_note-3]

### Classical antiquity and medieval origins



[Schism of 1054 (East–West Schism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism) in [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcedonian_Christianity)

[^cite_note-4]

[^cite_note-5]

Prior to the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) conquest, a large part of Western Europe had adopted the newly developed [La Tène culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_culture). As the Roman domain expanded, a cultural and linguistic division appeared between the mainly [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek)-speaking eastern provinces, which had formed the highly urbanised [Hellenistic civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), and the western territories, which in contrast largely adopted the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) language. This cultural and linguistic division was eventually reinforced by the later political east–west division of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). The [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) and the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) controlled the two divergent regions between the 3rd and the 5th centuries.

The division between these two was enhanced during [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity) and the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) by a number of events. The [Western Roman Empire collapsed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire), starting the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages). By contrast, the Eastern Roman Empire, mostly known as the Greek or [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), survived and even thrived for another 1000 years. The rise of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire) in the west, and in particular the [Great Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East-West_Schism) between [Eastern Orthodoxy](10186.md) and [Roman Catholicism](606848.md), enhanced the cultural and religious distinctiveness between Eastern and Western Europe.

After the [conquest of the Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Byzantine_Empire), center of the Eastern Orthodox Church, by the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in the 15th century, and the gradual fragmentation of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) (which had replaced the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire)), the division between Roman Catholic and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) became more important in Europe than that with Eastern Orthodoxy.

In [East Asia](19605700.md), Western Europe was historically known as *taixi* in China and *taisei* in Japan, which literally translates as the "[Far West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_West_\(Taixi\))". The term Far West became synonymous with Western Europe in China during the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty). The Italian Jesuit priest [Matteo Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ricci) was one of the first writers in China to use the Far West as an Asian counterpart to the European concept of the [Far East](11672.md). In Ricci's writings, Ricci referred to himself as "Matteo of the Far West".[^cite_note-6] The term was still in use in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

### Religion

[Christianity](5211.md) is the largest religion in Western Europe. According to a 2018 study by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), 71.0% of Western Europeans identified as Christians.[^cite_note-7]

In 1054, the [East–West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism) divided Christianity into [Western Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity) and [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity). This split Europe in two, with Western Europe primarily under the [Catholic Church](606848.md), and much of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) under the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md). Ever since the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) in the 16th century, [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) has also been a major denomination in Europe, mostly in the West.

### Cold War



Political spheres of influence in Europe during the [Cold War](325329.md); neutral countries (shaded grey) considered informally Western-oriented but not formally aligned to the West

During the four [decades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decade) of the [Cold War](325329.md), the definition of East and West was simplified by the existence of the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc). A number of historians and social scientists view the Cold War definition of Western and Eastern Europe as outdated or relegating.[^cite_note-cotf.edu-8][^cite_note-review-9][^cite_note-10]

During the final stages of [World War II](32927.md), the future of Europe was decided between the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in the 1945 [Yalta Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference), between the [British prime minister](24150.md), [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), the [U.S. president](24113.md), [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), and the [Premier of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Soviet_Union), [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin).

Post-war Europe was divided into two major spheres: the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc), influenced by the [United States](3434750.md), and the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc), influenced by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). With the onset of the Cold War, Europe was divided by the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain). This term had been used during [World War II](32927.md) by German [Propaganda Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Ministry_of_Public_Enlightenment_and_Propaganda) [Joseph Goebbels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Goebbels) and, later, Count [Lutz Schwerin von Krosigk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutz_Graf_Schwerin_von_Krosigk) in the last days of the war; however, its use was hugely popularised by Winston Churchill, who used it in his famous "Sinews of Peace" address on 5 March 1946 at [Westminster College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_College_\(Missouri\)) in [Fulton, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulton,_Missouri):

> From [Stettin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczecin) in the [Baltic](3335.md) to [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) in the [Adriatic](23275478.md) an *iron curtain* has descended across the Continent. Behind that line lie all the capitals of the ancient states of [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe). [Warsaw](32908.md), [Berlin](3354.md), [Prague](23844.md), [Vienna](55866.md), [Budapest](36787.md), [Belgrade](55904.md), [Bucharest](36877.md) and [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia); all these famous cities and the populations around them lie in what I must call the Soviet sphere, and all are subject, in one form or another, not only to Soviet influence but to a very high and in some cases increasing measure of control from Moscow.

Although some countries were officially [neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_country), they were classified according to the nature of their political and economic systems. This division largely defines the popular perception and understanding of Western Europe and its borders with [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) on the east side. On the western side is the Atlantic ocean.

The world changed dramatically with the fall of the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain) in 1989. [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) peacefully absorbed [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), in the [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification). [Comecon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comecon) and the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) were dissolved, and in 1991, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) ceased to exist. Several countries which had been part of the Soviet Union regained full independence.

### Western European Union



Member states of the [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union) (1995–2011)

In 1948 the [Treaty of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brussels) was signed between [Belgium](3343.md), [France](5843419.md), [Luxembourg](17515.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md). It was further revisited in 1954 at the [Paris Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_and_Paris_Conferences#Paris), when the [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union) was established. It was declared defunct in 2011 after the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon), and the Treaty of Brussels was terminated. When the Western European Union was dissolved, it had 10 member countries. Additionally, it had 6 associate member countries, 7 associate partner countries and 5 observer countries.

## Climate



European climate. The [Köppen-Geiger climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification) map is presented by the Climatic Research Unit of the University of East Anglia and the Global Precipitation Climatology Center of the Deutscher Wetterdienst.

The climate of Western Europe varies from [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) in the coasts of [Italy](14532.md), [Portugal](23033.md) and [Spain](26667.md) to [alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) in the [Pyrenees](24707.md) and the [Alps](981.md). The [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) of the south is dry and warm. The western and northwestern parts have a mild, generally humid climate, influenced by the [North Atlantic Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Current). Western Europe is a [heatwave](293270.md) hotspot, exhibiting upward trends that are three-to-four times faster compared to the rest of the northern midlatitudes.[^cite_note-18]

## Languages

See also: [Languages of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Europe), [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages), and [List of Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indo-European_languages)

Western European languages mostly fall within two [Indo-European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages) families: the [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages), descended from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire); and the [Germanic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages), whose ancestor language ([Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language)) came from southern [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia).[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-19] Romance languages are spoken primarily in the southern and central part of Western Europe, Germanic languages in the northern part (the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles) and the [Low Countries](51402.md)), as well as a large part of [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe) and [Central Europe](5188.md).[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-19]

Other Western European languages include the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) group (that is, [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language), [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic), [Manx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manx_language), [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language), [Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_language) and [Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breton_language)[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-19]) and [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language), the only currently living [Old European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_European_languages) [language isolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_isolate).[^cite_note-20]

Multilingualism and the protection of regional and minority languages are recognised political goals in Western Europe today. The [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) [Framework Convention for the Protection of National Minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_Convention_for_the_Protection_of_National_Minorities) and the Council of Europe's [European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Charter_for_Regional_or_Minority_Languages) set up a legal framework for language rights in Europe.[^cite_note-21]

## Economy

Western Europe is one of the richest regions of the world. [Germany](11867.md) has the highest [gross domestic product](12594.md) in Europe and the largest financial surplus of any country, [Luxembourg](17515.md) has the world's highest GDP per capita, and Germany has the highest [net national wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_total_wealth) of any European state.[^cite_note-22]

[Switzerland](26748.md) and Luxembourg have the highest [average wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_average_wage) in the world, in nominal and [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity), respectively. [Norway](21241.md) ranks highest in the world on the [Social Progress Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Progress_Index).[^cite_note-23]
