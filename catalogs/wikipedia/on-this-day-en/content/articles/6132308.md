The **Federal Senate** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Senado Federal*) is the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) of the [National Congress of Brazil](690287.md). When created under the [Imperial Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1824) in 1824, it was based on the [House of Lords](13658.md) of the [British Parliament](13964.md), but since the [Proclamation of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Republic_\(Brazil\)) in 1889 and under the [first republican Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1891) the Federal Senate has resembled the [United States Senate](24909346.md).

The current [president of the Federal Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Federal_Senate_\(Brazil\)) is [Davi Alcolumbre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davi_Alcolumbre), a member of [UNIÃO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_Union) from [Amapá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amap%C3%A1). He was re-elected in February 2025 for his two-year non-consecutive term, as he had already led the Senate between 2019-21 during the [presidendy of Jair Bolsonaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Jair_Bolsonaro).

## Membership

The Senate has 81 members, serving an eight-year [term of office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_of_office). There are three senators from each of the country's 27 federal units, the [Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_District_\(Brazil\)) and the 26 [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil). Elections are [staggered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staggered_elections) so that either one-third or two-thirds of senators in each federal unit are up for election every four years. The [most recent election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election#Federal_Senate_elections) took place in 2022, where one-third of the Senate was elected.

Elections are held under the [first-past-the-post](261709.md) and [block voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality-at-large_voting) systems. In years when a third of members are up for election, voters can cast only one vote and the candidate who receives a [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)) of votes within their state is elected. In years when two-thirds of members are up for election, voters can cast two votes. People can not vote for the same candidate twice, but each party can field up to two candidates in each state. The two highest-placed candidates in each state are elected.

## History

Following [Brazil's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil), in 1822, Emperor [Pedro I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_I_of_Brazil) summoned the [Legislative and Constituent General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constituent_Assembly_\(1823\)) to draft the country's first Constitution. After several disagreements with the elected constituent deputies (which included representatives from present-day Uruguay, then [a province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatina) of the [Brazilian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil)) over the Emperor's role, he [dissolved the Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_Agony) in November 1823 and tasked his Council of State with drafting a new Constitution. The following year, he issued [Brazil's first Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1824), which established the [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_\(Brazil\)) as the country's legislative branch, with the [Chamber of Deputies](1589485.md) as the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house), and the Senate as the upper house.&#x20;

The Imperial Senate was modelled on the [House of Lords](13658.md) of the [British Parliament](13964.md).[^cite_note-2] It had 50 members, who [served for life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator_for_life) and represented the [Empire's provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Brazil), each with a number of senators proportional to their population. In addition to these elected senators, [daughters and sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_imperial_family) of the Emperor aged at least 25 were senators [by right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member).

The elected members of the Senate had to be at least 40 years old and have an annual income of 800,000 *[contos-de-réis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_real_\(old\))*, which limited candidates to wealthy citizens and the landed aristocracy. Voters also faced an income qualification. Voting in an election for the Senate was limited to male citizens with an annual income of at least 200,000 *contos-de-réis*. Those who qualified for this did not vote directly for senators; instead, they voted for candidates to be Senate electors. To be a Senate elector required an annual income of 400,000 *contos-de-réis*. Once elected, these electors would then vote for senators. The election itself would not result in a winner automatically. The three candidates receiving the most votes would make up what was called a *triple list*, from which the Emperor would select one individual that would be considered *elected*. The Emperor usually chose the candidate with the most votes, but it was within his constitutional discretion to select whichever of the three individuals listed.

Following the adoption of the 1824 Constitution, the first session of the Senate took place in May 1826. The Emperor had repeatedly delayed calling the first election, which had led to accusations that he would attempt to establish an [absolutist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy) government.

The [Proclamation of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Republic_\(Brazil\)) in 1889 ended the Brazilian Empire in favor of the [First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Brazilian_Republic). The [1891 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1891) was then adopted, transforming Brazil's provinces into states and removing the Senate's life tenure. This was retained under later constitutions, including the current [1988 Constitution](26847507.md). Now known as the Federal Senate, it resembles the [United States Senate](24909346.md) in that each state has the same number of senators.
