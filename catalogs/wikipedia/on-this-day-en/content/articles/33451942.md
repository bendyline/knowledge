# Cost reduction

**Cost reduction** is the process used by organisations aiming to reduce their [costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost) and [increase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profit_maximization) their [profits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profit_\(economics\)), or to accommodate reduced income. Depending on a company’s [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_\(economics\)) or [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(business\)), the strategies can vary. Every decision in the [product development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_development) process affects cost: design is typically considered to account for 70–80% of the final cost of a project such as an engineering project[^cite_note-1] or the [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) of a building.[^cite_note-2] In the [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector), cost reduction programs can be used where income is reduced or to reduce [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt) levels.[^cite_note-3]

## Importance

Companies typically launch a new product without focusing too much on cost. Cost becomes more important when [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_\(economics\)) increases and [price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price) becomes a differentiator in the market. The importance of cost reduction in relation to other strategic [business goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_goal) is often debated.[^cite_note-4]

## Examples of cost reduction strategies and programmes

### Commercial businesses

Consultants [Deloitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte) reported in 2006 that over three-quarters of the [FTSE 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTSE_100_Index) listed companies had commenced a cost reduction programme during the preceding 12 months. Cost reduction is most frequently stimulated by recognition that profits and [profitability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profitability) are below expected levels.[^cite_note-del-5]<sup>: 1 </sup> A majority of cost reduction programmes reported in their survey had been sponsored by the [Chief Executive Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Executive_Officer) of the business concerned.[^cite_note-del-5]<sup>: 5 </sup> Some examples of programmes include:

- Reductions in [staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employee) numbers (head count). Doherty refers to staff reduction exercises as "a quick and easy method" whose use often means that companies lack the resources they need when business activities recover.

  [^cite_note-del-5]

  : 1&#x20;

- Component consolidation

- [Function cost analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_cost_analysis) / [Value analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_analysis) / [Value engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_engineering)

- [Design for manufacture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_for_manufacture) / [Design for assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_for_assembly)

- [Reverse costing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_costing)

- [Cost driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_driver) analysis

- [Activity-based costing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activity-based_costing) (ABC), which assigns a cost of each activity undertaken in the production and delivery of each product and service according to the actual consumption by each activity including a share of [overheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overheads). Peter Turney, in a 1989 article, examines the role of ABC in the achievement of [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) excellence and the product cost information needed by managers working towards this goal.

  [^cite_note-6]

- Product [benchmarking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmarking)

- Competitor benchmarking

- [Design to cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_to_cost)

- Property portfolio reviews addressing whether the [estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) occupied by a business continues to meet its needs, and also reviewing the [tenure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_tenure) which it holds on the estate: Deloitte's survey of British companies noted that "smaller firms in particular" rarely take advantage of the cost reductions they can secure through [sale-and-leaseback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sale-and-leaseback) and similar deals.

  [^cite_note-del-5]

  : 3&#x20;

- Incorporation of "low-cost thinking" into an organisation's [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_culture).

  [^cite_note-del-5]

  : 8&#x20;

- Half cost strategies: ambitious strategies which aim to reduce the costs of specific production processes or [value adding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_added) stages to 1/N of the previous cost.

  [^cite_note-7]

Examples specifically focussed on the use of suppliers and the costs of goods and services supplied include:

- Supplier [consolidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidation_\(business\)): see examples in the [aerospace manufacturing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer#Supply_chain)

- [Low-cost country sourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_country_sourcing)

- [Outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outsourcing): experience in the United States suggests that businesses primarily outsource to reduce peripheral and "non-core" business expenses.

  [^cite_note-8]

- [Requests for quotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_for_quotation) (RFQ)

- Supplier [cost breakdown analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_breakdown_analysis)

- Design workshops with suppliers

### Public sector

In a public sector [spending review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spending_Review), an overall target for reduction in expenditure may be identified: for example, in the United Kingdom, the 2010 spending review anticipated a reduction of £81bn in [public expenditure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_expenditure) over the four year budget planning period.[^cite_note-9] In order to meet the challenges of a significant reduction in expenditure, government departments are expected to look at how they can "take cost out of the business" rather than simply cut services.[^cite_note-nao-10] One of the main principles expected of government departments in order to reduce costs is a "data-driven approach", i.e. ensuring that staff within the department have "a good understanding of the distribution and profile of costs in their business".[^cite_note-nao-10]

Centralisation of [procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement) activity has been highlighted as a beneficial public sector cost reduction strategy.[^cite_note-nao-10]<sup>: 6 </sup>

Scott Brown *et al*, of "Excellence in Business", note that in most cases, a successful approach to cost reduction which aims to maintain service quality "has at its heart an approach based on the principles of [Systems Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_thinking)": a fundamental review of the whole service, its purpose and objectives.[^cite_note-brown-11] The "Route Map for Change" which they advocate has been used effectively by [Kent County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_County_Council) for their [highway maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_maintenance) contract, while the decommissioning of various warships and aircraft squadrons following the UK's [Strategic Defence Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_and_Security_Review_2010) in 2010 is seen as a good example of a strategic option appraisal.[^cite_note-brown-11]
