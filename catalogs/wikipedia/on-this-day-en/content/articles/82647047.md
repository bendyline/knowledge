The **Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council** (**SSNSC**) is an official in the [government of Iran](50913538.md), responsible for coordinating the [Supreme National Security Council](6295948.md), the highest [decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-making) body on matters of defense, intelligence, and [foreign policy](233404.md). The secretary manages the day-to-day affairs of the SNSC, oversees policy planning, and serves as the principal intermediary between the council and key institutions, including the [Iranian Armed Forces](14305018.md), the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC), and the [Office of the Supreme Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Supreme_Leader). In this capacity, the secretary plays a significant role in Iran's [national security](240468.md) doctrine and implementing decisions approved by the council and the Supreme Leader.

Although formally appointed by the [president of Iran](233913.md), the position operates at the intersection of Iran's civilian government, military establishment, and clerical leadership.

## Role and responsibilities

The secretary of the Supreme National Security Council functions as the [executive head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_\(head_of_government\)) and administrative coordinator of the council.[^cite_note-s259-2][^cite_note-r201-3] While the president of Iran formally chairs the SNSC, the secretary is responsible for organizing its work, preparing policy proposals, and ensuring that decisions are implemented across the state apparatus.[^cite_note-k221-4]

## Institutional context

The Supreme National Security Council itself was established under Article 176 of the [constitution of the Islamic Republic of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran). The council is responsible for safeguarding [national interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_interest), protecting the Islamic Revolution, and coordinating policies related to defense and [foreign affairs](64709.md).[^cite_note-d869-5] Its membership includes senior political and military officials such as; the president of Iran (chair), the speaker of the parliament, the [Chief Justice](35323582.md), senior commanders of the Armed Forces and IRGC, [Foreign affairs ministry](1166971.md), [intelligence](501536.md), and [interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Iran\)), representatives appointed by the Supreme Leader.[^cite_note-v964-6][^cite_note-k221-4]

## Political importance

Over time, the office of secretary has evolved into a powerful platform within [Iran's political system](64702.md). [Several holders of the position](#List) have become key figures in national politics or diplomacy, using the role to influence strategic policy and international negotiations. During sensitive diplomatic processes such as [negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation) over [Iran's nuclear program](721807.md), the secretary has often served as Iran's chief negotiator or senior coordinator, suggesting the office's strategic significance other than administrative duties.[^cite_note-s259-2]
