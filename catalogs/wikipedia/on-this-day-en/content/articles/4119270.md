The **United States national baseball team**, also known as **Team USA**, represents the [United States](3434750.md) in international [baseball](3850.md) competitions. Currently ranked third in the world by the [World Baseball Softball Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Softball_Confederation), the team won the [Olympic baseball tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Summer_Olympics) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics) and the [World Baseball Classic](1877416.md) (WBC) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic).

The U.S. national team debuted at the first [Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup) (originally the Amateur World Series) in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Amateur_World_Series). They won the tournament, which was the premier level of international baseball for some periods of its existence until it was discontinued in 2011, four times.[^cite_note-6] The U.S. team has participated in every baseball tournament at the Summer Olympics, except for [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics), and won its first Olympic gold at the [2000 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics). The United States was an inaugural member of the WBC, making its debut in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Baseball_Classic). In their first three appearances in the WBC, the best finish for the Americans was fourth place in 2009. In [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic), the team won the WBC title for the first time, defeating [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_baseball_team) in the championship game. This breakthrough was followed up by another championship game appearance in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic), with the team losing to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team). The U.S. reached the championship game for a third time in a row in the [2026](73255749.md), but lost to [Venezuela](4264306.md).

The U.S. team qualified for the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) by winning the eight-team [Americas Qualifying Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Qualification#Americas_Qualifying_Event) in June 2021. In the Olympic competition, held in Tokyo in July and August 2021, the team won the silver medal, losing to hosts Japan in the gold medal game. With baseball not featured at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in Paris, Team USA will next compete in the [2028 Los Angeles Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2028_Summer_Olympics).

The team is governed by [USA Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Baseball), and its headquarters and training facilities are located in [Cary, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary,_North_Carolina). Along with the professional national team, the U.S. also fields collegiate, [18U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_under-18_baseball_team), [15U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_under-15_baseball_team), and [12U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_under-12_baseball_team) national baseball teams. The performances of all of the teams contribute to the [WBSC ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_World_Rankings) of the national team through various tournaments hosted by the [World Baseball Softball Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Softball_Confederation).

## History

### Early years

Further information: [Baseball at the Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Summer_Olympics)



Leslie Mann in 1940

International baseball games were played informally by American teams, against Canadian clubs, as early as 1860.[^cite_note-7] However, the development of a national team occurred with the early [Summer Olympics](27566.md), which saw baseball debut unofficially in [1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics) in St. Louis (though few records exist). An ad-hoc American team, including future [Major League Baseball](38776.md) player [Jim Thorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Thorpe), competed against a [Swedish team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_national_baseball_team) (featuring four Americans) at the [1912 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1912_Summer_Olympics) in Stockholm, and a team of World War I servicemen played a [Canadian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_national_baseball_team) at the [1919 Inter-Allied Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_Inter-Allied_Games) in Paris.

[Leslie Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Mann_\(athlete\)), a former major leaguer himself, is credited with the formation of the first official U.S. national team. He formed the United States Amateur Baseball Association (also known as the USA Baseball Congress) on February 19, 1931, with the hopes of sponsoring an international tournament.[^cite_note-8] Mann's efforts to include baseball at the Olympics saw little success (no other country sent a team to [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) in Berlin, and the 1940 Games in Tokyo were cancelled due to [World War II](32927.md)), but he did hold the first [Amateur World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_World_Series) in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Amateur_World_Series). The U.S. team, made out of amateur players, lost a best-of-five series to [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_national_baseball_team).[^cite_note-9]



The United States team at the 1939 Amateur World Series

The USA Baseball Congress sent a national team to the Amateur World Series in [1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Amateur_World_Series), [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Amateur_World_Series) (tying for a silver medal), and [1941](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_Amateur_World_Series), all held in [Havana, Cuba](49719.md). The United States' international participation was hampered by World War II, as well as the country's early withdrawal from the [1942 Amateur World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1942_Amateur_World_Series) due to a brawl with the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) team and, purportedly, the influence of Dominican dictator [Rafael Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Trujillo).[^cite_note-Schiroli,_p._30-10] After 1942, the United States was largely absent from the international baseball scene, as it did not participate in another Amateur World Series until [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Amateur_World_Series).



[Dick Griesser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Griesser), a future [Collegiate All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_College_Baseball_All-America_Team), on the U.S. team at the [1956 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1956_Summer_Olympics)

While an American team competed against [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_baseball_team) at the [1956 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1956_Summer_Olympics) in Melbourne, the next "official" participation in an international tournament (i.e. recognized by USA Baseball) was at the [1959 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1959_Pan_American_Games).[^cite_note-11] Held at [Wrigley Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrigley_Field) and [Comiskey Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comiskey_Park) in Chicago, the 1959 team was made up of collegiate players, including future [Hall of Famer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baseball_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum) [Lou Brock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Brock).[^cite_note-12] The U.S. team made its first official appearance at the Olympics during the [1964 Tokyo Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics), managed by longtime [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Trojans_baseball) coach [Rod Dedeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Dedeaux).[^cite_note-13]

The United States victory at the [1967 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1967_Pan_American_Games) in [Winnipeg, Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg,_Canada) spurred renewed interest in international competitions. [William P. "Dutch" Fehring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Fehring), president of a rejuvenated [United States Baseball Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Baseball) (USBF), decided to actively participate in [International Baseball Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baseball_Federation) (FIBA) competitions.[^cite_note-baseballdecuba-14] In 1968, the U.S. won a [post-Olympic tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Amateur_Baseball_Tournament) in [Mexico City](18987.md). The following year, it returned to the Amateur World Series in Santo Domingo in [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Amateur_World_Series), narrowly losing the gold medal to [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_baseball_team). However, international baseball was fraught with tensions in the early 1970s; as a result of protracted disagreements with [FIBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baseball_Federation), the international governing body of baseball, the U.S. federation withdrew in 1973 and formed its own body, the World Amateur Baseball Federation (better known as FEMBA, or the *Federación Mundial de Béisbol Amateur*).[^cite_note-dom-15] That year, the United States won [its first Amateur World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Amateur_World_Series_\(FEMBA\)) in Nicaragua and repeated [a year later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Amateur_World_Series) in [St. Petersburg, Florida](84504.md) in the first Amateur World Series held in the U.S.[^cite_note-Schiroli,_p._30-10] The [FIBA-FEMBA split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEMBA) was resolved by 1975, and the U.S. and its followers rejoined the new group, AINBA.[^cite_note-16]



The United States at bat in the [1981 World Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1981_World_Games)

The U.S. national team won the gold medal in the first-ever [World Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Games), held at [San Jose Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Municipal_Stadium) in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California) in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_World_Games). The U.S. fielded a roster of collegiate players that included [Franklin Stubbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Stubbs), [Oddibe McDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oddibe_McDowell), [Spike Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Owen), [John Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Russell_\(catcher\)), [Ed Vosberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Vosberg), and [Pat Clements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Clements) among other future major leaguers. The U.S. national team completed the tournament undefeated, securing victories over [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_baseball_team), [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_baseball_team), and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team). Over the course of these games, they outscored their opponents by a 14-run differential. Their performance resulted in a gold medal win.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

### Olympic competitions





[Will Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Clark) (left) and [Shane Mack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Mack_\(baseball\)) (right) on the 1984 Olympic team

The [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics) in Los Angeles was the first to feature a tournament in the program. Eight teams competed in the tournament held at [Dodger Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodger_Stadium). Cuba, after winning the [gold medal](526488.md) at the [1983 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1983_Pan_American_Games), planned to participate but did not as a result of the [Soviet-led boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics_boycott). The United States national team finished second, falling to Japan in the final game, 6–3. No official medals were awarded as baseball was a [demonstration sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_sport).

Baseball returned as a demonstration sport at the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics) in Seoul. Again an eight-team tournament, the United States finished first in the tournament, defeating Japan, 5–3, in the final game.

Baseball became an official medal sport beginning with the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics) in Barcelona. Competition was open only to male amateurs in 1992 and 1996. As a result, the United States and other nations where professional baseball is developed relied on [college baseball](1370306.md) players, while Cuba used their most experienced veterans, who technically were considered amateurs as they nominally held other jobs. Professional baseball players were introduced in 2000, but the situation remained largely the same. No active players from [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) competed—as MLB declined to release its players—so Team USA utilized [minor-league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball) players and [free agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agent), while Cuba and some other nations were still able to use their best players, as they had no commitments with MLB.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-socog2-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Nevertheless, at the [1996 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics) in Atlanta, the United States won the bronze medal over [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_national_baseball_team) after finishing with a 6–1 record in pool play and losing to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) in the semifinals.

At the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics), the United States had a 6–1 record in pool play, then defeated [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) in the semifinals, followed by a stunning victory over the heavily favored [Team Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_baseball_team) in the gold-medal game—an upset later dubbed the "Miracle on Grass".[^cite_note-23]



The U.S. national team with [President George W. Bush](3414021.md) prior to a practice game at the [2008 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics)

The United States did not qualify for the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics) in Athens, despite being the defending gold medalists, after losing a qualifying game to [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_national_baseball_team), 2–1. That qualifier was controversial due to its format (single-elimination, rather than double-elimination), scheduling, and the fact that only two slots were given to nations from the Americas, while Europe received three.[^cite_note-24]

Further information: [Baseball at the 2004 Summer Olympics § Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics#Controversy)

The United States qualified for the 2008 Summer Olympics by winning the American Qualifying Tournament. At the [Beijing Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics), the U.S. finished with a 5–2 record in pool play. The U.S. lost to [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_baseball_team) in the semifinals but beat [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) in the bronze-medal match.

At the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC) meeting on July 8, 2005, baseball and [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball) were voted out of the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) in London, becoming the first sports voted out of the Olympics since [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo) was eliminated from the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-IOC_sports-25] The IOC cited the absence of the best players as the main reason for baseball being dropped from the Olympic program following the 2008 games.[^cite_note-socog2-20] Baseball returned to the Olympic program for the [2020 games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics), held in 2021 in Tokyo. It was not a part of the [2024 games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in Paris but is scheduled for the [2028 games](12695048.md) in Los Angeles.[^cite_note-26]

### The first World Baseball Classics

#### 2006

See also: [2006 World Baseball Classic rosters § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Baseball_Classic_rosters#United_States)

On January 17, [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_in_baseball), the United States announced its provisional 60-man roster (52 players in all) and whittled down the squad mixed with youth and experience to 30 players on February 14, 2006. Sixteen of the 30 [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) clubs were represented on the 2006 squad, including multiple representatives from the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) (4), [Houston Astros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Astros) (3), [Washington Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Nationals) (3), [Atlanta Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Braves) (2), [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) (2), [Chicago Cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cubs) (2), [Colorado Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rockies) (2), [Houston Astros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Astros) (2), and [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Rangers_\(baseball\)) (2). The fact that four Yankees were selected for the squad irked Yankees owner [George Steinbrenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Steinbrenner), who was opposed to the WBC being held in the middle of [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training) to the point where, at his team's complex in [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida), he posted a sign apologizing for their absence and mocking the tournament in the process. [MLB Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner_of_Baseball) [Bud Selig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud_Selig) promptly ordered him to take down the sign the next day.

Manager [Buck Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Martinez) brought his 17 years of professional experience as a major league [catcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catcher) and 1+ seasons as [Toronto Blue Jays](30847.md)' ([2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_in_baseball)–[2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_in_baseball)) skipper to the U.S. team. Former big-league managers [Davey Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davey_Johnson) and [Marcel Lachemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Lachemann) served as hitting coach and pitching coach, respectively.

Along with fellow North American rivals [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_national_baseball_team) and Mexico, the U.S. hosted [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_baseball_team). Round One games were held at [Chase Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Field) in [Phoenix, Arizona](49121.md), and [Scottsdale Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsdale_Stadium) in [Scottsdale, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsdale,_Arizona). The top two teams advanced to [Angel Stadium of Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Stadium_of_Anaheim) in [Anaheim, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim,_California). Despite a surprising loss to Canada, the United States advanced to the second round via tiebreaker. However, second-round losses to [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) and Mexico allowed [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) to advance over the Americans via tiebreaker. [Derek Jeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Jeter) and [Ken Griffey, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Griffey,_Jr.) were named to the [All-World Baseball Classic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Baseball_Classic#2006_All-World_Baseball_Classic_team).

#### 2009

See also: [2009 World Baseball Classic rosters § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Baseball_Classic_rosters#United_States)



[David Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wright) batting for Team USA at the 2009 World Baseball Classic

The United States competed in Pool C of the [2009 World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Baseball_Classic) along with [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_baseball_team), [Venezuela](4264306.md), and host Canada. The U.S. won the pool opener against Canada by a score of 6–5 and secured advancement into Round 2 by defeating Venezuela in a 15–6 slugfest. Venezuela, however, came back to defeat the U.S. in the championship game of Pool C, 5–3.

On March 14, in their first match of Round 2 against [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_baseball_team), in [Miami, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami,_Florida)'s [Dolphin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin_Stadium), the United States was [mercy ruled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy_rule) for the first time in international competition, losing 11–1 in seven [innings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inning). [Adam Dunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Dunn) and captain [Derek Jeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Jeter) were among the ones to voice their distaste with the severe beating. Manager Davey Johnson even stated, "I should have stayed there", referencing a wedding he was at earlier in the day.

The United States came on strong the following day against the surprising [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_baseball_team) (who had already eliminated a tournament superpower, the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team)), jumping out to a 6–0 lead in the fourth inning and winning 9–3. With Puerto Rico losing to Venezuela 2–0 the following day, the U.S. faced Puerto Rico once again in the qualifying round, with the loser being eliminated from the tournament. With Puerto Rico leading 5–3 in the 9th inning, singles by [Shane Victorino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Victorino) and [Brian Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Roberts_\(baseball\)) and walks by [Jimmy Rollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Rollins) and [Kevin Youkilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Youkilis) cut the lead to 5–4. [New York Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets) third baseman [David Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wright) then looped a barely-fair single into right field that brought in Roberts and Rollins to win the game, 6–5, advancing the U.S. to the semifinals while eliminating Puerto Rico. The United States went on to lose to Japan 9–4 in the second semifinal. Rollins was named to the [All-World Baseball Classic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Baseball_Classic#2009_All-World_Baseball_Classic_team)

#### 2013

See also: [2013 World Baseball Classic rosters § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic_rosters#United_States)

The United States competed in [Pool D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic_Pool_D) of the [2013 World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic), along with [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_baseball_team), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_national_baseball_team), and [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_national_baseball_team). The U.S. team lost to Mexico in the first round, 5–2, but later won games against Canada and Italy, securing their place for the second round, along with Italy, in [Pool 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic_Pool_2).

On March 12, the United States beat Puerto Rico, 7–1, which then led to a face-off against the Dominican Republic on March 14. Team USA lost, 3–1, thus prompting a game against Puerto Rico once again. The next day Puerto Rico beat the Americans, 4–3, and eliminated them from the tournament. David Wright was named to the [All-World Baseball Classic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic#2013_All-World_Baseball_Classic_team)

The United States came in second in the [inaugural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_WBSC_Premier12) [WBSC Premier12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Premier12) tournament in 2015.

### Classic success and return to the Olympics

#### 2017 Classic

See also: [2017 World Baseball Classic rosters § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic_rosters#United_States)

Team USA won its first game over Colombia, 3–2, in 10 innings on a walk-off single by [Adam Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Jones_\(baseball\)). Following a loss to the Dominican Republic in which it squandered a 5-run lead, the U.S. defeated Canada to reach the second round.

In the second round, the Americans won the first game by defeating Venezuela, 4–2. In the second game the U.S. was defeated by Puerto Rico, 6–5, after giving up four runs in the first inning. The U.S. then faced the Dominican Republic in a high-stakes, winner-take-all rematch, with a spot in the championship round on the line. Late in the game, with Team USA trailing, Adam Jones delivered a defining moment. Tracking a deep fly ball off the bat of his then-[Orioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Orioles) teammate [Manny Machado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Machado), Jones leaped into the wall to make the catch, creating an iconic image in the outfield. Building on that pivotal play, Team USA responded with a late-game rally, overcoming the deficit to secure the victory and advance to the next stage.[^cite_note-27]

In the championship round semifinals on March 21, the Americans defeated Japan, 2–1, to advance to their first-ever appearance in the Final. In the final on March 22, the U.S. once again faced Puerto Rico; this time, however, they ended up winning dominantly by a score of 8–0, capturing their first ever World Baseball Classic title. Following the conclusion of the tournament, [Eric Hosmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Hosmer), [Christian Yelich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Yelich), and [Marcus Stroman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Stroman) were named to the [All-World Baseball Classic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic#2017_All-World_Baseball_Classic_team), with Stroman being named [World Baseball Classic MVP](1877416.md#Most_Valuable_Player).[^cite_note-28]

#### 2020 Olympics

Main article: [Baseball at the 2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics)



Members of the 2020 Olympic squad before a scrimmage at the [USA Baseball National Training Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Baseball_National_Training_Complex) in 2021

The United States came in fourth in the 12-team [2019 WBSC Premier12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WBSC_Premier12) tournament in November 2019, with [Scott Brosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Brosius) as manager. With third-place Mexico as the top finisher from the Americas in that tournament, the U.S. missed the chance to immediately qualify for the [six spots open for the 2020 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Qualification).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-usabaseball1-30] The team subsequently qualified, with [Mike Scioscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Scioscia) as manager, by winning the [Americas Qualifying Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Qualification#Americas_Qualifying_Event) held from May 31 to June 5, 2021, in Florida.[^cite_note-31] [Luke Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Williams_\(baseball\)) led the team in batting average (.444), runs (6), hits (8), and RBIs (6), [Todd Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Frazier) and [Mark Kolozsvary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kolozsvary) led in home runs (2), and [David Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Robertson_\(baseball\)) led the team in saves (2).[^cite_note-32]

At the Olympics, the team first won its three-team pool with victories over [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_baseball_team). In the modified double-elimination bracket, the team lost to hosts [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) in the second round, then defeated the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) in the repechage and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) in the semifinals to advance to the gold medal game, where the team lost to Japan for the second time.[^cite_note-33] Team Japan was made up of players from [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball), which paused its season for the Olympics,[^cite_note-34] while Team USA fielded minor-league players and free agents with major-league experience.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

#### 2023 Classic

See also: [2023 World Baseball Classic rosters § United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic_rosters#United_States)

At the [2023 World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic), Team USA looked to defend their 2017 championship. Multiple-time [MLB MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) [Mike Trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Trout) was named team captain ahead of his first appearance in the tournament.[^cite_note-37] Team USA won their first game in pool play against [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_national_baseball_team) by a score of 6–2. They lost their next game against Mexico by a score of 11–5, which was their only pool-play loss. The United States won their final two games against Canada and Colombia and advanced to the knockout stage.

In their quarterfinal matchup against Venezuela, the Americans were trailing 7–5 in the 8th inning when [Trea Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trea_Turner) hit a go-ahead grand slam to win the game, 9–7, to advance to the semifinals.[^cite_note-38] There, the U.S. blew out Cuba, 14–2, and advanced to the championship round for the second tournament in a row.[^cite_note-39]

Team USA's championship round opponent was Japan, creating a highly anticipated matchup. The Americans jumped out to an early 1–0 lead on another Turner home run but couldn't hold on, ultimately falling to Japan, 3–2, as [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani) struck out then-teammate Trout to end the game.[^cite_note-40] Turner and Trout were named to the [All-WBC team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic#2023_All-World_Baseball_Classic_team).[^cite_note-41]

#### 2026 Classic

See also: [2026 World Baseball Classic](73255749.md)



Judge (left) being announced as captain of Team USA ahead of the [2026 World Baseball Classic](73255749.md) during a press conference with [Mark DeRosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_DeRosa) in April 2025



[Roman Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Anthony) during an exhibition game

Three-time [AL MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) [Aaron Judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Judge) was named captain of Team USA for the 2026 World Baseball Classic in April 2025.[^cite_note-42] In the months leading up to the tournament, several high-profile players committed to the roster, including [Paul Skenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Skenes), [Tarik Skubal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarik_Skubal), [Cal Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cal_Raleigh), [Bobby Witt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Witt_Jr.), and [Bryce Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_Harper).[^cite_note-43]

Team USA finished second in Pool B with a 3–1 record, avenging its 2023 loss to Mexico but losing to Italy.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The team went on to defeat Canada in the quarterfinals and the Dominican Republic in a highly anticipated and tightly contested semifinal.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

Advancing to the championship game for a third consecutive tournament, Team USA ultimately fell to Venezuela, 3–2, in a close final. Despite entering the tournament with a highly touted offense, the lineup struggled to produce in the final.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

[Brice Turang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brice_Turang), [Roman Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Anthony), [Logan Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Webb), and Skenes were named to the All-Tournament Team. Turang led the tournament with four doubles, while Webb and Skenes tied for the tournament lead in wins with two each.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### World Baseball Classic

In [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_in_baseball), [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) announced the formation of the [World Baseball Classic](1877416.md), a 16-nation international competition to be held in March [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_in_baseball) for the first time. The tournament was the first of its kind to have the national teams of IBAF's member federations feature professional players from the major leagues around the world, including MLB.

### WBSC Premier12

The WBSC created the [Premier12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Premier12) tournament in 2014,[^cite_note-66] following the dissolution of the [Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup). The tournament is held in every four years in November. The American roster includes professional ballplayers who are not on an MLB [40-man roster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40-man_roster). The roster includes some MLB prospects, though the tournament conflicts with the [Arizona Fall League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Fall_League) and [winter leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_league_baseball).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

### Baseball World Cup (1938–2011)

In [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_in_baseball), the Amateur World Series became the [International Baseball Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baseball_Federation)'s (IBAF) [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup).

Since 1938, the U.S. has won 15 [medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup#Medal_table) at the [Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup): four gold (1973, 1974, [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Baseball_World_Cup), [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Baseball_World_Cup)), eight silver ([1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Amateur_World_Series), [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Amateur_World_Series), 1969, 1970, 1972, [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Amateur_World_Series), [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Baseball_World_Cup), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Baseball_World_Cup)), and three bronze ([1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Amateur_World_Series), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Amateur_World_Series), [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Amateur_World_Series)).

The U.S. was usually represented by college players in these tournaments, while Cuba used its best players.

#### 2007

The U.S. was in group A of the [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_in_baseball) IBAF World Cup, along with Republic of China, Japan, Mexico, [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_baseball_team), Italy, [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_baseball_team) and South Africa. The U.S. went 6–1 to win their group, with their only loss coming on November 9, [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_in_baseball) against Italy. It was the U.S.'s first loss to Italy in 21 years and the first time it ever lost to Italy with professional players, as the team consisted of minor league prospects. It was, however, their only loss. The U.S. went on to beat Korea, Netherlands, and Cuba to capture the gold.

#### 2009

In Round 1 of the [2009 Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Baseball_World_Cup), the U.S. (2–1) finished second in Group E, advancing alongside first-place Venezuela (3–0). In Round 2, the U.S. was joined by the nine other first- and second-place teams from Round 1, four wild-card teams, and the two principal host teams (Italy and the Netherlands). The 16 teams were divided into Groups F and G, where the U.S. (7–0) defeated each of the other seven teams in Group G. In Round 3, the top four teams from Group F were renamed Group 1, while the top four teams from Group G were renamed Group 2. The U.S. finished first in Group 2 with a perfect 7–0 record, while Cuba finished first in Group 1 with a 5–2 record. In the Final Round, Group 1 and Group 2's fourth-place teams competed for overall seventh place, the third-place teams played for fifth place, and the second-place teams faced off for the bronze medal. In the gold-medal game, the U.S. defeated Cuba, 10–5.

[Tournament awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Baseball_World_Cup#Awards) were given to [Justin Smoak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Smoak) (MVP) and [Todd Redmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Redmond) (best won/loss average (pitcher)). Smoak (first base) was also named to the tournament All-Star Team, along with [Jon Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Weber_\(baseball\)) (outfield) and [Terry Tiffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Tiffee) (designated hitter).

### Minor tournaments

#### Intercontinental Cup

The Intercontinental Cup was an IBAF tournament first held in [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_in_baseball) in Italy, and held every other year until [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_in_baseball). The U.S. sat out the last three tournaments, in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_in_baseball), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_in_baseball), and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_in_baseball).

Future big leaguers who competed with the U.S. collegiate team in the Intercontinental Cup include [Joe Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Carter), [Terry Francona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Francona), [Mickey Morandini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Morandini), [John Olerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Olerud), and [Robin Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Ventura).

#### Pan American Games

The U.S. and Cuba have been archrivals at the [Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Games) ever since the event began in [1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_in_baseball). The U.S. has finished second behind Cuba eight of the 12 times they have brought home the gold. Likewise, when the U.S. won the gold medal at the [1967 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Pan_American_Games), Cuba finished second. The U.S. roster is usually composed of promising college players, while Cuba is able to send its best players.

In total, the U.S. has won one gold medal, eleven silver medals, and three bronze medals. The only games the U.S. failed to medal in were [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_in_baseball) and [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_in_baseball). For the 1995 games, the U.S. did not send their national team, but instead the [St. John's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_University_\(New_York\)) baseball team, who finished 0–4 against the international all-star teams.

## Collegiate National Team

American baseball players on a podium

The collegiate national team celebrating its victory at the 2002 [Haarlem Baseball Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarlem_Baseball_Week)

USA Baseball also fields a collegiate national team of top [collegiate baseball](1370306.md) players in the country. The team competes in exhibition games and international competitions against teams from across the world, including Canada, Japan, and the Netherlands.[^cite_note-111] The collegiate team has won several international tournaments, including the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics), [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Amateur_World_Series_\(FEMBA\)) and [1974 Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Amateur_World_Series), 2009 [World Baseball Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Challenge), and multiple [Haarlem Baseball Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarlem_Baseball_Week).[^cite_note-112]

Players for the collegiate national team include [Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baseball_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum) inductees [Barry Larkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Larkin) and [Todd Helton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Helton), [MLB Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) award winners [Dustin Pedroia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Pedroia), [Ryan Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Howard), and [Kris Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Bryant), and [Cy Young Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cy_Young_Award) winners [David Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Price_\(baseball\)), [Gerrit Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrit_Cole), and [Paul Skenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Skenes).[^cite_note-113]

## Other national teams

USA Baseball also fields [18U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_under-18_baseball_team), [15U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_under-15_baseball_team), and [12U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_under-12_baseball_team) national baseball teams. Former national teams included 16U and 14U national teams. USA Baseball also hosts two national team development programs in the age divisions of 17U and 14U. The selection processes for these programs can be found on the official website of USA Baseball and these team programs also have their respective social media web pages.
