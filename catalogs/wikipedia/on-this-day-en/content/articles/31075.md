**Tirana**[^cite_note-15] ([/tɪˈrɑːnə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/07/En-us-Tirana.ogg/En-us-Tirana.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/07/En-us-Tirana.ogg/En-us-Tirana.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*tih-RAH-nə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Albanian pronunciation: [\[tiˈɾana\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Albanian); [Gheg Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheg_Albanian_language): *Tirona*[^cite_note-18]) is the capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Albania) of [Albania](738.md). It is located in the centre of the country, enclosed by mountains and hills, with [Dajti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dajti) rising to the east and a slight valley to the northwest overlooking the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md) in the distance. It is among the wettest and sunniest cities in Europe, with 2,544 hours of sun per year.[^cite_note-19]&#x20;

Tirana was founded in 1614 by [Ottoman Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Albania) nobleman [Sylejman Pasha Bargjini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulejman_Bargjini), centered on the [Old Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulejman_Pasha_Mosque) and *[türbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulejman_Pasha_Tomb)*. The site of present-day Tirana has been continuously inhabited since the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) and was likely the core of the [Illyrian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_kingdom) of the [Taulantii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taulantii), which in [classical antiquity](252905.md) was centred in the hinterland of [Epidamnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durr%C3%ABs). Following the [Illyrian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_Wars), it was annexed by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). With the collapse of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) in the fourth century, most of Albania came under the control of the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). The city was fairly unimportant until the 20th century, when the [Congress of Lushnjë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Lushnj%C3%AB) proclaimed it as Albania's capital after the [Albanian Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Declaration_of_Independence) in 1912.&#x20;

Classified as a [gamma-world-city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city),[^cite_note-20] Tirana is the most important economic, financial, political, and trade centre in Albania due to its significant location in the centre of the country. As the seat of power of the [Government of Albania](67576.md), it hosts the official residences of the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Albania) and [Prime Minister of Albania](259136.md) and the [Parliament of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Albania). The city was announced as the [European Youth Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Capital) for 2022.&#x20;

### Early development



The mosaics of an [Ancient Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Mosaic) house from the 3rd century AD

The area of Tirana has been inhabited by humans since prehistoric times with the earliest recognised reference found at the [Cave of Pëllumbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_P%C3%ABllumbas) belonging to the Palaeolithic period. [Illyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians) were the first population of the area and formed most likely the core of the [Illyrian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_kingdom) of the [Taulantii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taulantii), which in classical antiquity was centered in the hinterland of [Epidamnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durr%C3%ABs).[^cite_note-Hammond-21] When the Romans arrived in Illyria after their victory of the [Illyrian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_Wars), they populated and integrated the area into their empire under the political control of the city of Rome. The oldest surviving discovery from this period is a [Roman house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Mosaic) dating to the 3rd century, which was transformed into an [aisleless church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisleless_church) with a mosaic floor. A [castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_of_Justinian), possibly known as *Tirkan*, was built by the Byzantine Emperor [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) between the 4th and 6th century and later restored by Ahmed Pasha Toptani in the 18th century.[^cite_note-Heppner1994-22]&#x20;



[Castle of Petrelë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrel%C3%AB_Castle) was founded in the 6th century by [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I).

Tirana is mentioned in [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) documents in the 1350s as well as in 1418: *"...the resident Pjeter, son of late Domenik from the village of Tirana..."*.[^cite_note-Heppner2-23] Records of the first land registrations under the [Ottomans](22278.md) in 1431–32 show that Tirana consisted of 60 inhabited areas, with nearly 2,028 houses and 7,300 inhabitants.[^cite_note-24] In 1510, [Marin Barleti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_Barleti), an Albanian Catholic priest and scholar, in the biography of the Albanian national hero [Skanderbeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderbeg), *Historia de vita et gestis Scanderbegi Epirotarum principis* (*The story of life and deeds of Skanderbeg, the prince of Epirotes*), referred to this area as a small village, distinguishing between "Little Tirana" and "Great Tirana".[^cite_note-Heppner2-23] It is later mentioned in 1572 as *Borgo di Tirana*.[^cite_note-25]&#x20;

According to [Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_von_Hahn), the settlement had already started to develop as a [bazaar](556904.md) and included several [watermills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermill),[^cite_note-Miho-26] even before 1614, when [Sulejman Bargjini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulejman_Bargjini), a local ruler, built the [Old Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylejman_Pasha_Mosque), a small commercial centre, and a *[hammam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammam)* (Turkish bathhouse). This is confirmed by oral sources, which state that there were two earlier mosques 300–400 m from the Old Mosque, towards today's [Ali Demi Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Demi_Street). The Mosque of Reç and the Mosque of Mujo were positioned on the left side of the [Lana River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_river) and were older than the Old Mosque.[^cite_note-Miho-26] Later, the [Et'hem Bey Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Et%27hem_Bey_Mosque), built by [Molla Bey of Petrela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molla_Bey_of_Petrela), was constructed. It employed the best [artisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisans) in the country and was completed in 1821 by Molla's son [Etëhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Et%C3%ABhem_Bey_Mollaj), who was also Sulejman Bargjini's great-nephew.&#x20;

In 1800, the first newcomers arrived in the [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement), the so-called *ortodoksit*. They were [Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) from villages near [Korçë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kor%C3%A7%C3%AB) and [Pogradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogradec), who settled around modern day [Tirana Park on the Artificial Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Park_on_the_Artificial_Lake).[^cite_note-27] They started to be known as the *llacifac* and were the first Christians to arrive after the creation of the town.[^cite_note-28] In 1807, Tirana became the centre of the [Subprefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefecture) of Krujë-Tirana. After 1816, Tirana languished under the control of the *[Toptani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toptani_family)* family of [Krujë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruj%C3%AB). Later, Tirana became a sub-prefecture of the newly created [Vilayet of Shkodër](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet_of_Shkod%C3%ABr) and the Sanjak of Durrës. In 1889, the [Albanian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) started to be taught in Tirana's schools, and the patriotic club Bashkimi was founded in 1908.&#x20;



The Old Bazaar at the turn of the 20th century. The [Sulejman Pasha Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulejman_Pasha_Mosque) is visible in the background. It was destroyed in [World War II](32927.md).

### Modern development

On 28 November 1912, the national flag was raised in [Vlorë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlor%C3%AB) by [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Albania) [Ismail Qemali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Qemali), marking the symbolic birth of Albania as a sovereign country. The next few years, however, were marked by turmoil during the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars). On the 27th November 1912 after over 400 years of [Ottoman occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Albania), the Serbian army occupied Tirana and annexed into the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia)'s [Durr*ë*s County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dra%C4%8D_County). In response to [massacres of Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacres_of_Albanians_in_the_Balkan_Wars) during Serbia's rule, Tirana took part in uprising of the villages led by [Haxhi Qamili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haxhi_Qamili).[^cite_note-29] Tirana was liberated in 1913 after the [Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_\(1913\)) which forced the Serbian army to retreat out of [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Albania)'s borders. In August 1916, the first city map was compiled by the specialists of the Austro-Hungarian army.[^cite_note-30] Following the capture of the town of [Debar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debar) by Serbia, many of its Albanian inhabitants fled to [Turkey](11125639.md), the rest went to Tirana.[^cite_note-Clayer306307-31] Of those that ended up in [Istanbul](3391396.md), some of their number migrated to Albania, mainly to Tirana where the Dibran community (people from [Dibër County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dib%C3%ABr_County)) formed an important segment of the city's population from 1920 onward and for some years thereafter.[^cite_note-Clayer306307-31] On 8 February 1920, the [Congress of Lushnjë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Lushnj%C3%AB) proclaimed Tirana as the temporary capital of Albania, which had gained independence in 1912.[^cite_note-Pearson-32] The city acquired that status permanently on 31 December 1925. In 1923, the first regulatory city plan was compiled by Austrian architects.[^cite_note-33] The centre of Tirana was the project of [Florestano Di Fausto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florestano_Di_Fausto) and [Armando Brasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Brasini), well-known architects of the [Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) period in Italy. Brasini laid the basis for the modern-day arrangement of the ministerial buildings in the city centre. The plan underwent revisions by Albanian architect Eshref Frashëri, Italian architect Castellani and Austrian architects Weiss and Kohler. The modern Albanian parliament building served as an officers' club. It was there that, in September 1928, [Zog of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zog_of_Albania) was crowned King Zog I, King of the Albanians.&#x20;



Old villa architecture in Tirana

Tirana was the venue for the signing of the Pact of Tirana between Fascist [Italy](14532.md) and Albania. During the rule of King Zog, many [Muhaxhirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhaxhir_\(Albanians\)) migrated towards Tirana, which led to a growing population in the capital city in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-34]&#x20;

In 1939, Tirana was captured by Fascist forces, who appointed a [puppet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_government). In the meantime, Italian architect [Gherardo Bosio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gherardo_Bosio) was asked to elaborate on previous plans and introduce a new project in the area of present-day [Mother Teresa Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa_Square_\(Tirana\)).[^cite_note-35] A failed assassination attempt was made on [Victor Emmanuel III of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_III_of_Italy) by a local resistance activist during a visit to Tirana. In November 1941, two emissaries of the [Communist Party of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Yugoslavia) (KPJ), Miladin Popović and Dušan Mugoša, called a meeting of three Albanian communist groups and founded the [Communist Party of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Labour_of_Albania), and [Enver Hoxha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Hoxha) soon emerged as its leader.&#x20;

The town soon became the centre of the Albanian communists, who mobilised locals against Italian fascists and later [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) Germans, while spreading ideological propaganda. On 4 February 1944, the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo), supported by the forces of [Xhafer Deva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhafer_Deva), executed 86 anti-fascists in Tirana.[^cite_note-36] On 17 November 1944, the [town was liberated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Tirana) after a [fierce battle between the Communists and German forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Resistance_of_World_War_II). The Nazis eventually withdrew and the communists seized power.&#x20;



[Skanderbeg Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderbeg_Square) in 1988, two years prior to the [Fall of communism in Albania](17545990.md)

From 1944 to 1991, massive socialist-style apartment complexes and factories were built, while [Skanderbeg Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderbeg_Square) was redesigned, with a number of buildings demolished. For instance, Tirana's former Old Bazaar and the [Orthodox](10186.md) Cathedral were razed to the ground in order to build the Soviet-styled Palace of Culture. The northern portion of the main boulevard was renamed [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) [Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zogu_I_Boulevard) and his statue was erected in the city square. Because private car ownership was banned, mass transportation consisted mainly of bicycles, trucks and buses. After Hoxha's death, a [pyramidal museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_of_Tirana) was constructed in his memory by the government.&#x20;

Before and after the proclamation of Albania's policy of self-imposed [isolationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolationism), a number of high-profile figures paid visits to the city, such as Soviet leader [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), [Chinese](5405.md) Premier [Zhou Enlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Enlai) and [East German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) Foreign Minister [Oskar Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Fischer_\(politician\)). In 1985, Enver Hoxha's funeral was held in Tirana.[^cite_note-37] A few years later, [Mother Teresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa) became the first religious figure[^cite_note-38] to visit the country after the end of Albania's long [anti-religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-religious) [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_atheism) stance. She paid respects to her mother and sister resting at a local cemetery.&#x20;



Tirana's main boulevard in 1991

Starting at the campus and ending at Skanderbeg Square with the toppling of Enver Hoxha's statue, the city saw [significant demonstrations](17545990.md) by [University of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tirana) students demanding political freedoms in the early 1990s. On the political aspect, the city witnessed a number of events. Personalities visited the capital, such as then-U.S. Secretary of State [James Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baker) and [Pope John Paul II](23805.md). The former visit came amidst the historical setting after the fall of communism, as hundreds of thousands were chanting in Skanderbeg Square Baker's famous saying of "Freedom works!".[^cite_note-39] Pope John Paul II became the first major religious leader to visit Tirana.&#x20;

During the Balkans turmoil in the mid-1990s, the city experienced dramatic events such as the unfolding of the [1997 unrest in Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_unrest_in_Albania) and a [failed coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatos_Nano#Second_and_third_premierships) on 14 September 1998.&#x20;

In 1999, following the [Kosovo War](16760.md), Tirana Airport became a NATO airbase, serving its mission in the former Yugoslavia.&#x20;

### Contemporary

See also: [2018–2019 student protest in Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932019_student_protest_in_Albania)

Further information: [2019 Albania earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Albania_earthquake)



Mustafa Matohiti Street near the [Pyramid of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_of_Tirana). After the [fall of communism in Albania](17545990.md), many areas saw new development.

During his term from 2000 to 2011, the former [mayor of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Tirana), [Edi Rama](277805.md), undertook a campaign to demolish illegal constructed buildings across Tirana as well as along the river banks of [Lanë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan%C3%AB) to bring the area to its pre-1990 state. In an attempt to widen roads, Rama authorized the bulldozing of private properties so that they could be paved over, thus widening streets. Most main roads underwent reconstruction, including the Unaza, [Rruga e Kavajës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavaja_Street) and the main boulevard. Rama also led the initiative to paint the façades of Tirana's buildings in bright colours, although much of their interiors continued to degrade. Rama's critics claimed that he focused too much attention on cosmetic changes without fixing any of the major problems such as shortages of drinking water and electricity.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]&#x20;

In June 2007, [George W. Bush](3414021.md) traveled to Tirana on an official state visit, becoming the first [U.S. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._President) to visit the former communist country.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] In 2008, the [Gërdec explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_G%C3%ABrdec_explosions) were felt in the capital as windows were shattered and citizens shaken. In January 2011, the [Albanian opposition demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Albanian_opposition_demonstrations) were triggered in front of the governmental buildings in Tirana protesting against political corruption and state capture, particularly associated with the former prime minister [Sali Berisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sali_Berisha)'s government.[^cite_note-44] In September 2014, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) made an official state visit to Tirana simultaneously becoming the second pontiff to visit Albania, after Pope John Paul II in 1992.[^cite_note-Pope_Francis-45][^cite_note-46]&#x20;

Following the municipal elections of 2015, power was transferred from the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Albania) representative [Lulzim Basha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulzim_Basha) to the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Albania) candidate [Erion Veliaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erion_Veliaj).[^cite_note-47] Albania then underwent a territorial reform, in which defunct communes were merged with municipalities.[^cite_note-48] Thirteen of Tirana's former communes were integrated as administrative units joining the existing eleven.[^cite_note-\:0-49] Since then, Tirana is undergoing significant changes in infrastructure, law enforcement and new projects, as well as continuing the ones started by Veliaj's predecessor. In their first few council meetings, 242 social houses got allocated to families in need.[^cite_note-50] Construction permits were suspended until the capital's development plan is revised and synthesized.[^cite_note-\:0-49]&#x20;

Between December 2018 and February 2019, a [series of demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932019_student_protest_in_Albania) erupted in the central areas of Tirana and other cities of the country in response to a controversial law on higher education, poor quality of teaching, high tuition rates and corruption.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In September 2019, Tirana was ravaged by a 5.6 [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) [earthquake](10106.md) with an epicenter located near Durrës.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Two months after, in November 2019, another strong [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Albania_earthquake) with the magnitude of 6.4 hit the region again resulting in comparatively few damages in Tirana.[^cite_note-57] The same month, Tirana was announced as the [European Youth Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Capital) for 2022 with a planned program including events of cultural and social importance.[^cite_note-58]&#x20;

In December 2022, Tirana hosted the [EU-Western Balkans summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_EU-Western_Balkans_summit), marking the inaugural summit to be held outside the European Union's borders.[^cite_note-59] On 21 September 2024, it was reported that [Prime Minister of Albania](259136.md) [Edi Rama](277805.md) was planning to create the [Sovereign State of the Bektashi Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_State_of_the_Bektashi_Order), a sovereign [microstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate) for the Order within Tirana.[^cite_note-nyt_article-60]&#x20;

## Geography



View of Tirana Metropolitan Area

Tirana extends at the [Plain of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_plain) in the centre of Albania between the mount of [Dajti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dajti) in the east, the hills of Kërrabe, Sauk and Vaqarr in the south, and a valley to the north overlooking the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md). The average altitude is about 110 meters (360 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level), with a maximum of 1,828 metres (5,997 feet) at Maja Mincekut of Mali me Gropa in Shenmeri.[^cite_note-61]&#x20;

The city is surrounded by two important [protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_Albania): the [Dajti National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dajti_National_Park) and [Mali me Gropa-Bizë-Martanesh Protected Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_me_Gropa-Biz%C3%AB-Martanesh_Protected_Landscape). In winter, the mountains are often covered with snow and are a popular retreat for the population of Tirana, which rarely receives snowfalls. In terms of [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity), the forests are mainly composed of [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), [oak](39696.md) and [beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech), while its interior relief is dotted with [canyons](78478.md), waterfalls, caves, lakes and other landforms.[^cite_note-62] Thanks to its natural heritage, it is considered the "Natural Balcony of Tirana". The mountain can be reached by a narrow asphalt mountain road onto an area known as Fusha e Dajtit. From this small area there is a view of Tirana and its plain.&#x20;

[Tiranë river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiran%C3%AB_\(river\)) flows through the city, as does the Lanë river. Tirana is home to several artificial lakes, including [Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Park_of_Tirana), Farka, Tufina, and Kashar. The present municipality was formed in the 2015 local government reform by the merger of the former municipalities of Baldushk, Bërzhitë, Dajt, Farkë, Kashar, Krrabë, Ndroq, Petrelë, Pezë, Shëngjergj, Tirana, Vaqarr, Zall-Bastar and Zall-Herr, which became municipal units. The seat of the municipality is the city of Tirana.[^cite_note-Law_2014-8]&#x20;

### Climate



Snow at the [Dajti National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dajti_National_Park). It generally melts quickly in the region.

[^cite_note-63]

Tirana has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*) according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) and receives enough [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) during summer to avoid the [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa*) classification.&#x20;

The average precipitation in Tirana is about 1,266 mm (49.8 in) per year. The city receives the majority of precipitation in winter months, which occurs from November to March, and less in summer months, from June to September. In terms of precipitation, both rain and snow, the city is ranked among the wettest cities in [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-currentresults.com-64]&#x20;

Temperatures vary throughout the year from an average of 6.7 °C (44.1 °F) in January to 24 °C (75 °F) in July. [Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(season\)) and [summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_\(season\)) are very warm to hot often reaching over 20 °C (68 °F) from May to September. During [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn) and [winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_\(season\)), from November to March, the average temperature drops and is not lower than 6.7 °C (44.1 °F). The city receives approximately 2500 hours of sun.[^cite_note-65]&#x20;

### Urbanism



The [artificial lake of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Park_of_Tirana) built from local waters in 1955

In September 2015, Tirana organized its first vehicle-free day, joining forces with numerous cities across the globe to fight against the existing problem of urban air pollution. This initiative resulted in a considerable drop in both air and noise pollution, encouraging the Municipality to organise a vehicle-free day every month.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]&#x20;

The city suffers from problems such as [waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management), high levels of [air pollution](10934212.md) and significant [noise pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution). Over the last decades, air pollution has become a pressing concern as the number of cars has increased. These are mostly 1990s and early 2000s [diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine) cars,[^cite_note-75] while it is widely believed that the [fuel](21188370.md) used in Albania contains larger amounts of [sulfur](27127.md) and [lead](17747.md) than in the [European Union](9317.md). Effective 1 January 2019, the government has imposed an import ban of used vehicles made prior to 2005 in an effort to curb pollution, encourage the buying of new cars from certified domestic dealerships, and to improve overall road safety. Another source of pollution are PM10 and PM2.5 [inhaled particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_particulate_matter) and [NO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide) gases[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] resulting from rapid growth in the construction of new buildings and expanding road infrastructure.[^cite_note-78]&#x20;

Untreated [solid waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_waste) is present in the city and outskirts. Additionally, there have been complaints of excessive noise pollution. Despite the problems, the Grand Park at the Artificial Lake has some effect on absorbing CO<sub>2</sub> emissions, while over 2.000 trees have been planted around sidewalks.&#x20;

Works for four new large parks have started in the summer of 2015 located in Kashar, [Farkë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farka_Lake), Vaqarr, and [Dajt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dajti_Mountain). These parks are part of the new urban plan striving to increase the concentration of green spaces in the capital.[^cite_note-79] The government has included designated green areas around Tirana as part of the Tirana Greenbelt where construction is not permitted or limited.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]&#x20;

### National capital



The [Kryeministria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister%27s_Office_\(Albania\)) at the [Dëshmorët e Kombit Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%ABshmor%C3%ABt_e_Kombit_Boulevard) is the official office of the [Prime Minister of Albania](259136.md).

Tirana is the [capital city](181337.md) of the [Republic of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Albania) thus playing an essential role in shaping the political and economic life of the country.[^cite_note-85] It is the home to government functions and institutions for which the government of Albania is responsible, as for instance the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), [juridical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) and [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative) branches of Albania.&#x20;

The President and [Prime Minister of Albania](259136.md) officially reside and work in Tirana specifically at the [Presidenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Office_Building,_Tirana) and [Kryeministria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister%27s_Office_\(Albania\)), respectively, nevertheless the [Parliament of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Albania) is headquartered at the [Dëshmorët e Kombit Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%ABshmor%C3%ABt_e_Kombit_Boulevard).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Tirana is also the home to the national [Constitutional Court](29207004.md) and [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Albania). Important national institutions housed in Tirana include the [Appeal Court](49668987.md) and [Administrative Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_Courts_in_Albania).&#x20;

The [Bank of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Albania) is located at the Skanderbeg Square while other institutions such as the ministries of [Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Albania\)), [Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Albania\)), [Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education,_Sports_and_Youth_\(Albania\)), [Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_and_Economy_\(Albania\)), [Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Europe_and_Foreign_Affairs_\(Albania\)), [Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Social_Protection), [Infrastructure](54109553.md), [Internal Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_\(Albania\)), [Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Albania\)) and [Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism_and_Environment_\(Albania\)) are spread over Tirana. The city is also the home to all the consulates and embassies in Albania, thereby making it an important centre for international diplomacy in the country.&#x20;

## Economy



[Bank of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Albania)

Tirana is the heart of the [economy of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Albania) and the most [industrialised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialised) and economically fastest growing region in Albania. Of the main [sectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_\(economy\)), the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector) is the most important for the economy of Tirana and employs more than 68% of work force of Tirana.[^cite_note-sectors-102] 26% of the working population makes up the [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector) followed by the [primary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector) with only 5%.[^cite_note-sectors-102]&#x20;

The city began to develop at the beginning of the 16th century as it was part of the Ottoman Empire, when a bazaar was established, and its craftsmen manufactured silk and cotton fabrics, leather, ceramics and iron, silver and gold artefacts.[^cite_note-103] In the 20th century, the city and its surrounding areas expanded rapidly and became the most heavily industrialised region of the country.&#x20;

The most significant contribution is made by the tertiary sector which has developed considerably since the fall of communism in Albania. Forming the financial centre of the country, the [financial industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_industry) is a major component of the city's tertiary sector and remains in good conditions overall due to [privatization](24661.md) and the commendable [monetary policy](297032.md).[^cite_note-104] All of the most important financial institutions, such as the Bank of Albania and the [Albanian Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Stock_Exchange) are centred in Tirana as well as most of the [banking companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banks_in_Albania) such as the [Banka Kombëtare Tregtare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banka_Komb%C3%ABtare_Tregtare), [Raiffeisen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raiffeisen_\(Albania\)), [Credins Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credins_Bank), [Intesa Sanpaolo Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intesa_Sanpaolo_Bank_\(Albania\)) and [Tirana Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Bank).&#x20;



[Maritim Plaza Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Hotel_Tirana) is in the centre of Tirana.

The [telecommunication industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication_industry) represents another major and growing contributor to the sector.[^cite_note-105] A rapid development occurred as well as after the end of communism and decades of isolationism mainly due to the new national policy of reform and opening up sped up the industry's development. [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Albania), [Telekom Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telekom_Albania) and [Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Mobile) are the leading telecommunication providers in Tirana, as in all the country.&#x20;

The [tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_industry) of the city has expanded in recent years to become a vital component of the economy.[^cite_note-106] Tirana has been officially dubbed as 'The Place Beyond Belief' by local authorities.[^cite_note-107] The increasing number of international arrivals at the [Tirana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_International_Airport) and [Port of Durrës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Durr%C3%ABs) from across Europe, Australia and Asia has rapidly grown the number of foreign visitors in the city.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]&#x20;

The largest hotels of the city are the [Tirana International Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_International_Hotel), [Maritim Plaza Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TID_Tower) both situated in the heart of the city near Skanderbeg Square, and the Hyatt-owned luxury Mak Hotel Tirana[^cite_note-110] located next to the [Air Albania Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Komb%C3%ABtare), where Mariott Tirana Hotel is also planned to open.[^cite_note-111] Other major hotels present in central Tirana include the Rogner Hotel, Hilton Garden Inn Tirana, Xheko Imperial Hotel, Best Western Premier Ark Hotel, and Mondial Hotel.&#x20;

## Infrastructure

### Transport



The terminal of [Nënë Tereza International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_International_Airport_N%C3%ABn%C3%AB_Tereza)

#### Air

Tirana is served by [Nënë Tereza International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_International_Airport_N%C3%ABn%C3%AB_Tereza), which is the main air gateway to the country. The airport was officially named in honour of the Albanian Roman Catholic nun and missionary, Mother Teresa. It connects Tirana with destinations across Europe, Africa, and Asia. The airport carried more than 3.3 million passengers in 2019.&#x20;

#### Road



The [Rruga Shtetërore 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH_2_\(Albania\)) (SH2) connecting Tirana with [Durrës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durr%C3%ABs)

Tirana's geographical location in the centre of Albania has long established the city as the core terminus for the [national road network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_Albania), thus connecting the city to all parts of Albania and neighbouring countries.[^cite_note-112] The [Rruga Shtetërore 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH_1_\(Albania\)) (SH1) connects Tirana with [Shkodër](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shkod%C3%ABr) and [Montenegro](20760.md) in the north, and constitutes a key section of the proposed [Adriatic–Ionian motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic%E2%80%93Ionian_motorway). The [Rruga Shtetërore 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH_2_\(Albania\)) (SH2) continues in the west and provides direct connection to Durrës on the Adriatic Sea. The Rruga Shtetërore 3 (SH3) is being transformed into the [Autostrada 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_\(Albania\)) (A3) and follows the ancient [Via Egnatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Egnatia). It is a significant section of the [Pan-European Corridor VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_VIII) and links the city with [Elbasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbasan), Korçë and Greece in the south. Tirana is further connected, through the Milot interchange in the northwest, with [Kosovo](17391.md) as part of the [Autostrada 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_motorway_\(Albania\)) (A1).&#x20;

During the [communist regime in Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism_in_Albania), a plan for the construction of a [ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road) around Tirana arose in 1989 but implementation was delayed until the 2010s.[^cite_note-113] It is of major importance, especially concerning demographic growth of the Tirana region as well as the Albanian economy. Although, constructions for the now completed southern section of the ring road started in 2011, the northern and eastern sections are still in the planning process.[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

#### Rail

Rail lines of [Hekurudha Shqiptare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hekurudha_Shqiptare) (HSH) connected Tirana with all of the major cities of Albania, including Durrës, Shkodër and Vlorë. In 2013, the [Tirana Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Railway_Station) was closed and moved to Kashar by the city government in order to create space for the New Boulevard ([Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language): *Bulevardi i Ri*) project.[^cite_note-115] The new Tirana Station will be constructed in [Laprakë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laprak%C3%AB), which is projected to be a multifunctional terminal for rail, tram and bus transportation.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] City officials have discussed a light rail extension to the location of the former rail station.[^cite_note-Linja_e_re_hekurudhore-118] Construction is underway on a new rail line from Tirana to Nënë Tereza International Airport and Durrës.[^cite_note-Linja_e_re_hekurudhore-118]&#x20;

In 2012, the Tirana municipality published a report that construction of two tram lines was under evaluation. The tram lines would have a total length of 16.7 kilometres (10.4 miles). That plan was never implemented. Under the plan, the two tram lines would have intersected in Skanderbeg Square. Public transport in Tirana is, for now, focused only in the city centre, people living in the suburbs have fewer or no public transport connections.[^cite_note-119]&#x20;

#### Bus

Tirana's bus network is its primary and only system of public transport. The system has 16 lines serving the urban core, with another 14 suburban lines serving its outer regions. The urban network is made up of 8 radial lines, 5 diagonal lines, and 3 circular or semi-circular lines.[^cite_note-Sustainable_Urban_Mobility_Plan-120]&#x20;

Busses often get stuck in traffic. There were only 7 kilometres (4.3 miles) of dedicated bus lines in 2020.[^cite_note-Sustainable_Urban_Mobility_Plan-120]&#x20;

There were 451 bus stops on the entire urban network in 2020, extending to over 170 kilometres (110 miles). Services operate every day, regardless of holidays and weekends, from 06\:00 to 24\:00. The entire network had an average [headway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headway) of 9 minutes on a daily basis in 2018 and in the first half of 2019.[^cite_note-Sustainable_Urban_Mobility_Plan-120]&#x20;

Nearly 257,000 residents lived within a 150 m (490 ft) walking area from a bus stop when surveyed in Tirana’s strategic transport study in 2019. That was approximately 35% of the population living inside the Municipal unit of Tirana.[^cite_note-Sustainable_Urban_Mobility_Plan-120]&#x20;

A single-ride ticket costs 40 Lek, and no hourly or daily options are available. Disabled people and veterans may ride for free. An electronic ticketing system is under implementation.[^cite_note-Sustainable_Urban_Mobility_Plan-120]&#x20;



An [Ecovolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecovolis) station near [Mother Teresa Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa_Square_\(Tirana\))

#### Cycling

During the administration of mayor Erion Veliaj, the government of Tirana has significantly increased the creation and expansion of a [cycling infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_infrastructure) in the city in order to reduce [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) as well as to improve the [sustainable transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transport).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] [Ecovolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecovolis) was launched in 2011 offering rental services for [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle) at different centrally located stations for a small fee.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] The international [bicycle sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_sharing_system), [Mobike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobike), launched its operations on 8 June 2018 by deploying 4000 bicycles in the city.[^cite_note-Mobike-126][^cite_note-127] Both services were discontinued.&#x20;

Safe cycling paths are still relatively rare, despite improvements in recent years. Paths are often blocked by parked cars or construction. Other obstructions which decrease ride quality, such as drain holes, are also prevalent. There were only around 30 kilometres (19 miles) of cycling routes in 2020.[^cite_note-Sustainable_Urban_Mobility_Plan-120]&#x20;

### Education



The [University of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arts_\(Albania\)) at [Mother Teresa Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa_Square_\(Tirana\))

Tirana has the highest concentration of institutions of [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) in Albania, consisting of numerous [academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy), [colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College) and [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities). Most prominent among these is the University of Tirana with campuses around the city and more than 28,000 students from all backgrounds.[^cite_note-Education-128] The [Polytechnic University of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Tirana) is another distinguished institution and also the most renowned engineering and technical university in the country. The four other public institutions in Tirana are the [University of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arts_\(Albania\)), [University of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_University_of_Tirana), [University of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Medicine,_Tirana) and [University of Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_University_of_Tirana).[^cite_note-Education-128]&#x20;

The educational system of Tirana has expanded substantially over the past years with the renovations of existing school facilities and the construction of new schools.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] For [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education) and [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education) education, a variety of schools are available, tagged with the public, private and international labels. Some of the international schools are the [Tirana International School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_International_School), Albanian International School, British School, Montessori School, Memorial School and World Academy. The public primary and secondary Servete Maçi School won an award at the 2020 [International Architecture Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Architecture_Awards).[^cite_note-131]&#x20;

### Health

There are numerous public and private [hospitals](18994221.md) as well as smaller public and private [health care facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_facilities) in the territory of Tirana. The [Mother Teresa University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_Medical_Centre_of_Tirana_%22Mother_Teresa%22\&action=edit\&redlink=1) is one of the largest medical tertiary institutions of the country. Other medical institutions include the [Shefqet Ndroqi University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Hospital_%22Shefqet_Ndroqi%22), Koço Gliozheni Hospital, Mbretëresha Gjeraldinë Hospital and the [Military Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Military_Hospital).&#x20;

According to the 2023 census, the municipality of Tirana had 598,176 residents, of whom 583,963 were born in Albania and 14,213 abroad. With a population density of 502 people per square kilometre, Tirana is the most densely populated municipality in the country.[^cite_note-PV-5] The encompassing [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area), consisting of the regions of Durrës and Tirana, has a combined population of approximately 1 million, amounting to nearly one third of the country's total population.[^cite_note-census15-138]&#x20;

Historically, Tirana has experienced a steady population increase, especially after the fall of communism in the late twentieth century as well as the beginning of the twenty-first century. The remarkable growth was, and still is, largely fuelled by migrants from all over the country, often in search of [employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment) and improved living conditions. Between 1820 and 1955, the population of Tirana tenfolded, while during the period from 1989 to 2011 the city's population grew annually by approximately 2.7%. In the 19th and 20th centuries, the city had a rate of growth of less than 1% annually until the 1970s, then down to less than 8% per year until the middle 20th century figures.[^cite_note-139]&#x20;

Among residents born in Albania, the majority were born in Tirana itself (310,084), but the municipality has long been the principal internal migration destination in the country. Large communities trace their birthplace to Dibër (38,612), Korçë (35,667), Fier (30,290), Berat (30,254), Kukës (27,246), Elbasan (26,258) and Gjirokastër (25,885), with additional groups from Shkodër (18,226), Vlorë (15,599), Durrës (14,371) and Lezhë (11,471). The inflows from north-eastern mountainous prefectures such as Dibër and Kukës, from the south-eastern and southern interior around Korçë, Berat, Gjirokastër and Elbasan, and from coastal areas including Durrës, Vlorë and Lezhë illustrate a predominantly rural-to-urban and intra-regional migration pattern oriented toward the capital. These movements intensified after the early 1990s, when internal mobility restrictions were lifted, and include both long-distance migration from remote highland municipalities and short-distance relocation within central Albania. The resulting settlement pattern combines older urban neighbourhoods with rapidly urbanised former rural areas on the city’s outskirts and along main transport corridors. This wide geographic spread reflects the city’s dominant role as Albania’s administrative, educational and economic centre and has contributed to its rapid demographic expansion and increasing social diversity since the early 1990s.[^cite_note-140]&#x20;

According to the 2023 Population and Housing Census profile for [Tirana County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_County), the population is predominantly of [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians) ethnicity (91.0%), followed by small minorities of [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Albania) (0.26%), [Aromanians (Vlachs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) (0.10%), [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_\(ethnic_group\)) (0.06%), [Montenegrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrins) (0.03%), [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Albania) (0.22%), [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkali_and_Balkan_Egyptians) (0.13%), [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks), [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_in_Albania), [Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians_in_Albania) and [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians_in_Albania) (each below 0.05%). About 8% of residents did not declare or provided no ethnic affiliation. The majority of inhabitants reported [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) as their mother tongue (94.7%), with smaller proportions declaring [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) (0.4%), [Aromanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanian_language) (0.2%), or other and multiple languages (1.1%).[^cite_note-141]&#x20;

The [life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) in Tirana is 82.8 years, 84.5 years for women and 81 years for men,[^cite_note-142] exceeding that of wealthier capital cities in [Western Europe](33800.md) such as [Vienna](55866.md)[^cite_note-143] and [Berlin](3354.md).[^cite_note-144]&#x20;

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Tirana)

The Albanians mosaic

Panorama of *The Albanians History* mosaic above the entrance of the [National History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_History_Museum_\(Albania\))

Tirana offers a blend of traditional and modern lifestyle with a variety of arts, food, entertainment, music and night life. Its population celebrates a wide range of religious and other festivals including [Christmas](6237.md), [Eid](2035419.md), [Hanukkah](7388.md) and [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz). Another festival is [Dita e Verës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Festival,_Albania) taking place every year on 14 March, during which the Albanians celebrate the end of winter and the arrival of spring.&#x20;

Among the local institutions are the [National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Albania), that keeps more than a million books, periodicals, maps, atlases, microfilms and other library materials. The city has five well-preserved traditional houses (museum-houses), 56 cultural [monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monuments), eight public [libraries](17727.md).[^cite_note-153]&#x20;

There are many domestic and foreign cultural institutions in Tirana, amongst them the [British Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Council), [Canadian Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Institute_of_Technology) as well as the German [Goethe-Institut](470336.md) and [Friedrich Ebert Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Ebert_Foundation).[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] Other cultural institutions include the Chinese [Confucius Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius_Institute), Greek [Hellenic Foundation for Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Foundation_for_Culture), Italian [Istituto Italiano di Cultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Italiano_di_Cultura) and the French [Alliance Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Fran%C3%A7aise).[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]&#x20;

### Architecture



The Toptani house from the 18th century

Tirana is home to a mixture of architectural styles reflecting the influential periods in its history. Its current appearance was considerably shaped by two totalitarian regimes, once by the fascist regime of Benito Mussolini during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) and the regime of Enver Hoxha in the [aftermath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II). The [Palace of Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Tirana), the ministries buildings, the government building and the municipality hall are designed by Florestano Di Fausto and Armando Brasini, both well-known architects of the Mussolini period in Italy. The Dëshmorët e Kombit Boulevard was built in 1930 and given the name [King Zog I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zog_I_of_Albania) Boulevard.&#x20;



[Florestano Di Fausto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florestano_Di_Fausto) and [Armando Brasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Brasini) designed the city plan for Tirana in [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Renaissance_architecture) style with articulate angular solutions and giant order fascias.

In the 20th century, the part from Skanderbeg Square up to the train station was named Stalin Boulevard. The Royal Palace or Palace of Brigades previously served as the official residence of King Zog I. It has been used by different Albanian governments for various purposes. Because of the outbreak of World War II, and the 1939 [Italian invasion of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_Albania), Zog I fled Albania and never had a chance to see the Palace fully constructed. The Italians finished it and used it as the Army Headquarters. The Palace took its nickname Palace of Brigades because it was taken from the Italians by a people's army brigade.[^cite_note-162]&#x20;

In the 21st century, Tirana turned into a proper modernist city, with large blocks of flats, modern new buildings, new shopping centres and many green spaces. In June 2016, the Mayor of Tirana Erion Veliaj and the Italian architect [Stefano Boeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Boeri) announced the start of the works for the redaction of the Master Plan *Tirana 2030*.[^cite_note-163]&#x20;



The entrance of the [Grand Park of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Park_of_Tirana)

Tirana is a densely-built area and still offers several public parks throughout its districts, graced with green gardens. The Grand Park is the most important green space in Tirana. It is one of the most visited areas by local citizens.[^cite_note-164] The park includes many children's playgrounds, sport facilities and landmarks such as the [Saint Procopius Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Procopius_Church_of_Tirana), the [Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace_of_Tirana), the [Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_Gardens_of_Tirana), the [Tirana Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Zoo), the Amphitheatre, the Monument of the Frashëri Brothers and many others.&#x20;

The [Rinia Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinia_Park) was built during the Communist era. It bordered by Dëshmorët e Kombit Boulevard to the east, Gjergi Fishta Boulevard and [Bajram Curri Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajram_Curri_Boulevard) to the south, Rruga Ibrahim Rugova to the west and Rruga Myslym Shyri to the north. The [Taivani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taivani) Centre is the main landmark in the park and houses cafés, restaurants, fountains, and a bowling lane in the basement. The Summer Festival takes place every year in the park, to celebrate the end of winter and the rebirth of nature and a rejuvenation of spirit amongst the Albanians.&#x20;

As of 2020, it was announced that the Municipality of Tirana will build more green spaces and will plant more trees.[^cite_note-165]&#x20;



The Alban Tower, designed by Archea Associati (Italy), completed in 2021

### Museums

Further information: [Museums in Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Museums_in_Tirana)



The [National History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_History_Museum_\(Albania\)) at the [Skanderbeg Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderbeg_Square)

Tirana is the home to a number of museums dedicated to a wide array of arts. The [National History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_History_Museum_\(Albania\)) is located at the Skanderbeg Square and the most representative museum of Tirana.[^cite_note-166] The mosaic above the entrance is the most dominant feature of the museum displaying the story of how the [Albanian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_people) have fought against invasion and occupation throughout history.&#x20;

Founded in 1948, the [National Museum of Archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Archaeology,_Albania) at the Mother Teresa Square displays a wide collection of research and discoveries belonging to the archaeological locations around Albania.[^cite_note-167] Its exhibits span from prehistory through antiquity and the Middle Ages to the twentieth century, offering an overview of the country's historical diversity.&#x20;



The *Cloud Pavilion* outside the [National Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Art_Gallery_of_Albania) installed by Japanese artist [Sou Fujimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sou_Fujimoto)

[^cite_note-168]

The [National Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Art_Gallery_of_Albania) is considered the most important gallery in Albania housing one of the greatest collections of paintings in the region.[^cite_note-169] Located at the Dëshmorët e Kombit Boulevard, it holds approximately 4.500 works of art including the most important collection of [Albanian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_art) of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.&#x20;

The [Bunk'art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunkers_in_Albania) consists of two underground bunkers built under the orders and direction of Hoxha during the communist era. Located at the Fadil Deliu Street and Abdi Toptani Street respectively, the bunkers have been transformed into a history museum and contemporary art gallery with exhibits from the Second World War and [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]&#x20;

The [Museum of Secret Surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Secret_Surveillance) was founded in 2017 and is housed within a twentieth century mansion, the building known as the *House of Leaves*, near the Dëshmorët e Kombit Boulevard.[^cite_note-172] It commemorates and honours the victims who fell to the communist terrorism and violence during the communist period in Albania. Other museums include the [Natural Sciences Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_Museum_of_Albania), which has branches in zoology, botany and geology, the former Enver Hoxha Museum and the Bunk'art Museum.&#x20;

The [Bektashi Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bektashi_Museum) was opened at the [World Headquarters of the Bektashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Headquarters_of_the_Bektashi) on 7 September 2015. The museum contains exhibits relating to Bektashi history and leadership.[^cite_note-173]&#x20;

### Cuisine

As in other parts of Albania, agricultural traditions are of great significance to the Albanians in Tirana, substantially appreciated for the production of food such as cheese, olives and wine. In 2016, Albania surpassed Spain by becoming the country with the most coffee houses per capita in the world with 654 coffee houses per 100,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-174] This is due to coffee houses closing down in Spain due to the economic crisis, and the fact that as many cafes open as they close in Albania. In addition, the fact that it was one of the easiest ways to make a living after the fall of communism in Albania, together with the country's Ottoman legacy further reinforce its strong dominance in Albania.&#x20;

The Tirana region is known for the Fergesa traditional dish made with either peppers or liver,[^cite_note-175] and is found at a number of traditional restaurants in the city and agri-tourism sites on the outskirts of Tirana.&#x20;

### Sports

Being the capital, Tirana is the centre of sport in Albania, where activity is organised across amateur and professional levels. It is home to many major sporting facilities. Starting from 2007, the Tirana Municipality has built up to 80 sport gardens in most of Tirana's neighborhoods. One of the latest projects is the reconstruction of the existing [Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Olympic_Park), that will provide infrastructure for most intramural sports.[^cite_note-176]&#x20;

Tirana hosted in the past three major events, the [2006 European Promotion Cup for Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_European_Promotion_Cup_for_Men), [2011 World Mountain Running Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_World_Mountain_Running_Championships) and the [2013 European Weightlifting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_European_Weightlifting_Championships).&#x20;



[Air Albania Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Arena_Stadium) in the city centre under construction

There are two major stadiums, the former [Qemal Stafa Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qemal_Stafa_Stadium) and the [Selman Stërmasi stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selman_St%C3%ABrmasi_stadium). The former was demolished in 2016 to make way for the new national stadium.[^cite_note-177] The new stadium called the *[Air Albania Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Arena_Stadium)* was constructed on the same site of the former Qemal Stafa Stadium and opened in November 2019.[^cite_note-178] It will have an underground parking lot, Marriott Tirana Hotel, shops and bars and will be used for entertainment events. Tirana's sports infrastructure is developing fast because of the investments from the municipality and the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism,_Cultural_Affairs,_Youth_and_Sports_\(Albania\)).&#x20;

[Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Albania) is the most widely followed sport in Tirana as well as in the country, having numerous club teams including the [KF Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KF_Tirana), [Partizani Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partizani_Tirana), and [Dinamo Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinamo_Tirana). It is popular at every level of society, from children to wealthy professionals. In football, as of April 2012, the Tirana-based teams have won a combined [57 championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Albanian_football_champions) out of 72 championships organised by the [FSHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FSHF), i.e. 79% of them. Another popular sport in Albania is basketball, represented in particular by the teams [KB Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KB_Tirana), [BC Partizani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Partizani_Tirana), [BC Dinamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Dinamo_Tirana), [Ardhmëria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardhm%C3%ABria_Basket) and also the women's [PBC Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBC_Tirana_\(women\)).&#x20;

Recently two [rugby teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_Albania) were created: [Tirana Rugby Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Rugby_Club),[^cite_note-179] founded in 2013 and Ilirët Rugby Club[^cite_note-180] founded in 2016.&#x20;

### Media



The former grounds of the headquarters of [Radio Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Tirana). Radio Televizioni Shqiptar (RTSH) was initially founded as Radio Tirana in 1938.

As the capital, Tirana is the most significant location for the Albanian media industry whose content is distributed throughout Albania, Kosovo and other Albanian-speaking territories. Tirana is the home to most of the national and international [television stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_station), including the national broadcaster, [Radio Televizioni Shqiptar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Televizioni_Shqiptar) (RTSH), along with all its television and radio networks. The three largest Albanian commercial broadcasters, such as [Televizioni Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Klan), [Top Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Channel) and [Vizion Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizion_Plus), also maintains their headquarters in the city. The European broadcaster, [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews), operates a franchise in the city as well as the American broadcaster [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_International).[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]&#x20;

Tirana is also a principal location for the largest Albanian newspapers, magazines and publications. The newspapers with the largest circulations in Albania are published in Tirana, including [Gazeta Shqip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Shqip), [Gazeta Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Tema), [Koha Jonë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koha_Jon%C3%AB) and Panorama. [Gazeta Shqiptare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Shqiptare), one of the oldest Albanian-language newspapers in Albania, operates and has its headquarters in the city.[^cite_note-183] Tirana also has a well-established [English-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) newspaper, notably the daily of [Tirana Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_Times).&#x20;
