**Kabardino-Balkaria** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Кабарди́но-Балка́рия), officially the **Kabardino-Balkarian Republic**,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) of [Russia](25391.md) located in the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus). As of the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\)), its population was 904,200.[^cite_note-2021Census-15] Its capital is [Nalchik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalchik). The area contains the highest mountain in Europe, [Mount Elbrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Elbrus), at 5,642 m (18,510 ft). Mount Elbrus has 22 glaciers that feed three rivers — [Baksan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baksan_\(river\)), [Malka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malka_\(river\)) and [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_\(river\)). The mountain is covered with snow year-round.

## Geography

The republic is situated in the North Caucasus mountains, with plains in the northern part. The republic shares an international border with [Georgia](48768.md).

- *Area*: 12,500 square kilometers (4,800 sq mi)

- *Borders*:&#x20;

  - *internal*: [Stavropol Krai](474074.md) (N/NE), [North Ossetia–Alania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ossetia%E2%80%93Alania) (E/SE/S), [Karachay–Cherkessia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachay%E2%80%93Cherkessia) (W/NW)

  - *international*: [Georgia](48768.md) ([Racha-Lechkhumi and Kvemo Svaneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racha-Lechkhumi_and_Kvemo_Svaneti), [Zemo Svaneti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svaneti)) (S/SW)

- *Highest point*: [Mount Elbrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Elbrus) (5,642 m)

- *Maximum N->S distance*: 167 kilometers (104 mi)

- *Maximum E->W distance*: 123 kilometers (76 mi)

Kabardino-Balkaria is traversed by the northeasterly line of equal latitude and longitude.

### Natural resources

Kabardino-Balkaria's natural resources include [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum), [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten), and [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal).

### Climate

The republic has a continental-type climate.

- *Average January temperature*: −12 °C (10 °F) (mountains) to −4 °C (25 °F) (plains)

- *Average July temperature*: +4 °C (39 °F) (mountains) to +23 °C (73 °F) (plains)

- *Average annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation)*: 500–2,000 mm.

## History

See also: [History of Kabardino-Balkaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kabardino-Balkaria)

The ancestors of the modern [Kabardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabardians), known as [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) or [Kassogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians), have inhabited the area since at least the 6th century BCE.[^cite_note-\:0-16] During this period, the region was known as [Zichia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zichia), a medieval Circassian land located on the northeastern shore of the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-17] Historical sources first mention Zichia in the 6th century, with [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) historian [Procopius of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procopius) recording that the people of the Zechoi had a king appointed by the [Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor) but had since become independent.[^cite_note-\:1-18] The [Notitiae Episcopatuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notitiae_Episcopatuum) of the [Patriarchate of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople) mentions an [autocephalous archbishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of Zichia from the 7th century onward, associated with [Tamatarcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tmutarakan) or the [Cimmerian Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait).[^cite_note-\:1-18]

Between 1242 and 1295, the region came under the control of the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols).[^cite_note-19] From 1295 to around 1427, it was governed by the [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians).[^cite_note-\:2-20] In the early 15th century, the area became part of [Circassia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassia), which remained independent until the death of [King Inal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inal_the_Great) in 1453. Afterward, the kingdom experienced internal divisions and external pressures, leading to a gradual decline in its sovereignty.[^cite_note-\:2-20]

Between 1769 and 1830, during the [Russo-Circassian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Circassian_War), the region fell under Russian occupation.[^cite_note-\:3-21] This period was marked by significant conflict and resistance from the indigenous populations. The war culminated in the annexation of Kabardino-Balkaria by [Russia](25391.md), leading to profound changes in the region's political and social structures.[^cite_note-\:3-21]

During the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union), Kabardino-Balkaria was part of the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), undergoing industrial growth but also facing cultural suppression. After the [USSR's collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), it became a republic within [Russia](25391.md) and, on 1 July 1994, signed a power-sharing agreement granting it limited autonomy.[^cite_note-asymmetries-22] This agreement allowed the republic to manage its own affairs to some extent, though it remained under the sovereignty of the Russian Federation.[^cite_note-23] In 2001, Kabardino-Balkaria adopted a new constitution that reaffirmed its status within the Russian Federation, explicitly preventing the republic from existing independently.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Politics

The head of government in Kabardino-Balkaria is the [Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Kabardino-Balkarian_Republic). The current Head is [Kazbek Kokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazbek_Kokov).[^cite_note-26] The legislative body of the Republic is the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Kabardino-Balkaria) comprising 70 deputies elected for a five-year term.[^cite_note-HeadLegis-3][^cite_note-Parliament-27]

The republic adopted a new constitution in 2001 which prevents the republic from existing independently of the Russian Federation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBell200378-28]

### Ethnic groups

Kabardino-Balkaria includes two major ethnic communities, the [Kabardins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabarday) (Circassians), who speak a [North-West Caucasian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Caucasian_languages), and the [Balkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkars) who speak a [Turkic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_language). According to the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\)),[^cite_note-census2021-38] Kabardins make up 57.1% of the republic's population, followed by [Russians](59510.md) (19.8%) and Balkars (13.7%). Other groups include [Cherkess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherkess) (3.0%), [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) (1.9%), [Ossetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetians) (0.8%), [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (0.5%), and a host of smaller groups, each accounting for less than 0.5% of the total population.

According to a 2012 survey which interviewed 56,900 people,[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-41] 70.8% of the population of Kabardino-Balkaria adhered to [Islam](6037917.md), 11.6% to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), 3.8% were non-Orthodox [Christians](18337522.md), and 1.8% followed Adyghe (Kabardian) folk religion and other indigenous faiths. In addition, 5.6% of the population declared to be "[spiritual but not religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_but_not_religious)" and 4.4% was [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) or followed other religions, including [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witness).[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-41]
