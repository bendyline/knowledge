The **Malawi Defence Force**[^cite_note-2] is the state military organisation responsible for defending [Malawi](19088.md). It originated from elements of the British [King's African Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_African_Rifles), colonial units formed before independence in 1964.

The military is organized under the purview of the Ministry of Defence.

## Malawi Army

Before independence, Malawi depended for its military supplies on the barracks in [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia), as British colonial military logistics was usually organized on a continental basis, rather than at the level of individual colonies.[^cite_note-3] The Malawi Rifles were formed when the country gained its independence from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) in 1964. Its first battalion was formed from the 1st Battalion, [King's African Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_African_Rifles). Upon independence the battalion became the 1st Battalion of the Malawi Rifles (Malawi Army). They were based at what became the headquarters of the Malawi Army at [Cobbe Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobbe_Barracks?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Zomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomba,_Malawi). Cobbe Barracks had been named in May 1958 in honor of the British General [Alexander Cobbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Cobbe) VC, who had served with the King's African Rifles. The Rifles were reportedly at a strength of 2,000 men at independence. On 6 July 1966 Malawi became a Republic and [His Excellency Dr Hastings Kamuzu Banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Excellency_Dr_Hastings_Kamuzu_Banda) became the first president. After the swearing in ceremony his first duty was to present the battalion with his own presidential colour and the new regimental colour.[^cite_note-4] It was under the leadership of Brigadier Paul Lewis, a British expatriate,[^cite_note-Phiri2000-5] and Welsh Colonel [Dudley Thornton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Thornton) commanded from 1965–67. In 1966, about 60% of the officers in the battalion were former [non-commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer).[^cite_note-6]

After the [Cabinet Crisis of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Crisis_of_1964), the Malawi Army destroyed [Henry Chipembere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Masauko_Blasius_Chipembere)'s insurrection in [Mangochi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangochi_District) and [Machinga District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinga_District) in 1965.[^cite_note-Phiri2000-5] Another of the ministers ousted during the Cabinet Crisis was [Yatuta Chisiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yatuta_Chisiza). Chisiza fled to Tanzania, and founded the [Socialist League of Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_League_of_Malawi), the most radical Malawian opposition party. He also began to conduct guerilla operations against Banda's government. In 1967 Chisiza and nine others entered [Mwanza District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwanza_District) from Tanzania.[^cite_note-7] In the following clash with the Army and Young Pioneers on 9 October 1967, he and two other members of the insurgent forces were killed, five were captured, and the others fled.[^cite_note-8]

In 1970, the International Institute for Strategic Studies listed the Army as comprising one infantry battalion \[1 MR at Zomba] and supporting services, having a strength of 1,150.[^cite_note-9]

Malawi was allied with Portugal during the [Mozambican War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambican_War_of_Independence) (1964–1974), and the Malawi Army consequently cooperated with the [Portuguese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Army) to secure the Mozambican-Malawian border and arrest [FRELIMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRELIMO) rebels. Following FRELIMO's victory and the independence of Mozambique, several Portuguese colonial secret police agents as well as FRELIMO deserters joined the Malawi Army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinnegan1992141-10]

[Gurkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) officer John "Johnny" Clements was advanced to Acting Brigadier in May 1971, and commanded the Malawi Army until September 1972.[^cite_note-11] The Army's first black commander, Brigadier [Graciano Matewere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciano_Matewere?action=edit\&redlink=1) was appointed during this time. Banda promoted Matewere instantly to Major General after the resolution of the [South African Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways) hijacking in Blantyre in 1972.[^cite_note-12] Matewere was retired by Banda in 1980 and died in 2001.[^cite_note-13]

Declassified [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency) reporting from 1985 states that "there is also a military college \[likely the Kamuzu Military College, at [Salima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salima,_Malawi) that is probably one of the finest, most efficiently organized, and operated military training schools in Sub-Saharan Africa. \[It] conducts recruit training, numerous enlisted courses, officer cadet courses, \[a] platoon leader course, company commanders course (*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*), communications courses, NCO courses, a catering course, and will add a staff officers course in the future."[^cite_note-14] The same source listed the Army Commander as General [Melvin Khanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Khanga?action=edit\&redlink=1), with the deputy commander, Lieutenant General [Issac Yohane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issac_Yohane?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the Director of Training Major General [Wilfred Mponela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Mponela?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### Transition to democracy

In 1992–1993, the army played a vital role in dismantling Banda's dictatorship.[^cite_note-Chirambo04-15]

After the 8 March 1992 pastoral letter:

> There were public demonstrations in support of the bishops—notably at the University in Blantyre and Zomba, where soldiers indicated their support for the students and deterred violent police action against the protesters. This was the first sign of the army's future political role. In May 1992 student protesters were joined by striking workers in Blantyre. In two days of riots dozens of protesters were killed by armed police and [Young Pioneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Young_Pioneers).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarver1994-16][^cite_note-17]

In December 1992:

> ... the army intervened to disarm the MYP forcibly. A bar-room argument in ... [Mzuzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzuzu) ended with Young Pioneers shooting two soldiers dead. The middle-ranking and junior officers effectively mutinied against the army commander, General [Isaac Yohane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Yohane?action=edit\&redlink=1), attacking the Ministry of Youth and other MYP installations in Lilongwe, as well as looting the MCP headquarters. The army then moved into MYP bases throughout the country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarver1994-16]

The operation was called "Bwezani" which means "taking back" or "returning". This event was vital in the history of the Malawi Army.[^cite_note-Chirambo04-15]

### Twenty-first century



Malawian soldier as part of UN peacekeeping in Kivu, D.R. Congo

Malawi was a pioneer among African countries with regards to the integration of women into its modern armed forces. Since 1999,[^cite_note-18] women have served as special forces (5% of the Force Intervention Brigade are female), paratroopers,[^cite_note-19] pilots,[^cite_note-20] and medical staff.[^cite_note-21]

[State Department](31975.md) [International Military Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Education_and_Training) documentation from Fiscal Year 2003 indicates the United States trained army personnel from the 2nd Battalion, Malawi Rifles, probably at Kamuzu Barracks, Lilongwe, 3rd Battalion, Malawi Rifles ([Moyale Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moyale_Barracks), Mzuzu), the Parachute Battalion, and the Combat Support Battalion ([Mvera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mvera?action=edit\&redlink=1)).[^cite_note-22]

In July 2004 General [Joseph Chimbayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Chimbayo?action=edit\&redlink=1) was succeeded by [Marko Chiziko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Chiziko). In 2011 General Chiziko was succeeded by Henry Odillo as MDF Commander.

On 5 April 2012 when President [Bingu wa Mutharika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingu_wa_Mutharika) died, there were rumours of an attempted [constitutional coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawian_Constitutional_Crisis_2012) intended to prevent vice-president [Joyce Banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Banda) from becoming president as outlined by the constitution.[^cite_note-nyasatimes.com-23] The military, under General [Henry Odillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Odillo), stepped in and vowed to support and uphold the constitution of Malawi. They reportedly stationed security members at Banda's residence following the news of Mutharika's death.[^cite_note-24] These acts had a direct impact on the transition of power.[^cite_note-nyasatimes.com-23]

Malawi has signed the initial agreements joining the [SADC Standby Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SADC_Standby_Brigade), the southern African component of the [African Standby Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Standby_Force).



A Malawian [Fox armoured car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_armoured_car) at the MDF Museum in [Zomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomba,_Malawi).

The [Force Intervention Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Intervention_Brigade) of the [United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Organization_Stabilization_Mission_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) was authorized by the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) on 28 March 2013 through [Resolution 2098](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_2098).[^cite_note-Res2098-25] 2,550 troops were drawn almost equally from Malawi, the [Tanzania People's Defence Force](3495135.md), and the [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force).

After [Peter Mutharika](29471682.md) became President in 2014, he replaced the Defence Force commander four times over the course of six years.[^cite_note-26] On August 4, 2014, General Henry Odillo was succeeded by General [Ignaious Maulana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaious_Maulana?action=edit\&redlink=1), the former Chief of Military Operations.[^cite_note-27] In July–August 2016, General Maulana was replaced by his former deputy [Griffin Supuni-Phiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Supuni-Phiri?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-28] Another change occurred in 2019.[^cite_note-29] In March 2020, General Vincent Nundwe, who had won praise for the army's handling of six months of protests over Mutharika's election victory in the [2019 Malawian general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malawian_general_election), was dismissed and replaced by Major General [Andrew Lapken Namathanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lapken_Namathanga?action=edit\&redlink=1), the former Air Force commander. Six months later, the new President, [Lazarus Chakwera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Chakwera), reinstated Nundwe.[^cite_note-30]

On 14 November 2018, during FIB Rotation VI, an officer, sergeant, corporal, and three soldiers of the MDF were killed in action in the Congo.[^cite_note-31]

## Air Force

The Malawi Air Force was established with German help in 1976 with the delivery of six single-engined [Dornier Do 27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_Do_27) and eight [Do 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_Do_28) light twins in 1976-1980. Also in the same era the air force received an [Alouette III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_III), an [AS 350](2691322.md) and an [AS 355 Ecureuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_AS355_%C3%89cureuil_2), as well as three [SA 330 H/L Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_SA_330_Puma) Helicopters from France. A single [BAe 125-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_125) was delivered in 1986. Four [Dornier 228](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_228) light twin turbo props were acquired between 1986 and 1989 in part to allow disposal of the older Dornier products. In 1990 two [Douglas C-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47_Skytrain) with PT6A turboprops were delivered from the US.

## Navy

As a landlocked country, Malawi has a very small Navy with no sizeable military craft. Malawi's naval force only operates on [Lake Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Malawi) and is based at [Monkey Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey_Bay). The Malawi Navy was organized in the early 1970s, with the help of the [Navy of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Navy) that ceded part of its boats of the Nyassa Flotilla operating from the then [Portuguese province of Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Mozambique).[^cite_note-wertheim-37] In some cases, the gunboats of the Malawian Navy were initially crewed by Portuguese.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinnegan1992141-10] In 2007, the navy had 220 personnel,[^cite_note-wertheim-37] and operated the following vessels:

  - 1 [(French) Antares-class (not to mix with Soviet Muravey-class) patrol boat](https://www.navypedia.org/ships/malawi/malaw_cg_chikala.htm) (P 703 *Kasungu*, formerly *Chikala*), out of service since 1993

    [^cite_note-wertheim-37]

- Service craft&#x20;
