**Al Dhafra Region** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مِنْطَقَة ٱلظَّفْرَة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Minṭaqah aẓ-Ẓafrah*), known until 2017 as **Al Gharbia** or the **Western Region** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ٱلْمِنْطَقَة ٱلْغَرْبِيَّة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Al-Minṭaqah al-Ġarbiyyah*),[^cite_note-GulfNews_03-2017-3][^cite_note-SCAD2018-1] is one of three [Municipal Regions in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi](8821238.md#Sub-divisions_and_settlements). Forming the western part of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), it is by far the largest region by area, occupying 71% of the Abu Dhabi emirate's total area, yet the smallest by population and population density, and thus a rather remote region of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi, similar to [the Eastern Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ain_Region).[^cite_note-AskExplorer-4] The capital of Al Dhafra Region is [Madinat Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madinat_Zayed)[^cite_note-OBG2010-5] (Bida Zayed). The new official name was already the historical name of the westernmost region of former [Trucial Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_Oman), which was *Dhafrah*.[^cite_note-6] It is significant for its natural resources, particularly gas and [petroleum](23195.md). Its reserves of [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) account for 90% of the Emirate's reserves, which in turn account for 90% of the country's reserves, and are important for the local economy.[^cite_note-OBG2016-7]

## Demographics

The Region had a population of 202,154 as of the Census of 2010 (including Islands Region with 17,646). With a given population density of 6, the area of the region could be calculated at 33,700 square kilometres (13,000 square miles).[^cite_note-8] Other official sources, however, state an area of 59,760 square kilometres (23,070 square miles), or an 83 percent share of the emirate total of 72,000 square kilometres (28,000 square miles).[^cite_note-9]

This region comprises seven townships, with population figures of the 2005 census of population (109,000 for the region):[^cite_note-10]

1. [Madinat Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madinat_Zayed) (Bida Zayed) (pop. 29,095)

2. [Ruwais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruwais) (pop. 15,511)

3. [Ghayathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghayathi) (pop. 14,022)

4. [Liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_Oasis) (pop. 20,192)

5. [Al Mirfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mirfa?action=edit\&redlink=1) (pop. 14,503)

6. [Sila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sila,_Abu_Dhabi) (pop. 7,900, includes [Ghuwaifat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghuwaifat))

7. [Dalma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalma_\(island\)) (pop. 4,811)

Also of note is [Habshan](8495134.md).[^cite_note-SCAD2018-1]

Al Gharbia's coastal communities are served with six [Western Region Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region_Ports) built, developed and managed by [Abu Dhabi Ports Company (ADPC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_Ports_Company_\(ADPC\)). These include Mugharrag, Al [Sila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sila,_Abu_Dhabi), [Sir Bani Yas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Bani_Yas), Dalma and Marfa Ports. The ports support local industries like fishing, tourism, logistics and leisure activities as well as facilitating the transport of people and goods to offshore islands.[^cite_note-11]

## Wildlife and prehistory

See also: [Wildlife of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates) and [Prehistory of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates)

7 million-year-old fossilized footprints of [elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stegotetrabelodon) have been discovered at a site named "Mleisa 1" in Al-Gharbia.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Within the [area of Baynunah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baynunah_Formation), a [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel)-slaughter site dating to about 6,000 years ago was discovered.[^cite_note-DCTAD2017-14]
