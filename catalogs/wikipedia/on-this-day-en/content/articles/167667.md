The **Eurofighter Typhoon** is a European multinational [twin-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinjet), [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic), [canard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canard_\(aeronautics\)) [delta wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_wing), [multirole fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft).[^cite_note-firstline-3][^cite_note-first2-4] The Typhoon was designed originally as an [air-superiority fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-superiority_fighter)[^cite_note-first3-5] and is manufactured by a [consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium) of [Airbus](26220236.md), [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) and [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.) that conducts the majority of the project through a joint [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company), [Eurofighter Jagdflugzeug GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_GmbH). The [NATO Eurofighter and Tornado Management Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Eurofighter_and_Tornado_Management_Agency), representing the [UK](31717.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Italy](14532.md) and [Spain](26667.md), manages the project and is the prime customer.[^cite_note-6]

The aircraft's development began in 1983 with the Future European Fighter Aircraft programme, a multinational collaboration among the UK, Germany, France, Italy and Spain. Previously, Germany, Italy and the UK had jointly developed and deployed the [Panavia Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado) combat aircraft and desired to collaborate on a new project with additional participating [EU nations](9317.md). However, disagreements over design authority and operational requirements led France to leave the consortium to develop the [Dassault Rafale](33731380.md) independently. A technology demonstration aircraft, the [British Aerospace EAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_EAP), first flew on 6 August 1986; a Eurofighter prototype made its maiden flight on 27 March 1994. The aircraft's name, Typhoon, was adopted in September 1998 and the first production contracts were also signed that year.

The sudden end of the [Cold War](325329.md) reduced European demand for fighter aircraft which led to debate over the aircraft's cost, division of work between the partner nations, and protracted development. The Typhoon entered operational service in 2003 and is now in service with the air forces of [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Air_Force), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force), the [United Kingdom](25679.md), [Spain](3853398.md), [Saudi Arabia](570367.md), [Oman,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force_of_Oman) [Kuwait](4641117.md), and [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Air_Force). [Turkey](11125639.md) has also ordered the aircraft, bringing the procurement total to 769 aircraft as of 30 June 2026.[^cite_note-orders\&deliveries-1]

The Eurofighter Typhoon is a highly agile aircraft, designed to be an effective [dogfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfight) in combat.[^cite_note-JDW-7] Later production aircraft have been increasingly better equipped to undertake air-to-surface strike missions and to be compatible with an increasing number of different armaments and equipment, including [Storm Shadow](306908.md), [Brimstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brimstone_\(missile\)) and [Marte ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Killer_/_Marte) missiles. The Typhoon had its combat debut during the [2011 military intervention in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya) with the UK's Royal Air Force (RAF) and the Italian Air Force, performing [aerial reconnaissance](20338498.md) and [ground strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction) missions. The type has also taken primary responsibility for air defence duties for the majority of customer nations.

## Development

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of the Eurofighter Typhoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Eurofighter_Typhoon).

### Origins

Further information: [British Aerospace EAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_EAP)

In the UK, as early as [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_in_the_United_Kingdom), work commenced on the development of a maneuverable, tactical aircraft to replace the [SEPECAT Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPECAT_Jaguar) (that was then about to enter service with the [RAF](25679.md)). This work soon expanded to include an [air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority) capability. A specification titled *Air Staff Target 403* (AST 403), in 1972, led to the [Hawker P.96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_P.96?action=edit\&redlink=1), an unbuilt design with a relatively conventional [planform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planform_\(aeronautics\)), including a separate tail structure, in the late 1970s.

Simultaneously, in [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), the requirement for a new fighter had resulted in competition between [Dornier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_Flugzeugwerke), [VFW-Fokker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VFW-Fokker) and [Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt-B%C3%B6lkow-Blohm) (MBB) for a future [Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force) contract known as *Taktisches Kampfflugzeug 90* ("Tactical Combat Aircraft 90"; TKF-90).[^cite_note-8] Dornier collaborated with [Northrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Corporation) in the US on an acclaimed but unsuccessful design known as the [Northrop-Dornier ND-102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop-Dornier_ND-102?action=edit\&redlink=1). MBB was successful, with a design including a [cranked delta wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_wing#Design_variations), close-coupled-[canard controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canard_\(aeronautics\)), and artificial stability.

In 1979, MBB and [British Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace) (BAe) presented a formal proposal to their respective governments for a collaboration, to be known as the European Collaborative Fighter,[^cite_note-butl2-9] or *European Combat Fighter* (ECF). In October 1979, French firm [Dassault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Aviation) joined the ECF project.[^cite_note-butl2-9] It was at this stage of development the Eurofighter name was first attached to the aircraft.[^cite_note-butler3-10] However, the development of three separate prototypes continued: MBB continued to refine its TKF-90 concept, and Dassault produced a design known as the ACX.



BAe P.106B

In the meantime, while the P.96 would have met the original UK specification, it had been cancelled because it was considered to offer little potential for future upgrades and redevelopment. In addition, there was a feeling within the UK aircraft industry that the P.96 would have been too similar to the [McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet](45998.md), which was then known to be at an advanced stage of development. The P.96 would not have been available until long after the Hornet, which would therefore likely have met and closed off most potential export markets for the P.96.[^cite_note-Boot-11] BAe then produced two new proposals: the P.106B,[^cite_note-13] a single-engined [lightweight fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_fighter), superficially resembling the future [Saab](26968.md) [JAS 39 Gripen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAS_39_Gripen) and the twin-engine P.110. The RAF rejected the P.106 concept on the grounds it had "half the effectiveness of the two-engined aircraft at two-thirds of the cost."[^cite_note-Boot-11]

The ECF project collapsed in 1981 for several reasons, including differing requirements, Dassault's insistence on "design leadership", and the British preference for a new version of the [RB199](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo-Union_RB199) to power the aircraft versus the French preference for the new [Snecma M88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snecma_M88).[^cite_note-butler3-10]



[British Aerospace EAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_EAP) *ZF534* (for "Experimental Aircraft Programme") at the [Farnborough Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Air_Show), 1986

Consequently, the [Panavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia) partners (MBB, BAe and [Aeritalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeritalia)) launched the *Agile Combat Aircraft* (ACA) programme in April 1982.[^cite_note-butler4-14] BAe designers agreed with the overall configuration of the proposed MBB TKF-90, although they rejected some of its more ambitious features such as engine [vectoring nozzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vectoring_nozzle) and vented [trailing edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailing_edge) controls—a form of [boundary layer control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_layer_control).[^cite_note-Boot-11] The ACA, like the BAe P.110, had a cranked delta wing, canards, and a twin tail. One major external difference was the replacement of the side-mounted engine intakes with a chin intake. The ACA was to be powered by a modified version of the RB199. The [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Kohl_cabinet) and Italian governments withdrew funding, and the UK [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) (MoD) agreed to fund 50% of the cost with the remaining 50% to be provided by industry. MBB and Aeritalia signed up and it was agreed that the aircraft would be produced at two sites: [BAe Warton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAe_Warton) and an MBB factory in Germany. In May 1983, BAe announced a contract with the MoD for the development and production of an ACA demonstrator, the [Experimental Aircraft Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_EAP).[^cite_note-butler4-14][^cite_note-KEY1-15]

In 1983, Italy, Germany, France, the UK and Spain launched the "Future European Fighter Aircraft" (FEFA) programme. The aircraft was to have [short take off and landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOL) (STOL) and beyond visual range (BVR) capabilities. In 1984, France reiterated its requirement for a [carrier-capable](2219.md) version and demanded a leading role. Italy, West Germany, and the UK opted out and established a new EFA programme. In [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) on 2 August 1985, West Germany, the UK, and Italy agreed to go ahead with the Eurofighter and confirmed France and Spain had chosen not to proceed as a member of the project.[^cite_note-LEWIS-16] Despite pressure from France, Spain rejoined the Eurofighter project in early September 1985.[^cite_note-SPAININ-17] France officially withdrew from the project to pursue its own ACX project, which was to become the [Dassault Rafale](33731380.md).

By 1986, the programme's cost had reached [£](270673.md)180 million.[^cite_note-FI-18] When the EAP programme had started, the cost was supposed to be equally shared by government and industry, but the West German and Italian governments wavered on the agreement, and the British government and private finance had to provide £100 million to keep the programme from ending. In April 1986, the [British Aerospace EAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_EAP) was rolled out at BAe Warton. The EAP first flew on 6 August 1986.[^cite_note-FAIRH-19] The Eurofighter bears a strong resemblance to the EAP. Design work continued over the next five years using data from the EAP. Initial requirements were: UK: 250 aircraft, Germany: 250, Italy: 165 and Spain: 100.[^cite_note-20] The share of the production work was divided among the countries in proportion to their projected procurement – BAe (33%), [DASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DaimlerChrysler_Aerospace) (33%), [Aeritalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeritalia) (21%), and [Construcciones Aeronáuticas SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construcciones_Aeron%C3%A1uticas_SA) (CASA) (13%).

The Munich-based Eurofighter Jagdflugzeug GmbH was established in 1986 to manage development of the project[^cite_note-COWTON-21] and [EuroJet Turbo GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroJet_Turbo_GmbH), the alliance of [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_plc), [MTU Aero Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Aero_Engines), FiatAvio (now [Avio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avio)) and [ITP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industria_de_Turbo_Propulsores) for development of the [EJ200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurojet_EJ200). The aircraft was known as Eurofighter EFA from the late 1980s until it was renamed EF 2000 in 1992.[^cite_note-22]

By 1990, the selection of the aircraft's radar had become a major obstacle. The UK, Italy and Spain supported the [Ferranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferranti) Defence Systems-led [ECR-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECR-90), while Germany preferred the [APG-65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APG-65)-based MSD2000 (a collaboration between [Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Aircraft), [AEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEG_\(German_company\)) and [GEC-Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEC-Marconi)). An agreement was reached after UK Defence Secretary [Tom King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_King,_Baron_King_of_Bridgwater) assured his West German counterpart [Gerhard Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Stoltenberg) that the British government would approve the project and allow the GEC subsidiary [Marconi Electronic Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marconi_Electronic_Systems) to acquire Ferranti Defence Systems from its parent, the Ferranti Group, which was in financial and legal difficulties. GEC thus withdrew its support for the MSD2000.[^cite_note-RADAR1-23]

### Delays

The financial burdens placed on Germany by [reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) caused [Helmut Kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Kohl) to make an [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_German_federal_election) promise to cancel the Eurofighter. In early to mid 1991, German Defence Minister [Volker Rühe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_R%C3%BChe) sought to withdraw Germany from the project in favour of using Eurofighter technology in a cheaper, lighter plane. Because of the amount of money already spent on development, the number of jobs dependent on the project, and the binding commitments on each partner government, Kohl was unable to withdraw; "Rühe's predecessors had locked themselves into the project by a punitive penalty system of their own devising."[^cite_note-BBCwomc-24]



RAF Typhoon FGR4 *ZK356* shows its delta wing, July 2016.

In 1995, concerns over workshare appeared. Since the formation of Eurofighter, the workshare split had been agreed at 33/33/21/13 (United Kingdom/Germany/Italy/Spain) based on the number of units being ordered by each contributing nation. All the nations then reduced their orders; the UK cut its orders from 250 to 232, Germany from 250 to 140, Italy from 165 to 121, and Spain from 100 to 87.[^cite_note-BBCwomc-24] According to these order levels, the workshare split should have been 39/24/22/15 UK/Germany/Italy/Spain; however, Germany was unwilling to give up such a large amount of work.[^cite_note-BBCwomc-24] In January 1996, after much negotiation between German and UK partners, a compromise was reached whereby Germany would purchase another 40 aircraft.[^cite_note-BBCwomc-24] The workshare split was therefore UK 37.42%, Germany 29.03%, Italy 19.52% and Spain 14.03%.

At the 1996 [Farnborough Airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airshow) the UK announced funding for the construction phase of the project.[^cite_note-Farn96-25] On 22 December 1997 the defence ministers of the four partner nations signed the contract for production of the Eurofighter.[^cite_note-CON97-26]

### Testing



Close-up view of [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF) Typhoon F2 *ZJ910*, showing the deflected [canard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canard_\(aeronautics\)) control surface immediately below the pilot

The [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) of the Eurofighter prototype took place in [Bavaria](3764.md) on 27 March 1994, flown by DASA chief test pilot Peter Weger.[^cite_note-maiden-2] In December 2004, Eurofighter Typhoon IPA4 began three months of Cold Environmental Trials (CET) at the [Vidsel Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidsel_Air_Base) in Sweden, the purpose of which was to verify the operational behaviour of the aircraft and its systems in temperatures between −25 and 31 °C.[^cite_note-hastings-27] The maiden flight of Instrumented Production Aircraft 7 (IPA7), the first fully equipped Tranche 2 aircraft, took place from EADS' [Manching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manching) airfield on 16 January 2008.[^cite_note-T2MAIDEN-28]

### Procurement, production and costs

The first production contract was signed on 30 January 1998 between Eurofighter GmbH, Eurojet and NETMA.[^cite_note-BBC-29] The procurement totals were as follows: the UK 232, Germany 180, Italy 121, and Spain 87. Production was again allotted according to procurement: BAe (37.42%), DASA (29.03%), Aeritalia (19.52%), and CASA (14.03%).

On 2 September 1998, a naming ceremony was held at [Farnborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airfield), United Kingdom. This saw the Typhoon name formally adopted, initially for export aircraft only. The name continues the storm theme started by the [Panavia Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado). Germany reportedly opposed this name; the [Hawker Typhoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Typhoon) was a fighter-bomber aircraft used by the RAF during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) to attack German targets.[^cite_note-NAMING-30] The name "Spitfire II" (after the famous British Second World War fighter, the [Supermarine Spitfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire)) had also been considered and rejected for the same reason early in the development programme.[^cite_note-SPITFIRE-31] In September 1998, contracts were signed for production of 148 Tranche 1 aircraft and procurement of long lead-time items for Tranche 2 aircraft.[^cite_note-CON98-32] In March 2008, the final Tranche 1 aircraft was delivered to the German Air Force.[^cite_note-holm-33] On 21 October 2008, the RAF's first two of 91 Tranche 2 aircraft, were delivered to [RAF Coningsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Coningsby).[^cite_note-RAFt1-34]

In July 2009, after almost 2 years of negotiations, the planned Tranche 3 purchase was split into 2 parts, and the Tranche 3A contract was signed by the partner nations.[^cite_note-CON09-35] The "Tranche 3B" order did not go ahead.[^cite_note-NOT3-36]

The Eurofighter Typhoon is unique in modern combat aircraft in that there are four separate assembly lines. Each partner company assembles its own national aircraft, but builds the same parts for all aircraft (including exports); [Premium AEROTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_AEROTEC) (main centre fuselage),[^cite_note-BUILD-37] [EADS CASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA) (right wing, leading edge slats), [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) (BAE) (front fuselage (including foreplanes), canopy, dorsal spine, tail fin, inboard flaperons, rear fuselage section) and [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.) (left wing, outboard [flaperons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaperon), rear fuselage sections).

Production is divided into three tranches (see table below). Tranches are a production/funding distinction and do not imply an incremental increase in capability with each tranche. Tranche 3 are based on late Tranche 2 aircraft with improvements added. Tranche 3 was split into A and B parts.[^cite_note-FI3A-38] Tranches were further divided up into production standard/capability blocks and funding/procurement batches, though these did not coincide; for example, the Eurofighter designated *[FGR4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_Typhoon_variants#Typhoon_FGR4)* by the RAF is a Tranche 1, block 5. Batch 1 covered block 1, but batch 2 covered blocks 2, 2B and 5. On 25 May 2011, the 100th production aircraft, ZK315, rolled off the production line at Warton.[^cite_note-ZK315-39]

In 1985, the estimated cost of 250 UK aircraft was £7 billion. By 1997 the estimated cost was £17 billion; by 2003, £20 billion, and the in-service date (2003, defined as the date of delivery of the first aircraft to the RAF) was 54 months late.[^cite_note-LATE54-40] After 2003, the MoD refused to release updated cost estimates on the grounds of commercial sensitivity.[^cite_note-COMM-41] However, in 2011, the [National Audit Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Audit_Office_\(United_Kingdom\)) estimated the UK's "assessment, development, production and upgrade costs eventually hit £22.9 billion" and total programme costs would reach £37 billion.[^cite_note-NAO-42]

By 2007, the [First Merkel cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Merkel_cabinet) (Germany) estimated the system cost (aircraft and training, plus spare parts), per year at €120 million, whereas the unit cost per Eurofighter was €57 million, and said it was in perpetual increase.[^cite_note-DEUCOST-43][^cite_note-44] On 17 June 2009, Germany ordered 31 aircraft of Tranche 3A for €2.8 billion, leading to a system cost of €90 million per aircraft.[^cite_note-kunden-45] The UK's [Committee of Public Accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Accounts_Committee_\(United_Kingdom\)) reported that mismanagement of the project had helped increase the cost of each aircraft by seventy-five per cent.[^cite_note-PACCOST-46] The [Spanish MoD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Spain\)) put the cost of their Typhoon project up to December 2010 at €11.718 billion, up from an original €9.255 billion and implying a system cost for their 73 aircraft of €160 million.[^cite_note-PEAs-47]

On 31 March 2009, a Eurofighter Typhoon fired an [AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) whilst having its radar in passive mode for the first time; the necessary target data for the missile was acquired by the radar of a second Eurofighter Typhoon and transmitted using the [Multifunctional Information Distribution System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunctional_Information_Distribution_System) (MIDS).[^cite_note-MIDSF-48] The entire Typhoon fleet passed the 500,000 flying hours milestone in 2018.[^cite_note-HOURS-49] As of August 2019, a total of 623 orders had been received.[^cite_note-airbus-50]

In July 2016, the ten-year Typhoon Total Availability Enterprise (TyTAN) support deal between the RAF and industry partners BAE and Leonardo was announced that aimed to reduce the Typhoon's per-hour operating cost by 30 to 40 per cent.[^cite_note-TYTAN-51] This would equate to a saving of at least £550 million (\$712 million), which "will be recycled into the programme" and, according to BAE, would result in the Typhoon having a per-hour operating cost "equivalent to a [F-16](11642.md)".[^cite_note-OPCOST-52] By 2022, it was estimated that savings would be "over £500 million".[^cite_note-53]

### Upgrades

In 2000, the UK selected the [Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_\(missile\)) from [MBDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA) as the long range air-to-air missile armament for its Typhoons with an in-service date of December 2011.[^cite_note-Meteorcontract-54] In December 2002, France, Germany, Spain and Sweden joined the British in a \$1.9bn contract for Meteor on Typhoon, the Dassault Rafale, and the Saab Gripen.[^cite_note-Meteorcontract-54] The protracted contract negotiations pushed the ISD to August 2012,[^cite_note-Meteorcontract-54] and it was further put back by Eurofighter's failure to make trials aircraft available to the Meteor partners.[^cite_note-METTRIAL-55] In 2014 the "second element of the Phase 1 Enhancements package known as 'P1Eb'" was announced, allowing "Typhoon to realise both its air-to-air and air-to-ground capability to full effect".[^cite_note-P1Eb-56]

In 2011 *Flight International* reported that budgetary pressures being encountered by the four original partner nations were limiting upgrades.[^cite_note-FILIMIT-57] For example, the four original partner nations were reluctant at that stage to fund enhancements that extend the aircraft's air-to-ground capability, such as integration of the [MBDA Storm Shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA_Storm_Shadow) cruise missile.[^cite_note-MEU-58]

Tranche 3 aircraft [electronic countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_countermeasures) (ECM) enhancements have focused on improving radiating jamming power with antenna modifications, while EuroDASS is reported to offer a range of new capabilities, including the addition of a digital receiver, extending band coverage to low frequencies ([VHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_high_frequency)/[UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high_frequency)) and introducing an interferometric receiver with extremely precise geolocation functionalities. On the jamming side, EuroDASS is looking to low-band[^cite_note-DASS0-59] (VHF/UHF) jamming, more capable antennae, new ECM techniques, while protection against missiles is to be enhanced through a new passive missile warning system in addition to the active devices already on board the aircraft. The latest support for self-protection will, however, originate from the new [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar, which is to replace the Captor system, providing in a spiralled programme with passive, active and cyberwarfare RF capabilities. [Selex ES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selex_ES) has developed a self-contained expendable [digital radio frequency memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_radio_frequency_memory) (DRFM) [jammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_jamming_and_deception) for fast jet aircraft known as [BriteCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BriteCloud) which is being studied for integration on the Typhoon.[^cite_note-centurion-60]



EJ200 TVC prototype

Eurojet is attempting to find funding to test [thrust vectoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_vectoring) control (TVC) nozzles on a flight demonstrator.[^cite_note-FITV-61] In April 2014, BAE announced new wind tunnel tests to assess the aerodynamic characteristics of [conformal fuel tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_fuel_tank) (CFTs). The CFTs, which can be fitted to any Tranche 3 aircraft, could carry 1,500 litres each to increase the Typhoon's [combat radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_radius) by a factor of 25% to 1,500 n miles (2,778 km).[^cite_note-CFT-62]

BAE has completed development of its Striker II Helmet-Mounted Display that builds on the capabilities of the original Striker Helmet-Mounted Display, which is already in service on the Typhoon.[^cite_note-STRIKER-63] Striker II features a new display with more colour and can transition between day and night seamlessly, eliminating the need for separate night vision goggles. In addition, the helmet can monitor the pilot's exact head position so it always knows exactly what information to display.[^cite_note-STRIKER2-64] The system is compatible with [ANR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), a [3D audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_audio) threats system and 3D communications; these are available as customer options.[^cite_note-STRIKER3-65] In 2015, BAE was awarded a £1.7 million contract to study the feasibility of a common weapon launcher that could be capable of carrying multiple weapons and weapon types on a single pylon.[^cite_note-CL-66]



AMK Leading Edge Root Extension

In 2015, Airbus flight tested a package of aerodynamic upgrades for the Eurofighter known as the Aerodynamic Modification Kit (AMK) consisting of reshaped (delta) [fuselage strakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strake_\(aeronautics\)), extended trailing-edge [flaperons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaperon) and [leading-edge root extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading%E2%80%93edge_extension#Leading%E2%80%93edge_root_extension). This increases wing lift by 25% resulting in an increased turn rate, tighter turning radius, and improved nose-pointing ability at low speed with angle of attack values around 45% greater and roll rates up to 100% higher.[^cite_note-AMK-67][^cite_note-AMK4-68][^cite_note-AMK5-69] Eurofighter's Laurie Hilditch said these improvements should increase subsonic turn rate by 15% and give the Eurofighter the sort of "knife fight in a phone box" turning capability enjoyed by rivals such as Boeing's F/A-18E/F or the Lockheed Martin F-16, without sacrificing the transonic and supersonic high-energy agility inherent to its delta wing-canard configuration.[^cite_note-AMK2-70] Eurofighter Project Pilot Germany Raffaele Beltrame said: "The handling qualities appeared to be markedly improved, providing more manoeuvrability, agility and precision while performing tasks representative of in-service operations. And it is extremely interesting to consider the potential benefits in the air-to-surface configuration thanks to the increased variety and flexibility of stores that can be carried."[^cite_note-AMK3-71] On 5 February 2026 it was announced that Eurofighter and NETMA have signed a contract for the development, testing and certification of the AMK. Airbus, Leonardo and BAE Systems will carry out development and production.[^cite_note-AMK6-72]

In April 2016, Finmeccanica (now Leonardo) demonstrated the air-to-ground capabilities of its Mode 5 Reverse-[Identification friend or foe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_friend_or_foe) (IFF) system which showed that it is possible to give pilots the ability to distinguish between friendly and enemy platforms in a simple fashion using the aircraft's existing transponder.[^cite_note-RIFF-73] Finmeccanica said [NATO](21133.md) is considering the system as a short- to mid-term solution for air-to-surface identification of friendly forces and thus avoid collateral damages due to friendly fire during close air support operations.[^cite_note-RIFF-73]

## Design

### Airframe overview

Typhoon flight demonstration

The Typhoon is a dual engine, loosely coupled delta canard tailless design featuring a 53-degree leading edge sweepback, relaxed stability, and a digital [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire) control system. It is a highly agile aircraft at all speeds, subsonic and supersonic, achieved by having intentionally [relaxed stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaxed_stability), combined canard and flaperon control surfaces, and a very low wing loading.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] The quadruplex digital fly-by-wire control system manages the inherent instability, allowing better maneuverability than direct pilot control. It is described as "carefree" and prevents the permitted manoeuvre envelope being exceeded. Roll control is primarily achieved by differential use of the flaperons. Pitch control is by coupled operation of the canards and flaperons. The wing leading edges are fitted with automatic movable slats.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] A single large rudder provides yaw control.[^cite_note-AERPERF-89] Engines are fed by a chin double [intake ramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intake_ramp) situated below a [splitter plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splitter_plate_\(aeronautics\)). A hydraulically operated air-brake is integrated behind the cockpit, moving into a near-vertical position to maximise drag when required. The Typhoon uses lightweight construction (82% composites consisting of 70% [carbon fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fibre) [composite materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material) and 12% [glass fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_fibre) reinforced composites) as well as aluminium lithium and titanium components on leading edge surfaces. The airframe has an estimated lifespan of 6,000 flying hours.[^cite_note-MATER-90][^cite_note-HRS-91][^cite_note-92]

#### Radar signature reduction features



[S-duct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-duct)-like air intake partially conceals engine fans, a major source of radar wave reflection

Although it is not considered a [stealth fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_aircraft), measures were taken to reduce the Typhoon's [radar cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross_section) (RCS), especially from the frontal aspect. For example, the Typhoon has jet inlets that conceal the front of the engines, a strong radar target, from radar. Many important potential radar targets, such as the wing, canard, and fin leading edges, are highly swept so they will reflect radar energy well away from the front.[^cite_note-93] Some external weapons are mounted semi-recessed into the aircraft, partially shielding them from incoming radar.[^cite_note-structure-94] In addition, [radar-absorbent materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar-absorbent_material) (RAM), developed primarily by [EADS](26220236.md)/DASA, coat many of the most significant reflectors, such as the wing leading edges, the intake edges and interior, the rudder surrounds, and strakes.[^cite_note-structure-94]

The manufacturers carried out tests on the early Eurofighter prototypes to optimise the low observability characteristics of the aircraft from the early 1990s. Testing at Warton on the DA4 prototype measured the RCS of the aircraft and investigated the effects of a variety of RAM coatings and composites.[^cite_note-iarpdf-95] Passive sensors (PIRATE IRST), which minimise the radiation of revealing electronic emissions, also reduce the likelihood of discovery. While canards generally have poor stealth characteristics from side because of corner to hull,[^cite_note-STECAN-96] the flight control system is designed to maintain the elevon trim and canards at an angle at which they have the smallest RCS.[^cite_note-STECAN2-97]

### Cockpit



MHDDs and pedestal panel with centre stick in the Typhoon cockpit

The Typhoon features a [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit) without any conventional instruments. It incorporates three full colour multi-function head-down displays (MHDDs). The display formats on these MHDDs are manipulated by means of dedicated controls, softkeys, XY cursor, and voice ([Direct Voice Input](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Voice_Input) or DVI) command. There is a wide-angle [head-up display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-up_display) (HUD) with forward-looking infrared ([FLIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FLIR)), a voice and hands-on throttle and stick (Voice+[HOTAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOTAS)), a Helmet Mounted Symbology System ([HMSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmet_mounted_display#Eurofighter_Helmet-Mounted_Symbology_System)), a manual data-entry facility (MDEF) located on the left glareshield and a fully integrated aircraft warning system with a dedicated warnings panel (DWP). There is also an interactive display panel for the [MIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunctional_Information_Distribution_System). Reversionary (backup) flying instruments, lit by [LEDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED), are located under a hinged right glareshield.[^cite_note-INST-98] Access to the cockpit is normally via either a telescopic integral ladder or an external version. The integral ladder is stowed in the port side of the fuselage, below the cockpit.[^cite_note-LAD-99]



Eurofighter Typhoon cockpit.

User needs were given a high priority in the cockpit's design; both layout and functionality was developed with feedback and assessments from military pilots and a specialist testing facility.[^cite_note-COCKP-100] The aircraft is controlled by means of a [centre stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_stick) (or control stick) and left hand [throttles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throttle), designed on a Hand on Throttle and Stick (HOTAS) principle to lower pilot [workload](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load).[^cite_note-CONTROL-101] Emergency escape is provided by a [Martin-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin-Baker) Mk.16A [ejection seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejection_seat), with the canopy being jettisoned by two rocket motors.[^cite_note-EJSEAT-102] The HMSS was delayed by years but should have been operational by late 2011.[^cite_note-HMSS-103] Standard *g*-force protection is provided by the *full-cover anti-*&#x67;*&#x20;trousers* (FCAGTs),[^cite_note-LIFES-104] a specially developed [*g* suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_suit) providing sustained protection up to nine *g*. German and Austrian Air Force pilots wear a hydrostatic *g*-suit called *Libelle* (*dragonfly*) Multi G Plus instead,[^cite_note-suit-105] which also provides protection to the arms, theoretically giving more complete *g* tolerance.



Helmet Mounted Symbology System (HMSS)

In the event of pilot [disorientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_disorientation), the [Flight Control System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_Control_System) allows for rapid and automatic recovery by the simple press of a button. On selection of this cockpit control, the FCS takes full control of the engines and flying controls and automatically stabilises the aircraft in a wings level, gentle climbing attitude at 300 knots until the pilot is ready to retake control.[^cite_note-AUTOR-106] The aircraft also has an Automatic Low-Speed Recovery system (ALSR) which prevents it from departing from controlled flight at very low speeds and high [angle of attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_attack). The FCS system is able to detect a developing low-speed situation and to raise an audible and visual low-speed cockpit warning. This gives the pilot sufficient time to react and to recover the aircraft manually. If the pilot does not react, however, or if the warning is ignored, the ALSR takes control of the aircraft, selects maximum dry power for the engines and returns the aircraft to a safe flight condition. Depending on the attitude, the FCS employs an ALSR "push", "pull" or "knife-over" manoeuvre.[^cite_note-FCS-107]



Eurofighter DASS Display, showing targets detected by the [Missile Approach Warners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_approach_warning_system) (MAW). Down left: System online (hourglass-shaped symbol) and dispenser numbers (C = chaff, F = Flares). Down right decoy status. Right side the elevation bar from ±60°, with marks at +5°, 0° and −5°. The coloured arrows may indicate rate of climb (green) or sink (red) by their thickness.

The Typhoon Direct Voice Input (DVI) system uses a speech recognition module (SRM), developed by [Smiths Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiths_Industries) and Computing Devices. It was the first production DVI system used in a military cockpit. DVI provides the pilot with an additional natural mode of command and control over approximately 26 non-critical cockpit functions to reduce pilot workload, improve aircraft safety, and expand mission capabilities. An important step in the development of the DVI occurred in 1987 when [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments) completed the TMS-320-C30, a [digital signal processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processor), enabling reductions in the size and system complexity required. The project was given the go-ahead in July 1997 with development carried out on the Eurofighter Active Cockpit Simulator at Warton.[^cite_note-DVI1-108] The DVI system is speaker-dependent, requiring each pilot to create a template. It is not used for safety-critical or weapon-critical tasks, such as weapon release or lowering of the undercarriage.[^cite_note-DVI2-109] Voice commands are confirmed by visual or aural feedback, and serves to reduce pilot workload. All functions are also achievable by means of a conventional button-press or soft-key selections; functions include display management, communications, and management of various systems.[^cite_note-EF_DVI-110] [EADS Defence and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassidian) in Spain has worked on a new non-template DVI module to allow for continuous speech recognition, speaker voice recognition with common databases (e.g. [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English), [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English), etc.) and other improvements.[^cite_note-EF_DVI-110]

BAE Systems has been awarded a contract to develop new touch screen displays in the cockpit and enhance data processing capability for Eurofighter Typhoon.

### Avionics

Navigation is via both [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS) and an [inertial navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system). The Typhoon can use [Instrument Landing System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_Landing_System) (ILS) for landing in poor weather. The aircraft also features an [enhanced ground proximity warning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_proximity_warning_system) (GPWS) based on the [TERPROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TERPROM) Terrain Referenced Navigation (TRN) system used by the Panavia Tornado.[^cite_note-gpws-111] MIDS provides a [Link 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_16) data link.[^cite_note-MIDS-112]



Praetorian DASS: \
&#x20;1\. Laser warners \
&#x20;2\. Flare launchers (IR decoys) \
&#x20;3\. Chaff dispensers \
&#x20;4\. Missile warners \
&#x20;5\. Wingtip pods for ESCM \
&#x20;6\. Towed decoy

The aircraft employs a sophisticated and highly integrated [Defensive Aids Sub-System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_aids_system) named [Praetorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorian_DASS) (formerly Euro-DASS).[^cite_note-DASS-113] Praetorian monitors and responds automatically to air and surface threats, provides an all-round prioritised assessment, and can respond to multiple threats simultaneously. Threat detection methods include a [Radar warning receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_warning_receiver) (RWR), a [missile warning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_approach_warning_system) (MWS) and a [laser warning receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_warning_receiver) (LWR, only on UK Typhoons). Protective countermeasures consist of [chaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaff_\(countermeasure\)), [flares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flare_\(countermeasure\)), an [electronic countermeasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_countermeasures) (ECM) suite, and a towed radar decoy (TRD).[^cite_note-DASS2-114] The ESM-ECM and MWS consists of 16 antenna array assemblies and 10 radomes.[^cite_note-EWITEMS-115]

Historically, each sensor in an aircraft is treated as a discrete source of information; however, this can result in conflicting data and limits the scope for the automation of systems, increasing pilot workload. To overcome this, the Typhoon employs [sensor fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_fusion) techniques. In the Typhoon, fusion of all data sources is achieved through the Attack and Identification System, or AIS. This combines data from the major on-board sensors along with any information obtained from off-board platforms such as [AWACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) and MIDS. Additionally the AIS integrates all the other major offensive and defensive systems (e.g. DASS & communications). The AIS physically comprises two essentially separate units: the Attack Computer (AC) and the Navigation Computer (NC).[^cite_note-AVI-116]

By having a single source of information, pilot workload should be reduced by removing the possibility of conflicting data and the need for cross-checking, improving [situational awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_awareness) and increasing systems automation. In practice the AIS should allow the Eurofighter to identify targets at distances in excess of 150 nmi (280 km; 170 mi) and acquire and auto-prioritise them at over 100 nmi (190 km; 120 mi). In addition, the AIS offers the ability to automatically control emissions from the aircraft, so called EMCON (from EMissions CONtrol). This should aid in limiting the detectability of the Typhoon by opposing aircraft further reducing pilot workload.[^cite_note-Paul_S._Owen-117]

In 2017, an RAF Eurofighter Typhoon demonstrated interoperability with the [F-35B](11812.md) using its [Multifunction Advanced Data Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunction_Advanced_Data_Link) (MADL) in a two-week trial known as Babel Fish III in the [Mojave Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert). This was achieved by translating the MADL messages into Link 16 format, allowing an F-35 in stealth mode to communicate directly with the Typhoon.[^cite_note-MADL-118]

#### IRST

Main article: [EuroFIRST PIRATE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroFIRST_PIRATE)

The Passive Infra-Red Airborne Track Equipment (PIRATE) system is an [infrared search and track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_search_and_track) (IRST) system mounted on the port side of the fuselage, forward of the windscreen. Selex ES is the lead contractor which, along with [Thales Optronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Optronics) (system technical authority) and Tecnobit of Spain, make up the EUROFIRST consortium responsible for the system's design and development. Eurofighters starting with Tranche 1 block 5 have the PIRATE. The first Eurofighter Typhoon with PIRATE-IRST was delivered to the Italian [Aeronautica Militare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautica_Militare) in August 2007.[^cite_note-PIRATE-143] More advanced targeting capabilities can be provided with the addition of a targeting pod such as the [Litening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litening) pod.[^cite_note-AUSTERE2-144]



PIRATE IRST

When used with the radar in an air-to-air role, it functions as an infrared search and track system, providing passive target detection and tracking. The system can detect variations in temperature at a long range.[^cite_note-PIRATE2-145] It also provides a navigation and landing aid. PIRATE is linked to the pilot's [helmet-mounted display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmet-mounted_display).[^cite_note-Airforce-TechEF2000-146] It allows the detection of both hot exhaust plumes of jet engines and surface heating caused by friction; processing techniques further enhance the output, giving a near-high resolution image of targets. The output can be directed to any of the Multi-function Head Down Displays, and can also be overlaid on both the Helmet Mounted Sight and the Head Up Display.

Up to 200 targets can be simultaneously tracked using one of several different modes; Multiple Target Track (MTT), Single Target Track (STT), Single Target Track Ident (STTI), Sector Acquisition and Slaved Acquisition. In MTT mode the system will scan a designated volume space looking for potential targets. In STT mode PIRATE will provide tracking of a single designated target. An addition to this mode, STT Ident allows for visual identification of the target, the resolution being superior to CAPTOR's. When in Sector Acquisition mode PIRATE will scan a volume of space under direction of another onboard sensor such as CAPTOR. In Slave Acquisition, off-board sensors are used with PIRATE being commanded by data obtained from an AWACS or other source. When a target is found in either of these modes, PIRATE will automatically designate it and switch to STT.

Once a target has been tracked and identified, PIRATE can be used to cue an appropriately equipped short range missile, i.e. a missile with a high off-boresight tracking capability such as ASRAAM. Additionally the data can be used to augment that of Captor or off-board sensor information via the AIS. This should enable the Typhoon to overcome severe ECM environments and still engage its targets.[^cite_note-Paul_S._Owen-117] PIRATE also has a passive ranging capability[^cite_note-PIRATE3-147] although the system remains limited when providing passive firing solutions, as it does not have a [laser rangefinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_rangefinder).

### Engines

Main article: [Eurojet EJ200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurojet_EJ200)

The Eurofighter Typhoon is fitted with two Eurojet EJ200 engines, each capable of providing up to 60 kN (13,500 lbf) of dry thrust and >90 kN (20,230 lbf) with afterburners. Using the "war" setting, dry thrust increases by 15% to 69 kN (15,511 lbf) per engine and afterburners by 5% to 95 kN (21,356 lbf) per engine and for a few seconds, up to 102 kN (22,930 lbf) thrust without damaging the engine.[^cite_note-ENGINESPECS-148] The EJ200 engine combines the leading technologies from each of the four European companies, using advanced [digital control and health monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FADEC); [wide chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_chord) aerofoils and [single crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_crystal) turbine blades; and a [convergent / divergent exhaust nozzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Laval_nozzle) to give high thrust-to-weight ratio, multimission capability, supercruise performance, low fuel consumption, low cost of ownership, modular construction and growth potential.[^cite_note-ENGINESPECSMTU-149]



EJ200 engine on display at [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show) 2013

The Typhoon is capable of supersonic cruise without using [afterburners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterburner) (referred to as [supercruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercruise)). *Air Forces Monthly* gives a maximum supercruise speed of Mach 1.1 for the RAF FGR4 multirole version,[^cite_note-Swing-150] however in a Singaporean evaluation, a Typhoon managed to supercruise at Mach 1.21 on a hot day with a combat load.[^cite_note-SUPERC-151] Eurofighter states that the Typhoon can supercruise at Mach 1.5.[^cite_note-web.archive.org-152] As with the F-22, the Eurofighter can launch weapons while under supercruise to extend their ranges via this "running start".[^cite_note-RUNNING-153] In 2007, the EJ200 engine had accumulated 50,000 Engine Flying Hours in service with the four Nation Air Forces (Germany, UK, Spain and Italy).[^cite_note-EJHOURS-154]



The aircraft's turbofan engine (front)

The EJ200 engine has the potential to be fitted with a thrust vectoring control (TVC) nozzle, which the Eurofighter and Eurojet consortium have been actively developing and testing, primarily for export but also for future upgrades of the fleet. TVC could reduce fuel burn on a typical Typhoon mission by up to 5%, as well as increase available thrust in supercruise by up to 7% and take-off thrust by 2%.[^cite_note-THRUSTV-155] Clemens Linden, Eurojet TURBO GmbH CEO, speaking at the 2018 Farnborough International Air Show, said "15 per cent more thrust would allow pilots to operate with a heavily loaded aircraft in the battlespace with the same performance levels as they have today. The technology insertion also provides more persistence – giving aircraft longer range or longer loitering time. To achieve more thrust we would increase the airflow and pressure ratios of the high and low pressure compressors and run higher temperatures in the turbines by using the latest generation single crystal turbine blade materials. And with higher aerodynamic efficiencies we can achieve a lower fuel burn. A third area of improvement would be the engine exhaust nozzle which would be upgraded with the installation of a 2-parametric version allowing independent and optimized adjustment of the throat and exit area at all flight conditions, providing fuel burn advantages. The technologies for the different components are at a [Technology readiness level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_readiness_level) of between 7 and 9. The nozzle has been at [ITP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industria_de_Turbo_Propulsores) in Spain on a test bed for 400 hours."[^cite_note-EJHRS-156]

### Performance

The Typhoon's combat performance, compared to the [F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) and F-35 Lightning II fighters and the French Dassault Rafale, has been the subject of much discussion.[^cite_note-COMPARE1-157] In March 2005, [United States Air Force](32090.md) Chief of Staff General [John P. Jumper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Jumper), then the only person to have flown both the Eurofighter Typhoon and the Raptor, said:

> The Eurofighter is both agile and sophisticated, but is still difficult to compare to the F/A-22 Raptor. They are different kinds of airplanes to start with; it's like asking us to compare a [NASCAR](21114.md) car with a [Formula One](10854.md) car. They are both exciting in different ways, but they are designed for different levels of performance. ... The Eurofighter is certainly, as far as smoothness of controls and the ability to pull (and sustain high G forces), very impressive. That is what it was designed to do, especially the version I flew, with the avionics, the color moving map displays, etc. — all absolutely top notch. The manoeuvrability of the aeroplane in close-in combat was also very impressive. The F/A-22 performs in much the same way as the Eurofighter. But it has additional capabilities that allow it to perform the \[US] Air Force's unique missions. ... The F/A-22 Raptor has stealth and supercruise. It has the ability to penetrate virtually undetected.[^cite_note-Rapt_Eurof-158]



German Air Force Eurofighter Typhoon *31+17* during takeoff, July 2010

In the 2005 Singapore evaluation, the Typhoon won all three combat tests, including one in which a single Typhoon defeated three RSAF F-16s, and reliably completed all planned flight tests.[^cite_note-159] In July 2009, Former Chief of Air Staff for the RAF, Air Chief Marshal Sir Glenn Torpy, said that "The Eurofighter Typhoon is an excellent aircraft. It is to be the backbone of the Royal Air Force along with the JSF."[^cite_note-TORPY-160]

In July 2007, Indian Air Force [Su-30MKI](3793034.md) fighters participated in the *Indra-Dhanush* exercise with the RAF's Typhoon. This was the first time the two fighters had taken part in such an exercise.[^cite_note-INDIA1-161] The IAF pilots were impressed by the Typhoon's agility.[^cite_note-AirSceneUK-162] In 2015, Indian Air Force Su-30MKIs again participated in a *Indra-Dhanush* exercise with RAF Typhoons.[^cite_note-INDIA3-163]

#### Air to ground

The Typhoon is a multi-role fighter with maturing air-to-ground capabilities. The initial absence of air-to-ground capability is believed to have been a factor in the type's rejection from Singapore's fighter competition in 2005. At the time it was claimed that Singapore was concerned about the delivery timescale and the ability of the Eurofighter partner nations to fund the required capability packages.[^cite_note-A2G-164] Tranche 1 aircraft could drop laser-guided bombs in conjunction with third-party designators but the anticipated deployment of Typhoon to Afghanistan meant that the UK required self-contained bombing capabilities before the other partners.[^cite_note-AUSTERE1-165] In 2006 the UK embarked on the £73m Change Proposal 193 (CP193) to give an "austere" air-to-surface capability using [GBU-16 Paveway II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-16_Paveway_II) and [Rafael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems)/[Ultra Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Electronics) [Litening III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litening) [laser designator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_designator) for Tranche 1 Block 5 aircraft.[^cite_note-AUSTERE2-144] Aircraft with this upgrade were designated Typhoon FGR4 by the RAF.



Eurofighter operated by BAE Systems as a demonstrator with a full weapons load. Seen displaying at the 2016 Royal International Air Tattoo (RIAT), Fairford, UK.

Similar capability was added to Tranche 2 aircraft on the main development pathway as part of the Phase 1 Enhancements. P1Ea (SRP10) entered service in 2013 Q1 and added the use of Paveway IV, EGBU16 and the cannon against surface targets.[^cite_note-EFW0712-120] P1Eb (SRP12) added full integration with GPS bombs such as [GBU-10 Paveway II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-10_Paveway_II), GBU-16 Paveway II, [Paveway IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paveway_IV) and a new real-time operating system that allows multiple targets to be attacked in a single run.[^cite_note-EFW0712-120] This new system will form the basis for future weapons integration by individual countries under the Phase 2 Enhancements. The Storm Shadow and [KEPD 350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEPD_350) (Taurus) cruise missiles, together with the Meteor Beyond Visual Range Air-to-Air missile flight trials had been successfully completed by January 2016.[^cite_note-CRTRIAL-166] The [Storm Shadow](306908.md) and Meteor firings are part of the Phase 2 Enhancement (P2E) programme which introduced a range of new and improved long range attack capabilities to Typhoon. In addition to Meteor and Storm Shadow, the first live firing of MBDA's Brimstone air-to-surface missile, part of the Phase 3 Enhancements (P3E) programme, was successfully completed in July 2017.[^cite_note-P3E-167]

German aircraft can carry four [GBU-48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paveway) 1000 lb bombs.[^cite_note-GERGBU-168]

An anti-ship capability has been studied but has not yet been contracted. Weapon options for this role could include [Boeing Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_\(missile\)), [MBDA Marte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Killer), "Sea Brimstone", and [RBS-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RBS-15).[^cite_note-shipping2-169][^cite_note-Wall-170][^cite_note-shipping3-171]

The Typhoon can carry a mixture of air-to-air weaponry to fulfill its role as an [air superiority fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority_fighter). Available weapons include the [AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM) and [MBDA Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_\(missile\)) [beyond visual range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond-visual-range_missile) radar-guided missiles and the [ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASRAAM), [IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T), and [AIM-9 Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) short-range [IR-guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing) missiles.



German ground crew mount an [IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T) to a Eurofighter

The Typhoon also carries a specially developed variant of the [Mauser BK-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauser_BK-27) 27 mm cannon that was developed originally for the Panavia Tornado. This is a single-barrel, electrically fired, gas-operated revolver cannon with a new linkless feed system which is located in the starboard wing root, and is capable of firing up to 1700 rounds per minute. There was a proposal on cost grounds in 1999 to limit UK gun-armament fit to the first 53 batch-1 aircraft and not used operationally, but this decision was reversed in 2006.[^cite_note-CANNON-172] The aircraft carries 150 rounds.[^cite_note-173]

In his 2022 book *Typhoon*, former RAF pilot Mike Sutton reported that his 27 mm cannon had jammed during a strafing run in [Syria](7515849.md), against [ISIS](9087364.md) targets, while supporting Allied ground units. According to his book, the Typhoon was originally intended to be built without an internal gun, like the [F-4 Phantom](11759.md) and the [Harrier jump jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrier_jump_jet). A decision to install an internal gun had led to "manufacturing issues". Sutton claimed that, during his strafing run, the gun jammed after 26 rounds, with the HUD showing a "GUN FAIL" warning legend. During the debrief it transpired that the problem was well known to both the pilots and ground crews.[^cite_note-174]

## Operational history

Main article: [Eurofighter Typhoon procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_Typhoon_procurement)

### Austrian Air Force (*Luftstreitkräfte*)



[Austrian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Air_Force) Eurofighter *7L-WA* in flight to [Zeltweg Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeltweg_Air_Base), July 2007

In 2002, Austria selected the Typhoon as its new air defence aircraft, it having beaten the F-16 and the Saab Gripen in competition.[^cite_note-AUSTRIANOS-178] The purchase of 18 Typhoons was agreed on 1 July 2003, however this was reduced to 15 in June 2007.[^cite_note-AUSTRIANOS-178] The first aircraft (*7L-WA*) was delivered on 12 July 2007 to [Zeltweg Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeltweg_Air_Base) and formally entered service with the [Austrian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Air_Force).[^cite_note-AUDEL-179] A 2008 report by the Austrian [Court of Audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Audit) calculated, that instead of getting 18 Tranche 2 jets at a price of €109 million each, as stipulated by the original contract, the revised deal, agreed to by Minister Norbert Darabos, meant that Austria was paying an increased unit price of €114 million for 15 partially used, Tranche 1 jets.[^cite_note-AUT1-180] In July 2008, the Luftstreitkräfte assigned the Eurofighter to [Quick Reaction Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_Reaction_Alert) (QRA) duties, by the end of the year they had been scrambled 73 times.[^cite_note-QRA73-181]

Austrian prosecutors are investigating allegations that up to €100 million was made available to lobbyists to influence the original purchase decision in favour of the Eurofighter.[^cite_note-AUP-182] By October 2013, all Typhoons in service with Austria had been upgraded to the latest *Tranche 1* standard.[^cite_note-austrias-183] In 2014, due to defence budget restrictions, there were only 12 pilots available to fly the 15 aircraft in Austria's Air Force.[^cite_note-AUF-184] In February 2017, Austrian defence minister [Hans Peter Doskozil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Peter_Doskozil) accused Airbus of fraudulent intent following a probe that allegedly unveiled corruption linked to the order of Typhoon jets.[^cite_note-AUSS-185]

In July 2017, the Austria Defence Ministry announced that it would be replacing all its Typhoon aircraft by 2020. The ministry said continued use of its Typhoons over their 30-year life span would cost about €5 billion with the bulk being for maintenance. By comparison it is estimated that buying and operating a new fleet of 15 single-seat and three twin-seat fighters would save €2 billion over that period. Austria plans to explore a government-to-government sale or lease agreement to avoid a lengthy and costly tender process with a manufacturer. Possible replacements include the Gripen and the F-16.[^cite_note-AUSTREP-186]

On 20 July 2020, a letter written by Indonesia's defence minister, [Prabowo Subianto](2829084.md), was published by Indonesian news outlets expressing interest in acquiring Austria's entire fleet of Typhoon jets.[^cite_note-indo1-187] The move was criticized due to the "secondhand" nature of the aircraft, its high operational cost, and past legal dispute between Austria Defence Ministry and Airbus.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]

### German Air Force (*Luftwaffe*)



Luftwaffe Eurofighter *30+25* departing RIAT, July 2019

On 4 August 2003, the German Air Force accepted its first series production Eurofighter (*30+03*) starting the replacement process of the [Mikoyan MiG-29s](185653.md) inherited from the [East German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German_Air_Force).[^cite_note-GER1ST-191] The first Luftwaffe Wing to accept the Eurofighter was *[Jagdgeschwader 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagdgeschwader_73)* "Steinhoff" on 30 April 2004 at [Rostock–Laage Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock%E2%80%93Laage_Airport).[^cite_note-FirstLWING-192] The second Wing was *[Jagdgeschwader 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagdgeschwader_74)* (JG74) on 25 July 2006, with four Eurofighters arriving at [Neuburg Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuburg_Air_Base), beginning the replacement of JG74's [McDonnell Douglas F-4F Phantom IIs](11759.md).[^cite_note-REPLACEF4-193]

The *Luftwaffe* assigned their Eurofighters to QRA on 3 June 2008, taking over from the F-4F Phantom II.[^cite_note-QRA-194]

On 28 October 2014, while deployed to [Ämari Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%84mari_Air_Base) in Estonia as part of the NATO [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) mission, German Eurofighters scrambled and intercepted seven [Russian Air Force](1526308.md) aircraft over the [Baltic Sea](3335.md).[^cite_note-BALTIC-195]

The Luftwaffe once again provided Baltic Air Policing at Ämari Air Base between 31 August 2020 and April 2021, having taken over from [Dassault Mirage 2000-5Fs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) of the [French Air and Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_and_Space_Force).[^cite_note-BALTIC2-196]

On 5 June 2024, the German chancellor announced plans to purchase another twenty Eurofighters.[^cite_note-197]

German Eurofighters took part in [Exercise Tarang Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Tarang_Shakti) held by the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) from 6 August 2024.[^cite_note-198]

During NATO's Steadfast Dart 26 exercise in February 2026, the German Eurofighter Typhoon and the Turkish [Baykar Bayraktar TB3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar_Bayraktar_TB3) conducted joint military training operations in the Baltic region.[^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB3_completes_8-hour_joint_sortie_with_Eurofighters_in_NATO_drill-199] The Bayraktar TB-3 launched from the [Turkish Naval Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Naval_Forces) [drone carrier](76996952.md) [TCG Anadolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCG_Anadolu) to provide real-time [intelligence, surveillance, target acquisition, and reconnaissance](650971.md) (ISTR) for the Eurofighter Typhoons using its [Aselsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aselsan) ASELFLIR 500 [EOTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-optical_targeting_system).[^cite_note-Amphibious_Demonstration_marks_maritime_highlight_of_STEADFAST_DART_26-200] After the Bayraktar TB-3 struck its own targets with two [MAM-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Ak%C4%B1ll%C4%B1_M%C3%BChimmat) [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition), the UCAV also successfully identified simulated hostile targets for the German Eurofighter Typhoons to engage with precision strikes by the multirole fighter.[^cite_note-Amphibious_Demonstration_marks_maritime_highlight_of_STEADFAST_DART_26-200][^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB3_tested_for_the_first_time_at_NATO_exercises-201][^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB3_conducts_first_overseas_operational_demonstration_during_NATO_exercise_in_Baltic_Sea_-_Video-202] Both aircraft completed [manned-unmanned teaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned-unmanned_teaming) joint missions to demonstrate successful platform integration and NATO standardization compliance.[^cite_note-Bayraktar_TB3_completes_8-hour_joint_sortie_with_Eurofighters_in_NATO_drill-199]

### Italian Air Force (*Aeronautica Militare*)



Italian F-2000A Typhoon *MM7286* of 936° GEA landing at [Rivolto Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivolto_Air_Base), September 2015

On 16 December 2005, the F-2000 Typhoon reached initial operational capability (IOC) with the [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force) (*Aeronautica Militare*). Its F-2000 Typhoons were put into service as air defence fighters at the [Grosseto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosseto) Air Base, and immediately assigned to QRA at the same base.[^cite_note-Italy_IOC-203]

On 17 July 2009, Italian Air Force F-2000A Typhoons were deployed to protect Albania's airspace.[^cite_note-ALBANIA-204] On 29 March 2011, Italian Air Force Eurofighter Typhoons began flying combat air patrol missions in support of NATO's [Operation Unified Protector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Unified_Protector) in Libya.[^cite_note-ITLIBYA-205]

Between January and August 2015, four *Aeronautica Militare* F-2000A Typhoons (from 36º and 37º *Stormo*) were deployed to [Šiauliai Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai_Air_Base) in northern Lithuania as part of the Baltic Air Policing mission.[^cite_note-ITLITH-206]

### Kuwait Air Force

On 11 September 2015, Eurofighter confirmed that an agreement had been reached to supply Kuwait with 28 aircraft.[^cite_note-kuwait-207] On 1 March 2016, the [Kuwaiti National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Kuwait\)) approved the procurement of 22 single-seat and six twin-seat Typhoons.[^cite_note-KUWOPTS-208] On 5 April 2016, Kuwait signed a contract with Leonardo valued at €7.957 billion (\$9.062 billion) for the supply of the 28 aircraft, all to tranche 3 standard.[^cite_note-KUWCONT-209] The Kuwaiti aircraft are to be the first Typhoons to receive the Captor-E AESA radar, with two instrumented production aircraft from the UK and Germany currently undergoing ground-based integration trials. The Typhoons are to be fitted with Leonardo's Praetorian defensive aids suite and PIRATE infrared search and track system. The contract involves the production of aircraft in Italy and covers logistics, operational support and the training of flight crews and ground personnel. It also encompasses infrastructure work at the [Ali Al Salem Air Base](1469367.md), where the Typhoons are to be based.[^cite_note-KUWFI-210]

Deliveries commenced in 2021, and by September 2025 Leonardo announced that the majority of the 28 aircraft ordered had been delivered and entered service.[^cite_note-211] Kuwait subsequently extended its contract with Leonardo for in-service support of the Eurofighter fleet until December 2029.[^cite_note-212]

### Qatar Emiri Air Force

From January 2011 the [Qatar Emiri Air Force](4014890.md) (QEAF) evaluated the Typhoon, alongside the Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet, the [McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle](495724.md), the Dassault Rafale, and the Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II, to replace its then inventory of [Dassault Mirage 2000-5s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000#Mirage_2000-5F). On 30 April 2015 Qatar announced that it would order 24 Rafales.[^cite_note-QATRAFA-213]

In December 2017 a deal for Qatar to buy 24 jets and a support and training package from BAE was announced, scheduled to begin in 2022.[^cite_note-Qatarbbc-214] In September 2018, Qatar made the first payment for the procurement of 24 Eurofighter Typhoons and nine [BAE Systems Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Hawk) aircraft to BAE.[^cite_note-QATPAYM-215]

By August 2023, BAE Systems reported that half of the Eurofighter Typhoon aircraft ordered by Qatar had been delivered to the Qatar Emiri Air Force.[^cite_note-216] In October 2025, Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) announced his interest in acquiring all 24 of Qatar's used Eurofighter Typhoon Tranche 3A aircraft.[^cite_note-217]

### Royal Air Force (UK)



Typhoon T1 *ZJ800* of [No. XVII (R) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._17_Squadron_RAF) at [RAF Waddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Waddington), June 2004. This was the first RAF full production aircraft to fly.

The UK's first Typhoon Development Aircraft (DA-2) *ZH588* made its maiden flight on 6 April 1994 from Warton.[^cite_note-DA2-218] On 1 September 2002, [No. XVII (Reserve) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._17_Squadron_RAF) was reformed at Warton as the Typhoon Operational Evaluation Unit (TOEU), receiving its first aircraft on 18 December 2003.[^cite_note-RAFFGR4-219] The first RAF production aircraft to take to the air was *ZJ800* (BT001) on 14 February 2003, completing a 21-minute flight.[^cite_note-FIRSTP-220] The next Typhoon squadron to be formed was [No. 29 (R) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._29_Squadron_RAF) which formed as the Typhoon Operational Conversion Unit (OCU).[^cite_note-RAFFGR4-219] The first operational RAF Typhoon squadron to be formed was [No. 3 (Fighter) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._3_Squadron_RAF) on 31 March 2006, when it moved to RAF Coningsby.[^cite_note-RAFOPER-221]

No. 3 (F) Squadron Typhoon F2s took over QRA responsibilities from the [Panavia Tornado F3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado_F3) on 29 June 2007, initially alternating with the Tornado F3 every month.[^cite_note-QRA2-222] On 9 August 2007, the UK's MoD reported that [No. XI (F) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._11_Squadron_RAF) of the RAF, which stood up as a Typhoon squadron on 29 March 2007,[^cite_note-11sqnRAF-223] had taken delivery of its first two multi-role Typhoons.[^cite_note-RAFMULTI-224] Two of No. XI (F) Squadron's Typhoons were sent to intercept a Russian [Tupolev Tu-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95) approaching British airspace on 17 August 2007.[^cite_note-RAFINT-225] The RAF Typhoons were declared combat ready in the air-to-ground role by 1 July 2008.[^cite_note-RAFSURF-226] The RAF Typhoons were projected to be ready to deploy for operations by mid-2008.[^cite_note-11sqnRAF-223]

In late 2009, four RAF Typhoons were deployed to [RAF Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mount_Pleasant), replacing the Tornado F3s of [No. 1435 Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._1435_Flight_RAF) defending the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md).[^cite_note-FALKLANDS-227] No. 6 Squadron stood up at [RAF Leuchars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Leuchars) on 6 September 2010, making Leuchars the second RAF base to operate the Typhoon.[^cite_note-6SQN-228]



A QRA Typhoon F2 (*ZJ932*) of [No. XI (F) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._11_Squadron_RAF) escorting a Russian [Tupolev Tu-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95) aircraft over the North Atlantic Ocean, August 2008

On 20 March 2011 ten Typhoons from RAF Coningsby and RAF Leuchars arrived at the [Gioia del Colle airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioia_del_Colle_Air_Base) in southern Italy to enforce a [no-fly zone in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_no-fly_zone) alongside Panavia Tornado GR4s.[^cite_note-LIBYANFZ-229] On 21 March, RAF Typhoons flew their first-ever combat mission while patrolling the no-fly zone.[^cite_note-RAF1ST-230] On 29 March, it was revealed that the RAF was having to divert personnel from Typhoon training to meet the shortfall in pilots available to fly the required number of sorties over Libya.[^cite_note-LIBYASHORT-231] On 12 April 2011, an RAF Typhoon and a Tornado GR4 dropped [precision-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_bomb) on ground vehicles operated by Gaddafi forces.[^cite_note-LIBYAPGB-232] The RAF said that each aircraft dropped one GBU-16 Paveway II 454 kg (1,000 lb) laser-guided bomb which struck "very successfully and very accurately \[and this] represented] a significant milestone in the delivery of multi-role Typhoon."[^cite_note-LIBYA1ST-233] Target designation was provided by the Tornados with their Litening III targeting pods due to the lack of Typhoon pilots trained in air-to-ground missions.[^cite_note-WITHTORNADO-234]

The National Audit Office observed in 2011 that the distribution of the Eurofighter's parts supply and repairs over several countries has led to parts shortages, long timescales for repairs, and the [cannibalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannibalization_\(parts\)) of some aircraft to keep others flying.[^cite_note-NAO-42] The UK's then Defence Secretary [Liam Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Fox) admitted on 14 April 2011 that Britain's Eurofighter Typhoon jets were grounded in 2010 due to shortage of spare parts. The RAF "cannibalised" aircraft for spare parts in a bid to keep the maximum number of Typhoons operational on any given day. The MoD warned that the problems were likely to continue until 2015.[^cite_note-RAFCAN-235]

On 15 September 2012, [No. 1 (F) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._1_Squadron_RAF) stood up at RAF Leuchars, joining No. 6 Squadron as the second Typhoon unit to operate in Scotland.[^cite_note-rafl-236] On 22 April 2013, [No. 41 (R) Test and Evaluation Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._41_Squadron_RAF) (TES) began operating the Typhoon from RAF Coningbsy.[^cite_note-RAF41-237]

By July 2014, a dozen RAF Tranche 2 Typhoons had been upgraded with Phase 1 Enhancement (P1E) capability to enable them to use the Paveway IV guided bomb; the Tranche 1 version had used the [GBU-12 Paveway II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-12_Paveway_II) in combat over Libya, but the Paveway IV can be set to explode above or beneath a target and to hit at a set angle.

No. II (AC) Squadron became the fifth RAF Typhoon squadron on 12 January 2015 at RAF Lossiemouth.[^cite_note-IISQN-238] In July 2015, it was reported that Typhoons from No. II (AC) Squadron were training with [Type 45 destroyers](466618.md) in an Air-Maritime Integration (AMI) role, conceding that the service had recently neglected the role following the decommissioning of the [Nimrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Siddeley_Nimrod) Maritime Patrol aircraft.[^cite_note-MARITIME-239] In the [2015 Strategic Defence and Security Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_and_Security_Review_2015) (SDSR), the UK decided to retain some of the Tranche 1 aircraft to increase the number of front-line squadrons from five to seven and to extend the out-of-service date from 2030 to 2040 as well as implementing the Captor-E AESA radar in later tranches.[^cite_note-SDSR-240] In 2015, Typhoons were deployed to Malta as security for the [Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting_2015).[^cite_note-GHOGM-241] On 3 December 2015, six Typhoon FGR4s deployed to [RAF Akrotiri](432784.md) to support operations against ISIL. The following evening the Typhoons, accompanied by Tornados, attacked targets in Syria.[^cite_note-SYRIA-242]

In October 2016, four Typhoon FGR4s from No. II (AC) Squadron, supported by an [Airbus Voyager KC3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330_MRTT) aerial tanker and a [Boeing C-17 Globemaster III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_C-17_Globemaster_III), deployed to [Misawa Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misawa_Air_Base) in Japan for the first bilateral exercises with non-US forces hosted by the JASDF.[^cite_note-JAPAN16-243]



Typhoon FGR4 *ZK439* on approach to [RAF Marham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Marham), May 2020. This was the penultimate Typhoon to be delivered to the RAF.

On 14 December 2017, it was announced No. 12 (B) Squadron would stand as a joint RAF/Qatari Air Force squadron, with the Qatari crew temporarily operating Typhoons to prepare them for their own Typhoon deliveries in 2022.[^cite_note-QATTEMP-244] On 29 January 2018, the RAF announced that 16 twin-seat Typhoons would undergo the Return to Produce (RTP) process in an effort to save £800 million, with each airframe producing £50M of spare parts.[^cite_note-RTP-245] This move also reflected the switch from two-seat trainer to single-seat pilot training and greater use of training simulators. In addition, the two-seat airframes were primarily from Tranche 1 and could not be equipped with Tranche 3 and later upgrades such as Captor-E.[^cite_note-RTP2-246]

On 1 April 2019, [No. IX (B) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._IX_Squadron_RAF) officially converted from the Tornado GR4 to the Typhoon FGR4, becoming an aggressor and air defence squadron at Lossiemouth.[^cite_note-9Sqn-247] In April, four Typhoons of No. XI (F) Squadron deployed from RAF Coningsby to Ämari Airbase, Estonia, to undergo a four-month long NATO Baltic policing mission (Op AZOTIZE).[^cite_note-AZOTIZE2019-248] Five Typhoons of No. 6 Squadron participated in the Arctic Challenge Exercise (ACE) in Sweden from 22 May to 4 June.[^cite_note-RAFACE-249] No. 12 Squadron were assigned their first Typhoon FGR4 in July 2019.[^cite_note-12SQN-250] The 160th, and last, Typhoon (*ZK437*) was delivered to the RAF on 27 September 2019.[^cite_note-RAFLAST-251] Between November and December 2019, No. 1(F) Squadron deployed to [Keflavik Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Keflavik) in Iceland as part of NATO's Icelandic Air Policing Mission.[^cite_note-252] During this one-month deployment the aircraft conducted more than 180 practice intercepts and 59 training sorties.[^cite_note-253]

Between April and September 2020, No. 6 Squadron deployed to [Šiauliai Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai_Air_Base), Lithuania, as part of Operation Azotize.[^cite_note-AZOTIZE2020-254][^cite_note-AZOTIZE2020end-255] While deployed the squadron participated in Exercise BALTOPs 2020.[^cite_note-BALTOPs-256] In July 2020, No. 12 Squadron began operating as a joint RAF-QEAF unit at RAF Coningsby.[^cite_note-12SQN_2-257]

On 22 March 2021 the [2021 Defence Command Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_in_a_Competitive_Age) announced the retirement of all Tranche 1 Typhoons by 2025, with the remaining fleet being upgraded.[^cite_note-2021DCP-258] Also in 2021 the UK launched the P3Ec package, due for delivery in 2024, including several upgrades, including replacing the [multifunction displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunction_display) with a Large Area Display (LAD).[^cite_note-259] On 14 December 2021 the RAF executed its first operational air-to-air engagement with a Typhoon, shooting down a small hostile drone with an [ASRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASRAAM) near the [Al-Tanf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tanf_\(U.S._military_base\)) coalition base in Syria.[^cite_note-a2akill-260][^cite_note-mod-20211216-261]

On 7 September 2022 during the joint UK/US [SinkEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_ship) 'Atlantic Thunder' a 41 Squadron Typhoon successfully hit the ex-[USS *Boone*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Boone) with Paveway IVs, becoming the first RAF Typhoon to strike a naval target with live ordnance.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263]

Between 18 and 22 September 2023, Typhoons from 41 Squadron took part in the Finnish led Exercise ‘Baana 23’. During this exercise, the aircraft performed landings and takeoffs from a highway in [Tervo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tervo), marking a first for any Eurofighter operator.[^cite_note-264]

On 12 January 2024, at 2\:30 am local time, four RAF Typhoons dropped [Paveway IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paveway_IV) bombs on two military facilities, used by [Houthis](17240332.md) to launch drone and missile strikes on ships in the [Red Sea](26191.md), as a part of the [2024 Yemeni airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_missile_strikes_in_Yemen).[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266] On 13 April 2024, RAF Typhoons shot down an unspecified number of [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) (UAVs) during the [2024 Iranian strikes in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2024_Iranian_strikes_against_Israel). The Typhoons, based in Cyprus and Romania, were operating in Iraqi and Syrian airspace as part of [Operation Shader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shader).[^cite_note-267]

On 1 March 2026, an RAF Typhoon of 12 Squadron operating from Qatar, shot down an Iranian UAV that was approaching Qatari airspace.[^cite_note-268] In May 2026, the RAF Typhoon was equipped with the US developed [Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Precision_Kill_Weapon_System) (APKWS) to shoot down UAVs more cheaply, and fielded in the Middle East.[^cite_note-bbc-20260517-269][^cite_note-raf-20260518-270]

### Royal Air Force of Oman

During the 2008 Farnborough Airshow it was announced that [Oman](22316.md) was in an "advanced stage" of discussions to order Typhoons as a replacement for its [SEPECAT Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPECAT_Jaguar) aircraft.[^cite_note-OMAN1-271] On 21 December 2012, the [Royal Air Force of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force_of_Oman) (RAFO) became the Typhoon's seventh customer when BAE and Oman announced an order for 12 Typhoons to enter service in 2017.[^cite_note-OMANORDER-272] The first of the Typhoons (plus Hawk Mk 166) ordered by Oman were "formally presented to the customer" on 15 May 2017. This included a flypast by an RAFO Typhoon.[^cite_note-OMAN2-273]

### Royal Saudi Air Force



RSAF Typhoon *1007* on a delivery flight passing through [Malta International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_International_Airport), December 2009

In August 2006, Saudi Arabia confirmed it had agreed to purchase 72 Typhoons for the [Royal Saudi Air Force](570367.md) (RSAF).[^cite_note-SAUDI1-274] In December 2006, it was reported in *The Guardian* that Saudi Arabia had threatened to buy Rafales because of a UK [Serious Fraud Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serious_Fraud_Office_\(United_Kingdom\)) (SFO) investigation into the [Al Yamamah defence deals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yamamah_arms_deal) which commenced in the 1980s.[^cite_note-SAUDI2-275]

On 14 December 2006, Britain's attorney general, [Lord Goldsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Goldsmith), ordered that the SFO discontinue its investigation into BAE Systems' alleged bribery of senior Saudi officials in the Al-Yamamah contracts, citing "the need to safeguard national and international security".[^cite_note-aly-276] *The Times* raised the possibility that RAF production aircraft would be diverted as early Saudi Arabian aircraft, with the RAF forced to wait for its full complement of aircraft.[^cite_note-TimesSaudi-277] This arrangement would mirror the diversion of RAF Tornados to the RSAF. *The Times* also reported that such an arrangement would make the UK purchase of its Tranche 3 commitments more likely.[^cite_note-TimesSaudi-277] On 17 September 2007, Saudi Arabia confirmed it had signed a £4.43 billion contract for 72 aircraft.[^cite_note-saucontract-278] 24 aircraft would be at the Tranche 2 build standard, previously destined for the UK RAF, the first being delivered in 2008. The remaining 48 aircraft were to be assembled in Saudi Arabia and delivered from 2011, however following contract renegotiations in 2011, it was agreed that all 72 aircraft would be assembled by BAE Systems in the UK, with the last 24 aircraft being built to Tranche 3 capability.[^cite_note-SAUDI3-279]

On 29 September 2008, the [United States Department of State](31975.md) approved the Typhoon sale, required because of a certain technology governed by the [International Traffic in Arms Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Traffic_in_Arms_Regulations) (ITAR) process which was incorporated into the MIDS of the Eurofighter.[^cite_note-ITAR-280]

On 22 October 2008, the first RSAF Typhoon made its maiden flight at Warton.[^cite_note-SAUDI4-281] Since 2010, BAE has been training Saudi Arabian personnel at Warton.[^cite_note-SAUDI5-282]

By 2011, 24 Tranche 2 Eurofighter Typhoons had been delivered to Saudi Arabia, consisting of 18 single-seat and six two-seat aircraft. After that, BAE and Riyadh entered into discussions over configurations and price of the rest of the 72-plane order. On 19 February 2014, BAE announced that the Saudis had agreed to a price increase.[^cite_note-SAUDI6-283] BAE announced that the last of the original 72 Typhoons had been delivered to Saudi Arabia in June 2017.[^cite_note-Saudi0617-284]

RSAF Typhoons are playing a central role in the Saudi-led [bombing campaign in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen).[^cite_note-YEMEN-285] In February 2015, Saudi Typhoons attacked ISIS targets over Syria using Paveway IV bombs for the first time.[^cite_note-SAUDISYR-286]

On 9 March 2018, a memorandum of intent for the additional 48 Typhoons was signed during Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's visit to the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-SAUDI7-287] however the deal has not been completed due to German arms sanctions implemented in November 2018 in response to the [assassination of Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Jamal_Khashoggi).[^cite_note-288]

### Spanish Air and Space Force



Spanish Eurofighter Tifón C.16–44 of 142 Escuadrón, May 2016

The first Spanish production Eurofighter Tifón to fly was *CE.16-01* (ST001) on 17 February 2003, flying from [Getafe Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe_Air_Base).[^cite_note-ST001-289] The Spanish Air and Space Force assigned their Typhoons to QRA responsibilities in July 2008.[^cite_note-QRA-194]

There were no casualties, but the ten-day search operation for missile remains was unsuccessful and the status of the missile is unknown, whether it self-destructed in the air or landed unexploded and left a hazardous situation for the public. The pilot was disciplined for negligence, but received only the minimum penalty in the light of undisclosed mitigating circumstances.[^cite_note-SPAEST-290]

### Germany

Germany placed an order for an additional 38 Tranche 4 Typhoons on 11 November 2020 under the Quadriga Agreement.[^cite_note-291] The aircraft are due to replace Tranche 1 aircraft currently in service, with the first airframe being announced as in production in November 2022. Deliveries are due to take place from 2025.[^cite_note-292]

In March 2022, the German government announced the decision to purchase Typhoon EK over the [Boeing EA-18G Growler](833647.md) to replace the ageing [Tornado ECR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado) variant from 2030.[^cite_note-293] On 30 November 2023, the [Budget Committee of the Bundestag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_Committee_of_the_Bundestag) formally announced the plans to convert 15 Typhoons to Electronic Warfare standard.[^cite_note-twz.com-294]

### Spain

The Spanish Air and Space Force has a requirement for a further 45 Typhoons split across two contracts.

Halcon I was signed in June 2022 for the purchase of 20 aircraft will begin deliveries from 2026.[^cite_note-299] The contract is for 16 single-seat and four twin-seat airframes, all at Tranche 4 standard. ## Variants

Main article: [Eurofighter Typhoon variants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_Typhoon_variants)

The Eurofighter is produced in single-seat and twin-seat variants. The twin-seat variant is used for training, though it is combat capable.

The Tranche 1 aircraft were produced from 2000 onwards. Aircraft capabilities are being increased incrementally, with each software upgrade resulting in a different standard, known as blocks and also Enhanced Operational Capability (EOS) segments.[^cite_note-efb5-332] The latest variant is Tranche 5 which is equipped with sophisticated avionics, electronic warfare suite and weapon integrations.[^cite_note-333]

The aircraft prototypes were manufactured in three major standards; Development Aircraft (DA), Instrumented Production Aircraft (IPA) for further system development, and a Series Production Aircraft.[^cite_note-IPA-334]

## Accidents

- On 21 November 2002, the Spanish twin-seat Typhoon prototype DA-6 crashed due to a double engine [flameout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flameout) caused by surges of the two engines at 45,000 ft. The two crew members escaped unhurt and the aircraft crashed in a military test range near [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Spain), some 110 kilometres (68 mi) from its base at [Getafe Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe_Air_Base).

  [^cite_note-SPACRASH-383]

- On 23 April 2008, an RAF Typhoon FGR4 from 17 Squadron at RAF Coningsby (ZJ943), made a [wheels–up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belly_landing) landing at the US Navy's [NAWS China Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Weapons_Station_China_Lake), in the United States.

  [^cite_note-CRASH9-384]

  &#x20;The aircraft was severely damaged however the pilot from [17 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._17_Squadron_RAF) did not sustain any significant injury. It is thought the pilot may have forgotten to deploy the [undercarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear) or that for some reason he was not alerted to the undercarriage having not been deployed.

  [^cite_note-CRASH9-384]

- On 24 August 2010, a Spanish twin-seat Typhoon crashed at Spain's [Morón Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mor%C3%B3n_Air_Base) moments after take-off for a routine training flight. It was being piloted by an RSAF pilot, who was killed, and a Spanish Air Force Major, who ejected safely.

  [^cite_note-CRASH4-385]

  &#x20;In September 2010 the German Air Force grounded its 55 planes and the RAF temporarily grounded all Typhoon training flights amidst concerns that after ejecting successfully the pilot had fallen to his death.

  [^cite_note-CRASH5-386]

  [^cite_note-CRASH6-387]

  &#x20;On 21 September, the RAF announced that the harness system had been sufficiently modified to enable routine flying from RAF Coningsby. The Austrian Air Force also said all its aircraft had been cleared for flight.

  [^cite_note-CRASH7-388]

  &#x20;On 24 August 2010, the ejection seat manufacturer Martin Baker commented: "... under certain conditions, the quick release fitting could be unlocked using the palm of the hand, rather than the thumb and fingers, and that this posed a risk of inadvertent release", adding that a modification had been rapidly developed and approved "to eliminate this risk" and was being fitted to all Typhoon seats.

  [^cite_note-CRASH8-389]

- On 9 June 2014, the [Spanish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Air_Force) announced that a Typhoon had crashed at Spain's Morón Air Base on landing after a routine training flight. The sole pilot, Captain Fernando Lluna Carrascosa of the Spanish Air Force, who had over 600 Eurofighter flying hours, died in the crash.

  [^cite_note-SPANCRASH-390]

- On 23 June 2014, a Typhoon of the German Air Force suffered [a mid-air collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsberg_mid-air_collision) with a [Learjet 35A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_35A), which crashed near [Olsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsberg,_Germany), Germany. The severely damaged Eurofighter made a safe landing at [Nörvenich Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B6rvenich_Air_Base), while the Learjet crashed with the two onboard killed.

  [^cite_note-CRASH3-391]

- On 1 September 2017, an RAF Typhoon [overran the runway](8731144.md) on landing at [Pardubice Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardubice_Airport), Czech Republic, after diverting for bad weather.

  [^cite_note-Pardubice-392]

- On 14 September 2017, an RSAF aircraft crashed on a combat mission in [Yemen](350939.md)'s [Abhyan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyan_Governorate), killing its pilot. According to the [Saudi Government](27272.md), the aircraft crashed due to technical reasons.

  [^cite_note-RSAFCRASH-393]

- On 24 September 2017, an Italian Air Force aircraft crashed during an airshow in [Terracina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracina), Lazio, Italy. The pilot did not eject and died in the accident.

  [^cite_note-CRASH2-394]

  &#x20;The Italian Air Force said the jet completed a loop but then failed to get enough lift as it approached sea level and hit the water just a few hundred metres offshore.

  [^cite_note-CRASH2-394]

- On 12 October 2017, a Spanish Air Force Typhoon crashed near its base at [Los Llanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albacete_Airport) [Albacete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albacete), Spain, when returning from the military parade for the [Spanish National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_Nacional_de_Espa%C3%B1a). The pilot was killed.

  [^cite_note-CRASH1-395]

- On 24 June 2019, two German Air Force aircraft collided mid-air during an exercise in the region of Müritz in [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) in northern Germany. Both aircraft were lost while the pilots ejected. The two planes were based at [Laage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laage), home to the "Steinhoff" [Tactical Air Force Wing 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taktisches_Luftwaffengeschwader_73). Neither plane was carrying weapons. One of the pilots died.

  [^cite_note-CRASH0-396]

- On 14 December 2022, an Italian Air Force Typhoon of 37° Stormo crashed during the landing sequence into [Trapani-Birgi Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapani-Birgi_Air_Base) in Sicily. The aircraft had been conducting a training mission with another Typhoon which landed safely. The pilot was killed during the crash.

  [^cite_note-397]

- On 24 July 2024, an Italian Air Force Typhoon crashed during a military training exercise in the [Douglas Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas-Daly,_Northern_Territory) region of the [Northern Territory](21638.md), Australia, during [Exercise Pitch Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Pitch_Black). The pilot ejected safely and was taken to [Royal Darwin Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Darwin_Hospital) by helicopter.

  [^cite_note-398]
