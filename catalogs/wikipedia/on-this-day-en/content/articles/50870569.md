**Tanagra Air Base** ([ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **LGTG**) is a large military air base situated in [Tanagra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanagra), [Greece](12108.md). The air base is adjacent to [Hellenic Aerospace Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Aerospace_Industry) facilities. It is used as the home-base of the **114th Combat Wing** (114 Πτέρυγα Μάχης, 114 ΠΜ) of the [Hellenic Air Force](363254.md).

## History

From [World War II](32927.md) until the early 1950s, Tanagra Air Base was used as a complementary airfield. After that period, and with [NATO](21133.md) contribution, it was upgraded to a main air base of the [Hellenic Air Force](363254.md). From 1975 to 2003 [Dassault Mirage F1CG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_F1) were operated from Tanagra. Starting in 2007, newly acquired [Dassault Mirage 2000-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) are operated by the [331st All Weather Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/331st_All_Weather_Squadron?action=edit\&redlink=1).

It had been proposed in the 1980s that a civil terminal would be built at the airport, rendering Tanagra as a secondary Athens airport, to complement [Ellinikon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellinikon_International_Airport), which was going to be expanded. This plan was dropped when it was decided to construct the current [Athens International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens_International_Airport) "Eleftherios Venizelos" in the Spata area east of Athens.

Since 2016, Tanagra Air Base has been the venue of the annual Athens Flying Week airshow, held in September each year[^cite_note-3].

On January 19, 2022 [332st All Weather Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/332st_All_Weather_Squadron?action=edit\&redlink=1) received its first 6 [Dassault Rafale F3R fighters](33731380.md) and retired its aging [Dassault Mirage-2000EG/BG fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) upon a ceremony held by the Hellenic Air Force.

On September 5, 2026, a [F-4E AUP Phantom II](11759.md) fighter of [338th Fighter-Bomber Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/338th_Fighter-Bomber_Squadron)[^cite_note-4] crashed during the Athens Flying Week[^cite_note-5]. Two pilots were killed[^cite_note-6].
