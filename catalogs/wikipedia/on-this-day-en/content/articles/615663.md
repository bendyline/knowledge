**Urmia** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): ارومیه; pronounced [\[oɾumiˈje\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/da/Urmia-2.ogg/Urmia-2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/da/Urmia-2.ogg/Urmia-2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-fn1-7] is the largest city in [West Azerbaijan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Azerbaijan_Province) of [Iran](14653.md). In the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Urmia_County\)) of [Urmia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_County), it is capital of the province, the county, and the district.[^cite_note-West_Azerbaijan_Province_Structure-8] The city is situated near the borders of [Iran](14653.md) with [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-9]

The city lies at an elevation of 1,330 metres (4,360 ft) above sea level along the [Shahar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahar_River) on the [Urmia Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_Plain). [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia), one of the world's largest [salt lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_lake), lies to the east of the city, and the [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Turkey_border) with [Turkey](11125639.md) lies to the west.

The city is the trading center for a fertile agricultural region where fruits (especially [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple) and [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape)) and [tobacco](30942.md) are grown. Among the villages in the Urmia region are [Lajani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajani) and [Mafaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafaran). Even though the majority of the residents of Urmia are Muslims, the Christian history of Urmia is well preserved and is especially evident in the city's many churches and cathedrals.

An important town by the 9th century, the city has had a diverse population which has at times included Muslims ([Shias](26961.md) and [Sunnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam)), Christians ([Catholics](214903.md), [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), [Nestorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East), and [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Church)), [Jews](25955086.md), [Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) and [Sufis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism). Around 1900, Christians made up more than 40% of the city's population; however, in the next decades most of the Christians were either killed by the advancing Ottoman troops or in raids by Kurdish tribes[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] or fled shortly after the end of the war.[^cite_note-Columbia-12][^cite_note-nineveh.com-13][^cite_note-E.J._Brill_1936-14]

Urmia, [Takab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takab) and [Piranshahr](30865712.md) respectively have the highest number of registered provincial sites in the list of national sites.[^cite_note-15]

Urmia is a historically diverse city where [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijanis), [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Kurds), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Iran), and [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Armenians) have long lived side by side. It has also been home to [Jewish](25955086.md) and [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) communities, reflecting its longstanding multicultural and multireligious character.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Etymology

[Richard Nelson Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nelson_Frye) suggested [Urartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) origin for the name,[^cite_note-20] while [T. Burrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Burrow) connected the origin of the name Urmia to [Indo-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Iranian_languages) urmi- "wave" and urmya- "undulating, wavy".[^cite_note-21]

The name could also derive from the combination of the [Assyrian Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language) words *[Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur)* (ܐܘܪ; a common name for cities around [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), meaning "city") and *Mia* ([Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language): ܡܝܐ, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'water'), "City of Water" referring to the great [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia) nearby.[^cite_note-22] Compare [Urhay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edessa), [Ur of the Chaldees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur_of_the_Chaldees).

In his [seyahatname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyahatname), [Evliya Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_%C3%87elebi) referred to the city as *Rûmiyye* ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): رومیه), also mentioning that the Mongols called the city *Urumiye* ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): اورمیه), Persians *Rûmiyye-i Kübrâ* ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): رومیه كبری), and some historians *Türkistân-ı İrân* ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): تركستان ایران), which he justified by the considerable amount of Turkoman [awliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awliya) in the city.[^cite_note-Çelebi-26]

## History

### Early history



People of Urmia awaiting [Ahmad Shah Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Qajar), 1911



Urmia fruit market, 1911

According to [Vladimir Minorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Minorsky), there were villages in the [Urmia Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_Plain) as early as 2000 BC, with their civilization under the influence of the [Kingdom of Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Van). Excavations of the ancient ruins near Urmia led to the discovery of utensils that date to the 20th century BC. In ancient times, the west bank of Urmia Lake was part of [Gilzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilzan), and in the 9th century BC an independent government ruled there, which later joined the [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) or Manna empire; in the 8th century BC, the area was a vassal of the Asuzh government until it joined the [Median kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_kingdom).

[Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) who did survive the invasion of [Baghdad](4492.md) by [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) fled through northern Iraq up into the Hakkari Mountains to the west of Lake Urmia and the area remained as their homeland until the 19th century.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Skutsch2013-33]

During the [Safavid era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran), the neighboring [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), who were the archrivals of the Safavids,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERothman2015236-34] made several incursions into the city and captured it on more than one occasion, but the Safavids successfully regained control over the area. When in 1622, during the reign of the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) Shah [Abbas the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_the_Great) (r. 1588–1629), [Qasem Sultan Afshar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasem_Sultan_Afshar) was appointed governor of [Mosul](200475.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENasiriFloor2008248-35][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOberling1984582–586-36] he was forced to leave his office shortly afterwards due to the outbreak of a [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_\(disease\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOberling1984582–586-36] He moved to the western part of [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_\(Iran\)), and became the founder of the [Afshar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshar_people) community of Urmia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOberling1984582–586-36] The city was the capital of the [Urmia Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_Khanate) from 1747 to 1865. The first monarch of Iran's [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty), [Agha Mohammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan_Qajar), was crowned in Urmia in 1795.

### 19th century

Due to the presence of a substantial [Christian](18337522.md) minority at the end of the 19th century, Urmia was also chosen as the site of the first Christian missionaries from the United States in Iran in 1835 led by [Justin Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Perkins) (1805–1869) with [Asahel Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahel_Grant) (1807–1844); and followed by [Fidelia Fiske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidelia_Fisk) (1816–1864), [Joseph Gallup Cochran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gallup_Cochran) (1817–1871), and [Joseph Plumb Cochran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cochran) (1855–1905).[^cite_note-\:1-37] Another mission was soon underway in nearby [Tabriz](439791.md) as well. During [World War I](4764461.md), the population was estimated by Dr. Caujole to be 30,000 people, a quarter of which (7,500) were Assyrians and 1,000 Jews.[^cite_note-E.J._Brill_1936-14]

During the 19th century, the region became the center of a short-lived Assyrian renaissance with many books and newspapers being published in [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language). Urmia was also the seat of a [Chaldean diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioceses_of_the_Church_of_the_East_after_1552).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-naby-39]

### 20th century

In late 1914, Ottoman forces under the command of [Enver Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Pasha) stepped up clandestine activity in the region with the aim of committing the Ottoman Empire to war.[^cite_note-Gaunt2015-40] During [World War I](4764461.md), the city changed hands several times between the [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia) and the Ottoman troops and their Kurdish allies in the following two years.[^cite_note-TejirianSimon2012-41] In 1914, before the declaration of war against Russia, Ottoman forces crossed the border into [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and destroyed Christian villages. Large-scale attacks in late September and October 1914 targeted many Assyrian villages, and the attackers neared Urmia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt2006129-42] Due to Ottoman attacks, thousands of Christians living along the border fled to Urmia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot-Bellier2018117,_125-43]

Many Christians fled during the Russian withdrawal from Azerbaijan at the beginning of January 1915,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot-Bellier2018120-44] and 20,000 to 25,000 refugees were left stranded in Urmia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt2006110-45] Nearly 18,000 Christians sought shelter in the city's Presbyterian and [Lazarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission) missions. Although there was reluctance to attack the missionary compounds, many died of disease.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot-Bellier2018122-46] Between February and May (when the Ottoman forces pulled out), there was a campaign of mass execution, looting, kidnapping, and extortion against Christians in Urmia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt2006110-45] More than 100 men were arrested at the Lazarist compound, and dozens (including Mar Dinkha, bishop of Tergawer) were executed on 23 and 24 February.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot-Bellier2018126-47]

The Russian army advanced later in 1915. After Russia's withdrawal as a result of the 1917 [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt201594-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot2003138-49] about 5,000 Assyrian[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoohi-Kamali200376-50] and Armenian militia policed the area, but they frequently abused their power and killed Muslims without provocation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202078-51]



Armenian and Assyrian refugees in the avenue leading out of the Kurdish Gate, Urmia, July 1918

From February to July 1918, [the region was engulfed by ethnic violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_clashes).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot2003138–139-52][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202080-53] On 22 February, local Muslims and the Iranian governor began an uprising against the Christian militias in Urmia. The better-organized Christians, led by Agha Petros, brutally crushed the uprising; hundreds (possibly thousands) were killed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202078-51] On 16 March, Mar Shimun and many of his bodyguards were killed by the Kurdish chieftain [Simko Shikak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simko_Shikak), probably at the instigation of Iranian officials fearing Assyrian separatism, after they met to discuss an alliance. Assyrians went on a killing and looting spree; unable to find Simko, they murdered Iranian officials and inhabitants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoohi-Kamali200376–77-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202079-55] The Kurds responded by massacring Christians, regardless of denomination or ethnicity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202080-53] Christians were massacred in Salmas in June and in Urmia in early July,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot2003138–139-52] and many Assyrian women were abducted.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaby2017167-56]

Christian militias in Azerbaijan were no match for the Ottoman army when it invaded in July 1918.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202078-51] Tens of thousands of Ottoman and Iranian Assyrians fled south to [Hamadan](43610555.md), where the British [Dunsterforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunsterforce) was garrisoned, on 18 July to escape Ottoman forces approaching Urmia under [Ali İhsan Sâbis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_%C4%B0hsan_S%C3%A2bis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHellot-Bellier202017-57][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKévorkian2011744-58] The Ottoman invasion was followed by killings of Christians, including Chaldean archbishop [Toma Audo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toma_Audo), and the sacking of Urmia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoohi-Kamali200377-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt202080-53]

### 21st century

On 22 March 2025, large-scale demonstrations were held in the city. Slogans against Kurdistan were chanted during the demonstrations. [Anadolu Agency](43356543.md) reported that there was ethnic tension between Turks and Kurds in the city and that Turks were protesting the [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz) celebrations held by Kurds living in the city a few days ago. Some protesters stated that the Iranian government was trying to change the demographics by bringing Kurds to the city. It was reported that those who participated in the demonstration chanted Turkish slogans such as "Urumiyah is Turkish and will remain Turkish" and "Urumiyah's Turkish identity is not negotiable". 2 days after the protest, 22 people were arrested.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

## Demographics

### Ethnic composition



Assyrian Christian women praying in [Mart Maryam Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary_Church,_Urmia) in Urmia, [Iran](14653.md).

The city has been home to various [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups) during its history. In late 19th century, [George Curzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Curzon,_1st_Marquess_Curzon_of_Kedleston) reported a population of 30 to 40 thousand people, chiefly [Afshars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshar_people), Nestorians, Jews, and Armenians,[^cite_note-63] while other sources also referred to an additional Persian community.[^cite_note-64] At the beginning of the 20th century, the city had a significant Christian minority (Assyrians and Armenians).[^cite_note-Columbia-12][^cite_note-65] According to Macuch, and Ishaya, the city was the spiritual capital of the [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), who were influenced by four Christian missions that had been established in the city in the period from 1830 to the end of [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-Macuch-66] A large number of the Assyrians and Armenians were killed in 1914 during the [Armenian](140376.md) and [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_genocide) genocides,[^cite_note-67] which resulted in a change in the city's demographics.[^cite_note-Macuch-66] In the fourteenth edition of Encyclopædia Britannica from 1929, the town's population was roughly estimated to be 45 thousand before the war, mainly being Turkish with Armenian and Nestorian minorities.[^cite_note-68] During the era of [Reza Shah Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah_Pahlavi), Iranian Assyrians were invited to return to the region, and several thousand did return. There are around 5,000 Assyrians remaining in the city.[^cite_note-69]

Until the [Iran crisis of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946) and the [Establishment of the State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Establishment_of_the_State_of_Israel) in 1947, several thousand Jews also lived Urmia, and their language ([Lishán Didán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lish%C3%A1n_Did%C3%A1n)) is still spoken by an ageing community in [Israel](9282173.md).

According to the [Federal Research Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Research_Division) of [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress), ethnic Azeris form around 40% of the population of Urmia region.[^cite_note-70] The majority of the city's residents are Azerbaijanis, with a large minority of Kurds, and a smaller number of Assyrians, and Armenians, as well as Persian-speakers who moved to the city mostly for employment.[^cite_note-Sadeghi-71]

The majority of the population can speak the official language of Iran, [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), in addition to their own native tongue.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

### Religion



[Saint Mary Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary_Church,_Urmia): an ancient [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East) church located in the city of Urmia.



[Mar Toma church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thomas_Church,_Balowlan) near Urmia.

The city is the archiepiscopal see of the [Eastern Catholic](214903.md) Metropolitan [Chaldean Catholic Archeparchy of Urmyā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldean_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Urmy%C4%81), which has a [suffragan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_bishop) in [Salmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmas). There are also [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East) adherents and [Armenian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Orthodox). There are four churches in the central part of the city, two being Assyrian Church of the East, one Armenian, and one Chaldean.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

When 17th-century explorer [Evliya Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_%C3%87elebi) visited the region, the city's Muslim population was mostly Sunni and not yet converted to Shia Islam.[^cite_note-Çelebi-26] Around 1900, [Christians](18337522.md) made up more than 40% of the city's population; however, most of the Christians were either killed when the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) invaded [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and committed [genocide](12441.md) against Urmia's [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_genocide) and [Armenian](140376.md) population[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGaunt2006-77] or fled shortly after the end of the war. Approximately 15,000 [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Iran) reside in northern Iran, in Urmia and various Assyrian villages in the surrounding area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHooglund2008100–101-78] The [Christian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_history) of Urmia is well preserved and is especially evident in the city's many churches and cathedrals.

### Population

Urmia is the [10th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Iran_cities_by_population) city in [Iran](14653.md). At the time of the 2006 National Census, the city's population was 577,307 in 153,570 households.[^cite_note-2006_West_Azerbaijan_Province-79] The following census in 2011 counted 667,499 people in 197,749 households.[^cite_note-2011_West_Azerbaijan_Province-80] The 2016 census measured the population of the city as 736,224 people in 225,050 households.[^cite_note-2016_West_Azerbaijan_Province-3]

## Climate

Urmia's climate is [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSk*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *BS*), bordering on [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dsa*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dc*), with cold winters, mild springs, hot dry summers, and cool autumns. Precipitation is heavily concentrated in late autumn, winter (mostly in the form of snow), and especially spring, while precipitation is scarce in summer. Temperatures in Urmia are much colder than most of the remainder of Iran. The drought of [Urmia Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_Lake) will have a negative impact on the climate of the region.

Being on the [downwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downwind) and [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) side of the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains), its winters are relatively drier and less snowy than [Hakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakk%C3%A2ri_\(city\))'s (to the west) in southeastern Turkey due to the [foehn effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foehn_effect).[^cite_note-83]

Sports are an important part of Urmia's culture. The most popular sport in Urmia is volleyball. Urmia is considered Iran's volleyball capital, and that is because of the ranks that [Shahrdari Urmia VC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrdari_Urmia_VC) got in [Iranian Volleyball Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Volleyball_Super_League) and for the great volleyball players who play on the [Iran men's national volleyball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_men's_national_volleyball_team) (such as [Saed Marouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saed_Marouf), [Abdolreza Alizadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdolreza_Alizadeh), and [Milad Ebadipour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milad_Ebadipour)) and first-class coaches in Iran. Recently, Urmia has also been called "the city of volleyball lovers" by the [Fédération Internationale de Volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Internationale_de_Volleyball) (International Volleyball Federation, FIVB) official website.

The [2010 Asian Men's Cup Volleyball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Asian_Men's_Cup_Volleyball_Championship) was held in [Ghadir Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadir_Arena) in Urmia, [2012 WAFF Futsal Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_WAFF_Futsal_Championship), and the [2012 Asian Junior Men's Volleyball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Asian_Junior_Men's_Volleyball_Championship) was also held in Urmia. It is also one of the venues of the [2019 FIVB Volleyball Men's Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIVB_Volleyball_Men's_Nations_League).

## Culture



An Ashik music group



[Jameh Mosque of Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameh_Mosque_of_Urmia)

[Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis) hold festivals and ceremonies such as [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz) and [Eid al-Adha](358226.md) like other Iranian ethnic groups with small differences.[^cite_note-86] [Ashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashik) music is one of the features of the Turkish speaking people of the world. It has different versions in Iran. Meanwhile, as many experts of this art testify the Urmia Ashik, is the most original and oldest version in the world, which has preserved its origin until the present day. Ashik music has its unique styles. As a piece of the culture of Azerbaijan, Urmia Ashik music has been registered in Iran's national heritage.

### Higher education

Urmia was an important centre for [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) approximately a century ago; indeed, the medical college of Urmia, which was built by [Joseph Cochran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cochran) and a team of American medical associates in 1878, is the first modern university of Iran. Unfortunately, the college was shut down even before the establishment of the first official University of Iran, [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran). Today, Urmia has become an important centre of education, with several state and private universities and institutes, including those listed below.

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Most of Urmia's residents travel by car through the system of roads and [highways](48519.md). Urmia is also served by [taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) and public [buses](4146.md). There are also some private groups that provide services called "Phone-taxi." Two Tram-lines for Urmia are Planned.

Urmia is linked to [Europe](9239.md) through [Turkey](11125639.md)'s [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_32_\(Iran\)) and Sero border crossing. [Urmia Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_Airport), which opened in 1964, was the first international airport in [West Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Azerbaijan) county, [Iran](14653.md). As of April 2015 it only has regularly scheduled domestic flights to [Tehran](57654.md)'s [Mehrabad International Airport](2025041.md), although there are plans to establish a direct flight between Urmia and [Erbil](24534207.md), due to the large number of passengers travelling between the two cities.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] The city is recently connected to [Iran National Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_National_Railways) (IRIR, رجا).
