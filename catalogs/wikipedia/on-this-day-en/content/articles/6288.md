**Cambridgeshire** (abbreviated **Cambs.**) is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) in the [East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_England) and [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia). It is bordered by [Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire) to the north, [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk) to the north-east, [Suffolk](27886.md) to the east, [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex) and [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) to the south, [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire) to the west, and [Bedfordshire](69769.md) to the south-west. The city of [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough) is the largest settlement.

The county has an area of 3,389 km<sup>2</sup> (1,309 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 906,814 in 2022. Peterborough is in the north-west and the city of [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) in the south. The remainder of the county is rural, and contains the city of [Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Cambridgeshire) in the east, [Wisbech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisbech) in the north-east, and [St Neots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Neots) and [Huntingdon](250589.md) in the west. For [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes Cambridgeshire comprises a [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county), with five [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_England), and the [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) area of [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Peterborough); their local authorities collaborate through [Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_and_Peterborough_Combined_Authority). The county did not [historically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) include [Huntingdonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdonshire) or the [Soke of Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soke_of_Peterborough), which was part of Northamptonshire.

The north and east of the county are dominated by [the Fens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fens), an extremely flat, drained marsh maintained by drainage ditches and dykes; [Holme Fen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holme_Fen) is the UK's lowest physical point, at 2.75 m (9 ft) below sea level. The flatness of the landscape makes the few areas of higher ground, such as that Ely is built on, very conspicuous. The landscape in the south and west is gently undulating. Cambridgeshire's principal rivers are the [Nene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Nene), which flows through the north of the county and is [canalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canalised) east of Peterborough; the [Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse), which flows from west to east past Huntingdon and Ely; and the [Cam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Cam), a tributary of the Great Ouse which flows through Cambridge.[^cite_note-5]

## History

Main article: [History of Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cambridgeshire)

Cambridgeshire is noted as the site of [Flag Fen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Fen) in [Fengate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fengate), one of the earliest-known [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) permanent settlements in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), compared in importance to [Balbridie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balbridie) in Aberdeen, Scotland. [Must Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Must_Farm) quarry, at [Whittlesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittlesey), has been described as "Britain's [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) due to its relatively good condition, including the 'best-preserved [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) dwellings ever found in the UK'". A great quantity of [archaeological](18951655.md) finds from the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age), the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), and the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) were made in [East Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Cambridgeshire). Most items were found in [Isleham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isleham).[^cite_note-6]

The area was settled by the [Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons) starting in the fifth century. [Genetic testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_testing) on seven skeletons found in Anglo-Saxon era graves in [Hinxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinxton) and [Oakington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakington) found that five were either migrants or descended from migrants from the continent, one was a native Briton, and one had both continental and native ancestry, suggesting intermarriage.[^cite_note-7]



Map showing the historical administrative boundaries in the modern ceremonial county of Cambridgeshire. Historical administrative counties showed in the background, short-lived combined counties of 1965–1974 in red outlines, and modern county council areas, unitary authorities, and districts in black outlines.

Cambridgeshire was recorded in the *[Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book)* as "Grantbridgeshire" (or rather *Grentebriġesċīre*) (related to the [river Granta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Granta)). Covering a large part of [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia), Cambridgeshire today is the result of several local government unifications. In 1888 when [county councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_council) were introduced, separate councils were set up, following the traditional division of Cambridgeshire, for

- the area in the south around Cambridge, and

- the liberty of the [Isle of Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Ely).

In 1965, these two administrative counties were merged to form [Cambridgeshire and the Isle of Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_and_the_Isle_of_Ely).[^cite_note-8] Under the [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972) this merged with the county to the west, [Huntingdon and Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_and_Peterborough), which had been formed in 1965, by the merger of Huntingdonshire with the Soke of Peterborough (the latter previously a part of [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire) with its own county council). The resulting county was called simply Cambridgeshire.[^cite_note-9]

Since 1998, the [City of Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Peterborough) has been separately administered as a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) area. It is associated with Cambridgeshire for ceremonial purposes such as [Lieutenancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord-Lieutenant) and joint functions such as policing and the fire service.[^cite_note-10]

In 2002, the conservation charity [Plantlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantlife) unofficially designated Cambridgeshire's [county flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_flower) as the [Pasqueflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsatilla_vulgaris).[^cite_note-11]

The [Cambridgeshire Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_Regiment) (nicknamed the Fen Tigers), the county-based army unit, fought in the [Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_War) in South Africa, the First World War and Second World War.[^cite_note-12]

Due to the county's flat terrain and proximity to the continent, during the Second World War the military built many airfields here for [RAF Bomber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Bomber_Command), [RAF Fighter Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Fighter_Command), and the [USAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAAF). In recognition of this collaboration, the [Cambridge American Cemetery and Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_American_Cemetery_and_Memorial) is located in [Madingley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madingley).[^cite_note-13]

Most English counties have nicknames for their people, such as a "[Tyke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyke_\(dialect\))" from [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) and a "[Yellowbelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowbelly_\(Lincolnshire\))" from [Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire). The historical [nicknames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickname) for people from Cambridgeshire are "Cambridgeshire Camel"[^cite_note-14] or "Cambridgeshire Crane", the latter referring to the wildfowl that were once abundant in the Fens. The term "Fen Tigers" is sometimes used to describe the people who live and work in the Fens.[^cite_note-Pryor1991-15]

## Flag

Main article: [Flag of Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Cambridgeshire)



The flag of the county of Cambridgeshire

Cambridgeshire's county flag was selected as an entry from a design competition that ran during 2014.[^cite_note-16] The design features three golden crowns, two on the top, one on the bottom that are separated by two wavy lines in the middle. The crowns are meant to represent East Anglia, and the two lines represent the [River Cam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Cam) and are in Cambridge University's colours.[^cite_note-17]

## Geography

See also: [Geology of Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Cambridgeshire)



Hand-drawn map of Northampshire, Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire, Huntingdonshire and Rutland by Christopher Saxton from 1576

Large areas of the county are extremely low-lying and [Holme Fen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holme_Fen) is notable for being the UK's lowest physical point at 2.75 m (9 ft) below sea level. The highest point of the modern administrative county is in the village of [Great Chishill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chishill) at 146 m (480 ft) above sea level. However, this parish was historically a part of [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex), having been moved to Cambridgeshire in boundary changes in 1895. The historic county top is close to the village of [Castle Camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Camps_\(village\)) where a point on the disused RAF airfield reaches a height of 128 metres (420 ft) above sea level (grid reference TL 63282 41881).

Other [prominent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_prominence) hills are [Little Trees Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Trees_Hill) and [Wandlebury Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandlebury_Hill) (both at 74 m (243 ft)) in the [Gog Magog Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gog_Magog_Hills), [Rivey Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivey_Hill) above [Linton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linton,_Cambridgeshire), [Rowley's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowley's_Hill) and the Madingley Hills.

[Wicken Fen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicken_Fen) is a 254.5-hectare (629-acre) biological [Site of Special Scientific Interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_of_Special_Scientific_Interest) west of [Wicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicken,_Cambridgeshire). A large part of it is owned and managed by the [National Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust_for_Places_of_Historic_Interest_or_Natural_Beauty).[^cite_note-18]

The [Cambridge Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Green_Belt) around the city of [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) extends to places such as [Waterbeach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterbeach), [Lode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lode,_Cambridgeshire), [Duxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duxford), [Little & Great Abington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Abingtons,_Cambridgeshire) and other communities a few miles away in nearby districts, to afford a protection from the conurbation. It was first drawn up in the 1950s.

### Proposed local government reorganisation

Main article: [Upcoming structural changes to local government in England § Cambridgeshire and Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England#Cambridgeshire_and_Peterborough)

As of 2026, there are four separate proposals for local government in Cambridgeshire and Peterborough to be reorganised into two new [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England), which would replace the existing structure of the county council and six districts (including Peterborough). Government consultation on these four proposals ran from 5 February 2026 to 26 March 2026.[^cite_note-Cambridgeshire_Peterborough_consultation-20]

## Education

### Primary and secondary

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of schools in Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Cambridgeshire).

Cambridgeshire has a comprehensive education system with over 240 state schools, not including [sixth form colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form_college). The independent sector includes [King's Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Ely) and [Wisbech Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisbech_Grammar_School), founded in 970 and 1379 respectively, they are two of the oldest schools in the country.[^cite_note-26]

Some of the secondary schools act as [Village Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_College), institutions unique to Cambridgeshire. For example, [Comberton Village College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comberton_Village_College).

### Tertiary

Cambridgeshire is home to a number of institutes of [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education):

- The [University of Cambridge](25978572.md) – [second-oldest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation) university in the English-speaking world, and regarded as one of the most prestigious academic institutions in the world

- [Anglia Ruskin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglia_Ruskin_University) – has [campuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus) located in Cambridge and Peterborough and a base at [Fulbourn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbourn)

- The [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) – has a regional centre located in Cambridge

- The [University Centre Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Centre_Peterborough) – operated by Anglia Ruskin University and [Peterborough Regional College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_Regional_College), located in Peterborough

- The [College of West Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_West_Anglia) has a campus at Milton, on the northern outskirts of Cambridge and a campus at [Wisbech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisbech).

In addition, [Cambridge Regional College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Regional_College) and [Huntingdonshire Regional College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdonshire_Regional_College) both offer a limited range of higher education courses in conjunction with partner universities.

Cambridgeshire has a maritime temperate climate which is broadly similar to the rest of the United Kingdom, though it is drier than the UK average due to its low altitude and easterly location, the prevailing southwesterly winds having already deposited moisture on higher ground further west. Average winter temperatures are cooler than the English average, due to Cambridgeshire's inland location and relative nearness to continental Europe, which results in the moderating maritime influence being less strong. Snowfall is slightly more common than in western areas, due to the relative winter coolness and easterly winds bringing occasional snow from the North Sea. In summer temperatures are average or slightly above, due to less cloud cover. It reaches 25 °C (77 °F) on around ten days each year, and is comparable to parts of Kent and East Anglia.

## Culture

### Sports

Various [forms of football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football) have been popular in Cambridgeshire since medieval times at least. In 1579 one match played at [Chesterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterton,_Cambridge) between townspeople and [University of Cambridge](25978572.md) students ended in a violent brawl that led the Vice-Chancellor to issue a decree forbidding them to play "footeball" outside of college grounds.[^cite_note-36] During the nineteenth century, several formulations of the laws of football, known as the [Cambridge rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_rules), were created by students at the university. One of these codes, dating from 1863, had a significant influence on the creation of the original [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_the_Game_\(association_football\)) of the [Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association).[^cite_note-37]

Cambridgeshire is also the birthplace of [bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy),[^cite_note-\:0-38] now an IOC accepted sport.[^cite_note-39] According to documents from 1813, [Bury Fen Bandy Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_Fen_Bandy_Club) was undefeated for 100 years. A member of the club, [Charles Goodman Tebbutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Goodman_Tebbutt), wrote down the first official rules in 1882.[^cite_note-\:0-38] Tebbutt was instrumental in spreading the sport to many countries.[^cite_note-40] [Great Britain Bandy Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_Bandy_Association) is based in Cambridgeshire.[^cite_note-41]

[Fen skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fen_skating) is a traditional form of skating in the [Fenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fens). The [National Ice Skating Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ice_Skating_Association) was set up in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) in 1879, they took the top Fen skaters to the world speedskating championships where [James Smart (skater)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Smart_\(skater\)) became world champion.[^cite_note-42]

On 6–7 June 2015, the inaugural Tour of Cambridgeshire cycle race took place on closed roads across the county. The event was an official [UCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Cycliste_Internationale) qualification event, and consisted of a Time Trial on the 6th, and a [Gran Fondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Fondo) event on the 7th. The Gran Fondo event was open to the public, and over 6000 riders took part in the 128 km (80 mi) race.[^cite_note-43]

The [River Cam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Cam) is the main river flowing through Cambridge, parts of the [River Nene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Nene) and [River Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse) lie within the county. In 2021 the latter was used as the course for [The Boat Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boat_Race). The [River Cam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Cam) serves as the course for the university [Lent Bumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent_Bumps) and [May Bumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Bumps) and the non-college [rowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing) organised by [Cambridgeshire Rowing Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_Rowing_Association).

There is only one racecourse in Cambridgeshire, located at [Huntingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_Racecourse).

### Contemporary art

Cambridge is home to the [Kettle's Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle's_Yard) gallery and the artist-run Aid and Abet project space. Nine miles west of Cambridge next to the village of [Bourn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourn) is [Wysing Arts Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wysing_Arts_Centre).[^cite_note-44] Wisbech has been home to the Wisbech Gallery, South Brink since 2023. Cambridge Open Studios is the region's large arts organisation with over 500 members. Every year, more than 370 artists open their doors to visitors during four weekends in July.[^cite_note-45]

### Literature

The annual Fenland Poet Laureate awards were instigated for poets in the North of the county in 2012 at [Wisbech & Fenland Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisbech_&_Fenland_Museum).[^cite_note-46]

### Theatre

The county was visited by travelling companies of comedians in the Georgian period. These came from different companies. The Lincoln Circuit included, at various times, Wisbech and Whittlesey. The Wisbech Georgian theatre still survives as an operating theatre now known as The [Angles Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_Theatre). In Cambridge the [ADC Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADC_Theatre) is the venue for the [Footlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footlights).
