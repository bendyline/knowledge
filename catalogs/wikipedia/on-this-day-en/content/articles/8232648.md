The **Upper Galilee** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): הגליל העליון, *HaGalil Ha'Elyon*; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجليل الأعلى, *Al Jaleel Al A'alaa*) is a geographical region located in northern [Israel](9282173.md) and [southern Lebanon](4205968.md). Part of the larger [Galilee](12639.md) region, it is characterized by its higher elevations and mountainous terrain. The term "Upper Galilee" is ancient, and has been in use since the end of the [Second Temple period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple_period). From a political perspective, the Upper Galilee is situated within the administrative boundaries of the [Northern District](324281.md) of Israel.

The Upper Galilee is known for its natural beauty, including lush landscapes, [Mediterranean forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_forests,_woodlands,_and_scrub), and scenic [vistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sightline_\(architecture\)). Significant natural sites include [Nahal Amud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Amud) and the [Keshet Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshet_Cave). It is also an area where [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyards) and [wineries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wineries) thrive, producing quality wines. [Mount Meron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Meron) stands as the highest point in the area, reaching an elevation of 1,208 m (3,963 ft) above sea level. [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) is a main city in the region and also hosts an [artists' quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Quarter_of_Safed) that was a major center of [Israeli art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts_in_Israel).[^cite_note-\:02-1][^cite_note-\:3-2]



A street in the [artists' quarter of Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Quarter_of_Safed). Safed is the major Israeli city in the region.

Although historical definitions encompass parts of southern Lebanon within the boundaries of the Upper Galilee, the Lebanese government does not use the term "Galilee" for any part of its territory.

## Boundaries

Originally, the term "Upper Galilee" referred to a larger region, encompassing the mountainous regions of what is today northern Israel and [southern Lebanon](4205968.md). The boundaries of this region were the [Litani River](1920925.md) in the north, the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) in the west, the [Lower Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Galilee) in the south (from which it is separated by the [Beit HaKerem Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_HaKerem_Valley)), and the upper [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River) and the [Hula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley) in the east.[^cite_note-ariel-3]

According to the first century Jewish historian [Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus), the bounds of Upper Galilee stretched from [Bersabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bersabe) in the Beit HaKerem Valley to [Baca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baca_\(Galilee\)) (Peki'in) in the north, and from Meloth to [Thella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thella).[^cite_note-4] The extent of this region is approximately 470 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-5]

In present-day usage, the [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy) mainly refers only to the northern part of the [Galilee](12639.md) that is under Israeli sovereignty. That is, the term today does not include the portion of [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md) up to the Litani River, or the corresponding stretches of the [Israeli Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Coastal_Plain) to the west, or the [Jordan Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Rift_Valley) to the east. These are considered to be separate geographical areas that are not part of “Upper Galilee.”

## History

See also: [Galilee § history](12639.md#history)

### Ancient times

In the late 2nd century BCE, the Upper Galilee was conquered by [Hasmonean Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_Judea), under either King [Judah Aristobulus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_Aristobulus_I) or his brother and successor, [Alexander Jannaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Jannaeus). The change of hands is archaeologically attested at the [Qeren Naftali fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qeren_Naftali_fortress), which was originally a Hellenistic fort but became the site of a Jewish garrison following the conquest.[^cite_note-6]

The [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) period saw growing population density in the Upper Galilee. The western part was home to Christian agricultural villages, some possibly [monasteries](45856.md) or monastic farms, many of which today lie hidden beneath forest overgrowth, while the eastern part, beyond Mount Meron, had a Jewish population. [Olive oil production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil_extraction) was an important part of the local economy; some surviving stands of wild olive trees may be remnants of Byzantine-era orchards.[^cite_note-7]

The Upper Galilee is home to numerous significant [ancient synagogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_synagogues_in_Israel), dating from [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and Byzantine times. Those include the synagogues of [Bar'am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Bar'am_synagogue),[^cite_note-8] [Nabratein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabratein_synagogue),[^cite_note-9] [Gush Halav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Halav),[^cite_note-10] [Huqoq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huqoq),[^cite_note-11] [Chorazin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorazin),[^cite_note-NegevGibson2005-12] [Meron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meron,_Israel#Classical_Antiquity)[^cite_note-13] and a few others. Remains of synagogues has also been found in [Qision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qision)[^cite_note-\:1-14] and [Alma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma,_Safad).[^cite_note-\:0-15]

Following the dissolution of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration) in which the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) promised to create "A Jewish National Home" in Mandatory Palestine, the [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist) Movement presented to the [Versailles Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_Peace_Conference) a document calling for including in the [British Mandate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) the entire territory up to the [Litani river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litani_river)—with a view to this becoming eventually part of a future Jewish state.

However, only less than half this area was actually included in British Mandatory Palestine, the final border being influenced both by diplomatic maneuverings and struggles between Britain and France and by fighting on the ground, especially the March 1920 battle of [Tel Hai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Hai).

For a considerable time after the border was defined so to make the northern portion of the territory concerned part of the French mandated territory that became Lebanon, many Zionist geographers—and Israeli geographers in the state's early years—continued to speak of "The Upper Galilee" as being "the northern sub-area of the [Galilee](12639.md) region of [Israel](9282173.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md)".

Under this definition, "The Upper Galilee" covers an area spreading over 1,500 km<sup>2</sup>, about 700 in Israel and the rest in Lebanon. This included the highland region of [Belad Bechara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belad_Bechara) in [Jabal Amel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amel) located in [South Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon),[^cite_note-16] which was at for some time known in Hebrew as "The Lebanese Galilee".[^cite_note-ariel-3] As defined in geographical terms, "it is separated from the [Lower Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Galilee) by the Beit HaKerem valley; its mountains are taller and valleys are deeper than those in the [Lower Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Galilee); its tallest peak is [Har Meron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Har_Meron) at 1,208 m above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) is one of the major cities in this region".

In recent decades, however, this usage has virtually disappeared from the general Israeli discourse, the term "Upper Galilee" being used solely in reference to the part located in Israel.

## In art

See also: [Artists Quarter of Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Quarter_of_Safed)

The Upper Galilee region, specifically the city of [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) was a centre of Israeli and Jewish art since 1920 when the [School of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Paris) painter, [Yitzhak Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Frenkel) visited the region and was the first to settle in Safed where he later established an art school.[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:82-18] In Safed was established the [Artists' quarter of Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Quarter_of_Safed). Safed harbored mysticism and was reminiscent of picturesque villages, which drew artists such as Frenkel, [Marc Chagall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Chagall), [Moshe Castel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Castel), [Mordechai Levanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordechai_Levanon) and others.[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-\:32-19][^cite_note-FRENKEL_FRENEL_MUSEUM-20] Artists were drawn to the upper galilee due to its mountainous landscapes and "mysticsm". Prior to the air conditioner, Safed also attracted artists due to its climatic conditions which were more comfortable than Tel Aviv during the summer.[^cite_note-\:3-2] Safed also offered different themes for the artists, from the Klezmer musicians to the Hasidim and the city itself.[^cite_note-\:32-19][^cite_note-\:02-1] [Mount Meron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Meron) was also an object in artworks by Jewish artists with artists such as Frenkel and [Rolly Schaffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolly_Schaffer) painting serene or fiery scenes of sunset over the mountain.[^cite_note-\:32-19]
