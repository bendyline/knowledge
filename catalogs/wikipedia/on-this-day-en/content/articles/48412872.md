**MarineTraffic** is a maritime analytics provider, which provides real-time information on the movements of ships and the current location of ships in harbors and ports.[^cite_note-MarineInsight-2] A database of information on the vessels includes for example details of the location where they were built plus dimensions of the vessels, gross tonnage and [International Maritime Organisation (IMO) number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMO_number). Users can submit photographs of the vessels which other users can rate.

The basic MarineTraffic service can be used without cost; more advanced functions such as satellite-based tracking are available subject to payment.[^cite_note-3]

The site has six million unique visitors on a monthly basis. In April 2015, the service had 600,000 registered users.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-5]

## History

MarineTraffic was originally developed as an academic project at the [University of the Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Aegean) in [Ermoupoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ermoupoli), [Greece](12108.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

In late 2007, Professor Dimitris Lekkas published it as a trial version.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

In 2022, MarineTraffic made available a free [AIS](1825702.md) processing toolbox under a Creative Commons license.[^cite_note-8]

In February 2023, Belgian data and analytics firm [Kpler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kpler?action=edit\&redlink=1) announced the acquisition of MarineTraffic and Fleetmon for an undisclosed sum.[^cite_note-9] The acquisitions closed in March 2023.[^cite_note-10]
