[Formula One](10854.md), abbreviated to F1, is the highest class of [open-wheeled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_wheel_car) [auto racing](1022.md) defined by the [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile](248019.md) (FIA), motorsport's world governing body.[^cite_note-1] The "formula" in the name refers to a set of rules to which all participants and cars must conform.[^cite_note-2] The [F1 World Championship season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_seasons) consists of a series of races, known as [Grands Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_Grands_Prix), held usually on purpose-built [circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_circuits), and in a few cases on [closed city streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_circuit).[^cite_note-3] The polesitter is the driver that has qualified for a [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_Grands_Prix) in [pole position](630713.md), at the front of the starting grid.[^cite_note-4] Out of the 1,164 completed Grands Prix (as of the [2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Azerbaijan_Grand_Prix)), the driver that has started in first has gone on to win the race 507 times.[^cite_note-5]

Qualifying is traditionally contested on the Saturday (Friday for some events) of a Grand Prix weekend to determine the drivers' positions on the starting [grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_motorsport_terms#G) of the race held on the Sunday (although across [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Formula_One_World_Championship), in a small number of the events, so-called sprint races were held, which determined the starting grid of the race on Sunday.[^cite_note-SkySprintFormat2023-6] In 2021, the winner of the sprint was credited with pole position.[^cite_note-7] In 2022, the pole-sitter of the sprint race was officially awarded the pole position in those events.[^cite_note-8] Since 2023, sprint races have been held at select events, these have had their own qualifying session, the polesitter for these races is not credited with a pole position.)[^cite_note-SkySprintFormat2023-6][^cite_note-9] Historically, there have been a number of different qualifying systems; previously, each driver was only allowed a single lap to set his qualifying time.[^cite_note-10] Drivers currently have to compete in three rounds before pole position is determined. The first round, known as Q1, is contested by twenty drivers in an 18-minute session, at the end of which the five slowest cars are eliminated. This is followed by Q2, a 15-minute session, where the slowest five are again eliminated. The remaining ten cars contest Q3, the final 13-minute session, to determine their places on the grid and who will sit on pole position.[^cite_note-11]

[Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton) holds the record for the most pole positions,[^cite_note-12] having qualified first on 104 occasions.[^cite_note-PoleNoStatsF1-13] [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher) is second with 68 pole positions.[^cite_note-Schumi-14] [Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) is third with 65 poles.[^cite_note-PoleNoStatsF1-13] Senna and [Max Verstappen](41758713.md) jointly hold the [record for most consecutive pole positions](13599687.md#Most_consecutive_pole_positions); Senna qualified in first place in eight Grands Prix in a row from the [1988 Spanish Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Spanish_Grand_Prix) to the [1989 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_United_States_Grand_Prix), with Verstappen's record streak lasting from the [2023 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Abu_Dhabi_Grand_Prix) to the [2024 Emilia Romagna Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Emilia_Romagna_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] [Kimi Antonelli](70530918.md) is the youngest polesitter; he was 19 years, 201 days old when he qualified in first place for the [2026 Chinese Grand Prix](80284337.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-19] The oldest person to qualify in pole position was [Nino Farina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nino_Farina), who was 47 years, 79 days old when he was polesitter for the [1954 Argentine Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Argentine_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-20] As of the [2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Azerbaijan_Grand_Prix), 109 drivers have been on pole position in the 1,164 Grands Prix since the first [World Championship race](10854.md), the [1950 British Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_British_Grand_Prix), with the most recent driver to achieve their first pole position being [Pierre Gasly](40843850.md) at the [2026 Italian Grand Prix](81006624.md).[^cite_note-PoleNoStatsF1-13][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] From 2014 to 2017, the driver with the most pole positions in a season was awarded the [Pole Trophy](630713.md#Pole_position_trophy).[^cite_note-23] The inaugural Pole Trophy was won by [Nico Rosberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Rosberg).[^cite_note-24] In 2018, the FIA Pole Trophy was discontinued and replaced with the [Pirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli) Pole Position Award, where the polesitter at each race is awarded a Pirelli wind tunnel tyre with the name of the polesitter and their time.[^cite_note-25]

Chart showing the progression of the record for most Formula One pole positions

Progression of the record for most Formula One pole positions

Photo of Lewis Hamilton driving a silver Mercedes on a racetrack

Seven-time world champion [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton) holds the [record for most pole positions](13599687.md#Pole_positions), and for the [most wins from pole](13599687.md#Wins_from_pole_position).

Photo of Michael Schumacher driving a red Ferrari on a race track

Seven-time world champion [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher) achieved 68 pole positions, 10 with [Benetton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Formula) and 58 with [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari).

Photo of Ayrton Senna driving a red and white McLaren on a race track

[Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) was known for consistently high qualifying performance.

Photo of Sebastian Vettel driving a blue Red Bull car on a race track

[Sebastian Vettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Vettel) achieved 44 of his pole position tally with [Red Bull Racing](1173193.md), for whom he also won four consecutive titles.

Black and white photo of Jim Clark driving a car on a race track

[Jim Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Clark) held the record for most F1 pole positions for 22 years (1967–1989).

Photo of Juan Manuel Fangio driving a silver Mercedes on a race track

[Juan Manuel Fangio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Fangio) is a five-time world champion, and holds the [record for the highest percentage of pole positions](13599687.md#Pole_positions) (qualified on pole in 56% of races entered).

Photo of Lewis Hamilton wearing a red cap and silver overalls holding his arms aloft with a yellow helmet in his left hand

Lewis Hamilton raises Ayrton Senna's helmet, offered by the latter's family on matching the Brazilian driver's record tally of pole positions at the [2017 Canadian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Canadian_Grand_Prix).



[Kimi Antonelli](70530918.md) is the youngest first-time polesitter, having secured his first pole position at the [2026 Chinese Grand Prix](80284337.md) at the age of 19 years, 201 days.



[Pierre Gasly](40843850.md) is the 109th and latest first-time polesitter, having secured pole position at the [2026 Italian Grand Prix](81006624.md).
