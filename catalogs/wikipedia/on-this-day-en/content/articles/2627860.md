The **Legal system of Argentina** is a [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_system). The pillar of the civil system is the [Constitution of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Argentina) (1853).

The [Argentine Constitution of 1853](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Constitution_of_1853) was an attempt to unite the unstable and young country of the [United Provinces of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) under a single law, creating, as well, the various institutions needed to run a country. This constitution was finally approved after failed attempts in 1813 (see [Assembly of 1813](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asamblea_del_A%C3%B1o_XIII)), 1819, and 1831 ([Pacto Federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacto_Federal)).

[Civil Code of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Code_of_Argentina)

The first Civil Code was written by Argentine [jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist) [Dalmacio Vélez Sársfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmacio_V%C3%A9lez_S%C3%A1rsfield), and came into effect on 1 January 1871 and remained law until 1 August 2015, when it was replaced by a new Civil and Commercial Code - *Código Civil y Comercial de la Nación*.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

The 1871 Argentine Civil Code was largely inspired by the [Spanish](26667.md) legal tradition, and also by the [Brazilian](3383.md) Civil Code, the Spanish Civil Code of 1851, the [Napoleonic Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Code), and the [Chilean Civil Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Code_\(Chile\)). The sources of this Civil Code also include various theoretical legal works, mainly of the great French jurists of the 19th century. It was the first civil law to adopt, as its cornerstone, consciously, the distinction between rights and obligations and between real property rights, thus distancing itself from the French model.

The new *Código Civil y Comercial de la Nación*[^cite_note-3] brings many changes, in particular the modernization of [family law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_law).[^cite_note-4]
