**Davao Oriental** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Sidlakang Dabaw*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Silangang Davao*), officially the **Province of Davao Oriental** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Lalawigan sa Sidlakang Dabaw, Lalawigan sa Davao Oriental*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lalawigan ng Silangang Davao, Lalawigan ng Davao Oriental*), is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Davao Region](296162.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md). Its capital is the city of [Mati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mati,_Davao_Oriental) which is the most populous, and it borders the province of [Davao de Oro](296993.md) to the west, and [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur) and [Surigao del Sur](267469.md) to the north. The province is the traditional homeland of the Mandaya and [Kalagan/Kaagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_people).

Davao Oriental is the easternmost province in the country with Pusan Point in the municipality of [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga,_Davao_Oriental) as the easternmost location. The [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea), part of the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), faces Davao Oriental to the east. Part of the province lies on an unnamed peninsula that encloses [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) to the west.

The province is the top producer of [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) and [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra) in the country, earning the province the title *Coconut Capital of the Philippines*.[^cite_note-PIA-1][^cite_note-MindaNews-2] It is also home to [Mount Hamiguitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hamiguitan) Wildlife Sanctuary, a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).

## Etymology

The name of the province was derived from the word, Dabaw, of which the center was west of the province.

## History

### Early history

Further information: [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao)

The present territory of Davao Oriental was covered by the historical region of Caraga, which was partly under the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao).

### Spanish colonial era

The Spaniards established a permanent settlement in the region in 1591, and in 1844, the area was ceded by the Sultanate to Spain. An expedition was sent by the colonial government in Manila in 1847 to control the region, led by the Spaniard [Jose Oyanguren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Oyanguren?action=edit\&redlink=1), who settled in the [town of Davao](489401.md) and organized the surrounding areas into the province of [Nueva Guipozcoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Guipozcoa) in 1849. The province existed until 1858, when the politico-military *commandancia*s of Davao and Bislig were created in its place. Bislig was incorporated into the District of Davao two years later.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-DavaoOriental-7]

### American colonial era

Further information: [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province), [Department of Mindanao and Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mindanao_and_Sulu), and [Davao (province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\))



[Davao province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\)) in 1918 encompassing the current provinces of [Davao Region](296162.md)

In 1903, the colonial American government created the [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) comprising several districts, one of which was Davao. The Moro province was converted in 1914 into the [Department of Mindanao and Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mindanao_and_Sulu), and its component districts were made into independent provinces.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-DavaoOriental-7]

### Japanese occupation

In 1942, the territory of what is now Davao Oriental was [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_\(1941%E2%80%931942\)) by the [Japanese Imperial forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_forces).

In 1945, the [Battle of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Davao) began with the combined forces of the British, Australian, Dutch and the Filipino troops of the 6th, 101st, 102nd, 103rd, 104th, 106th, 107th and 110th Infantry Division of the [Philippine Commonwealth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) and 10th Infantry Regiment of the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) being supported by Davaoeño guerrilla fighter units in attacking Japanese Imperial forces during the [liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_Campaign_\(1944%E2%80%9345\)) in Eastern Davao or Eastern Davao Campaign until the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

### Philippine independence

#### Foundation

On May 8, 1967, through *[Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) No. 4867*, the [province of Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\)) was divided into three independent provinces, one of which was Davao Oriental.[^cite_note-ChanRobles-RA-8][^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-DavaoOriental-7] Former president [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) appointed Paciano Bangoy as governor and Leopoldo Lopez as vice governor on June 2.[^cite_note-9]

### Contemporary

The capital town of [Mati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mati,_Davao_Oriental) became a component city by virtue of *Republic Act No. 9408* which sought to convert the municipality into a city. The law was ratified on June 16, 2007. However, the cityhood status was lost twice in the years 2008 and 2010 after the [LCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Cities_of_the_Philippines) questioned the validity of the cityhood law. The cityhood status was reaffirmed after the [court](1335534.md) finalized its ruling on February 15, 2011 which declared the cityhood law constitutional.

## Geography



Eastern coast showing [Pujada Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pujada_Bay)

Davao Oriental covers a total area of 5,679.64 square kilometres (2,192.92 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-10] occupying the eastern tip of the [Davao Region](296162.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md). The province is bordered by [Davao de Oro](296993.md) to the west, [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur) and [Surigao del Sur](267469.md) to the north, [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) to the southwest, and the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea) to the east and southeast.

The [Eastern Pacific Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Pacific_Cordillera) mountain range encompasses the province. To the east of the range lie narrow strips of coastal areas, which feature several inlets and bays.[^cite_note-ThePhilippineIslandWorld-11][^cite_note-HumanHelminthiases-12] The province encloses [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf) to the southwest.

### Climate

Davao Oriental has a pronounced rainy season and a short dry season, with maximum rainfall occurring from November to January especially at coastal areas.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-DavaoOriental-7]

The population of Davao Oriental in the 2024 census was 590,042 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–11-5] with a density of 100 inhabitants per square kilometre or 260 inhabitants per square mile.

The province is mostly inhabited by [Cebuanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people), who settled the province in the early 20th century from Visayas. Other ethnic groups include [Bicolanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicolano_people), [Hiligaynons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people), [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people), [Tagalogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people) and [Warays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_people), as well as the indigenous [Mandayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaya), [Mansakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansaka), [Manobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo) and the [Kalagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_people), who are all natives in Davao Oriental.[^cite_note-FastFactsPhilProv-DavaoOriental-7]

While the native languages spoken in the province are [Kalagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalagan_language), [Mandaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaya_language), and [Davaoeño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davaoe%C3%B1o_language). [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) is the dominant first language. [Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano_language) is spoken by a minority, while [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) are widely used in business, education, and government. [Bikol Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikol_Central) is also spoken in parts of Mati.

Even some of the natives adhere to [Christian](5211.md) faith (mostly Catholics), they are syncretic in some of their beliefs. Muslim Kagans traditionally inhabited the plains and coastal areas of the Municipalities of Banaybanay, Lupon, San Isidro, Governor Generoso, Mati and Tarragona.

Davao Oriental is the top coconut and *[copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra)* (dried coconut meat) producer in the Philippines.[^cite_note-PIA-1] It also is a major producer of [abacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abac%C3%A1), and exports crude oil and copra pellets.[^cite_note-MindaNews-2]

On March 20, 2018, Davao Oriental signed a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with Pionaire Finance Limited, a Hong Kong-based foreign firm for a \$27-billion industrial park.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

[^cite_note-MindaNews-2]: &#x20;["Davao Oriental Gov Malanyaon joins GMA in Spain"](http://www.mindanews.com/mailbox/2007/11/30/davao-oriental-gov-malanyaon-joins-gma-in-spain/). *Mindanao News and Information Cooperative Center*. As the "coconut capital" of the Philippines, Davao Oriental produces one billion nuts annually covering a total crop area of 154,000 hectares. The major products exported by the province through the International Copra Exporter's Corporation are crude oil and copra pellets. Records show that export of 816,863 metric tons of crude oil and pellets from 1997 to 2001 is valued at \$185.7 million. Davao Oriental is also the top producer of abaca fiber in Southern Mindanao. A total of 7,110 hectares is planted to abaca with production volume of 7,500 metric tons. The provincial government says that another 200,000 hectares is being targeted for the expansion of abaca production. Abaca plantations are located in the municipalities of Manay, Baganga, Cateel, Boston, Tarragona, Lupon, Banaybanay and in the City of Mati, the province's capital. Recently, the province started to plant banana for export particularly in the municipality of Lupon, San Isidro, Governor Generoso and Mati.
