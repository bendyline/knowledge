**British Jews** (often referred to collectively as **British Jewry** or **Anglo-Jewry**) are [citizens of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_subject) who are [Jewish](25955086.md), one of the country's [ethnoreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnoreligious_group) minority communities. Jewish presence in Britain dates to at least the 11th century, when [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) encouraged Jewish merchants and financiers to migrate from northern [France](5843419.md) to [London](17867.md). The Jews were [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Expulsion) from England under [Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) in 1290, but a continuous community was re-established following the [readmission of Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resettlement_of_the_Jews_in_England) under [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) in the 1650s.[^cite_note-5] From the 18th century onwards, British Jews became increasingly integrated into national life, contributing to commerce, science, literature, and politics while maintaining distinctive religious and communal institutions. Some 60,000-70,000 British Jews served in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Second_World_War) during the [Second World War](32927.md), serving in all branches and theatres of war. Prominent British Jews have included [Prime Minister](24150.md) [Benjamin Disraeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli); [financier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investor) and activist [Moses Montefiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Montefiore); scientist [Rosalind Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Franklin); playwright [Harold Pinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Pinter); businessman [Michael Marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Marks); and philosopher Sir [Isaiah Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Berlin).

Waves of migration from [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) in the late 19th and early 20th centuries transformed Jewish life in Britain, particularly in [London](17867.md)’s [East End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_of_London), [Manchester](20206.md), and [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds), substantially increasing the community's numbers while altering its cultural composition. The community has since diversified, including both long-established Anglo-Jewish families and more recent arrivals from [Europe](9239.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), and the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).

According to the [2021 UK census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_United_Kingdom_censuses), 277,613 people identified as Jewish, representing about 0.4% of the total population. The majority of British Jews live in and around [London](17867.md), which is home to about two-thirds of the national Jewish population. The largest concentrations are in the boroughs of [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Barnet), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow,_London), and [Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackney,_London), and in nearby [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) suburbs such as [Borehamwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borehamwood), [Bushey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushey), and [Elstree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elstree), which together form one of the largest Jewish urban areas in Europe. The second major centre is [Greater Manchester](79819.md), particularly [Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salford), [Prestwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestwich), and [Whitefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitefield,_Greater_Manchester), where a rapidly growing [Haredi](53835.md) community has developed alongside long-established congregations. Smaller but historic Jewish populations remain in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds), [Gateshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateshead), [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), [Liverpool](18081.md), and [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham).

## History

Main articles: [History of the Jews in England (1066–1290)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_England_\(1066%E2%80%931290\)), [History of the Jews in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_England), [History of the Jews in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Scotland), [History of the Jews in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Wales), and [History of the Jews in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Northern_Ireland)

The first recorded Jewish community in [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) was brought to [England](9316.md) in 1070 by King [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_England) who believed the Jewish population's commercial skills would make his newly won country more prosperous. At the end of the 12th century, a series of [blood libels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_libels) and fatal [pogroms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogroms) were perpetrated in England, particularly on the east coast. On 16 March 1190, during the run up to the [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade), the [Jewish population of York was massacred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Castle#York_Massacre_of_1190) at the site where [Clifford's Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford's_Tower) now stands.[^cite_note-6]

In 1275, King [Edward I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) passed the [Statute of the Jewry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_the_Jewry) (*Statutum de Judaismo*). This restricted the community's business activities, outlawing the practice of [usury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usury) (charging interest).[^cite_note-Prestwich-7] Fifteen years later, finding that many of these provisions were ignored, Edward [expelled the Jews from England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Expulsion). The Jewish population [emigrated to countries such as Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) which [protected them by law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Kalisz). A small English community persisted in hiding despite the expulsion. Jews were not banned from [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Scotland), which was an independent kingdom until 1707; however, there is no record of a Jewish presence in Scotland before the 18th century. Jews were also not banned in Wales at the time, but England eventually annexed Wales under [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII). When Henry VIII's England annexed Wales, the English ban on Jews extended to Wales. There is only one known record of a Jew in Wales between 1290 and the annexation, but it is possible individuals did persist there after 1290.

A small community of [conversos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversos) was identified in Bristol in 1609 and banished. In 1656, [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) made it clear that the ban on Jewish settlement in England and Wales would no longer be enforced, but when Rabbi [Manasseh Ben Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasseh_Ben_Israel) brought a petition to allow Jews to return, the majority of [the Protectorate Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protectorate) turned it down. Despite the Protectorate government's rejection of the Rabbi's petition, the community considers 1656 to mark the readmission of the Jews to England and Wales. In mid-nineteenth century British-ruled Ireland, [Daniel O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_O'Connell), known as "The Liberator" for his work on [Catholic Emancipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Emancipation), worked successfully for [the repeal of the "De Judaismo" law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ireland), which prescribed a special [yellow badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_badge) for Jews.[^cite_note-8] [Benjamin Disraeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli) (1804–1881), of Jewish birth although he joined the [Church of England](5955.md), served in government for three decades, twice as [prime minister](24150.md).

The oldest Jewish community in Britain is the [Spanish and Portuguese Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_and_Portuguese_Jews), which traces back to the 1630s when it existed clandestinely in London before the readmission and was unofficially legitimised in 1656, which is the date counted by the Jewish community as the re-admittance of the Jews to England (which at the time included Wales). A trickle of Ashkenazi immigration primarily from German countries continued from the late 17th century to the early 19th century, incrementally growing the population from 8,000 in the middle of the eighteenth century, to 35,000 in 1860, to 60,000 by 1880.[^cite_note-\:2-9] As for the second wave of Ashkenazi immigration, a large wave of [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) Jewish immigrants fleeing persecution in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) due to [pogroms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogroms) and the [May Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Laws) between 1880 and the imposition of tighter [immigration restrictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliens_Act_1905) in 1905 sought their way to the Isles, which rose the population further to 250,000 by 1914 and then 350,000 by start of the [Second World War](32927.md).[^cite_note-\:2-9] Many German and Polish Jews seeking to escape the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) arrived in Britain before and after the [Second World War](32927.md).[^cite_note-10]

During the Second World War, approximately 60,000—70,000 British Jews (of a total population of approximately 370,000) served in the [British Army](4887.md), representing a higher proportion than the general population.[^cite_note-\:5-11][^cite_note-\:6-12] British Jews served in all branches and theatres of war, including as pilots during the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain), and were overrepresented in elite forces like the [Parachute Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute_Regiment_\(United_Kingdom\)) and [Royal Air Force](25679.md) aircrew.[^cite_note-\:5-11]

More than 3,000 British Jewish servicemen and women lost their lives in the war and many others received decorations for bravery and conduct, with three [Victoria Crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Cross), 168 [Military Crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Cross) and 188 [Distinguished Flying Crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Flying_Cross_\(United_Kingdom\)) awarded to Jewish personnel during the war.[^cite_note-\:5-11][^cite_note-\:6-12] Many European Jewish refugees who had fled Nazi persecution also served in the British Army during the war.[^cite_note-13]

The [Jewish Brigade Group](887206.md) was formed in 1944 and fought with distinction in the [Spring 1945 Offensive in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_1945_offensive_in_Italy). The group was largely recruited from [Old Yishuv Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Yishuv) in [British Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) and commanded by British-Jewish officers. They received 7 [Military Medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Medal), 7 [Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) medals, 4 [Military Crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Cross), and 2 US awards. The group was formally disbanded in the summer of 1946.

Following de-colonisation, the late twentieth century saw [Yemeni Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemenite_Jews), [Iraqi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Iraq), and [Baghdadi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdadi_Jews) settle in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] A multicultural community, in 2006 British Jews celebrated the 350th anniversary of the resettlement in England.[^cite_note-17]

According to the [2021 United Kingdom census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Kingdom_census), there were 271,327 Jews in [England and Wales](275187.md), or 0.5% of the overall population,[^cite_note-21] whilst in the 2021 Northern Irish census, there were 439 self-identified Jews constituting just 0.02% of the population, but marking a 31% increase in numbers since the census of 2011.[^cite_note-22] According to the 2011 census, 5,887 [Jews lived in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Scotland) for a total of 277,653 self-identified Jews in the United Kingdom. This does not include much smaller communities in the [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies) and [Overseas Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Territories); notably, there are Jewish communities in [Gibraltar](7607314.md), [Jersey](15693.md) and [Bermuda](3460.md), amongst others. However, this final figure is considered an undercount. Demographers David Graham and Stanley Waterman give several reasons as for why: the underenumeration for censuses in general; the question did not record secular Jews; the voluntary nature of the question; suspicion by Jews of such questions; and the high non-response rate for large numbers of Haredi Jews.[^cite_note-GrahamWaterman-23] By comparison, the [Jewish Virtual Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Virtual_Library) estimated a Jewish population of 291,000 ([not limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_is_a_Jew%3F) to adherents of Judaism) in 2012, making Britain's Jewish community the fifth [largest in the world.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_population_by_country)[^cite_note-JVL-24] This equates to 0.43% of the population of the United Kingdom. The absolute number of Jews has been gradually rising since records began; in the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_United_Kingdom_census), 263,346 people in England and Wales answered "Jewish" to the voluntary question on religion, compared with 259,927 in of 2001.

The [2001 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_Census) included a (voluntary) religion question ("What is your religion?") for the first time in its history;[^cite_note-26] 266,740 people listed their religion as "Jewish".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahamSchmoolWaterman20073-27] However, the subject of [who is a Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_is_a_Jew) is complex, and the religion question did not record people who may be Jewish through other means, such as ethnically and culturally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahamSchmoolWaterman200712–13-28] Of people who chose Jewish as their religion, 97% put White as their ethnic group. However, a report by the [Institute for Jewish Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Jewish_Policy_Research) (JPR) suggests that, although there was an apparent option to write down "Jewish" for this question, it did not occur to many, because of "skin colour" and nationality bias, and that if "Jewish" was an explicit option, the results—only 2,594 respondents were Jewish solely by ethnicity—would have been different.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahamSchmoolWaterman200720–21-29] The religion question appeared in the [2011 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UK_Census), but there was still no explicit option for "Jewish" in the ethnic-group question. The Board of Deputies had encouraged all Jews to indicate they were Jewish, either through the religion question or the ethnicity one.[^cite_note-30]

From 2005 to 2008, the Jewish population increased from 275,000 to 280,000, attributed largely to the high birth rates of [Haredi (or ultra-Orthodox) Jews](53835.md).[^cite_note-Pigott-31] Research by the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) in 2007 showed that 75% of British Jewish births were to the Haredi community.[^cite_note-UofM-32] Ultra-Orthodox women have an average of 6.9 children, and secular Jewish women 1.65.[^cite_note-33] In 2015, the [Institute for Jewish Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Jewish_Policy_Research) reported that in England the orthodox community was growing by nearly 5% per year, while the non-haredi community was decreasing by 0.3% per year.[^cite_note-jp-20151120-34] It has been also documented that in terms of births, between 2007 and 2015, the estimated number of Ultra-Orthodox births per annum increased by 35%, rising from 1,431 to 1,932. Meanwhile, the estimated number of 'Mainstream' (non-Ultra-Orthodox) births per annum increased to a lesser extent over the same period, going from 1,844 to 1,889 (+2.4%).[^cite_note-35]

### Historical population

Going into the 19th century, the Jewish population was small, likely no more than 20,000 individuals. However, the population quadrupled in just a few decades after 1881 as a large number of Jews fled oppression in the Russian Empire. The population increased by as much as 50% between 1933 and 1945, with the United Kingdom admitting around 70,000 Jews between 1933 and 1938, and a further 80,000 between 1938 and 1945. The late 1940s and early 1950s proved to be the high point, numerically speaking, for British Jewry. A decline followed, as many of the new arrivals moved to Israel, moved back to Europe, or emigrated elsewhere, and many other individuals assimilated. The decline continued into the 1990s, but has since reversed. The estimates given before the 2001 Census are likely not directly comparable to the Census, as the Census is based purely on self-identification, whereas the estimates are based on community membership, and it is probably the decline from 450,000 to 266,740 is more like a decline from 450,000 to somewhere between 300,000 and 350,000 going by the metrics of the estimators. Contemporary Jewish demographers like Sergio DellaPergola give figures around 300,000 for the British Jewish population in the early 2010s, since when it has grown.

#### Migration

The great majority (83.2%) of Jews in England and Wales were born in the UK.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahamSchmoolWaterman20075-36] In 2015, about 6% of Jews in England held an Israeli passport.[^cite_note-jp-20151120-34] In 2019, the [Office for National Statistics](324029.md) estimated that 21,000 people resident in the UK were born in Israel, up from 11,890 in 2001. Of the 21,000, 8,000 had Israeli nationality.[^cite_note-37]

In 2013, it was reported that [antisemitic attacks in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_France) led to an exodus of [French Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Jews) to the UK. This has resulted in some synagogues establishing French-language [Shabbat services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabbat_services).[^cite_note-38]

In 2018, 534 Britons [emigrated to Israel](351513.md), representing the third consecutive annual decline. The figure was one third down on 2015 and was the lowest for five years. Meanwhile, immigration of Jews from Israel is consistently higher than emigration of Jews to Israel, at a ratio of about 3\:2, meaning the British Jewish community has a net gain of Jewish immigrants, to the point Israelis now represent around 6% of the British Jewish community.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] As of 2025, only about 1% of all British Jews made [aliyah](351513.md) over the past seven years.[^cite_note-\:1-41] However, the Institute for Jewish Policy Research warned that rising levels of antisemitism in Britain were causing many British Jews to be more receptive to the option of moving to Israel.[^cite_note-\:1-41]

### Geographic distribution

The majority of the Jews in the UK live in southeastern England, particularly in and around [Greater London](45367389.md). Around 145,480 Jews live in London itself - more than half the Jewish population of the entire country - notably the London boroughs of [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Barnet) (56,620), [Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hackney) (17,430), [Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Camden) (10,080), [Haringey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Haringey) (9,400), [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Harrow) (7,300), [Redbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Redbridge) (6,410), [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Westminster) (5,630), [Brent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Brent) (3,720), [Enfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Enfield) (3,710), [Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Islington) (2,710) and [Kensington and Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Kensington_and_Chelsea) (2,680). There are also 30,220 Jews living in districts close to London, including 21,270 in southern Hertfordshire and 4,930 in southwestern Essex, giving a total population of 175,690 Jews in London and the districts and boroughs immediately surrounding it, as compared to 95,640 in the rest of England and Wales combined.

In total, including communities some distance from London, just under 46,000 Jews live in the six counties bordering Greater London, of which two-thirds live in areas immediately adjacent to London. There are, in total, more than 26,400 Jews in [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire), of which 18,350 are in the borough of [Hertsmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertsmere) in southwestern Hertfordshire adjacent to Jewish areas in [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Barnet) and [Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Harrow). Towns and villages in Hertsmere with large Jewish populations include [Borehamwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borehamwood) (6,160), [Bushey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushey) (5,590), and [Radlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radlett) (2,980). Some 30% of Radlett's population is Jewish, as is 20% of Bushey's and 17% of Borehamwood's, 21% of neighbouring [Shenley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenley)'s and 36% of nearby [Elstree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elstree), which has a Jewish plurality. Further afield from London, there is also a significant community in [St Albans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Albans), as well as other smaller communities throughout the county.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-42] There are over 10,300 Jews in [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex), of which 4,380 live in the district of [Epping Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest), in the county's southwest. There is also a significant community in [Southend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southend). In total, London and the counties around it are host to 70.56% of England and Wales' Jewish population, as of 2021.

The next most significant population is in Greater Manchester, a community of more than 28,000, mostly in Bury (10,730), Salford (10,370), Manchester (2,630), and Trafford (2,410).[^cite_note-ons.gov.uk-43] There are also significant communities in Leeds (6,270),[^cite_note-ons.gov.uk-43] Gateshead (2,910),[^cite_note-ons.gov.uk-43] Brighton (2,460),[^cite_note-ons.gov.uk-43] St Albans (2,240),[^cite_note-ons.gov.uk-43] and [Southend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southend) (2,060).[^cite_note-ons.gov.uk-43] Some historically sizeable communities like [Liverpool](18081.md), [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth) and [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) have experienced a steady decline and now number fewer than 2,000 self-identifying Jews each; conversely, there are small but growing communities in places like [Bristol](36741.md), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford) and [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge).

The most Jewish county in the UK is Hertfordshire, which is 2.23% Jewish; this is followed by the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), at 2.06%, and then Greater London at 1.63%. Greater Manchester is 1.00% Jewish, Essex is 0.70% and East Sussex is 0.65%. No other county is as much as 0.50% Jewish. The least Jewish county or principal area in England and Wales is [Merthyr Tydfil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merthyr_Tydfil), which is less than 0.01% Jewish despite once having had a significant community. [Hertsmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertsmere) and [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Barnet) councils are the most Jewish local authorities in England, with Jews composing one in six and seven residents respectively. [Finchley and Golders Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finchley_and_Golders_Green_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) is the political constituency with the largest Jewish population in the UK.[^cite_note-jpr.org.uk-44]

The Scottish population is concentrated in [Greater Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Glasgow), which counts around 2,500 Jews. Around 30% of the [Scottish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Scotland) population, or around 1,510 people, resides in East Renfrewshire, largely in or around the Glasgow suburb of [Newton Mearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_Mearns). Glasgow itself has around 970 Jews. [Edinburgh](9602.md) counts 1,270 Jews; the remaining 35% of Scottish Jewry is scattered throughout the country. The largest [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Wales) community is in [Cardiff](5882.md), with almost 700 Jews, constituting about a third of the Welsh Jewish population and 0.19% of the population of Cardiff itself. The only synagogue in Northern Ireland is in [Belfast](5046.md), where the community has fewer than 100 active members,[^cite_note-45] although 439 people recorded their religion as Jewish in the Northern Irish census of 2021; despite remarkable growth since the previous census in 2011, this still leaves the Northern Irish community as the smallest of the four [Home Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Nations) both in overall numbers and percentage terms. There are small communities throughout the Channel Islands, and there is an active synagogue in [St Brelade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Brelade), [Jersey](15693.md), although the Jewish population of the island is only 49.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] There is only a small number of Jews on the Isle of Man, with no synagogue.[^cite_note-48]

### Age profile



Two boys with [kippot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kippah) at a bus stop in [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon), north London

The British Jewish population has an older profile than the general population. In England and Wales, the median age of male Jews is 41.2, while the figure for all males is 36.1; Jewish females have a median age of 44.3, while the figure for all females is 38.1.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahamSchmoolWaterman20073-27] About 24% of the community are over the age of 65 (compared to 16% of the general population of England and Wales). In the 2001 census, Jews were the only group in which the number of persons in the 75-plus cohorts outnumbered those in the 65–74 cohort.

### Education

As of February 2025, there are 136 Jewish schools in the UK, a net increase of 3 schools since 2021.[^cite_note-school_population-49] The total figure is composed of 45 mainstream [Jewish schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_day_school) and 91 strictly [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) schools. 36,064 Jewish pupils studied in these schools in the academic year 2023/24.[^cite_note-school_population-49] The composition of the sector has shifted over time: whereas in the 1990s the majority of Jewish school pupils were in the mainstream sector, today around 60% of pupils in Jewish schools are in schools serving the strictly Orthodox community, with around 40% in the mainstream sector.

[Jewish day schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_day_school) and [yeshivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) are found throughout the country. [Jewish cultural studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_studies) and [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) language instruction are commonly offered at synagogues in the form of supplementary Hebrew schools or Sunday schools.

Jewish schools often rank among the top in the country for academic achievement and British Jews generally have high levels of educational achievement. For example, at JFS (a prominent London Jewish school), 55% of all [A-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-level_\(United_Kingdom\)) grades in 2025 were at A\*–A, and 84% were at A–B. Several Jewish schools also reported more than half of [GCSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCSE) exams passed at grades 9 to 7 (the highest range) in 2025, with [Immanuel College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_College,_Bushey) seeing 71.4% at this top level.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] British Jews were more likely to report a Level 4 or above qualification as their highest level of qualification compared with the population of England and Wales (50.7% compared with 33.8%).[^cite_note-52]

Security is a prominent concern for Jewish educational institutions in the United Kingdom. All Jewish schools operate under security guidance provided by the [Community Security Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Security_Trust) (CST), which works in partnership with the [Home Office](225695.md) and the [Department for Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Education). The government funds security guards and protective measures through the Jewish Community Protective Security Grant, first introduced in 2015 and renewed annually.[^cite_note-53] Typical arrangements include controlled entry systems, perimeter surveillance, and on-site guards during school hours. These measures have been significantly expanded with greater government funding following increases in antisemitic incidents reported by the CST, particularly during periods of tension in the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

The majority of Jewish schools in Britain are funded by the government. Jewish educational centres are plentiful, large-scale projects. One of the country's most famous Jewish schools is the state-funded [JFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFS_\(school\)) in London which opened in 1732 and has about 2,100 students. It is heavily over-subscribed and applies strict rules on admissions, which led to a discrimination court case, *[R (E) v Governing Body of JFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_\(E\)_v_Governing_Body_of_JFS)*, in 2009.[^cite_note-56] In 2011, another large state-funded school opened in North London named [JCoSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JCoSS), the first cross-denomination Jewish secondary school in the UK.[^cite_note-57] However, some of the all-day educational establishments in the Haredi community of [Stamford Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Hill) have been accused of neglecting secular skills such as English and maths, but claim not to be schools under the technical meaning of the Department for Education.[^cite_note-58]

The annual [Limmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmud) educational charity produces a large annual winter festival and several other regional events throughout the year on the theme of Jewish learning.[^cite_note-59] The [Union of Jewish Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Jewish_Students) is an umbrella organisation that represents Jewish students at university, totalling more than 9,000 university students and 75 university Jewish Societies.[^cite_note-60]

### Economics

According to the [2021 UK census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_United_Kingdom_censuses), of people aged 16–64 who identified as Jewish, 17.5% were self-employed compared with 11.3% in the general population. By contrast 55.2% of Jewish-identified individuals in this age band were in employee roles, compared with 59.6% of the wider population. As of 2019, employees who identified as Jewish had the highest median hourly earnings of all religious groups in England and Wales in both 2012 and 2018 (£15.17 and £19.22 respectively).[^cite_note-\:4-61] In 2018, among Jewish people in employment, 46% were employed in high-skill occupations in England and Wales, and 40% of employees who identified as Jewish reported being employed as a manager, the highest such share reported across all religious groups.[^cite_note-\:4-61]

The 2001 UK Census showed that 30.5% of economically active Jews were self-employed, compared to a figure of 14.2% for the general population. Jews aged 16–24 were less likely to be economically active than their counterparts in the general population; 89.2% of these were students.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahamSchmoolWaterman200787-62] In a 2010 study, average income per working adult was £15.44 an hour. Median income and wealth were significantly higher than other religious groups.[^cite_note-63]

Home ownership remains high in the Jewish population. The 2021 census for England and Wales recorded 72.3% of Jews either owning their home with a mortgage (32.5%) or outright (39.8%). 20.9% rent privately or live rent free and the remaining 6.8% live in [social housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_housing_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-64]

### Marriage

In 2016, the [Institute for Jewish Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Jewish_Policy_Research) (JPR) reported that the [interfaith marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfaith_marriage_in_Judaism) rate for the Jewish community in the United Kingdom was 26 percent.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-\:3-66] This was less than half the rate found among [American Jews](1131183.md), which stood at 58 percent, and showed relatively little change from the British rate of 23 percent in the early 1980s. The figure, however, was more than twice the 11 percent rate recorded in the late 1960s. The study also found that around one third of the children of mixed Jewish–non-Jewish marriages are brought up in the [Jewish faith](15624.md).

Patterns of interfaith marriage differ significantly by age, denomination, and geography. Rates are highest among secular or non-affiliated Jews and among those living outside the main Jewish population centres of London and Manchester, and lowest among Orthodox Jews, where intermarriage is rare. Younger cohorts of Jews who identify culturally rather than religiously are more likely to marry outside the faith than older generations.[^cite_note-\:3-66]

The same JPR report noted that cohabitation without marriage was relatively uncommon among British Jews compared to the general population, though more prevalent among the least religiously observant. Divorce rates were similar to national averages, but considerably lower among strictly Orthodox Jews.

## British Jewish culture

### Media

There are a number of [Jewish newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_newspaper), magazines and other media published in Britain on a national or regional level. The most well known is *[The Jewish Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_Chronicle)*, founded in 1841, and is the world's oldest continuously published Jewish newspaper.[^cite_note-70]

Other publications include the *[Jewish News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_News)*, *[Jewish Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Telegraph)*, *[Hamodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamodia)*, the *[Jewish Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Tribune_\(UK\))* and *[Jewish Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Renaissance)*.

*The Jewish Chronicle* has traditionally combined coverage of British and international Jewish affairs with cultural commentary and opinion pieces. *Jewish News* is a free weekly newspaper based in London that focuses on community events, British politics, and Jewish cultural life. The *Jewish Telegraph*, published from Manchester, has regional editions for Leeds, Glasgow, and Liverpool, reflecting the historic distribution of Jewish communities across northern England and Scotland. The strictly [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) community is served by titles such as *Hamodia* and the *Jewish Tribune*, while *Jewish Renaissance* is a quarterly magazine devoted to Jewish arts and culture.

In April 2020, *The Jewish Chronicle* and the *Jewish News*, which had announced plans to merge in February and later announced plans for a joint liquidation, continued as separate entities after the former was acquired by a consortium with a [charitable trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charitable_trust) established to steer the newspaper.[^cite_note-71]

### Food

Cookbooks grew in popularity in Britain during the mid-19th century and began to shape the domestic cooking practices of Jewish households, particularly by guiding and inspiring women in their kitchens. As Jewish communities in Britain were formed largely by successive waves of migration, from early [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews) arrivals in the seventeenth century to [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) immigrants from [Central](5188.md) and Eastern Europe in the nineteenth, their cuisine reflected a fusion of traditions adapted to local British ingredients and tastes but is mostly drawn from [Ashkenazi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish_cuisine).[^cite_note-72]

Early Anglo-Jewish cookbooks, such as *The Jewish Manual* (1846) by [Judith Montefiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Montefiore), sought to reconcile the requirements of [kashrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashrut) with the conventions of [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) dining, introducing readers to both traditional [Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbath) dishes and new, Anglicised preparations. In London’s [East End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_of_London), where many Ashkenazi immigrants settled, distinctive dishes emerged that became hallmarks of British Jewish cuisine. Among these were fried [gefilte fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gefilte_fish) balls, served hot and crisp rather than [poached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching_\(cooking\)) in [aspic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspic) as on the continent, and [corned beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corned_beef), which consisted of brined and boiled [brisket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisket) served with mustard and pickles in a chewy beigel, rather than [pastrami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastrami) as is more common elsewhere.[^cite_note-73] That dish became an icon of the area's delicatessens. Other staples included chopped [herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herring), egg and onion, [lockshen pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noodle_kugel) (a sweet [noodle kugel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noodle_kugel)), kneidlach ([matzo balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matzah_ball)), and [cholent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholent), the slow-cooked Sabbath stew traditionally made with beans, barley, and potatoes.



[Fish and chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_chips)

[Fish and chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_chips), often considered Britain's national dish, was likely introduced to the country through the [Chuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuts) and [Spanish and Portuguese Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_and_Portuguese_Jews) who lived in the Netherlands before settling in the UK in the 16th and 17th centuries. The earliest known fish and chip shops were opened in London during the 1860s by Eastern European Jewish immigrant Joseph Malin,[^cite_note-74] and by John Lees in [Mossley, Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mossley).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

Sephardi communities contributed their own influences, particularly through the [Spanish and Portuguese Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_and_Portuguese_Jews) of London and later arrivals from [Iraq](7515928.md) and the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_basin). Their cooking introduced [bourekas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourekas), spiced meatballs, fried aubergines, and almond pastries, along with seasonings such as [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon), [allspice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allspice), and [amba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amba_\(condiment\)), a tangy mango pickle.

Today, British Jewish cuisine continues to evolve. Traditional East End fare survives in long-established shops such as the [Brick Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Lane) [Beigel Bake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beigel_Bake) and [Golders Green](94014.md)'s [Grodzinski Bakery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodzinski_Bakery), while modern kosher delis and restaurants have combined classic Ashkenazi comfort food with contemporary British and [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_cuisine) influences.

### Popular culture

In comedy, British Jews have been among the most prominent writers and performers. [Peter Sellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sellers) was one of the central writers and stars of [The Goon Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goon_Show), a landmark in post-war [radio comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_comedy), but is best-known for his portrayal of [Chief Inspector Clouseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_Clouseau) on [The Pink Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pink_Panther) series. Later generations included [Sacha Baron Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacha_Baron_Cohen), creator of [Ali G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_G), [Borat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borat), and [The Dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dictator); [Stephen Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Fry), known for [A Bit of Fry and Laurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bit_of_Fry_&_Laurie) and [Jeeves and Wooster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeeves_and_Wooster); and [Matt Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lucas) and [David Baddiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baddiel), who brought Jewish humour and identity into mainstream British entertainment. [Maureen Lipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Lipman) and [Miriam Margolyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Margolyes) are also well-known Jewish actors with long careers in stage and screen comedy. Other notable Jewish comedians, actors and writers include [Simon Amstell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Amstell), [David Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Schneider_\(actor\)), [Ben Elton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Elton), [Helena Bonham Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Bonham_Carter), and [Daniel Radcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Radcliffe).

British Jews have had a strong influence in music and broadcasting. [Brian Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Epstein), the manager of [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles), played a decisive role in the global rise of [British pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_pop_music) in the 1960s. Other notable figures include producer [Mark Ronson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ronson), singer [Amy Winehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Winehouse), and composer [Lionel Bart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Bart), best known for the musical [Oliver!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver!). In broadcasting, Jewish executives and journalists such as [Alan Yentob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Yentob) and [Jon Sopel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Sopel) have held senior roles in the BBC and other media institutions.

### British Jewish literature

British-born Jews have made a sustained contribution to post-war British letters across fiction, drama, poetry, and children’s literature. In drama, [Harold Pinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Pinter) of Hackney, the son of Jewish immigrants, became one of the most influential playwrights of the twentieth century and received the [2005 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature). His work is frequently read through the lens of wartime evacuation and antisemitic prejudice in 1930s-40s London.

Alongside Pinter, [Oscar Lewenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Lewenstein), [Arnold Wesker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Wesker) and [Bernard Kops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Kops) were central figures in post-war British theatre, associated with socially engaged writing that often drew on East End Jewish experience.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

On television and stage, [Jack Rosenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Rosenthal) wrote [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_of_Film_and_Television_Arts)-winning works on Jewish family and social life, including *[Bar Mitzvah Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Mitzvah_Boy_\(musical\)),* *[The Evacuees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Evacuees)* and [Oscar](324.md)-award-winning *[Yentl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yentl_\(film\)).*

In fiction, [Howard Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Jacobson) is the best-known contemporary Anglo-Jewish novelist; his comic novel [The Finkler Question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Finkler_Question) won the [2010 Man Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Man_Booker_Prize) and explores Jewish identity in modern Britain.

Earlier Booker laureates include [Bernice Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernice_Rubens), the first woman to win the prize for *[The Elected Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elected_Member)* (1970), and [Anita Brookner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anita_Brookner), who won in 1984 for [Hotel du Lac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_du_Lac). Prose writers [Clive Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Sinclair_\(author\)) and [Elaine Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Feinstein) produced notable novels, poems and short stories that engaged Jewish themes and European literary influences.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

In poetry, [Dannie Abse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dannie_Abse) articulated a [Welsh-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Jews) voice that became prominent in post-war verse, and is widely considered one of the most important Welsh writers of the past century, also writing novels and plays.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

In children’s literature, [poet laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet_laureate) [Michael Rosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rosen) has been a leading author whose work reflects a London Jewish upbringing, best-known for *[We're Going on a Bear Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We're_Going_on_a_Bear_Hunt)* (1989) and *[Sad Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sad_Book)* (2004).

### Business, science and academia

From the late nineteenth century onward, Jewish immigrants and their descendants became known for entrepreneurship, education, and social mobility, with many family-run firms contributing to British industry, textiles, and retail. Notable examples include the [co-operatively owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative) department store [Marks & Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marks_&_Spencer), founded by [Michael Marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Marks) and [Thomas Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Spencer_\(businessman\)) in the 1880s, and the long-established retailer [Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_\(retailer\)), founded by [Montague Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montague_Burton) in 1903, which became one of the country’s largest tailoring companies. Other major British businesses founded by British Jews include the multinational supermarket chain [Tesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesco), the fashion chains [River Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Island) and [Debenhams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debenhams), and the advertising agency [Saatchi & Saatchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saatchi_&_Saatchi).

In the twentieth century, British Jews made significant contributions to science and the academy. Among Nobel laureates of Jewish heritage are physicist [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born), biochemist [César Milstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Milstein), and molecular biologist [Aaron Klug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Klug), each of whom conducted pioneering research at British universities. Mathematician [Dennis Sciama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_W._Sciama) and biochemist [Frederick Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger) were influential mentors to a generation of British scientists. In the humanities and social sciences, Jewish scholars such as [Sir Isaiah Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Berlin), [Sir Ernst Gombrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Gombrich), [Sir Geoffrey Elton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Elton), and [Eric Hobsbawm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Hobsbawm) became leading figures in philosophy, art history, history, and political theory. Contemporary academics including [Simon Schama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Schama), [Sir Jonathan Sacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Sacks,_Baron_Sacks), [Simon Sebag Montefiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Sebag_Montefiore) and [Timothy Garton Ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Garton_Ash) have maintained a strong presence in British intellectual life and public debate, while theoretical physicist [David Deutsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Deutsch) pioneered the field of [quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing) by formulating a description for a [quantum Turing machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Turing_machine), as well as specifying an algorithm designed to run on a quantum computer.

Jewish participation in business and finance has also been prominent. The [Rothschild family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_family), based in London since the early nineteenth century, played a central role in the development of the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London) as a global financial centre. Later generations of Jewish entrepreneurs contributed to sectors such as property, manufacturing, and media, among them [Lord Alan Sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Sugar), [Sir Philip Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Green), and [Sir Martin Sorrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Sorrell).

## Politics



[Benjamin Disraeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli) in 1878, the only Prime Minister who was Jewish by birth; he was otherwise a practicing Christian.

During the 19th century, the British political system was gradually reformed to allow those outside the established [Church of England](5955.md) to participate fully in public life. The [Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Relief_Act_1829) removed religious tests that had excluded [Roman Catholics](606848.md) from Parliament, and the first Roman Catholics entered the [House of Commons](13828.md) in 1832. The [Jews Relief Act 1858](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_Relief_Act_1858) later removed the requirement for Members of Parliament to swear a Christian oath, permitting observant Jews to take their seats.

[Lionel de Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_de_Rothschild), first elected in 1847, became the first practising Jewish MP in 1858 after Parliament agreed to amend the [oath of allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaths_of_Allegiance,_etc.,_and_Relief_of_Jews_Act_1858). Earlier, [David Salomons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Salomons) had briefly sat in 1851 before being required to withdraw for refusing the Christian declaration. He was the first Jewish [Sheriff of the City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriffs_of_the_City_of_London) and [Lord Mayor of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mayor_of_London). [Benjamin Disraeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli), born Jewish but baptised [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism), served twice as Prime Minister in the later 19th century, and is widely regarded as one of the most important and influential Conservative Prime Ministers in the United Kingdom’s history.

By the early 20th century, Jewish representation in Parliament had broadened across the political spectrum. Figures such as [Herbert Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Samuel) (Liberal), [Leslie Hore-Belisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Hore-Belisha) (Conservative and later Liberal), and [Manny Shinwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Shinwell) (Labour) served as Cabinet ministers, reflecting the community’s integration into national political life. The [Board of Deputies of British Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Deputies_of_British_Jews), established in 1760, functioned as the main representative body of British Jewry and continues to act as a liaison between the community and government on political and social issues. Many Jews were also active in wider political movements, including trade unionism and opposition to fascism in the 1930s, most notably during the [Battle of Cable Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cable_Street).

By the early 21st century, polls consistently indicated that a majority of British Jews favoured the Conservatives. [Ed Miliband](1290018.md), the leader of the Labour Party between 2010-2015, was the first Jewish leader of the Labour Party. Some MPs, such as [Robert Jenrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jenrick) (Conservatives) and [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) (Labour), while not Jewish themselves, are married to Jews and have Jewish children.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] Notable British-Jewish politicians include [Herbert Samuel, 1st Viscount Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Samuel,_1st_Viscount_Samuel), [Edwin Montagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Montagu), [Sir Keith Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Joseph), [John Bercow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bercow), and [Michael Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Howard).

Before the [2015 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_United_Kingdom_general_election), 69% of British Jews surveyed were planning to vote for the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)), while 22% would vote for the [Labour Party](19279158.md).[^cite_note-88] A May 2016 poll of British Jews showed 77% would vote Conservative, 13.4% Labour, and 7.3% [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)).[^cite_note-jc-20170530-89] An October 2019 poll of British Jews showed 64% would vote Conservative, 24% Liberal Democrat, and only 6% Labour, reflecting broader community concerns about [antisemitism in the Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_British_Labour_Party) under the leadership of [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md).[^cite_note-90]

According to polling in 2015, politicians' attitudes towards [Israel](9282173.md) influence the vote of three out of four British Jews.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] 80% of British Jews support Israel's right to exist as a Jewish state.[^cite_note-\:7-93] 75% of British Jews feel emotionally attached to Israel, with 49% "very attached", and 45% saying Israel is very important to their Jewish identity. 64% identify as [Zionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism), though younger Jews are more critical of Israel, with 24% of 18-25 year olds identifying as [Anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) and 20% as [Non-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Zionism).[^cite_note-\:7-93]

In London, most of the top constituencies with the largest Jewish populations voted Conservative in the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election) - these are namely, [Finchley and Golders Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finchley_and_Golders_Green_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Harrow East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_East_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Chipping Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipping_Barnet_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), [Ilford North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilford_North_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), and [Hertsmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertsmere_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) in Hertfordshire. The exceptions were [Hackney North and Stoke Newington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackney_North_and_Stoke_Newington_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and [Hampstead and Kilburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead_and_Kilburn_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), which both voted Labour in the election. Outside the region, large Jewish constituencies voted for Labour, namely [Bury South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_South_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and [Blackley and Broughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackley_and_Broughton_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)).[^cite_note-jpr.org.uk-44]

In a poll by the [Institute for Jewish Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Jewish_Policy_Research) (JPR) in 2024, British Jews voted broadly in line with larger national trends, with about 46% support for the Labour Party and 30% support for the Conservative Party, though support for Reform UK was substantially lower (6%) than the national average (15%).[^cite_note-94] In 2025, following the [Gaza war](74998519.md), the JPR reported that support for the two major parties, Labour and Conservatives, among British Jews had fallen to 58% in July 2025 from 84% in 2020. Support for the [Green party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_party) had increased to 18% and [Reform UK](59757202.md) to 11%, compared to 7% and 28% respectively in the wider population. The JPR executive director said "Reform UK is more likely to attract male, older, orthodox, and Zionist Jews; the Greens are more likely to attract younger, unaffiliated and anti-Zionist \[Jews]".[^cite_note-guardian-20251120-95]

In response to Foreign Secretary [Ed Miliband](1290018.md) announcing on 8 September 2026 that the [United Kingdom would introduce sanctions](1290018.md#Foreign_Secretary_\(2026%E2%80%93present\)) on goods from [illegal settlements in the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_settlements_in_the_West_Bank) to support the prospects of the [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution)[^cite_note-96] the [pro-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Zionism) [Board of Deputies of British Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Deputies_of_British_Jews) and the [Jewish Leadership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Leadership_Council) condemned the move though other senior rabbis, including from [progressive Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Judaism_\(United_Kingdom\)), supported the move.[^cite_note-guardian-20260909-97]

## Antisemitism

Main article: [Antisemitism in the United Kingdom](39358903.md)

The earliest Jewish settlement was recorded in 1070, soon after the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England). Jews living in England at this time experienced religious discrimination and it is thought that the [blood libel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_libel) which accused Jews of ritual murder originated in Northern England, leading to [massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Massacre) and increasing discrimination.<sup>[\[2\]](39358903.md#cite_note-2)</sup> The Jewish presence continued until [King Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England)'s [Edict of Expulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Expulsion) in 1290.<sup>[\[3\]](39358903.md#cite_note-3)</sup>

Jews were readmitted into the [Commonwealth of England, Scotland and Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protectorate) by [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) in 1655, though it is believed that [crypto-Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto-Judaism) lived in England during the expulsion.<sup>[\[4\]](39358903.md#cite_note-4)</sup> Jews were regularly subjected to discrimination and humiliation which waxed and waned over the centuries, gradually declining.<sup>[\[5\]](39358903.md#cite_note-5)</sup>

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the number of Jews in Britain greatly increased due to the exodus from [Russia](25391.md), which resulted in a large community forming in the [East End of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_of_London).<sup>[\[6\]](39358903.md#cite_note-6)</sup> Popular sentiment against immigration was used by the [British Union of Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Union_of_Fascists) to incite hatred against Jews, leading to the [Battle of Cable Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cable_Street) in 1936, when the fascists were forced to abandon their march through an area with a large Jewish population when the police clearing the way were unable to remove barricades defended by trade unionists, left wing groups and residents.<sup>[\[7\]](39358903.md#cite_note-7)</sup>

In the aftermath of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust), undisguised racial hatred of [Jews](25955086.md) became unacceptable in [British society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_society). Outbursts of antisemitism emanating from [far right groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_right_in_the_United_Kingdom) continued, however, leading to the formation of the [43 Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43_Group) led by Jewish ex-servicemen which broke up fascist meetings from 1945 to early 1950.

Records of antisemitic incidents have been compiled since 1984, although changing reporting practices and levels of reporting make comparison over time difficult. The [Community Security Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Security_Trust) (CST) was formed in 1994 to "\[protect] British Jews from antisemitism and related threats".[^cite_note-104] It works in conjunction with the police and other authorities to protect Jewish schools, Synagogues, and other community institutions.

Polling data from the [Campaign Against Antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_Against_Antisemitism) reveals that almost half of British Jews have contemplated leaving the UK since the [2023 Hamas attack on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas-led_attack_on_Israel) due to rising antisemitism.[^cite_note-\:0-105]
