**Volcán de Fuego&#x20;**&#x20;(Spanish pronunciation: [\[bolˈkan de ˈfweɣo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); Spanish for "Volcano of Fire", often shortened to **Fuego**) or **Chi Q'aq'** ([Kaqchikel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaqchikel_language) for "where the fire is") is an active [stratovolcano](162404.md) in [Guatemala](17238567.md), on the borders of [Chimaltenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimaltenango_Department), [Escuintla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escuintla_Department) and [Sacatepéquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacatep%C3%A9quez_Department) departments.

Part of the mountain range of the [Sierra Madre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_de_Chiapas), the volcano sits about 16 kilometres (9.9 mi) west of [Antigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala), one of Guatemala's most famous cities and a tourist destination.[^cite_note-usatoday13-2] It has erupted frequently, most recently in [June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Volc%C3%A1n_de_Fuego_eruption)[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] and [November](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Volc%C3%A1n_de_Fuego_eruption#November_eruption) 2018,[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] 23 September 2021, 11 December 2022,[^cite_note-8] 4 May 2023,[^cite_note-4-may-2023-fuego-9] 4 June 2025 and 4 August 2026.[^cite_note-apnews.com_Aug_2026-10]

Fuego is famous for being almost constantly active at a low level. Small explosions of gas and ash occur every 15 to 20 minutes, but larger eruptions are less frequent. [Andesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite) and [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) lava types dominate.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

The volcano is joined with [Acatenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acatenango) volcano to its north and collectively the complex is known as La Horqueta. Between Fuego and Acatenango is La Meseta, a [scarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) marking the remains of an older volcano that collapsed around 8,500 years ago.[^cite_note-13] Fuego volcano started to grow after the collapse of La Meseta.[^cite_note-14]

## Early expeditions



Volcán de Fuego summit as seen from the saddle that separates it from [Acatenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acatenango) in 1899. Photographed by [Alfred Percival Maudslay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Percival_Maudslay).

In 1881, French writer [Eugenio Dussaussay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Dussaussay?action=edit\&redlink=1) climbed the volcano, then practically unexplored.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDussaussay1897180-15] First, he needed to ask for permission to climb from the Sacatepéquez governor, who gave him a letter for the [Alotenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alotenango) mayor asking for his assistance with guides to help the explorer and his companion, Tadeo Trabanino.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDussaussay1897179-182-16] They wanted to climb the central peak, unexplored at the time, but could not find a guide and had to climb to the active cone, which had a recent eruption in 1880.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDussaussay1897179-17]

British archeologist [Alfred Percival Maudslay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Percival_Maudslay) climbed the volcano on 7 January 1892. Here is how he described his expedition:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaudslayMaudslay189937–39-18]

> &#x20;\[...] we arranged to start the very next day for the village of [Alotenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alotenango). On 7 January we left that village about 7 o'clock in the morning with seven Mozos, carrying food, clothing, and my camp-bed, and rode for an hour towards the mountains, when we dismounted and sent back our mules. The first two hours' climb was not so very steep, but it was tiring work walking over the loose mould and dry leaves under the thick forest. \[...] we recommenced our climb under shadow of the forest by a steep path cut through the undergrowth. At the height of about 9500 feet we, for the first time since starting, got a sight of the peak rising on the other side of a deep ravine. The whole of the slope on which we looked was bare of vegetation, and presented to the eye nothing but desolate slopes of ashes and scoriae broken higher up with patches of burnt rock; we scrambled on through the thick undergrowth, often with loose earth under foot, and by degrees the vegetation changed and we got amongst the pine-trees. At about 11,200 feet we came to a spot where the earth had been levelled for a few yards by the Indians, and there we determined to pass the night. \[...] then returned and watched the reflection of the sunset over the more distant peaks and against the perfect cone of [Agua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volc%C3%A1n_de_Agua). \[...] but the cold which followed the sunset soon took all our attention. \[...] We turned out of our shelter at about half-past four in the morning, and felt all the better after drinking hot coffee; we then sat for an hour watching a most beautiful dawn and sunrise. At the opposite side of the valley rose the Volcano of Agua, sloping on one side to the plain of [Antigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala), and on the other in a long unbroken sweep to the sea, more than forty miles away. Peak after peak stood out against the red light into the far distance, and on the right the low coast-line and the sea showed up very clearly. As soon as the sun was up we started for the summit. I stopped on the way to get a photograph of the cone, which lay to the left of us as we ascended; but the clouds came over just as I was ready, and I had to give it up. A little over 12,000 feet we left the scraggy pine-trees and arrived at the northern end of a cinder ridge, called the Meseta, which is at the summit of the slope we had been climbing.

— Alfred Percival Maudslay, A Glimpse at Guatemala[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaudslayMaudslay189939-19]

### 1717 destruction of Santiago de los Caballeros



Ruins of the [Society of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_and_Convent_of_Society_of_Jesus_\(Antigua_Guatemala\)) church interior in 1880.

The strongest earthquakes experienced by the city of Santiago de los Caballeros before its final move in 1776 were the [San Miguel earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1717_Guatemala_earthquake) in 1717. In the city, people also believed that the proximity of the Volcán de Fuego (English: Volcano of Fire) was the cause of earthquakes; the great architect Diego de Porres even said that all the earthquakes were caused by volcano explosions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMelchor_Toledo2011103-34]

On 27 August there was a strong eruption of Volcán de Fuego, which lasted until 30 August; the residents of the city asked for help to Santo Cristo of the cathedral and to the Virgen del Socorro who were sworn patrons of the Volcan de Fuego. On 29 August a Virgen del Rosario procession took to the streets after a century without leaving her temple, and there were many more holy processions until 29 September, the day of San Miguel. Early afternoon earthquakes were minor, but at about 7\:00 pm there was a strong earthquake that forced residents to leave their homes; tremors and rumblings followed until four o'clock. The neighbors took to the streets and loudly confessed their sins, bracing for the worst.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMelchor_Toledo2011104-35]

The San Miguel earthquake damaged the city considerably, to the point that some rooms and walls of the Royal Palace were destroyed. There was also a partial abandonment of the city, food shortages, lack of manpower and extensive damage to the city infrastructure; not to mention numerous dead and injured.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMelchor_Toledo2011104-35] These earthquakes made the authorities consider moving to a new city less prone to seismic activity. City residents strongly opposed the move, and even took to the Royal Palace in protest; in the end, the city did not move, but the number of elements in the Army Battalion to safeguard the order was considerable.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodríguez_GirónFloresGarnica1995585-36] The damage to the palace was repaired by Diego de Porres, who finished repairs in 1720; although there are indications that there were more jobs done by Porres until 1736.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodríguez_GirónFloresGarnica1995585-36]

In 1773, the [Santa Marta earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Marta_earthquake) destroyed much of the town, which led to the third change in location for the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFoster2000-37] The [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_monarchy) ordered, in 1776, the removal of the capital to a safer location, the Valley of the Shrine, where [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City), the modern capital of Guatemala, now stands. This new city did not retain its old name and was christened Nueva Guatemala de la Asunción (New Guatemala of the Assumption), and its patron saint is Our Lady of the Assumption. The badly damaged city of Santiago de los Caballeros was ordered abandoned, although not everyone left, and was thereafter referred to as la Antigua Guatemala (the Old Guatemala).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFoster2000-37]

### Eruption of 3 June 2018

Main article: [2018 Volcán de Fuego eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Volc%C3%A1n_de_Fuego_eruption)



Satellite view of the eruption, showing the ash plume emitted from the volcano

Fuego's most recent period of activity began in 1999 and continues to the present day. This period consists of persistent low-level eruptive activity interspersed with occasional violent explosive "paroxysmal" eruptions. Paroxysmal eruptions (or 'paroxysms') produce a sustained eruptive plume from which ash falls on communities within 20 km of the volcano, lava flows reaching 1–2 km from the summit, and occasional pyroclastic density currents.[^cite_note-38] The largest eruption of Fuego in its current period of activity happened on 3 June 2018, also the most powerful eruption of the volcano since 1974. Fuego generated large pyroclastic flows that gradually filled its drainage ravines (known locally as "barrancas"). The greatest impacts were on the east side of Fuego, where pyroclastic flows filled and eventually overcame the capacity of the Las Lajas ravine to descend on the nearby communities of San Miguel Los Lotes and [El Rodeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Rodeo,_Escuintla) in [Escuintla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escuintla_Department) and the private golf resort of La Reunión in [Sacatepéquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacatepequez_Department). The flows that descended on Los Lotes buried the town in pyroclastic material and killed many of the residents, who had received limited warnings to evacuate. On 5 June, [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported that at least 99 people are dead and nearly 200 others were unaccounted for following the eruption.[^cite_note-foxnews.com_Aug_2018-39][^cite_note-apnews.com_05_June-40] Ash fall extended as far as the capital, [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City) forcing the closure of [La Aurora International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Aurora_International_Airport). The military assisted in clearing ash off the runway. Rescue attempts were seriously hampered as routes into the affected regions were seriously damaged by the pyroclastic flows.[^cite_note-BBC_3_June-41]
