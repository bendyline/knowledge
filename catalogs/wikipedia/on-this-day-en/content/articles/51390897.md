**Brazil–Germany relations** are the [current and historical relations](429154.md) between the [Federative Republic of Brazil](3383.md) and the [Federal Republic of Germany](11867.md). Both nations enjoy friendly relations, the importance of which centers on the history of [German migration to Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Brazilians). There are approximately 7 to 12 million Brazilians of German descent.[^cite_note-1] Both nations are members of the [G20](2206608.md) and the [United Nations](31769.md).

## History



German communities (pink) in [Southern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Brazil) in 1905

In 1822, soon after the declaration of Brazil's independence from [Portugal](23033.md), Brazilian Major Jorge Antonio Schäffer was sent to the various German courts to recruit colonists and soldiers to immigrate to Brazil.[^cite_note-Relations-2] In 1824, the first German migrants began arriving to Brazil and settled in [Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md). In 1825, [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) and the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) recognized Brazil's independence. Soon afterwards, Brazil opened a consulate in Hamburg in 1826.[^cite_note-Relations-2][^cite_note-Bilateral-3]

Brazil and Germany established diplomatic relations in 1871, shortly after [German Unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany) and the creation of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). That same year, Emperor [Pedro II of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_II_of_Brazil) included Germany in his first voyage to Europe. He would visit Germany two separate occasions more as Emperor in 1876 and lastly in 1887.[^cite_note-4]

In April 1917 during [World War I](4764461.md), Brazil broke diplomatic relations with Germany after the sinking of the Brazilian steamship *Paraná* by a German submarine. In October 1917, [Brazil declared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_during_World_War_I) on the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) after three more Brazilian steamships were hit by German submarines. During the war, Brazil's navy mainly patrolled the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) and the Brazilian army sent a small military mission to the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)).

In August 1942, [Brazil declared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Expeditionary_Force) on Germany and [Italy](14532.md) and entered [World War II](32927.md). Brazilian and German soldiers fought each other during the [Italian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_campaign_\(World_War_II\)). Diplomatic relations were resumed between Brazil and [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in 1951.[^cite_note-Relations-2] That same year, Brazil opened an embassy in [Bonn](3295.md) and West Germany opened an embassy in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md).[^cite_note-Relations-2]

In 1964, West German President [Heinrich Lübke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_L%C3%BCbke) became the first German head-of-state to pay a visit to Brazil. In 1979, West German Chancellor [Helmut Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Schmidt) became the first German head-of-government to visit Brazil. In 1978, Brazilian President [Ernesto Geisel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Geisel) paid an official visit to West Germany, the first by a Brazilian President. On 3 October 1990, West Germany and [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) [reunified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) to a single Germany. In 1991, German Chancellor [Helmut Kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Kohl) became the first head-of-government to visit Brazil. In 1995, Brazilian President [Fernando Henrique Cardoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso) paid an official visit to Germany. There would be numerous visits between leaders of both nations.[^cite_note-Relations-2]

The bilateral relationship between both nations is solid and dense, marked by the convergence of perceptions, values and interests. The [German diaspora in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Brazilians) are well integrated in the Brazilian society and maintain strong economic, historical and cultural ties with Germany. The [Brazilian community in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians_in_Germany) is distributed throughout the country and consists mainly of Brazilians married to German nationals; employees of German companies with branches in Brazil; Brazilians with dual nationality; and students attending local universities. According to data from the Brazilian consular system, there is approximately 102,000 Brazilians living in Germany.[^cite_note-Relations-2]

## Bilateral agreements

Both nations have signed several bilateral agreements such as a Trade and Navigation Treaty between the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil) and the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) and between the Empire of Brazil and the [Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) cities of [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck), [Bremen](9420388.md) and [Hamburg](13467.md) (1827); Cultural Agreement (1969); Space Research Agreement (1973); Agreement for the Joint Commission for Economic Cooperation (COMISTA) (1974); Agreement on Agricultural Cooperation (1974); [Agreement of Cooperation for the Peaceful uses of Nuclear Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Brazil%E2%80%93West_Germany_nuclear_agreement) (1975); Agreement on the Avoidance of Double Taxation (1975); Basic Technical Cooperation Agreement (1996); Agreement of Cooperation in Scientific Research and Technological Development (1996); Agreements on Financial Cooperation for the Execution of Projects and for the Preservation of Tropical Forests (2002); Agreement on the statute of Cultural Institutions (2005); Agreement on Cinematographic Coproduction (2005); Public Security Partnership and Cooperation Agreement (2008); and an Agreement of Cooperation in the Energy Sector with a Focus on Renewable Energies and Energy Efficiency (2008).[^cite_note-Relations-2][^cite_note-Bilateral-3]
