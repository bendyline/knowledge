**Prince Edward Island**[^cite_note-10] is an island [province](75763.md) of Canada. It is the smallest province by both land area and population, but has the highest [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) in Canada. The island has several nicknames: "Garden of the Gulf", "Birthplace of [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation)" and "Cradle of Confederation".[^cite_note-11] Its capital and largest city is [Charlottetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown). It is one of the three [Maritime provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_provinces) and one of the four [Atlantic provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_provinces).

Historically, the island formed an integral part of the [Mi'kmaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kmaq) homeland, [Mi'kma'ki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kma'ki), comprising one part of the district ***Epekwitk** aq Piktuk* (also spelled ***Epegwitg** aq Pigtug*, lit. 'PEI and [Pictou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictou)').[^cite_note-Hist-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-districts-14] In 1604, Epekwitk was colonized by the French as part of the colony of [Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia), where it became known as Isle St-Jean (St. John's Island). It was later ceded to the British at the conclusion of the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War) in 1763 and became part of the colony of [Nova Scotia](21184.md). In 1769, St. John's Island became its own British colony and its name was changed to Prince Edward Island (PEI) in 1798. PEI hosted the [Charlottetown Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Conference) in 1864 to discuss a [union of the Maritime provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Union); however, the conference became the first in a series of meetings which led to [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) on July 1, 1867. Prince Edward Island initially balked at Confederation but, facing bankruptcy from the [Land Question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Question_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)) and construction of a [railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Railroad), joined as Canada's seventh province on July 1, 1873.

According to Statistics Canada, the province of Prince Edward Island had 182,989 residents in 2026.[^cite_note-popest-6] Farming is central to the island's economy; it produces 25% of Canada's [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potatoes). Other important industries include [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery), tourism, [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace), [biotechnology](4502.md), information technology and [renewable energy](25784.md).[^cite_note-15] As Prince Edward Island is one of Canada's older settled areas, its population still reflects the origins of its earliest settlers, with [Acadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadians), Scottish, Irish, and English surnames being dominant. Prince Edward Island is located in the [Gulf of St. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_St._Lawrence), about ten kilometres (6.2 mi) across the Northumberland Strait from both [Nova Scotia](21184.md) and [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick).&#x20;

It is about 200 kilometres (120 mi) north of [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) and 600 kilometres (370 mi) east of [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City). It has a land area of 5,686.03 square kilometres (2,195.39 sq mi),[^cite_note-poppei-16] and is the [104th-largest island in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) and [Canada's 23rd-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canadian_islands_by_area). It is the only Canadian province consisting entirely of islands. However, according to the [United States Geological Survey](23814944.md), [Esri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esri), and the [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) – [World Conservation Monitoring Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Conservation_Monitoring_Centre), as of 2026, the area of the main island is 5,740.96 km<sup>2</sup> (2,216.60 sq mi),[^cite_note-bigis1-17][^cite_note-Canada-18] making it the 105th largest island worldwide.

## Etymology

The island is known in [Mi'kmawi'simk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kmaq_language), the indigenous [Mi'kmaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kmaq) language, as *Abegweit* or *Epekwitk*, roughly translated as "land cradled on the waves".[^cite_note-19] When the island was part of [Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia), originally settled by French colonists, its French name was *[Île Saint-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_Saint-Jean)* ('St. John's Island'). In French, the island is today called *Île-du-Prince-Édouard (ÎPÉ)*. The island was split from the British colony of Nova Scotia in 1769, and renamed in 1798 after [Prince Edward, Duke of Kent and Strathearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Kent_and_Strathearn) (1767–1820), the fourth son of [King George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III) and, in 1819, father of the future [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria). Thus, Prince Edward has been called "Father of the Canadian Crown".[^cite_note-20] In [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic), the island's name is *Eilean a' Phrionnsa* (lit. 'the Island of the Prince', the local form of the longer *Eilean a' Phrionnsa Iomhair/Eideard*) or *Eilean Eòin* (literally, 'John's Island' in reference to the island's former French name) for some Gaelic speakers in Nova Scotia, though not on PEI.

## Geology

Main article: [Geology of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Prince_Edward_Island)



Topographical map of Prince Edward Island

Between 250 and 300 million years ago, freshwater streams flowing from ancient mountains brought silt, sand and gravel into what is now the Gulf of St. Lawrence. These sediments accumulated to form a [sedimentary basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_basin), and make up the island's [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock). When the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) glaciers receded about 15,000 years ago, glacial debris such as [till](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_till) were left behind to cover most of the area that would become the island. This area was connected to the mainland by a strip of land, but when ocean levels rose as the glaciers melted, this land strip was flooded, forming the island. As the land [rebounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isostatic_uplift) from the weight of the ice, the island rose up to elevate it farther from the surrounding water.[^cite_note-PEICE-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Although commercial deposits of minerals have not been found, exploration in the 1940s for natural gas beneath the northeastern end of the province resulted in the discovery of an undisclosed quantity of gas. The Island was reported by government to have only 0.08 tcf of "technically recoverable" natural gas. Twenty exploration wells for hydrocarbon resources have been drilled on Prince Edward Island and offshore. The first reported well was Hillsborough No.#1, drilled in Charlottetown Harbour in 1944 (the world's first offshore well), and the most recent was New Harmony No.#1 in 2007. Since the resurgence of exploration in the mid-1990s, all wells that have shown promising gas deposits have been stimulated through [hydraulic fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing) or "fracking". All oil and natural gas exploration and exploitation activities on the Island are governed by the *Oil and Natural Gas Act* R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. 0-5 and its associated regulations and orders.[^cite_note-24]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Prince_Edward_Island)



Satellite picture of Prince Edward Island

Prince Edward Island is located in the [Gulf of St. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_St._Lawrence), west of [Cape Breton Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Breton_Island), north of the [Nova Scotia peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia_peninsula), and northeast of [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick). Its southern shore bounds the [Northumberland Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland_Strait). The island has two urban areas, and in total, is the most densely populated province in Canada.

### Climate

The island has a [maritime climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), moderate and strongly influenced by the surrounding [Gulf of St. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_St._Lawrence).[^cite_note-PEICE-21][^cite_note-ECclimate-25] As such, it is generally milder than many areas of New Brunswick and Nova Scotia due to the warmer waters of the [Gulf of St. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_St._Lawrence).[^cite_note-PEIclimate-26] The climate is characterized by changeable weather throughout the year; in which specific weather conditions seldom last for long.[^cite_note-ECclimate-25]



Because the [Gulf of Saint Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Saint_Lawrence) freezes over, the island's climate is similar to a [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) as opposed to an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate).

During July and August, the average daytime high in PEI is 23 °C (73 °F); however, the temperature can sometimes exceed 30 °C (86 °F) during these months. In the winter months of January and February, the average daytime high is −3.3 °C (26 °F). The Island receives an average yearly rainfall of 855 millimetres (33.7 in) and an average yearly snowfall of 2.85 metres (9.4 ft). Winters are moderately cold and long but are milder than inland locations, with clashes of cold Arctic air and milder Atlantic air causing frequent temperature swings.[^cite_note-ECclimate-25] The climate is considered to be more [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) than [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) since the Gulf of St. Lawrence freezes over, thus eliminating any moderation.[^cite_note-ECclimate-25] The mean temperature is −7 °C (19 °F) in January.[^cite_note-PEIclimate-26] During the winter months, the island usually has many storms (which may produce rain as well as snow) and blizzards since during this time, storms originating from the North Atlantic or the Gulf of Mexico frequently pass through.[^cite_note-ECclimate-25] Springtime temperatures typically remain cool until the sea ice has melted, usually in late April or early May. ### Water supply

The Province of Prince Edward Island is completely dependent on groundwater for its source of drinking water, with approximately 305 high capacity wells in use as of December 2018.[^cite_note-33] As groundwater flows through an aquifer, it is naturally filtered. The water for the city of Charlottetown is extracted from thirteen wells in three wellfields and distributed to customers. The water removed is replenished by precipitation.[^cite_note-cws-34]



The Winter River in February 2014. The river provides about 92 per cent of [Charlottetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown)'s water supply.

Infrastructure in Charlottetown that was installed in 1888 is still in existence. With the age of the system in the older part of Charlottetown, concern has been raised regarding lead pipes. The Utility has been working with its residents on a lead-replacement program. A plebiscite in 1967 was held in Charlottetown over fluoridation, and residents voted in favour. Under provincial legislation, the Utility is required to report to its residents on an annual basis. It is also required to do regular sampling of the water and an overview is included in each annual report.[^cite_note-cws-34] The Wintere River watershed provides about 92 per cent of the daily 18-thousand-cubic-metre (4.8-million-US-gallon) water supply for the city of Charlottetown,[^cite_note-cbc11-35] which had difficulty in each of 2011, 2012 and 2013 with its supply,[^cite_note-cbc11-35][^cite_note-cwscbc-36][^cite_note-cbc13-37] until water meters were installed.[^cite_note-cwscbc-36] Government tabled a discussion paper on the proposed *Water Act* for the province on July 8, 2015. The use of groundwater came under scrutiny as the potato industry, which accounts for \$1 billion every year and 50% of farm receipts,[^cite_note-PEIPotato-38] has pressed the government to lift a moratorium on high-capacity water wells for irrigation. The release of the discussion paper was to set off a consultation process in the autumn of 2015.[^cite_note-39] Detailed information about the quality of drinking water in PEI communities and watersheds can be found on the provincial government's official website. It provides a summary of the ongoing testing of drinking water done by the Prince Edward Island Analytical Laboratories. Average drinking-water quality results are available, and information on the following parameters are provided: alkalinity; cadmium; calcium; chloride; chromium; iron; magnesium; manganese; nickel; nitrate; pH; phosphorus; potassium; sodium; and sulfate, as well as the presence of pesticides.[^cite_note-40] Water-testing services are provided for a variety of clients through the PEI Analytical Laboratories which assesses according to the recommendations of the Guidelines for Canadian Drinking Water Quality published by [Health Canada](216516.md).[^cite_note-41]

### Flora and fauna

Prince Edward Island used to have native [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_moose), [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_black_bear), [caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_woodland_caribou), [wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_wolf), and other larger species. Due to hunting and [habitat disruption](1707053.md) these species are no longer found on the island. Some species common to P.E.I. are [red foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_fox), [coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_coyote),[^cite_note-42] [blue jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_jay), and [robins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_robin). [Skunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_skunk) and [raccoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon) are common non-native species. Species at risk in P.E.I. include [piping plovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piping_plover), [American eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_eel), [bobolinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobolink), [little brown bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_brown_bat), and beach pinweed.[^cite_note-43] Some species are unique to the province. In 2008, a new [ascomycete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascomycete) species, *[Jahnula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahnula) apiospora* ([Jahnulales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahnulales), [Dothideomycetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dothideomycetes)), was collected from submerged wood in a freshwater creek on Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-44] [North Atlantic right whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_right_whale), one of the rarest whale species, once thought to be rare visitors into St. Lawrence regions until 1994, have been showing dramatic increases (annual concentrations were discovered off [Percé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perc%C3%A9) in 1995 and gradual increases across the regions since 1998),[^cite_note-45] and since 2014, notable numbers of whales have been recorded around [Cape Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Breton) to Prince Edward Island as 35 to 40 whales were seen in these areas in 2015.[^cite_note-46]

## History

### Mi'kmaw district

Main article: [Mi'kma'ki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kma'ki)

Further information: [First Nations in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada)

The [Mi'kmaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kmaq) are the Indigenous inhabitants of what is now Prince Edward Island, calling their country [Mi'kma'ki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kma'ki). The island's land base formed one part of the district *Epekwitk aq [Piktuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictou)* (also spelled *Epegwitg aq Pigtug*). Named "Epekwitk" (and rendered as "Abegweit" in English)[^cite_note-infopei-47]—meaning "cradled on the waves"—the island was governed by *Saqamaq*, or community chiefs, a women's council (*Saqama'sgw*), and wampum keepers (*Putu's*), eventually falling under the jurisdiction of the [*Sante' (or Mi'kmawey) Mawio'mi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Council_\(Mi'kmaq\)) and the [Grand Chief, or *Kji Sagamaw*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_grand_chiefs_\(Mi'kmaq\)). Today, Epekwitk (aq Piktuk), along with the other seven districts of Mi'kma'ki, are protected by the [Peace and Friendship Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_and_Friendship_Treaties) that the Mi'kmaq have with [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Crown); however, rather than district-level governance, administration is currently overseen by [band governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_government). On Epekwitk, the two [communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) are the [Abegweit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abegweit_First_Nation) and [Lennox Island First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lennox_Island_First_Nation).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Stretching back into deep history, the earliest stories of the Mi'kmaq go back to the time of [Glooscap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glooscap), a cultural hero and first human in [Wabanaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy) mythology.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Big in size and power, Mi'kmaw legend says that when Glooscap finished painting the splendour of the world, he dipped his brush into a blend of all the colours and created Epekwitk—his favourite island. When Glooscap slept, *Enmigtaqamu'g* (or mainland Nova Scotia) was his bed and Epekwitk his pillow.[^cite_note-53] Another legend tells us that *Minegoo*—another name for the island, meaning, simply, "island" in [Mi'kmawi'simk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kmawi'simk)—was formed by the Great Spirit placing on the Blue Waters some dark red crescent-shaped clay.[^cite_note-minegoo-54] Mi'kmaw oral history recalls a time when the world was [covered in water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_myth). It was then that the being Sebanees, arriving in *kjiktu’lnu&#x20;*("our great boat"), landed on the shores of Epekwitk. The boat, made of ice, carried all the animals and fish his family would need for survival, and it is said that Epekwitk's unique land formation was a result of the melting of the ice boat.[^cite_note-55] Archaeological evidence, such as [shell middens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_midden) and campsite remains, corroborate Mi'kmaw stories which indicate an ancient presence in Epekwitk.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Prior to [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), Mi'kmaq engaged in varied relations with neighbouring nations, such as the [Wolastoqiyik (Maliseet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maliseet), [Passamaquoddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passamaquoddy), and [Abenaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abenaki), with whom they formed the [Wabanaki Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy) in Dawnland.

### French colony

In 1534, [Jacques Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Cartier) was the first European to see the island.[^cite_note-infopei-47] In 1604, the [Kingdom of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) laid claim to the lands of the Maritimes under the [discovery doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_doctrine), including Prince Edward Island, establishing the [French colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colony) of [Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia) in Mi'kma'ki. The island was named *Île Saint-Jean* (St. John's Island) by the French. The Mi'kmaq never recognized the claim but welcomed the French as trading partners and allies.[^cite_note-58]



Map of Prince Edward Island and French settlements c. 1744

During the 18th century, the French were engaged in a [series of conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Hundred_Years'_War) with the [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) and its colonies. Several battles between the two belligerents occurred on Prince Edward Island during this period. Following the British [capture of Louisbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Louisbourg_\(1745\)) during the [War of the Austrian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession), New Englanders launched an attack on Île Saint-Jean (now Prince Edward Island); with a British detachment landed at Port-la-Joye. The island's capital had a garrison of 20 French soldiers under the command of [Joseph du Pont Duvivier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_du_Pont_Duvivier).[^cite_note-59] The troops fled the settlement, and the New Englanders burned the settlement to the ground. Duvivier and the twenty men retreated up the Northeast River (Hillsborough River), pursued by the New Englanders until the French troops were reinforced with the arrival of the Acadian militia and the Mi'kmaq.[^cite_note-Harvey,_p._111-60] The French troops and their allies were able to drive the New Englanders to their boats. Nine New Englanders were killed, wounded or made prisoner. The New Englanders took six Acadian [hostages](146794.md), who would be executed if the Acadians or Mi'kmaq rebelled against New England control.[^cite_note-Harvey,_p._111-60] The New England troops left for Louisbourg. Duvivier and his 20 troops left for Quebec. After the fall of Louisbourg, the resident French population of Île Royale (now [Cape Breton Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Breton_Island)) were deported to France, with the remaining Acadians of Île Saint-Jean living under the threat of deportation for the remainder of the war.[^cite_note-61] New Englanders had a force of 200 soldiers stationed at [Port-La-Joye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port-la-Joye%E2%80%93Fort_Amherst), as well as two warships boarding supplies for its journey of Louisbourg. To regain Acadia, Ramezay was sent from Quebec to the region to join forces with the [Duc d'Anville expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duc_d'Anville_expedition). Upon arriving at Chignecto, he sent Boishebert to Île Saint-Jean to ascertain the size of the New England force.[^cite_note-62] After Boishebert returned, Ramezay sent Joseph-Michel Legardeur de Croisille et de Montesson along with over 500 men, 200 of whom were Mi'kmaq, to Port-La-Joye.[^cite_note-63] In July 1746, the battle happened near York River.[^cite_note-64] Montesson and his troops killed forty New Englanders and captured the rest. Montesson was commended for having distinguished himself in his first independent command.[^cite_note-65] Hostilities between the British and French were ended in 1748 with the [Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Aix-la-Chapelle_\(1748\)) in 1748.



Monument for the [Acadian expulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Acadians) in Prince Edward Island. A large number of Acadians were forcibly removed from the island in the mid 18th century.

Roughly one thousand [Acadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadians) lived on the island prior to the [Acadian Exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadian_Exodus) from Nova Scotia. The population grew to nearly 5,000 the late 1740s and early 1750s, as Acadians from Nova Scotia fled to the island during the [Acadian Exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadian_Exodus), and the subsequent [British-ordered expulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Upheaval) beginning in 1755.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Hostilities between British and French colonial forces resumed in 1754, although formal declarations of war were not issued until 1756. After French forces were defeated at the [siege of Louisbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Louisbourg_\(1758\)), the British performed a [military campaign on Ile Saint-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ile_Saint-Jean_Campaign) (now Prince Edward Island) to secure the island. The campaign was led by Colonel [Andrew Rollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Rollo,_5th_Lord_Rollo) under orders from General [Jeffery Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffery_Amherst). The following campaigns saw the [deportation of most Acadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Acadians) from the island. Many Acadians died in the expulsion en route to France; on December 13, 1758, the transport ship *[Duke William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_William)* sank and 364 died. A day earlier the *[Violet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violet_\(ship\))* sank and 280 died; several days later [*Ruby*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_\(1758_ship\)) sank with 213 on board.[^cite_note-68] The French formally ceded the island, and most of [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) to the British in the [Treaty of Paris of 1763](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)).

### British colony

Initially named St. John's Island by the British, the island was administered as part of the colony of Nova Scotia until it was split into a separate colony in 1769. In the mid-1760s, a survey team led by [Samuel Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Holland_\(surveyor\)) divided the Island into 67 lots. On July 1, 1767, these properties were allocated to supporters of [King George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III) by means of a lottery. Ownership of the land remained in the hands of landlords in England, angering Island settlers who were unable to gain title to the land on which they worked and lived. Significant rent charges to absentee landlords created further anger. The land had been given to the absentee landlords with a number of conditions attached regarding upkeep and settlement terms, many of which were not satisfied. Islanders spent decades trying to convince the Crown to confiscate the lots; however, the descendants of the original owners were generally well-connected to the British government and refused to give up the land. After the island was detached from Nova Scotia to become a separate colony, [Walter Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Patterson_\(governor\)) was appointed the first British governor of St. John's Island in 1769. Assuming the office in 1770, he had a controversial career during which land title disputes and factional conflict slowed the initial attempts to populate and develop the island under a [feudal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal_system). In an attempt to attract settlers from Ireland, in one of his first acts (1770), Patterson led the island's colonial assembly to rename the island "New Ireland", but the British Government promptly vetoed this as it exceeded the authority vested in the colonial government; only the Privy Council in London could change the name of a colony.[^cite_note-69]



Map of the island with lot boundaries in 1775. The island was divided into lots in 1767.

During the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) Charlottetown was raided in 1775 by a pair of American-employed privateers.[^cite_note-70] Two armed schooners, *Franklin* and *Hancock*, from [Beverly, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly,_Massachusetts), made prisoner of the attorney-general at Charlottetown, on advice given them by some Pictou residents after they had taken eight fishing vessels in the [Gut of Canso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_of_Canso).[^cite_note-71] During and after the American Revolutionary War, from 1776 to 1783, the colony's efforts to attract exiled [Loyalist refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalists) from the rebellious North American colonies met with some success. Walter Patterson's brother, John Patterson, one of the original grantees of land on the island, was a temporarily exiled Loyalist and led efforts to persuade others to come. Governor Patterson's dismissal in 1787 and his recall to London in 1789 dampened his brother's efforts, leading John to focus on his interests in the United States. [Edmund Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Fanning_\(colonial_administrator\)), also a Loyalist exiled by the Revolution, took over as the second governor, serving until 1804. His tenure was more successful than Patterson's. A large influx of Scottish Highlanders in the late 1700s also resulted in St. John's Island having the highest proportion of Scottish immigrants in Canada. This led to a higher proportion of Scottish Gaelic speakers and a thriving culture surviving on the island than in Scotland itself, as the settlers could more easily avoid English influence overseas. On November 29, 1798, during Fanning's administration, the British government approved changing the colony's name from St. John's Island to Prince Edward Island to distinguish it from areas with similar names in what is now [Atlantic Canada](71095.md), such as the cities of [Saint John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John,_New_Brunswick) in New Brunswick and [St. John's](70581.md) in Newfoundland. The colony's new name honoured the fourth son of [King George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III), [Prince Edward Augustus, the Duke of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Augustus,_Duke_of_Kent) (1767–1820), who subsequently led the British military forces on the continent as [Commander-in-Chief, North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_North_America) (1799–1800), with his headquarters in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Halifax). In 1853, the Island government passed the Land Purchase Act, which empowered them to purchase lands from those owners who were willing to sell, and then resell the land to settlers for low prices. This scheme collapsed when the Island ran short of money to continue with the purchases. Many of these lands were also fertile, and were some of the key factors to sustaining Prince Edward Island's economy.

### Confederation



Delegates of the [Charlottetown Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Conference) in 1864. Although PEI hosted a conference, it did not join the [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) until 1873.

From September 1 to 7, 1864, Prince Edward Island hosted the [Charlottetown Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Conference), which was the first meeting in the process leading to the [Quebec Resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Resolutions) and the creation of Canada in 1867. Prince Edward Island found the terms of union unfavourable and balked at joining in 1867, choosing to remain a colony of the United Kingdom. In the late 1860s, the colony examined various options, including the possibility of becoming a discrete dominion unto itself, as well as entertaining delegations from the United States, who were interested in Prince Edward Island joining the United States.[^cite_note-72] In 1871, the colony began construction of the [Prince Edward Island Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Railway) (PEIR) and, frustrated by Great Britain's Colonial Office, began negotiations with the United States.[^cite_note-73] In 1873, Canadian Prime Minister [John A. Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Macdonald), anxious to thwart American expansionism and facing the distraction of the [Pacific Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Scandal), negotiated for Prince Edward Island to join Canada. The Dominion Government of Canada assumed the colony's extensive railway debts and agreed to finance a buy-out of the last of the colony's absentee landlords to free the island of leasehold tenure and from any new immigrants entering the island (accomplished through the passage of the *[Land Purchase Act, 1875](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Purchase_Act_\(1875\))*).[^cite_note-74] Prince Edward Island entered Confederation on July 1, 1873.[^cite_note-75]



The [Confederation Centre of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Centre_of_the_Arts) was completed in 1964, and commemorates the centenary of Confederation.

As a result of having hosted the inaugural meeting of Confederation, the Charlottetown Conference, Prince Edward Island presents itself as the "Birthplace of Confederation" and this is commemorated through several buildings, a [ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland_Ferries_Limited) vessel, and the [Confederation Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Bridge) (constructed 1993 to 1997). The most prominent building in the province honouring this event is the [Confederation Centre of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Centre_of_the_Arts), presented as a gift to Prince Edward Islanders by the 10 provincial governments and the Federal Government upon the centenary of the Charlottetown Conference, where it stands in Charlottetown as a national monument to the "[Fathers of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathers_of_Confederation)". The centre is one of the 22 [National Historic Sites of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Historic_Sites_of_Canada_in_Prince_Edward_Island) located in Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

Dominant self-identified ethnic origin of the population of Prince Edward Island

Traditionally, the population has been evenly divided between Catholic and Protestant affiliations. The 2001 census indicated number of adherents for the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Canada) with 63,240 (47%) and various Protestant churches with 57,805 (43%). This included the [United Church of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Canada) with 26,570 (20%); the Presbyterian Church with 7,885 (6%) and the [Anglican Church of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Canada) with 6,525 (5%); those with no religion were among the lowest of the provinces with 8,705 (6.5%).[^cite_note-86] If one considers that the founders of the United Church of Canada were largely [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterians) in Prince Edward Island, the Island has one of the highest percentages of Presbyterians in the country. Since 2016 there are two [Amish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Order_Amish) settlements on Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-87]

## Economy



A harvester at work in a soybean field. Agriculture remains a major part of the province's economy.

Agriculture remains the dominant industry in the provincial economy, as it has since colonial times. In 2015, agriculture and agri-food manufacturing was responsible for 7.6% of the province's GDP.[^cite_note-88] The Island has a total land area of 1.4 million acres (570,000 [hectares](24919513.md)) with approximately 594,000 acres (240,383 hectares) cleared for agricultural use.[^cite_note-agpei-89] In 2016, the Census of Agriculture counted 1,353 farms on the Island, which is a 9.5% decrease from the previous census (2011).[^cite_note-90] During the 20th century, [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potatoes) were grown as a [cash crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crop) across more than a million acres of farmland. Traditionally, crops were grown on a [rotational basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_rotation): common examples would be either potatoes, hay, clover, or oats being grown on a piece of land at any given time.[^cite_note-91] More recently, the total amount of farms used for potatoes has decreased, but the province is still Canada's largest supplier of the crop.[^cite_note-PEICE-21] The number of acres under potato production in 2010 was 88,000,[^cite_note-PEIPotato-38] while [soy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy) accounted for 55,000.[^cite_note-92] There are approximately 330 potato growers on PEI, with the grand majority of these being family farms, often with multiple generations working together.[^cite_note-PEIPotato-38] The province currently accounts for a quarter of Canada's total potato production, producing approximately 1.3 billion kilograms (1,400,000 [short tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton)) annually.[^cite_note-PEIPotato-38][^cite_note-93] Comparatively, the state of [Idaho](14607.md) produces approximately 6.2 billion kilograms (6,800,000 short tons) annually, with a population approximately 9.5 times greater.[^cite_note-94] The province is a major producer of seed potatoes, exporting to more than twenty countries around the world.[^cite_note-PEIPotato-38] An estimated total of 70% of the land is cultivated and 25% of all potatoes grown in Canada originate from P.E.I.[^cite_note-95] The processing of frozen fried potatoes, green vegetables, and berries is a leading business activity.[^cite_note-96] As a legacy of the island's colonial history, the provincial government enforces strict rules for non-resident land ownership, especially since the PEI *Lands Protection Act* of 1982.[^cite_note-iraclpa-97] Residents and corporations are limited to maximum holdings of 400 and 1,200 hectares respectively. There are also restrictions on non-resident ownership of shorelines.[^cite_note-iraclpa-97] Some groups, however, such as the Buddhist organization [Bliss and Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bliss_and_Wisdom), have been accused of exploiting loopholes in the lands protection legislation to purchase vast quantities of land.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]



[Lobster traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster_trap) at the harbour of [Naufrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naufrage,_Prince_Edward_Island). [Lobster fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster_fishing) continues to be a major economic sector for coastal communities.

Many of the province's coastal communities rely upon shellfish harvesting, particularly [lobster fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster_fishing)[^cite_note-100] as well as [oyster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster) fishing and [mussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussel) farming. The island's economy has grown significantly over the last decade in key areas of innovation. Aerospace, bioscience, [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology), and renewable energy have been a focus for growth and diversification. Aerospace alone now accounts for over 25% of the province's international exports and is the island's fourth largest industry at \$355 million in annual sales. The bioscience industry employs over 1,300 people and generates over \$150 million in sales.[^cite_note-101] The sale of carbonated beverages such as beer and soft drinks in non-refillable containers, such as [aluminum cans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum_can) or [plastic bottles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_bottle), was banned in 1976 as an environmental measure in response to public concerns over litter. Beer and soft drink companies opted to use refillable glass bottles for their products which were redeemable at stores and bottle depots. Though often environmental and economic agendas may be at odds, the ‘ban the can’ legislation, along with being environmentally driven, was also economically motivated as it protected jobs. Seaman's Beverages, a bottling company and carbonated beverage manufacturer, was established in 1939 and a major employer in Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-102] Making it illegal to retail cans led to a bigger share of the carbonated beverage market for Seamans. Seamans Beverages was eventually acquired by Pepsi Bottling Group Inc in 2002 prior to the lifting of the legislation.[^cite_note-103]



Farming on Prince Edward Island

The introduction of recycling programs for cans and plastic bottles in neighbouring provinces in recent years (also using a redemption system) has seen the provincial government introduce legislation to reverse this ban with the restriction lifted on May 3, 2008.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] Prior to harmonization in 2013, Prince Edward Island had one of Canada's highest provincial retail [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) rates at 10%. On April 1, 2013, the provincial tax was harmonized with the federal [Goods and Services Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_and_Services_Tax_\(Canada\)), and became known as the *[harmonized sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonized_sales_tax)*.[^cite_note-107] The 15% tax is applied to almost all goods and services except some clothing, food and home heating fuel. This rate is the same as the neighbouring [Atlantic provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_provinces), with the exception of Nova Scotia. The provincial government provides consumer protection in the form of regulation for certain items, ranging from apartment rent increases to petroleum products including [gas](23639.md), [diesel](81761.md), [propane](23643.md) and [heating oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating_oil). These are regulated through the [Prince Edward Island Regulatory and Appeals Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Regulatory_and_Appeals_Commission) (IRAC).[^cite_note-108] IRAC is authorized to limit the number of companies who are permitted to sell petroleum products. As of 2015, the median family income on Prince Edward Island is \$76,607/year.[^cite_note-109] The minimum wage is \$16.00/hour as of October 1, 2024.[^cite_note-110]

### Energy



A [wind farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm) at [North Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Cape_\(Prince_Edward_Island\))

See also: [List of generating stations in Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_generating_stations_in_Prince_Edward_Island)

Since 1918, [Maritime Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Electric) has delivered electricity to customers on the Island. The utility is currently owned and operated by [Fortis Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortis_Inc.)[^cite_note-111] Approximately twenty-five percent of electricity consumed on the island is generated from [renewable energy](25784.md) (largely [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine)); the provincial government had set a renewable energy target for 30–50% for electricity consumed by 2015, though this goal has not been met. The total capacity of wind power on the island is 204 MW from 89 turbines.[^cite_note-112] There are eight wind farms on the island, the largest being [West Cape Wind Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Cape_Wind_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) with a capacity of 99 MW from 55 turbines. All of the turbines have been manufactured by [Vestas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestas): the [Vestas V-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestas_V-80?action=edit\&redlink=1), Vestas V90, and [Vestas V-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestas_V-47?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-113] A thermal oil-fired generating station, the [Charlottetown Thermal Generating Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Thermal_Generating_Station), is used sometimes for emergencies. It is being decommissioned.[^cite_note-114] A second thermal generation station exists in [Borden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borden-Carleton), the [Borden Generating Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borden_Generating_Station).[^cite_note-115] The majority of electricity consumed on Prince Edward Island comes from New Brunswick through [undersea cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_communications_cable).[^cite_note-116] A recent \$140M upgrade brought the capacity of the cable system from 200 MW to 560 MW.[^cite_note-117] The [Point Lepreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Lepreau_Nuclear_Generating_Station) nuclear plant in [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick) was closed for refurbishments from 2008 to 2012, resulting in a steep price hike of about 25 per cent, but the province later subsidized rates.[^cite_note-118] Residents were to pay 11.2 per cent more for electricity when the [harmonized sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonized_sales_tax) was adopted in April 2013, according to the P.E.I. Energy Accord that was tabled in the legislature on December 7, 2012.[^cite_note-119] and passed as the *Electric Power (Energy Accord Continuation) Amendment Act*, which establishes electric pricing from April 1, 2013, to March 1, 2016. Regulatory powers are derived for IRAC from the *Electric Power Act*.[^cite_note-iraclpa-97]

## Education

Prince Edward Island's public school system has an English school district named the [Public Schools Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Schools_Branch) (previously the English Language School Board),[^cite_note-120] as well as a Francophone district, the [Commission scolaire de langue française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_scolaire_de_langue_fran%C3%A7aise). The English language district has a total of 10 secondary schools and 54 intermediate and elementary schools while the Francophone district has 6 schools covering all grades. 22 per cent of the student population is enrolled in French [immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_immersion). This is one of the highest levels in the country.



The SDU Main Building at the [University of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Prince_Edward_Island), the only university on the island

Three public [post-secondary institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) operate in the province, including one university, and two [colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_\(Canada\)). The [University of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Prince_Edward_Island) is the province's only public university, and is located in the city of [Charlottetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown). The university was created by the Island legislature to replace [Prince of Wales College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales_College) and [St. Dunstan's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Dunstan's_University). UPEI is also home to the [Atlantic Veterinary College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Veterinary_College), which offers the region's only veterinary medicine program.

## Government and politics

Main articles: [Government of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Prince_Edward_Island) and [Politics of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Prince_Edward_Island)

The provincial government is responsible for such areas as health and social services, education, economic development, labour legislation and civil law. These matters of government are overseen in the provincial capital, Charlottetown. Prince Edward Island is governed by a [parliamentary government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) within the construct of [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy); the [monarchy in Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_in_Prince_Edward_Island) is the foundation of the executive, [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), and [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) branches.[^cite_note-121] The sovereign is [King Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Charles_III), who also serves as head of state of [14 other Commonwealth countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), each of Canada's nine other provinces, and the Canadian federal realm, and resides in the United Kingdom. As such, the King's representative, the [Lieutenant Governor of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Prince_Edward_Island) (presently [Wassim Salamoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassim_Salamoun)), carries out most of the royal duties in Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-122]



The exterior of [Province House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_House_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)), meeting place for the [Legislative Assembly of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Prince_Edward_Island)

The direct participation of the royal and viceroyal figures in any of these areas of governance is limited; in practice, their use of the executive powers is directed by [the Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Prince_Edward_Island), a committee of [ministers of the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Crown) responsible to the unicameral, elected [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Prince_Edward_Island) and chosen and headed by the [Premier of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Prince_Edward_Island) (presently [Rob Lantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Lantz)), the [head of government](37570.md). To ensure the stability of government, the lieutenant governor will usually appoint as premier the person who is the current leader of the political party that can obtain the confidence of a [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)) in the Legislative Assembly. The leader of the party with the second-most seats usually becomes the [Leader of His Majesty's Loyal Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)) (presently [Hal Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Perry_\(politician\))) and is part of an adversarial parliamentary system intended to keep the government in check.[^cite_note-123] Each of the 27 [Members of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly) (MLA) is elected by simple plurality in an [electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_electoral_district). General elections are called by the lieutenant governor for the first Monday in October four years after the previous election, or may be called earlier on the advice of the premier.[^cite_note-124] Historically, politics in the province have been dominated by the [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Liberal_Party) and the [Progressive Conservative Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Prince_Edward_Island) since the province joined Confederation. From the [2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Prince_Edward_Island_general_election), the [Green Party of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Prince_Edward_Island) gained a small representation in the Legislative Assembly, and in the [2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Prince_Edward_Island_general_election) gained an additional six seats to form the [Official Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Opposition). The PC party increased its majority in the [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Prince_Edward_Island_general_election), while the Liberals regained Official Opposition status. The Greens lost six seats, returning to third party status. The Mi'kmaq Confederacy of PEI is the tribal council and provincial-territorial organization in the province that represents both the Lennox Island and Abegweit First Nations.

Prince Edward Island is divided into three [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_of_Prince_Edward_Island) that have historically been used as administrative divisions for the provincial government, and prior to [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) (in 1873), the colonial government. ## Health care and sanitation

See also: [Health care in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_Canada)

The province has a single health administrative region (or district health authority) called [Health PEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_PEI). Health PEI receives funding for its operations and is regulated by the [Department of Health and Wellness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_and_Wellness_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)). Many PEI homes and businesses are served by central sewage collection and treatment systems. These are operated either by a municipality or a private utility. Many industrial operations have their own wastewater treatment facilities. Staff members with the [Department of Environment, Water and Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Environment,_Water_and_Climate_Change?action=edit\&redlink=1) provide advice to operators, as needed, on proper system maintenance.[^cite_note-129] The [IRAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Regulatory_and_Appeals_Commission) regulates municipal water and sewer in the province,[^cite_note-iraclpa-97] now under the *Environmental Protection Act*.[^cite_note-iraclpa-97] Since around 1900, the residents of the City of Charlottetown have benefited from a central sanitary sewer service. Early disposal practices, while advanced for their time, eventually were found to compromise the ecological integrity of the nearby [Hillsborough River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_River_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)) and the [Charlottetown Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Harbour). By 1974, the commission had spearheaded the development of a primary wastewater treatment plant, known as the [Charlottetown Pollution Control Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Pollution_Control_Plant?action=edit\&redlink=1), together with the construction of several pumping stations along the city's waterfront, and outfall piping deep into the Hillsborough River.[^cite_note-130] Until 2016, Prince Edward Island was the only province in Canada that did not provide [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Canada) services through its hospitals. Until that time, the last abortion that had been performed in the province was in 1982 prior to the opening of the [Queen Elizabeth Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Hospital,_Prince_Edward_Island) which saw the closure of the Roman Catholic-affiliated [Charlottetown Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Hospital) and the non-denominational [Prince Edward Island Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Hospital); a condition of the "merger" being that abortions not be performed in the province. In 1988, following the court decision *[R. v. Morgentaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._v._Morgentaler)*, the then-opposition [Progressive Conservative Party of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Prince_Edward_Island) tabled a motion demanding that the ban on abortions be upheld at the province's hospitals; the then-governing [Prince Edward Island Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Liberal_Party) under Premier [Joe Ghiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Ghiz) acquiesced and the ban was upheld.[^cite_note-131] Until more local access was guaranteed, the Government of Prince Edward Island funded abortions for women who travelled to another province. Women from Prince Edward Island also travelled to the nearest private user-pay clinic, where they were required pay for the procedure using their own funds. Formerly this was the [Morgentaler Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morgentaler) in [Fredericton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericton), New Brunswick[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] until this clinic closed due to lack of funds in July 2014.[^cite_note-137] The clinic was reopened under new ownership in 2016 as Clinic 554 with expanded services.[^cite_note-138] During that gap, women had to travel to Halifax or further. In 2016, the Liberal government led by Premier Wade MacLauchlan announced they would open a women's reproductive health clinic to provide abortions within the province.[^cite_note-139] Abortions are now provided in Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-140]

## Transportation

Prince Edward Island's transportation network has traditionally revolved around its seaports of Charlottetown, Summerside, Borden, [Georgetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown,_Prince_Edward_Island), and [Souris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Souris). Before the 20th century, the island's road network was only used by pedestrians, horse-drawn carts and carriages, and by 1908, there were only seven motorized [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) on the island. In the same year, the [Prince Edward Island automobile ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_automobile_ban) was enacted, banning the use of motor cars due to residents' concerns over their safety, and complaints that the loud vehicles frightened horses and livestock. The ban was partially lifted after a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) in 1913 and completely rescinded in 1919.[^cite_note-islandmag5-141]

[The Prince Edward Island Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince_Edward_Island_Railway), the construction of which started in 1871, connected all major towns of the island and ran from [Tignish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tignish) in the west to [Elmira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmira,_Prince_Edward_Island) in the west. The railway system was abandoned by [CN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Railway) in 1989 in favour of an agreement with the federal government to improve major highways.



In May 1997, the [Confederation Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Bridge) was opened, providing a fixed link between the island and the mainland.

Until May 1997, the province was linked by two passenger-vehicle ferry services to the mainland: one, provided by [Marine Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Atlantic), operated year-round between [Borden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borden-Carleton,_Prince_Edward_Island) and [Cape Tormentine, New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Tormentine,_New_Brunswick); the other, provided by [Northumberland Ferries Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland_Ferries_Limited), operates seasonally between [Wood Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_Islands,_Prince_Edward_Island) and [Caribou, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribou,_Nova_Scotia). A third ferry service provided by [CTMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coop%C3%A9rative_de_transport_maritime_et_a%C3%A9rien) operates all year round with seasonal times between [Souris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souris,_Prince_Edward_Island) and [Cap-aux-Meules, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-aux-Meules,_Quebec), in the [Magdalen Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_Islands). In May 1997, the [Confederation Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Bridge) opened, connecting Borden-Carleton to [Cape Jourimain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Jourimain,_New_Brunswick), New Brunswick. The world's longest bridge over ice-covered waters,[^cite_note-142] it replaced the Marine Atlantic ferry service. Since then, the Confederation Bridge's assured transportation link to the mainland has altered the province's tourism and agricultural and fisheries export economies.

The island has the highest concentration of roadways in Canada. The provincially managed portion of the network consists of around 4,500 kilometres (2,800 mi) of paved roadways and 1,800 kilometres (1,100 mi) of non-paved or clay roads.[^cite_note-143] The province has very strict laws regarding use of roadside signs. [Billboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_\(advertising\)) and the use of portable signs are banned. There are standard direction information signs on roads in the province for various businesses and attractions in the immediate area. The [by-laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-law) of some municipalities also restrict the types of permanent signs that may be installed on private property.

The island has [two main airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Prince_Edward_Island): Several airlines service the [Charlottetown Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Airport) (CYYG); the [Summerside Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerside_Airport) (CYSU) is an additional option for general aviation.

There is an extensive bicycling and hiking trail that spans the island. The [Confederation Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Trail) is a 470 kilometres (290 mi) recreational trail system. It was developed in the 1990s, on the land of the former Prince Edward Island Railway, following the abandonment of all railway lines in the province.

## Culture

### Arts

See also: [Music of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Prince_Edward_Island)

The island's cultural traditions of art, music and creative writing are supported through the public education system. There is an annual arts festival, the [Charlottetown Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Festival), hosted at the [Confederation Centre of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Centre_of_the_Arts).



[Green Gables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Gables_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)) in [Cavendish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish,_Prince_Edward_Island) was a farm featured in *[Anne of Green Gables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_of_Green_Gables)*.

[Lucy Maud Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Maud_Montgomery), who was born in Clifton (now New London) in 1874, drew inspiration from the land during the late [Victorian Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Era) for the setting of her classic novel *[Anne of Green Gables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_of_Green_Gables)* (1908). The musical play *[Anne of Green Gables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_of_Green_Gables_%E2%80%93_The_Musical)* has run every year at the Charlottetown festival for more than four decades. The sequel, *[Anne & Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_&_Gilbert)*, premiered in the Playhouse in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Prince_Edward_Island) in 2005. The actual location of [Green Gables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Gables_\(Prince_Edward_Island\)), the house featured in Montgomery's *Anne* books, is in [Cavendish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish,_Prince_Edward_Island), on the north shore of PEI. Prince Edward Island's documented music history begins in the 19th century with religious music, some written by the local pump and block maker and organ-importer Watson Duchemin. Several big bands including the Sons of Temperance Band and the Charlottetown Brass Band were active. Today, Acadian, Celtic, folk, and rock music prevail, with exponents including [Gene MacLellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_MacLellan), his daughter [Catherine MacLellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_MacLellan), [Al Tuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Tuck), [Lennie Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lennie_Gallant), [Two Hours Traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Hours_Traffic) and [Paper Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_Lions). The celebrated singer-songwriter [Stompin' Tom Connors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stompin'_Tom_Connors) spent his formative years in [Skinners Pond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinners_Pond,_Prince_Edward_Island). Celtic music is certainly the most common traditional music on the island, with fiddling and step dancing being very common. This tradition, largely Scottish, Irish and Acadian in origin is very similar to the music of Cape Breton and to a lesser extent, Newfoundland and is unique to the region. Due to the Islands influence as a former Highlander Clans Scottish colony, a March 4/4 for bagpipes was composed in honour of Prince Edward Island.[^cite_note-144]

### Festivals

See also: [Category\:Festivals in Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Festivals_in_Prince_Edward_Island)



The [Cavendish Beach Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Beach_Music_Festival) is an annual music festival held in mid-July.

There is an annual arts festival, the [Charlottetown Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Festival), hosted at the [Confederation Centre of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_Centre_of_the_Arts) as well as the [Island Fringe Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Fringe_Festival) that takes place around Charlottetown.[^cite_note-145] An annual jazz festival, the P.E.I. Jazz and Blues Festival.[^cite_note-P.E.I._Jazz-146] is a week-long series of concerts taking place at several venues including Murphy's Community Centre, outdoor stages, and churches in Charlottetown. The moving of its date to mid-August caused in 2011 a serious loss in funding from Ottawa's regional development agency ACOA.[^cite_note-ACOA-147] The musician's line up in 2011 included [Oliver Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Jones_\(pianist\)), [Sophie Milman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Milman), [Matt Dusk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Dusk), [Jack de Keyzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_de_Keyzer), [Jack Semple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Semple), [Meaghan Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaghan_Smith) and [Jimmy Bowskill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Bowskill). There is also [Canada Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Rocks), and the [Cavendish Beach Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Beach_Music_Festival). With agriculture and fishery playing a large role in the economy, P.E.I. has been marketed as a food tourism destination.[^cite_note-148]

### Sports

The province is home to a [major junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_junior) ice hockey team, the [Charlottetown Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottetown_Islanders) of the [Quebec Maritimes Junior Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Maritimes_Junior_Hockey_League), and a Junior A ice hockey team, the [Summerside Western Capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerside_Western_Capitals), which play in the [Maritime Junior A Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Junior_A_Hockey_League). The [Prince Edward Island Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Senators) played in the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) from 1993 to 1996. Relocated from [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut), the team was affiliated with the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators). The team eventually ended up in [Binghamton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton,_New_York), New York and became the [Binghamton Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton_Senators) in 2002. The [Island Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Storm) were a professional basketball team that played in the [National Basketball League of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_of_Canada). The team was founded in 2011 as the Summerside Storm for the league's inaugural season and became the Island Storm in 2013. The team was granted a one-year leave of absence in 2021 but have not returned since. Prince Edward Island has hosted three [Canada Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Games): two winter editions in 1991 and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Canada_Winter_Games); and a summer edition in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Canada_Summer_Games). The [UPEI Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPEI_Panthers) represent the [University of Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Prince_Edward_Island) in the [Atlantic University Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_University_Sport) conference of [U Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Sports). The Holland Hurricanes represent [Holland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_College) in the [Atlantic Collegiate Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Collegiate_Athletic_Association) conference of the [Canadian Collegiate Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Collegiate_Athletic_Association). The island was a part of the [IIGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Island_Games_Association), the association that organises the biannual [Island Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Games). During their time participating in the multi sport competition, they won 6 gold medals, 6 silver medals and 9 bronze medals, totalling to 21 medals awarded during the 9 times they competed. The island resigned from the IIGA in 2011.[^cite_note-149]
