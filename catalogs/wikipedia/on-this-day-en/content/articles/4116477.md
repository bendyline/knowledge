**Siverek** ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Սեւավերակ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Armenian): *Sevaverag*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'black ruins';[^cite_note-2] [Zazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazaki_language): *Soyreg*; [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): *Sêwreg*)[^cite_note-3] is a municipality and [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Turkey) of [Şanlıurfa Province](1353138.md), Turkey.[^cite_note-4] Its area is 3,936 km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-5] and its population is 267,942 (2022).[^cite_note-tuik-1] Siverek is in the Şanlıurfa province but is geographically closer to the larger city of [Diyarbakır](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbak%C4%B1r) (approx. 83 km).

## Etymology

Siverek was historically known in medieval Arabic as **Hisn ar-Ran** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حصن الران), which was corrupted into Greek as **Chasanara** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Χασαναρᾶ), as found in the [Escorial Taktikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escorial_Taktikon).[^cite_note-Dumbarton_Oaks_1991-6]

The word "Siverek" is of [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) origin. In [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) sources, it is mentioned as **Sevaverag**. The literal meaning of this name used for Siverek in Byzantine and Armenian sources is Sev "black" and Averag "ruins".[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Other ways to spell Sevaverag include **Sebabarak**, **Sebabarok**, **Sevaverak**, **Severags** and **Suveyda**.[^cite_note-9]

## History

The town came under Byzantine control sometime after AD 956 and had become the seat of a *[strategos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategos#Byzantine_use)* by the early 970s.[^cite_note-Dumbarton_Oaks_1991-6] Together with [Edessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edessa), [Gargar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerger), [Samosata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsat) and [Hisn Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad%C4%B1yaman) formed part of the Byzantine defence system up to the 1060s when 200 Frankish horsemen were stationed there.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeihammer2017115-10]

In the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) period, Siverek was within the [Diyarbekir vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbekir_vilayet), and it had several Christian settlements.[^cite_note-\:022-11]

### 2026 school shooting

This section is an excerpt from [2026 Siverek school shooting](82950772.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2026_Siverek_school_shooting\&action=edit)]

On 14 April 2026, a school shooting occurred at the Ahmet Koyuncu Vocational and Technical Anatolian High School in Siverek, [Şanlıurfa Province](1353138.md), Turkey. Sixteen people were injured before the perpetrator, 19-year-old Ömer Ket, committed suicide. Twenty-eight hours after the shooting, [another school shooting](82958189.md) occurred in the [Onikişubat](39632302.md) district of [Kahramanmaraş](727324.md).[^cite_note-2026_Siverek_school_shooting_Reuters-12]

## Demographics

9,275 Armenians lived in the [kaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaza) on the eve of the First World War according to the [Armenian Patriarchate of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople). They had eight churches and three schools. 5,450 Armenians and 1,200 [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) lived in the town of Siverek. 6,550 Assyrians lived in 32 surrounding villages.[^cite_note-vgm-13] Armenian men were massacred in May 1915, followed by the deportation of the women and children, as part of the [Armenian genocide](140376.md).[^cite_note-vgm-13] [Faiz El-Ghusein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faiz_El-Ghusein) reported that the road between Urfa and Severek was littered with corpses.[^cite_note-vgm-13]

## Politics

In common with other districts of Şanlıurfa, business and politics in Siverek are strongly influenced, even controlled, by a powerful clan. Siverek is the home town of [Sedat Bucak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedat_Bucak), the former [DYP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DYP) member of parliament who survived the car crash in the [Susurluk scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susurluk_scandal). He is the leader of the Bucak tribe, one of whom has represented the area in the Turkish Parliament since its foundation. Sedat Bucak remains a friend of former DYP leader [Mehmet Ağar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmet_A%C4%9Far).[^cite_note-14] In the [local elections in March 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Turkish_local_elections), Şehmus Aydın was elected mayor.[^cite_note-15] He resigned due to health problems in 2020, and was succeeded by Ayşe Çakmak (AKP).[^cite_note-16] The current [kaymakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymakam) is Musa Aydemir.[^cite_note-17]
