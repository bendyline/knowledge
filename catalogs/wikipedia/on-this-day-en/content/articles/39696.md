An **oak** is a [hardwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwood) tree or shrub in the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) ***Quercus*** of the [beech family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagaceae). They have spirally arranged leaves, often with lobed edges, and a nut called an [acorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn), borne within a cup. The genus is widely distributed in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), with some 500 species,[^cite_note-powo-1] both [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) and [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen). Fossil oaks date back to the Middle [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene). [Molecular phylogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_phylogeny) shows that the genus is divided into [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) and New World [clades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade), but many oak species [hybridise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)) freely, making the genus's history difficult to resolve.

Ecologically, oaks are [keystone species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_species) in habitats from Mediterranean semi-desert to subtropical [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest). They live in association with many kinds of [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi), including [truffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truffle). Oaks support more than 950 species of [caterpillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar), many kinds of [gall wasp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall_wasp) which form distinctive [galls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall) (roundish woody lumps such as the [oak apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_apple)), and a large number of pests and diseases. Oak leaves and acorns contain enough [tannin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannin) to be toxic to cattle, but pigs are able to digest them safely. Oak timber is strong and hard, and has found many uses in construction and furniture-making. The bark was traditionally used for [tanning leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_leather). [Wine barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_\(wine\)) are made of oak; these are used for aging alcoholic beverages such as [sherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry) and [whisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisky), giving them a range of flavours, colours, and aromas. The spongy bark of the [cork oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_oak) is used to make traditional wine bottle corks. Almost a third of oak species are threatened with extinction due to climate change, [invasive pests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_pests), and [habitat loss](1707053.md).

In culture, the oak tree is a symbol of strength and serves as the [national tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_tree) of many countries. In Indo-European and related religions, the oak is associated with [thunder gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_god). Individual oak trees of cultural significance include the [Royal Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak) in Britain, the [Charter Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_Oak) in the United States, and the [Guernica Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gernikako_Arbola) in the [Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)).

## Etymology

The [generic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) name *Quercus* is [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "oak", derived from [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) *\*kwerkwu-*, "oak", which is also the origin of the name "[fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir)", another important or sacred tree in [Indo-European culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_culture).[^cite_note-2] The word "cork", for the bark of the [cork oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_oak), similarly derives from *Quercus*.[^cite_note-3] The common name "oak" is from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) *ac* (seen in placenames such as [Acton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acton,_London), from *ac* + *tun*, "oak town/village"),[^cite_note-4] which in turn is from [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) *\*aiks*, "oak".[^cite_note-5]

## Description

Oaks are [hardwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwood) ([dicotyledonous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicotyledon)) trees, [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) or [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen), with spirally arranged leaves, often with [lobate margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentate_leaf); some have [serrated leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serrated_leaves) or [entire leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entire_\(botany\)) with smooth margins. Many deciduous species are [marcescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcescent), not dropping dead leaves until spring. Oaks can be grouped into "[red oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_oak)" that have soft bristles on their leaves' lobes, and "[white oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_oak)" that don't have those bristles.[^cite_note-JCFC-6]

In spring, a single oak tree [produces both](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoecy) male and female flowers. The [staminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staminate) (male) flowers are arranged in [catkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catkin), while the small [pistillate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistillate) (female) flowers[^cite_note-7] produce an [acorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn) (a kind of [nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\))) contained in a capped [peduncle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peduncle_\(botany\)) called a [cupule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calybium_and_cupule).[^cite_note-8] Each acorn usually contains one seed.[^cite_note-JCFC-6] The acorns and leaves contain [tannic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannic_acid),[^cite_note-9] which helps to guard against fungi and insects.[^cite_note-10] There are some 500 extant species of oaks.[^cite_note-powo-1]

Trees in the genus are usually large and slow-growing; *Q. alba* can reach an age of 600 years, a diameter of 4 metres (13 ft) and a height of 44 metres (144 ft).[^cite_note-Core-11] The [Granit oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granit_oak) in Bulgaria, a *Q. robur* specimen, has an estimated age of 1,637 years, making it the oldest oak in Europe.[^cite_note-12] The Wi'aaSal tree, a [live oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_oak) in the reservation of the [Pechanga Band of Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechanga_Band_of_Indians), California, is at least 1,000 years old, and might be as much as 2,000 years old, which would make it the oldest oak in the US.[^cite_note-13] Among the smallest oaks is *[Q. acuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_acuta)*, the Japanese evergreen oak. It forms a bush or small tree to a height of some 9 metres (30 ft).[^cite_note-14]

## Evolution

### Fossil history

Potential records of *Quercus* have been reported from [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous) deposits in North America and East Asia. These are not considered definitive, as macrofossils older than the [Paleogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleogene), and possibly from before the [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) are mostly poorly preserved without critical features for certain identification. Amongst the oldest unequivocal records of *Quercus* are pollen from Austria, dating to the [Paleocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleocene)-Eocene boundary, around 55 million years ago. The oldest records of *Quercus* in North America are from [Oregon](26811621.md), dating to the Middle Eocene, around 44 million years ago, with the oldest records in Asia from the Middle Eocene of Japan; both forms have affinities to the *[Cyclobalanopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclobalanopsis)* group.[^cite_note-15]

### Internal phylogeny

Molecular techniques for phylogenetic analysis show that the genus *Quercus* consisted of [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) and [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) clades.[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] The entire [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome) of *Quercus robur* (the pedunculate oak) has been [sequenced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing),[^cite_note-21] revealing an array of [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation) that may underlie the evolution of longevity and [disease resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_disease_resistance) in oaks.[^cite_note-22] In addition, hundreds of oak species have been compared (at [RAD-seq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAD-seq) loci), allowing a detailed phylogeny to be constructed. However, the high signal of [introgressive hybridization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introgressive_hybridization) (the transfer of genetic material by repeated backcrossing with hybrid offspring) in the genus has made it difficult to resolve an unambiguous, unitary history of oaks. ### Taxonomy

See also: [List of Quercus species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Quercus_species)

#### Taxonomic history

The genus *Quercus* was [circumscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumscription_\(taxonomy\)) by [Carl Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus) in the first edition of his 1753 *[Species Plantarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_Plantarum)*.[^cite_note-24] He described 15 species within the new genus, providing type specimens for 10 of these, and giving names but no types for *[Q. cerris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_cerris)*, *[Q. coccifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_coccifera)*, *[Q. ilex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_ilex)*, *[Q. smilax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_smilax)*, and *[Q. suber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_suber)*.[^cite_note-25] He chose *[Q. robur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)*, the pedunculate oak, as the [type species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_species) for the genus.[^cite_note-26]

A 2017 classification of *Quercus*, based on multiple molecular phylogenetic studies, divided the genus into two [subgenera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgenera) and eight [sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_\(botany\)):[^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Subgenus *Quercus* – the New World clade (or high-latitude clade), mostly native to North America&#x20;

  - Section *Lobatae* Loudon – North American red oaks

  - Section *Protobalanus* (Trelease) O.Schwarz – North American intermediate oaks

  - Section *Ponticae* Stef. – with a [disjunct distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunct_distribution) between western [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) and western North America

  - Section *Virentes* Loudon – American southern live oaks

  - Section *Quercus* – white oaks from North America and Eurasia

- Subgenus *Cerris* Oerst. – the Old World clade (or mid-latitude clade), exclusively native to Eurasia&#x20;

  - Section *Cyclobalanopsis* Oerst. – cycle-cup oaks of East Asia

  - Section *Cerris* Dumort. – cerris oaks of subtropical and temperate Eurasia and North Africa

  - Section *Ilex* Loudon – ilex oaks of tropical and subtropical Eurasia and North Africa

The subgenus division supports the evolutionary diversification of oaks among two distinct clades: the Old World clade (subgenus *Cerris*), including oaks that diversified in [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia); and the New World clade (subgenus *Quercus*), oaks that diversified mainly in the [Americas](29833.md).[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-Manos_Hipp_2021-28]

#### Subgenus *Quercus*

Main article: [*Quercus* subg. *Quercus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_subg._Quercus)

- Sect. *Lobatae* ([synonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonym_\(taxonomy\)) *Erythrobalanus*), the [red oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythrobalanus) of North America, [Central America](6121.md) and northern [South America](26769.md). [Styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_\(botany\)) are long; the acorns mature in 18 months and taste very bitter. The inside of the acorn shell appears woolly. The actual nut is encased in a thin, clinging, papery skin. The leaves typically have sharp lobe tips, with spiny bristles at the lobe.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Sect. *Protobalanus*, the [canyon live oak and its relatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protobalanus), in the southwestern [United States](3434750.md) and northwest [Mexico](3966054.md). Styles are short; the acorns mature in 18 months and taste very bitter. The inside of the acorn shell appears woolly. The leaves typically have sharp lobe tips, with bristles at the lobe tip.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Sect. *Ponticae,* a disjunct including just two species. Styles are short, and the acorns mature in 12 months. The leaves have large [stipules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stipule), high secondary veins, and are highly toothed.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Sect. *Virentes,* the southern live oaks of the Americas. Styles are short, and the acorns mature in 12 months. The leaves are evergreen or subevergreen.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Sect. *Quercus* (synonyms *Lepidobalanus* and *Leucobalanus*), the [white oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidobalanus) of [Europe](9239.md), [Asia](689.md) and North America. Trees or shrubs that produce nuts, specifically acorns, as fruits. Acorns mature in one year for annual trees and two years for biannual trees. Acorn is encapsulated by a spiny cupule as characterized by the family Fagaceae. Flowers in the *Quercus* genera produce one flower per node, with three or six styles, as well as three or six [ovaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovary_\(botany\)), respectively. The leaves mostly lack a bristle on their lobe tips, which are usually rounded. The type species is *[Quercus robur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)*.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

  [^cite_note-29]

#### Subgenus *Cerris*

Main article: [*Quercus* subg. *Cerris*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_subg._Cerris)

The type species is *Quercus cerris*.

- Sect. *[Cyclobalanopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Quercus_species#Section_Cyclobalanopsis)*, the [ring-cupped oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Quercus_species#Section_Cyclobalanopsis) of eastern and southeastern Asia. These are evergreen trees growing 10–40 metres (33–131 feet) tall. They are distinct from subgenus *Quercus* in that they have acorns with distinctive cups bearing concrescent rings of scales; they commonly also have densely clustered acorns, though this does not apply to all of the species. Species of *[Cyclobalanopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclobalanopsis)* are common in the evergreen subtropical [laurel forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_forest), which extend from southern Japan, southern Korea, and Taiwan across southern China and northern Indochina to the eastern Himalayas, in association with trees of the genus *[Castanopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castanopsis)* and the laurel family ([Lauraceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauraceae)).

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Sect. *Cerris*, the [Turkey oak and its relatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerris) of Europe and Asia. Styles are long; acorns mature in 18 months and taste very bitter. The inside of the acorn's shell is hairless. Its leaves typically have sharp lobe tips, with bristles at the lobe tip.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

- Sect. *[Ilex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Quercus_species#Section_Ilex)*, the Ilex oak and its relatives of Eurasia and northern [Africa](5334607.md). Styles are medium-long; acorns mature in 12–24 months, appearing hairy on the inside. The leaves are evergreen, with bristle-like extensions on the teeth.

  [^cite_note-DenkGrimManoDeng17-27]

The genus *Quercus* is native to the Northern Hemisphere and includes [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) and [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen) species extending from [cool temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cool_temperate) to [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) latitudes in the [Americas](29833.md), Asia, Europe, and North Africa. North America has the largest number of oak species, with approximately 160 species in Mexico, of which 109 are endemic, and about 90 in the United States. The second greatest area of oak diversity is China, with approximately 100 species.[^cite_note-38]

In the Americas, *Quercus* is widespread from [Vancouver](32706.md) and [Nova Scotia](21184.md) in the south of Canada, south to [Mexico](3966054.md) and across the whole of the eastern United States. It is present in a small area of the west of [Cuba](5042481.md); in [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) it occurs mainly above 1,000 metres (3,300 ft).[^cite_note-Nixon_2006-39] The genus crossed the [isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama) when the northern and southern continents came together[^cite_note-Hooghiemstra_2006-40] and is present as one species, *[Q. humboldtii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_humboldtii)*, above 1,000 metres in Colombia.[^cite_note-Nixon_2006-39] The oaks of North America are of many sections (*Protobalanus*, *Lobatae*, *Ponticae*, *Quercus*, and *Virentes*) along with related genera such as *Notholithocarpus*.[^cite_note-Liu_Yang_Tian_Yang_2023-37]

In the Old World, oaks of section *Quercus* extend across the whole of Europe including European Russia apart from the far north, and north Africa (north of the Sahara) from Morocco to Libya. In Mediterranean Europe, they are joined by oaks of the sections *Cerris* and *Ilex*, which extend across Turkey, the Middle East, Iran, Afghanistan and Pakista&#x6E;*,* while section *Ponticae* is endemic to the western Caucasus in [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Georgia](48768.md). Oaks of section *Cyclobalanopsis* extend in a narrow belt along the Himalayas to cover mainland and island Southeast Asia as far as [Sumatra](28679.md), [Java](69336.md), [Borneo](4518.md), and [Palawan](240395.md).[^cite_note-powo-1][^cite_note-41] Finally, oaks of multiple sections (*Cyclobalanopsis*, *Ilex*, *Cerris*, *Quercus* and related genera like *Lithocarpus* and *Castanopsis*) extend across east Asia including China, Korea, and Japan.[^cite_note-Liu_Yang_Tian_Yang_2023-37]

## Ecology



Caterpillars of the North American oak leafroller, *[Archips semiferanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archips_semiferanus)*, can defoliate oak forests.

Oaks are [keystone species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_species) in a wide range of habitats from Mediterranean semi-desert to subtropical rainforest. They are important components of hardwood forests; some species grow in associations with members of the [Ericaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ericaceae) in [oak–heath forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak%E2%80%93heath_forest).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Several kinds of [truffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truffle), including two well-known varieties – black [Périgord truffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuber_melanosporum)[^cite_note-44] and the white Piedmont truffle[^cite_note-r13-45] – have symbiotic relationships with oak trees. Similarly, many other fungi, such as *[Ramaria flavosaponaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramaria_flavosaponaria)*, associate with oaks.[^cite_note-Nirschl-46][^cite_note-Petersen-47]

Oaks support more than 950 species of caterpillars, an important food source for many birds.[^cite_note-48] Mature oak trees shed widely varying numbers of acorns (known collectively as mast) annually, with large quantities in [mast years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_\(botany\)). This may be a [predator satiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator_satiation) strategy, increasing the chance that some acorns will survive to germination.[^cite_note-Bogdziewicz_Marino_Bonal_Zwolak_2018_pp._2575–2582-49]



[Jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay) feed on acorns and help to [disperse these seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_dispersal).

Animals including [squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel)[^cite_note-Steele_Yi_2020-50] and jays – [Eurasian jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_jay) in the Old World, [blue jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_jay) in North America – feed on acorns, and are important agents of [seed dispersal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_dispersal) as they carry the acorns away and bury many of them as food stores.[^cite_note-Mitrus_Szabo_2020-51][^cite_note-Enroth_2022-52][^cite_note-Bossema_1979-53] However, some species of squirrel selectively excise the embryos from the acorns that they store, meaning that the food store lasts longer and that the acorns will never germinate.[^cite_note-Steele_Yi_2020-50]

### Hybridisation



A hybrid white oak, possibly *[Quercus stellata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_oak)* × *[Q. muehlenbergii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkapin_oak)*

[Interspecific hybridization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interspecific_hybridization) is quite common among oaks, but usually between species within the same section only,[^cite_note-54] and most common in the white oak group. White oaks cannot discriminate against pollination by other species in the same section. Because they are [wind pollinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_pollinated) and have weak internal barriers to hybridization, hybridization produces functional seeds and fertile hybrid offspring. Ecological stresses, especially near habitat margins, can also cause a breakdown of mate recognition as well as a reduction of male function (pollen quantity and quality) in one parent species.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

Frequent hybridization among oaks has consequences for oak populations around the world; most notably, hybridization has produced large populations of hybrids with much [introgression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introgression) and the [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) of new species.[^cite_note-r2-57] Introgression has caused different species in the same populations to share up to 50% of their genetic information.[^cite_note-r3-58] As a result, genetic data often does not differentiate between clearly morphologically distinct species, but instead differentiates populations.[^cite_note-r4-59] The maintenance of particular loci for adaptation to ecological niches may explain the retention of species identity despite significant gene flow.[^cite_note-60]

The [Fagaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagaceae), or beech family, to which the oaks belong, is a slowly-evolving [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade) compared to other [angiosperms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiosperm),[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] and the patterns of hybridization and introgression in *Quercus* pose a significant challenge to the [concept of a species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_concept) as a group of "actually or potentially interbreeding populations which are reproductively isolated from other such groups."[^cite_note-Raven_2005-63] By this definition, many species of *Quercus* would be lumped together according to their geographic and ecological habitat, despite clear distinctions in morphology and genetic data.[^cite_note-Raven_2005-63]

### Diseases and pests



Oak [powdery mildew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powdery_mildew) on pedunculate oak, caused by *[Erysiphe alphitoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erysiphe_alphitoides)*

Oaks are affected by a large number of pests and diseases. For instance, *Q. robur* and *Q. petraea* in Britain host 423 insect species.[^cite_note-Kennedy_Southwood_1984-64] This diversity includes 106 [macro-moths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrolepidoptera), 83 [micro-moths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microlepidoptera), 67 [beetles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beetle), 53 [cynipoidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynipoidea) wasps, 38 [heteropteran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroptera) bugs, 21 [auchenorrhynchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auchenorrhyncha) bugs, 17 [sawflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawfly), and 15 [aphids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphid).[^cite_note-Kennedy_Southwood_1984-64] The insect numbers are seasonal: in spring, chewing insects such as caterpillars become numerous, followed by insects with sucking mouthparts such as aphids, then by [leaf miners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_miner), and finally by gall wasps such as *[Neuroterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroterus)*.[^cite_note-65] Several [powdery mildews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powdery_mildew) affect oak species. In Europe, the species *[Erysiphe alphitoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erysiphe_alphitoides)* is the most common.[^cite_note-66] It reduces the ability of leaves to photosynthesize, and infected leaves are shed early.[^cite_note-Hajji_Dreyer_2009-67] Another significant threat, the [oak processionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_processionary) moth (*Thaumetopoea processionea*), has emerged in the UK since 2006. The caterpillars of this species defoliate the trees and are hazardous to human health; their bodies are covered with poisonous hairs which can cause rashes and respiratory problems.[^cite_note-forestry-68] A little-understood disease of mature oaks, [acute oak decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_oak_decline), has affected the UK since 2009.[^cite_note-69] In California, goldspotted oak borer (*[Agrilus auroguttatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrilus_auroguttatus)*) has destroyed many oak trees,[^cite_note-Coleman_2008-70] while sudden oak death, caused by the [oomycete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oomycete) pathogen *[Phytophthora ramorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytophthora_ramorum)*, has devastated oaks in California and Oregon, and is present in Europe.[^cite_note-Scientific_Societies_2008-71] Japanese oak wilt, caused by the fungus *[Raffaelea quercivora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaelea_quercivora)*, has rapidly killed trees across Japan.[^cite_note-Kuroda_Yamada_1996-72]

### Gall communities

Further information: [Gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall) and [Gall wasp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall_wasp)

Many [galls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galls) are found on oak leaves, buds, flowers, and roots. Examples are [oak artichoke gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_artichoke_gall), [oak marble gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_marble_gall), [oak apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_apple) gall, [knopper gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knopper_gall), and [spangle gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotus_quercus-baccarum). These galls are the handiwork of tiny wasps from the [Cynipidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynipidae). In a complex ecological relationship, these gall wasps become hosts to [parasitoid wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitoid_wasp)—primarily from the order [Chalcidoidea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcidoidea)—which lay their larvae inside the gall wasps, ultimately leading to the hosts' demise. Additionally, [inquilines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquiline) live [commensally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commensalism) within the galls without harming the gall wasps.[^cite_note-73]

## Conservation

An estimated 31% of the world's oak species are threatened with extinction, while 41% of oak species are considered to be of [conservation concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_status). The countries with the highest numbers of threatened oak species (as of 2020) are China with 36 species, Mexico with 32 species, Vietnam with 20 species, and the US with 16 species. Leading causes are [climate change](5042951.md) and [invasive pests](67554.md) in the US, and [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) in Asia.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] In the Himalayan region of India, oak forests are being invaded by [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine) trees due to global warming. The associated pine forest species may cross frontiers and integrate into the oak forests.[^cite_note-77] Over the past 200 years, large areas of oak forest in the highlands of Mexico, Central America, and the northern Andes have been cleared for [coffee plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_plantation) and [cattle ranching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_ranching). There is a continuing threat to these forests from exploitation for timber, fuelwood, and [charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal).[^cite_note-78] In the US, entire oak ecosystems have declined due to a combination of factors thought to include fire suppression, increased consumption of acorns by growing mammal populations, herbivory of seedlings, and introduced pests.[^cite_note-79] However, disturbance-tolerant oaks may have benefited from grazers like [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_bison), and suffered when the bison were removed following [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-Hitchmough_Vera_2002-80][^cite_note-81]

## Toxicity



Photograph of single white oak leaf with condensation

The leaves and acorns of oaks are poisonous to [livestock](25160767.md), including [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle) and [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse), if eaten in large amounts, due to the toxin [tannic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannic_acid), which causes kidney damage and [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis).[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-Smith_Naylor_Knowles_Mair_2014-83] An exception is the [domestic pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_pig), which, under the right conditions, may be fed entirely on acorns,[^cite_note-84] and has traditionally been pastured in oak woodlands (such as the Spanish *[dehesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehesa_\(pastoral_management\))*[^cite_note-85] and the English system of [pannage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannage)).[^cite_note-86] Humans can eat acorns after [leaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaching_\(chemistry\)) out the tannins in water.[^cite_note-87]

## Uses

### Timber



Heart of oak [beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_\(structure\)) of the frame of the [Église Saint-Girons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Girons) in [Monein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monein), France

Oak timber is a strong and hard wood with many uses, such as for [furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture), floors, building frames, and [veneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_veneer).[^cite_note-88] The wood of *[Quercus cerris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_cerris)* (the Turkey oak) has better mechanical properties than those of the white oaks *[Q. petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_petraea)* and *[Q. robur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)*; the heartwood and sapwood have similar [mechanical properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_of_materials).[^cite_note-89] Of the North American red oaks, the [northern red oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_red_oak), *Quercus rubra*, is highly prized for lumber.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] The wood is resistant to insect and fungal attack.[^cite_note-92] Wood from *Q. robur* and *Q. petraea* was used in Europe for [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding), especially of naval [men of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-of-war), until the 19th century.[^cite_note-93]

### Other traditional products

[Oak bark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_bark), with its high tannin content, was traditionally used in the Old World for [tanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_\(leather\)) [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather). The best bark material came from trees aged twenty years of age that grew from [coppicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppicing) older trees, bark from old trees that fell naturally have less quality.[^cite_note-94]<sup>: 139 </sup> Oak [galls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall) were used for centuries as a main ingredient in [iron gall ink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_gall_ink) for manuscripts, harvested at a specific time of year.[^cite_note-95] In Korea, [sawtooth oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawtooth_oak) bark is used to make [shingles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof_shingle) for [traditional roof construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Korean_roof_construction).[^cite_note-Jeon_2016-96] The dried bark of the white oak was used in [traditional medical preparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_medicine); its tannic acid content made it astringent and antiseptic.[^cite_note-97]

### Culinary

Main article: [acorn § Culinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn#Culinary)

Acorns of various oak species have been used as food for millennia, in Asia, Europe, the Middle East, North Africa, and among the native peoples of North America. Given their high tannin content, acorns have often been leached to remove tannins before processing.[^cite_note-UoA-98] Acorns have been ground to make a flour,[^cite_note-99] and roasted as [imitation coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_substitute).[^cite_note-100] In Korea, acorn starch is made into jelly called [dotori-muk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotori-muk).

[Barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_\(storage\)) for [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_barrel) wines, [sherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry), and spirits such as [brandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy) and [Scotch whisky](28896.md) are made from oak, with [single barrel malt whiskies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_barrel_whiskey) fetching a premium.[^cite_note-Piggott_2003-101] The use of [oak in wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_\(wine\)) adds a range of flavours. Oak barrels, which may be charred before use, contribute to their contents' colour, taste, and aroma, imparting a desirable oaky [vanillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanillin) flavour. A dilemma for wine producers is to choose between French and American oakwoods. French oaks (*Quercus robur*, *Q. petraea*) give greater refinement and are chosen for the best, most expensive wines. American oak contributes greater texture and resistance to ageing, but produces a more powerful bouquet.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

Oak wood chips are used for [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_\(cooking\)) foods such as fish, meat, and [cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese).[^cite_note-Varlet_Prost_Serot_2007-104][^cite_note-105] In Japan, [Children's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Day) is celebrated with *[Kashiwa-mochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashiwa-mochi)* rice cakes, filled with a [sweet red bean paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Red_Bean_Paste), and wrapped in a *kashiwa* oak leaf.[^cite_note-106] The bark of the [cork oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_oak) is used to produce cork stoppers for [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) bottles. This species grows around the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md); [Portugal](23033.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Algeria](358.md), and [Morocco](19291.md) produce most of the world's supply.[^cite_note-Amorim-107]

In North Africa, acorns have been pressed to make acorn oil: the [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil) content can be as high as 30%. Oaks have also been used as [fodder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fodder), both leaves and acorns being fed to livestock such as pigs.[^cite_note-UoA-98]

## Culture

### Symbols



The oak features in many coats of arms, such as [that of Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Estonia).

[^cite_note-Estonian_Institute-108]

The oak is a widely used symbol of strength and [endurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endurance).[^cite_note-Leroy_Plomion_Kremer_2019-109] It is the [national tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_tree) of many countries,[^cite_note-Venables_2012-110] including the US,[^cite_note-111] [Bulgaria](3415.md), [Croatia](5573.md), [Cyprus](5593.md) ([golden oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_alnifolia)), [Estonia](28222445.md), [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Moldova](19260.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Latvia](17514.md), [Lithuania](17675.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Romania](25445.md), [Serbia](29265.md), and [Wales](69894.md).[^cite_note-Venables_2012-110] The common name of the city of [Derry](9055.md) in [Northern Ireland](21265.md) derives from the [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *Doire*, meaning 'oak'.[^cite_note-112] Oak branches are displayed on some German coins, both of the former [Deutsche Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark) and the [euro](9472.md).[^cite_note-113] Oak leaves symbolize [rank](150237.md) in armed forces including those [of the United States](32212.md). Arrangements of oak leaves, acorns, and sprigs indicate different branches of the [United States Navy staff corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_staff_corps) officers.[^cite_note-Websters_Online_Dictionary-114][^cite_note-115] The oak tree is used as a symbol by several political parties and organisations. It is the symbol of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md),[^cite_note-116] and formerly of the [Progressive Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Democrats) in [Ireland](14560.md).[^cite_note-r9-117]

### Religion



Grīdnieku ancient oak in Rumbas parish, [Latvia](17514.md), girth 8.27 metres (27.1 ft) 2015

The prehistoric [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) tribes worshipped the oak and connected it with a [thunder god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_god), and this tradition descended to many classical cultures. In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), the oak is the tree sacred to [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus), king of the gods. In Zeus's [oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle) in [Dodona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodona), [Epirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epirus), the sacred oak was the centerpiece of the precinct, and the priests would [divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divination) the pronouncements of the god by interpreting the rustling of the oak's leaves.[^cite_note-118] Mortals who destroyed such trees were said to be punished by the gods since the ancient Greeks believed beings called [hamadryads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadryad) inhabited them.[^cite_note-Bell1790-119] In [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_mythology) and [Baltic mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_mythology), the oak was sacred to the thunder gods [Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor) and [Perkūnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perk%C5%ABnas) respectively.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] In [Celtic polytheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_polytheism), the name [druid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druid), Celtic priest, is connected to Proto-Indo-European *\*deru*, meaning oak or tree.[^cite_note-Credo-122] Veneration of the oak survives in [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) tradition. [Christmas](6237.md) celebrations include the [*badnjak*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badnjak_\(Serbian\)), a branch taken from a young and straight oak ceremonially felled early on Christmas Eve morning, similar to a [yule log](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yule_log).[^cite_note-123]

### History

Main article: [List of individual trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_individual_trees)

Several oak trees hold cultural importance; such as the [Royal Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak_\(tree\)) in Britain,[^cite_note-124] the [Charter Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_Oak) in the United States,[^cite_note-125] and the [Guernica oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernica_oak) in the [Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)).[^cite_note-ElMundoTrees-126] "[The Proscribed Royalist, 1651](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Proscribed_Royalist,_1651)", a famous painting by [John Everett Millais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Everett_Millais), depicts a [Royalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist) hiding in an oak tree while fleeing from [Cromwell's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) forces.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

In the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic), a crown of oak leaves was given to those who had saved the life of a citizen in battle; it was called the "[Civic Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Crown)".[^cite_note-Credo-122] In his 17th century poem *[The Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_\(poem\))*, [Andrew Marvell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Marvell) critiqued the desire to be awarded such a leafy crown: "How vainly men themselves amaze / To win the [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae), the oak, or [bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_wreath); And their uncessant labors see / Crowned from some single herb or tree, ..."[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]
