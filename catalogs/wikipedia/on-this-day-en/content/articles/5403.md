The **Comoros**,[^cite_note-13] officially the **Union of the Comoros**,[^cite_note-14] is an [archipelagic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_country) made up of three islands in [Southeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Africa), located at the northern end of the [Mozambique Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_Channel) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). Its capital and largest city is [Moroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroni,_Comoros). The predominant religion of the population—and the official [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion)—is [Islam](6037917.md). Comoros proclaimed its [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_date_of_formation) from France on 6 July 1975. The country has three official languages: [Comorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorian_languages), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic). The Comoros is the only country of the [Arab League](52625.md) which is entirely in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere). It is also a member state of the [African Union](10634933.md), the *[Organisation internationale de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie)*, the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), and the [Indian Ocean Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Commission).&#x20;

At 1,659 square kilometres (641 sq mi), the Comoros is the third-smallest African country by area after [São Tomé and Príncipe](20026306.md) and [Seychelles](27288.md).[^cite_note-15] Although its population was estimated in 2026 to only number 944,388,[^cite_note-\:populationest-7] the Comoros ranks as one of the [most densely populated countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population_density) in the world.[^cite_note-\:1-16] The sovereign state consists of three major islands, namely [Grande Comore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Comore), [Anjouan](184799.md), and [Mohéli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moh%C3%A9li), and numerous smaller islands, all of the volcanic [Comoro Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoro_Islands) with the exception of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte). Mayotte voted against independence from France in [a referendum in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Comorian_independence_referendum) and continues to be administered by France as an [overseas department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_department). France has repeatedly [vetoed United Nations Security Council resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vetoed_United_Nations_Security_Council_resolutions) that would have affirmed Comorian sovereignty over the island.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-GlobalPolicy_SC-Vetoes-19][^cite_note-article33-20] Mayotte became the overseas department and a region of France in 2011 following a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Mahoran_status_referendum) which was passed overwhelmingly. However, the Comoros continues to claim the island,[^cite_note-21] with backing from the AU and UN.[^cite_note-22]

The Comoros were likely first settled by [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages)-speaking [Malagasy peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_people), [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_language) speakers from [East Africa](260987.md), and seafaring Arab traders.[^cite_note-23] From 1500 the [Sultanate of Anjouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_on_the_Comoros#Sultans_of_Ndzuwani_\(Anjouan\)) dominated the islands, with Grande Comore split between [several sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_on_the_Comoros#Sultans_on_Ngazidja_\(Grande_Comore\)). It became part of the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) during the 19th century, before its independence in 1975. It has experienced more than 20 coups or attempted coups, with various heads of state assassinated.[^cite_note-20Coups-24][^cite_note-25] Along with this political instability, it ranks in the [medium quartile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Human_Development_Index#Low_human_development) on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index).[^cite_note-26] In 2023, the Comoros had the least [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_income_inequality) among its peers regionally, though poverty remains widespread especially in [rural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_area).[^cite_note-\:2-27] Between 2009 and 2014, about 19% of the population lived below the [international poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_poverty_line) of US \$1.90 a day by [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity).[^cite_note-28]

## Etymology

The name "Comoros" derives from the Arabic *Jazr al-Qumr* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جزر القمر), "Islands of the Moon". Alfred Gevrey, a colonial judge who worked in [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) wrote in *Essai sur les Comores* (1870) proposing that *Qumr* may have referred to a general southern region where Arab sailors would have observed the bright [Magellanic Clouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellanic_Clouds) (as elaborated by [Yaqut al-Hamawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqut_al-Hamawi) from inquiries with [Zanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanj) peoples), later fixed to the entire [Mozambique waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_Channel).[^cite_note-29]

## History

Main article: [History of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Comoros)

### Settlement



A large [dhow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhow) with [lateen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateen) sail rigs



A [vanilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla) plantation

According to [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythology), a [jinni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinn) dropped a [jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone), which formed a great circular inferno. This became the [Karthala volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Karthala), which created the island of Ngazidja (Grande Comore). [King Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Solomon) is also said to have visited the island accompanied by his queen [Bilqis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Sheba).

The first attested human inhabitants of the Comoro Islands are now thought to have been [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people) settlers travelling by boat from [islands in Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Southeast_Asia).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] These people arrived in the area no later than the eighth century AD, the date of the earliest known archaeological site, found on [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte), although settlement beginning in the first century has been postulated.[^cite_note-32]

Subsequent settlers came from the east coast of Africa, the Arabian Peninsula and the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago), and [Madagascar](18964.md). [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages)-speaking settlers were present on the islands from the beginnings of settlement. [^cite_note-Walker,_Iain_2019-33]

Development of the Comoros is divided into phases. The earliest reliably recorded phase is the Dembeni phase (eighth to tenth centuries), during which there were several small settlements on each island.[^cite_note-spear-34] From the eleventh to the fifteenth centuries, trade with the island of Madagascar and merchants from the [Swahili coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_coast) and the [Middle East](19323.md) flourished, more villages were founded and existing villages grew. Settlers from the Arabian peninsula, particularly [Hadhramaut](325752.md), arrived during this period.

### Medieval Comoros and European contact

According to legend, in 632, upon hearing of [Islam](6037917.md), islanders are said to have dispatched an emissary, Mtswa-Mwindza, to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca)—but by the time he arrived there, the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) had died. Nonetheless, after a stay in Mecca, he returned to Ngazidja, where he built a mosque in his home town of Ntsaweni, and led the gradual conversion of the islanders to Islam.[^cite_note-verin-35]

In 933, the Comoros was referred to by [Omani](22316.md) sailors as the Perfume Islands.[^cite_note-36]

Among the earliest accounts of East Africa, the works of [Al-Masudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Masudi) describe early Islamic trade routes, and how the coast and islands were frequently visited by [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) including [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) and [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) merchants and sailors in search of [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral), [ambergris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambergris), [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory), [tortoiseshell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoiseshell), [gold](12240.md) and slaves for the [Arabic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_slave_trade). They also brought Islam to the people of the [Zanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanj) including the Comoros. As the importance of the Comoros grew along the East African coast, both small and large [mosques](19894.md) were constructed. The Comoros are part of the [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Coast) cultural and economic complex and the islands became a major hub of trade and an important location in a network of trading towns that included [Kilwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa), in present-day Tanzania, Sofala (an outlet for [Zimbabwean](34399.md) gold), in [Mozambique](19301.md), and [Mombasa](200152.md) in [Kenya](188171.md).[^cite_note-spear-34]

The Portuguese arrived in the Indian Ocean at the end of the 15th century, and the first Portuguese visit to the islands seems to have been that of [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama)'s second fleet in 1503.[^cite_note-37] For much of the 16th century, the islands provided provisions to the Portuguese fort at Mozambique, and although there was no formal attempt by the Portuguese crown to take possession, some Portuguese traders settled and married local women.

By the end of the 16th century, local rulers on the African mainland were beginning to push back and, with the support of the Omani Sultan [Saif bin Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saif_bin_Sultan), they began to defeat the Dutch and the Portuguese. One of his successors, [Said bin Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_bin_Sultan), increased Omani Arab influence in the region, moving his administration to nearby [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar), which came under [Omani](22316.md) rule. Nevertheless, the Comoros remained independent, and although the three smaller islands were usually [politically unified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_on_the_Comoros), the largest island, Ngazidja, was divided into a number of [autonomous kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_on_the_Comoros) (*ntsi*).[^cite_note-Thomas_Spear_1984_291–305-38]

The islands were well placed to meet the needs of Europeans, initially supplying the Portuguese in Mozambique, then ships, particularly the English, on the route to India, and, later, slaves to the plantation islands in the [Mascarenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascarenes).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Thomas_Spear_1984_291–305-38]

### French colonisation



French map of the Comores, 1747



An 1808 map refers to the islands as "Camora".



A public square, Moroni, 1908

In the last decade of the 18th century, Malagasy warriors, mostly [Betsimisaraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsimisaraka_people) and [Sakalava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakalava), started raiding the Comoros for [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave) and the islands were devastated as crops were destroyed and the people were slaughtered, taken into captivity or fled to the African mainland: it is said that by the time the raids finally ended in the second decade of the 19th century only one man remained on Mwali.[^cite_note-40] The islands were [repopulated by slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_Comoros) from the mainland, who were [traded to the French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_slave_trade) in Mayotte and the Mascarenes. On the Comoros, it was estimated in 1865 that as much as 40% of the population consisted of slaves.[^cite_note-41]

France first established colonial rule in the Comoros by taking possession of Mayotte in 1841 when the [Sakalava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakalava) usurper sultan [Andriantsoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriantsoly?action=edit\&redlink=1) (also known as Tsy Levalo) signed the Treaty of April 1841,[^cite_note-Encyclopaedia_of_Islam_1979,_vol._v,_p._381-42] which ceded the island to the French authorities.[^cite_note-43] After its annexation, France attempted to convert Mayotte into a sugar plantation colony.

Meanwhile, [Ndzwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndzwani) (or Johanna as it was known to the British) continued to serve as a way station for English merchants sailing to India and the Far East, as well as American whalers, although the British gradually abandoned it following their possession of [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Mauritius) in 1814, and by the time the [Suez Canal](29323.md) opened in 1869 there was no longer any significant supply trade at Ndzwani. Local commodities exported by the Comoros were, in addition to slaves, coconuts, timber, cattle, and tortoiseshell. British and American settlers, as well as the island's sultan, established a plantation-based economy that used about one-third of the land for export crops. In addition to sugar on Mayotte, ylang-ylang and other perfume plants, vanilla, cloves, coffee, cocoa beans, and sisal were introduced.[^cite_note-44]

In 1886, Mwali was placed under French protection by its Sultan Mardjani Abdou Cheikh. That same year, Sultan Said Ali of [Bambao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bambao), one of the sultanates on Ngazidja, placed the island under French protection in exchange for French support of his claim to the entire island, which he retained until his abdication in 1910. In 1908 the four islands were unified under a single administration (*Colonie de Mayotte et dépendances*) and placed under the authority of the French colonial [Governor-General of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Madagascar). In 1909, Sultan Said Muhamed of Ndzwani abdicated in favour of French rule, and in 1912, the protectorates were abolished and the islands administered as a single colony. Two years later, the colony was abolished, and the islands became a province of the [colony of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Madagascar).[^cite_note-Walker,_Iain_2019-33]

An agreement was reached with France in 1973 for the Comoros to become independent in 1978, despite the deputies of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) voting for increased integration with France. A [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Comorian_independence_referendum) was held on all four of the islands. Three voted for independence by large margins, while Mayotte voted against. On 6 July 1975, however, the Comorian parliament passed a unilateral resolution declaring independence. Ahmed Abdallah proclaimed the independence of the Comorian State (*État comorien*; دولة القمر) and became its first president. France did not recognise the new state until 31 December, and retained control of Mayotte.

The next 30 years were a period of political turmoil. On 3 August 1975, less than one month after independence, president [Ahmed Abdallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abdallah) was removed from office in an armed coup and replaced with United National Front of the Comoros (FNUK) member [Said Mohamed Jaffar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Mohamed_Jaffar). Months later, in January 1976, Jaffar was ousted in favour of his Minister of Defence [Ali Soilihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Soilihi).[^cite_note-Mukonoweshuro\:555-577-45]

The population of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) voted against independence from France in three referendums during this period. The [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Comorian_independence_referendum), held on all the islands on 22 December 1974, won 63.8% support for maintaining ties with France on Mayotte; the [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Mahoran_status_referendum), held in February 1976, confirmed that vote with an overwhelming 99.4%, while the third, in April 1976, confirmed that the people of Mayotte wished to remain a French territory. The three remaining islands, ruled by President Soilihi, instituted a number of socialist and isolationist policies that soon strained relations with France. On 13 May 1978, [Bob Denard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Denard), once again commissioned by the French intelligence service ([SDECE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SDECE)), returned to [overthrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Comorian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) President Soilihi and reinstate Abdallah with the support of the French, Rhodesian and South African governments. Ali Soilihi was captured and executed a few weeks later.[^cite_note-Mukonoweshuro\:555-577-45][^cite_note-46]

In contrast to Soilihi, Abdallah's presidency was marked by authoritarian rule and increased adherence to traditional Islam[^cite_note-Bakar\:181-191-47] and the country was renamed the Federal [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic) of the Comoros (*République Fédérale Islamique des Comores*; جمهورية القمر الإتحادية الإسلامية). Bob Denard served as Abdallah's first advisor; nicknamed the "Viceroy of the Comoros", he was considered the real strongman of the regime. Very close to South Africa, which financed his "presidential guard", he allowed Paris to circumvent the international embargo on the apartheid regime via Moroni. He also set up from the archipelago a permanent mercenary corps, called upon to intervene at the request of Paris or Pretoria in conflicts in Africa. Abdallah continued as president until 1989, when, fearing a probable coup, he signed a decree ordering the Presidential Guard, led by Bob Denard, to disarm the armed forces. Shortly after the signing of the decree, Abdallah was allegedly shot dead in his office by a disgruntled military officer, though later sources claim an antitank missile was launched into his bedroom and killed him.[^cite_note-48]. Denard was also injured; it is suspected that Abdallah's killer was a soldier under his command.[^cite_note-49]

A few days later, Bob Denard was evacuated to South Africa by French paratroopers. [Said Mohamed Djohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Mohamed_Djohar), Soilihi's older half-brother, then became president, and served until September 1995, when Bob Denard returned and attempted another coup. This time, France intervened with paratroopers and forced Denard to surrender.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The French removed Djohar to Reunion, and the Paris-backed [Mohamed Taki Abdoulkarim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Taki_Abdoulkarim) became president by election. He led the country from 1996, during a time of labour crises, government suppression, and secessionist conflicts, until his death in November 1998. He was succeeded by Interim President [Tadjidine Ben Said Massounde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadjidine_Ben_Said_Massounde).[^cite_note-52]

The islands of [Ndzwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndzwani) and [Mwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moh%C3%A9li) declared their independence from the Comoros in 1997, in an attempt to restore French rule. But France rejected their request, leading to bloody confrontations between federal troops and rebels.[^cite_note-53] In April 1999, Colonel [Azali Assoumani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azali_Assoumani), Army Chief of Staff, seized power in a [bloodless coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Comorian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), overthrowing the Interim President Massounde, citing weak leadership in the face of the crisis. This was the Comoros' 18th coup, or attempted coup d'état since independence in 1975.[^cite_note-54]

Assoumani failed to consolidate power and reestablish control over the islands, which was the subject of international criticism. The [African Union](10634933.md), under the auspices of President [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki) of South Africa, imposed sanctions on Ndzwani to help broker negotiations and effect reconciliation.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Under the terms of the Fomboni Accords,[^cite_note-57] signed in December 2001 by the leaders of all three islands, the official name of the country was changed to the Union of the Comoros; the new state was to be highly decentralised and the central union government would devolve most powers to the new island governments, each led by a president. The Union president, although elected by national elections, would be chosen in rotation from each of the islands every five years.

Assoumani stepped down in 2002 to run in the democratic election of the President of the Comoros, which he won. Under ongoing international pressure, as a military ruler who had originally come to power by force, and was not always democratic while in office, Assoumani led the Comoros through constitutional changes that enabled new elections.[^cite_note-58] A *Loi des compétences* law was passed in early 2005 that defines the responsibilities of each governmental body, and is in the process of implementation. The elections in 2006 were won by [Ahmed Abdallah Mohamed Sambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abdallah_Mohamed_Sambi), a Sunni Muslim cleric nicknamed the "Ayatollah" for his time spent studying Islam in Iran. Assoumani honoured the election results, thus allowing the first peaceful and democratic exchange of power for the archipelago.[^cite_note-59]

Colonel [Mohammed Bacar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Bacar), a French-trained former gendarme elected President of Ndzwani in 2001, refused to step down at the end of his five-year mandate. He staged a vote in June 2007 to confirm his leadership that was rejected as illegal by the Comoros federal government and the African Union. On 25 March 2008, hundreds of soldiers from the African Union and the Comoros seized rebel-held Ndzwani, generally welcomed by the population: there have been reports of hundreds, if not thousands, of people tortured during Bacar's tenure.[^cite_note-60] Some rebels were killed and injured, but there are no official figures. At least 11 civilians were wounded. Some officials were imprisoned. Bacar fled in a speedboat to Mayotte to seek asylum. Anti-French protests followed in the Comoros (see [2008 invasion of Anjouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_invasion_of_Anjouan)). Bacar was eventually granted asylum in Benin.

Since independence from France, the Comoros experienced more than 20 coups or attempted coups.[^cite_note-20Coups-24]

Following elections in late 2010, former Vice-president [Ikililou Dhoinine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikililou_Dhoinine) was inaugurated as president on 26 May 2011. Dhoinine is the first President of the Comoros from the island of Mwali. Following the 2016 elections, [Azali Assoumani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azali_Assoumani), from Ngazidja, became president for a third term. In 2018, Assoumani held a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Comorian_constitutional_referendum) on constitutional reform that would permit a president to serve two terms. The amendments were passed, although the vote was widely contested and boycotted by the opposition, and in April 2019, to widespread opposition, Assoumani was [re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Comorian_presidential_election) president to serve the first of potentially two five-year terms.[^cite_note-61] In January 2020, the legislative [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Comorian_legislative_election) in Comoros were dominated by President Azali Assoumani's party, the [Convention for the Renewal of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_for_the_Renewal_of_the_Comoros), CRC. It took an overwhelming majority in the parliament.[^cite_note-62]

In 2021, Comoros signed and ratified the [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons), making it a nuclear-weapon-free state.[^cite_note-63] and in 2023, Comoros was invited as a non-member guest to the G7 summit in [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima).[^cite_note-64] On 18 February 2023 the Comoros assumed the presidency of the [African Union](10634933.md).[^cite_note-65]

In January 2024, President Azali Assoumani was re-elected with 63% of the vote in the disputed presidential [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Comorian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-66] In January 2025, the ruling party of president Azali Assoumani won parliamentary [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Comorian_parliamentary_election), taking 28 out of 33 parliamentary seats. The opposition parties rejected the results.[^cite_note-67]

In 2026, the Historic Sultantes of the Comoro Islands were inscribed as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md), making them the first Islamic sites to be listed in the Comoros.

Main article: [Geography of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Comoros)



A map of the Comoros

The Comoros is formed by [Ngazidja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngazidja) (Grande Comore), [Mwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moh%C3%A9li) (Mohéli) and [Ndzwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndzwani) (Anjouan), three major islands in the Comoros Archipelago, as well as many minor islets. The islands are officially known by their Comorian-language names, though international sources still use their French names (given in parentheses above). The capital and largest city, [Moroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroni,_Comoros), is located on Ngazidja and the most densely populated city is Anjouan.[^cite_note-68] The archipelago is situated in the Indian Ocean, in the [Mozambique Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_Channel), between the African coast (nearest to [Mozambique](19301.md) and [Tanzania](30118.md)) and [Madagascar](18964.md), with no land borders.

At 1,659 km<sup>2</sup> (641 sq mi), it is one of the smallest countries in the world. The Comoros also has a claim to 320 km<sup>2</sup> (120 sq mi) of territorial seas. The interiors of the islands vary from steep mountains to low hills.

The areas and populations (at the 2017 Census) of the main islands are as follows:[^cite_note-ReferenceA-69]

| Ngazidja | 1,024 | 379,367 |
| Ndzwani | 424 | 327,382 |
| ***Totals*** | 1,659 | 758,316 |

Ngazidja is the largest of the Comoros Archipelago, with an area of 1,024 km<sup>2</sup>. It is also the most recent island, and therefore has rocky soil. The island's two volcanoes, [Karthala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karthala) (active) and [La Grille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grille) (dormant), and the lack of good harbours are distinctive characteristics of its terrain. Mwali, with its capital at [Fomboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomboni), is the smallest of the four major islands. Ndzwani, whose capital is [Mutsamudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutsamudu), has a distinctive triangular shape caused by three mountain chains – [Shisiwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sima,_Comoros), [Nioumakele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nioumakele?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Jimilime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimilime) – emanating from a central peak, [Mount Ntingui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ntingui?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1,575 metres or 5,167 feet).



Grande Comore landscape

The islands of the Comoros Archipelago were formed by volcanic activity. Mount Karthala, an active [shield volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_volcano) located on Ngazidja, is the country's highest point, at 2,361 metres (7,746 feet). It contains the Comoros' largest patch of disappearing rainforest. Karthala is currently one of the most active volcanoes in the world, with a minor eruption in May 2006, and prior eruptions as recently as April 2005 and 1991. In the 2005 eruption, which lasted from 17 to 19 April, 40,000 citizens were evacuated, and the [crater lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_crater_lake) in the volcano's three-by-four-kilometre (2-by-2+1⁄2-mile) [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera) was destroyed.

The Comoros also lays claim to the [*Îles Éparses* or *Îles éparses de l'océan indien*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_Islands_in_the_Indian_Ocean) (Scattered Islands in the Indian Ocean) – [Glorioso Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorioso_Islands), comprising [Grande Glorieuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Glorieuse), [Île du Lys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_du_Lys), [Wreck Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_Rock?action=edit\&redlink=1), [South Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Rock?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Verte Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verte_Rocks?action=edit\&redlink=1) (three islets) and three unnamed islets – one of France's overseas districts. The Glorioso Islands were administered by the colonial Comoros before 1975, and are therefore sometimes considered part of the Comoros Archipelago. [Banc du Geyser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banc_du_Geyser), a former island in the Comoros Archipelago, now submerged, is geographically located in the *Îles Éparses*, but was annexed by [Madagascar](18964.md) in 1976 as an unclaimed territory. The Comoros and France each still view the Banc du Geyser as part of the Glorioso Islands and, thus, part of its particular exclusive economic zone.

The climate is generally tropical and mild, and the two major seasons are distinguishable by their raininess. The temperature reaches an average of 29–30 °C (84–86 °F) in March, the hottest month in the rainy season (called kashkazi/kaskazi \[meaning north monsoon], which runs from November to April), and an average low of 19 °C (66 °F) in the cool, dry season (kusi (meaning south monsoon), which proceeds from May to October).[^cite_note-70] The islands are rarely subject to [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone).

### Biodiversity

Main articles: [Comoros forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_forests), [Comoro Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoro_Islands), and [Wildlife of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_the_Comoros)

The Comoros constitute an [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion) in their own right, [Comoros forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_forests).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-72] It had a 2018 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 7.69/10, ranking it 33rd globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-73]

In December 1952 a specimen of the [West Indian Ocean coelacanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_Ocean_coelacanth) fish was re-discovered off the Comoros coast. The 66-million-year-old species was thought to have been long extinct until its first recorded appearance in 1938 off the South African coast.[^cite_note-74] Between 1938 and 1975, 84 specimens were caught and recorded.[^cite_note-75]

There are six national parks in the Comoros – [Karthala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karthala), Coelacanth, and Mitsamiouli Ndroudi on Grande Comore, [Mount Ntringui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ntringui) and [Shisiwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shisiwani_National_Park) on Anjouan, and [Mohéli National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moh%C3%A9li_National_Park) on Mohéli. ## Government

Main article: [Politics of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Comoros)

See also: [List of rulers of Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Comoros)



[Moroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroni,_Comoros), capital of the Comoros, with the port and Badjanani Mosque

Politics of the Comoros takes place in a framework of a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_system) [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic),[^cite_note-cia-4] whereby the [President of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Comoros) is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md), and of a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). The Constitution of the Union of the Comoros was ratified by referendum on 23 December 2001, and the islands' constitutions and executives were elected in the following months. It had previously been considered a military dictatorship, and the transfer of power from Azali Assoumani to Ahmed Abdallah Mohamed Sambi in May 2006 was a watershed moment as it was the first peaceful transfer in Comorian history.

[Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forms_of_government) and parliament. The preamble of the constitution guarantees an Islamic inspiration in governance, a commitment to human rights, and several specific enumerated rights, democracy, "a common destiny" for all Comorians.[^cite_note-77] Each of the islands (according to Title II of the Constitution) has a great amount of autonomy in the Union, including having their own constitutions (or Fundamental Law), president, and Parliament. The presidency and Assembly of the Union are distinct from each of the islands' governments. Up to a referendum on 30.7.2018 (62.7% participation, 92.34% for the amendment according to the Comorian government), the presidency of the Union rotated between the islands.[^cite_note-CfHR_Law-of-the-Union-78]

### Legal system

The Comorian [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_system) rests on [Islamic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_law), an inherited French ([Napoleonic Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Code)) legal code, and customary law (mila na ntsi). Village elders, kadis or civilian courts settle most disputes. The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is independent of the [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative) and the executive. The Supreme Court acts as a Constitutional Council in resolving constitutional questions and supervising presidential elections. As High Court of Justice, the Supreme Court also arbitrates in cases where the government is accused of malpractice. The Supreme Court consists of two members selected by the president, two elected by the Federal Assembly, and one by the council of each island.[^cite_note-CfHR_Law-of-the-Union-78]

### Political culture

Around 80 percent of the central government's annual budget is spent on the country's complex administrative system which provides for a semi-autonomous government and president for each of the three islands and a rotating presidency for the overarching Union government.[^cite_note-79] A referendum took place on 16 May 2009 to decide whether to cut down the government's unwieldy political bureaucracy. 52.7% of those eligible voted, and 93.8% of votes were cast in approval of the referendum. Following the implementation of the changes, each island's president became a governor and the ministers became councillors.[^cite_note-allafrica20090519-80]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Comoros)

In November 1975, the Comoros became the 143rd member of the [United Nations](31769.md). The new nation was defined as comprising the entire [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago), although the citizens of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) chose to become French citizens and keep their island as a French territory.[^cite_note-81]

The Comoros has repeatedly pressed its claim to Mayotte before the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md), which adopted a series of resolutions under the caption "Question of the Comorian Island of Mayotte", opining that Mayotte belongs to the Comoros under the principle that the territorial integrity of colonial territories should be preserved upon independence. As a practical matter, however, these resolutions have little effect and there is no foreseeable likelihood that Mayotte will become *de facto* part of the Comoros without its people's consent. More recently, the Assembly has maintained this item on its agenda but deferred it from year to year without taking action. Other bodies, including the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity), the [Movement of Non-Aligned Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Non-Aligned_Countries) and the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), have similarly questioned French sovereignty over Mayotte.[^cite_note-GA31_first-resolution-82][^cite_note-83] To close the debate and to avoid being integrated by force in the Union of the Comoros, the population of Mayotte overwhelmingly chose to become an [overseas department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_department) and a [region of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_France) in a [2009 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Mahoran_status_referendum). The new status was effective on 31 March 2011 and Mayotte has been recognised as an [outermost region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outermost_region) by the [European Union](9317.md) on 1 January 2014. This decision legally integrates Mayotte in the [French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Republic).

The Comoros is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), the [African Union](10634933.md), the [Arab League](52625.md), the [World Bank](36752.md), the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), the [Indian Ocean Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Commission) and the [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank). On 10 April 2008, the Comoros became the 179th nation to accept the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change.[^cite_note-84] The Comoros signed the UN [treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-85] [Azali Assoumani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azali_Assoumani), President of the Comoros and Chair of the African Union, attended the [2023 Russia–Africa Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Russia%E2%80%93Africa_Summit) in Saint Petersburg.[^cite_note-86]

In May 2013, the Union of the Comoros became known for filing a referral to the Office of the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court (ICC) regarding the events of "the 31 May 2010 Israeli raid on the Humanitarian Aid Flotilla bound for the [Gaza Strip](12047.md)". In November 2014 the ICC Prosecutor eventually decided[^cite_note-87] that the events did constitute war crimes but did not meet the gravity standards of bringing the case before ICC.[^cite_note-88]

The emigration rate of skilled workers was about 21.2% in 2000.[^cite_note-89]

### Military

Main article: [Army of National Development](6008.md)

The military resources of the Comoros consist of a small standing army and a 500-member police force, as well as a 600-member defence force.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] A defence treaty with France[^cite_note-92] provides naval resources for protection of territorial waters, training of Comorian military personnel, and air surveillance. France maintains the presence of a few senior officers in the Comoros at government request, as well as a small maritime base and a [Foreign Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Foreign_Legion) Detachment (DLEM) on [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte).

Once the new government was installed in May–June 2011, an expert mission from UNREC (Lomé) came to the Comoros and produced guidelines for the elaboration of a national security policy, which were discussed by different actors, notably the national defence authorities and civil society.[^cite_note-93] By the end of the programme in end March 2012, a normative framework agreed upon by all entities involved in SSR will have been established. This will then have to be adopted by Parliament and implemented by the authorities.

### Human rights

Main article: [Human rights in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_Comoros)

Both male and female [same-sex sexual acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_Comoros) are illegal in Comoros.[^cite_note-94] Such acts are punished with up to five years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-95]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Comoros)



GDP per capita development, since 1950

The level of [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty) in the Comoros is high, but "judging by the [international poverty threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_threshold) of \$1.9 per person per day, only two out of every ten Comorians could be classified as poor, a rate that places the Comoros ahead of other [low-income countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-income_countries) and 30 percentage points ahead of other countries in [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md)."[^cite_note-2018WorldBank-96] Poverty declined by about 10% between 2014 and 2018, and living conditions generally improved.[^cite_note-2018WorldBank-96] [Economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) was historically higher in the country relative to other lower-middle income African countries, though it has declined significantly in recent years.[^cite_note-\:2-27] There is a major gap between urban and rural areas, with nearly 80% of the poor living in the latter.[^cite_note-2018WorldBank-96][^cite_note-\:2-27] [Remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) through the sizable Comorian diaspora form a substantial part of the country's GDP[^cite_note-CSIS-97] and have contributed to decreases in poverty and increases in living standards.[^cite_note-2018WorldBank-96] According to the [French Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Development_Agency), remittances account for around 20% of the Comoros' GDP in 2023,[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] with the country being among the top recipients of remittances globally relative to its GDP.[^cite_note-100]

According to [ILO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILO)'s ILOSTAT statistical database, between 1991 and 2019 the [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) as a percent of the total labor force ranged from 4.38% to 4.3%.[^cite_note-101] An October 2005 paper by the Comoros Ministry of Planning and Regional Development, however, reported that "registered unemployment rate is 14.3 percent, distributed very unevenly among and within the islands, but with marked incidence in urban areas."[^cite_note-Office_of_the_General_Commissioner_for_Planning,_Ministry_of_Planning_and_Regional_Development-102]

In 2019, more than 56% of the labor force was employed in agriculture, with 29% employed in industry and 14% employed in services.[^cite_note-103] The islands' agricultural sector is based on the export of [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice), including [vanilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla), [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon), and [cloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloves), and thus susceptible to price fluctuations in the volatile world [commodity market](48190.md) for these goods.[^cite_note-CSIS-97] The Comoros is the world's largest producer of [ylang-ylang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ylang-ylang), a plant whose extracted [essential oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_oil) is used in the [perfume](98581.md) industry; some 80% of the world's supply comes from the Comoros.[^cite_note-104]

High population densities, as much as 1000 per square kilometre in the densest agricultural zones, for what is still a mostly rural, agricultural economy may lead to an environmental crisis in the near future, especially considering the high rate of population growth. In 2004 the Comoros' real GDP growth was a low 1.9% and real GDP per capita continued to decline. These declines are explained by factors including declining investment, drops in consumption, rising inflation, and an increase in trade imbalance due in part to lowered cash crop prices, especially vanilla.[^cite_note-Office_of_the_General_Commissioner_for_Planning,_Ministry_of_Planning_and_Regional_Development-102]

Fiscal policy is constrained by erratic fiscal revenues, a bloated civil service wage bill, and an external debt that is far above the [HIPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavily_indebted_poor_countries) threshold. Membership in the franc zone, the main anchor of stability, has nevertheless helped contain pressures on domestic prices.[^cite_note-105]

The Comoros has an inadequate transportation system,[^cite_note-106] a young and rapidly increasing population, and few natural resources. The low educational level of the labour force contributes to a subsistence level of economic activity, high [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment),[^cite_note-107] and a heavy dependence on foreign grants and technical assistance. Agriculture contributed 36,6% to the [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) in 2024[^cite_note-108] and provides most of the exports, with [cloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clove) alone accounting for 64,9% in 2023.[^cite_note-109]

The government is struggling to upgrade education and technical training, to privatise commercial and industrial enterprises, to improve health services, to diversify exports, to promote tourism, and to reduce the high population growth rate.[^cite_note-110]

The Comoros is a member of the Organisation for the Harmonisation of Business Law in Africa ([OHADA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHADA)).[^cite_note-ohada.com-111] In 2024, the Comoros joined the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization).[^cite_note-Comoros_-_People_{{!}}_Britannica-1]

With about 940,000 residents,[^cite_note-\:populationest-7] the Comoros is one of the least populous countries in the world, but its population density is high, with an average of 457 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,180/sq mi).[^cite_note-\:1-16] In 2001, 34% of the population was considered urban, but the urban population has since grown; in recent years rural population growth has been negative, while overall population growth is still relatively high.[^cite_note-113] In 1958 the population was 183,133.[^cite_note-114]

In 2009, almost half the population of the Comoros was under the age of 15.[^cite_note-115] Major urban centres include [Moroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroni,_Comoros), [Mitsamihuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsamihuli), [Foumbouni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foumbouni), [Mutsamudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutsamudu), [Domoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domoni), and [Fomboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomboni). There are between 200,000 and 350,000 Comorians in France.[^cite_note-116]

### Ethnic groups

The islands of the Comoros are 97.1% ethnically [Comorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Comoros), which is a mixture of [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples), the [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) admixed Bantu [Malagasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_people), and [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) people.[^cite_note-Comoros_-_People_{{!}}_Britannica-1] Minorities include [Makua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makua_people) and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_ethnic_groups) (mostly [Ismaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili)). There are recent immigrants of [Chinese](372983.md) origin in Grande Comore (especially Moroni). Although most French left after independence in 1975, a small [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples) community, descended from settlers from France, Madagascar and Réunion, lives in the Comoros.[^cite_note-117]

### Languages

Further information: [Languages of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Comoros)

The most common [languages in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Comoros) are the [Comorian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorian_languages), spoken by over 95% of the population.[^cite_note-\:0-118] These languages are collectively known as *Shikomori* and are related to [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language). The four different variants of Comorian (Shingazidja, Shimwali, Shindzwani and Shimaore) are spoken on each of the four islands. [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script) and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_script) scripts are both used, Arabic being the more widely used, and an [official orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_script) has recently been developed for the Latin script.[^cite_note-chamanga-119]

[Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) are also official languages. Because no accepted standardized written form currently exists for Comorian, Arabic and French are used as the [mediums of instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_of_instruction) from primary to tertiary education.[^cite_note-\:0-118] Arabic is widely known as a second language, being the language of Quranic teaching. French is the administrative language and the language of most non-Quranic formal education. In addition, English is taught in later grades.[^cite_note-120]

A 2025 [Afrobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrobarometer) survey found that 89.9% of Comorians primarily speak Shikomori at home while 9.9% have French as their main home language.[^cite_note-\:afrobarometer-2]

### Religion

Further information: [Religion in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Comoros)



A view of Domoni, [Anjouan](184799.md) including mosque

[Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) is [the dominant religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Comoros), followed by as much as 99% of the population.[^cite_note-121] Comoros is the only Muslim-majority country in Southern Africa and one of the three southernmost Muslim-majority territories, along with [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) and the Australian territory of [Cocos Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_Islands). [A minority of the population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_the_Comoros) of the Comoros are Christian, both Catholic and Protestant denominations are represented, and most Malagasy residents are also Christian. Immigrants from metropolitan France are mostly [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic).[^cite_note-122]

### Health

Further information: [Health in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_the_Comoros)

The Comoros has 15 [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_doctor) per 100,000 people. The [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) was 4.7 births per adult woman in 2004. [Life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) at birth is 67 years for females and 62 years for males,[^cite_note-afro.who.int-123] with the average life expectancy being 63.42 years in 2021. The Comorian National Institute of Statistics has found that the [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) (TFR) in the country has decreased over time, from a peak of 7.14 in 1973 to 3.98 in 2021.[^cite_note-\:populationest-7] Although the [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality) in the Comoros declined sharply from 152 to 47 deaths per 1,000 live births between 1970 and 2020,[^cite_note-\:populationest-7] it remains high compared to rates in [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries).[^cite_note-124]

In the 2024 Global Hunger Index (GHI), Comoros ranks 81st out of 127 countries with sufficient data, with a score of 18.8, which indicates a moderate level of hunger.[^cite_note-125]

## Education

Further information: [Education in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Comoros)

Almost all children attend Quran religious schools, usually before, although increasingly in tandem with regular schooling. Children are taught about the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an), and memorise it, and learn the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) script.

Most parents prefer their children to attend Koran religious schools before moving on to the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)-[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) based schooling system. Although the state sector is plagued by a lack of resources, and the teachers by unpaid salaries, there are numerous private and community schools of relatively good standard. The national curriculum, apart from a few years during the revolutionary period immediately post-independence, has been very much based on the French system, both because resources are French and most Comorans hope to go on to further education in [France](5843419.md), [Spain](26667.md) and [Italy](14532.md). There have recently been moves to "Comorianise" the syllabus and integrate the two systems, the formal and the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) religious schools, into one, thus moving away from the secular educational system inherited from France.[^cite_note-126]

Pre-[colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization) education systems in Comoros focused on necessary skills such as agriculture, caring for livestock and completing household tasks. Religious education also taught [Islam](6037917.md). The education system underwent a transformation during [colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonisation) in the early 1900s which brought secular education based on the [French](5843419.md) system. This was mainly for children of the elite. After Comoros gained independence in 1975, the education system changed again. Funding for teachers' salaries was lost, and many went on strike. Thus, the public education system was not functioning between 1997 and 2001. Since gaining independence, the education system has also undergone a democratisation and options exist for those other than the elite. Enrollment has also grown.

In 2000, 44.2% of children aged 5 to 14 years were attending school. There is a general lack of facilities, equipment, qualified teachers, textbooks and other resources. [Salaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaries) for teachers are often so far in arrears that many refuse to work.[^cite_note-ilab-127]

Prior to 2000, students seeking a university education had to attend school outside of the country In [Europe](9239.md) and [Americas](29833.md) especially [France](5843419.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md).

However, in the early 2000s a university was created in the country. This served to help economic growth and to fight the "flight" of many educated people who were not returning to the islands to work.[^cite_note-128]

[Comorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorian_language) has no native script, but both the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) alphabets are used. In 2004, about 57 percent of the population was literate in the [Latin script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_script) while more than 90 percent were literate in the [Arabic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script).[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

## Culture

See also: [Public holidays in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Comoros)

Traditionally, women on Ndzwani wear red and white patterned garments called *shiromani*, while on Ngazidja and Mwali colourful shawls called *leso* are worn. Many women apply a paste of ground [sandalwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandalwood) and [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral) called *[msindzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msindzano)* to their faces.[^cite_note-131] Traditional male clothing is a long white shirt known as a *nkandu*, and a bonnet called a *kofia*.[^cite_note-132]

### Marriage

There are two types of marriages in Comoros, the little marriage (known as *Mna daho* on Ngazidja) and the customary marriage (known as *ada* on Ngazidja, *harusi* on the other islands). The little marriage is a simple legal marriage. It is small, intimate, and inexpensive, and the bride's dowry is nominal. A man may undertake a number of *Mna daho* marriages in his lifetime, often at the same time, a woman fewer; but both men and women will usually only undertake one *ada*, or grand marriage, and this must generally be within the village. The hallmarks of the grand marriage are dazzling gold jewelry, two weeks of celebration and an enormous bridal dowry. Although the expenses are shared between both families as well as with a wider social circle, an ada wedding on Ngazidja can cost up to €50,000.[^cite_note-133] Many couples take a lifetime to save for their ada, and it is not uncommon for a marriage to be attended by a couple's adult children.[^cite_note-134]

The *ada* marriage marks a man's transition in the Ngazidja age system from youth to elder. His status in the social hierarchy greatly increases, and he will henceforth be entitled to speak in public and participate in the political process, both in his village and more widely across the island. He will be entitled to display his status by wearing a *mharuma*, a type of shawl, across his shoulders, and he can enter the mosque by the door reserved for elders, and sit at the front. A woman's status also changes, although less formally, as she becomes a "mother" and moves into her own house. The system is less formalised on the other islands, but the marriage is nevertheless a significant and costly event across the archipelago. The *ada* is often criticized because of its great expense, but at the same time it is a source of social cohesion and the main reason why migrants in France and elsewhere continue to send money home. Increasingly, marriages are also being taxed for the purposes of village development.[^cite_note-Walker,_Iain_2010-135]

### Kinship and social structure



Villagers in Bangwa Kuuni, Ngazidja

Comorian society has a [bilateral descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_descent) system. Lineage membership and inheritance of immovable goods (land, housing) is matrilineal, passed in the maternal line, similar to many [Bantu peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples) who are also matrilineal, while other goods and patronymics are passed in the male line. However, there are differences between the islands, the matrilineal element being stronger on Ngazidja.[^cite_note-Walker,_Iain_2010-135]

### Music

Further information: [Music of the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Comoros)

[Twarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twarab) music, imported from [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar) in the early 20th century, remains the most influential genre on the islands and is popular at *ada* marriages.[^cite_note-136]

### Media

Further information: [Mass media in the Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_the_Comoros)

There are two daily national [newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper) published in the Comoros, the government-owned *[Al-Watwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Watwan)*,[^cite_note-137] and the privately owned *La Gazette des Comores*, both published in [Moroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroni,_Comoros). There are a number of smaller newsletters published on an irregular basis as well as a variety of news websites. The government-owned ORTC (Office de Radio et Télévision des Comores) provides national radio and television service. There is a TV station run by the [Anjouan](184799.md) regional government, and regional governments on the islands of [Grande Comore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Comore) and Anjouan each operate a radio station. There are also a few independent and small community radio stations that operate on the islands of Grande Comore and [Mohéli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moh%C3%A9li), and these two islands have access to Mayotte Radio and French TV.[^cite_note-138]
