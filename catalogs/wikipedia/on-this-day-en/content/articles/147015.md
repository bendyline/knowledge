The **MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System** (**ATACMS** [/əˈtækəmz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is an American [short-range](1320069.md) [tactical ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_ballistic_missile). It is launched from the [M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_Multiple_Launch_Rocket_System) (MLRS), the [M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System](147011.md) (HIMARS), and the [GMARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMARS). Depending on the variant, it has a maximum range of up to 190 miles (300 km).

ATACMS originated in the Joint Tactical Missile System (JTACMS) program, which began in 1980 to replace the [MGM-52 Lance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-52_Lance). After the [United States Air Force](32090.md) withdrew from the program in 1984, the missile was redesignated the Army Tactical Missile System and its development continued under the U.S. Army. [Ling-Temco-Vought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ling-Temco-Vought) (LTV) later won the missile design contract, and ATACMS entered service in 1991, before evolving through several variants with different guidance systems, warheads, and ranges.

ATACMS has been used in combat in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), the [Iraq War](5043324.md), the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), and the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). It has undergone modernization through the ATACMS Service Life Extension Program (SLEP) and is being replaced in U.S. service by the [Precision Strike Missile](71176097.md) (PrSM).

## History

### Pre-development

The concept of a conventional tactical ballistic missile was made possible by the doctrinal shift of the late [Cold War](325329.md), which rejected the indispensability of an early nuclear strike on the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) forces in the event the Cold War went hot.[^cite_note-Romanczuk-9] The [AirLand Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirLand_Battle) and [Follow-on Forces Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follow-on_Forces_Attack) doctrines, which emerged in the late 1970s and early 1980s, necessitated a conventional-armed (hence much more accurate) missile to strike enemy reserves, so the [United States Army Aviation and Missile Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Aviation_and_Missile_Command) sponsored the Simplified Inertial Guidance Demonstrator (SIG-D) program.[^cite_note-Romanczuk-9]

Within this program, [Ling-Temco-Vought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ling-Temco-Vought) developed a solid-fuel analog of the [MGM-52 Lance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-52_Lance) missile, designated T-22,[^cite_note-10] with a new [RLG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_laser_gyroscope)-based [inertial guidance package](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system), which demonstrated unprecedented accuracy.[^cite_note-Romanczuk-9] In 1978, [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) started the [Assault Breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Breaker) technology demonstration program to attack armor formations with many mobile hard targets at standoff ranges. It used the T-22 missile and the Patriot-based [Martin Marietta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Marietta) T-16 missile with cluster warheads.

In March 1980 the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) decided to replace the Lance with a similar nuclear-tipped solid-fuel missile with simplified usability dubbed the Corps Support Weapon System (CSWS). In a year, concerned about the fact Army started to develop the weapon with a similar objectives to interdict the [second-echelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-echelon) massed targets to already developing by [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF)'s Conventional Standoff Weapon (CSW) program with only difference of surface/air-launched and both positioned as the part of same Short Range Nuclear Forces of Non-Strategic Nuclear Force Program, the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) subdued CSWS Project Office (Provisional) to [MICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MICOM) renaming it to the System Development Office. That new office acquired the [Assault Breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Breaker) effort thus started to manage the Assault Breaker and CSWS efforts together, that way slowly summing up and moving forward the weapon development progress for the JTACMS program to be created.[^cite_note-history.redstone.army.mil-11][^cite_note-12]

### Development

Development of the missile now known as ATACMS started in 1980, when the [US Army](32087.md) decided to replace the Lance with a similar nuclear, but also chemical or biological, tipped solid-fuel missile dubbed the Corps Support Weapon System (CSWS). Concerned that two branches were developing too many similar missiles with different warheads, the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) merged the program with DARPA's Assault Breaker in 1981, and with [United States Air Force](32090.md) (USAF)'s Conventional Standoff Weapon (CSW) in 1982–1983.[^cite_note-history.redstone.army.mil-11]

The new missile system, designated Joint Tactical Missile System (JTACMS), soon encountered USAF resistance to the idea of an [air-launched ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-launched_ballistic_missile). As a result, in 1984 the USAF ended its participation in the non-cruise missile portion of the program, leading to the missile being redesignated as the Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS).[^cite_note-history.redstone.army.mil-11]

#### Details

In [fiscal year](5752099.md) 1982, the [United States House Committee on Appropriations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Appropriations) approved the Corps Support Weapon System (CSWS) program, which was the successor to the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) [Assault Breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Breaker) program in cooperation with [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA), was merged with the Conventional Standoff Weapon (CSW) [US Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Air_Force) and renamed the Joint Tactical Missile System (JTACMS), the goal of which was to create a weapon that meets the combined requirements of both programs, namely, that it can attack and destroy the [second-echelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-echelon) of enemy forces, in particular [armored vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_vehicle), and scatter submunitions against such vehicles. In this project, it was planned to use the technologies of Assault Breaker to develop a [surface-to-surface](1483499.md) weapon system, which should be used for the so-called "deep interdiction" (some sort of preventive measure, the prototype of which is [air interdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interdiction) used by air force) – by which is meant the destruction or causing significant damage by the joint activity of air and ground forces to the specific distant from the [front line](11043.md) targets, such as buildings, bridges, [oil refineries](195137.md) and other [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(manufacturing\)), that way slowing down logistics or providing or supporting and, therefore, advancing enemy troops with the aim of [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics), even albeit short-term, superiority of allied troops, which can significantly affect the [military theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_theater) in a positive way,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] – using [conventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_weapons) or [nuclear](21785.md) weapons on the [battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlefield). Although both services were to participate in the development of the weapon, it was the US Army who led the JTACMS program.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

The program was initially led by Colonel James B. Lincoln, who was a full-time and continuous student at numerous military schools (from 1960, when he graduated from the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy), until 1980, when he graduated from the [Industrial College of the Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_College_of_the_Armed_Forces)),[^cite_note-20] in 1977 on the basis of [Defense Systems Management College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Systems_Management_College) graduated with a [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) of "Managing Total Acquisition Time: A New Priority for Major Weapon Systems", where, in particular, he focused on the significant decrease in the pace of procurement of the main missile complexes compared to 1971[^cite_note-21] and in 1980, heading the [TRADOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRADOC) program at [Fort Sill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sill) in the direction of [MLRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLRS), spoke rather defiantly about field army systems, where, in particular, he compared the struggle of the US Army for limited resources during the development of new systems with [bow wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_wave), which prevents the ship from accelerating, and military projects are either canceled or refinanced by the state, with waves diverging from it,[^cite_note-22] and was noticed by [DARCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARCOM).[^cite_note-23] In April 1984, he was transferred to be the head of the [TOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-71_TOW) project, and in the current project he was replaced by Colonel William J. Fiorentino, who by that time had already been the head of the [Pershing Project Manager's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pershing_Project_Manager's_Office) for more than 5 years, which during his leadership developed two-stage solid fuel [mobile-launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_launcher_vehicle) [ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missiles) with a nuclear warhead both [short](1320069.md) ([Pershing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-31_Pershing)) and [medium](478272.md) ([Pershing II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pershing_II)) ranged.[^cite_note-24] Dr. Billy Tidwell who was program manager during JTACMS while and Acting Program Manager for a short period.[^cite_note-25]

In FY 1984 Congress prohibited the development of a nuclear warhead for JTACMS, despite the Army claiming it could place US forces at a disadvantage if it became necessary to make the system nuclear-capable. In FY 1985 the Army denied having R\&D funds programmed for the development of a JTACMS nuclear warhead.[^cite_note-26]

On May, 22nd, 1984 the US Army and USAF signed an agreeing on a list of 31 initiatives. Item 18 on that list states about services will develop a different types of same rocket for each of it – preferences for Army was a development of shorter-ranged ground-launched system, for air-force – air-launched system.[^cite_note-auto2-27]

JTACMS was intended to be a jointly funded program with NATO allies; the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany), [Belgium](3343.md), [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Netherlands) and [Italy](14532.md) were initially contacted about joining the program, with the British and Germans expressing interest, while the others declined due to lacking adequate funds.[^cite_note-auto2-27]

Starting from at least the end of year 1986, the ATACMS program was led by Colonel Thomas J. Kunhart.[^cite_note-28]

### Production

In March 1986, Ling-Temco-Vought won the contract for the missile design. The system was assigned the MGM-140 designation. The first test launch came two years later, thanks to earlier experience of the company with previous programs.

In 2007, the US Army terminated the ATACMS program due to cost, ending the ability to replenish stocks. To sustain the remaining inventory, the ATACMS Service Life Extension Program (SLEP) was launched, which refurbishes or replaces propulsion and navigation systems, replaces [cluster munition](79745.md) warheads with the unitary [blast fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast-fragmentation) warhead, and adds a [proximity fuze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximity_fuze) option to obtain area effects. Deliveries were projected to start in 2018. The ATACMS SLEP is a bridging initiative to provide time to complete analysis and development of a successor capability to the aging ATACMS stockpile, which could be ready around 2022.[^cite_note-29]

The [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023) authorized the production and procurement of up to 1,700 additional ATACMS, but this was not funded by the 2023 Defense Appropriations Act.[^cite_note-Altman_2023_l764-30][^cite_note-NDAA_2023-31]

Whilst domestic orders remain zero, in recent times Lockheed Martin has enjoyed great success on the export market securing a 440 million dollars worth of orders for various international customers, including Taiwan, Poland and Germany. These missiles are being produced at a new industrial site in Camden, Ark, as well as a new facility run by Rheinmetall in Unterluess, Germany, which will produce the ATACAMS under licence. Deliveries from the Camden site began in 2024 with the Rheinmetall site to begin production at a later date. [^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

### Stockpile upgrades

In January 2015, Lockheed Martin received a contract to develop and test new hardware for Block I ATACMS missiles to eliminate the risk of unexploded ordnance by 2016.[^cite_note-armyreco8jan15-4][^cite_note-armedint8jan15-5] The first modernized Tactical Missile System (TACMS) was delivered in September 2016 with updated guidance electronics and added capability to defeat area targets using a unitary warhead, without leaving behind unexploded ordnance.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Lockheed was awarded a production contract for launch assemblies as part of the SLEP in August 2017.[^cite_note-36] In 2021, Lockheed Martin was contracted to upgrade existing M39 munitions to the M57 variant with a WDU-18/B warhead from the [Harpoon missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_missile) by 2024.[^cite_note-militaryaerospace-m57-37]

A plan announced in October 2016 to add an existing seeker to enable the ATACMS to strike moving targets on land and at sea[^cite_note-38] was terminated in December 2020 to pursue other missile efforts.[^cite_note-39]

### Replacement

Starting in 2016, the [Long-Range Precision Fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Artillery_Branch_\(United_States\)#Long_Range_Precision_Fires_\(LRPF\)_CFT) (LRPF) program began to be developed,[^cite_note-40] which was later renamed [Precision Strike Missile](71176097.md) (PrSM), with the idea of replacing ATACMS missiles with the "Increment 1" phase (version) of PrSM.[^cite_note-Turner-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] PrSM Increment 1 missiles began delivery in late 2023.[^cite_note-44]

## Operation

The ATACMS was first used in combat in 1991: 32 were fired from the [M270 MLRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_MLRS) during [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm).[^cite_note-71] In 2003, more than 450 were fired in [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom).[^cite_note-72] By early 2015, more than 560 ATACMS missiles had been used in combat.[^cite_note-armyreco8jan15-4][^cite_note-armedint8jan15-5]

### During the Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)

Main article: [Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)](70149799.md)

Starting from October 2023 Ukraine began using the earliest (short-ranged) versions of ATACMS during the Russo-Ukrainian war.[^cite_note-Court_2023_l481-73] These missiles were meant to threaten the Russian-occupied "land corridor" to [Crimea](163045.md) in the southern part of Ukraine[^cite_note-Brennan_2023_i217-74] as well as the vast majority of the Russian-operated air bases in the north of Crimea, which would theoretically complicate Russia's use of [attack helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopters) from those bases against Ukrainian targets.[^cite_note-Forbes_101723-75] The first recorded Ukrainian ATACMS strikes destroyed numerous Russian Military helicopters on the ground, confirmed by satellite imagery and video from the ground.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

Starting from 19 February 2024 there were rumors about possible near-future use of later (longer-ranged) versions of ATACMS by Ukraine,[^cite_note-78] which were shortly proven correct when an ATACMS missile attack on the Russian-occupied [Dzhankoi air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzhankoi_\(air_base\)) resulted in six main explosions and several reported [secondary explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathetic_detonation#Military). The base was positioned much further than earlier versions of ATACMS' strike range from Ukraine-controlled territory.[^cite_note-79] It was then officially confirmed when US officials revealed Ukraine had already received and deployed the missiles to a combat-ready status a month prior.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-apnews.com-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] These strikes with longer-range ATACMS deeper into Crimea destroyed components of Russian air defense systems, such as launchers part of the [S-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-300_missile_system) or [S-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-400_missile_system), along with destroying Russian Military aircraft on the ground, such as two MiG-31 interceptors, confirmed by satellite and ground imagery.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

ATACMS has shown an ability to destroy air defense sites, and was used to destroy an active Russian S-300 or S-400 site. Drone footage showed the air defense system launching interceptors before the cluster submunitions of ATACMS impacted, causing numerous detonations of air defense system components such as launchers.[^cite_note-88]

On 23 June an incident occurred during an [attack on Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2024_Sevastopol_missile_attack), where Russian [air defense missiles](1099196.md) were fired at multiple ATACMS missiles resulting in explosions that caused 4 deaths and more than 150 injuries on [Uchkuyivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uchkuyivka?action=edit\&redlink=1) beach, where locals reported that no air raid warning had taken place and therefore people on the beach were not able to evacuate.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

In September Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) threatened retaliation for [attacks on Russian territory](70539619.md).[^cite_note-91] Experts said Putin's threats are aimed at dissuading the United States, the United Kingdom and France from allowing Ukraine to use Western-supplied long-range missiles such as the [Storm Shadow](306908.md) and ATACMS in strikes against Russia.[^cite_note-92]

On 17 November the United States changed policy, allowing Ukraine to use ATACMS for military targets inside mainland Russia.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] On 19 November, ATACMS were for the first time fired at a target within the internationally recognized borders of Russia. An ammunition storage facility was destroyed in the Bryansk region of Russia, with videos of explosions and fires on the ground at the site emerging.[^cite_note-95] On 25 November, ATACMS were used to hit a Russian S-400 air defense system at an airbase in the [Kursk](480916.md) region of Russia. Video emerged of ATACMS cluster munitions hitting the airbase, and imagery emerged of a destroyed 92N6E radar station of the S-400 system.[^cite_note-96]

On 24 February 2026, ATACMS were fired by Ukraine on a Russian 5th Army “auxiliary command” near [Novopetrykivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novopetrykivka) in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md).[^cite_note-97]

#### EW vs usability

The ATACMS uses multiple inertial navigation units knitted together with software, so it is reportedly able to maintain accuracy when GPS is lost due to [electronic warfare](82272.md) better than other GPS-guided weapons.[^cite_note-98]

#### Reverse engineering

On 1 July 2024, Russia claimed to have recovered an ATACMS missile guidance system intact, and stated that Russian officials are studying the guidance system to "identify any weak spots".[^cite_note-99]

### During the 2026 Iran war

Main article: [2026 Iran war](82533582.md)

The ATACMS was used in combat in the 2026 Iran war.[^cite_note-100] The US Army reportedly expended almost all of its ATACMS inventory during the first five months of the conflict.[^cite_note-101] It fired more than 1,300 of the Army's tactical ballistic missiles in the initial weeks of fighting.[^cite_note-102]
