"Catholic Cardinals" redirects here. For sports teams of the Catholic University of America, see [Catholic University Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_Cardinals).

A **cardinal**[^cite_note-1] is a senior member of the [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy) of the [Catholic Church](606848.md). As titular members of the clergy of the [Diocese of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome), they serve as advisors to the [pope](23056.md), who is the bishop of Rome and the [visible head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Church#Catholic_Church)[^cite_note-2] of the worldwide Catholic Church. Cardinals are chosen and formally created by the pope, and typically hold the title for life. Collectively, they constitute the [College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Cardinals). The most solemn responsibility of the cardinals is to elect a new pope in a [conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_conclave) when the [Holy See](13393.md) is [vacant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sede_vacante#Vacancy_of_the_Holy_See). With a few historical exceptions, popes are elected from among the College of Cardinals.[^cite_note-dol-in.org-3]

During the period between a pope's death or resignation and the election of his successor, the day-to-day governance of the Holy See is in the hands of the College of Cardinals. The right to participate in a conclave is limited to cardinals who have not reached the age of 80 years by the day the vacancy occurs.[^cite_note-dol-in.org-3] With the pope, cardinals collectively participate in [papal consistories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_consistory), in which matters of importance to the Church are considered and new cardinals may be created. Cardinals of working age are also often appointed to roles overseeing [dicasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery) (departments) of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia), the central administration of the Catholic Church.

Cardinals are drawn from a variety of backgrounds, being appointed as cardinals in addition to their existing roles within the Church. Most cardinals are [bishops](8799424.md#Diocesan_bishops_or_eparchs) and [archbishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) leading [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) and [archdioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese) around the world – often the most prominent diocese or archdiocese in their country. Others are [titular bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_bishop) who are current or former officials within the Roman Curia, generally the heads of dicasteries and other bodies linked to the Curia. A very small number are priests recognised by the pope for their service to the Church. Canon law requires them to be generally [consecrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecration#Roman_Catholic_Church) as bishops before they are made cardinals,[^cite_note-4] but some are granted a papal dispensation.[^cite_note-6] There are no strict criteria for elevation to the College of Cardinals. Since 1917, a potential cardinal must already be at least a priest, but laymen have been cardinals in the past. The selection is entirely up to the pope, and tradition is his only guide.

As of 3 August 2026, [there are 240 serving cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_cardinals), of whom 116 are eligible to vote in a conclave to elect a new pope.

## History

Further information: [Papal selection before 1059](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_selection_before_1059)



[Cardinal Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu), chief minister of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) in the early 17th century

There is general disagreement about the origin of the term, but a chief consensus is that the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *cardinalis* comes from the term *[cardo](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cardo#Latin)* (meaning 'pivot' or 'hinge'). It was first used in [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity) to designate a bishop or priest who was incorporated into a church for which he had not originally been ordained. In [Rome](25458.md) the first persons to be called cardinals were the deacons of the seven regions of the city at the beginning of the 6th century, when the word began to mean 'principal', 'eminent', or 'superior'.[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-7]

The name was also given to the senior priest in each of the "title" churches (the parish churches) of Rome and to the bishops of the seven sees surrounding the city. By the 8th century the Roman cardinals constituted a privileged class among the Roman clergy. They took part in the administration of the Church of Rome and in the papal liturgy. By decree of [a synod of 769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateran_Council_\(769\)), only a cardinal was eligible to become Bishop of Rome. Cardinals were granted the privilege of wearing the red hat by Pope [Innocent IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innocent_IV) in 1244.[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-7]

In cities other than Rome, the name cardinal began to be applied to certain churchmen as a mark of honour. The earliest example of this occurs in a letter sent by [Pope Zacharias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Zacharias) in 747 to [Pippin the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_the_Short), ruler of the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), in which Zacharias applied the title to the priests of Paris to distinguish them from country clergy. This meaning of the word spread rapidly, and from the 9th century various episcopal cities had a special class among the clergy known as cardinals. The use of the title was reserved for the cardinals of Rome in 1567 by [Pius V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_V).

In 1059, five years after the [East-West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East-West_Schism), the right of electing the pope was reserved to the principal clergy of Rome and the bishops of the seven [suburbicarian sees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbicarian_diocese). In the 12th century the practice of appointing ecclesiastics from outside Rome as cardinals began, with each of them assigned a church in Rome as his [titular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church) or linked with one of the suburbicarian dioceses, while still being incardinated in a diocese other than that of Rome.

The term *cardinal* at one time applied to any priest permanently assigned or [incardinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incardinated) to a church,[^cite_note-CE-cardinal-8] or specifically to the senior priest of an important church, based on the Latin *cardo* ('hinge'), meaning 'pivotal' as in "principal" or "chief". The term was applied in this sense as early as the 9th century to the priests of the *tituli* ([parishes](23623.md)) of the [diocese of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome).[^cite_note-CE-cardinal-8]

In the year 1563, the Ecumenical Council of Trent, headed by Pope [Pius IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_IV), wrote about the importance of selecting good cardinals: "nothing is more necessary to the Church of God than that the holy Roman pontiff apply that solicitude which by the duty of his office he owes the universal Church in a very special way by associating with himself as cardinals the most select persons only, and appoint to each church most eminently upright and competent shepherds; and this the more so, because our Lord [Jesus Christ](1095706.md) will require at his hands the blood of the sheep of Christ that perish through the evil government of shepherds who are negligent and forgetful of their office."[^cite_note-9]

The earlier influence of temporal rulers, notably the [kings of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_of_France), reasserted itself through the influence of cardinals of certain nationalities or politically significant movements. Traditions even developed entitling certain monarchs, including those of Austria, Spain, and France, to nominate one of their trusted clerical subjects to be created cardinal, a so-called "[crown-cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown-cardinal)".[^cite_note-Chadwick-10]

In [early modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period) times, cardinals often had important roles in secular affairs. In some cases, they took on powerful positions in government. In [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII)'s England, his chief minister was for some time [Cardinal Wolsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Wolsey). [Cardinal Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu)'s power was so great that he was for many years effectively the ruler of France.[^cite_note-11] Richelieu's successor was also a cardinal, [Jules Mazarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Mazarin). [Guillaume Dubois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Dubois) and [André-Hercule de Fleury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9-Hercule_de_Fleury) complete the list of the four great cardinals to have ruled France.[^cite_note-Chadwick-10] In Portugal, due to a succession crisis, one cardinal, [Henry of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry,_King_of_Portugal), was crowned king, the only example of a cardinal-king (although [John II Casimir Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_Casimir_Vasa) was a cardinal from 1646 until he resigned in 1647, later being elected and crowned King of Poland, in 1648 and 1649, respectively).[^cite_note-12]

While the incumbents of some sees are regularly made cardinals, and some countries are entitled to at least one cardinal by [concordat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordat) (usually earning either its [primate](24672.md) or the metropolitan of the capital city the cardinal's hat), almost no see carries an actual right to the cardinalate, not even if its bishop is a [patriarch](175409.md#Catholic_Church): the notable exception is the [Patriarch of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Lisbon) who, by [Pope Clement XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII)'s 1737 bull *[Inter praecipuas apostolici ministerii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_praecipuas_apostolici_ministerii)*, is accorded the right to be elevated to the rank of cardinal in the consistory following his appointment.[^cite_note-13]

## Papal elections

In 1059, [Pope Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II) gave cardinals the right to elect the [Bishop of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome) in the [papal bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull) *[In nomine Domini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_nomine_Domini)*. For a time this power was assigned exclusively to the cardinal bishops, but in 1179 the [Third Lateran Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Lateran_Council) restored the right to the whole body of cardinals.[^cite_note-TCE1-14]

This council also established a two-thirds majority of the College of Cardinals as a requirement for a valid papal election.[^cite_note-15]

## Numbers

In 1586, [Pope Sixtus V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V) limited the number of cardinals to 70:[^cite_note-16] six [cardinal bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_bishop), 50 [cardinal priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_priest), and 14 [cardinal deacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_deacon). The number of seventy was in reference to the [Sanhedrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanhedrin) and to the [seventy disciples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy_disciples). [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII) exceeded that limit citing the need to staff church offices.[^cite_note-17] In November 1970, in *[Ingravescentem aetatem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingravescentem_aetatem)*, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) established that electors would be under the age of 80 years. When it took effect on 1 January 1971, it deprived 25 cardinals of the right to participate in a conclave.[^cite_note-18] In October 1975 in *[Romano Pontifici eligendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Pontifici_eligendo)*, he set the maximum number of electors at 120, while establishing no limit on the overall size of the college.[^cite_note-19]

Popes can [set aside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispensation_\(Catholic_Church\)) church laws[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] and they have regularly brought the number of cardinals under the age of 80 to more than 120, reaching as high as 140 with [Pope Francis' consistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinals_created_by_Francis#7_December_2024) of December 2024.[^cite_note-22] No more than 120 electors participated in a [conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_conclave) until the conclave following the death of Pope Francis, in which 133 cardinals participated.[^cite_note-23]

Pope Paul VI also increased the number of cardinal bishops by assigning that rank, in 1965, to patriarchs of the [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md) when named cardinals.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In 2018, Pope Francis expanded the cardinal bishops of Roman title, because this had not been done despite recent decades' expansion in the two lower orders of cardinals, besides having all six such cardinals being over the age limit for a conclave.

## Titular churches

Main articles: [Titular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church) and [List of titular churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_titular_churches)



[Cardinal Innitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Innitzer), [Archbishop of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Vienna) and Cardinal-Priest of [San Crisogono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crisogono), pictured in the early 1930s

Each cardinal is assigned a [titular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church) upon his creation, which is always a church in the [city of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Rome). Through the process of opting (*optazione*), a cardinal can rise through the ranks from cardinal deacon to cardinal priest, and previously could rise from cardinal priest to cardinal bishop. If a cardinal bishop, he usually obtains one of the [suburbicarian sees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbicarian_diocese) located around the city of Rome.[^cite_note-26] The only exception is for patriarchs of the Eastern Catholic Churches.[^cite_note-Paul_VI._1965-27]

Nevertheless, cardinals possess no power of governance nor are they to intervene in any way in matters which pertain to the administration of goods, discipline, or the service of their titular churches.[^cite_note-28] They are allowed to celebrate [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) and hear confessions and lead visits and pilgrimages to their titular churches, in coordination with the staff of the church. They often support their churches monetarily, and many cardinals do keep in contact with the pastoral staffs of their titular churches.

The [Dean of the College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_the_College_of_Cardinals) in addition to such a titular church also receives the [titular bishopric of Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Ostia), the primary suburbicarian see. Cardinals governing a [particular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church) retain that church.[^cite_note-29]

## Title and reference style

In 1630, [Pope Urban VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII) decreed their title to be *[Eminence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Eminence)* (previously, it had been *illustrissimus* and *reverendissimus*)[^cite_note-30] and decreed that their secular rank would equate to prince, making them second only to the pope and crowned monarchs.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In accordance with tradition, they sign by placing the title "Cardinal" (abbreviated *Card.*) after their personal name and before their surname as, for instance, "John Card(inal) Doe" or, in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), "Ioannes Card(inalis) Doe". Some writers, such as James-Charles Noonan,[^cite_note-33] hold that, in the case of cardinals, the form used for signatures should be used also when referring to them in English.

Official sources, such as the [Catholic News Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_News_Service),[^cite_note-34] say that the correct form for referring to a cardinal in English is normally as "Cardinal \[First name] \[Surname]". This is the rule given also in stylebooks not associated with the church.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] This style is also generally followed on the websites of the Holy See and [episcopal conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_conference).[^cite_note-39] Oriental patriarchs who are created cardinals customarily use *Sanctae Ecclesiae Cardinalis* as their full title,[^cite_note-40] probably because they do not belong to the Roman clergy.[^cite_note-Paul_VI._1965-27]

The "\[First name] Cardinal \[Surname]" order is used in the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) [proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habemus_Papam) of the election of a new pope by the cardinal protodeacon,[^cite_note-42] if the new pope is a cardinal, as has been the case since 1389.

The title *Prince of the Church* has historically been applied to cardinals of the Catholic Church, and sometimes more broadly to senior members of the church hierarchy. It has been rejected by [Pope Francis](1687680.md), who stated to a group of newly created cardinals "He (Jesus) does not call you to become 'princes' of the Church, to 'sit on his right or on his left.' He calls you to serve like Him and with Him."[^cite_note-43] The title is still applied contemporarily, both officially and other times in criticism of the perceived attitudes of some cardinals.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

## Orders and their chief offices



[Choir dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir_dress) of a cardinal

### Cardinal bishops



[Giovanni Battista Re](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Re) has been the [Dean of the College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_the_College_of_Cardinals) since 2020.

Cardinal bishops (cardinals of the episcopal order; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *cardinales episcopi*) are the senior order of cardinals. Though in modern times the vast majority of cardinals are also [bishops](8799424.md) or [archbishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishops), few are "cardinal bishops". Until 1150, there were seven cardinal bishops, each presiding over one of the seven [suburbicarian sees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbicarian_diocese) around Rome: [Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Ostia), [Albano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Albano), [Porto and Santa Rufina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Porto-Santa_Rufina), [Palestrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Palestrina), [Sabina and Mentana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Sabina-Poggio_Mirteto), [Frascati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Frascati), and [Velletri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Velletri-Segni).[^cite_note-46] Of these seven, Velletri was united with Ostia from 1150 until 1914, when [Pope Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_X) separated them again, but decreed that whichever cardinal bishop became [Dean of the College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_the_College_of_Cardinals) would keep the suburbicarian see he already held, adding to it that of Ostia, with the result that there continued to be only six cardinal bishops.[^cite_note-47] The actual number of cardinal bishops for the majority of the second millennium was thus six. Since 1962, the cardinal bishops have only a [titular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church) relationship with the suburbicarian sees, each of which is governed by a separate [ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_\(Catholic_Church\)).[^cite_note-48]

Until 1961, membership in the order of cardinal bishops was achieved through precedence in the [College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Cardinals). When a suburbicarian see fell vacant, the most senior cardinal by precedence could exercise his option to claim the see and be promoted to the order of cardinal bishops.[^cite_note-droit-49][^cite_note-51] [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII) abolished that privilege on 10 March 1961 and made the right to promote someone to the order of cardinal bishops the sole prerogative of the pope.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-55]

In 1965, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) decreed in his *[motu proprio](793396.md) Ad purpuratorum Patrum Collegium* that [patriarchs](175409.md) of the [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md) who were named cardinals (i.e. "cardinal patriarchs") would also be cardinal bishops, ranking after the six Latin Church cardinal bishops of the suburbicarian sees.[^cite_note-56] [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) patriarchs who become cardinals are [cardinal priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_priest), not cardinal bishops: for example [Angelo Scola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Scola) was made the [Patriarch of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice) in 2002 and cardinal priest of [Santi XII Apostoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_XII_Apostoli) in 2003. Those of cardinal patriarch rank continue to hold their patriarchal see and are not assigned any Roman title (suburbicarian see, title or deaconry).

At the June 2018 consistory, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) increased the number of Latin Church cardinal bishops to match the expansion in cardinal priests and cardinal deacons in recent decades. He elevated four cardinals to this rank granting their [titular churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church) and deaconries suburbicarian rank *[pro hac vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_hac_vice)* (temporarily)[^cite_note-57] and making them equivalent to suburbicarian see titles. At the time of the announcement, all six cardinal bishops of suburbicarian see titles, as well as two of the three cardinal patriarchs, were non-electors as they had reached the age of 80.[^cite_note-58] Pope Francis created another cardinal bishop in the same way on 1 May 2020,[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-RapplerCardinalBishop-60] bringing the number of Latin Church cardinal bishops to 11.

The [dean of the College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_the_College_of_Cardinals), the highest-ranking cardinal, was formerly the longest-serving cardinal bishop, but since 1965 is elected by the Latin Church cardinal bishops from among their number, subject to papal approval. Likewise the vice-dean, formerly the second longest-serving, is also elected. Seniority of the remaining Latin Church cardinal bishops is still by date of appointment to the rank. The current dean is [Giovanni Battista Re](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Re) and the vice-dean is [Leonardo Sandri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Sandri).

### Cardinal priests

Cardinal priests ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *cardinales presbyteri*) are the most numerous of the three orders of cardinals in the Catholic Church, ranking above the cardinal deacons and below the cardinal bishops.[^cite_note-61] Those who are named cardinal priests today are generally also [bishops](8799424.md) of important [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) throughout the world, though some hold [Curial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia) positions.

In modern times, the term *cardinal priest* is interpreted as meaning a cardinal who is of the order of priests. Originally this referred to certain key priests of important churches of the [Diocese of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome), who were recognized as the *cardinal* priests – the important priests chosen by the pope to advise him in his duties as [Bishop](4092.md) of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome). Certain clerics in many dioceses at the time, not just that of Rome, were said to be the key personnel—the term gradually became exclusive to Rome to indicate those entrusted with electing the Bishop of Rome, the pope.



Cardinal-priest [Thomas Wolsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Wolsey)

While the cardinalate has long been expanded beyond the Roman pastoral clergy and [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia), every cardinal priest has a [titular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church) in Rome, though they may be bishops or archbishops elsewhere, just as cardinal bishops were given one of the [suburbicarian dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbicarian_diocese) around Rome. [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) abolished all administrative rights cardinals had with regard to their titular churches, though the cardinal's name and coat of arms are still posted in the church, and they are expected to celebrate Mass and preach there if convenient when they are in Rome.

While the number of cardinals was small from the times of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) to the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), and frequently smaller than the number of recognized churches entitled to a cardinal priest, in the 16th century the college expanded markedly. In 1587, [Pope Sixtus V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V) sought to arrest this growth by fixing the maximum [size of the college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Size_of_the_College_of_Cardinals) at 70, including 50 cardinal priests, about twice the historical number. This limit was respected until 1958, and the list of titular churches modified only on rare occasions, generally when a building fell into disrepair. When [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII) abolished the limit, he began to add new churches to the list, which Popes [Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) and [John Paul II](23805.md) continued to do. Today there are close to 150 titular churches, out of more than 300 churches in Rome.

The cardinal who is the longest-serving member of the order of cardinal priests is titled *cardinal [protopriest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protopriest_of_the_College_of_Cardinals)*. He had certain ceremonial duties in the conclave that have effectively ceased because he would generally have already reached age 80, at which cardinals are barred from the conclave. The current cardinal protopriest is [Michael Michai Kitbunchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Michai_Kitbunchu) of [Thailand](30128.md).

### Cardinal deacons

The cardinal deacons ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *cardinales diaconi*) are the lowest-ranking cardinals. Cardinals elevated to the diaconal order are either officials of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia) or priests elevated after their 80th birthday, chosen mainly for the honor of it, since those over 80 are not able to vote in a conclave. While bishops with diocesan responsibilities are created cardinal priests, it is generally not so for cardinal deacons.

Cardinal deacons derive originally from the seven deacons in the [Papal Household](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_Household) who supervised the church's works in the [14 districts of Rome during the early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_regions_of_Medieval_Rome), when church administration was effectively the [government of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States) and provided all [social services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_services). They came to be called "cardinal deacons" by the late eighth century, and they were granted active rights in papal elections and made eligible for the election as pope by the [Lateran Council of 769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateran_Council_of_769).[^cite_note-62]

Cardinals elevated to the diaconal order are mainly officials of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia) holding various posts in the church administration. Their number and influence has varied through the years. While historically predominantly Italian, the group has become much more internationally diverse in later years. In 1939, about half were Italian. In 1994, approximately one third were Italian. Their influence in the election of the pope has been considered important. They are better informed and connected than the dislocated cardinals but their level of unity has been varied.[^cite_note-Thomas_J._Reese_pp._92–93-63]

Under the 1587 decree of [Pope Sixtus V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V), which fixed the maximum size of the College of Cardinals, there were 14 cardinal deacons. Later the number increased. As late as 1939 almost half of the cardinals were members of the Curia. Pius XII reduced this percentage to 24 percent. John XXIII brought it back up to 37 percent but Paul VI brought it down to 27 percent. John Paul II maintained this ratio.[^cite_note-Thomas_J._Reese_pp._92–93-63]

As of 2005, there were more than 50 churches recognized as cardinalatial deaconries, though there were only 30 cardinals of the order of deacons. Cardinal deacons have long enjoyed the right to "opt for the order of cardinal priests" (*optazione*) after they have been cardinal deacons for ten years. They may on such elevation take a vacant "[title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_church)" (a church allotted to a cardinal priest as the church in Rome with which he is associated) or their diaconal church may be temporarily elevated to a cardinal priest's "title" for that occasion. When elevated to cardinal priests, they take their precedence according to the day they were first made cardinal deacons, thus ranking above cardinal priests who were elevated to the college after them, regardless of order.

When not celebrating Mass, but still serving a liturgical function, such as the semiannual *[Urbi et Orbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbi_et_Orbi)* [papal blessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_blessing), some [Papal Masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_Mass) and some events at Ecumenical Councils, cardinal deacons can be recognized by the [dalmatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatic) they would don with the simple white mitre (so called *mitra simplex*).

#### Cardinal protodeacon

The cardinal protodeacon is the senior cardinal deacon in order of appointment to the College of Cardinals. If he is a cardinal elector and participates in a conclave, he [announces a new pope's election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habemus_Papam) and name[^cite_note-65] from the central balcony of [St. Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica) in [Vatican City](32408.md). The protodeacon also bestows the [pallium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallium) on the new pope and crowns him with the [papal tiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_tiara), although the crowning has not been celebrated since [Pope John Paul I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_I) opted for a simpler papal inauguration ceremony in 1978.[^cite_note-66] The current cardinal protodeacon is [Dominique Mamberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Mamberti).[^cite_note-MartinoDies-67]

## Special types of cardinals

### Camerlengo

The Cardinal [Camerlengo of the Holy Roman Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camerlengo_of_the_Holy_Roman_Church), assisted by the Vice-Camerlengo and the other prelates of the office known as the Apostolic Camera, has functions that in essence are limited to a period of *[sede vacante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sede_vacante)* of the papacy. He is to collate information about the financial situation of all administrations dependent on the Holy See and present the results to the College of Cardinals, as they gather for the [papal conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_conclave).[^cite_note-70]

### Cardinals who are not bishops



[Reginald Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Pole) was a cardinal for 18 years before he was ordained a priest.

Until 1918, any cleric, even one only in [minor orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_orders), could be created a cardinal (see "[lay cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_cardinal)", below), but enrolled only in the order of cardinal deacons. For example, in the 16th century, [Reginald Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Pole) was a cardinal for 18 years before he was ordained a priest. The [1917 Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Code_of_Canon_Law) mandated that all cardinals, even cardinal deacons, had to be priests,[^cite_note-71] and, in 1962, [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII) set the norm that all cardinals be consecrated as [bishops](8799424.md), even if they are only priests at the time of appointment.[^cite_note-72]

As a consequence of these two changes, canon 351 of the [1983 Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law) requires that a cardinal be at least in the order of [priesthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_\(Catholic_Church\)) at his appointment, and that those who are not already bishops must receive episcopal consecration. Several cardinals near to or over the age of 80 when appointed have obtained [dispensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispensation_\(Catholic_Church\)) from the rule of having to be a bishop.[^cite_note-73] These were all appointed cardinal-deacons, but [Roberto Tucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Tucci) and [Albert Vanhoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Vanhoye) lived long enough to exercise the right of option and be promoted to the rank of cardinal-priest. Since the 1962 rule change, [Timothy Radcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Radcliffe) has been the only non-bishop cardinal who took part in a papal election, in the [2025 papal conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_papal_conclave).

A cardinal who is not a bishop is entitled to wear and use the episcopal vestments and other [pontificalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontificalia), episcopal regalia being the [mitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitre), [crozier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crozier), [zucchetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchetto), [pectoral cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_cross), and ring. He has both actual and honorary precedence over archbishops, and bishops who are not cardinals. However, he cannot perform the sacrament of [ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Orders) or other rites reserved solely to bishops.

### "Lay cardinals"

Main article: [Lay cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_cardinal)

At various times, there have been cardinals who had only received [first tonsure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonsure#Western_Christianity) and [minor orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_orders) but not yet been [ordained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church) as [deacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) or priests. Though [clerics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy), they were inaccurately called "[lay cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_cardinal)". [Teodolfo Mertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodolfo_Mertel) was among the last of these cardinals. When he died in 1899 he was the last surviving cardinal who was not at least ordained a priest. With the revision of the [Code of Canon Law promulgated in 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Code_of_Canon_Law) by [Pope Benedict XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV), only those who are already priests or bishops may be appointed cardinals.[^cite_note-74] Since the time of [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII), a priest who is appointed a cardinal must be consecrated a bishop,[^cite_note-75] unless they receive a papal dispensation from this requirement.

### Cardinals *in pectore* or secret cardinals

Main article: [In pectore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_pectore)

In addition to the named cardinals, the pope may name secret cardinals or cardinals *[in pectore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_pectore)* (Latin for 'in the breast'). During the [Western Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Schism), many cardinals were created by the contending popes. Beginning with the reign of [Pope Martin V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Martin_V),[^cite_note-CE-cardinal-8] cardinals were created without publishing their names until later, a practice termed *creati et reservati in pectore*.[^cite_note-Rickaby_1913_337-76] A cardinal named *in pectore* is known only to the pope. In the modern era, popes have named cardinals *in pectore* to protect them or their congregations from political persecution or other danger.[^cite_note-Rickaby_1913_337-76]

If conditions change in respect of that persecution, the pope may make the appointment public. The cardinal in question then ranks in terms of precedence with those who were made cardinals at the time of the *in pectore* appointment. If a pope dies before revealing the identity of an *in pectore* cardinal, the person's status as cardinal expires. The last pope known to have named a cardinal *in pectore* is [Pope John Paul II](23805.md), who named four, including one whose identity was never revealed.[^cite_note-78]

When in [choir dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir_dress), a [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) cardinal wears [scarlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_\(color\)) garments—the blood-like red symbolizes a cardinal's willingness to die for his faith.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] Excluding the [rochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochet)—which is always white—the scarlet garments include the [cassock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassock), [mozzetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozzetta), and [biretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biretta), over the usual scarlet [zucchetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchetto). The biretta of a cardinal is distinctive not merely for its scarlet color, but also for the fact that it does not have a pompom or tassel on the top as do the birettas of other prelates.[^cite_note-vesture-81]

Until the 1460s, it was customary for cardinals to wear a [violet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violet_\(color\)) or [blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue) cape unless granted the privilege of wearing red when acting on papal business. His normal-wear cassock is black but has scarlet [piping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piping_\(sewing\)) and a scarlet [fascia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascia_\(vestment\)) (sash). Occasionally, a cardinal wears a scarlet *[ferraiolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferraiolo)* which is a cape worn over the shoulders, tied at the neck in a bow by narrow strips of cloth in the front, without any 'trim' or piping on it.[^cite_note-vesture-81] The [cardinal bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(bird\)) is named after the clerics due to the similarity of their scarlet colors.[^cite_note-82]



*A Cardinal in Profile*, 1880, by [Jehan Georges Vibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehan_Georges_Vibert), [Morgan Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Library_and_Museum), New York City

[Eastern Catholic](214903.md) cardinals continue to wear the normal dress appropriate to their liturgical tradition, though some may line their cassocks with scarlet and wear scarlet fascias, or in some cases, wear Eastern-style cassocks entirely of scarlet.[^cite_note-83]

In previous times, at the [consistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_consistory) at which the pope named a new cardinal, he would bestow upon him a distinctive wide-brimmed hat called a [galero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galero). This custom was discontinued in 1969[^cite_note-vesture-81] and the investiture now takes place with the scarlet [biretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biretta). In [ecclesiastical heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_heraldry), the scarlet galero is still displayed on the cardinal's [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms). Cardinals had the right to display the galero in their cathedral, and when a cardinal died, it would be suspended from the ceiling above his tomb. Some cardinals will still have a galero made, even though it is not officially part of their apparel.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

To symbolize their bond with the [papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papacy), the pope gives each newly appointed cardinal a gold ring,[^cite_note-86] which is traditionally kissed by Catholics when greeting a cardinal, as with a bishop's episcopal ring. Before the new uniformity imposed by John Paul II, each cardinal was given a ring, the central piece of which was a gem, usually a sapphire, with the pope's stemma engraved on the inside.[^cite_note-Nainfa1909-87] There is now no gemstone, and the pope chooses the image on the outside: under [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) it was a modern depiction of the crucifixion of Jesus, with [Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary) and [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Evangelist) to each side. The ring includes the pope's coat of arms on the inside.[^cite_note-88]

Cardinals have in [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law_\(Catholic_Church\)) a "privilege of forum", i.e., exemption from being judged by ecclesiastical tribunals of ordinary rank. Only the pope is competent to judge them in matters subject to ecclesiastical jurisdiction, cases that refer to matters that are spiritual or linked with the spiritual, or with regard to infringement of ecclesiastical laws and whatever contains an element of sin, where culpability must be determined and the appropriate ecclesiastical penalty imposed. The pope either decides the case himself or delegates the decision to a tribunal, usually one of the tribunals or congregations of the Roman Curia. Without such delegation, no [ecclesiastical court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_court), even the [Roman Rota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rota), is competent to judge a canon law case against a cardinal.[^cite_note-89]

Additionally, canon law gives cardinals the faculty (ability) to hear confessions validly and licitly everywhere; while bishops too have this global confession-hearing faculty, they can be restricted in their use of it in a particular area by the local bishop.[^cite_note-90]
