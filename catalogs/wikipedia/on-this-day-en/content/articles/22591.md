**Oracle Corporation** is an American [multinational](214491.md) [technology company](44211754.md) headquartered in [Austin, Texas](1998.md).[^cite_note-6] Co-founded in [Santa Clara, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara,_California), in 1977 by [Bob Miner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Miner), [Ed Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Oates), and current chairman of the board and chief technology officer [Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison),[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Oracle is among the [50 largest companies in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_corporations_by_market_capitalization)[^cite_note-10] by [market cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization), and ranked 52nd[^cite_note-11] on the [*Forbes* Global 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Global_2000) as of 2026.

The company sells [database software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database) (particularly the [Oracle Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Database)), [enterprise applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_software), and [cloud infrastructure](19541494.md) and hardware. Oracle's core application software is a suite of enterprise software products, including [enterprise resource planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_resource_planning) (ERP), [human capital management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital_management) (HCM), customer experience (CX) and [supply chain management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain_management) (SCM) software.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:4-13]

## History

See also: [List of acquisitions by Oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_acquisitions_by_Oracle)



[Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison), executive chairman and co-founder of Oracle



Oracle Corporation's former headquarters in [Redwood Shores, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwood_Shores,_California)

[Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison), [Bob Miner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Miner), and [Ed Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Oates) co-founded Oracle on June 16, 1977, in Santa Clara, California, as **Software Development Laboratories** (**SDL**).[^cite_note-founders-2][^cite_note-rdbmsearlyyearsoh20070612-14] Beginning as consultants with a background in large-scale memory after a project for [Ampex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampex),[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15] Ellison took inspiration[^cite_note-16] from the 1970 paper written by [Edgar F. Codd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_F._Codd) on [relational database management systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relational_database_management_system) (RDBMS) named "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks."[^cite_note-17] He heard about the [IBM System R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_System_R) database[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15] from an article in an IBM research journal provided by Oates. Ellison wanted to make Oracle's product compatible with System R, but failed to do so as IBM kept the error codes for its DBMS a secret. SDL changed its name to **Relational Software, Inc** (**RSI**) in 1979,[^cite_note-niemiec-18] then again to **Oracle Systems Corporation** in 1983,[^cite_note-19] to align itself more closely with its flagship product [Oracle Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Database). The name also drew from the codename of a 1977 project for the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md), Oracle's first customer;[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-rdbmsearlyyearsoh20070612-14] the company received permission to use the code name for the new product.[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15] (According to Oracle executive Mike Humphries, Miner told him that the new company had the choice of the CIA database project or another offer to develop a compiler for the [PDP-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-4), and the founders flipped a coin to decide.)[^cite_note-rdbmsfinancing20070612-22]

Miner served as a senior programmer, and Oates also worked in development. The three founders decided that Ellison was the worst programmer so he became the salesman. Understanding both customers and technology, Ellison designed [database tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_table) that he used to demonstrate the power of [SQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL) to customers.[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15] By February 1983 the *[Rosen Electronics Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosen_Electronics_Letter)* said that Oracle was "the most comprehensive offering we've seen" among databases, with good marketing and a substantial installed base encouraging developers to write software for it. The newsletter said that revenue in fiscal 1983 would be about \$8 million and would double in 1984.[^cite_note-rosen19830222-23] On March 12, 1986, the company had its [initial public offering](63879.md).[^cite_note-24] In 1989, Oracle moved its world headquarters to the [Redwood Shores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwood_Shores) neighborhood of [Redwood City, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwood_City,_California), though its campus was not completed until 1995.[^cite_note-Cerny_Page_164-25] The company hired so many from top universities that Humphries compared it to "[Cargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill) buying crops". Some new employees worked as receptionists or distributed coffee until more suitable positions became available.[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15]

Oracle in the late 1980s began selling [enterprise software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_software) running on the database, starting with [financial software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_software), then manufacturing. Many at Oracle wanted to discontinue applications; the first several versions were weak, they competed with the company's [independent software vendors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_software_vendor) and [value-added reseller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value-added_reseller) partners, and applications were never profitable for Oracle until after 2000. Selling them (and acquiring vendors such as [JD Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Edwards) and [PeopleSoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeopleSoft), the latter being the second [hostile takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_takeover) in the history of software) nonetheless allowed Oracle to compete with [SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP); by the mid-2000s it was the world's largest enterprise software vendor. The company's Ken Jacobs later said:[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15]

> It created a strategic footprint in our customers. It gave us a whole stack, a credible stack. And we could now sell at a higher point into the companies, into the board room. And, our large customers wanted to consider us a strategic partner, rather than just a vendor of technology. So, it has, actually, had a big impact on the way our sales force could sell.

In 1995, Oracle Systems Corporation changed its name to **Oracle Corporation**,[^cite_note-26] officially named Oracle, but is sometimes referred to as Oracle Corporation, the name of the holding company.[^cite_note-27]

In 2010, Oracle launched Fusion Applications to unify the features and technologies of acquisitions PeopleSoft, Siebel Systems, and JD Edwards, into a single suite.[^cite_note-28]

On July 15, 2013, Oracle transferred its stock listing from [Nasdaq](21559.md) to the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md). At the time, it was the largest-ever U.S. market transfer.[^cite_note-29]

In 2018, Oracle opened a new office in the neighboorhod of [East Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Riverside,_Austin,_Texas?action=edit\&redlink=1) in southeast Austin, Texas.[^cite_note-30] In December 2020, Oracle announced that it was moving its world headquarters from Redwood Shores to Austin, Texas.[^cite_note-Li-31]

In an effort to compete with [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md) and its products, Oracle announced in 2019 it was partnering with former rival [Microsoft](19001.md). The alliance claimed that [Oracle Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Cloud) and [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure) would be directly connected, allowing customers of each to store data on both [cloud computing](19541494.md) platforms and run software on either Oracle or Azure. Some saw this not only as an attempt to compete with Amazon but also with [Google](1092923.md) and [Salesforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesforce), which acquired [Looker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looker_\(company\)) and [Tableau Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tableau_Software), respectively.[^cite_note-32] In 2023, Oracle and Microsoft expanded their partnership to deliver Oracle database services on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure running inside Microsoft Azure.[^cite_note-33]

A year later, Oracle announced similar partnerships with Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud, making the Oracle database available in all four major hyperscaler providers.[^cite_note-34]

In December 2021, Oracle announced the acquisition of [Cerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerner), a [health information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_information_technology) company.[^cite_note-35] The acquisition of Cerner was completed on June 8, 2022, for US\$28.3 billion in cash.[^cite_note-36] Also in December 2021, Oracle announced the acquisition of Federos, an [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI) and automation tools company for network performance.[^cite_note-37]

In February 2023, the company announced it was going to invest \$1.5 billion into the [Kingdom of Saudi Arabia](349303.md), including opening a data center in the country's capital, [Riyadh](51554.md).[^cite_note-38]

In April 2024, Oracle announced it was moving its world headquarters from Austin to a new complex in [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md).[^cite_note-39] No timeframe was given.

In June 2024, Oracle announced a \$1 billion investment in Spain to enhance artificial intelligence and cloud computing. This investment will create a new cloud region in Madrid in partnership with [Telefónica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telef%C3%B3nica). The goal is to help Spanish businesses and the public sector with digital transformation and to meet [European Union regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_\(European_Union\)).[^cite_note-40] The same year, the company announced investments in Japan and Malaysia for US\$8 billion and US\$6.5 billion, respectively.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

In January 2025, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) announced [Stargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_LLC), a joint venture by Oracle, [OpenAI](48795986.md), [SoftBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoftBank_Group) and investment firm [MGX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGX_\(company\)) to invest \$500 billion over four years in artificial intelligence infrastructure in the US.[^cite_note-43] Also in 2025, the company announced additional investments in the United Kingdom, Germany and the Netherlands for US\$5 billion and US\$3 billion, respectively.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

In September 2025, Oracle announced it was promoting Clay Magouyrk, president of cloud infrastructure, and Mike Sicilia, president of industries, to become co-CEOs. They replaced Safra Catz, who held the position for 11 years, and transitioned to a new role as executive vice chair.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

In January 2026, social media platform [TikTok](56822861.md) announced it finalized a deal to change ownership of its U.S. operations, with Oracle, MGX and private equity firm [Silver Lake](10432896.md) each owning a 15% stake in the venture, while ByteDance retained a 19.9% stake.[^cite_note-48] As of March 2026, Oracle's TikTok stake was worth an estimated \~\$2B.[^cite_note-49]

In March 2026, Oracle signed a voluntary "ratepayer protection pledge" where companies pledge to build or buy energy needed to power data centers at a different rate to regular consumers, allowing them to keep utility bills down substantially.[^cite_note-50]

In April 2026, it was reported that Oracle laid off nearly 12,000 staff in India, according to the impacted employees. The company was believed to be planning another mass layoff round within a month.[^cite_note-51]

In May 2026, Oracle reported that it employed 141,000 full-time workers, down from 162,000 a year earlier, according to its annual regulatory filing. The decline represented a reduction of approximately 21,000 employees, or nearly 13% of its workforce, amid broader layoffs across the technology sector as companies increased investments in artificial intelligence.[^cite_note-52]

For the second quarter of [fiscal year](5752099.md) 2026 (ending November 2025), Oracle reported total revenue of \$14.1 billion, with cloud infrastructure (IaaS) revenue growing 68% year-over-year to \$4.1 billion. Total remaining performance obligations surged to \$130 billion, reflecting strong demand for AI infrastructure capacity.[^cite_note-53]

## Products and services

Oracle provides cloud infrastructure, AI services, database and database management software, enterprise applications, analytics, and developer tools that help businesses manage their data, operations, and IT environments.[^cite_note-\:4-13]

### Software applications

Oracle provides software applications, such as integrated enterprise software applications, Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications, human capital management (HCM), customer relationship management (CRM), and supply chain management (SCM).[^cite_note-\:4-13][^cite_note-54]

### Databases

The [Oracle Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Database) was released in 2004 as 10g. Subsequently, the product was released several times, with the newest issue released as 12c in 2018. Oracle released its Autonomous Database in 2018, Oracle Database 23ai in 2024, and issued its newest release Oracle AI Database 26ai in 2025.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

Additionally, Oracle Corporation acquired and developed the following additional database technologies:

- [Berkeley DB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_DB), which offers [embedded database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_database) processing

- [Oracle Rdb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Rdb), a relational database system running on [OpenVMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenVMS) platforms. Oracle acquired Rdb in 1994 from [Digital Equipment Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Equipment_Corporation). Oracle has since made many enhancements to this product and development continues as of 2008.

- [TimesTen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TimesTen), which features in-memory database operations

- [Oracle Essbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essbase), which continues the [Hyperion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperion_Solutions) Essbase tradition of [multi-dimensional database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_database) management

- [MySQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MySQL), a relational database management system licensed under the [GNU General Public License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_General_Public_License), initially developed by [MySQL AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MySQL_AB)

- [Oracle NoSQL Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_NoSQL_Database), a scalable, distributed key-value [NoSQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NoSQL) database

### Data centers

In March 2026, Oracle announced plans to build 72 multicloud data centers embedded with Microsoft, Amazon, and Google clouds. At the same time, Oracle had more than 200 live or in-progress cloud regions throughout the world—more than any of its competitors. The multicloud database side of the business grew 817% in Q2 of fiscal year 2026.[^cite_note-60]

Oracle’s data center expansion has drawn opposition from some residents and environmental groups over concerns about water consumption, energy demand, air quality, and the effects of large-scale construction on surrounding communities. Such concerns have emerged around projects including the planned Project Jupiter campus in southern New Mexico, where critics have questioned the development’s water and power requirements and its potential effects on local resources.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-\:5-62]

Oracle and its development partners have announced measures intended to reduce the environmental impact of new data centers. The planned Port Washington, Wisconsin, campus is expected to use closed-loop liquid cooling, match non-renewable energy consumption with renewable-energy purchases, and pursue [LEED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LEED) certification. Project Jupiter’s developers replaced plans for gas turbines and diesel generators with solid oxide fuel cells and have said the campus will use recycled, non-potable water to reduce freshwater consumption.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-\:5-62]

### Services

#### Oracle Cloud

[Oracle Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Cloud) is a [cloud computing](19541494.md) service offered by Oracle Corporation providing servers, storage, network, applications and services through a global network of Oracle Corporation managed [data centers](579730.md). The company allows these services to be [provisioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisioning_\(IT\)) on demand over the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet).[^cite_note-71]

Oracle Cloud provides [Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure_as_a_service), [Platform as a Service (PaaS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_as_a_service), [Software as a Service (SaaS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_as_a_service) and [Data as a Service (DaaS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_as_a_service). These services are used to build, deploy, integrate and extend applications in the cloud.

Oracle has continued to make updates to its generative AI service, which is built and supported on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and large language models (LLM) from Cohere as of 2023,[^cite_note-72] Meta's Llama as of 2024,[^cite_note-73] and xAI's Grok 3 as of 2025.[^cite_note-74] The company has also been working to integrate generative AI into its other services.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] In April 2025, Oracle incorporated HeatWave GenAI into its database.[^cite_note-77] In September 2025, Oracle and OpenAI signed a \$300 billion contract for 4.5 gigawatts of power capacity over five years.[^cite_note-78] In October 2025, Oracle and Microsoft announced a partnership to enhance supply chain efficiency and responsiveness for manufacturers.[^cite_note-79] It was also announced that Oracle will offer cloud services using Advanced Micro Devices' upcoming MI450 artificial intelligence chips.[^cite_note-80]

##### OPERA Cloud

OPERA Cloud is a property management software (PMS) positioned under Oracle Hospitality. It's available across 236 countries and territories. In 2026, [IHG Hotels & Resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHG_Hotels_&_Resorts) onboarded OPERA Cloud as its official PMS. In 2025, [Accor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accor) onboarded it as its official PMS, moving away from [Mews (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mews_\(software\)).[^cite_note-81] In 2023, it was a market leader among PMSs.[^cite_note-82]

## Marketing

### Sales practices

In 1990, Oracle laid off 10% (about 400 people) of its work force because of accounting errors.[^cite_note-zdnet200620-83] This crisis came about because of Oracle's "up-front" marketing strategy, in which sales people urged potential customers to buy the largest possible amount of software all at once. The sales people then booked the value of future license sales in the current quarter, thereby increasing their bonuses.[^cite_note-84] This became a problem when the future sales subsequently failed to materialize. Oracle eventually had to restate its earnings twice, and also settled (out of court) [class-action](7200.md) lawsuits arising from its having overstated its earnings. Ellison stated in 1992 that Oracle had made "an incredible business mistake".[^cite_note-zdnet200620-83]

Humphries described the cause ("building for years") as a combination of technical problems that benefited rivals, and the sales force using [side letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_letter) and other improper tactics to meet the company's longstanding goal of doubling revenue each year. Ellison hired ("bring in adults", Jerry Held said) new executives [Jeff Henley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Henley) and [Raymond J. Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_J._Lane), who helped the company recover from what Ken Jacobs later described as a "near death experience", in which Oracle required an investment from a Japanese steel company to meet payroll.[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15]

### Competition

The relational database industry was unusually concentrated. Besides being headquartered near each other in the [San Francisco Bay area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_area), database technology experts attended the same universities and served on the same standards committees (with [Jim Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Gray_\(computer_scientist\)) serving as neutral arbiter as companies tried to get competing proposals enacted).[^cite_note-rdbmsgrowingandselling20070613-85] Sales and marketing people were both fierce rivals and often moved between the companies, even as engineers socialized with each other. The smaller, later-founded database vendors viewed Oracle as their top enemy,[^cite_note-rdbmsingressybase20070613-86] with Bob MacDonald of [Informix Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informix_Software) calling Oracle "the [evil empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_empire)", while Humphries described his company as "the [Klingons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingons) of" the bay.[^cite_note-rdbmsgrowingandselling20070613-85]

Held later described his company's philosophy as "it's not good enough for Oracle to win. It was important for somebody else to lose". [Ingres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingres_\(database\)) was its first major competitor; Oracle's strategy of focusing on one opponent at a time. Even publicly complimenting other rivals "to take the pressure off", Held said, while effective, caused it to neglect [Sybase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sybase) as a threat. Conversely, when Sybase had technical problems "it was: how do you put every ounce of product marketing, sales effort and focus on that".[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15] [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)'s endorsement of SQL in [Db2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Db2) in the early 1980s benefited Oracle and forced rivals like Ingres and [Informix Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informix_Corporation) to adopt SQL to compete.[^cite_note-morgenthaler20051208-87][^cite_note-rdbmsinformix20070612-88] Oracle acknowledged IBM as the standard while emphasizing its own superiority, touting "total IBM SQL compatibility" while IBM's software ran "*only on IBM mainframes*".[^cite_note-oracle19850520-89] By the mid-1980s the company described its database as "the last DBMS", bragging that Oracle now had larger database revenue than [dBASE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DBASE) maker [Ashton-Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashton-Tate),[^cite_note-oracle19880118-90] and stating that Microsoft, Ashton-Tate, and Sybase's planned [Microsoft SQL Server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_SQL_Server) "jumped on Oracle's SQL bandwagon".[^cite_note-mace19880118-91]

MacDonald credited Oracle with "being marketing oriented before any of the competitors ... pushing the envelope on selling the future way ahead of the rest of us".[^cite_note-rdbmsinformix20070612-88] Stu Schuster of Sybase said "Larry taught us a lot about marketing". While other database companies' brochures emphasized technical features, Oracle advertisements showed an Oracle jet fighter shooting down Ashton-Tate and Borland biplanes;[^cite_note-oracle19880118-90][^cite_note-oracle19920511-92][^cite_note-rdbmsfinancing20070612-22][^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15] dBASE, IBM, and Db2 were among the many competitors Oracle marketing criticized by name.[^cite_note-oracle19850520-89] Conversely, another Oracle ad quoted Microsoft's [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates), Sun's [Scott McNealy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_McNealy), Hewlett-Packard's [John A. Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Young), and [Apple Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Computer)'s [John Sculley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sculley) as agreeing with Ellison on Oracle's database breakthroughs.[^cite_note-oracle19920615-93] Larry Rowe of Ingres said "you could never come up with a strategy to beat Oracle because whatever you said today, two days later Ellison was saying it with more marketing dollars".[^cite_note-rdbmsingressybase20070613-86] Jacobs said, by contrast:[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15]

> It really surprises me when people say Oracle's a great marketing machine because by definition, if you think a company has great marketing, they don't. If you think they have great technology then they do have a great marketing machine. Larry's belief has fundamentally always been that marketing isn't critical; he's famous for saying, "If you're not building the product and you're not selling it, tell me what it is you do." Because those are the things that he felt were important. So marketing has never been a focus.

The "benchmark wars" began in the early 1980s;[^cite_note-rdbmsingressybase20070613-86] [Roger Sippl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Sippl) of Informix said that when his company's product beat Oracle on 27 of 30 [benchmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmark_\(computing\)), advertisements titled "Oracle wins again!" appeared citing the three it won,[^cite_note-rdbmsinformix20070612-88] and Ellison allegedly called Dave DeWitt at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) after he developed a benchmark unfavorable to Oracle, threatening to get the professor fired.[^cite_note-rdbmsingressybase20070613-86] Schuster and Held recalled "a not very pleasant environment because it was so directly competitive". They and Sippl described "a vicious cycle" of companies battling each other over, for example, whose [distributed database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_database) had the best [two-phase commit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-phase_commit):[^cite_note-oracle19920914-94][^cite_note-rdbmslateryears20070612-95][^cite_note-rdbmsinformix20070612-88][^cite_note-rdbmsfinancing20070612-22]

> The ads weren't to let me show you how my product helps you solve your business problem, Mr. Customer. It's let me tell you how much better I am than the guy down the street to the point of taking out billboards.

Even IBM participated in the "billboard wars".[^cite_note-rdbmsfinancing20070612-22] By 1995 Oracle had 44% of the \$2.4 billion relational database market; Sybase had 17% and Informix had 16%. By 1996 Informix, after acquiring [Illustra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illustra), became Oracle's most important rival.[^cite_note-clark19961202-96] The intense war between Informix CEO Phil White and Ellison made front-page news in [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) for three years. Informix claimed that Oracle had hired away Informix engineers to disclose important trade secrets about an upcoming product. Informix finally dropped its lawsuit against Oracle in 1997.[^cite_note-97] Held said[^cite_note-rdbmsoracle20070612-15]

> And, in one year, we kind of turned the focus from Sybase to Informix, and basically they never knew what hit them. It was such a concerted effort. I mean, it was amazing how you could get product management, marketing, and sales focused. We had a thing, "Where in the world is Phil White," because Phil White was their best sales person. If he went into an account, one of our best guys was in right after them to make sure that they didn't win that business. It was such amazingly focused effort. And within 12 months, Informix was basically on the floor.

In November 2005, a book detailing the war between Oracle and Informix was published, titled *The Real Story of Informix Software and Phil White*. It gave a detailed chronology of the battle of Informix against Oracle, and how Informix Software's CEO Phil White landed in jail because of his obsession with overtaking Ellison.

After what Held described as "the period of time where some of the wheels came off, first at Ingres then at Sybase, and then at Informix", Oracle was the only substantial independent database vendor until Microsoft SQL Server became widespread in the late 1990s[^cite_note-rdbmslateryears20070612-95] and IBM acquired Informix Software in 2001 (to complement its Db2 database). Oracle has competed for new database licenses on UNIX, GNU, and Windows operating systems primarily against IBM's Db2 and Microsoft SQL Server.[^cite_note-98]

In 2004, Oracle's sales grew at a rate of 14.5% to \$6.2 billion, giving it 41.3% and the top share of the relational-database market (*InformationWeek* – March 2005), with market share estimated at up to 44.6% in 2005 by some sources.[^cite_note-Oracle.com-99] Oracle Corporation's main competitors in the database arena remain IBM Db2 and Microsoft SQL Server, and to a lesser extent Sybase and [Teradata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teradata),[^cite_note-rdbmslateryears20070612-95][^cite_note-Oracle.com-99] with [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software) databases such as [PostgreSQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostgreSQL) and [MySQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MySQL) also having a significant[^cite_note-100] share of the market. [EnterpriseDB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EnterpriseDB), based on PostgreSQL, has recently made inroads[^cite_note-101] by proclaiming that its product delivers Oracle-compatible SQL and procedural language features at a much lower price-point.

Oracle's main competitors in the database market include IBM and Microsoft, and in enterprise applications,[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] SAP. On March 22, 2007, [Oracle sued SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Corp._v._SAP_AG), accusing it of fraud and unfair competition.[^cite_note-104]

In the market for [business intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_intelligence) software, many other software companies, both small and large, have successfully competed in quality with Oracle and SAP products. Business intelligence vendors can be categorized into the "big four" consolidated BI firms such as Oracle, who has entered BI market through a recent trend of acquisitions (including [Hyperion Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperion_Solutions)), and the independent "pure play" vendors such as [MicroStrategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroStrategy), [Actuate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuate_Corporation), and [SAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc.).[^cite_note-105]

Oracle Financials was ranked in the Top 20 Most Popular Accounting Software Infographic by Capterra in 2014, beating out SAP and a number of its other competitors.[^cite_note-106]

#### Oracle and SAP

From 1988, Oracle Corporation and the German company [SAP AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP_AG) had a decade-long history of cooperation, beginning with the integration of SAP's [R/3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP_R/3) enterprise application suite with Oracle's relational database products. Despite the SAP partnership with Microsoft, and the increasing integration of SAP applications with Microsoft products (such as Microsoft SQL Server, a competitor to Oracle Database), Oracle and SAP continue their cooperation. According to Oracle Corporation, the majority of SAP's customers use Oracle databases.[^cite_note-107]

In 2004, Oracle began to increase its interest in the enterprise-applications market (in 1989, Oracle had already released Oracle Financials). A series of acquisitions by Oracle Corporation began, most notably with those of [PeopleSoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeopleSoft), [Siebel Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siebel_Systems), and [Hyperion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperion_Solutions).

SAP recognized that Oracle had started to become a competitor in a market where SAP had the [leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership), and saw an opportunity to lure in customers from those companies that Oracle Corporation had acquired. SAP would offer those customers special discounts on the licenses for its enterprise applications.

Oracle Corporation would resort to a similar strategy, by advising SAP customers to get "OFF SAP" (a play on the words of the [acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym) for its middleware platform "Oracle Fusion for SAP"),[^cite_note-108] and also by providing special discounts on licenses and services to SAP customers who chose Oracle Corporation products.

Oracle and SAP (the latter through its recently acquired subsidiary [TomorrowNow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TomorrowNow)) competed in the third-party enterprise software maintenance and support market. On March 22, 2007, Oracle filed a lawsuit against SAP. In [Oracle Corporation v. SAP AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Corporation_v._SAP_AG) Oracle alleged that TomorrowNow, which provides discount support for legacy Oracle product lines, used the accounts of former Oracle customers to systematically download patches and support documents from Oracle's website and to appropriate them for SAP's use.[^cite_note-109] Some analysts have suggested the suit could form part of a strategy by Oracle Corporation to decrease competition with SAP in the market for third-party enterprise software maintenance and support.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

On July 3, 2007, SAP admitted that TomorrowNow employees had made "inappropriate downloads" from the Oracle support website. However, it claims that SAP personnel and SAP customers had no access to Oracle intellectual property via TomorrowNow. SAP's CEO [Henning Kagermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henning_Kagermann) stated that "Even a single inappropriate download is unacceptable from my perspective. We regret very much that this occurred." Additionally, SAP announced that it had "instituted changes" in TomorrowNow's operational oversight.[^cite_note-112]

On November 23, 2010, a [U.S. district court](891692.md) jury in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California), found that SAP AG must pay Oracle Corp \$1.3 billion for copyright infringement, awarding damages that could be the largest-ever for copyright infringement. While admitting liability, SAP estimated the damages at no more than \$40 million, while Oracle claimed that they are at least \$1.65 billion. The awarded amount is one of the 10 or 20 largest jury verdicts in U.S. legal history. SAP said it was disappointed by the verdict and might appeal.[^cite_note-113] On September 1, 2011, a federal judge overturned the judgment and offered a reduced amount or a new trial, calling Oracle's original award "grossly" excessive.[^cite_note-114] Oracle chose a new trial.

On August 3, 2012, SAP and Oracle agreed on a judgment for \$306 million in damages, pending approval from the U.S. district court judge, "to save time and expense of \[a] new trial". After the accord has been approved, Oracle can ask a federal appeals court to reinstate the earlier jury verdict. In addition to the damages payment, SAP has already paid Oracle \$120 million for its legal fees.[^cite_note-115]

## Controversies

### Trashgate

In 2000, Oracle attracted attention from the [computer industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_industry) and the press after hiring [private investigators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_investigator) to uncover unfavorable information from organizations sympathetic to rival Microsoft. During the investigation, Oracle was accused of attempting to purchase trash from a janitor at the Association for Competitive Technology. This resulted in an [antitrust trial against Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Microsoft_Corp._\(2001\)).[^cite_note-142]

The Chairman of Oracle Corporation, Larry Ellison, defended his company's hiring of an East Coast detective agency, calling the snooping a "public service". The investigation reportedly included a \$1,200 offer to janitors at the Association for Competitive Technology to look through Microsoft's trash.[^cite_note-143]

### "Can't break it, can't break in"

In 2002, Oracle Corporation marketed many of its products using the slogan "Can't break it, can't break in", or "Unbreakable".[^cite_note-144]

However, two weeks after its introduction, [David Litchfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Litchfield), Alexander Kornbrust, Cesar Cerrudo and others demonstrated a suite of successful attacks against Oracle products.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] Oracle Corporation's chief security officer [Mary Ann Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Ann_Davidson) said that, rather than representing a literal claim of Oracle's products' impregnability, she saw the campaign in the context of fourteen independent security evaluations[^cite_note-147] that Oracle Corporation's database server had passed.

### Relationship with John Ashcroft

In 2004, then-[United States Attorney General](44002.md) [John Ashcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ashcroft) sued Oracle Corporation to prevent it from acquiring a multibillion-dollar intelligence contract. After Ashcroft's resignation from government, he founded a lobbying firm, [The Ashcroft Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ashcroft_Group), which Oracle hired in 2005. With the group's help, Oracle went on to acquire the contract.

### Cover Oregon Healthcare Exchange

Oracle Corporation was awarded a contract by the State of Oregon's [Oregon Health Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Health_Authority) (OHA) to develop [Cover Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_Oregon), the state's healthcare exchange website, as part of the U.S. [Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act). When the site tried to go live on October 1, 2013, it failed, and registrations had to be taken using paper applications until the site could be fixed.[^cite_note-148]

In August 2014, Oracle Corporation sued Cover Oregon for breach of contract,[^cite_note-149] and then later that month the state of Oregon sued Oracle Corporation, in a civil complaint for breach of contract, fraud, filing false claims and "[racketeering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racketeering)".[^cite_note-150] In September 2016, the two sides reached a settlement valued at over \$100 million to the state, and a six-year agreement for Oracle to continue modernizing state software and IT.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]

### Class action tracking lawsuit

In August 2022, a [class action lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class-action_lawsuit) was filed against Oracle by the law firm [Lieff Cabraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieff_Cabraser). The lawsuit alleged that Oracle engaged in "deliberate and purposeful surveillance of the general population via their digital and online existence", specifically focusing on Oracle operating a surveillance machine that tracked and recorded personal data in real-time. The litigants alleged that through such surveillance, the company violated the [Federal Electronic Communications Privacy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Communications_Privacy_Act), [California's state constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_California), the California Invasion of Privacy Act, competition law, and [California Common Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_California).[^cite_note-\:0-155][^cite_note-\:1-156]

The lawsuit was settled in July 2024 for \$115 million.[^cite_note-157]

### Violations of the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act

Between 2011 and 2024, Oracle was investigated for engaging in bribery practices that violated the [Foreign Corrupt Practices Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Corrupt_Practices_Act) across its subsidiaries in South Africa, India, Turkey, and the United Arab Emirates. In all instances, the company settled with the United States [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission).[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

### Reaction to the Gaza war

Following the beginning of the [Gaza war](74998519.md) in 2023, Oracle's top executives, including Safra Catz and Larry Ellison, publicly aligned the company with Israel's military operations. They issued statements of solidarity, paid double salaries to Israeli employees, and donated to organizations connected to Israel's wartime response.[^cite_note-Ynet2025-163] In 2024, Catz said to Israeli business news outlet CTech, "For employees, it's clear: if you're not for America or Israel, don't work here".[^cite_note-Shulman_24-164] In 2025, *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* spoke with Oracle employees who supported Palestine, but were afraid to speak out in their workplace. Sixty-eight Oracle employees signed an open letter criticizing the company's ties to Israel.[^cite_note-Gee_25-165]

### Fraud Accusations by the US Department of Justice

On July 29, 2010, the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) (DoJ) filed suit against Oracle Corporation alleging fraud. The lawsuit argues that the government received deals inferior to those Oracle gave to its commercial clients. The DoJ added its heft to an already existing whistleblower lawsuit filed by Paul Frascella, who was once senior director of contract services at Oracle.[^cite_note-166] It was settled in 2011.[^cite_note-167]

### Lawsuit against Google

Main article: [Oracle v. Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_v._Google)

Oracle, the plaintiff, acquired ownership of the Java computer programming language when it acquired [Sun Microsystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Microsystems) in January 2010.[^cite_note-ReutersJune2011-168] The Java software includes sets of pre-developed software code to allow programs and apps to accomplish common tasks in a consistent manner. The pre-developed code is organized into separate "[packages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Package_manager)" which each contain a set of "[classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_\(programming\))". Each class contains numerous [methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_\(computer_programming\)), which instruct a program or app to do a certain task. Software developers "became accustomed to using Java's designations at the package, class, and method level".[^cite_note-Quimbee-169]

Oracle and [Google](1092923.md) (the defendant) tried to negotiate an agreement for Oracle to license Java to Google, which would have allowed Google to use Java in developing programs for mobile devices using the [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system). However, the two companies never reached an agreement. After negotiations failed, Google created its own programming platform, which was based on Java, and contained 37 copied Java packages as well as new packages developed by Google.[^cite_note-Quimbee-169]

### HP and Oracle lawsuit

On June 15, 2011, HP filed a lawsuit in California Superior Court in Santa Clara, claiming that Oracle had breached an agreement to support the Itanium microprocessor used in HP's high-end enterprise servers.[^cite_note-170] Oracle called the lawsuit "an abuse of the judicial process"[^cite_note-171] and said that had it known [SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP_AG)'s [Léo Apotheker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9o_Apotheker) was about to be hired as HP's new CEO, any support for HP's Itanium servers would not have been implied.[^cite_note-172]

On August 1, 2012, a California judge said in a tentative ruling that Oracle must continue porting its software at no cost until HP discontinues its sales of Itanium-based servers.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] HP was awarded \$3 billion in damages against Oracle in 2016.[^cite_note-175] HP argued Oracle's canceling support damaged HP's Itanium server brand. Oracle had announced that it would appeal both the decision and damages, but the decision stayed.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177]

### GSA business bidding ban

On April 20, 2012, the US General Services Administration banned Oracle from the most popular portal for bidding on GSA contracts for undisclosed reasons. Oracle has previously used this portal for around four hundred million dollars a year in revenue.[^cite_note-178] In 2011, Oracle settled with the General Services Administration for \$199.5 million in a lawsuit filed under the False Claims Act.[^cite_note-179]

## Events and acquisitions

### Acquisition of Sun Microsystems

Main article: [Acquisition of Sun Microsystems by Oracle Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_Sun_Microsystems_by_Oracle_Corporation)

In January 2010, Oracle completed its acquisition of [Sun Microsystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Microsystems), valued at more than \$7 billion, a move that transformed Oracle from solely a software company to a manufacturer of both software and hardware. The acquisition was delayed for several months by the [European Commission](9974.md) because of concerns about [MySQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MySQL), but was unconditionally approved in the end.[^cite_note-180] In September 2011, U.S. State Department Embassy [cables were leaked to WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak). One cable revealed that the U.S. pressured the [E.U.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.U.) to allow Oracle to acquire Sun.[^cite_note-181]

The Sun acquisition was closely watched by [free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software) users and some companies, due to the fear that Oracle might end Sun's traditional support of free projects.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] Since the acquisition, Oracle has discontinued [OpenSolaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSolaris) and [StarOffice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarOffice), and sued [Google](1092923.md) over the [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_\(programming_language\)) patents Oracle acquired from Sun.[^cite_note-arsjava2-186][^cite_note-187]

### Cerner acquisition

Main article: [Oracle Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Health)

On December 20, 2021, Oracle announced that it agreed to acquire [Cerner Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerner) (now *Oracle Health* and *Oracle Life Sciences*) for approximately US\$28.3 billion, creating a dedicated Industry Business Unit within the company. Cerner is the largest international supplier of health information technology, such as [electronic health records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_health_record) (EHR), [revenue cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_cycle_management) solutions, and biomedical device integration platforms, and has its headquarters in Kansas City, Missouri, US.[^cite_note-\:2-188] The deal closed in early July 2022 after receiving final approval from European regulators, making it Oracle's largest acquisition and one of the largest in corporate history.[^cite_note-\:3-189]

Oracle's purchase of Cerner was part of an effort to introduce Oracle products into the healthcare market, particularly in the United States although Oracle plans to expand Cerner's global operations.[^cite_note-\:3-189]

In September 2024, Oracle announced new investments and the release of Oracle Clinical Digital Assistant, a generative AI system that automatically creates consultation documentation and proposes orders.[^cite_note-190]

### Oracle Health data breach

In March 2025, a threat actor claimed to have stolen 6 million data records from Oracle Health (formerly [Cerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerner)).[^cite_note-202] Oracle claimed that its Oracle Cloud Infrastructure was not breached and that no customer data from it was exposed.[^cite_note-203] Some legacy Cerner servers experienced unauthorized access, but Oracle has not confirmed the scale of impact.[^cite_note-204] In April 2025, the [Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency](59407369.md) (CISA) issued a warning to Oracle customers to secure their environments.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206]

### U.S. TikTok's operations

On September 13, 2020, [Bloomberg News](3057255.md) reported that Oracle won a bidding war with other U.S.-based companies to take over social media company TikTok's operations in the United States following the company's pressure to forcibly be shut down by the [Trump administration](52231341.md). Oracle was described as a "trusted tech partner" by [TikTok](56822861.md).[^cite_note-207] On September 19, 2020, the Trump administration approved of the sale of TikTok's US operations to Oracle "\[delaying] — by one week — restrictions that were originally to take effect" on September 20 as indicated by the [United States Department of Commerce](70243.md).[^cite_note-208]

As September 25, 2025, TikTok was bought by [Silver Lake](10432896.md) and [Abu Dhabi's MGX](78957486.md). Oracle will own 45% of the app in the US, and ByteDance 35% after delays with the ban for the 4th time.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

Oracle will store data of US TikTok users on its [cloud computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computer),[^cite_note-reuters-211] and it will be involved in the new TikTok spinoff's cybersecurity.[^cite_note-pbs-134]

### Anduril partnership

Oracle partnered with the arms company [Anduril Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anduril_Industries) to provide their [Lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anduril_Lattice?action=edit\&redlink=1) military software platform to the governments of United States, United Kingdom and Australia.[^cite_note-212]

## Offices

Oracle Corporation's world headquarters are located in Austin, Texas.[^cite_note-213] In April 2024, Oracle announced plans to build its largest office hub, with 8,500 jobs, in [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md).[^cite_note-214]

Oracle has a large office complex located on the [San Francisco Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Peninsula) in the [Redwood Shores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwood_Shores) area of [Redwood City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwood_City,_California). This complex was home to Oracle world headquarters from 1989 to 2020. It is located on the former site of [Marine World/Africa USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_World/Africa_USA), which moved from Redwood Shores to [Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallejo,_California) in 1986. Oracle Corporation originally leased two buildings on the Oracle Parkway site, moving its finance and administration departments from the corporation's former headquarters on Davis Drive, [Belmont, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_California). Eventually, Oracle purchased the complex and constructed four additional buildings.[^cite_note-215]

The distinctive Oracle Parkway buildings, nicknamed the Emerald City,[^cite_note-216] served as sets for the futuristic headquarters of the fictional company "NorthAm Robotics" in the [Robin Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Williams) film *[Bicentennial Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicentennial_Man_\(film\))* (1999).[^cite_note-217] The campus also represented the headquarters of [Cyberdyne Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberdyne_Systems) in the movie *[Terminator Genisys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_Genisys)* (2015).[^cite_note-218]
