# Galilee

For other uses, see [Galilee (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee_\(disambiguation\)).



Map showing the Lower and Upper Galilee. The narrow "Galilee Panhandle" to the east may be seen geologically and geographically as a separate area (as part of the [Jordan Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Rift_Valley)), or as part of historical Galilee. The same applies to the western shore of the [Sea of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee) and the [Jordan Valley](12105764.md) down to [Beit She'an](323286.md), as well as the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley) in the south and the [coastal strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_coastal_plain#Northern_Coastal_Plain) bordering the Galilee to the west.

**Galilee** ([/ˈɡælɪliː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-1] [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): הַגָּלִיל, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *hag-Gālīl*; [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): *Galilaia*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Γαλιλαία'; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Galilaea*;[^cite_note-2] [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجليل, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Jalīl*) is a region located in [northern Israel](324281.md) and [southern Lebanon](4205968.md) consisting of two parts: the [Upper Galilee](8232648.md) (הגליל העליון, *hag-Galil ha-ʿElyon*; الجليل الأعلى, *al-Jalīl al-Aʿlā*) and the [Lower Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Galilee) (הגליל התחתון, *hag-Galil hat-Taḥton*; الجليل الأسفل, *al-Jalīl al-Asfal*). Other sub-regions are the **Western Galilee** and the [Galilee Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee_Panhandle).

Galilee encompasses the area north of the [Mount Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel)–[Mount Gilboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gilboa) ridge and south of the east–west section of the [Litani River](1920925.md). It extends from the [Israeli coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_coastal_plain) and the shores of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) with [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel) in the west, to the [Jordan Valley](12105764.md) to the east; and from the Litani in the north plus a piece bordering on the [Golan Heights](7515865.md) to [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_\(ancient_city\)) at the base of [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon) in the northeast, to Mount Carmel and Mount Gilboa in the south.

In some definitions, it may include the plains of the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley) north of [Jenin](50997.md) and the [Beit She'an Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_She'an_Valley), the [Sea of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee), and the [Hula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley).

## Etymology

The Galilee is first mentioned in [Ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) [inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precinct_of_Amun-Re#Seventh_Pylon) from the [15th century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Late_Bronze_Age), chronicling the [military campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Megiddo_\(15th_century_BC\)#Annals_of_Thutmose_III) led by [Tuthmosis III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuthmosis_III) against [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan) kings in the region.[^cite_note-Byrce-3] The [Egyptian hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs) can either be read as GLL, or the same as in [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language): GRR, *garāru*, meaning 'roll'.[^cite_note-4]

The region's name in [Biblical Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Hebrew_language): גָּלִיל, romanized: *gālíl*, means 'district' or 'circle'.[^cite_note-Placenames-5] The form used in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible)'s [Isaiah 8\:23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_8#Verse_23) ([Isaiah 9\:1](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Isaiah%209\:1\&version=nrsv) in the Christian [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament)) is in the [construct state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construct_state), leading to [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): גְּלִיל הַגּוֹיִם, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *gəlil haggóyim* "Galilee of the nations", which refers to [gentiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentile) who settled there at the time the book was written, either by their own volition or as a result of the [resettlement policy of the Neo-Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resettlement_policy_of_the_Neo-Assyrian_Empire).[^cite_note-Placenames-5] The region is also referred to as the "land of Naphtali" or "country of Naphtali", in [2 Kings 15\:29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Kings_15#Pekah,_king_of_Israel_\(15\:27%E2%80%9331\)), after the [eponymous tribe of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Naphtali).[^cite_note-6]

The region's Arabic name is الجليل, *[Al-Jalil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Jalil)*, meaning "illustrious" or "grand", and the 1881 [Palestine Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund) survey says it is derived from the Hebrew.[^cite_note-Palmer-7] [Ali Fahmi Khashim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fahmi_Khushaim)'s 2005 *Akkadian-Arabic: A Comparative Dictionary and Introduction* gives the root of *al-Jalil* as [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جلّ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *jalla* meaning "great" and cites it as a cognate of the [Sumerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumerian_language) word [GAL (cuneiform)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAL_\(cuneiform\)), also meaning "great".[^cite_note-Khasheem-8]

## Borders and geography



Galilee district in [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) (1920-1948).



An [orchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard) in [Upper Galilee](8232648.md)

The extent of Galilee can be defined in two ways: in more stricter, geological terms, which would exclude the low-lying areas on its margins, or in more fluid historical (political) terms, which are based on the territory's affiliation with one state or administrative unit or another. Either way, it is usually split into [Upper Galilee](8232648.md) and [Lower Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Galilee). The political-administrative borders of 1st-century Galilee were described by [Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus) in his *[Jewish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_War)*:[^cite_note-Zange-9]

> Now [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\)) encompass about the Galilees, which are two, and called the Upper Galilee and the Lower. They are bounded toward the sun-setting, with the borders of the territory belonging to [Ptolemais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemais_in_Phoenicia), and by [Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel); which mountain had formerly belonged to the Galileans, but now belonged to the [Tyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_\(Lebanon\)); to which mountain adjoins [Gaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaba',_Haifa), which is called the City of Horsemen, because those horsemen that were dismissed by [Herod the king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great) dwelt therein; they are bounded on the south with [Samaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria) and [Scythopolis](323286.md), as far as the [river Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Jordan); on the east with [Hippeae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippos_\(Golan_Heights\)) and [Gadaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadara), and also with [Gaulonitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulonitis), and the borders of the kingdom of Agrippa; its northern parts are bounded by Tyre, and the country of the Tyrians. As for that Galilee which is called the Lower, it extends in length from [Tiberias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias) to [Zabulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabul), and of the maritime places Ptolemais is its neighbor; its breadth is from the village called [Xaloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xaloth), which lies in the great plain, as far as [Bersabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bersabe), from which beginning also is taken the breadth of the Upper Galilee, as far as the [village Baca](2162385.md), which divides the land of the Tyrians from it; its length is also from [Meloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marus,_Safad) to [Thella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thella), a village near to Jordan.[^cite_note-10]



[Keshet Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshet_Cave) (Rainbow Cave or Cave of the Arch), a [natural arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_arch) on the ridge north of [Nahal Betzet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Betzet), Galilee

Most of Galilee consists of rocky terrain, at heights of 500–700 m (1,600–2,300 ft). Several high mountains are in the region, including [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor) and [Mount Meron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Meron), which have relatively low temperatures and high rainfall. As a result of this climate, [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) thrive in the region. At the same time, many birds annually migrate from colder climates to Africa and back through the [Hula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley)–Jordan corridor. The streams and waterfalls, the latter mainly in Upper Galilee, along with vast fields of greenery and colourful [wildflowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildflowers), as well as numerous towns of [biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical) importance, make the region a popular [tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Israel).

Due to its high rainfall 900–1,200 millimetres (35–47 in), mild temperatures and high mountains (Mount Meron's elevation is 1,000–1,208 m (3,281–3,963 ft)), the Upper Galilee region contains some distinctive flora and fauna: prickly juniper (*[Juniperus oxycedrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniperus_oxycedrus)*), Lebanese cedar (*[Cedrus libani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus_libani)*), which grows in a small grove on Mount Meron, [cyclamens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclamen), [paeonias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peony), and *[Rhododendron ponticum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhododendron_ponticum)* which sometimes appears on Meron.

**Western Galilee** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): גליל מערבי, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *Galil Ma'aravi*) is a modern term referring to the western part of the Upper Galilee and its shore, and usually also the northwestern part of the Lower Galilee, mostly overlapping with Acre sub-district.

**Galilee Panhandle**, in Hebrew **Finger of the Galilee**, is a common term referring to the "panhandle" in the east that extends to the north, where Lebanon is to the west, and includes [Hula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley) and [Ramot Naftali mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naftali_Mountains) of the Upper Galilee.

## History

Further information: [History of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Palestine)

### Iron Age



A map of Galilee, c. 50 CE

[1 Kings 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Kings_9) states that [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) rewarded his Phoenician ally, [King Hiram I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hiram_I) of [Sidon](28366.md), with twenty cities in the land of Galilee, which would then have been either settled by foreigners during and after the reign of Hiram or by those who had been forcibly deported there by later conquerors such as the [Neo-Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire). Hiram, to reciprocate previous gifts given to [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David), accepted the upland plain among the Naftali Mountains and renamed it "the land of [Cabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabul)" for a time.[^cite_note-Phoenicia-11]

In the Iron Age II, Galilee was part of the [Kingdom of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Israel_\(Samaria\)), which [fell to the Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_captivity). Archaeological survey conducted by Zvi Gal in Lower Galilee indicates that the area became deserted following the Assyrian conquest in the 8th century and remained so for several centuries; the local Israelite population [was carried off to Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_captivity) after 732 BCE.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:0-13] Yardenna Alexandre discovered minor short-lived Israelite settlements in the Naḥal Ẓippori basin, which were built by survivors of the Assyrian conquest. Elsewhere, Galilee was depopulated.[^cite_note-Atiqot98-14] But there is evidence of Assyrian presence, based on artefacts in [Cana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cana),[^cite_note-Skinner_113–125-15] and [Konrad Schmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Schmid_\(theologian\)) and Jens Schroter believe it was likely that Assyrians settled in the region.[^cite_note-16]

### Hellenistic period

Up until the end of the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) and before the [Hasmonean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_dynasty) conquest, the Galilee was sparsely populated, with the majority of its inhabitants concentrated in large fortified centers on the edges of the western and central valleys. Based on archaeological evidence from [Tel Anafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Anafa), [Kedesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedesh), and [ash-Shuhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash-Shuhada), the Upper Galilee was then home to a [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_religion) population with close ties to the [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) coast.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]

### Hasmonean period

Main article: [Hasmonean dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_dynasty)

During the expansion of the [Hasmonean kingdom of Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_Judea), much of the Galilee region was conquered and annexed by the first [Hasmonean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_dynasty) king [Aristobulus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristobulus_I) (104–103 BCE). Following the Hasmonean conquest, there was a significant Jewish influx into the area. Sites including [Yodfat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yodfat), [Meiron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiron), [Sepphoris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepphoris), [Shikhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikhin), [Qana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Kanna), [Bersabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bersabe), [Zalmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selamin), Mimlah, [Migdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdala), [Arbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbel), Kefar Hittaya, and Beth Ma'on have archaeological-chronological evidence for this settlement wave.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]



The hill where ancient [Yodfat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yodfat) stood

[Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus), who based his account on [Timagenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timagenes) of Alexandria, claimed that Aristobulus I had forcibly converted the [Itureans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iturea) to Judaism while annexing a portion of their territory. Schürer believed this information to be accurate and came to the conclusion that the "Jewish" Galilee of Jesus' day was actually inhabited by the offspring of those same Iturean converts. Other scholars have suggested that the Itureans underwent a voluntary conversion to Judaism in the Upper Galilee, or at the very least in the Eastern Upper Galilee. However, archaeological information does not support either proposal, as Iturean material culture has been identified clearly in the northern Golan Heights and Mount Hermon, and not in the Galilee, and it is clear that this area remained outside Hasmonean borders.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]

### Roman period

#### Early Roman period

In the early Roman period, Galilee was predominantly Jewish. Archaeological evidence from multiple sites reveals Jewish customs, including the use of [limestone vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_vessels_in_ancient_Judaea), [ritual baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikveh) for [purity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumah_and_taharah), and secondary burial practices.[^cite_note-\:7-18] A significant wave of Jewish settlement arrived in the region following the [Roman conquest of 63 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_\(63_BC\)).[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17] Large towns such as [Kefar Hananya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_'Inan), [Parod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farradiyya), [Ravid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravid), Mashkaneh, Sabban, and [Tiberias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias) were established by the end of the first century BCE or the start of the first century CE.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17] By the end of the first century CE, the Galilee was dotted with small towns and villages. While Josephus writes there were 204 small towns, modern scholars consider this an exaggeration.

Galilee's economy under Roman rule thrived on a combination of agriculture, fishing, and specialized crafts. Excavations in villages like Nazareth have revealed extensive agricultural infrastructure, including numerous olive presses and granaries.[^cite_note-\:7-18] [Olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) was extensively grown in parts of Upper Galilee.[^cite_note-\:23-19] Many towns and villages, particularly those around the Sea of Galilee benefited from both fertile land and a thriving fishing industry.[^cite_note-\:7-18] In [Tarichaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarichaea) ([Magdala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdala)), salted, dried, and pickled fish were significant export goods.[^cite_note-\:7-18][^cite_note-\:23-19] Galilee also had specialized production centers.[^cite_note-\:7-18] [Shihin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikhin), near Sepphoris, produced most of the region's storage jars. [Kefar Hananya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Hananya) in Upper Galilee manufactured various tableware forms, supplying markets across Galilee, the Golan Heights, the Decapolis, coastal areas, and the Beth Shean Valley.[^cite_note-\:7-18]

Josephus describes the Jewish population of Galilee as being nationalist and hostile to Jewish city-dwellers, making them the first target for the Romans during the [Jewish-Roman wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_wars). [Bargil Pixner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargil_Pixner) believes they descended from a [Davidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidic_line) Jewish clan from [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire).[^cite_note-Skinner_113–125-15] But according to archaeological and literary evidence, upper and lower Galilee were 'very much in constant touch with the gentile, Greek-speaking cities that surrounded them.' Many Galileans were bilingual and made daily contacts with Jerusalem and gentiles around the Roman territory.[^cite_note-20]

Markus Cromhout states that while Galileans, Judeans and diasporic Judeans were all Jewish, the Galileans had their unique social, political and economic matrix. In terms of ethnicity, Galileans were ethnic Judeans, which generally saw themselves also as Israelites, but could be also identified with localized characteristics, such as Sepphorean.[^cite_note-\:12-21] Others argue that Galileans and Judeans were distinct people groups. Outsiders generally conflated them due to Hellenistic-Roman culture, which grouped all diverse groups in Palestine and their related diasporas as "Judean".[^cite_note-22]



As a Roman [client ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state), [Herod Antipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_Antipas), the [tetrarch of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodian_Tetrarchy) from 4 BCE–39 CE, was permitted to mint his own coinage (*shown above*).

[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23]

In 4 BCE, a rebel named Judah plundered Galilee's largest city, [Sepphoris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepphoris). According to Josephus, the Syrian governor [Publius Quinctilius Varus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publius_Quinctilius_Varus) responded by sacking Sepphoris and selling the population into slavery, but the region's archaeology lacks evidence of such destruction.[^cite_note-Meyers1999-24][^cite_note-Casey2010-25] After the death of [Herod the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great) that same year, his son [Herod Antipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_Antipas) was appointed as [tetrarch of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodian_Tetrarchy) by the Roman emperor [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus). Galilee remained a Roman [client state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state) and Antipas paid tribute to the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in exchange for Roman protection.[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23]

The Romans did not station troops in Galilee, but threatened to retaliate against anyone who attacked it. As long as he continued to pay tribute, Antipas was permitted to govern however he wished[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23] and was permitted to mint his own coinage. Antipas was relatively observant of Jewish laws and customs. Although his palace was decorated with animal carvings, which many Jews regarded as a transgression against the law prohibiting idols, his coins bore only agricultural designs, which his subjects deemed acceptable.

In general, Antipas was a capable ruler. Josephus does not record any instance of his use of force to put down an uprising and he had a long, prosperous reign. However, many Jews probably resented him as not sufficiently devout.[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23] Antipas rebuilt the city of Sepphoris,[^cite_note-Casey2010-25] and in either 18 CE or 19 CE, he founded the new city of [Tiberias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias). These two cities became Galilee's largest cultural centers.[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23] They were the main centers of Greco-Roman influence, but were still predominantly Jewish. A massive gap existed between the rich and poor,[^cite_note-Casey2010-25] but lack of uprisings suggest that taxes were not exorbitantly high and that most Galileans did not feel their livelihoods were being threatened.[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23]

Late in his reign, Antipas married his half-niece [Herodias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodias), who was already married to one of her other uncles. His wife, whom he divorced, fled to her father [Aretas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aretas_IV_Philopatris), an Arab king, who invaded Galilee and defeated Antipas's troops before withdrawing. Both Josephus and the [Gospel of Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark)[^cite_note-26] record that the itinerant preacher [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) criticized Antipas over his marriage, and Antipas consequently had him [imprisoned and then beheaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beheading_of_John_the_Baptist).[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23] In around 39 CE, at the urging of [Herodias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodias), Antipas went to Rome to request that he be elevated from the status of tetrarch to the status of king. The Romans found him guilty of storing arms, so he was removed from power and exiled, ending his forty-three-year reign. During the [Great Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War) (66–73 CE), a Jewish mob destroyed Herod Antipas's palace.[^cite_note-Sanders1993-23]

Overall, Galilee under Antipas's rule was marked by significant demographic instability. Diseases like [malaria](20423.md) were rampant, internal migration between urban and rural areas were frequent and women generally gave birth at young ages while married to older men. [Birth control](18978770.md), including [infanticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanticide), was not practiced. Many young men, especially marginal villagers, migrated to urban areas to find wives or alternatively, employment. Finding wives was presumed to be competitive since widows often refused to marry past the age of 30 compared to widowers. According to Jonathan L. Reed, this can provide insight on the tropes of New Testament literature, such as miraculous healings and the itinerant lifestyle of Jesus and his disciples.[^cite_note-27]

In 66 CE, during the [Great Jewish Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War), Josephus was appointed by the [Jerusalem provisional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judean_provisional_government) to command Galilee. The region experienced internal conflicts among cities such as Sepphoris and Tiberias, with factions opposing Josephus's authority and warring for control. Sepphoris and other strong cities attempted to remain neutral by maintaining alliances with Rome. Despite opposition, Josephus managed to secure internal peace and fortified nineteen cities in preparation for the Roman invasion; nearly half of them were uncovered by archaeologists. In 67 CE, the Roman army, led by general [Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian), arrived in Acre. Josephus's account, *[The Jewish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_War)*, details the Roman [campaign in Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee_campaign_\(67\)), starting with the siege and capture of Gabara, followed by Jotapata (where Josephus was captured), and continuing with Tiberias, Taricheae, Gamala, Tabor, and ending in Gischala. While not all of Galilee was devastated, the conquered cities were razed, and many inhabitants were sold into slavery.[^cite_note-\:0-13]

#### Late Roman period

Judaism reached its political and cultural zenith in the Galilee during the late second and early third century CE. According to rabbinic sources, [Judah ha-Nasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_ha-Nasi)'s political leadership was at its strongest in relation to the Jewish community in [Syria Palaestina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Palaestina), the Diaspora, and the Roman Authorities during this time. Judah's redaction of the [Mishnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnah) at this time period represented the peak of intense cultural activity. Archaeological surveys in the Galilee have revealed that the region experienced its height of thriving settlement during this time.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]

According to medieval Hebrew legend, [Shimon bar Yochai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_bar_Yochai), one of the most famed of all the [tannaim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannaim), wrote the *[Zohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohar)* while living in Galilee.[^cite_note-28]

### Byzantine period

After the completion of the Mishnah, which marked the conclusion of the [tannaitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannaim) era, came the period of the *[amoraim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoraim)*. The [Jerusalem Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Talmud), the principal work of the amoraim in Palestine, is primarily discussions and interpretations of the Mishnah, and according to academic research, most of it was edited in [Tiberias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias). The vast majority of the *amoraim* named there, as well as the majority of the settlements or place names referenced, were Galileans.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17] By the middle of the fourth century, the Jerusalem Talmud's compilation and editing processes abruptly came to a halt, as Talmudic scholar Yaacov Sussmann put it: "The development of the Jerusalem Talmud seems to have abruptly ceased, as if severed by a sharp and sudden blade".[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]

Demographically, during the fourth century the entire region witnessed a significant population decrease, resulting in the abandonment of several notable settlements.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17] In approximately 320 CE, Christian bishop [Epiphanius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis) reported that all the major cities and villages in Galilee were entirely Jewish.[^cite_note-30] During the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Palestine) period, however, Galilee's Jewish population experienced a decline, while Christian settlement grew. Archaeological data indicates that in the third and fourth centuries, several Jewish sites were abandoned, and some Christian villages were established on or near these deserted locations. Certain settlements, such as [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rameh), [Magdala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdala), [Kafr Kanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Kanna), [Daburiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daburiyya), and [Iksal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iksal), which were materially Jewish during the Roman period, were now predominantly inhabited by Christians or had a significant Christian population. Safrai and Liebner argue that the decline of the Jewish population and the expansion of the Christian population in the region were separate events that happened at different times. Throughout this period, religious segregation between Christian and Jewish villages endured, with few exceptions like [Capernaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capernaum) and perhaps [Nazareth](21702.md), due to their sanctity in Christian tradition.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]

Leibner has proposed tying the end of the Palestinian Amoraic period, the impact of historical occurrences like the [Christianization of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography_of_Christianization_of_the_Roman_Empire) and of Palestine, the apparent cessation of activities of at least some of the [*batei midrash*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_midrash) and the transformation of the Galilee from a densely populated Jewish area to a collection of communities surrounded by non-Jewish areas to this demographic crisis. He assumed that Christian population in Galilee was not composed of Jews who converted to Christianity. This is supported by the fact that trustworthy historical records, which mention Jewish conversion to Christianity in [Byzantine Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Palestine), refer to individual cases rather than entire villages, unlike the records from the western part of the empire.[^cite_note-Leibner2009-17]

Eastern Galilee retained a Jewish majority until at least the seventh century.[^cite_note-31] Over time, this area experienced a decline in population due to raids by nomadic groups and insufficient protection from the central government.[^cite_note-32]

### Early Islamic, Crusader and Middle Islamic periods

After the [Muslim conquest of the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Levant) in the 630s, the Galilee formed part of [Jund al-Urdunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jund_al-Urdunn) (the military district of Jordan), itself part of [Bilad al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilad_al-Sham) (Islamic Syria). Its major towns were Tiberias the capital of the district, [Qadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadas), [Beisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beisan), [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel), [Saffuriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffuriya), and [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul,_Israel).[^cite_note-LeStrange-33] During the early Islamic period, Galilee underwent a process of [Arabization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabization) and [Islamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam), similar to other areas in the region. Under [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) rule, Islamic rule was gradually consolidated in newly conquered territories, and some Muslims settled in the villages, establishing residency there.[^cite_note-\:1-34] Later, under [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) rule, geographer [al-Ya'qubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ya'qubi) (d. 891), who referred to the region as *'Jabal al-Jalil'*, noted that its inhabitants were Arabs from the [Amila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amila) tribe.[^cite_note-35]

The Islamization process in which began with the settlement of nomadic tribes. Michael Ehrlich suggests that during the Early Islamic period, the majority of people in the Western Galilee and Lower Galilee likely converted to Islam, while in the Eastern Galilee, the Islamization process continued for a more extended period, lasting until the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) period.[^cite_note-\:5-36] According to Moshe Gil, Jews in rural Galilean areas frequently succeeded in upholding community life during and for decades after the Umayyad period. He comes to the conclusion that several Galilean Jewish communities "retained their ancient character".[^cite_note-\:1-34]

The [Shia](26961.md) [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid) conquered the region in the 10th century; a breakaway sect, venerating the Fatimid caliph [al-Hakim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakim_\(title\)), formed the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) religion, centered in [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) and partially in the Galilee. During the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusade), Galilee was organized into the [Principality of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Galilee), one of the most important Crusader seigneuries. According to Moshe Gil, during the periods of [Fatimid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate) and [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) rule, the rural Jewish population of Galilee experienced a gradual decline and flight. He supports his argument by referring to 11th-century [Cairo Geniza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Geniza) documents related to transactions in [Ramla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramla) and other areas in central Palestine, where Jews claimed to have ancestral ties to places like [Gush Halav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jish), [Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton,_Israel), or ['Amuqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammuqa), suggesting that Jewish flight from Galilee occurred during that time.[^cite_note-\:1-34]

Sunni Muslims began to immigrate to [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) and its surroundings starting in the [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) period, and in particular during the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) period. These immigrants included [Sufi preachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) who were crucial in converting the locals to Islam in Safed's rural area. Jewish immigrants did, however, come to the area in waves, during the period of the destruction of Tyre and Acre in 1291 and particularly after the [Jewish expulsion from Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Jews_from_Spain) in 1492. These immigrants, who included scholars and other urban elites, turned the Jewish community from a rural community into an urban hub which exerted its influence well beyond the regional boundaries of Upper Galilee.[^cite_note-\:5-36]

### Ottoman period

See also: [Safed Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed_Sanjak) and [Zayadina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayadina)



Safed

The Jewish population of Galilee increased significantly following [their expulsion from Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra_Decree) and welcome from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). The community for a time made [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) an international center of cloth weaving and manufacturing, as well as a key site for Jewish learning.[^cite_note-jafi-37] Today it remains one of Judaism's [four holy cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Holy_Cities) and a center for [kabbalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah).[^cite_note-38]

The Galilee, as part of [Safed Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed_Sanjak), came under the governance of the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) emir and Ottoman governor [Fakhr al-Din Ma'n](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhr_al-Din_Ma'n) and his family starting in 1602. He forged close ties with the Sunni religious establishment in Safed, including its [mufti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mufti) [Ahmad al-Khalidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khalidi_al-Safadi), who acclaimed the improvements in the Galilee's conditions under Fakhr al-Din. These were affirmed in an Ottoman *[firman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firman)* from 1605, crediting the Druze emir for "guarding the country, keeping the Bedouins in check, ensuring the welfare and tranquility of the population, promoting agriculture, and increasing prosperity".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbu-Husayn198583–84-39] For the first time since the fall of the Crusader state, the [Franciscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans) reestablished a permanent Latin Christian presence in [Nazareth](21702.md) when Fakhr al-Din granted them permission to reestablish a church at the [site of the Annunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Annunciation) in 1620.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEmmett1995101-40] The [Ma'n dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'n_dynasty) of tax farmers continued to govern the Galilee, with some interruption, until 1660, when the Ottomans administratively reorganized the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirro199245-41] The Druze presence in the Galilee, which dated from the 11th century, was reinforced under the Ma'ns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirro199243–45-42] Their rule also saw the establishment or revival of [Greek Orthodox Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Christian) communities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhamisy20182-43]

After 1660, the administration of the Galilee becomes less clear, with the traveler [Evliya Celebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_Celebi) noting the *[liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak)*&#x73; of Safed and Acre. By the end of the 17th century or start of the 18th century, the Galilee, commonly referred to then as *bilad Safad* (lands of Safed), was administratively divided into lower-tier [nahiyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiye_\(Ottoman\)) (subdistricts), also called *[mukataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqata'ah)*&#x73; (fiscal districts), of [Sidon Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidon_Eyalet).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen1973120–121,_123-44] These were often farmed out by the Ottomans to local *[multezim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iltizam)*&#x73; (tax farmers), who were also responsible for protecting and developing their areas, sometimes through the Druze emirs of Mount Lebanon as intermediaries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen19737–8,_123–124-45] In 1698, [Bashir Shihab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shihab_dynasty#Regency_of_Bashir), emir of the Druze, gained the tax farm of *bilad Safad*, after suppressing local Shia clans in neighboring [Jabal Amil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amil) (southern Lebanon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen19737–8-46]

The Shihabs entrusted their Galilee tax farms to the [Zayadina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayadina) (sing. Zaydani), a local family of Bedouin origin based in the [Tiberias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias) area. The Zaydani sheikhs were already well established potentates in the Galilee, and by the first decade of the 18th century, their members controlled parts of the eastern Galilee, the area around Nazareth, and the western Lower Galilee. Other potentates included the Husayn sheikhs who dominated the plain of Acre from their fortress at [Jiddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiddin) and the Nafi sheikhs who dominated the countryside around Safed from their fort at [Suhmata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhmata).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen19738–9,_83–84-47] During this period, there developed increasing demand from European, mainly French, merchants for the Galilee's cotton. The booming trade spurred increased cultivation in the Galilee's villages and enriched the sheikhs and *multezim*s, especially the Zayadina, who controlled the villages' output. The Zaydani sheikh [Daher al-Umar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daher_al-Umar), in alliance with the unruly [Banu Saqr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Saqr) tribe of the [Beisan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beisan_Valley), emerged as the paramount sheikh of his family and took over Tiberias from its government appointee in 1730. Over the next ten years, he gained control of the rest of the Galilee countryside, after subduing other local sheikhs and thwarting assaults by the governors of Sidon and Damascus.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen197310–12,_14–17-48] To establish law and order, Daher suppressed his erstwhile Bedouin allies, who had often raided villages and threatened the roads.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen197390–91-49] He and his family built and rebuilt forts across the region, the most important at Tiberias, [Deir Hanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Hanna), [Shefa-Amr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shefa-Amr), Safed and [Saffuriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffuriya).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen197383–85,_123–124-50]

As he consolidated control, Daher furthered his trade ties with the French merchants and accumulated substantial wealth. In the mid-1740s, Daher took over Acre, then a small fishing town with considerable ruins and home to the budding mercantile community. By 1750, he completed his walls around Acre, made it his capital and established a monopoly on the production and sale of cotton to the French.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen1973128–130-51] By the following decade, Acre's population had grown to 20,000 from 300 at the start of the century, making it the third largest city in Syria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYazbak2013721-52] At Daher's initiative, the Jewish community was reestablished in Tiberias under Rabbi [Chaim Abulafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Abulafia) of [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna) and the [Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Catholic), Greek Orthodox and [Maronite Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites) communities in Nazareth and Acre grew substantially, with many Christians immigrating from neighboring regions. Through the security and financial incentives Daher provided, the peasants of the Galilee thrived during this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPhilipp200132,_38-53] During the 1760s and early 1770s, Daher faced several rebellions from his sons, who maintained their own power bases from their Galilee forts, though these were generally limited skirmishes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen197385–88-54] Daher's rule came to an end when he was eliminated by the Ottomans in 1775.&#x20;

In 1831, the Galilee, a part of [Ottoman Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria), switched hands from Ottomans to [Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_of_Egypt) until 1840. During this period, aggressive social and politic policies were introduced, which led to a violent [1834 Arab revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Peasant_Revolt_\(1834%E2%80%9335\)). In the process of this revolt the Jewish community of [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) was greatly reduced, in the event of [Safed Plunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed_Plunder) by the rebels. The Arab rebels were subsequently defeated by the Egyptian troops, though in 1838, the Druze of Galilee led another [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1838_Druze_rebellion). In 1834 and [1837](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee_earthquake_of_1837), major earthquakes leveled most of the towns, resulting in great loss of life.

Following the 1864 [Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat) reforms in the Ottoman Empire, the Galilee remained within [Acre Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_Sanjak), but was transferred from [Sidon Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidon_Eyalet) to the newly formed [Syria Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Vilayet) and shortly, from 1888, became administered from [Beirut Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_Vilayet). In 1866, Galilee's first hospital, [the Nazareth Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nazareth_Hospital), was founded under the leadership of American-Armenian missionary Dr. [Kaloost Vartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaloost_Vartan), assisted by German missionary [John Zeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Zeller).



The territory of the Ottoman [Beirut Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_Vilayet), encompassing the Galilee

In the early 20th century, Galilee remained part of [Acre Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_Sanjak) of Ottoman Syria. It was administered as the southernmost territory of the [Beirut Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_Vilayet).

### British Mandatory period

Following the defeat of the Ottoman Empire in [World War I](4764461.md), and the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros), it came under British rule, as part of the [Occupied Enemy Territory Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Enemy_Territory_Administration). Shortly after, in 1920, the region was included in the British Mandate territory, officially a part of Mandatory Palestine from 1923.

### Israel

After the [1948 Arab–Israeli war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_war), nearly the whole of Galilee came under Israel's control. A large portion of the population fled or was forced to leave, leaving dozens of entire villages empty; however, a large [Israeli Arab](492331.md) community remained based in and near the cities of Nazareth, [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel), [Tamra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamra), [Sakhnin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhnin), and [Shefa-'Amr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shefa-'Amr), due to some extent to a successful rapprochement with the Druze. The [kibbutzim](106200.md) around the [Sea of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee) were sometimes shelled by the [Syrian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_army)'s artillery until Israel seized Western [Golan Heights](7515865.md) in the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md).

During the 1970s and the early 1980s, the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) [launched multiple attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_insurgency_in_South_Lebanon) on towns and villages of the Upper and Western Galilee from [Lebanon](17771.md). This came in parallel to the general destabilization of [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md), which became a scene of fierce sectarian fighting which deteriorated into the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War). On the course of the war, Israel initiated [Operation Litani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Litani) (1979) and [Operation Peace For Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Peace_For_Galilee) (1982) with the stated objectives of destroying the PLO infrastructure in Lebanon, protecting the citizens of the Galilee and supporting allied Christian Lebanese militias. Israel took over much of southern Lebanon in support of Christian Lebanese militias until 1985, when it withdrew to a [narrow security buffer zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_\(1982%E2%80%932000\)).

From 1985 to 2000, [Hezbollah](13919.md), and earlier [Amal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Movement), [engaged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\)) the [South Lebanon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army) supported by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md), sometimes shelling [Upper Galilee](8232648.md) communities with [Katyusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rocket_launcher) rockets. In May 2000, [Israeli prime minister](24898.md) [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak) unilaterally withdrew IDF troops from southern Lebanon, maintaining a security force on the [Israeli side of the international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)) recognized by the [United Nations](31769.md). The move brought a collapse to the [South Lebanon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army) and takeover of Southern Lebanon by Hezbollah. However, despite Israeli withdrawal, clashes between Hezbollah and Israel continued along the border, and UN observers condemned both for their attacks.

The [2006 Israel-Lebanon conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Israel-Lebanon_conflict) was characterized by round-the-clock Katyusha rocket attacks (with a greatly extended range) by Hezbollah on the whole of Galilee, with long-range, ground-launched missiles hitting as far south as the [Sharon Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Plain), [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley), and [Jordan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Valley_\(Middle_East\)) below the Sea of Galilee.

## Demography

Further information: [Demographics of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel)



The [Sea of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee) as seen from the [Moshava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshava) [Kinneret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinneret,_Israel)



A sign in front of the [Galil Jewish–Arab School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galil_Jewish%E2%80%93Arab_School), a joint Arab-Jewish [primary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education) in the Galilee

In 2006, there were 1.2 million residents in Galilee, of whom 47% were Jewish.[^cite_note-OferP-55] The [Jewish Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Agency) has attempted a [Judaization of the Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaization_of_the_Galilee),[^cite_note-56] to offset the high growth rate of non-Jewish [Arab citizens of Israel](492331.md).[^cite_note-OferP-55]

The largest cities in the region are Acre, Nahariya, Nazareth, Safed, [Karmiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmiel), [Shaghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaghur,_Israel), [Shefa-'Amr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shefa-'Amr), [Afula](323344.md), and Tiberias.[^cite_note-57] The port city of [Haifa](55606.md) serves as a commercial center for the whole region.[^cite_note-58]

Because of its hilly terrain, most of the people in the Galilee live in small villages connected by relatively few roads.[^cite_note-59] A railroad runs south from [Nahariya](323332.md) along the [Mediterranean coast](19006.md), and a [fork to the east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley_railway) was opened in 2016. The main sources of livelihood throughout the area are agriculture and tourism. [Industrial parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_park) are being developed, bringing further employment opportunities to the local population which includes many recent immigrants. The Israeli government is contributing funding to the private initiative, the Galilee Finance Facility, organised by the [Milken Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milken_Institute) and Koret Economic Development Fund.[^cite_note-60]

The majority [Arab](492331.md) population[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] is primarily Muslim by religious affiliation, with two smaller populations, of [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Druze) and [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christians) (including [Maronites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites_in_Israel)) in comparable sizes. Both Druze and Christians have their majorities in the Galilee.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] Also among the inhabitants of the Galilee are [Bedouins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Bedouin) and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Israel).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

The north-central portion of the Galilee is also known as **Central Galilee**, stretching from the border with [Lebanon](17771.md) to the northern edge of the Jezreel Valley. It includes the cities of Nazareth and Sakhnin, has an Arab majority of 75%, with most of the Jewish population living in hilltop cities like [Upper Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Nazareth). The northern half of the central Lower Galilee, surrounding [Karmiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmiel) and [Sakhnin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhnin), is known as the "**Heart of the Galilee**".

The eastern Galilee is nearly 100% Jewish. This part includes the [Galilee Finger or Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_of_the_Galilee), the Jordan River Valley, and the shores the Sea of Galilee, and contains two of Judaism's [Four Holy Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Holy_Cities), Safed and Tiberias.

The southern part of the Galilee, including the Jezreel Valley, and the [Gilboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gilboa) region are nearly 100% Jewish, with a few small Arab villages near the [West Bank](33209.md) border. About 80% of the population of the Western Galilee is Jewish, all the way up to the Lebanese border. Jews form a small majority in the mountainous [Upper Galilee](8232648.md), with a significant minority Arab population, mainly Druze and Christians.

As of 2011, the Galilee is attracting significant internal migration of [Haredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi) (Ultraorthodox) Jews, who are increasingly moving to the Galilee and Negev as an answer to rising housing prices in central Israel.[^cite_note-ynet-67]

## Tourism

Further information: [Tourism in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Israel)

Galilee is a popular destination for domestic and foreign tourists who enjoy its scenic, recreational, and gastronomic offerings. The Galilee attracts many [Christian pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage), as many of the [miracles of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracles_of_Jesus) occurred, according to the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament), on the shores of the Sea of Galilee—including [his walking on water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_walks_on_water), [calming the storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calming_the_storm), and [feeding five thousand people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_the_multitude) in [Tabgha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabgha). Numerous sites of [biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical) importance are located in the Galilee, such as [Megiddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Megiddo), Jezreel Valley, Mount Tabor, [Hazor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Hazor), [Horns of Hattin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horns_of_Hattin), and more.

A popular hiking trail known as the *yam leyam*, Hebrew for 'sea-to-sea', starts hikers at the Mediterranean. They then hike through the Galilee mountains towards Meron to their final destination, the Kinneret (Sea of Galilee).[^cite_note-68]

In April 2011, Israel unveiled the [Jesus Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Trail), a 40-mile (60-km) [hiking trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiking_trail) in the Galilee for Christian pilgrims. The trail includes a network of footpaths, roads, and bicycle paths linking sites central to the lives of Jesus and his disciples, including [Tabgha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabgha), the traditional site of Jesus's miracle of the loaves and fishes, and the [Mount of Beatitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_of_Beatitudes), where he delivered his Sermon on the Mount. It ends at [Capernaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capernaum) on the shores of the Sea of Galilee, where Jesus espoused his teachings.[^cite_note-69]



The [Church of the Transfiguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Transfiguration) on [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor)

Many [kibbutz](106200.md) and [moshav](1597952.md) families operate *Zimmerim*, from the Yiddish word for 'room', צימער, from 'Zimmer' in German, with the Hebrew ending for plural, -im; the local term for a [Bed and breakfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bed_and_breakfast). Numerous festivals are held throughout the year, especially in the autumn and spring holiday seasons. These include the Acre (Acco) Festival of Alternative Theater,[^cite_note-accofestival-70] the olive harvest festival, music festivals featuring Anglo-American folk, [klezmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klezmer), Renaissance, and [chamber music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_music), and [Karmiel Dance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmiel_Dance_Festival).

## Cuisine

The cuisine of the Galilee is very diverse. The meals are lighter than in the central and southern regions. Dairy products are heavily consumed, especially the Safed cheese that originated in the mountains of the [Upper Galilee](8232648.md). Herbs like thyme, mint, parsley, basil, and rosemary are very common with everything, including dips, meat, fish, stews and cheese. In the eastern part of the Galilee, there is freshwater fish as much as meat, especially the [Tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia) that lives in the Sea of Galilee, Jordan river, and other streams in the region.

Fish is filled with thyme and grilled with rosemary to flavor, or stuffed with oregano leaves, then topped with parsley and served with lemon to squash. This technique exists in other parts of the country including the coasts of the Mediterranean and the Red Sea. A specialty of the region is a baked Tilapia flavored with celery, mint and a lot of lemon juice. Baked fish with tahini is also common in Tiberias. The coastal Galileans prefer to replace the tahini with yogurt and add [sumac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumac) on top.

The Galilee is famous for its olives, pomegranates, wine and especially its [Labneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labneh) w'[Za'atar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Za'atar) which is served with pita bread, meat stews with wine, pomegranates and herbs such as akub, parsley, [khalmit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malva), mint, fennel, etc. are common. Galilean [kubba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibbeh) is usually flavored with cumin, cinnamon, cardamom, concentrated [pomegranate juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate_juice), onion, parsley and pine nuts and served as meze with tahini dip.

Kebabs are made almost in the same way, with sumac replacing cardamom and with carob sometimes replacing the pomegranate juice. Because of its climate, beef has become more popular than lamb, although both are still eaten there. Dates are popular in the tropical climate of the Eastern Galilee.

## Subregions

The definition of Galilee varies depending on the period, author, and point of view (geological, geographical, administrative). Ancient Galilee consisted in broad terms of the Upper and Lower Galilee. Today the northwestern part of the Upper Galilee is in Southern Lebanon, with the rest being in Israel. The Israeli Galilee is often divided into these subregions, which often overlap:

- [Upper Galilee](8232648.md) extends from the Beit HaKerem Valley northwards into southern Lebanon. Its eastern border is the [Hula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley) and the Sea of Galilee separating it from the Golan Heights. To the west it reaches to the Coastal Plain which separates it from the Mediterranean.

- [Lower Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Galilee) covers the area north of the Valleys ([Jezreel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley), [Harod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harod_Valley) and [Beth Shean Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Shean_Valley)) and south of the [Beit HaKerem Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_HaKerem_Valley). Its borders to the east on the [Jordan Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Rift_Valley). It contains the Arab city of [Nazareth](21702.md) and the village of [Cana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cana).

- The "[Galilee Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilee_Panhandle)" ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): אצבע הגליל, *Etzba HaGalil*, lit. "Finger of Galilee") is a [panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salient_\(geography\)) along the [Hulah Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulah_Valley), squeezed between the Lebanese border and the Golan Heights; it contains the towns of [Metulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metulla) and [Qiryat Shemona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiryat_Shemona), the [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_River_\(Israel\)) and part of the [Banias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Hermon) rivers.

The following subregions are sometimes regarded, from different points of view, as distinct from the Galilee, for instance the entire Jordan Valley including the Sea of Galilee and its continuation to the south as one geological and geographical unit, and the Jezreel, Harod, and Beit She'an valleys as "the northern valleys".

- The [Hula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley)

- The [Korazim Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korazim_Plateau)

- The [Sea of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee) and its valley

- The [Jordan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Valley_\(Middle_East\)) from the southern tip of the Sea of Galilee down to Beit She'an

- The [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley), including in its eastern part, the Harod Valley, which stretches between [Afula](323344.md) and the Beit She'an Valley

- The [Beit She'an Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_She'an_Valley) at the junction of the Jordan Valley and the extended Jezreel Valley

- [Mount Gilboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gilboa)

- The **Western Galilee** is a modern Israeli term, which in its minimal definition refers to the coastal plain just west of the Upper Galilee, also known as Plain of Asher or Plain of the Galilee, which stretches from north of Acre to [Rosh HaNikra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_HaNikra_\(kibbutz\)) on the [Israel-Lebanon border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)), and in the common broad definition adds the western part of Upper Galilee, and usually the northwestern part of Lower Galilee as well, corresponding more or less to Acre sub-district or the [Northern District](324281.md).
