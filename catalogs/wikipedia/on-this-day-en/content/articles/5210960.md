**Right-wing terrorism**, **hard right terrorism**, **extreme right terrorism** or **far-right terrorism** is [terrorism](30636.md) that is motivated by a variety of different [right-wing](56522.md) and [far-right](221220.md) ideologies. It can be motivated by [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism), ultraconservatism, [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism), neo-Nazism, extreme anti-communism, neo-fascism, Islamofascism, ecofascism, ethnonationalism, religious nationalism, anti-immigration, anti-multiculturalism, [antisemitism](1078.md), [Islamophobia](162173.md), anti-government sentiment, [patriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_movement) movements, secessionist or [sovereign citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_citizens) beliefs, [opposition to abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion_movements), [ableism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ableism), [anti-homeless people sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_homeless_people), [anti-poor people sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aporophobia), [misogyny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misogyny) or [homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAubrey200445-1][^cite_note-Vincent_A-2] Modern right-wing terrorism largely emerged in Western Europe in the 1970s, and after the [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989) and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, it emerged in Eastern Europe and [Russia](25391.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoghadamEubank200657-3]

Right-wing terrorists aim to overthrow governments and replace them with right-wing regimes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAubrey200445-1] They believe that their actions will trigger events that will ultimately lead to the establishment of these [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) governments.[^cite_note-Cameron,_Gavin_1999,_p._115-4] Although they frequently take inspiration from [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy_\(1922%E2%80%931943\)) and [Nazi Germany](21212.md) with some exceptions, right-wing terrorist groups frequently lack a rigid ideology.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoghadamEubank200658-5] Right-wing terrorists tend to target people who they consider members of foreign communities, but they may also target political opponents, such as [left-wing](18499.md) groups and individuals. The attacks which are perpetrated by right-wing terrorists are not indiscriminate attacks which are perpetrated by individuals and groups which simply seek to kill people; the targets of these attacks are carefully chosen. Because the targets of these attacks are often entire sections of communities, they are not targeted as individuals, instead, they are targeted because they are representatives of groups which are considered foreign, inferior and threatening by them.[^cite_note-Cameron,_Gavin_1999,_p._115-4][^cite_note-6]

According to an analysis by the [Institute for Economics and Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Economics_and_Peace), there has been a surge in far-right terror incidents since 2010, with a 320% increase between 2014 and 2018.[^cite_note-Vincent_A-2]

## Causes

### Economy

German economist [Armin Falk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Falk) *et al.* wrote in a 2011 article that Right-Wing Extremist Crime (REC), which includes [xenophobic](34141.md) and racist motivations, is associated with unemployment rates; as unemployment rates increase, REC also increases.[^cite_note-FalkKuhn2011-7] A 2014 paper argues that right-wing terrorism increases with economic growth, seemingly due to its proponents often being people who lose out under economic modernisation.[^cite_note-8] Conversely, a 2019 study found that economic predictors did not predict right-wing terrorism in Europe, rather, levels of extra-European immigration did; right-wing terrorists did not want immigrants in their countries and they sought to drive them out with force. Thus, increased migration caused greater resentment and thus, their greater resentment was a greater motive for their attacks.[^cite_note-9]

### Right-wing populist politics

In 2016, Thomas Greven suggested that [right-wing populism](8354382.md) is a cause of right-wing terrorism. More simply put, populism supports the advancement of "the average citizen", not the agendas of the privileged elite. Greven defines right-wing populists as those who support [ethnocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocentrism), and [oppose immigration](6491626.md). Because right-wing populism creates a climate of "us versus them", terrorism is more likely to occur.[^cite_note-Greven-10] Vocal opposition to [Islamic terrorism](566821.md) by [Donald Trump](4848272.md) has been obscuring right-wing terrorism in the US,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] where right-wing terror attacks outnumber Islamist, left-wing, and refugee attacks combined in America.[^cite_note-13]

In the wake of the [Christchurch mosque shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch_mosque_shootings) at the Al Noor Mosque and the Linwood Islamic Centre in Christchurch, New Zealand, by terrorist Brenton Harrison Tarrant, expert on terrorism Greg Barton, at [Deakin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deakin_University) in [Australia](4689264.md) (the home country of Tarrant), wrote about the "toxic political environment that allows hate to flourish". Saying that although right-wing extremism in Australia is not nearly as serious as the European neo-Nazi movements or the various types of [white supremacy](33178.md) and toxic nationalism seen in American politics, both major parties attempted to win votes by repeating some of the tough language and inhumane policies which appeared to reward right-wing populists. He further argued: "The result has been such a cacophony of hateful rhetoric that it has been hard for those tasked with spotting the emergence of violent extremism to separate it from all the background noise of extremism."[^cite_note-14]

### Fringe groups

Main articles: [Far-right subcultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_subcultures) and [Hate group](437067.md)

Further information: [Radical right (Europe)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_\(Europe\)) and [Radical right (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_\(United_States\))

References to individual groups may also appear by country below. This section is related to their role in inspiring terrorism.

According to Moghadam and Eubank (2006), groups which are associated with right-wing terrorism include [white power skinhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_power_skinhead) gangs, far-right hooligans, and their sympathizers. The "intellectual guides" of right-wing terrorist movements espouse the view that the state must "rid itself of the foreign elements that undermine it from within" so the state can "provide for its rightful, natural citizens".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoghadamEubank200657–58-15]

In Australia, experts, police and others have been commenting on the failure of the authorities to act effectively in order to combat right-wing radicalisation,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] and the government has vowed to put right-wing extremist individuals and groups under greater scrutiny and pressure, with the home affairs secretary [Mike Pezzullo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pezzullo) making strong comments to a parliamentary committee.[^cite_note-18] A week after the Christchurch mosque shootings in Christchurch, New Zealand, it emerged that three years earlier, Australian-born Brenton Harrison Tarrant, the perpetrator of the shootings, had been active on the [Facebook](7529378.md) pages of two Australian-based white nationalist groups, the [United Patriots Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Patriots_Front) (UPF) and [True Blue Crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blue_Crew) (TBC) and praised the UPF's leader neo-Nazi Blair Cottrell as they all celebrated Donald Trump's victory in the [2016 presidential election in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election). Tarrant was also offered but declined a membership in the [Lads Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lads_Society), a white nationalist fight club which was founded by Cottrell.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

In the United States, Brian Levin, director of the Center for the Study of Hate & Extremism at [California State University, San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_San_Bernardino) and former NYPD officer, wrote about the growth of white nationalism by saying that the [political climate of polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarization) "has provided an opportunity for violent bigots, both on- and offline. Times of change, fear and conflict offer extremists and conspiracists a chance to present themselves as an alternative to increasingly distrusted traditional mainstream choices." He quotes former FBI agent Erroll Southers' view that white supremacy "is being globalized at a very rapid pace", and he urged the government to hold hearings to investigate homegrown extremism.[^cite_note-21] Sociologists at the [University of Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dayton) pointed to the origin of white nationalism in the US and its spread to other countries, and they also noted that the Christchurch attacker's hatred of Muslims was inspired by American white nationalism.[^cite_note-22]

The [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) reports that white supremacist propaganda and recruitment efforts both on and around college campuses have been increasing sharply, with 1,187 incidents in 2018 compared to 421 incidents in 2017, far exceeding any previous year.[^cite_note-23] Far-right terrorists rely on a variety of strategies such as leafleting, the performance of violent rituals, and house parties in order to recruit, mostly targeting angry and marginalized youth who are looking for solutions to their problems. But their most effective recruitment tool is extremist music, which avoids monitoring by moderating parties such as parents and school authorities. Some risk factors which are facilitating recruitment include exposure to racism during childhood, dysfunctional families such as divorced parents, neglect, and physical, emotional and [sexual abuse](7397019.md).[^cite_note-24]

## Role of the media

### Social media

Main article: [Cyberterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberterrorism)

#### "Unite the Right" rally

Main article: [Unite the Right rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unite_the_Right_rally)

The *Post* reported that the upsurge in right-wing violence began during the [Barack Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_administration) and picked up steam under the presidency of Donald Trump, whose remarks after the [Unite the Right rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unite_the_Right_rally) in [Charlottesville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia), in 2017 that there were "some very fine people on both sides" is widely seen as giving confidence to the right that the administration looked favorably on their goals, providing them with "tacit support". A former FBI assistant director for counterintelligence, is quoted as saying that "\[political leaders] from the White House down, used to serve as a check on conduct and speech that was abhorrent to most people. I see that eroding. ... The current political rhetoric is at least enabling, and certainly not discouraging, violence."[^cite_note-wapo112518-99]

According to analysis by the newspaper of data from the [Global Terrorism Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Terrorism_Database), 92 of 263 domestic terrorism events – 35% – that occurred from 2010 to 2017 were right-wing related, while 38 (14%) were Islamist extremist-related, and 34 (13%) were left-wing related. Not only that, but a criminologist from [John Jay College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jay_College) stated that right-wing attacks were statistically more likely to result in fatalities.[^cite_note-wapo112518-99]

#### 2021 United States Capitol attack

Main article: [January 6 United States Capitol attack](66307490.md)



A mob of Trump supporters storm the [United States Capitol](31979.md) building on January 6, 2021.

On January 6, 2021, a mob of rioters supporting President Trump's [attempts to overturn](65905202.md) the [2020 United States presidential election](48410011.md), [stormed the U.S. Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol) during speeches which were made by Trump and his allies at a rally. After breaching multiple police perimeters, they damaged and occupied parts of the building for several hours. [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Guard) units from several states were called up to deal with the violence, while the riots resulted in six deaths (four rioters and two police officers), over 80 arrests, and 116 officers being injured. Several high-profile members of the government and Capitol security resigned, including the chief of the [Capitol Police](1915214.md), the Sergeant-at-Arms of both the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_at_Arms_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_at_Arms_of_the_United_States_Senate). Over 70 other countries and international organizations expressed their concerns over the protests and condemned the violence. One group involved, the [Proud Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proud_Boys), was designated a terrorist organization in [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-\:12-109][^cite_note-GOCL13-110][^cite_note-NCAPBP-111][^cite_note-auto2-112] Since then, at least two dozen Proud Boys members and affiliates have been indicted for their alleged roles in the insurrection.[^cite_note-NCAPBP-111][^cite_note-\:15-113][^cite_note-114]

During congressional testimony two months after the Capitol assault, FBI director [Christopher Wray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Wray) characterized the incident as "domestic terrorism". Although he did not attribute the assault to a specific group, he made clear that the evidence showed a connection to right-wing extremism, particularly militia groups. When asked if "right-wing white supremacist groups played an instrumental role", Wray explained that the FBI did not use labels about political positioning, but agreed "we're basically saying the same thing."[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] Wray testified that the top threat of domestic violent extremists were "specifically those who advocate for the superiority of the white race", alongside the threat posed by [ISIL](9087364.md).[^cite_note-117] Despite efforts by many conservatives, including during the congressional hearing, to blame [antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifa_\(United_States\)) for the attack, Wray reiterated that the FBI had found no evidence to support the allegations. A February 2021 poll found that 58% of Republicans believe the Capitol riot was "mostly an antifa-inspired attack that only involved a few Trump supporters".[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

### Finland

See also: [Far-right politics in Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_Finland) and [White Terror (Finland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Terror_\(Finland\))



Murdered leftists in [Tampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampere) (1918)

In the 1920s–1940s, far-right and fascist groups attacked left-wing events and politicians systematically, resulting in deaths. The groups were responsible for bombing and burning down gathering places of the leftists. Minister of the Interior [Heikki Ritavuori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heikki_Ritavuori) was assassinated for supposedly being too lenient towards communists.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Conservative and [White Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Guard_\(Finland\)) authorities supported the far-right to a large extent, for instance the social democrat politician [Onni Happonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onni_Happonen) was arrested by police who then turned him over to a fascist lynch mob to be killed.[^cite_note-125]

In 1945, after the armistice with the Soviet Union, nationalist groups bombed multiple left-wing targets in Helsinki. Attacks in [Haaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaga) and [Vallila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallila) against left-wing papers and meeting halls followed.[^cite_note-OVS-126] A group identifying themselves as "fascists from [Munkkiniemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munkkiniemi)" used dynamite and IEDs built from anti-aircraft shells to blow up the headquarters of *Vapaa Sana* newspaper.[^cite_note-127]

During the Cold War, far-right activism was limited to small illegal groups such as the [Turku Society for the Spiritual Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku_Society_for_the_Spiritual_Sciences), led by [Pekka Siitoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekka_Siitoin) who made headlines after [arson and bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursiivi_printing_house_arson) of the printing houses of the [Communist Party of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Finland). His associates also sent [letter bombs](49760.md) to leftists, including to the headquarters of the [Finnish Democratic Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Youth_\(Finland\)).[^cite_note-128] Another group called the "New Patriotic People's Movement" bombed the left-wing *[Kansan Uutiset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansan_Uutiset)* newspaper and the embassy of communist Bulgaria.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-OVS-126] Member of the [Nordic Realm Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Realm_Party) Seppo Seluska was convicted of the torture and murder of a gay [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew).[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-\:1-132][^cite_note-133]

In 1975 in [Petäjävesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C3%A4j%C3%A4vesi) an election campaign event of the communist [SKDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKDL) was bombed by self-declared neo-fascists. There were no deaths although the bomb caused material damages.[^cite_note-134] In November 1978, the office of the Southern Saimaa Union of Socialist Youth was destroyed in an arson attack. The perpetrators left behind a swastika painted on the wall.[^cite_note-135]

The skinhead culture gained momentum during the late 1980s and peaked during the late 1990s. In 1991, Finland received a number of Somali immigrants who became the main target of Finnish skinhead violence in the following years, including four attacks using explosives and a racist murder. Asylum seeker centres were attacked, in [Joensuu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joensuu) skinheads would force their way into an asylum seeker centre and start shooting with shotguns. At worst Somalis were assaulted by 50 skinheads at the same time.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

The most prominent neo-Nazi group [Nordic Resistance Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Resistance_Movement) that is tied to multiple murders, attempted murders and assaults of political enemies was found in 2006 and proscribed in 2019.[^cite_note-138] During the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md) 40 asylum seeker reception centres were targets of arson attacks.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] In its annual threat assessment for 2020, the [National Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Investigation_\(Finland\)) found that despite the ban, the threat of far-right terrorism had risen and identified 400 persons of interest "motivated and with the capacity to perform terrorism in Finland". International links and funding networks were pointed out as a special source of concern.[^cite_note-141]

On 4 December 2021, the Finnish police arrested a [five-man cell in Kankaanpää](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankaanp%C3%A4%C3%A4_terrorism_arrests) on suspicion of planning a terror attack and confiscated numerous firearms including assault rifles and forty kilos of explosives and hundreds of litres of explosive precursors. According to the Finnish media the men adhered to the ideology of [Atomwaffen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomwaffen) and [James Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mason_\(neo-Nazi\)) and used Atomwaffen-like symbols.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

In July 2022, a group of youth stole all the rainbow flags from a library in [Lapua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapua) and left an improvised explosive device behind. There were no casualties but a gay pride event was interrupted by the explosion.[^cite_note-145] On 26 August 2022 a bomb exploded near a pride in [Savonlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savonlinna), the police has arrested two locals for the act.[^cite_note-146] In July 2023 the Finnish police arrested five men in [Lahti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahti) who possessed assault rifles and adhered to accelerationism and [Siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_\(Mason_book\)) and planned to ignite a race war by attacking the infrastructure, electric grid and railroads.[^cite_note-147] A man affiliated with the Lahti group is also suspected of plotting a ritual murder and sending a string of letter bombs sent to Social Democrat, Green and Left party offices.[^cite_note-148]

In mid-June 2024, there was a series of racist stabbings in [Oulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oulu). One of the perpetrators was on the terror watchlist for connections to the outlawed terror group [Nordic Resistance Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Resistance_Movement).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] Another perpetrator was a supporter of the NRM as well. The third attacker was unaffiliated. The three men stabbed several people with a perceived immigrant background, causing life-threatening injuries.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

### France

See also: [Terrorism in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_France)

France has a modern history of right-wing terrorism that dates back to the middle of the 20th century. Historically, right-wing terrorism was tied to rage over the loss of France's colonial possessions in Africa, particularly, the loss of [Algeria](358.md). In 1961, the [Organisation armée secrète](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_arm%C3%A9e_secr%C3%A8te) or OAS, a right-wing terrorist group that protested against Algerian independence from France, launched a bomb attack on board a [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg)–[Paris](22989.md) train which killed 28 people.[^cite_note-LeFigaro-7-Jan-2015-153]

On 14 December 1973, the far-right [Charles Martel Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Martel_Group) orchestrated a bomb attack at the Consulate of Algeria, killing 4 people and injuring 20.[^cite_note-154]

In the town of [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon), a far-right extremist group called SOS-France existed. On 18 August 1986, four members were driving a car carrying explosives, apparently in an attempt to bomb the offices of [SOS Racisme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOS_Racisme). However it exploded while they were still in it, killing all four of them.[^cite_note-155]

In more recent times, far-right extremism in France has been fueled by the rise of anti-immigrant and far-right political movements. Neo-Nazi members of the [French and European Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_European_Nationalist_Party) were responsible for a pair of [anti-immigrant terror bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Cannes_and_Nice_attacks) in 1988. Sonacotra hostels in [Cagnes-sur-Mer](3546923.md) and Cannes were bombed, killing Romanian immigrant George Iordachescu and injuring 16 people, mostly Tunisians. In an attempt to [frame Jewish extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_flag) for the Cagnes-sur-Mer bombing, the terrorists left leaflets bearing [Stars of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_David) and the name [Masada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masada) at the scene, with the message "To destroy [Israel](9282173.md), Islam has chosen the sword. For this choice, Islam will perish."[^cite_note-156]

On 28 May 2008, members of the neo-Nazi Nomad 88 group fired machine guns at people from their car in [Saint-Michel-sur-Orge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Michel-sur-Orge).[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]

In the aftermath of the [*Charlie Hebdo* shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hebdo_shooting), six mosques and a restaurant were attacked, the attacks were classified as acts of right-wing terrorism by the authorities.[^cite_note-Europol16-159]

### Germany

See also: [Far-right politics in Germany](18526492.md) and [terrorism in Germany](35484188.md)



Memorial commemorating victims of the Oktoberfest bombing

In 1980, a right-wing terrorist attack, known as [Oktoberfest bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest_bombing) in [Munich](19058.md), Germany, killed 13 people, including the attacker, and injured 215. Fears of an ongoing campaign of major right-wing terrorist attacks did not materialize.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAubrey200445-1]

Right-wing extremist offenses in Germany rose sharply in 2015 and 2016.[^cite_note-Bleiker-160] Figures from the German government tallied 316 violent xenophobic offences in 2014 and 612 such offenses in 2015.[^cite_note-Bleiker-160]

In August 2014, a group of four Germans founded a Munich-based far-right terrorist group, the Oldschool Society. The group, which held racist, antisemitic, and anti-Muslim views, eventually attracted 30 members.[^cite_note-APOldschoolsentence-161] They stockpiled weapons and explosives and plotted to attack a refugee shelter in [Saxony](28395.md),[^cite_note-APOldschoolsentence-161] but the group's leaders were arrested in May 2015 before carrying out the attack.[^cite_note-162] In March 2017 four of the group's leaders were sentenced to prison terms.[^cite_note-APOldschoolsentence-161]

The perpetrator of a [mass shooting in Munich in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Munich_shooting) had far-right views.[^cite_note-163]

According to figures which were released by the interior ministry in May 2019, of an estimated 24,000 far-right extremists in the country, 12,700 Germans are inclined towards violence. Extremists belonging to [Der Dritte Weg/The III. Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_III._Path) marched in through a town in Saxony on 1 May, the day before the Jewish remembrance of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust), carrying flags and a banner saying "Social justice instead of criminal foreigners".[^cite_note-164]

[Walter Lübcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_L%C3%BCbcke), a [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU) politician from [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) was assassinated at his home via gunshot because of his pro-migrant views by Stephan Ernst, a German Neo-Nazi who was a member of the British neo-Nazi terrorist group [Combat 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_18) (C18) and the [National Democratic Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Party_of_Germany) (NPD) who had engaged in a series of anti-migrant crimes and had been convicted for knife and bomb attacks against minorities.[^cite_note-165] Following the murder, the self-described ["doomsday prepper"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivalism) group [Nordkreuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordkreuz?action=edit\&redlink=1) (German: Northern Cross) was discovered to have made kill lists of politicians and acquired body bags for a hypothetical "Day X" doomsday scenario; using the messaging app [Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram_\(app\)) and a police database with 25,000 names, the group amassed firearms and ammunition.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-BennholdNYT2020-167]

On 19 February 2020, [two mass shootings occurred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanau_shootings) at two [shisha bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hookah_lounge) in [Hanau](442088.md), resulting in the death of nine people, all with an immigrant background. The attacker then killed his mother at their house and committed suicide. The 43-year-old attacker was identified as a far-right extremist, who expressed a hatred for immigrants.[^cite_note-168]

In February 2020, following the observation of a meeting of a dozen right-wing extremists, those involved were arrested after they had decided to launch attacks on mosques in Germany to trigger a civil war.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

### Italy

Main article: [Terrorism in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Italy)

See also: [Armed, far-right organizations in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed,_far-right_organizations_in_Italy)

In the 1970s and 1980s, Italy endured the [Years of Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_\(Italy\)), a period of time in which terrorist attacks were frequently perpetrated: between 1969 and 1982, the nation suffered 8,800 terrorist attacks, in which a total of 351 people were killed and 768 were injured.[^cite_note-Hof-171] The terrorist attacks have been both ascribed both to the far-left and the far-right, yet many of the terrorist attacks remain without a clear culprit; many have claimed that responsibility for the attacks could be ascribed to rogue members of the Italian secret service. Some of the terrorist attacks ascribed to a particular political group may have actually been the work of these rogue agents: this has been claimed, among many others, by [Francesco Cossiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Cossiga),[^cite_note-172] who was the Prime Minister during the last years of lead, and by [Giulio Andreotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Andreotti),[^cite_note-173] who, during the same period of time, held the office of Prime Minister more than once.



Ruins of the Bologna station west wing after the 1980 [Bologna Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Massacre)

The [Years of Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_\(Italy\)) are considered to have begun with the [Piazza Fontana bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Fontana_bombing) in [Milan](36511.md) in December 1969,[^cite_note-Hof-171] perpetrated by [Ordine Nuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordine_Nuovo), a right-wing [neofascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neofascist) group.[^cite_note-McHugh-174] Sixteen people were killed, and 90 injured, in the bombing.[^cite_note-McHugh-174]

In July 1970, this same group carried out a bombing on a train traveling from Rome to Messina, killing six and wounding almost 100. The group also carried out the [Piazza della Loggia bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_della_Loggia_bombing) in 1974, killing eight antifascist activists.[^cite_note-McHugh-174] Perhaps the most infamous right-wing terrorist attack in post-war Italy is the [August 1980 Bologna bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_1980_Bologna_bombing), in which neo-fascist [Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclei_Armati_Rivoluzionari) ('Armed Revolutionary Nuclei'), an Ordine Nuovo offshoot, killed 85 people and injured 200 at the [Bologna railroad station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Centrale_railway_station).[^cite_note-McHugh-174][^cite_note-1988Convictions-175] [Valerio Fioravanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerio_Fioravanti), [Francesca Mambro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesca_Mambro), and two others were convicted of mass murder in the attacks,[^cite_note-1988Convictions-175] although both have always denied any connection with them.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177]

In December 2011, Gianluca Casseri targeted Senegalese peddlers in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [killing two and injuring three others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Florence_shootings) before killing himself.[^cite_note-LinksFarRight-178][^cite_note-Kington-179] The perpetrator was a sympathizer of [CasaPound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CasaPound),[^cite_note-LinksFarRight-178][^cite_note-Kington-179] a neo-fascist party that Italian judges have recognized as not posing a threat to public or private safety.[^cite_note-180]

### Latvia



[Igors Šiškins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igors_%C5%A0i%C5%A1kins) at the [Remembrance day of the Latvian legionnaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_day_of_the_Latvian_legionnaires) on March 16, 2009

On the night of 5 June 1997, members of the far-right [Pērkonkrusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%93rkonkrusts) unsuccessfully bombed the [Monument to the Liberators of Soviet Latvia and Riga from the German Fascist Invaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Liberators_of_Soviet_Latvia_and_Riga_from_the_German_Fascist_Invaders). Two of them were killed in the explosion,[^cite_note-181] while six others, including [Igors Šiškins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igors_%C5%A0i%C5%A1kins), were sentenced for up to three years in prison in 2000.[^cite_note-182] The group ceased organised activities or was banned around 2006.[^cite_note-183]

In late 2018, the State Security Service arrested a self-proclaimed follower of the ideas of Anders Behring Breivik who was planning to perform terrorist attacks on an [ethnic minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minority) school and several commercial outlets in [Jūrmala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%ABrmala) on 13 February, the birthday of Breivik. The individual had previously published comments on different websites for an extended period of time aimed against the Roma and Russian people, including calls to exterminate them. He was found guilty but exempted from [criminal liability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_liability) on medical grounds and assigned to a psychiatric hospital for treatment.[^cite_note-184]

### Norway

On 22 July 2011, Norwegian right-wing extremist with neo-Nazi[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186] and fascist[^cite_note-187] sympathies Anders Behring Breivik carried out the [2011 Norway attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Norway_attacks), the deadliest attack in Norway since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Norway). First, he bombed several government buildings in [Oslo](22309.md), killing eight people and injuring more than 200. After the bombings, he went to [Utøya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ut%C3%B8ya) island in a fake police uniform and began firing on people attending a political youth camp for the [Worker's Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Youth_League_\(Norway\)) (AUF), a left-wing political party, killing 69 and injuring more than 110. Overall, the two terrorist attacks in Utøya and Oslo, Norway resulted in 77 dead and 319 injured. Anders Behring Breivik also had written a manifesto *[2083: A European Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Behring_Breivik#Manifesto_%E2%80%93_2083:_A_European_Declaration_of_Independence)* in which he accused [Islam](6037917.md), [Cultural Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Marxism), [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism), and [feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism) of causing a "cultural suicide" of Europe and claimed to belong to an organization called the Knights' Templar (named after [the medieval military order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Templar)).

Philip Manshaus was arrested for [attacking Al-Noor Islamic Centre in Baerum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baerum_mosque_shooting), on the outskirts of Oslo, on August 10, [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_in_Norway). According to police, the man appeared to hold "far-right" and "anti-immigrant" views and had expressed sympathy for [Vidkun Quisling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidkun_Quisling) – the fascist World War II leader of Norway – as well as Australian-born terrorist Brenton Harrison Tarrant, the perpetrator of the Christchurch, New Zealand mosque shootings, [John T. Earnest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poway_synagogue_shooting#Perpetrator) the perpetrator of the [Escondido, California mosque fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escondido_mosque_fire) and the [Poway, California synagogue shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poway_synagogue_shooting), as well as [Patrick Crusius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_El_Paso_shooting#Suspect) the man behind the [El Paso, Texas Walmart shooting targeting Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_El_Paso_shooting). He has been charged with attempted murder in this case and with the murder of his 17-year-old stepsister in an unrelated incident. The mosque shooting is being investigated as a possible act of terrorism.[^cite_note-188]

### Russia

Media reports indicate that the market, located near [Cherkizovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherkizovsky), was targeted due to its high volume of [Central Asian](6742.md) and [Caucasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) clientele.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] The [Russian Imperial Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Imperial_Movement) is a Russian [ultranationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalist), white supremacist,[^cite_note-Hudson-191] far-right [paramilitary organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramilitary_organization)[^cite_note-DN_151027-192] based in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md). It has been designated as a [terrorist group](63578.md) by the United States[^cite_note-ICCT-193] and Canada.[^cite_note-GOCL13-110]

[Russian separatist forces in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) include several right-wing militias, connected to the official armed forces of the right-wing breakaway [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md) and [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) in eastern [Ukraine](31750.md), which are designed terrorist organisations by the Ukrainian government. International volunteers for the militias have been arrested for plotting terror attacks.[^cite_note-BBC_News_2016-194]

According to [The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md), Russian state militarization of the youth and narratives of external threats have been responsible for youth violence and school attacks against perceived outsiders. There were at least seven school attacks in the first two months of 2026, like the [Bashkir State Medical University attack](82360474.md) in which a teen neo-Nazi stabbed seven foreign students while shouting Nazi slogans and painting a swastika with the victims' blood. There is also a convergence with militants fighting in Ukraine, the neo-Nazi paramilitary [Rusich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusich_Group) group supported the 15-year-old white supremacist who stabbed a Tajik student to death in the [2025 Odintsovo school attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Odintsovo_school_attack).[^cite_note-195]

Between 2007 and 2022, seventy people who were associated with the far-right have been convicted of more than two hundred terrorist offences, based on eight different offences from the [Terrorism Act 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2000) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2006).[^cite_note-\:5-202]

In 2013, the UK government assessed that right-wing extremism was a "very low risk to national security"; this changed the following year after the emergence of the neo-Nazi group [National Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Action_\(UK\)). Since 2014, there has been a significant increase in the threat from [extreme right-wing terrorism](221220.md) (ERWT) in the UK, referring to "the segment of the far-right movement involved in politically motivated violence". In response, experts in security and intelligence have increased monitoring of threats from groups and individuals which hold extreme right-wing views. [Counter Terrorism Policing](54350607.md) reported that between March 2017 and December 2021, 12 of 32 imminent terrorism plots that were thwarted were ERWT. The [Independent Reviewer of Terrorism Legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Reviewer_of_Terrorism_Legislation) reported in 2022 that one in five counter-terrorism investigations and 40% of terrorism-related arrests were related to far-right perspectives. As of 2025, ERWT includes [cultural nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_nationalism), [white nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism) and white supremacist ideologies.[^cite_note-\:5-202]

In the UK, the history of ERWT can be traced back to the activism of [Sir Oswald Mosley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Oswald_Mosley)’s [British Union of Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Union_of_Fascists), an [ultranationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism) group that was subsequently banned in 1940. In *[Studies in Conflict & Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studies_in_Conflict_&_Terrorism)*, Jupp wrote that "Politically motivated violence and terrorism have been persistent features of this movement, which comprises a complex assortment of political parties, street movements, extremist organizations and atomized lone actors advocating a diverse range of ideologies and narratives." This includes individuals associated with the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_\(UK\)) and [British National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_National_Party), with David Copeland's [London bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_London_nail_bombings) serving as a notable example.[^cite_note-\:5-202]

On 16 June 2016, [member of parliament, Jo Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Jo_Cox), was murdered by a 53-year-old [white nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalist) who had links to British and American far-right political groups including the NF, the U.S. neo-Nazi organisation [National Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Alliance_\(United_States\)), and the EDL.[^cite_note-Guardian332-203]

On 19 June 2017, a 47-year-old man [used a van to attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Finsbury_Park_van_attack) Muslim worshippers at Finsbury Park mosque, killing 1 and injuring 11.[^cite_note-204]

#### Northern Ireland

[Loyalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_loyalism) paramilitaries such as the [Ulster Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Volunteer_Force) (UVF), [Ulster Defence Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Defence_Association) (UDA), [Loyalist Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalist_Volunteer_Force) (LVF) and [Orange Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Volunteers) (OVF) have been aligned with far-right politics and ideology and have been involved in numerous sectarian attacks and killings on Catholics both during and after [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles). During the conflict, British far-right activists supplied funds and weaponry to these groups in [Northern Ireland](21265.md).[^cite_note-Goodrick-Clarke2003-205] Following the [Good Friday Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Agreement), some members of Loyalist groups orchestrated racist attacks in Northern Ireland,[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-chrisafis-207][^cite_note-bbcjan04-208] including [pipe bomb](233935.md) and gun attacks on the homes of immigrants.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] As a result, Northern Ireland has a higher proportion of racist attacks than other parts of the UK,[^cite_note-bbcjan04-208][^cite_note-212] and was branded the "race-hate capital of Europe".[^cite_note-213]

## Oceania

### Australia

Main article: [Far-right terrorism in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_terrorism_in_Australia)

In August 2016, Phillip Galea was charged with several terrorist offences. Galea had conducted "surveillance" of "left-wing premises" and planned to carry out bombings. Explosive ingredients were found at his home. Galea had links with organisations such as [Combat 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_18) (C18) and the [United Patriots Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Patriots_Front) (UPF).[^cite_note-214] On 5 December 2019, a jury found Galea guilty of planning and preparing a terror attack.[^cite_note-215]

In 2017, the *[Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Morning_Herald)* reported on the conviction of neo-Nazi Michael James Holt, 26 who had threatened to carry out a mass shooting attack and considered [Westfield Tuggerah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_Tuggerah) as a target. He had manufactured home-made guns, knuckle dusters and slingshots in his grandfather's garage. Raids on his mother's home and a hotel room discovered more weapons including several firearms, slingshots and knuckle dusters.[^cite_note-216]

## Asia

### India

Main articles: [Hindu terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_terrorism) and [Cow vigilante violence in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow_vigilante_violence_in_India)

Further information: [Violence against Muslims in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_Muslims_in_India) and [Violence against Christians in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_Christians_in_India)

In 1992, the 16th-century [Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babri_Masjid) in the city of [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya), in [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) was [demolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_of_the_Babri_Masjid) by the [Vishva Hindu Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishva_Hindu_Parishad), a far-right [Hindutva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva) organisation. According to them, the mosque was built on the site of a Hindu temple in the 19th century. The demolition resulted in [intercommunal rioting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_violence) between India's [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_India) and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India) communities, causing the deaths of at least 2,000 people.[^cite_note-Guha2007a-217][^cite_note-218]

### Israel

Main articles: [Far-right politics in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_Israel), [Israeli settler violence](47246246.md), [Jewish terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_terrorism), [Racism in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_Israel), [Zionist political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist_political_violence), and [Baruch Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Goldstein)

A number of right-wing [Revisionist Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revisionist_Zionism) groups have been designated as terrorist organisations. [Lehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehi_\(militant_group\)), known as the Stern Gang, was a Zionist paramilitary and terrorist organization founded in [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) in 1940, professing [National Bolshevism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bolshevism) and influenced by [Italian fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism). It carried out assassinations and it was also accused of committing massacres until it was disbanded in 1949. The [Jewish Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Underground) was a radical right-wing organization[^cite_note-219] which was considered a terrorist organization by the Israeli government.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] It plotted and carried out car and bus bombings, and it also plotted and carried out attacks on students and religious sites in the early 1980s until the arrest of its main activists in 1984. [Kach and its splinter group Kahane Chai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach_\(political_party\)) was a right-wing [Orthodox Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism), [ultranationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism) [political party in Israel](240166.md), which was formed in 1971 and was designated as a terrorist organization by Israel in the 1990s,[^cite_note-222] Canada,[^cite_note-223] the European Union,[^cite_note-eu-2009/67/CFSP-224] Japan,[^cite_note-Japan_ban-225] and the United States.[^cite_note-state.gov-226]
