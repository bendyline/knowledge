The **Moderna COVID‑19 vaccine**, sold under the brand name **Spikevax** among others, is a [COVID-19 vaccine](63319438.md) developed by the American company [Moderna](39743243.md), the United States [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](1013155.md) (NIAID), and the [Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_Advanced_Research_and_Development_Authority) (BARDA). Depending on the jurisdiction, it is authorized for use in humans aged six months,[^cite_note-47] twelve years, or eighteen years and older. The Moderna COVID‑19 vaccine provides protection against [COVID-19](63030231.md), which is caused by infection by the [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2) virus.[^cite_note-Moderna_COVID-19_Vaccine_FDA_label-3][^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2][^cite_note-CA_Spikevax-23][^cite_note-TGA_provisional_approval_2-15] A different version of the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand name **Mnexspike** (mRNA-1283), was approved for medical use in the United States in May 2025,[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] and in the European Union in February 2026.[^cite_note-Mnexspike_EPAR-45][^cite_note-Mnexspike_PI-46]

Spikevax is designed to be administered in two or three 0.5-[mL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litre#SI_prefixes_applied_to_the_litre) doses given by [intramuscular injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuscular_injection), primarily into the deltoid muscle, at an interval of at least 28 days apart.[^cite_note-CDC_standing_order-51][^cite_note-IDSA-52][^cite_note-FDA_PR_20210812-53][^cite_note-CDC_immunocompromised-54] The [World Health Organization](33583.md) advises an eight-week interval between doses to optimize efficacy. Additional booster doses are approved in some regions to maintain immunity. Clinical trials and real-world data have demonstrated the vaccine's high efficacy, with significant effectiveness observed two weeks post-administration of the second dose, offering 94% protection against Covid and robust defense against severe cases. The vaccine's efficacy spans various demographics, including age, sex, and those with high-risk medical conditions.

Spikevax is an [mRNA vaccine](63136688.md) composed of [nucleoside-modified mRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoside-modified_messenger_RNA) (modRNA) encoding a [spike protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_spike_protein) of SARS-CoV-2, which is encapsulated in [lipid nanoparticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_lipid_nanoparticle).[^cite_note-Baden-55] In August and September 2022, bivalent versions of the vaccine (Moderna COVID-19 Vaccine, Bivalent) containing elasomeran/elasomeran 0-omicron (Spikevax Bivalent Zero/Omicron)[^cite_note-\:1-56] were authorized for use as booster doses in individuals aged 18 or older in the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-MHRA_PR_20220815-57][^cite_note-\:2-58] Switzerland,[^cite_note-59] Australia,[^cite_note-60] Canada,[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-\:3-62] the European Union,[^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2] and the United States.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20220831-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-Moderna_PR_20210805-68] The second component of the version of the bivalent vaccine used in the United States[^cite_note-Moderna_PR_2022.08.23_re_mRNA-1273.222-69] is based on the [Omicron BA.4/BA.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Omicron_variant#BA.4/5) variant,[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20220831-63] while the second component of the bivalent vaccine version used in other countries is based on the [Omicron BA.1 variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Omicron_variant#BA.1_3).[^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2][^cite_note-\:1-56][^cite_note-\:2-58][^cite_note-MHRA_PR_20220815-57][^cite_note-\:3-62] The vaccine's effectiveness against variants has been extensively studied, indicating varying degrees of protection. For instance, during the prevalence of the Delta variant, effectiveness against infection slightly decreased over time. The vaccine's longevity and continuous protection are under study, with ongoing research focusing on its duration of effectiveness, which remains partially undetermined as of the latest updates.

The safety profile of the vaccine is favorable, with common side effects including injection site pain, fatigue, and headaches. Severe reactions like [anaphylaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaphylaxis) are exceedingly rare. Concerns regarding [myocarditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocarditis), have been identified but are typically mild and manageable. The vaccine's formulation utilizes mRNA technology, encapsulated within lipid nanoparticles to ensure cellular uptake and immune system response.

## Medical uses

The Moderna COVID‑19 vaccine is used to provide protection against infection by the [SARS‑CoV‑2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2) virus in order to prevent COVID‑19.[^cite_note-Moderna_COVID-19_Vaccine_FDA_label-3][^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2]

The vaccine is given by [intramuscular injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuscular_injection) into the [deltoid muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deltoid_muscle) of the arm.[^cite_note-CDC_standing_order-51] The initial course consists of two doses.[^cite_note-CDC_standing_order-51] The [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) recommends an interval of eight weeks between doses.[^cite_note-who-guidance-70]

A third, fourth, or fifth dose can be added in some countries.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20210812-53][^cite_note-CDC_immunocompromised-54][^cite_note-EMA_extra_doses-71]

### Efficacy



Cumulative Incidence Curves for the First COVID‑19 Occurrence (Moderna mRNA-1273 vaccine)

Evidence of [vaccine efficacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine_efficacy) starts about two weeks after the first dose.[^cite_note-who-back-72][^cite_note-73] High efficacy is achieved with full immunization, two weeks after the second dose, and was evaluated at 94.1%: at the end of the vaccine study that led to emergency authorization in the US, there were eleven cases of COVID‑19 in the vaccine group (out of 15,181 people) versus 185 cases in the [placebo group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo) (15,170 people).[^cite_note-who-back-72] Moreover, there were zero cases of severe COVID‑19 in the vaccine group, versus eleven in the placebo group.[^cite_note-\:0-74] This efficacy has been described as "astonishing"[^cite_note-astonishing-75] and "borderline historic"[^cite_note-contagionlive-76] for a respiratory virus vaccine, and it is similar to the efficacy of the [Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine](65797150.md).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

Efficacy estimates were similar across age groups, sexes, racial and ethnic groups, and participants with medical comorbidities associated with high risk of severe COVID‑19.[^cite_note-briefing-79] Only individuals aged 18 or older were studied. Studies are underway to gauge efficacy and safety in children aged 0–11 (KidCOVE) and 12–17 (TeenCOVE).[^cite_note-80]

A further study conducted by the US [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) between December 2020, and March 2021, on nearly 4 thousand health care personnel, [first responders](500273.md), and other essential and frontline workers concluded that under real-world conditions, mRNA vaccine effectiveness of full immunization (14 days or more after second dose) was 90% against [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2) infections, regardless of symptoms, and vaccine effectiveness of partial immunization (14 days or more after first dose but before second dose) was 80%.[^cite_note-cdc0-realworld-81]

The duration of protection provided by the vaccine is unknown as of April 2021,[^cite_note-Baden-55][^cite_note-nejm-antibody-persistence-82] and a two-year followup study is underway to determine the duration.[^cite_note-contagionlive-76]

Preliminary results from a phase III trial indicate that vaccine efficacy is durable, remaining at 93% six months after the second dose.[^cite_note-83]

### Effectiveness

A vaccine is generally considered effective if the estimate is ≥50% with a >30% lower limit of the 95% [confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval).[^cite_note-84] Effectiveness is generally expected to slowly decrease over time.[^cite_note-85]

In August 2021, results from a study suggested that the effectiveness against infection decreased from 91% (81–96%) to 66% (26–84%) when the [Delta variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Delta_variant) became predominant in the US, which may be due to unmeasured and residual [confounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confounding) related to a decline in vaccine effectiveness over time.[^cite_note-86]

### Specific populations

Limited data are available on the safety of the Moderna COVID‑19 vaccine during pregnancy.[^cite_note-cdc-pregnancy-92] The initial study excluded pregnant women or discontinued them from vaccination upon a positive pregnancy test.[^cite_note-who-back-72] Studies in animals found no safety concerns and clinical trials are underway to evaluate the safety and efficacy of COVID‑19 vaccines in pregnant women.[^cite_note-cdc-pregnancy-92] Real-world observations through the CDC v-safe tracking program have not uncovered unusual numbers of adverse events or outcomes of interest.[^cite_note-93] Based on the results of a preliminary study, the US CDC recommends that pregnant women get vaccinated with the COVID‑19 vaccine.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

The [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) stated that "the safety data supported a favorable safety profile" and that the vaccine's AE ([adverse event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_event)) profile "did not suggest any specific safety concerns".[^cite_note-who-back-72] The most common adverse events were pain at the injection site, fatigue, headache, [myalgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myalgia) (muscle pain), and [arthralgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthralgia) (joint pain).[^cite_note-who-back-72]

The US [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) has reported [anaphylaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaphylaxis) (a severe allergic reaction) in 2.5 cases per million doses administered and has recommended a 15-minute observation period after injection.[^cite_note-97] Delayed cutaneous reactions at injection sites resulting in rash-like [erythemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythema) have also been observed in rare cases but are not considered serious or contraindications to subsequent vaccination.[^cite_note-98] The incidence rate for local adverse erythema is about 10.8%. In 1.9% of cases, redness may extend to a size of 100 mm or greater.[^cite_note-EUA_fact_sheet-99]

In June 2021, the US CDC confirmed that [myocarditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocarditis) or [pericarditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericarditis) occurs in about 13 of every 1 million young people, mostly male and over the age of 16, who received the Moderna or the [Pfizer–BioNTech vaccine](65797150.md).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Most affected individuals recover quickly with adequate treatment and rest.[^cite_note-102]

Additional side effects include extensive swelling of the vaccinated limb.[^cite_note-EU_safety_20220803-103]

## Pharmacology

Moderna's technology uses a [nucleoside-modified messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoside-modified_messenger_RNA) (modRNA) compound codenamed mRNA-1273. The mRNA-1273 drug delivery system uses a [PEGylated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGylation) [lipid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liposome) [nanoparticle drug delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoparticle_drug_delivery) (LNP) system.[^cite_note-nylj1-104] Once the compound is inside a human cell, the mRNA links up with the cell's [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum). The mRNA-1273 is encoded to trigger the cell into making a specific protein using the cell's normal manufacturing process. The vaccine encodes a version of the spike protein with a modification called 2P, in which the protein includes two stabilizing mutations in which the original amino acids are replaced with [prolines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proline), developed by researchers at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) and the [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](1013155.md)' [Vaccine Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine_Research_Center).[^cite_note-C\&EN092920-105][^cite_note-WAPO120620-106][^cite_note-2020-NatGeo-107] Once the protein is expelled from the cell, it is eventually detected by the immune system, which begins generating efficacious antibodies.

## Manufacturing



An insulated shipping container with Moderna vaccine boxes ensconced by cold packs



Moderna vaccine being stored in a conventional medical freezer

Moderna is relying extensively on [contract manufacturing organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_manufacturing_organization) to scale up its vaccine manufacturing process. The first step of the process—synthesis of DNA [plasmids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmid) (to be used as a template for synthesis of mRNA)—has been handled by a contractor called Aldevron based in [Fargo, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota).[^cite_note-Springer-112] For the remainder of the process, Moderna contracted with [Lonza Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonza_Group) to manufacture the vaccine at facilities in [Portsmouth, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_New_Hampshire) in the United States, and in [Visp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visp) in Switzerland, and purchased the necessary lipid [excipients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excipient) from CordenPharma.[^cite_note-Mullin-113] Besides CMOs, Moderna also manufactures the vaccine at its own production facility in [Norwood, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwood,_Massachusetts).[^cite_note-Cote-114] Another manufacturing site for the vaccines for the market outside the U.S. (since the end of 2021) is in [Geleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geleen) in the Netherlands, produced by its manufacturing partner [Lonza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonza_Group).[^cite_note-115] Earlier, Lonza did produce the vaccine for the EU, U.K. and Canada at its site in Switzerland only, but had to cut projected deliveries to the U.K. and Canada earlier in 2021 due to production issues.[^cite_note-116]

For the tasks of filling and packaging vials ([fill and finish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fill_and_finish)), Moderna entered into contracts with [Catalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalent) in the United States and Laboratorios Farmacéuticos Rovi in Spain.[^cite_note-Mullin-113] In April 2021, Moderna expanded its agreement with Catalent to increase manufacturing output at the latter's plant in [Bloomington, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana). The expansion will allow Catalent to manufacture up to 400 vials per minute and fill an additional 80 million vials per year.[^cite_note-117] Later that month, Moderna announced its plans to spend billions of dollars to boost production of its vaccines, potentially tripling the output in 2022, claiming as well that it would make no less than 800 million doses in 2021. The increase in production is in part attributed to improvements made by the company in manufacturing methods.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

The Moderna news followed preliminary results from the [Pfizer](62304.md)-[BioNTech](64671486.md) vaccine candidate, [BNT162b2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNT162b2), with Moderna demonstrating similar efficacy, but requiring storage at the temperature of a standard medical refrigerator of 2–8 °C (36–46 °F) for up to thirty days or −20 °C (−4 °F) for up to four months, whereas the Pfizer-BioNTech candidate requires [ultracold freezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ULT_freezer) storage between −80 and −60 °C (−112 and −76 °F).[^cite_note-Vaccination_Storage-121][^cite_note-cnbc11-16-122] Low-income countries usually have [cold chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_chain) capacity for only standard refrigerator storage, not ultracold freezer storage.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] In February 2021, the restrictions on the Pfizer vaccine were relaxed when the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) updated the emergency use authorization (EUA) to permit undiluted frozen vials of the vaccine to be transported and stored at between −25 and −15 °C (−13 and 5 °F) for up to two weeks before use.[^cite_note-EUA_fact_sheet-99][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-Pfizer_PR_20210219-126] The Moderna vaccine should not be stored at a temperature below −50 °C (−58 °F).[^cite_note-127]

In November 2020, *[Nature](43427.md)* reported that "While it's possible that differences in LNP formulations or mRNA secondary structures could account for the thermostability differences \[between Moderna and BioNtech], many experts suspect both vaccine products will ultimately prove to have similar storage requirements and shelf lives under various temperature conditions."[^cite_note-nature1-128]

## History

In June 2025, the [European Medicines Agency](1427054.md) announced that they are making data from the marketing authorization applications for COVID‑19 mRNA vaccines [Comirnaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comirnaty) and Spikevax publicly available.[^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2][^cite_note-129]

### Original version

In January 2020, Moderna announced development of an [RNA vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_vaccine), codenamed mRNA-1273, to induce immunity to SARS-CoV-2.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-carey-131][^cite_note-everett-132]

Moderna received US\$955 million from the [Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_Advanced_Research_and_Development_Authority) (BARDA), an office of the [US Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services). BARDA funded 100% of the cost of bringing the vaccine to FDA licensure.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

The United States government provided \$2.5 billion in total funding for the Moderna COVID‑19 vaccine (mRNA-1273).[^cite_note-135] Private donors also made contributions to the vaccine's development. The [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton) COVID-19 Research Fund contributed \$1 million.[^cite_note-136]

#### Phase I–II clinical trials

In March 2020, the [phase I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research#Phase_I) human trial of mRNA-1273 began in partnership with the US [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](1013155.md).[^cite_note-137] In April, the US [Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_Advanced_Research_and_Development_Authority) (BARDA) allocated up to \$483 million for Moderna's vaccine development.[^cite_note-bigbet-138] Plans for a [phase II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research#Phase_II) dosing and efficacy trial to begin in May were approved by the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA).[^cite_note-keown-139] Moderna signed a partnership with Swiss vaccine manufacturer [Lonza Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonza_Group),[^cite_note-140] to supply 300 million doses per annum.[^cite_note-141]

In May 2020, Moderna began a [phase IIa clinical trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research) recruiting six hundred adult participants to assess safety and differences in [antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody) response to two doses of its candidate vaccine, mRNA-1273, a study expected to complete in 2021.[^cite_note-mod-04405076-142]

In July 2020, Moderna scientists published preliminary results of the phase I dose escalation clinical trial of mRNA-1273, showing dose-dependent induction of neutralizing antibodies against S1/S2 as early as 15 days post-injection. Mild to moderate [adverse reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_effect), such as [fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever), fatigue, headache, [muscle ache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myalgia), and pain at the injection site were observed in all dose groups, but were common with increased dosage.[^cite_note-jackson-110] The vaccine in low doses was deemed safe and effective in order to advance a [phase III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research#Phase_III) clinical trial using two 100-μg doses administered 29 days apart.[^cite_note-jackson-110]

In July 2020, Moderna announced in a preliminary report that its [Operation Warp Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Warp_Speed) candidate had led to production of [neutralizing antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutralizing_antibody) in healthy adults in [phase I clinical testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research).[^cite_note-jackson-110][^cite_note-143] "At the 100-microgram dose, the one Moderna is advancing into larger trials, all 15 patients experienced side effects, including fatigue, chills, headache, muscle pain, and pain at the site of injection."[^cite_note-mhdg-144] The troublesome higher doses were discarded in July from future studies.[^cite_note-mhdg-144]

In September 2021, a study funded by the [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](1013155.md) reported a strong immune response after six months, even at low doses, suggesting that more doses could be deployed from a limited vaccine supply. Six months after low-dose vaccination, 67% of participants still had [memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_T_cell) [cytotoxic T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytotoxic_T_cell), suggesting that immune memory is stable. The study also found that [cross-reactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-reactivity) T cells acquired during infection with other coronaviruses that cause the common cold increased the response to the vaccine.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

#### Phase III clinical trials

Moderna and the [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](1013155.md) began a phase III trial in the US in July 2020, with a plan to enroll and assign thirty-thousand volunteers to two groups – one group receiving two 100-μg doses of mRNA-1273 vaccine and the other receiving a [placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo) of 0.9% sodium chloride.[^cite_note-palca-147] As of 7 August, more than 4,500 volunteers had enrolled.

In September 2020, Moderna published the detailed study plan for the clinical trial.[^cite_note-148] In September 2020, CEO Stéphane Bancel said that, if the trial is successful, the vaccine might be available to the public as early as late March or early April 2021.[^cite_note-149] As of October 2020, Moderna had completed the enrollment of 30,000 participants needed for its phase III trial.[^cite_note-150] The US National Institutes of Health announced in November 2020, that overall trial results were positive.[^cite_note-151]

Since September 2020, Moderna has used [Roche Diagnostics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche_Diagnostics)' Elecsys Anti-SARS-CoV-2 S test, authorized by the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) under an emergency use authorization (EUA) in November 2020. According to an independent supplier of clinical assays in microbiology, "this will facilitate the quantitative measurement of SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and help to establish a correlation between vaccine-induced protection and levels of anti-receptor binding domain (RBD) antibodies." The partnership was announced by Roche on 9 December 2020.[^cite_note-152]

A review by the FDA in December 2020, of interim results of the phase III clinical trial on mRNA-1273 showed it to be safe and effective against COVID‑19 infection resulting in the issuance of an EUA by the FDA.[^cite_note-nih-eua-153]

In February 2021, results from [phase III clinical trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research#Phase_III) were published in *[the New England Journal of Medicine](486492.md)*, indicating 94% efficacy in preventing COVID‑19 infection.[^cite_note-Baden-55][^cite_note-cnbc11-16-122][^cite_note-NYT-20201120-154] [Side effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_effect) included flu-like symptoms, such as pain at the injection site, fatigue, [muscle pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myalgia), and headache.[^cite_note-cnbc11-16-122] The clinical trial is ongoing and is set to conclude in late 2022.[^cite_note-NCT04470427-155]

Pregnant and breastfeeding women were also excluded from the initial trials used to obtain the emergency use authorization,[^cite_note-156] though trials in those populations were expected to be performed in 2021.[^cite_note-157]

In March 2021, in order to increase the span of vaccination beyond adults, Moderna started the clinical trials of vaccines on children age 6-months to 11-years-old in the US and in Canada (KidCove),[^cite_note-158] in addition to the existing and fully-enrolled study on 12-17 year-olds (TeenCOVE).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-Moderna_PR_20210805-68]

Additional trials were completed—NCT04796896,[^cite_note-160] NCT04649151,[^cite_note-161] and NCT05436834.[^cite_note-162]

In October 2022, the FDA amended the authorization for the bivalent booster to cover people aged six years of age and older.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20221012-216] In December 2022, the FDA amended the authorization for the bivalent booster to cover people aged six months and older.[^cite_note-217]

#### XBB.1.5 monovalent vaccine

In September 2023, the FDA approved an updated a monovalent (single) component Omicron variant [XBB.1.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XBB.1.5) version of the vaccine (Spikevax 2023-2024 formula) as a single dose for individuals aged twelve years of age and older;[^cite_note-FDA_Spikevax-36] and authorized the Moderna COVID-19 Vaccine 2023-2024 formula under emergency use for individuals aged 6 months through 11 years of age.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20230911-218][^cite_note-219] The updated version was tested in a small human trial of 101 participants; 50 received the monovalent XBB.1.5 version, compared to 51 who received a version containing XBB.1.5, BA.4 and BA.5. All participants had previously received four doses of older formulations of the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine. The safety profile of the authorized XBB.1.5 was found to be "consistent with previously authorized vaccines."[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] The approvals and emergency authorizations for the bivalent version of the vaccine were revoked.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20230911-218] Health Canada authorized the Moderna Spikevax COVID-19 vaccine (Omicron XBB.1.5 subvariant) (andusomeran) in September 2023.[^cite_note-CA_COVID-19_authorizations-5][^cite_note-CA_Spikevax-23][^cite_note-222] The MHRA approved the use of the Moderna (Spikevax) XBB.1.5 vaccine in September 2023.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224]

#### JN.1 monovalent vaccine

In September 2024, the UK's [Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicines_and_Healthcare_products_Regulatory_Agency) (MHRA) approved Moderna's JN.1-adapted COVID-19 vaccine for use in adults and children aged six months and older.[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227]

In September 2024, the European Union authorized the Spikevax JN.1 vaccine.[^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2][^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]

In September 2024, Swissmedic authorized the Spikevax JN.1 vaccine.[^cite_note-231]

In September 2024, the Taiwan Food & Drug Administration authorized the Spikevax JN.1 vaccine.[^cite_note-232]

#### KP.2 monovalent vaccine

In August 2024, the FDA approved and granted emergency authorization for a monovalent Omicron KP.2 version of the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20240822-233] The approval of Spikevax (COVID-19 Vaccine, mRNA) (2024-2025 Formula) was granted to ModernaTX Inc. and the EUA amendment for the Moderna COVID-19 Vaccine (2024-2025 Formula) was issued to ModernaTX Inc.[^cite_note-FDA_PR_20240822-233][^cite_note-234]

## Society and culture

About 155 million doses of the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine, including about 3.1 million doses in children and adolescents (below 18 years of age) were administered in the EU/[EEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area) from authorization to 26 June 2022.[^cite_note-EU_safety_20220803-103]

### Brand names

mRNA-1273 was the code name during development and testing,[^cite_note-Baden-55][^cite_note-pmid33382675-170] elasomeran is the [international nonproprietary name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_nonproprietary_name) (INN),[^cite_note-AU_COVID-19_vaccines_regulatory_status-11][^cite_note-TGA_determination-235][^cite_note-recommended_INN:_list_86-236] and Spikevax is the [brand name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_name).[^cite_note-Spikevax_FDA_label-1][^cite_note-Spikevax_EPAR-2]

Davesomeran is the INN for the BA.5 variant in the bivalent version of the vaccine.[^cite_note-AU_COVID-19_vaccines_regulatory_status-11][^cite_note-237][^cite_note-recommended_INN:_list_88-238] Andusomeran is the INN for the XBB 1.5 variant version of the vaccine.[^cite_note-AU_COVID-19_vaccines_regulatory_status-11][^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240]

### Economics



[Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris), Vice President of the United States, receiving her second dose of the Moderna vaccination in January 2021.

In June 2020, [Singapore](27318.md) signed a pre-purchase agreement for Moderna, reportedly paying a price premium in order to secure early stock of vaccines, although the government declined to provide the actual price and quantity, citing commercial sensitivities and confidentiality clauses.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242]

In August 2020, the US government signed an agreement to buy 100 million doses of Moderna's anticipated vaccine,[^cite_note-243] which the *[Financial Times](136566.md)* said Moderna planned to price at US\$50–60 per course.[^cite_note-244] In November 2020, Moderna said it will charge governments who purchase its vaccine between US\$25 and US\$37 per dose while the EU is seeking a price of under US\$25 per dose for the 160 million doses it plans to purchase from Moderna.[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246]

In 2020, Moderna obtained purchase agreements for mRNA-1273 with the European Union for 160 million doses and with Canada for up to 56 million doses.[^cite_note-ec-247][^cite_note-pmo-248] In December 2020, a [tweet](9988187.md) by the [Belgium Budget State Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Budget_\(Belgium\)) revealed the E.U. would pay US\$18 per dose, while *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that the US would pay US\$15 per dose.[^cite_note-249]

Moderna reported revenue of US\$200 million from its COVID‑19 vaccine in 2020,[^cite_note-Moderna_Annual_Report_2020-250] and \$17.7 billion in 2021.[^cite_note-Moderna_Annual_Report_2021-251]

### Paused vaccinations

Out of concern that the vaccine may increase the risk of [myocarditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocarditis) in young people under age 30, Finland, Sweden, Germany, and France recommended Moderna vaccinations not be used for this age group in October/November 2021.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255][^cite_note-taylor-256]

In May 2020, after releasing partial and non-peer-reviewed results for only eight of 45 candidates in a preliminary pre-phase I stage human trial directly to financial markets, the CEO announced on [CNBC](223441.md) an immediate \$1.25 billion rights issue to raise funds for the company, at a \$30 billion valuation,[^cite_note-257] while *[Stat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stat_\(website\))* said, "Vaccine experts say Moderna didn't produce data critical to assessing COVID‑19 vaccine."[^cite_note-258]

In July 2020, disputes between Moderna and government scientists over the company's unwillingness to share data from the clinical trials were revealed.[^cite_note-reuters6-259]

Moderna also faced criticism for failing to recruit people of color in clinical trials.[^cite_note-260]

In August 2021, the [US Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services) announced a plan to offer a [booster dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booster_dose) eight months after the second dose, citing evidence of reduced protection against mild and moderate disease and the possibility of reduced protection against severe disease, hospitalization, and death.[^cite_note-261] Scientists and the WHO reaffirmed the lack of evidence on the need for a booster dose for healthy people and that the vaccine remains effective against severe disease months after administration.[^cite_note-nature-boosters-who-position-262] In a statement, the WHO and [SAGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Advisory_Group_of_Experts) said that, while protection against infection may be diminished, protection against severe disease will likely be retained due to [cell-mediated immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-mediated_immunity).[^cite_note-who-statement-boosters-263] Research into optimal timing for boosters is still ongoing, and a booster too early may lead to less robust protection.[^cite_note-264]

#### Misinformation

See also: [COVID-19 misinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_misinformation)

Videos on [video-sharing platforms](5897742.md) circulated around May 2021 showing people having [magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnets) stick to their arms after receiving the vaccine, purportedly demonstrating the [conspiracy theory that vaccines contain microchips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_misinformation#Vaccines), but these videos have been debunked.[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268]

In November 2021, a White House correspondent for the conservative outlet [Newsmax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsmax) falsely tweeted that the Moderna vaccine contained [luciferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciferase) "so that you can be tracked."[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270]

### Patent litigation

The [PEGylated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGylation) [lipid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liposome) [nanoparticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoparticle_drug_delivery) (LNP) drug delivery system of mRNA-1273 has been the subject of ongoing patent litigation with [Arbutus Biopharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbutus_Biopharma), from whom Moderna had previously licensed LNP technology.[^cite_note-nylj1-104][^cite_note-271] On 4 September 2020, *[Nature Biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Biotechnology)* reported that Moderna had lost a key challenge in the ongoing case.[^cite_note-272]
