The **prime minister of the Republic of Iraq** is the foremost executive of the Iraqi government and the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [Iraqi Armed Forces](4627429.md). The premier is responsible for the general policy of the state and directs the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Iraq\)), with the power to dismiss and name any senior executive, including ministers and generals.[^cite_note-3] In addition to the MoD armed forces, the premier has direct authority over all of those intelligence and security agencies under the jurisdiction of the Office of the Commander in Chief, such as the [CTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Counter_Terrorism_Service) and the [INIS](1012921.md).

Under the informally adopted ethno-sectarian quota system known as *Muhasasa*, the position of prime minister is reserved for a [Shia Muslim](22350732.md).[^cite_note-Arab_Gulf_States_Institute-4]

On 14 May 2026, [Ali al-Zaidi](83052003.md) became the prime minister.

## History

The prime minister was originally an appointed office, subsidiary to the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), and the nominal leader of the [Iraqi parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_parliament). Under the [2005 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Iraq) the prime minister is the country's active [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) authority. [Nouri al-Maliki](4723094.md) (formerly Jawad al-Maliki) was selected to be prime minister on 21 April 2006.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] On 14 August 2014, al-Maliki agreed to step down as prime minister of [Iraq](7515928.md) to allow [Haider al-Abadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haider_al-Abadi) to take his place.[^cite_note-7] On 25 October 2018, [Adil Abdul-Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adil_Abdul-Mahdi) was sworn into office five months after the [2018 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Iraqi_parliamentary_election) until his resignation in 2019.[^cite_note-8] He was once again appointed, this time as a caretaker prime minister due to political dispute. Abdul-Mahdi was replaced by [Mustafa Al-Kadhimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Al-Kadhimi), who was approved by the parliament on 7 May 2020.[^cite_note-9] Al-Kadhimi was replaced by Al-Sudani after the [2021 Iraqi parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iraqi_parliamentary_election).

## Appointment

After an election, the [Council of Representatives](1445450.md) elects the [Presidency Council of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_Council_of_Iraq), including the president of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Iraq\)). The Presidency Council must then name a prime minister unanimously within two weeks. If it fails to do so, then the responsibility of naming the prime minister reverts to the Council of Representatives. In that event, the Council of Representatives must confirm the nomination by an absolute majority. If the prime minister is unable to nominate his Council of Ministers within one month, the Presidency Council must name another prime minister.

## Agencies directly subordinate

The [Counter Terrorism Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Counter_Terrorism_Service), [National Intelligence Service](1012921.md), [National Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_National_Security_Service?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Popular Mobilization Commission](45671374.md) report to the prime minister directly. The Iraqi CTB oversees the Iraqi Counter Terrorism Command, a formation that includes all [Iraqi Special Operations Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Special_Operations_Forces). In June 2009, there were ongoing efforts to make the Iraqi CTB a separate ministry.[^cite_note-10]

## Seat

The prime minister's office is located in the [Al Zaqura Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Zaqura_Building) in the [Green Zone](856542.md), Baghdad.
