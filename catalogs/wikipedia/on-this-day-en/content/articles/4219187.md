The **International Criminal Court Act 2001** (c. 17) is an [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament_\(United_Kingdom\)) of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](13964.md). The Act incorporates into [English law](85441.md) and [Northern Ireland law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_law) the [Rome Statute](68762.md) of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md).[^cite_note-4]

The principal aims of the Act are:[^cite_note-5]

- to incorporate into domestic law the offences contained in the Rome Statute ([genocide](12441.md), [war crimes](48626.md) and [crimes against humanity](61099.md));

  [^cite_note-6]

- to fulfill the United Kingdom's obligations under the Statute, particularly in relation to the arrest and surrender of persons wanted by the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) (ICC) and the provision of assistance with respect to ICC investigations; and

- to create a legal framework so that persons convicted by the ICC can serve prison sentences in the United Kingdom.

In 2006, three British military personnel were charged with inhumane treatment, a war crime, under the Act.[^cite_note-7] Two of the three soldiers were cleared but the third, Corporal [Donald Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Payne_\(soldier\)), became the first British person to be convicted of a war crime under this Act, when he admitted to inhumanly treating [Baha Mousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baha_Mousa).[^cite_note-8] The first prosecution for crimes against humanity under the Act occurred in 2026 when a former member of the [Syrian Air Force Intelligence Directorate](19343088.md) was charged with murder and conduct ancillary to murder.[^cite_note-9]

The corresponding [Act of the Scottish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_the_Scottish_Parliament) is the [International Criminal Court (Scotland) Act 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_\(Scotland\)_Act_2001?action=edit\&redlink=1) (asp 13).

This section was inserted by section 1 (The Special Court for Sierra Leone) of the act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom called the **International Tribunals (Sierra Leone) Act 2007** (c. 7).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The [International Tribunals (Sierra Leone) (Application of Provisions) Order 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tribunals_\(Sierra_Leone\)_\(Application_of_Provisions\)_Order_2007?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([SI 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_2007)/2140) was made under this section.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

This act and the order made under it authorised the detention of [Charles Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Taylor_\(Liberian_politician\))[^cite_note-17] in the UK,[^cite_note-18] but their application is not confined to him.[^cite_note-19]
