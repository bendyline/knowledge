**Australia–Japan relations** are the [bilateral relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_relations) between the [Commonwealth of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Australia) and [Japan](15573.md).



Embassy of Australia in [Tokyo](30057.md)



Embassy of Japan in Canberra

Geographically, the two countries are situated in the [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific) region, with neither country sharing land borders with any other nation.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The relationship is generally warm and has continued to grow and strengthen over the years, particularly in relation to defence and security. Both nations are [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) [constitutional monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) with close ties to the Western world. Additionally, Australia and Japan are both close allies of the [United States](3434750.md), and play an important role in the regional security architecture, as well as the rules-based international order. Both countries are heavily dependent on international trade, with Japan relying on imports of Australian [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Australia) and [energy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_Australia), and Australia importing significant quantities of Japanese [end-products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_product) and [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_good). Australian troops constituted the majority of [Commonwealth Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth_Occupation_Force) which [occupied large parts of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan) following the [Second World War](32927.md). In turn, Australia has an extensive history of Japanese influence, with successive waves of immigrants contributing to the social and cultural fabric of modern Australia. Consequently, Australia and Japan have developed a substantial bilateral relationship anchored by mutual interests and reinforced by strong historical, cultural, and people-to-people ties. Today, Japan is Australia's “second largest trading partner and an increasingly important source of capital investment". The relationship continues to expand beyond economic and commercial ties to other spheres, including cultural exchanges, tourism, defence and scientific cooperation.[^cite_note-3]

At the beginning of the 20th century, Japan and Australia enjoyed cordial relations, and fought on the same side in the [First World War](4764461.md). Early points of contention centred on discrimination against Japanese migrants vis-à-vis the [White Australia policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Australia_policy). [Japanese expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshin-ron) and [colonial ambitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_colonial_empire) further strained relations, with many Australians fearing the possibility of a [Japanese invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Japanese_invasion_of_Australia_during_World_War_II). Diplomatic relations were disrupted and considerably damaged by the Second World War; in particular, [Japanese air raids on Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_air_raids_on_Australia) and Japan’s [treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_war_crimes) of Australian and allied [prisoners of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_prisoners_of_war_of_Japan). Australian suspicion of Japan remained elevated following the re-establishment of diplomatic ties in the early 1950s, and was exacerbated by Japan's perceived economic domination throughout the latter half of the century.[^cite_note-4] However, by the 1980s and early 1990s, Australian government and business leaders had come to see Japan as a vital export market and an essential element in Australia's future growth and prosperity in the Asia-Pacific region. Japan, for its part, regards Australia as an important partner, a reliable source of energy, minerals and other primary products, a popular tourist destination, and the only other middle-ranking economic power in the Asia-Pacific. In 2013, Australia's former Prime Minister [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott) hailed Japan as Australia's closest friend in Asia and proceeded with a [free trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan-Australia_Economic_Partnership_Agreement) (FTA) between the two nations in the following year.[^cite_note-5] Australia and Japan acknowledge one another as key strategic partners in the Asia-Pacific, with both being prosperous [liberal democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracies) and key allies of the United States. Former [Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Minister_of_Australia) [Marise Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marise_Payne) described Japan as a "key partner" in the region; for his part, [Japanese Prime Minister](24833.md) [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) described the relationship as the linchpin of security in the Indo-Pacific.

Australia maintains an embassy in [Tokyo](30057.md), and a consulate-general in [Osaka](52230.md).[^cite_note-6] Japan maintains an embassy in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), consulates-general in [Sydney](27862.md), [Melbourne](17306237.md), [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane), and [Perth](24355.md), along with a consulate in [Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairns).[^cite_note-7] Both countries are members of regional groupings such as the [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation) and [Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_and_Progressive_Agreement_for_Trans-Pacific_Partnership).[^cite_note-8]

## History

### Prehistory

Anthropologists have noted that Japanese [Jomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomon_period) skulls and the skulls of [Ainu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainu_people) (descendants of the Jomon) shared certain physical traits with [Indigenous Australians](12598742.md) more than they did with modern [East Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asians), like the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) or the later [Yayoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period) Japanese settlers.[^cite_note-9] Fossils found in [Okinawa](22477.md) (dated around 20,000 years ago) were theorized to be closely related to Australia’s [Keilor remains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keilor_archaeological_site), suggesting a common ancestor.[^cite_note-10]

### Colonial period

Between 1635 and 1854, Japanese citizens were in a self-imposed period of isolation from the outside world, which limited their knowledge of it. Australia began appearing on Japanese maps towards the end of the 18th century, which was prior to its colonization by the British. Up until 1794, the Japanese drew Australia as being double its actual size, with [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea) still attached.[^cite_note-japand-11] A 1791 [Russian](25391.md) map with Australia depicted correctly reached Japan in 1792, and influenced their later maps. This Russian map arrived in Japan through [Daikokuya Kōdayū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daikokuya_K%C5%8Dday%C5%AB), who came into contact with Russians in 1783, after getting shipwrecked on [Amchitka Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amchitka_Island) in the North Pacific (part of [Alaska](624.md)'s [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands), then controlled by Russia).[^cite_note-japand-11] Kōdayū lived among Russians and the native [Aleuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleut), who enacted an uprising in 1784, before eventually being returned home to Japan in October 1792.[^cite_note-12]

The first recorded import of Australian coal by Japan occurred in 1865, and the first recorded Japanese imports of Australian wool occurred in 1888.[^cite_note-dfat.gov.au-13] The first Japanese person known to have settled in Australia was a merchant who migrated to Queensland in 1871. By the start of the [Australian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Federation) in 1901, it was estimated that Australia had 4000 Japanese immigrants, mostly based around [Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville) where the Japanese government had established its first consulate in 1896. The immigrants worked mostly in the sugar cane and maritime industries including turtle, [trochus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trochus), [trepang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber) and pearl harvesting.[^cite_note-kodansha-14]

The Japanese government established a second consulate in Sydney in 1897, which was upgraded to the status of consulate-general in 1902.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorikawa20206–7-15] In the 19th century and early 20th century, the Japanese used the term *Nan’yō* (South Sea) to refer to Australia and neighboring areas such as [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia) and [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-16] By the 1920s, the term evolved into two distinct subgroupings that divided the space into inner and outer parts: the inner or *UchiNan’yō* referred to the Micronesian island groups encompassing [Palau](23372.md), the [Caroline Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Islands), the [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands), the [Marshall Islands](19147.md), and the [Gilbert Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Islands); the outer or *UraNan’yō* took on a wider expanse of territory, extending into the Indonesian archipelago and stretching as far as Australia.[^cite_note-17]

### Post-Federation (1901–1941)

Following the [federation of the Australian colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) in 1901, the new federal government formally enacted the [White Australia policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Australia_policy) with the aim of barring non-European peoples, including the Japanese, from immigrating to Australia. Hisakichi Eitaki, the Japanese consul in Sydney, lodged a formal protest to Prime Minister [Edmund Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Barton) over the proposed *[Immigration Restriction Act 1901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Restriction_Act_1901)* and asked that Japanese citizens be exempted from the [dictation test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictation_test) requirements as "the Japanese belong to an Empire whose standard of civilization is so much higher than that of [Kanakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanakas), Negroes, Pacific Islanders, Indians, or other Eastern peoples".[^cite_note-18]

In 1904, the [Watson government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_government) introduced the so-called "passport agreement", whereby Japanese citizens were exempted from the dictation test if they bore a valid passport specifying the purpose and duration of their visits. This arrangement was similar to the [Gentlemen's Agreement of 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentlemen's_Agreement_of_1907) later established between the United States and Japan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorikawa20209-19] In 1905, the [Deakin government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deakin_government_\(1905%E2%80%931908\)) passed the *Immigration Restriction Amendment Act 1905*, which amended the provisions of the dictation test to remove the reference to "any European language" to "any prescribed language". While the amended legislation did not contain any special provisions for Japanese citizens, it was seen as a concession to the Japanese following the Japanese victory in the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War) which reinforced Japan's military equality with European powers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorikawa202012-20] However, this was only a minor concession as it "made no difference to the Commonwealth's basic policy of excluding Japanese permanent settlers from Australia".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorikawa202013-21]

Both countries were among the victorious [allied powers of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_powers_of_World_War_I). At the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)), which resulted in the creation of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations), Australian prime minister [Billy Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Hughes) strongly opposed the Japanese government's [Racial Equality Proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_Equality_Proposal).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECuffe202117–18-22] Hughes believed the proposal was a ploy for the Japanese to send migrants to New Guinea (which Australia controlled at the time) as part of their southward expansion efforts. After World War I, the Japanese had gained control of most Micronesian islands from Germany, and Hughes viewed New Guinea as being an important buffer between these areas and Australia.[^cite_note-Iwamoto1996-23]

In 1930–31, Japan was "Australia's third most important trading partner".[^cite_note-dfat.gov.au-13] Economic relations continued to flourish, and by the mid-1930s, Japan had become Australia's second largest export market after the [United Kingdom](31717.md).

Relations with Japan were the primary focus of the [Australian Eastern Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Eastern_Mission) of 1934, a diplomatic tour of East and South-East Asia led by deputy prime minister [John Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Greig_Latham).[^cite_note-24] Latham met with Japanese foreign minister [Kōki Hirota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dki_Hirota) in May 1934 and covered a range of topics, including the [Manchurian question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo) and Japanese readmission to the League of Nations. This meeting marked the first direct interaction between government ministers of the two countries and has been described by one writer as "one of the most important in Australian diplomatic history".[^cite_note-25]

### World War II (1941–1945)

During [World War II](32927.md), Australian territory was [directly threatened by Japanese invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Japanese_invasion_of_Australia_during_World_War_II), and Japanese forces [attacked Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Darwin_\(February_1942\)), [Broome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Broome), and [Sydney Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Sydney_Harbour). Australia represented Japan's southernmost region of operation in the [Pacific theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War), which covered an area of the Pacific Ocean extending as far north as the Aleutian Islands.[^cite_note-26] In 1941, the ethnic Japanese population in Australia was interned, and most were deported to Japan at the end of the war.[^cite_note-27] Australian forces played an active combat role in battles throughout the [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_theatre_of_World_War_II) and [South West Pacific theater of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Pacific_theater_of_World_War_II), most notable events of the war among both parties were the [Kokoda Track campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokoda_Track_campaign) and the [Sandakan Death Marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Death_Marches) (of which in 2014, the then Japanese Prime Minister [Shinzo Abe](2634434.md) offered his sincere condolences on behalf of the Japanese people to the Australian Parliament).[^cite_note-28] Australian forces also played a significant role in the post-war [Occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan).

### During the Occupation period



30 August 1945. [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka,_Kanagawa) Naval Base, [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay). Commander Yuzo Tanno hands over the keys of the Yokosuka Naval Base to Captain Herbert James Buchanan, [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md). Buchanan led the first British Commonwealth party to go ashore in Japan.

The first time a large number of Australians were in Japan was during the postwar [Occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan). Australians were part of the [British Commonwealth Occupation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth_Occupation_Force). Around 16,000 Australians served in the force. For the entire length of its history the BCOF had an Australian officer. The Australian contribution to the force was 4,700 infantry, 5,300 base personnel, 2,200 from the [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force), and 130 from the Australian General Hospital. The [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) was also present as part of the [British Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pacific_Fleet). For two-thirds of the period of occupation the Commonwealth was represented solely by Australians.[^cite_note-29]



General Blamey signing the Japanese instrument of surrender on behalf of Australia

Australia played a minor role in the [Japan campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_campaign) in the last months of the war and was preparing to participate in the [invasion of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Downfall) at the time the war ended. Several Australian warships operated with the [British Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pacific_Fleet) (BPF) during the [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) and Australian destroyers later escorted British aircraft carriers and battleships during attacks on targets in the [Japanese home islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_archipelago).[^cite_note-30] Despite its distance from Japan, Australia was the BPF's main base and a large number of facilities were built to support the fleet.[^cite_note-31]

Australia's participation in the planned invasion of Japan would have involved elements of all three services fighting as part of Commonwealth forces. It was planned to form a new [10th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Division_\(Australia\)) from existing AIF personnel which would form part of the [Commonwealth Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Corps) with British, [Canadian](5042916.md) and [New Zealand](4913064.md) units. The corps' organisation was to be identical to that of a US Army corps, and it would have participated in the invasion of the Japanese home island of [Honshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honsh%C5%AB) which was scheduled for March 1946.[^cite_note-32] Australian ships would have operated with the BPF and US Pacific Fleet and two RAAF heavy bomber squadrons and a transport squadron were scheduled to be redeployed from Britain to Okinawa to join the [strategic bombardment of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Japan) as part of [Tiger Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Force_\(air\)).[^cite_note-33]

General Blamey signed the [Japanese Instrument of Surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender) on behalf of Australia during the ceremony held on board [USS *Missouri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Missouri_\(BB-63\)) on 2 September 1945.[^cite_note-34] Several RAN warships were [among the Allied ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Allied_ships_at_the_Japanese_surrender) anchored in [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay) during the proceedings.[^cite_note-35] Following the main ceremony on board *Missouri*, Japanese field commanders surrendered to Allied forces across the Pacific theatre. Australian forces accepted the surrender of their Japanese opponents at ceremonies conducted at Morotai, several locations in Borneo, Timor, Wewak, Rabaul, Bougainville and Nauru.[^cite_note-36]

### Late 20th century



Attendees at the 1972 Australia-Japan Ministerial Meeting in Canberra: [Nigel Bowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Bowen), [John McEwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEwen), [Yasuhiro Nakasone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuhiro_Nakasone), and [Doug Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Anthony).

Diplomatic relations between Australia and Japan were re-established in 1952, following the termination of the [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) occupation, and [Haruhiko Nishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruhiko_Nishi) was appointed as Japanese ambassador to Australia. In 1957, Australian Prime Minister [Robert Menzies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Menzies) visited Japan with the aim of strengthening economic and political ties between the two countries.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In Australia, trade minister [John McEwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEwen) led a push for closer economic ties with Japan in the early 1950s, seeing the resumption with trade as important for Australian producers as Australia sought new markets outside the pre-war framework of [Imperial Preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Preference).[^cite_note-39] After years of negotiations, McEwen and his Japanese counterpart [Kishi Nobusuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishi_Nobusuke) signed the Japan–Australia Commerce Agreement (JACA) in July 1957, with each country conferring [most favoured nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_favoured_nation) status on the other and Australia providing a commitment to revoke its Article 35 exception to the [General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Agreement_on_Tariffs_and_Trade) (GATT) which had allowed it to discriminate against Japan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGolding1996194-40] The removal of the Article 35 exception eventually occurred in 1960 after McEwen secured Japanese concessions on imports of Australian beef. The final discriminatory trade provisions were removed in a new agreement signed in 1963.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGolding1996197–198-41] The JACA "ushered in a new era of Australian trade which would make Japan immeasurably Australia's biggest trading partner".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGolding1996195-42]

In 1976, Australia and Japan signed the [Basic Treaty of Friendship and Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Treaty_of_Friendship_and_Cooperation), which established a broad framework of principles to guide and enhance future bilateral relations in the political, economic, cultural and other fields. The treaty was historically significant as the first comprehensive treaty of its kind for both countries.[^cite_note-\:9-43] The first proposal for a comprehensive treaty had been raised by the Japanese delegation in May 1970 at the eighth annual meeting of the Australia-Japan Business Cooperation Committee (AJBCC).[^cite_note-44] A more formal request was raised in October at the Australia-Japan Official Level Talks and then reaffirmed by the Japanese Ambassador in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) in 1971 who asked that the prospect of a treaty be "seriously looked at".[^cite_note-45] Formal negotiations took place over a three-year period and encompassed multiple Australian and Japanese governments.[^cite_note-46]

By the late 1980s, Australia and Japan had moved beyond a purely bilateral trade relationship to become among the primary architects of Asia-Pacific regionalism, a collaboration that culminated in the 1989 establishment of the [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation) (APEC) forum. Prior to the formation of APEC, Japanese and Australian leaders had already collaborated on the founding of three previous regional institutions: the [Pacific Basin Economic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Basin_Economic_Council) (PBEC), the Pacific Trade and Development (PAFTAD) forum and the [Pacific Economic Cooperation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Economic_Cooperation_Council) (PECC). The initial public proposal for APEC was delivered by Australian Prime Minister [Bob Hawke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hawke) during a state visit to [Seoul](19159283.md) in January 1989, and given institutional form a mere 9 months later,[^cite_note-47] since becoming a quintessential example of effective Australian [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power) diplomacy. The structural blueprint for the forum had already been co-developed by Japan's Ministry of International Trade and Industry (MITI). It has been claimed that Japan deliberately chose to act as a "silent partner" in the foundation of APEC, and encouraged Australia to take the lead because a Japanese-led proposal might have triggered regional anxieties regarding Japan's wartime history and economic dominance.[^cite_note-48] APEC initially consisted of 12 countries from across the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim), including Australia, [Brunei Darussalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei_Darussalam), Canada, Indonesia, Japan, the [Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea), [Malaysia](3607937.md), New Zealand, the [Philippines](23440.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Thailand](30128.md), and the United States. By 1998, it had expanded to 21 Pacific Rim countries, including [China](5405.md), Russia, [Papua New Guinea](22943.md) and countries in [Latin America](18524.md). However, no new members have been admitted since 1998, in order to focus on managing the already broad array of countries.[^cite_note-49]

A number of Australian politicians have been awarded the [Order of the Rising Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Rising_Sun), the first national decoration awarded by the Japanese government. Recipients include former Prime Ministers of Australia such as [Edmund Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Barton), [Robert Menzies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Menzies), [John McEwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEwen), [Malcolm Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Fraser), Bob Hawke and [John Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Australia and Japan have agreed to work together towards the reform of the [United Nations](31769.md), including the realisation of Japan's permanent membership of the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council), and to strengthen various regional forums, including not only APEC, but also the [ASEAN Regional Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Regional_Forum) (ARF) and the [East Asia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit) (EAS).

### 21st century

In March 2007, Australia and Japan signed a joint security pact.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The scope of security cooperation includes:

- Law enforcement on combating [transnational crime](6760775.md), including trafficking in illegal narcotics and precursors, people smuggling and trafficking, counterfeiting currency and arms smuggling

- Border security

- Counter-terrorism

- Disarmament and counter-proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and their means of delivery

- Peace operations

- Exchange of strategic assessments and related information

- Maritime and aviation security

- Humanitarian relief operations, including disaster relief

- Contingency planning, including for pandemics

During the deployment of the [Japan Self-Defense Forces](2785204.md) on a [humanitarian and reconstruction mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Iraq_Reconstruction_and_Support_Group) to [Iraq](7515928.md) from 2004 to 2006, Australian units assisted Japanese Special Forces in the protection of Japanese bases.

Diplomatic relations have come under pressure over ideological differences regarding Japan's scientific whaling program. In May 2010, Australia started legal action to halt Japanese [whale hunts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_hunt),[^cite_note-55] despite senior Australian officials and bureaucrats expressing the opinion that the legal action would likely fail.[^cite_note-56] Japan's repeated requests that Australia cease its support for Sea Shepherd's violent attacks upon its whaling fleet have been refused. Although in 2013, Foreign Minister [Julie Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Bishop) stated while on a diplomatic trip to Japan that the [Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia) does not officially support [Sea Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Shepherd_Conservation_Society) and disapproves of Sea Shepherd and their violent activities in halting whaling.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

This turned around in 2013 with the new [Abbott government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_government) calling Japan Australia's "closest friend" in Asia, when Japanese Prime Minister [Shinzo Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinz%C5%8D_Abe) visited Australia to conclude the [Japan–Australia Economic Partnership Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Australia_Economic_Partnership_Agreement) and to address the future of the relationship between Australia and Japan, being the first Japanese Prime Minister to address the [Parliament of Australia](302298.md).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

With the new [Turnbull government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnbull_government) starting in 2015, Prime Minister [Malcolm Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Turnbull) made a few changes to the bilateral relationship between Australia and Japan. This included a slight pullback in terms of the recent bid for the upgrade of the [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) submarine fleet in 2016, which the new government eventually decided on the French bid, therefore resulting in slight outcry from the [Japanese Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Abe_Cabinet); its worth noting that the previous Australian Prime Minister [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott) had closely hinted for his government to choose the Japanese bid over both the French and German bids.[^cite_note-61]

However, Prime Minister Turnbull decided to stimulate values from the previous Abbott government to his government by incorporating themes such as the "closest friend" in Asia. This was seen with increasing bilateral ties in terms of military co-operation, trade, and cultural friendship. In late 2016, Turnbull stopped by on a lighting trip to Tokyo and started to develop a close relationship with Prime Minister Shinzo Abe, following his predecessor's example. Abe later visited Turnbull in Sydney early 2017 during a pivot to [South-East Asia](28741.md), where both increased military, trade, cultural, and sporting ties. Both also discussed the [South China Sea Crisis](32005536.md), [North Korea](21255.md), and their anxiety to co-operate with their mutual ally, the [United States](3434750.md)' [new Trump Administration](52231341.md).[^cite_note-62]

In December 2021, Australia and Japan, along with the United States, pledged a new undersea internet cable for the Micronesian countries [Nauru](21302.md), [Kiribati](16674.md) and the [Federated States of Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia).[^cite_note-63] That year, the governments of Australia and Japan also decided to fund two major law enforcement developments in the Marshall Islands.[^cite_note-64]

At a virtual summit on 6 January 2022, Japanese Prime Minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) and Australian Prime Minister [Scott Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Morrison) formally signed the [Reciprocal Access Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_Access_Agreement) (RAA) - to allow their respective militaries to work seamlessly with each other on defence and humanitarian operations.[^cite_note-65]

During the 2022 Australia-Japan leaders meeting in [Perth](24355.md), Australian Prime Minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) and Japanese Prime Minister Fumio Kishida agreed to boost Japan's access to hydrogen, LNG and minerals to improve energy security while making a shared push to hit net zero. The two countries have also promised to help other [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) countries in their efforts to combat climate change. Albanese said that there was a common desire shared by Australia and Japan for peace in the Indo-Pacific. He also said that "We concurred that our special strategic partnership has risen to a new and higher level."[^cite_note-66]

In 2024 it was announced that members of the Japanese [Amphibious Rapid Deployment Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Rapid_Deployment_Brigade) would be stationed in [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), where they would serve alongside Australian and American forces.[^cite_note-67]

In March 2026, Australia and Japan celebrated 50 years of the Basic Treaty of Friendship and Cooperation. To honor the occasion, Japan's Ambassador to Australia Kazuhiro Suzuki delivered an address at the National Press Club in Canberra, titled "Girt by sea and in the same boat: 50 years of Japan-Australia relations and beyond".[^cite_note-68]



Australian Prime Minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) with Japanese Prime Minister [Sanae Takaichi](8125532.md) in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), 4 May 2026

On 4 May 2026, Albanese and [Japanese Prime Minister](24833.md) [Takaichi Sanae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takaichi_Sanae) signed four agreements in Canberra to strengthen bilateral cooperation in the areas of energy, defence and critical minerals and a joint declaration on economic security cooperation. The critical minerals agreement identified six rare earth strategic projects to diversify supply chains including the [Lynas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynas) rare earth project in [Kalgoorlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalgoorlie) and [Alcoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoa)'s Gallium Recovery Project. The two heads of government also discussed international issues in East Asia, the [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) and the Middle East.[^cite_note-69]

## Economic relations



Monthly value (A\$ millions) of merchandise imported to Australia from Japan since 1988



Monthly value (A\$ millions) of merchandise exported from Australia to Japan since 1988

Australian trade had shifted away from other [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) countries toward Asia around the 1960s and 1970s. Japan in particular had emerged as the leading trading partner. In 1966–67, Japan surpassed the United Kingdom "to become the largest market for Australian exports".[^cite_note-dfat.gov.au-13] Japan is now the second largest export market for Australia (after China), although Japan is ranked only third as a source of imports to Australia after the United States and China. Because of this, Australia has had a trade surplus with Japan.

As Japan protects its agriculture, Australia faces quotas, high tariffs, and standards barriers in exporting agricultural products including beef, butter, and apples to Japan. Japan is "Australia's largest beef export market, taking 35.8% of all beef shipped in 2011".[^cite_note-70]

Negotiations commenced in 2007 on a bilateral free trade agreement between Australia and Japan.[^cite_note-dfat.gov.au-13]

As Australia trades raw minerals to Japan for large amounts of earnings, while Japan trades technology such as televisions, computers and cars. Japan is one of the leading suppliers of a number of manufactured goods imported to Australia: Japan has been the principal source of cars and motorcycles being imported to Australia.[^cite_note-dfat.gov.au-13]

## Military

In April 2026, Australia and Japan signed contracts to provide the Australian navy with 11 [*Improved Mogami*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_FFM).[^cite_note-71]

## Migration

According to [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics) data from 2006, 40,968 Australian people claimed Japanese ancestry.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] According to Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, there were approximately 9,900, [Australian citizens resident in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australians_in_Japan) as of 30 June 2017.[^cite_note-mofa-74]

## Tourism

In 2018, Japan was the fifth largest source of tourism for Australia, with 484,000 Japanese citizens visiting Australia that year. It placed behind the United Kingdom, the United States, New Zealand and China.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Visitors from Japan to Australia peaked at 814,000 in 1997.[^cite_note-77] According to data from [Google](1092923.md), Japan was Australia's top-searched destination between 2002 and 2022, ranking ahead of New Zealand.[^cite_note-78]

In 2023, Japan became the fourth most-visited overseas destination for Australians behind New Zealand, Indonesia and India.[^cite_note-79] That year, it ranked as the eight largest source of tourism to Japan, with nearly one million of the 36.8 million visitors that year being from Australia.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

## Multilateral organizations

Both nations are members of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation, [Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_and_Progressive_Agreement_for_Trans-Pacific_Partnership), [G-20 major economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-20_major_economies), [Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development](33853117.md), [United Nations](31769.md), [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), and among others. Australia and Japan were also part of [Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Partnership), for the boost of Indo-Pacific cooperation. Universities in both countries are members of [Association of Pacific Rim Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Pacific_Rim_Universities), an organization founded in 1997.[^cite_note-82]

Since 1989, Japan has been a dialogue partner of the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum), which Australia was a founding member of in 1971. The organization was created to serve as a regional body for Pacific Island countries, but did not include island countries near Asia such as Japan and the Philippines.[^cite_note-83] Between the end of World War II and the mid-1980s, Japan had been less politically engaged with geographically-adjacent Micronesian islands, due to the United States gaining control of these islands, with some of the islands later gaining independence from the US beginning in the late 1980s. Japan also viewed the non-US controlled islands in [Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia) and [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia) as being part of the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations)'s zone of influence or Australia and New Zealand's zone of influence, and was merely content with having relations with Australia and New Zealand, rather than developing deep ties with the islands themselves.[^cite_note-84] In 1997, Japan launched the Pacific Islands Leaders Meeting (PALM). This is a summit held every three years between Japan and the Pacific Islands Forum members to discuss regional goals.[^cite_note-85]

## Opinion polls

According to a 2017 [BBC World Service](37627.md) Poll, 78% of [Australians](11197621.md) view Japan's influence positively, with 17% expressing a negative view, making Australia one of the most pro-Japanese countries in the world.[^cite_note-BBC-87]

According to a 2021 poll by the [Lowy Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowy_Institute), Japan is the third most positively viewed country by Australians, with a 73% positivity rating. It ranked behind the United Kingdom and New Zealand. In the 2020 version of the poll, it again ranked at third with a 69% rating, behind the United Kingdom and Canada (New Zealand was not included in the 2020 poll, nor was Canada in the 2021 poll).[^cite_note-88] In the 2022 version of the poll, Japan placed fourth with a 74% rating, behind the United Kingdom which was third with a 77% rating, and Canada and New Zealand, which were the two most favorably viewed countries with ratings of 80% and 86%.[^cite_note-89]

In a 2018 survey by the Japanese government, 65% of respondents had a favorable view of Australia.[^cite_note-gov-90] It was the second most favorably viewed region in the survey, behind the United States, and garnered a higher rating than South Korea, [Latin America](18524.md), [Africa](5334607.md), China and Russia, which was the least favorably viewed.[^cite_note-gov-90] 71% of respondents also felt that Japan had a good relationship with Australia, while 74% felt that the future development of relations was important for the two countries and the Asia-Pacific area.[^cite_note-gov-90]

## Twin towns and sister cities

As of 2021, Australia has had 112 [twin town and sister city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_city) affiliations with Japan, with the oldest affiliation dating back to 1963. Japan is currently the country with the highest number of Australian twin town and sister city affiliations.[^cite_note-21sis-91] Initiatives between twin towns include the establishment of a Japanese garden in the New South Wales town of [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington,_New_South_Wales), which housed over 126 different plant species, and which was officially opened in 1999 by Tadao Nakasai, mayor of Wellington's Japanese twin town [Ōsawano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csawano,_Toyama).[^cite_note-nswgov-92] The sister cities of [Nagoya](61720.md) and [Sydney](27862.md) have collaborated on student exchange programs and created relationships between their respective zoos, while the sister cities of [Osaka](52230.md) and [Melbourne](17306237.md) have created strong ties between business agencies, in addition to creating a 5,500 mile yacht race in 1987 called the "Melbourne Osaka Cup". In response to the [1995 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hanshin_earthquake) in [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe), the people of its Australian sister city [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) raised \$82,000. Conversely, following a [2011 flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%932011_Queensland_floods) in Brisbane, the Kobe City Government, City Assembly and Kobe City Government Workers' Union donated more than \$50,000 to assist in the recovery effort.[^cite_note-93] Many sister cities in Japan also undertook fundraising activities for Australia during the bushfires in [2019-2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019-2020_Australian_bushfires).[^cite_note-94] In October 2017, Japanese Consul-General Keizo Takewaka visited several Australian twin towns and sister cities, including [Orange, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_New_South_Wales) (affiliated with [Ushiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushiku,_Ibaraki)), [Coffs Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffs_Harbor), [New South Wales](21654.md) (affiliated with [Sasebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo)) and [Liverpool, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool,_New_South_Wales) (affiliated with [Toda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toda,_Saitama)).[^cite_note-95]
