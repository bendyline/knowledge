# Libya–Sudan border



Map of Libya-Sudan border

The **[Libya](17633.md)–[Sudan](27421.md) border** is 382 km (237 mi) in length and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Egypt](8087628.md) in the north to the tripoint with [Chad](5488.md) in the south.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the north at the tripoint with Egypt on [Gabal El Uweinat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabal_El_Uweinat), proceeding south along the [25th meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_meridian_east) for 223 km (138 mi) down to the [20th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_parallel_north). It then turns west along this parallel for 105 km (65 mi), before turning south at the [24th meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_meridian_east), where it turns south, running for 56 km (35 mi) down to the tripoint with Chad. The entire border runs through a remote, scarcely inhabited section of the [Sahara desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_desert).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]

## History

Britain invaded [Egypt](8087628.md) in 1882, establishing a protectorate over an area that had hitherto being nominally subject to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] In the 1890s the British conquered Sudan, and in 1899 a condominium was established which created [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan).[^cite_note-IBS10-3][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] In September 1911 [Italy](14532.md) invaded the nominally-Ottoman [Vilayet of Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet_of_Tripolitania), and the [Treaty of Ouchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ouchy) was signed the following year by which the Ottomans formally ceded sovereignty of the area over to Italy.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] Italy organised the newly conquered regions into the colonies of [Italian Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Cyrenaica) and [Italian Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Tripolitania) and gradually began pushing further south; in 1934 they united the two territories as [Italian Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]

In 1925 Britain and Italy signed a boundary treaty, whereby the [22nd parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_parallel_north) was utilised starting at the tripoint with Egypt and proceeding westward, terminating at the border between Italian Libyan and [French Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Equatorial_Africa) (i.e. the modern [Chad-Libya border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad-Libya_border)).[^cite_note-IBS10-3][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] The north-west corner of Anglo-Sudan Egyptian thus created was referred to as the [Sarra Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarra_Triangle); this latter area was ceded to Italy on 20 July 1934, and the boundary re-drawn at its current position.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] On 7 January 1935 France and Italy signed [a treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Italian_Agreement_of_1935) which shifted the French Equatorial Africa-Libya boundary southwards (creating the [Aouzou Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aouzou_Strip), thereby also shifting the Libya-Sudan southwards slightly, however this agreement was never formally ratified by both parties and was thus never implemented.[^cite_note-locked-7][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]

During the [North African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Campaign) of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) Italy was defeated and its African colonies were occupied by the Allied powers, with Libya split into British and French zones of occupation.[^cite_note-IBS10-3] Libya was later granted full independence on 2 December 1951, followed by Sudan in 1956.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]
