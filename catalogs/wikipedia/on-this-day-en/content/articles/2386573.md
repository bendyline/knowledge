The post of **high representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina**, together with the **Office of the High Representative** (**OHR**) in [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), was created in 1995 immediately after the signing of the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement) which ended the 1992 to 1995 [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War). The purpose of the high representative and the OHR is to oversee the civilian implementation of the Dayton Agreement. They are appointed by and serve to represent the countries of the [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) (PIC).

So far, all of the high representatives named have been from [European Union](9317.md) countries, and their principal deputies have typically been from the United States. The principal deputy high representative serves as [International Supervisor for Brčko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Supervisor_for_Br%C4%8Dko), representing the international community in the [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District).

The so-called [Bonn Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Powers) remain a highly contested attribute of the high representative. Although their legality is questionable, the Bonn Powers give the high representative a wide range of powers, including imposing and annulling laws and appointing and removing officials.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEErikssonKostić201335-1] Due to the vast powers of the High Representative over Bosnian politics and essential [veto](74661.md) powers, the position has been compared to that of a [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy),[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] or of a [colonial governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_governor).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Legal basis in the Dayton Peace Agreements

The [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement) created the legal basis for the OHR. Its Annex 10 provides for the institution of the *Office of the High Representative (OHR) in Bosnia and Herzegovina* to oversee the civilian implementation of the agreement, representing the countries involved in the Dayton Accords through the [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council). The annex also states that the high representative is to be appointed "consistent with relevant [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) resolutions" and to carry out his tasks "as entrusted by a United Nations Security Council resolution". The issue of Security Council approval of high representatives has been a point of controversy in the case of [Christian Schmidt](39499479.md)'s appointment in 2021, which was considered illegitimate by Russia and China as well as some politicians in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-n1ba-23082021-11][^cite_note-sarajevotimes-17082023-12][^cite_note-rfe/rl-17082023-13][^cite_note-14]

## The "Bonn Powers" of the OHR

Main article: [Bonn Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_Powers)

At its December 1997 meeting in [Bonn](3295.md), the [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) agreed to grant further substantial powers to the OHR, to avoid the implementation of the Dayton Agreement being delayed or obstructed by local nationalist politicians. The OHR was requested to:

1. Adopt binding decisions when local parties seem unable or unwilling to act;

2. Remove from office public officials who violate legal commitments or, in general, the [DPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement).

   [^cite_note-15]

   [^cite_note-venneri-16]

The legality of the Bonn Powers is questionable, and they were introduced after the Dayton Agreement was signed. Additionally, the PIC does not have the authority to grant powers to the high representative. Despite this, the Bonn Powers give the high representative broad authority, including the ability to make and annul laws, as well as appoint and remove officials.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEErikssonKostić201335-1]

The *Bonn Powers* were extensively used by the OHR in the following decade. Some examples include the adoption of the Defence reform in April 2003, with the suppression of the Supreme Defence Council of the [Republika Srpska](347422.md), and the amendment of Entity Constitutional Laws.[^cite_note-venneri-16]

Until 2004, the OHR had dismissed a total of 139 officials, including judges, ministers, civil servants and members of parliaments, sometimes along with freezing their bank accounts. After the 2002 elections, the OHR scrutinised all political candidates for major ministerial positions at entity and state level.[^cite_note-venneri-16]

## History

### Fusion with the EUSR post

Between 2002 and 2011, the high representative also served as the [European Union special representative](2899127.md) to [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Vogel-18]

Under [Christian Schwarz-Schilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Schwarz-Schilling), the OHR seemed to soften its invasiveness, thanks to pressures from the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and a growing EU involvement. The number of OHR legislative initiatives and of dismissed officials lowered.[^cite_note-venneri-16]

On 27 February 2008, the [PIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) decided to end the high representative's mandate on 30 June 2008. The EU decision to shut down the OHR by June 2007 unexpectedly aroused disappointment and concern in the Bosnian population, NGOs, and politicians.[^cite_note-venneri-16] However, since the [PIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) February 2008 review, it was decided to extend that mandate indefinitely until a set of positive benchmarks have been fulfilled.[^cite_note-closure-19]

The "double hatting" between EUSR and OHR was discontinued in 2011, when the EU representative post was fused between the EUSR and the head of the EU delegation.

### Resignation of Christian Schmidt

On 11 May 2026, [Christian Schmidt](39499479.md) announced his resignation as High Representative, with a new representative planning to be appointed by 14 July.[^cite_note-20] This came after the U.S. demanded Schmidt's immediate resignation, away from a previous agreement where he would stay in his post until [elections in October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bosnian_general_election).[^cite_note-guardian-20260630-21] His term ended on 1 July 2026, and was succeeded by [International Supervisor for Brčko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Supervisor_for_Br%C4%8Dko) and Principal Deputy High Representative [Louis J. Crishock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_J._Crishock) as acting High Representative.[^cite_note-ohr-20260630-22]

The U.S. has presented Italian ambassador [Antonio Zanardi Landi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Zanardi_Landi) as its preferred candidate. France, Britain, and Germany, meanwhile, have mostly supported René Troccaz, France's special envoy for the Western Balkans, as their candidate. European officials reportedly believe that U.S. manoeuvring is related to an attempt to secure a contract for the \$1 billion Southern Interconnection gas pipeline, meant to connect Bosnia's energy supply to Europe, for AAFS Infrastructure and Energy, a company personally connected to [Donald Trump](4848272.md). Officials further believe that if Landi assumes the position of High Representative he may take actions in support of the pipeline, such as by transferring nationally owned land in [Republika Srpska](347422.md) to the [government of the entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entities_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). Landi has unofficially promised to not overturn decrees from previous High Representatives, not make substantial actions without consulting the PIC, use the Bonn powers as a last resort, and not unilaterally dissolve the OHR.[^cite_note-guardian-20260630-21][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

## Conditions for closure of the Office of the High Representative



OHR building in [Grbavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grbavica_\(Sarajevo\)), [Sarajevo](26786.md).

In February 2008, the [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) set the conditions for closure of the OHR, which the PIC Steering Board considered a pre-condition for [accession of Bosnia and Herzegovina to the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-constitution-25][^cite_note-26] From a long list of known priorities, the PIC selected:[^cite_note-closure-19]

- Resolution of State Property

- Resolution of Defence Property

- Completion of the [Brčko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District) Final Award – *District created on March 8, 2000.[^cite_note-27] Brčko Tribunal jurisdiction remains.[^cite_note-28]*

- Fiscal Sustainability of the State – *Completed as of May 2010, should be continuously sustained[^cite_note-report37-29]*

- Entrenchment of the Rule of Law – *Completed as of May 2010, should be continuously sustained[^cite_note-report37-29]*

In addition to these objectives there are also two conditions:

- Signing of the [Stabilisation and Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilisation_and_Association_Agreement) – *completed on 16 June 2008*

- Positive assessment of the situation in BiH by the PIC Steering Board – *to be assessed after all others are completed*

An additional unwritten condition to be enforced through the "assessment by the PIC Steering Board" final condition, was adopted later by the [US](3434750.md) and some [EU countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_member_states):[^cite_note-constitution-25]

- Reform of the [constitution](12844303.md) to comply with [ECHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) decision of December 2009

## Criticisms

In the many years attempting to facilitate the integration of a stable federal Bosnian state, it has been argued that the OHR posed challenges in accomplishing its principal task.[^cite_note-30] The OHR's prolonged intervention in the [politics of Bosnia and Herzegovina](3606.md) has been considered by some as a potential cause of the allegedly low commitment of citizens towards the state, as perceived by the low [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), and of the purported low accountability of politicians, whose actions may be subject to the OHR's review.[^cite_note-venneri-16]

Former HR [Paddy Ashdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Ashdown) noted the high representative has "powers that ought to make any liberal blush."[^cite_note-31] Criticisms of the action of the OHR through its *Bonn powers* include:[^cite_note-venneri-16][^cite_note-32]

- the lack of accountability of the position, which is answerable only to the [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council);

- the lack of [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process) available to those affected by HR decisions.

  [^cite_note-33]

In addition to the potential consideration of imposing legislation on contentious issues, the OHR "developed the practice of dismissing public officials from their offices and banning them from holding any public employment again ... often without even admitting the dismissed persons to confront the charges brought against them, let alone granting them a fair hearing or a right to appeal."[^cite_note-Banning_2014-34] In June 2004, for example, Paddy Ashdown dismissed 58 public officials in a single day.[^cite_note-35] Politicians and judges have been subject to potential removal. Some of those dismissed were allegedly banned for life from holding public office and their [bank accounts been frozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frozen_bank_account) as well.[^cite_note-HoC_FASC-36] Doubts about the legality of both the OHR's interpretation of its mandate and particular actions have been expressed by the Council of Europe and others.[^cite_note-Banning_2014-34]

In 2004, the Parliamentary Assembly of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), which [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) had joined in 2002, voiced complaints against OHR's actions, and expressed the need "to define a clear strategy for transferring responsibilities from the high representative to domestic authorities."[^cite_note-37] Russia and China have rejected the continuation of the office and have tried at the UN to have it terminated.[^cite_note-38] They argue that it violates BiH's sovereignty, and they claim that the West persists with the setup so it can remain the "guardian" of the country.[^cite_note-39] [Valentin Inzko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Inzko), during his tenure as high representative, declared: "We have to wait for the moment that Bosnia–Herzegovina is irreversibly on its way to Euro-Atlantic integration, then we should shut down the Office."[^cite_note-40]
