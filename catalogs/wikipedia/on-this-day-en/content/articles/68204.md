**Extremadura** ([/ˌɛkstrəməˈdjʊərə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*EK-strə-mə-DURE-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Spanish: [\[e(ɣ)stɾemaˈðuɾa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e5/Pronunciation_of_Extremadura_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Extremadura_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e5/Pronunciation_of_Extremadura_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Extremadura_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Extremaduran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremaduran_language): *Estremaúra* [\[ehtːɾemaˈuɾa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Astur-Leonese); [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Estremadura*; [Fala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fala_language): *Extremaúra*) is a landlocked [autonomous community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_communities_in_Spain) of [Spain](26667.md). Its capital city is [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain), and its largest city is [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz). Located in the central-western part of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), it is crossed from east to west by the [Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) and [Guadiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadiana) rivers. The autonomous community is formed by the two largest [provinces of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain): [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1ceres) and [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Badajoz). Extremadura is bordered by [Portugal](23033.md) to the west and by the autonomous communities of [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n) to the north, [Castilla–La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla%E2%80%93La_Mancha) to the east, and [Andalusia](2736.md) to the south.

It is an important area for wildlife, particularly with the major reserve at [Monfragüe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monfrag%C3%BCe), which was designated a National Park in 2007, and the International Tagus River Natural Park (*Parque Natural Tajo Internacional*). The [regional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Extremadura) is led by the [president of the Regional Government of Extremadura](32304517.md), a post currently held by [María Guardiola](72221033.md) of the [People's Party](404727.md).

The Day of Extremadura is celebrated on 8 September.[^cite_note-ley1985-4] It coincides with the [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) festivity of [Our Lady of Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe_in_Extremadura).[^cite_note-ley1985-4] The region, featuring an enormous energy surplus and hosting [lithium](17561.md) deposits, is at the forefront of Spain's plans for [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) and [decarbonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbon_economy).

## Name

The two most common views on the origin of the name are:&#x20;

1. It refers to the areas surrounding the south of the [river Duero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Duero), from Latin *Extrema Dorii*;

2. Perhaps simply being formed from *extremo* ('extreme') plus the common Spanish suffix *-dura* referring to the outer regions of the kingdoms of León and Castilla.

## Geography

### Physical environment



Towering over 2,400 m, the Calvitero is considered to be Extremadura's highest point.



The Garganta de Cuartos in northeastern Extremadura

Extremadura is contained between 37° 57′ and 40° 29′ N latitude, and 4° 39′ and 7° 33′ W longitude.

The [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area) of Extremadura is 41,633 km<sup>2</sup> (16,075 sq mi), making it the fifth largest of the Spanish autonomous communities. It is located in the Southern Plateau (a subdivision of the Spanish [Central Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meseta_Central)).

The region is crossed from West to East by two large rivers, the [Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) and the [Guadiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadiana), lining up three basic areas from North to South by combining mountain ranges and rivers: the territory spanning from the [Sistema Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Central) to the Tagus, the so-called *Mesopotamia extremeña* in between the Tagus and the Guadiana and the territory from the Guadiana to [Sierra Morena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Morena).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOngil_ValentínSauceda_Pizarro1986155-5] Besides the catchment basins of the Tagus and the Guadiana covering most of the territory by far, fringe areas of the region are drained by the [Douro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douro) (north) and the [Guadalquivir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalquivir) (south). Notable Tagus tributaries include the [Tiétar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti%C3%A9tar_River) and the [Alagón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alag%C3%B3n_River) (rightbank) and the [Almonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almonte_\(river\)), [Ibor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibor), [Salor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salor) and the Sever (leftbank). Regarding the Guadiana, important leftbank tributaries include [Guadarranque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarranque) and [Ruecas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruecas?action=edit\&redlink=1) and rightbank tributaries include the [Zújar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BAjar_River) and the [Matachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matachel).

The highest point in Extremadura, the 2,401 m (7,877 ft) high [Calvitero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvitero?action=edit\&redlink=1) (or El Torreón),[^cite_note-6] is located in the Sistema Central, in the northeastern end of the region, bordering with Castile and León. The main subranges of the Sistema Central in Extremadura are the [Sierra de Gata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_Gata) and [Sierra de Béjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_B%C3%A9jar_\(mountain_range\)).

The modest heights of [Sierra de las Villuercas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_las_Villuercas) (topping at 1,603 m (5,259 ft) on the [Pico de las Villuercas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Villuerca)) rise in the *Mesopotamia extremeña*. Other notable ranges include the [Sierra de Montánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_Mont%C3%A1nchez) and the [Sierra de San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_San_Pedro), part of the larger [Montes de Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montes_de_Toledo) system.[^cite_note-7]

The [Sierra Morena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Morena)—the limit between Extremadura and [Andalusia](2736.md)—and the [Sierra de Tentudía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_Tentud%C3%ADa?action=edit\&redlink=1) (topping at 1,104 m (3,622 ft) on the [Pico Tentudía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_Tentud%C3%ADa?action=edit\&redlink=1)) rise in the south.

There are four different [hydrographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography) basins:

- The [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus_Basin) of the [Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Tajo*), with two principal tributaries: on the right, the [Tiétar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti%C3%A9tar_River) and the [Alagón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alag%C3%B3n_River); and on the left, the [Almonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almonte_\(river\)), [Ibor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibor), [Salor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salor) and the [Sever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus_River). The tributaries on the right edge carry a large quantity of water, which feed the gorges of the [Sistema Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Central) where the rainfall is abundant and the winter brings a great quantity of snow.

### Climate

The climate of Extremadura is predominantly [hot-summer Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean) (*Csa* in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), with some regions being [cold semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) (*BSk* in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)).[^cite_note-8] Extremadura generally presents average annual temperatures somewhat warmer than most of the hinterland of the Iberian Peninsula, featuring nonetheless a north–south gradient.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPulidoSanzAbelEzquerra2007103-9] Annual thermal amplitude generally ranges from 16 to 19 °C.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPulidoSanzAbelEzquerra2007103-9] Average annual temperature is around 16.4 °C (61.5 °F), while the average annual precipitation stands at around 588 mm.[^cite_note-10] Parts of the Sistema Central presents more than 1,500 mm while it barely rains 400 mm in parts of the province of Badajoz.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPulidoSanzAbelEzquerra2007103–104-11] Summers are very hot and dry, with the rain concentrated in the cold months instead, leading to a high degree of [water stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_stress) during the summer months.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPulidoSanzAbelEzquerra2007104-12]

## History



[Archaeological Roman Ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain) in Mérida (*[Emerita Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Emerita)*), capital of the Roman province of [Hispania Lusitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitania).

During the time of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the area that is known today as Autonomous Community of Extremadura was part of [Lusitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitania), a Roman province that included most of current day Portugal (except for the northern area today known as [Norte Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Region,_Portugal)) and the central western portion of the current day Spain. [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain) (now capital of Extremadura) became the capital of the Roman province of Lusitania, and one of the most important cities in the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).

Like the bulk of the Iberian Peninsula, the territory [was conquered by the Umayyads in the early 8th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania). As part of the [Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) and later [Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba), it largely constituted a territorial subdivision (*[kūra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_\(al-Andalus\))*) of the former polities centered on Mérida. Following the collapse of the Caliphate in the early 11th century during the so-called [Fitna of al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitna_of_al-Andalus) and its ensuing fragmentation into ephemeral statelets ([taifas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa)), the bulk of the territory of current day Extremadura became part of the (First) [Taifa of Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Badajoz) (*Baṭalyaws*), centered around the namesake city and founded by [Sapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabur_al-Saqlabi), a [saqaliba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqaliba) previously [freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedman) by [Al-Hakam II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hakam_II).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDomené_Sánchez2009102-14]



The bull of Plasencia in the *[Cantigas de Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantigas_de_Santa_Maria)*.

Conversely, the kingdoms of [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Le%C3%B3n), [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile) and [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) (most notably the first one) made advances in the 11th and 12th centuries across the territory (with for example the successive Leonese conquests of [Coria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coria,_C%C3%A1ceres) in 1079[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Fitz200247-15] and 1142,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_RamosMontaña_Conchiña200014-16] the Portuguese attempts at expanding across the [Guadiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadiana) basin in the second half of the 12th century,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_RamosMontaña_Conchiña200018-17] or the Castilian founding of [Plasencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasencia) in 1186)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_RamosMontaña_Conchiña200020-18] not free from setbacks either caused by the [Almoravid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid) and [Almohad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad) impetus, which also entailed the demise of the first and second taifa of Badajoz in 1094 and 1150,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDomené_Sánchez2009103-19] respectively. In the Almohad case, their 1174 offensive removed Leonese control from every fortress south of the [Tagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus) (including [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1ceres,_Spain)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_RamosMontaña_Conchiña200019-20] After the Almohad disaster at the 1212 [Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Las_Navas_de_Tolosa), the remaining part of current-day Extremadura under Muslim control fell to the troops led by [Alfonso IX of León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_IX_of_Le%C3%B3n)—[Alcántara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alc%C3%A1ntara,_Spain) (1214),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVillarroel_Escalante20081257-21] Cáceres (1227–1229),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBullón_de_Mendoza200146-22] Mérida (1230),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPorrinas_González2018651-23] [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz) (1230)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDomené_Sánchez2009101-24]—and later to the military orders of [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Santiago) and [Alcántara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Alc%C3%A1ntara)—[Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo,_Spain) (1232),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPino_García1985381-25] [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn,_Spain) (1234)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDíaz_Gil2010211-26]—on behalf of [Ferdinand III of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_III_of_Castile). The last fortresses in the Lower Extremadura were conquered by Christians by 1248.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_RamosMontaña_Conchiña200027-27]

By the late Middle Ages, the territory of the current-day region consisted of mayorazgos of the military orders of [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Santiago) and [Alcántara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Alc%C3%A1ntara) (about half the territory), nobiliary lordships (about a quarter of the territory) and royal demesne towns (the other quarter of the territory).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELadero_Quesada1992238-28]

In between the 15th and 16th centuries, the concept of the Leonese and Castilian *extremaduras* diluted and the name eventually came to refer to the territory of the current-day region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELadero_Quesada1992230-29] The territory lacked nonetheless shared government and administration institutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELadero_Quesada1992239-30]

In between 1570 and 1572, the Crown forcibly relocated about 11,000 *[moriscos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morisco)* into the territory as part of the deportation of Granadans that followed the defeat of the [Alpujarras revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion_of_the_Alpujarras_\(1568%E2%80%931571\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHernández_BermejoSánchez_RubioTestón_Núñez199589-31] The distribution was somewhat chaotic although some places with an already "threatening" population of old moriscos (such as [Hornachos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornachos), [Magacela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magacela) and [Benquerencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benquerencia)) were avoided as resettlement locations for the Granadan moriscos.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHernández_BermejoSánchez_RubioTestón_Núñez199593-32]

Two [generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation) later, the [expulsion of the moriscos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_moriscos) from the region began in 1609, starting with the moriscos of Hornachos, the first expulsion in the Crown of Castile.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHernández_BermejoSánchez_RubioTestón_Núñez1995114-33] By September 1610 about two thirds of the moriscos of Extremadura had been already expelled and by 1611 the number amounted to 12,776.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHernández_BermejoSánchez_RubioTestón_Núñez1995117-34] Those who avoided the early orders of expulsion abided to reports of being 'good Christians' or claimed a status as 'old moriscos'.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHernández_BermejoSánchez_RubioTestón_Núñez1995116-35] At the height of 1612, there were reports of remaining moriscos in Trujillo, Mérida and Plasencia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHernández_BermejoSánchez_RubioTestón_Núñez1995117–118-36]



17th century panorama of the city of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz).

Located in the most convenient path from the [Meseta Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meseta_Central) to Portugal, the territory suffered greatly due to warfare from the 1640–1668 [Portuguese Restoration War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Restoration_War),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Barriga200831-37] characterised not by the movement of large armies but by pillage, skirmishes, raids, and destruction of economic resources and settlements across both sides of the *[Raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_border)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Barriga200833-38] The growing role of the fortified place of Badajoz (halfway between Lisbon and Madrid), in the wake of the installment of the Captaincy General of Extremadura consolidated the clout of the military in the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaranjo_SanguinoRoso_DíazRuiz_Rodríguez201325-39]

By the late 18th century, the Extremaduran countryside languished, experiencing a deep crisis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_PérezSánchez_Marroyo1984213-40] There was a diminishing share of land dedicated to crops.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_PérezSánchez_Marroyo1984214-41] The growing cattle sector induced the creation of yet more pastures,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_PérezSánchez_Marroyo1984214-41] adding up to the structural problem stemmed from the extraordinary degree of [concentration of land ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_land_ownership).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_PérezSánchez_Marroyo1984213-40] By the end of the Ancien Régime, the clergy, municipal councils and the royal army mattered more than the lesser role of the entitled nobility.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaranjo_SanguinoRoso_DíazRuiz_Rodríguez201325-39]

Railways were developed in the second half of the 19th century. In September 1863, a passenger train arrived to Badajoz from [Elvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvas), Portugal. It was the first train in the region and the first international service in the Iberian Peninsula.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlanch_Sánchez2013446-43] In 1866, the [Badajoz–Ciudad Real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Real%E2%80%93Badajoz_railway?action=edit\&redlink=1) was completed, enabling the link with Madrid.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlanch_Sánchez2013448–449-44] The [Madrid–Valencia de Alcántara line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Valencia_de_Alc%C3%A1ntara_railway), a new connection passing through the province of Cáceres, was fully completed in 1881.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlanch_Sánchez2013453-45]

During the 1936–1939 [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), the [Nationalist faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_faction_\(Spanish_Civil_War\)) Columna Madrid advanced quickly across the province of Badajoz in August 1936 and left merciless repression and mass casualties behind.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChaves_Palacios2007205–206-46] Badajoz was the Spanish province where the [Francoist repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_repression) comparatively took the highest relative toll of victims during the war and the immediate Post-War period; there were around 12,000 executions in the province (out of the 14,000 in the whole region), compared to around 1,600 victims of the Republican repression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChaves_Palacios2007205-47]

In the mid 20th century, the [Francoist dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_dictatorship) pursued a policy of colonization and agrarian reform in the region to foster the economy, transforming thousands of hectares of dryland crops into irrigated lands, also favouring the erection of 63 new settlements by the [Instituto Nacional de Colonización](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Nacional_de_Colonizaci%C3%B3n) (INC).[^cite_note-48] The second half of the 20th century saw a massive [rural flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_flight) out of the region, both to the industrialised areas of Spain (already started in 1955) as well as to richer European countries (such as Germany, France and Switzerland), both of which notably intensified after 1961, in the wake of the [1959 Stabilization Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilization_Plan) (and in the second case also after bilateral agreements reached with destination countries).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECayetano_Rosado20071290,_1309-49] The region henceforth was handed a demographic blow in the ensuing years, with the effective expulsion of nearly a 40% of the population, particularly young people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECayetano_Rosado20071309-50]

A pre-autonomous government entity in Extremadura (the "Junta Regional de Extremadura") with jurisdiction over the provinces of Badajoz and Cáceres was created by means of a 1978 law.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChaves_Palacios2002545-51] The draft of the regional [Statute of Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy) began in 1980, the first step toward Extremadura becoming one of the Spanish [autonomous communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_communities).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChaves_Palacios2002544-52] The text passed its final hurdle as it was enshrined as [Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Law_\(Spain\)) in 1982.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChaves_Palacios2002545-51] The [first election to the Assembly of Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Extremaduran_regional_election) took place in May 1983.

## Government and administration

### Autonomous

The [Statute of Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy) of Extremadura (enacted in 1983) is the fundamental organic law regulating the autonomous government, and it establishes the institutions through which the autonomous community exerts its powers:[^cite_note-estatuto-53]



The hemicycle of the Assembly of Extremadura

- [Assembly of Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Extremadura). The following are some of the functions conferred to the legislature: exerting legislative power in the autonomous community, the promotion and control of the Junta of Extremadura, the passing of the regional budget, the designation of senators correspondent to the autonomous community or the control of the media dependent on the regional government.

  [^cite_note-estatuto-53]

  &#x20;Its members (currently 65) are directly elected through the means of proportional representation and close party lists with an [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 5% (the most benign between the total voting percentage and the voting percentage in a particular electoral district) in two electoral districts: [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz_\(Assembly_of_Extremadura_constituency\)) and [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1ceres_\(Assembly_of_Extremadura_constituency\)), corresponding to the two provinces of the region.

- [Junta of Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junta_of_Extremadura). It is the [collegiate body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_body) comprised by the regional president, the vice-president and the ministers (*consejeros*) exerting the executive and administrative functions of the regional government.

  [^cite_note-estatuto-53]

- [President of the Junta of Extremadura](32304517.md). The officeholder is charged with directing and coordinating the action of the Junta of Extremadura, being the highest representative of Extremadura while also holding the ordinary representation of the State in the region. The regional president is elected by the legislature from among its members, needing to command an absolute majority of votes in the first round of investiture or a simple majority of positive votes in successive rounds. The president personally selects the ministers of the Junta.

  [^cite_note-estatuto-53]

### Provincial

The government body for each of the provinces is the [deputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_deputation_\(Spain\)) (*diputación*): the [Provincial Deputation of Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Deputation_of_Badajoz?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Provincial Deputation of Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Deputation_of_C%C3%A1ceres?action=edit\&redlink=1). The members of the plenary of the deputation are indirectly elected from among the municipal councillors based on the results of the municipal elections. In turn, the plenary elects the president of the deputation from among its members.

### Administrative divisions

Extremadura is divided into 383 municipalities. 164 are part of the [Province of Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Badajoz) and the other 219 are part of the [Province of Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1ceres).

There are also traditional [comarcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarcas_of_Spain) in Extremadura, like [Las Villuercas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Villuercas) and [Las Hurdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Hurdes), but these do not have much official recognition.

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of Extremadura was 20 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 1.7% of Spanish economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 20,100 euros or 67% of the EU27 average in the same year. Extremadura was the community with the second lowest GDP per capita in Spain before [Melilla](20226.md).[^cite_note-54]

Export goods (mostly consisting of food and semimanufactures) are primarily sent to the [European market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market), but there has been a growing share of non-EU export destinations throughout the 2010s.[^cite_note-caixa-55] [Balance of trade](4810.md) is generally positive.[^cite_note-caixa-55]

The unemployment rate stood at 17.3% in 2022, significantly lower than in the previous decade.[^cite_note-56]

### Agriculture



Iberian pigs in Extremadura



Tobacco field in La Vera

Wild [black Iberian pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Iberian_pig) roam in the area and consume [acorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn) from [oak](39696.md) groves. These pigs are caught and used for the cured ham dish *[jamón ibérico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jam%C3%B3n_ib%C3%A9rico)*. The higher the percentage of acorns eaten by the pigs, the more valuable the ham. For example, *jamón ibérico* from pigs whose diet consists of 90% acorns or more can be sold for more than twice as much as ham whose pigs ate on average less than 70% acorns. In the US, *jamón ibérico* directly from Extremadura, with bone, was illegal until around 2005. At that time, enough US restaurants were in demand for the delicacy that Spain decided to export it as boneless, which the [US Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Agriculture)'s health codes would approve (and continue to do).

85 Extremaduran municipalities constitute the jurisdiction of the "Dehesa de Extremadura" [Protected Designation of Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_designation_of_origin) (PDO), which protects *jamones* and *paletas* (hind and front pig legs) originated from [Iberian pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Iberian_pig) and mixed Iberian/[Duroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duroc_pig)-Jersey pigs.[^cite_note-57]

As of 2021, Extremadura produces about 98% of the [tobacco](30942.md) produced in Spain, also being the leading European producing region.[^cite_note-tobacco-58] Tobacco production concentrates in [La Vera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Vera) and [Campo Arañuelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_Ara%C3%B1uelo).[^cite_note-tobacco-58]

[Tomato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato) production (2,122,000 tonnes in 2017) primarily concentrates in the riverbanks of the Guadiana and the Alagón-Árrago.[^cite_note-59]

A large part of the region falls within the scope of the [Ribera del Guadiana PDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribera_del_Guadiana), which is further divided in the Ribera Alta, [Tierra de Barros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_de_Barros), Matanegra, Ribera Baja, Montánchez, and Cañamero wine subregions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiñero_Chacón1999201–202-60] The PDO protects the [wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wines) made of several varieties of black and white grapes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiñero_Chacón1999202-61]

As of 2021, Extremadura is the second largest [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) producing region in Spain, after Andalusia.[^cite_note-efeagro-62] Due to drought and high water demands from rice fields, non-irrigated rice fields have been favoured since the late 2010s.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-efeagro-62] Together with Murcia, Extremadura is a major producer of [paprika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paprika), primarily destined to the Spanish market.[^cite_note-qcom-64] Peppers are grown in the ["Pimentón de la Vera" PDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piment%C3%B3n_de_la_Vera?action=edit\&redlink=1), consisting of the *comarcas* of La Vera, Campo Arañuelo, [Valle del Ambroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_del_Ambroz) and [Valle del Alagón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_del_Alag%C3%B3n?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-qcom-64] The PDO produced 3,860 tonnes in 2020.[^cite_note-qcom-64]

### Energy

About half the value of the regional industrial production belongs to the [energy sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_sector).[^cite_note-colonization-65] Extremadura presents a huge energy surplus, producing about four times the energy it consumes.[^cite_note-colonization-65] This situation has led to the characterization of Extremadura as a potential "colony" of the private electricity companies, which are not taxed in the region and employ a relatively low share of the industrial workforce.[^cite_note-colonization-65] The Tagus is dammed in the reservoirs of [Alcántara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alc%C3%A1ntara_Dam), Torrejón and [Valdecañas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdeca%C3%B1as_reservoir) whereas the Guadiana is dammed in the reservoirs of [Cíjara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%ADjara_Dam), [Puerto Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garc%C3%ADa_Sola_Reservoir), Orellana and Zújar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMateos_DávilaHernández_González198371-66] Due to the orographic conditions, the Tagus is better suited for [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) use than the Guadiana.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMateos_DávilaHernández_González198371-66] As of 2021, the region has around 2,193.84 MW of installed hydroelectric power, primarily controlled by [Endesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endesa) and [Iberdrola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberdrola), with a lesser role of Grupo Pitarch.[^cite_note-colonization-65]

The two reactors of the [Almaraz Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaraz_Nuclear_Power_Plant) (which were put in operation in 1981 and 1983, respectively) are jointly operated by Endesa, Iberdrola and [Naturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturgy).[^cite_note-colonization-65] They generate a power of 1,048.43 MW and 1,044.45 MW.[^cite_note-eldiario-67]

The region is at the forefront of Spain's plans for [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) and a [decarbonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbon_economy), thanks to the installation of large solar power plants and the granting of [lithium](17561.md) mining licenses.[^cite_note-pilaverde-68] Such prospects sparked criticism and concern regarding how to avoid a "third energy colonisation" after those of the construction of reservoirs for [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) use and the building of [nuclear power plants](207347.md).[^cite_note-pilaverde-68] Two of the largest [photovoltaic power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_power_plant) in Europe are located in the region: Francisco Pizarro (590 MW) in [Torrecillas de la Tiesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrecillas_de_la_Tiesa) and Núñez de Balboa (500 MW) in [Usagre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usagre); both are operated by Iberdrola, which is developing another 6 photovoltaic plants collectively amounting to 1,300 MW.[^cite_note-eldiario-67] The first [solar thermal power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power) in the region, [Alvarado I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvarado_I), (50 MW) opened in 2009.[^cite_note-69]

A project to build a lithium-ion battery factory participated by [Envision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envision_Energy) in [Navalmoral de la Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navalmoral_de_la_Mata) was announced in June 2022.[^cite_note-70]

## Transport

### Air

[Badajoz Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz_Airport) is the only airport in the Extremadura region which provides direct routes to Madrid, Barcelona, Gran Canaria and Palma de Mallorca which are operated by [Iberia](98613.md). Generally, residents in the region would also use other nearby airports such as [Madrid–Barajas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Barajas_Airport), [Seville Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville_Airport), and [Humberto Delgado Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Airport) in the nearby border of Portugal.

## Population

As of 1 January 2012, the population of Extremadura is 1,109,367 inhabitants, representing 2.36% of the Spanish population (46,745,807).

The population density is very low—25/km<sup>2</sup> (65/sq mi)—compared to Spain as a whole.

The urban network is dominated by three municipalities between 50,000 and 200,000 inhabitants ([Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz), [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1ceres,_Spain) and [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain)), followed by [Plasencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasencia), the [Don Benito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Benito)-[Villanueva de la Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanueva_de_la_Serena) conurbation and [Almendralejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almendralejo).[^cite_note-caixa-55] Other municipalities with a population above 10,000 inhabitants include [Zafra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafra), [Montijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montijo,_Spain), [Villafranca de los Barros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villafranca_de_los_Barros), [Navalmoral de la Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navalmoral_de_la_Mata) and [Coria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coria,_C%C3%A1ceres).[^cite_note-caixa-55]

The most populous province is that of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz_\(province\)), with a population of 691,715 and a population density of 31.78/km<sup>2</sup> (82.3/sq mi). With an area of 21,766 km<sup>2</sup> (8,404 sq mi), it is the largest province in Spain. 413,766 people live in the [province of Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1ceres) at a density of 20.83/km<sup>2</sup> (53.9/sq mi), having an area of 19,868 km<sup>2</sup> (7,671 sq mi), making it the largest province in Spain after Badajoz.&#x20;

As of 2022, the largest immigrant community is Moroccan with 9,218 people, followed by Romanian with 4,324. There are 98 Icelanders and 6 Liechtensteiners. Brazilians account for 1,676 and Colombians make up 1,409. Among sub-Saharan African immigrants, the largest community is Senegalese with 88 people. Among Asian immigrants, the Chinese make up the largest group with 631 people. There are also 3,492 Portuguese citizens living within the region.[^cite_note-ineextranjeros-72]

The Extremaduran population, according to the 1591 census of the provinces of the [Kingdom of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile), was around 540,000 people, making up 8% of the total population of Spain. No other census was performed until 1717, when 326,358 people were counted as living in Extremadura. The population in 1833 was 547,420.[^cite_note-73]

From this period, the population grew steadily until the 1960s (1,379,072 people in 1960).[^cite_note-74] After 1960, emigration to more prosperous regions of Spain and Europe drained the population.

## Languages

The only official language is Spanish (whose local dialects are collectively called [Castúo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast%C3%BAo)), but other languages and dialects are also spoken. The [Fala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fala_language), a [Galician-Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician-Portuguese) language, is a specially protected language and is spoken in the valley of [Jálama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1lama). The [Extremaduran language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremaduran_language), the collective name for a group of vernacular dialects related to [Leonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonese_language)[^cite_note-75] is endangered. Local variants of [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) are native to [Cedillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedillo) and [Herrera de Alcántara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herrera_de_Alc%C3%A1ntara).[^cite_note-76] Portuguese has also been accounted to be spoken as well by some people (mainly those born before the 1940s[^cite_note-77]) in [Olivenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivenza).

Reported phonological distinctive features of the Spanish dialectal variants spoken in the region include instances of *[seseo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seseo)* (in some areas of the province of Badajoz), loss of intervocalic /d/, j and word-initial h [aspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspirated_consonant), r → l substitution, and *[yeísmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye%C3%ADsmo)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonzález_Salgado20141157-78]
