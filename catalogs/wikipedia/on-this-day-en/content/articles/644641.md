**Ksar el-Kebir** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القصر الكبير, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Qaṣr al-Kabīr*, [Spanish](26825.md) : Alcazarquivir), also known as **al-Qasr al-Kabir**, and **Alcazarquivir** is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in northwestern [Morocco](19291.md), about 160 kilometres (99 mi) north of [Rabat](57400.md), 32 kilometres (20 mi) east of [Larache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larache) and 110 kilometres (68 mi) south of [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier). It recorded a population of 124,701 in the [2024 Moroccan census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Moroccan_census).[^cite_note-census2024-1]

The name means "the big castle". The city is located nearby the [Loukous River](22848386.md), making El-Ksar-el-Kebir one of Morocco's richest agricultural regions. El-Ksar el-Kebir provides almost 20% of the needed sugar of Morocco.

Neighbouring cities and towns include [Larache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larache), [Chefchaouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chefchaouen), [Arbaoua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbaoua), and [Tatoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatoft).

## History

It came under Roman control with the name *Oppidum Novum*.[^cite_note-2]

In 1578, King [Sebastian of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_of_Portugal) suffered a crushing defeat in the [Battle of Alcácer Quibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alc%C3%A1cer_Quibir) at the hands of the [King Abd al-Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Marwan_Abd_al-Malik_I_Saadi) of [Morocco](19291.md), which ended Portugal's ambitions to invade and Christianize the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb). Both kings died during the battle, as did [Abdallah Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Abdallah_Mohammed_II_Saadi), who was allied with Sebastian.[^cite_note-EB1911-3] The death of King Sebastian started the events which led to the temporary [union of the crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Union) of [Portugal](23033.md) and [Spain](26667.md) under [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain). King Abd al-Malik's victory gave Morocco substantial strength and international prestige.

The city experienced substantial growth with the settling of a critical garrison in 1911 as a part of the [Spanish protectorate in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_protectorate_in_Morocco). After Morocco's independence and the [Oued el Makhazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oued_el_Makhazine?action=edit\&redlink=1) reservoir was built by [King Hassan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_II_of_Morocco) to manage the Loukkos' river regime, and the city became an important regional agricultural distribution center.

- 12th century: According to [Leo Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Africanus), city walls were built by the command of the [Abu Yusuf Yaqub al-Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Yusuf_Yaqub_al-Mansur).

  [^cite_note-EB1911-3]

- 1578: The Battle of Alcácer Quibir, or Battle of the Three Kings, is fought here.

- 17th century: Sultan Moulay Ismail destroyed the city walls of Ksar el-Kebir after being angered by a local chief.

- 1912: Establishment of the spanish Protectorate in northern Morocco including the city of Ksar el-Kebir

On 12 February 2026, severe flooding hit Ksar el-Kebir after heavy rains caused the Loukkos River to overflow, submerging neighborhoods and forcing thousands to flee. The army and emergency forces helped evacuate residents, leaving large parts of the city nearly deserted as cleanup operations began.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## Culture

El-Ksar el-Kebir is reputed for the leading artists, writers, poets, and sportsmen on the national plane.

- In sports, football player [Abdeslam Laghrissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdeslam_Laghrissi) still keeps his record as the best marksman in the Moroccan championship with 26 goals in 1986.

- In music, [Abdessalam Amer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdessalam_Amer?action=edit\&redlink=1) (died 1979), who is well known in the Arab world as a unique music composer. He left such eternal songs as: *Red Moon*, *Beach*, *Leaving*, *The Last Oh!*.

- In poetry, [Mohamed El Khammar El Guennouni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_El_Khammar_El_Guennouni?action=edit\&redlink=1) (died 1991) was a pioneer in modern Moroccan poetry and is regarded as master of free poetry in Morocco. There is also poet [Ouafae El Amrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouafae_El_Amrani?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the new poetic generation.

- In novel-writing, there are such novelists as [Mohamed Aslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Aslim?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mohamed Harradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Harradi?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mohamed Tetouani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Tetouani?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mohamed Sibari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Sibari) and [Moustafa Jebari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moustafa_Jebari?action=edit\&redlink=1).

- In short-story writing, there is [Mohamed Said Raihani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Said_Raihani?action=edit\&redlink=1), who is a trilingual writer (he writes in Arabic, French and English) and who has finished his fortieth manuscript before reaching the age of forty.
