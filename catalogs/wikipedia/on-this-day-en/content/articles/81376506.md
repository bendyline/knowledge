On 19 October 2025, thieves disguised as construction workers stole eight pieces of the [French Crown Jewels](1149165.md) valued at approximately €88 million from the [Galerie d'Apollon](37651655.md) (lit. 'Apollo's Gallery') of the [Louvre](17546.md) in [Paris](22989.md), France. The robbery took less than eight minutes, of which the thieves spent four in the museum itself and the rest using an elevated furniture lift to enter and exit the gallery through a window. It occurred during regular opening hours. It was the first [art theft](425514.md) from the Louvre since the painting *[Le chemin de Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_chemin_de_S%C3%A8vres)* was stolen in 1998.

Within a week, two men from [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) were arrested in connection with the robbery. Nine further suspects were detained throughout the course of the investigation, six of whom were released. Charges have been filed against five suspects; the location of the jewels remains unknown.

## Background

The 16th-century Galerie d'Apollon within the Louvre displayed what remained of the [French Crown Jewels](1149165.md#Regalia_and_jewels_at_the_Louvre), including the [Crown of Louis XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Louis_XV_of_France) and the [Hortensia diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hortensia_diamond).[^cite_note-1]

The first documented theft from the Louvre occurred in 1911, when [Vincenzo Peruggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Peruggia), a former employee of the museum, stole the *[Mona Lisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona_Lisa)* (recovered two years later in Italy).[^cite_note-2] The most recent art theft before 2025 was in 1998, when the painting *[Le chemin de Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_chemin_de_S%C3%A8vres)* by [Jean-Baptiste-Camille Corot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste-Camille_Corot) was stolen. The artwork has not yet been found; at the time, the Louvre's director, [Pierre Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Rosenberg), warned that the museum's security was "fragile".[^cite_note-france24-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

[Laurence des Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_des_Cars), director of the museum at the time of the 2025 robbery, had asked the Paris police to conduct a security audit of the museum. Although recommendations were made after the audit, they were only beginning to be implemented at the time of the robbery.[^cite_note-france24-3] According to labour unions, Louvre security had been undermined by staff reductions, while museum attendance had soared. The [Union syndicale Solidaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_syndicale_Solidaires) issued a statement on 19 October 2025, complaining about "the destruction of security jobs" at the Louvre.[^cite_note-france24-3] Other security lapses came to light in the aftermath, including insufficient CCTV coverage with only 39% of the museum's rooms being monitored by cameras, and the CCTV in the Apollo Gallery facing the wrong way.[^cite_note-6] A 2014 audit by [France's National Cybersecurity Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_nationale_de_la_s%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_des_syst%C3%A8mes_d'information) had warned the museum about serious security flaws, including the use of "trivial" passwords and outdated software.[^cite_note-7] It was reported that the password to get into the surveillance system was "Louvre".[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Other French museums that have also recently been targeted and had items stolen include the [Cognacq-Jay Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_Cognacq-Jay) and the [Hiéron Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi%C3%A9ron_Museum), both in November 2024, and the [Adrien Dubouché Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_national_Adrien-Dubouch%C3%A9) in September 2025.[^cite_note-BBC-know-10] The [National Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Natural_History,_France) in Paris was also robbed of gold worth €600,000 on 16 September 2025.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Theft



The robbers entered the museum through the *Balcon de Charles IX*



A [furniture lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture_lift?action=edit\&redlink=1) similar to the one used in the robbery

The robbery occurred at approximately 9\:30 am [CEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Summer_Time), 30 minutes after the museum opened to visitors.[^cite_note-Reuters-13][^cite_note-Cambriolage_au_Louvre-2025-14] The thieves arrived at the building and entered from the side of the [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine) river, bringing tools. They wore [yellow-and-orange vests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-visibility_clothing) to disguise themselves as construction workers.[^cite_note-Cambriolage_au_Louvre-2025-14] Two members,[^cite_note-PorterBreeden-15] with their faces concealed with [balaclavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaclava_\(clothing\)),[^cite_note-Reuters-13] gained entry to a [first-storey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey)[^cite_note-Cambriolage_au_Louvre-2025-14][^cite_note-BBC-know-10] balcony of the building's south side using a [furniture lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture_lift?action=edit\&redlink=1), which are common in Paris.[^cite_note-PorterBreeden-15][^cite_note-\:0-16]

From the balcony, they used a [disc cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_cutter)[^cite_note-Cambriolage_au_Louvre-2025-14][^cite_note-adamsonap-17] to cut through a glass window to access the gallery, triggering security alarms.[^cite_note-PorterBreeden-15] After threatening the guards with their power tools, the thieves took nine pieces from two glass display cases.[^cite_note-BBC-know-10][^cite_note-Vandoorne-18] Leaving the museum using the lift, they met up with two other members of the crew who were waiting on [motor scooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooter_\(motorcycle\)).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-\:0-16] During their exit, they dropped the [Crown of Empress Eugénie](1975332.md) in the street, reducing their haul to eight items.[^cite_note-20] They attempted to set fire to the basket of the lift before fleeing.[^cite_note-PorterBreeden-15] The thieves fled along the banks of the [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine) to the [Boulevard Périphérique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_P%C3%A9riph%C3%A9rique) and then took the [A6 autoroute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_autoroute) southward.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The entire robbery took just under eight minutes, of which the thieves spent only four in the building proper.[^cite_note-minutes-23]

## Investigation

The Paris prosecutor's office opened an investigation into the incident,[^cite_note-PorterBreeden-15] with the prosecutor [Laure Beccuau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laure_Beccuau) saying that 60 people had been assigned to the investigation.[^cite_note-36] Interior minister [Laurent Nuñez](60527935.md) said in a later statement that more than 100 investigators were assigned;[^cite_note-Henley-37] more than 150 pieces of evidence were collected, including [fingerprints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerprints) and [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_DNA_analysis).[^cite_note-38]

Police reviewed [CCTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCTV) footage along the escape route.[^cite_note-BBC-know-10] Photographs showed a small truck parked next to the museum with its lift extended up to a first-floor balcony.[^cite_note-39] *[Le Parisien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Parisien)* reported that the police recovered [power tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_tool), a [blowtorch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowtorch), gasoline, gloves, a [walkie-talkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkie-talkie), a blanket and a crown at the scene.[^cite_note-Vandoorne-18]

Four people were suspected of participating in the robbery itself.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-bbc1026-41] Two individuals were arrested in connection with the robbery on 25 October, one of them at [Charles de Gaulle Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport) while trying to board a plane bound for [Algeria](358.md). Both were already known to police from past burglary cases. Investigators said they matched trace DNA evidence recovered from a helmet left at the scene of the crime to one of the suspects.[^cite_note-globo1026-42][^cite_note-bbc1026-41][^cite_note-43] After four days, the prosecutor's office revealed that the two arrested had partially admitted to their involvement,[^cite_note-44] and that they were charged with *vols en bande organisée* (organised gang theft) and *association de malfaiteurs* (criminal conspiracy).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-atv-47]

Five more suspects were arrested on 29 October, only one of them thought to be part of the four-man team.[^cite_note-48] Two were charged before a magistrate on 31 October: a 37-year-old-man (theft and criminal conspiracy) and a 38-year-old-woman (organised theft and criminal conspiracy with a view to committing a crime).[^cite_note-49] Both denied any involvement.[^cite_note-50] Three were released.[^cite_note-51]

The prosecutor's office reported on 25 November that four more suspects from Paris were detained by police.[^cite_note-52] One was charged before a magistrate on 27 November: a 39-year-old man (organised gang theft and criminal conspiracy). The man is believed to be the final member of the four-man team.[^cite_note-finalarrest-53] The other three detained on 27 November were released.[^cite_note-54]

The location of the jewels remains unknown as of August 2026.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-\:1-56]

## Aftermath

The museum was evacuated and closed to the public immediately after the robbery.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] [Interpol](14338129.md) added the jewels to its Stolen Works of Art database on 20 October.[^cite_note-60] The museum re-opened on 22 October, with the Galerie d'Apollon reopening on 22 July 2026.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

After the robbery, French President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) ordered a "speeding-up" of the implementation of the recommendations made during a previous audit.[^cite_note-Henley-37]

The museum's director, [Laurence des Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_des_Cars), appeared before the [Senate](567323.md)'s Committee on Culture on 22 October 2025 to address questions by the lawmakers, and acknowledged shortcomings in the museum's surveillance systems, noting that parts of the building were not adequately covered by external cameras at the time of the theft.[^cite_note-BBC_hearing-64] Des Cars confirmed that she had offered her resignation to the Ministry of Culture on the day of the burglary, but that the offer was declined.[^cite_note-BBC_hearing-64][^cite_note-LouvreDirector-27] On 24 February 2026, it was announced that President Macron had accepted director des Cars' resignation.[^cite_note-65]

As a precaution, a number of precious jewels were transferred from the museum to the [Bank of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_France) in the weeks following the heist.[^cite_note-66] In February 2026, the Louvre began the bidding process to hire a restorer for the [Crown of Empress Eugénie](1975332.md), which had been damaged after having been pulled through a hole in its case far too small to entirely fit and later being dropped by the thieves during the escape part.[^cite_note-\:1-56]

## Reactions

President Macron condemned the robbery, saying it was "an attack on a heritage that we cherish because it is our history." He pledged to recover the jewels and bring the perpetrators to justice.[^cite_note-67] Justice Minister [Gérald Darmanin](54070416.md) said it gave France "a terrible image" but was confident the perpetrators would be apprehended.[^cite_note-lockhart-68] Interior Minister [Laurent Nuñez](60527935.md) called it a "major robbery" and said the stolen goods were "of immeasurable heritage value".[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-adamsonap-17] Culture Minister [Rachida Dati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachida_Dati) told [TF1](571365.md): "We saw some footage: they don't target people, they enter calmly in four minutes, smash display cases, take their loot, and leave. No violence, very professional."[^cite_note-70]

Opposition politicians have used the robbery to harshly criticise the current government. [Jordan Bardella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Bardella) of [National Rally](67119.md) called it a humiliation and asked how far the State will continue to decay.[^cite_note-lockhart-68] [Éric Ciotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Ciotti), [UDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_the_Right_for_the_Republic) leader in the [National Assembly](299616.md), described the robbery as an "ultimate symbol of \[the current government's] collapse". He also said that by allowing such an event to take place "the entire nation is threatened". [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party) senator [Ian Brossat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Brossat) also criticised the government's feeble reaction to a recent strike over "untenable conditions" at the museum.[^cite_note-PorterBreeden-15]

Böcker, the company that made the furniture lift used in the theft, published a social media post that went [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_phenomenon) highlighting the speed and quietness of their products.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

A report by the *[Cour des Comptes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cour_des_Comptes_\(France\))* found that in the years prior to the heist, the museum had "favoured operations that were visible and attractive" over essential maintenance and security upgrades. French journalist [Didier Rykner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Rykner) accused the museum's director, [Laurence des Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_des_Cars), of prioritising her flagship projects over the basic protection of existing collections.[^cite_note-73]
