"**Bangaranga**" ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Бангаранга)[^cite_note-5] is a song by Bulgarian singer [Dara](31712238.md), released on 2 March 2026 and produced by [Monoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoir) and [Dimitris Kontopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Kontopoulos). It [represented Bulgaria](81478817.md) in the [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md), winning the contest with 516 points. It was the first entry since [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2017) to top both the public and jury votes, and it gave [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) its first victory in the competition.[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-7] The song reached number one in Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, Germany, Greece, Israel, Lithuania, Sweden, and Turkey, as well as the top ten in Australia, Finland, Luxembourg, and Switzerland. It also reached number 90 on the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) chart.

## Background and composition

"Bangaranga" was written and composed by [Darina 'Dara' Yotova](31712238.md), [Anne Judith Wik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Judith_Wik), [Cristian 'Monoir' Tarcea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoir), and [Dimitris Kontopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Kontopoulos); the latter two were responsible for the production.[^cite_note-\:0-6] The song is partly inspired by the Bulgarian tradition of [Kukeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukeri).[^cite_note-8] Its title is in [Jamaican Patois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_Patois) and has been translated as 'a joyful kind of disorder',[^cite_note-violins-4] 'uproar',[^cite_note-wins-9] or 'mischief'.[^cite_note-telegraph-2] Dara stated that she wrote the lyrics to help her overcome anxiety and [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder) (ADHD), following her diagnosis with the condition in 2025.[^cite_note-wins-9]

## Music video and promotion

Along with the song's release, an accompanying [music video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_video) directed by K2ID Productions, was released on 18 March 2026 through the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md)'s [YouTube](3524766.md) channel.[^cite_note-10]

To promote "Bangaranga" ahead of the [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md), Dara performed at several Eurovision pre‑parties throughout the months of March and April. She appeared at the *Nordic Eurovision Party 2026* at [Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Oslo) in [Oslo](22309.md), Norway, on 21 March 2026,[^cite_note-11] and at the *Eurovision Pre‑Party Bucharest 2026* at [Arenele Romane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenele_Romane?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Bucharest](36877.md), Romania, on 18 April 2026.[^cite_note-12]

[Neil McCormick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_McCormick) of *[The Telegraph](23797577.md)* praised the song's catchiness.[^cite_note-telegraph-2] Jon O'Brien of *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_\(website\))* ranked "Bangaranga" seventh among all Eurovision 2026 entries, calling it a "solid return" for Bulgaria.[^cite_note-vulture-17] [NPR](29697232.md)'s [Glen Weldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Weldon) placed the song fifth on his top-ten list,[^cite_note-NPR-18] while Ed Potton of *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* placed it first in his ranking of the 25 finalists.[^cite_note-times-15] The song was also included in unranked top-ten lists by Robert van Gijssel and Els de Grefte of *[de Volkskrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Volkskrant)*, and by Maria Sherman of the [Associated Press](18935732.md).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

The song's refrain drew comparisons to "[A Little Bit Alexis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Little_Bit_Alexis)" from *[Schitt's Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schitt's_Creek)*,[^cite_note-vulture-17][^cite_note-NPR-18] while its verses were likened to "[Uninvited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uninvited_\(song\))" by [Alanis Morissette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanis_Morissette).[^cite_note-vulture-17] Mark Savage of the [BBC](19344654.md) described the song as "brilliantly unhinged and full of sass" while also noting it was "totally lacking in substance".[^cite_note-21]

Following its Eurovision victory, German [Chancellor](20890626.md) [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) played the song to welcome Bulgaria's [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) during a meeting in [Berlin](3354.md).[^cite_note-22]

## Eurovision Song Contest 2026

Main article: [Bulgaria in the Eurovision Song Contest 2026](81478817.md)

### National selection

After qualifying for the second show,[^cite_note-23] Dara was selected as the winning artist by a combination of votes from the public and the judges of the show.[^cite_note-24] On 28 February, for the song selection, she performed three candidate songs; among the three, "Bangaranga" finished first in both the jury vote and the public vote.[^cite_note-25] Dara also stated that she had considered withdrawing from competing at Eurovision owing to the negative comments following her win and speculation that the artist final was rigged.[^cite_note-26]

### At Eurovision



Dara performing "Bangaranga" at the [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md)

The [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md) took place at the [Wiener Stadthalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Stadthalle) in [Vienna](55866.md), Austria, and consisted of two semi‑finals (held on 12 and 14 May respectively) and a final on 16 May 2026. During the allocation draw held on 12 January 2026, Bulgaria was drawn to compete in the second semi‑final and was allocated to the first half of the show.[^cite_note-27] Dara was later drawn to open the semi‑final.[^cite_note-28] Dara's performance was choreographed by [Fredrik Rydman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrik_Rydman) and [Keisha von Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisha_von_Arnold?action=edit\&redlink=1), and she was backed by dancers Iker Cederblom Herrera, Ellinea Siambalis, Lisa Högström, and Mateo Cordova Pomo.[^cite_note-29] She qualified for the grand final, having won the second semi‑final with 278 points.[^cite_note-30]

In the final, Dara won with 516 points, securing victory in both the jury vote and the public vote, which gave [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) its first win in the history of the Eurovision Song Contest.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]
