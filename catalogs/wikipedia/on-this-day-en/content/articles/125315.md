**Lahore**[^cite_note-20] is the capital and largest city of the [Pakistani province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_province) of [Punjab](24751.md). It is the [second-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Pakistan_by_population) city in Pakistan, after [Karachi](17123.md), and 27th [largest in the world](14649921.md), with a population of over 14 million.[^cite_note-21] Lahore is one of country's major industrial, educational and economic hubs,[^cite_note-thebusinessyear.com-15][^cite_note-finance.gov.pk-16] and is considered the historic capital and cultural centre of the wider [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) region.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Luther1990-24]

The site of the city has been inhabited for over two [millennia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennia), although Lahore itself rose to prominence in the late 10th century.[^cite_note-antiquity-25] At times Lahore has served as the capital of several empires, including the [Odi Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odi_Shahi_dynasty), [Ghaznavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid_Empire) and [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate). It was one of the principal cities in the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire), being its capital city during the reign of [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar). During this period, it was one of the largest cities in the world.[^cite_note-Dyson-26] In the 18th century Lahore fell into a period of decay while being contested among the [Durranis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) and the [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs); it regained some of its former grandeur when it became capital of the Sikh [Kingdom of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lahore) in the early 19th century. It was annexed by the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in 1849 and became the capital of [Punjab Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_\(British_India\)).[^cite_note-27] Lahore was central to the independence movements of British India, with the city being the site of both the [Declaration of Indian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna_Swaraj) and the [resolution for the establishment of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Resolution). It suffered rioting during the [partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India) in 1947.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004175:"Of_all_the_capital_cities_caught_in_the_drama_of_partition,_it_was_Lahore_that_experienced_the_worst_of_the_violence_and_carnage_that_took_place_in_1947._It_became_the_most_disputed_territory_in_the_claims_and_counter_claims_that_were_made_before_the_Boundary_Commission._All_three_communities_of_the_Punjab—Muslims,_Sikhs_and_Hindus—staked_a_claim_to_it._The_violence_which_Lahore_experienced_was_unprecedented_even_by_the_horrific_standards_of_1947,_and_when_it_hadĺ_subsided,_what_emerged_was_a_city_which_had_been_completely_'cleansed'_of_its_'minority'_communities."-28] Following the [independence of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Pakistan), Lahore remained the capital of Punjab province.

Lahore is located in central-eastern Punjab, along the [Ravi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River), and has a strong cultural and political influence over the country being capital of its most populated province.[^cite_note-29] It is considered a major [socially liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socially_liberal),[^cite_note-30] [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism),[^cite_note-31] and [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism) city in the country.[^cite_note-32] It is a UNESCO [City of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Literature) and major centre for Pakistan's publishing industry, and is as such a foremost centre of Pakistan's literary scene. The city is also a major centre of education,[^cite_note-2005_pg7_15-33] with some of the country's leading universities based there.[^cite_note-34] Lahore is home to Pakistan's [Punjabi film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lollywood), and is a major centre of *[Qawwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali)* music.[^cite_note-Windsor_all-35] The city hosts a significant portion of [Pakistan's tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Pakistan),[^cite_note-Windsor_all-35][^cite_note-Planet-36] with major attractions including the [Walled City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore), the [Badshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque) and [Wazir Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque) mosques, as well as several [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) and [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) shrines. Lahore is also home to the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort) and [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens,_Lahore), both of which are UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-Planet-36]

## Etymology

Main article: [Etymology of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Lahore)

The origin of Lahore's name is unclear. The city's name has been variously recorded by early Muslim historians as *Luhawar*, *Lūhār*, and *Rahwar*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif18925-37] The Iranian polymath and geographer, [Abu Rayhan al-Biruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Biruni), referred to the city as *Luhāwar* in his 11th century work, *Qanun*,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif18925-37] while the poet [Amir Khusrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Khusrow), who lived during the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) period, recorded the city's name as *Lāhanūr*.[^cite_note-\:22-38] [Yaqut al-Hamawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqut_al-Hamawi) records the city's name as *Lawhūr*, mentioning that it was famously known as *Lahāwar*.[^cite_note-39] Persian historian [Firishta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firishta) mentions the city as *Alahwar* in his work, with *al-Ahwar* being another variation.[^cite_note-briggs-40][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink2002a121-41]

One theory suggests that Lahore's name is a corruption of the word *Ravāwar,* as R to L shifts are common in languages derived from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit).[^cite_note-\:32-42] *Ravāwar* is the simplified pronunciation of the name *Iravatyāwar,* a name possibly derived from the [Ravi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River), known as the Iravati River in the *[Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas)*.[^cite_note-\:32-42][^cite_note-43] Another theory suggests the city's name may derive from the word *Lohar*, meaning "blacksmith".[^cite_note-44]

According to a legend,[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Lahore's name derives from *Lavpur* or *Lavapuri* (City of *[Lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_\(Ramayana\))*),[^cite_note-47] and is said to have been founded by Prince Lava,[^cite_note-48] the son of [Sita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sita) and [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama). The same account attributes the founding of nearby [Kasur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasur) to his twin brother [Kusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusha_\(Ramayana\)),[^cite_note-49] though it was actually established in the 16th century.[^cite_note-50]

### Origins

No definitive record of Lahore's early history exists, and its ambiguous historical background has given rise to various theories about its establishment and history.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeville2006xii-51]

[Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great)'s historians make no mention of any city near Lahore's location during his invasion in 326 BCE, suggesting the city had not been founded by that point or was not noteworthy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892352-52] [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) mentions in his *[Geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_\(Ptolemy\))* a city called *Labokla* situated near the [Chenab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenab_River) and Ravi rivers which may have been in reference to ancient Lahore, or an abandoned predecessor of the city.[^cite_note-53] Chinese pilgrim [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) gave a vivid description of a large and prosperous unnamed city that may have been Lahore when he [visited the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taank_Kingdom) in 630 CE during his tour of India.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

The first document that mentions Lahore by name is the *[Hudud al-'Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudud_al-'Alam)* ("The Regions of the World"), written in 982 CE,[^cite_note-55] in which Lahore is mentioned as a town which had "impressive temples, large markets and huge orchards".[^cite_note-antiquity-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink2002b\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-56]

Few other references to Lahore remain from before its capture by the [Ghaznavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) Sultan [Mahmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) in the 11th century. During this time, Lahore appears to have served as the capital of Punjab under maharaja [Anandapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anandapala) of the [Odi Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odi_Shahi_dynasty), who moved his capital there from [Udabhandapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udabhandapura).[^cite_note-bosworth-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWink2002b235-57]

### Mediaeval era

Main article: [Early Muslim period in Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_period_in_Lahore)

#### Ghaznavid



The [Data Darbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_Darbar) shrine was built to commemorate saint [Ali Hujwiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hujwiri), who lived in the city during 11th century.

Sultan Mahmud conquered Lahore between 1020 and 1027, making it part of Ghaznavid Empire.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] He appointed [Malik Ayaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Ayaz) as its governor in 1021. During the reign of Sultan [Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_of_Ghazna), Malik Ayaz rebuilt and repopulated the city, which had been devastated after the Ghaznavid invasion. He also erected city walls and a masonry fort in 1037–1040 on the ruins of a previous one.[^cite_note-Petersen1996-58] During his tenure a confederation of Hindu princes unsuccessfully laid siege to Lahore in 1043–44.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

Lahore was formally made the eastern capital of Ghaznavid Empire during the reign of [Khusrau Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khusrau_Shah) in 1152.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59][^cite_note-60] After the fall of Ghazni in 1163, It became the sole capital.[^cite_note-61] Under their patronage, poets and scholars from other cities of Ghaznavid Empire congregated in Lahore.[^cite_note-Ikram-62] The city became a cultural and academic centre, renowned for [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-WescoatWolschke-Bulmahn199622-64] The entire city of Lahore during the Ghaznavid era was probably located west of the modern [Shah Alami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Alami) Bazaar and north of the [Bhatti Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhatti_Gate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

#### Mamluk

Following the [siege of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Lahore_\(1186\)) in 1186, the [Ghurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid) ruler [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_of_Ghor) captured the city and imprisoned Khusrau Malik,[^cite_note-bosworth-54] thus ending Ghaznavid rule over Lahore. Lahore was the first capital of the [Mamluk dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_dynasty_\(Delhi\)) of what would later come to be known as the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) following the assassination of Muhammad of Ghor in 1206. Under the reign of Mamluk sultan [Qutb ud-Din Aibak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_ud-Din_Aibak), Lahore attracted poets and scholars from medieval [Muslim World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_World). Lahore at this time had more poets writing in Persian than any other city.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Following the death of Aibak, Lahore first came under the control of the governor of Multan, [Nasir ad-Din Qabacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_ad-Din_Qabacha), and then was briefly captured in 1217 by the sultan in Delhi, [Iltutmish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iltutmish).[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

In an alliance with local [Khokhars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khokhars) in 1223, the [Khwarazmian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarazmian_Empire) sultan [Jalal al-Din Mangburni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_al-Din_Mangburni) captured Lahore after fleeing from [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan)'s invasion of his realm.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Mangburni then fled from Lahore to the city of [Uch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uch) after Iltutmish's army re-captured Lahore in 1228.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]



Tomb of Qutb-ud-Din Aybak in [Anarkali Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarkali_Bazaar), Lahore

The threat of Mongol invasions and political instability in Lahore caused future sultans to regard Delhi as a safer capital for the sultanate,[^cite_note-jackson-67] even though Delhi was considered a forward base while Lahore was widely considered as the centre of Islamic culture in northeastern Punjab.[^cite_note-jackson-67]

Lahore came under progressively weaker central rule under Iltutmish's descendants in Delhi, to the point that governors in the city acted with great autonomy.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Actual Sultanate rule on Lahore lasted only a few decades until the locals reclaimed their autonomy.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Lahore was sacked and ruined by the Mongol army in 1241,[^cite_note-68] with the Mongols holding the city for a few years under the rule of the Mongol chief [Toghrul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toghrul).[^cite_note-jackson-67]

In 1266, sultan [Balban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghiyas_ud_din_Balban) reconquered Lahore, but in 1287 under the Mongol ruler [Temür Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tem%C3%BCr_Khan),[^cite_note-jackson-67] the Mongols again overran northern Punjab. Because of Mongol invasions, Lahore region became a city on a frontier, with the region's administrative centre shifted south to [Dipalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipalpur).[^cite_note-bosworth-54] The Mongols [again invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_India_\(1297%E2%80%931298\)) northern Punjab in 1298, though their advance was eventually stopped by [Ulugh Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Khan), brother of sultan [Alauddin Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Khalji) of Delhi.[^cite_note-jackson-67] The Mongols again attacked Lahore in 1305.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeville2006xiii-69]

#### Tughluq

Lahore briefly flourished again under the reign of [Ghiyath al-Din Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghiyath_al-Din_Tughlaq) (Ghazi Malik) of the [Tughlaq dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaq_dynasty) between 1320 and 1325, though the city was again sacked in 1329 by [Tarmashirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarmashirin) of the Central Asian [Chagatai Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate), and then again by the Mongol chief Hülechü.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Khokhars seized Lahore in 1342,[^cite_note-70] but the city was retaken by Ghazi Malik's son, [Muhammad bin Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Tughlaq).[^cite_note-bosworth-54] The weakened city then fell into obscurity and was captured once more by the Khokhar chief [Shaikha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaikha_Khokhar) in 1394.[^cite_note-dsal.uchicago.edu-71] By the time the Mongol conqueror [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) captured the city in 1398 from Shaikha, he did not loot it because it was no longer wealthy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeville2006xii-51]

#### Late Sultanates

Timur gave control of the Lahore region to [Khizr Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khizr_Khan), governor of Multan, who later established the [Sayyid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_dynasty) in 1414, the fourth dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate.[^cite_note-Ahmed-72] The city was twice besieged by [Jasrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasrat), the ruler of [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot), during the reign of [Mubarak Shah II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Shah_II), the longest of which being in 1431–32.[^cite_note-jackson-67] To combat Jasrat, the city was granted by the Sayyid dynasty to [Bahlul Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahlul_Lodi) in 1441, though Lodi would then displace the Sayyids in 1451 by establishing himself upon the throne of Delhi.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

Bahlul Lodi installed his cousin, Tatar Khan, to be governor of the city, though Tatar Khan died in battle with [Sikandar Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_Lodi) in 1485.[^cite_note-dhillon-73] Governorship of Lahore was transferred by Sikandar Lodi to Umar Khan Sarwani, who quickly left the management of this city to his son Said Khan Sarwani. Said Khan was removed from power in 1500 by Sikandar Lodi, and Lahore came under the governorship of [Daulat Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Khan_Lodi), son of Tatar Khan and former employer of [Guru Nanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Nanak), the founder of [Sikhism](27964.md).[^cite_note-dhillon-73]

### Mughals

Main articles: [Subah of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah_of_Lahore) and [Mughal period in Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_period_in_Lahore)

#### Early Mughal

[Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur), the founder of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire), captured and sacked Lahore and Dipalpur, in 1525.[^cite_note-bosworth-54][^cite_note-74] The city became a refuge to [Humayun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun) and his brother [Kamran Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamran_Mirza) when [Sher Shah Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Shah_Suri) rose in power in the Gangetic plains, displacing Mughals. Sher Shah Suri seized Lahore in 1540, though Humayun reconquered Lahore in February 1555.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] The establishment of Mughal rule eventually led to the most prosperous era of Lahore's history.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Lahore's prosperity and central position has yielded more Mughal-era monuments in Lahore than either [Delhi](37756.md) or [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra).[^cite_note-mughaleconomist-75]

By the time of the rule of the Mughal empire's greatest emperors, a majority of Lahore's residents did not live within the walled city itself but instead lived in suburbs that had spread outside the city's walls.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] Only 9 of the 36 urban quarters around Lahore, known as *guzars*, were located within the city walls during the [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] During this period, Lahore was closely tied to smaller market towns known as *qasbahs*, such as [Kasur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasur) and [Eminabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminabad), as well as [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar) and [Batala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batala) in modern-day India, which in turn, linked to supply chains in villages surrounding each *qasbah*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

#### Akbar

Beginning in 1584, Lahore became the Mughal capital when Akbar began re-fortifying the city's ruined citadel, laying the foundations for the revival of the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] A Lahore-born commander, [Shahbaz Khan Kamboh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahbaz_Khan_Kamboh) was one of his foremost generals. Akbar made Lahore one of his original twelve *[subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah)* or provinces,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] and in 1585–86, relegated governorship of the city and *subah* to [Bhagwant Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagwant_Das), brother of [Mariam-uz-Zamani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariam-uz-Zamani), who was commonly known as "Jodhabhai".[^cite_note-Chandra-76]



The Alamgiri Gate of the Lahore Fort

Akbar also rebuilt the city's walls and extended their perimeter east of the Shah Alami bazaar to encompass the sparsely populated area of Rarra Maidan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. #### Jahangir



The [mausoleum of Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Jahangir) in Shahdara Bagh

During the reign of Emperor [Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir) in the early 17th century, Lahore's bazaars were noted to be vibrant, frequented by foreigners, and stocked with a wide array of goods.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] In 1606, Jahangir's rebel son [Khusrau Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khusrau_Mirza) laid siege to Lahore after obtaining the blessings of the Sikh [Guru Arjan Dev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Arjan_Dev).[^cite_note-Holt-78] Jahangir quickly defeated his son at Bhairowal, and the roots of Mughal–Sikh animosity grew.[^cite_note-Holt-78] Sikh Guru Arjan Dev was executed in Lahore in 1606 for his involvement in the rebellion.[^cite_note-79] Jahangir chose to be buried in Lahore, and [his tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Jahangir) was built in Lahore's [Shahdara Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahdara_Bagh) suburb in 1637 by his wife [Nur Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Jahan), [whose tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Nur_Jahan) is also nearby.

#### Shah Jahan



Wazir Khan Mosque painting by [William Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Carpenter_\(painter\)) (1866)

Jahangir's son, [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan) (reigned 1628–1658), was born in Lahore in 1592. He renovated large portions of the Lahore Fort with luxurious white marble and erected the iconic [Naulakha Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naulakha_Pavilion) in 1633.[^cite_note-UNESCO-80] Shah Jahan lavished Lahore with some of its most celebrated and iconic monuments, such as the [Shalamar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens,_Lahore) in 1641. His Punjabi viceroy and royal physician [Wazir Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_\(Lahore\)) also built a number of monuments in the city, including the richly decorated [Wazir Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque), the [Wazir Khan Baradari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Baradari), and the [Shahi Hammam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahi_Hammam), during his tenure.[^cite_note-81] The population of pre-modern Lahore probably reached its zenith during his reign, with suburban districts home to perhaps 6 times as many compared to within the [Walled City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

#### Aurangzeb



Aerial view of [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque)

Shah Jahan's son, [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb), last of the great Mughal Emperors, further contributed to the development of Lahore. Aurangzeb built the Alamgiri Bund embankment along the Ravi river in 1662 to prevent its shifting course from threatening the city's walls.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] The area near the embankment grew into a fashionable locality, with several nearby pleasure gardens laid by Lahore's gentry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] The largest of Lahore's Mughal monuments, the [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque), was raised during Aurangzeb's reign in 1673, as well as the iconic Alamgiri Gate of the Lahore fort in 1674.[^cite_note-82]

#### Late Mughal



The [Sunehri Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunehri_Mosque,_Lahore) was built in the walled city in the early 18th century, when the Mughal Empire was in decline.

Civil wars regarding succession to the Mughal throne following Aurangzeb's death in 1707 led to weakening control over Lahore from Delhi, and a prolonged period of decline in Lahore.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83] Mughal preoccupation with the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathas) in the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) eventually resulted in Lahore being governed by a series of governors who pledged nominal allegiance to the ever-weaker Mughal emperors in Delhi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

Mughal Emperor [Bahadur Shah I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahadur_Shah_I) died enroute to Lahore as part of a campaign in 1711 to subdue Sikh rebels under the leadership of [Banda Singh Bahadur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_Singh_Bahadur).[^cite_note-bosworth-54] His sons fought a battle outside Lahore in 1712 for succession to the Mughal crown, with [Jahandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahandar_Shah) winning the throne.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Sikh rebels were defeated during the reign of [Farrukhsiyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrukhsiyar) when Abd as-Samad and Zakariyya Khan suppressed them.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

[Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah)'s brief invasion of the Mughal Empire [in early 1739](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah's_invasion_of_the_Mughal_Empire) wrested control away from [Zakariya Khan Bahadur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakariya_Khan_Bahadur). Though Khan was able to win back control after the Persian armies had left,[^cite_note-bosworth-54] the trade routes had shifted away from Lahore, and south towards [Kandahar](17260.md) instead.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] Indus ports near the Arabian Sea that served Lahore also silted up during this time, reducing the city's importance even further.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

Struggles between Zakariyya Khan's sons following his death in 1745 further weakened Muslim control over Lahore, thus leaving the city in a power vacuum, and vulnerable to foreign marauders.[^cite_note-84]

### Durrani invasions

The [Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) ruler [Ahmad Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) occupied Lahore [in 1748](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lahore_\(1748\)).[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Following Ahmed Shah Durrani's quick retreat, the Mughals entrusted Lahore to Mu’īn al-Mulk [Mir Mannu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Mannu).[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Ahmad Shah again invaded in 1751, forcing Mir Mannu into signing a treaty that nominally subjected Lahore to Durrani rule.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] Lahore was third time conquered by Ahmad Shah [in 1752](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lahore_\(1752\)). The Mughal [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mughal_grand_viziers) [Ghazi-ud-Din Imad al-Mulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_ud-Din_Khan_Feroze_Jung_III) seized Lahore in 1756, provoking Ahmad Shah to invade for fourth time in 1757, after which he placed the city under the rule of his son, [Timur Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur_Shah_Durrani).[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

Durrani rule was interrupted when Lahore was conquered by [Adina Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adina_Beg) [Arain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arain) with the assistance of Marathas in 1758 during their [campaigns against Afghans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan%E2%80%93Maratha_War).[^cite_note-K.RoyIHB-85] After Adina Beg's untimely death in 1758, however, Marathas temporarily occupied the city. The following year, the Durranis again marched into Lahore and conquered it.[^cite_note-86] After the Durranis withdrew from the city in 1765, Sikh forces quickly occupied it.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] By this time, the city had been ravaged several time and had lost all of its former grandeur.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] The Durranis invaded two more times — in 1797 and 1798 — under [Shah Zaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Zaman), but the Sikhs re-occupied the city after both invasions as the Durranis were forced to attend to other problems on their western borders.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

#### Early

Expanding Sikh [Misls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misl) secured control over Lahore in 1767, when the [Bhangi Misl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhangi_Misl) state captured the city.[^cite_note-eos-87] In 1780, the city was divided among three rulers: [Gujjar Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujjar_Singh_Bhangi), Lahna Singh, and [Sobha Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobha_Singh_\(Sikh_chieftain\)). Instability resulting from this arrangement allowed nearby [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar) to establish itself as the area's primary commercial centre in place of Lahore.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

Ahmad Shah Durrani's grandson, Zaman Shah, captured Lahore in 1796, and again in 1798–99.[^cite_note-bosworth-54] [Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh) negotiated with the Afghans for the post of *[subahdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subahdar)* to control Lahore following the second invasion.[^cite_note-bosworth-54]

By the end of the 18th century, the city's population drastically declined, with its remaining residents living within the city walls, while the extramural suburbs lay abandoned, forcing travellers to pass through abandoned and ruined suburbs for a few miles before reaching the city's gates.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

#### Sikh Empire



The [Lahore Darbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire#Government) by Ágoston Schoefft (1842)

In the aftermath of Zaman Shah's 1799 invasion of Punjab, Ranjit Singh, of nearby [Gujranwala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujranwala), began to consolidate his position. Singh was able to seize control of the region after a series of battles with the Bhangi chiefs who had seized Lahore in 1780.[^cite_note-bosworth-54][^cite_note-KakshiPathak2007-88] His army marched to Anarkali, where according to tradition, the gatekeeper of the [Lohari Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohari_Gate,_Lahore), Mukham Din Chaudhry, opened the gates allowing Ranjit Singh's army to enter Lahore.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83] After capturing Lahore, Sikh soldiers immediately began plundering Muslim areas of the city until their actions were reined in by Ranjit Singh.[^cite_note-89]



The marble [Hazuri Bagh Baradari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazuri_Bagh_Baradari) was built in 1818 to celebrate Ranjit Singh's acquisition of the [Koh-i-Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koh-i-Noor) diamond.

[^cite_note-bansal2-90]

Ranjit Singh's rule restored some of Lahore's lost grandeur, but at the expense of destroying the remaining Mughal architecture for building materials.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] He established a mint in the city in 1800,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83] and moved into the Mughal palace at the Lahore fort after repurposing it for his own use in governing the Sikh Empire.[^cite_note-91] In 1801, he established a [Gurdwara Ram Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Janam_Asthan_Guru_Ram_Das) to mark the site where [Guru Ram Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Ram_Das) was born in 1534.

Lahore became the empire's administrative capital, though the nearby economic centre of Amritsar had also been established as the empire's spiritual capital by 1802.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] By 1812, Singh had mostly refurbished the city's defences by adding a second circuit of outer walls surrounding Akbar's original walls, with the two separated by a moat. Singh also partially restored Shah Jahan's decaying Shalimar Gardens[^cite_note-92] and built the [Hazuri Bagh Baradari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazuri_Bagh_Baradari) in 1818 to celebrate his capture of the [Koh-i-Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koh-i-Noor) diamond from [Shuja Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuja_Shah_Durrani) in 1813.[^cite_note-bansal-93] He erected the [Gurdwara Dera Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Dera_Sahib) to mark the site of [Guru Arjan Dev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Arjan_Dev)'s death (1606). The Sikh royal court also endowed religious architecture in the city, including a number of Sikh gurdwaras, Hindu temples, and havelis.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]



The [Tomb of Asif Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Asif_Khan) was one of several monuments plundered for its precious building materials during the Sikh period.

[^cite_note-GHF22-96]

Under Ranjit Singh's rule, Mughal monuments suffered during the Sikh period as his armies plundered most of Lahore's most precious Mughal monuments, and stripped the white marble from several monuments to send to different parts of the Sikh Empire during his reign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESidhwa2005\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83][^cite_note-Archaeological_Survey_of_India-98] Ranjit Singh's army also desecrated the Badshahi Mosque by converting it into an ammunition depot and a stable for horses.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESidhwa2005\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. #### Late

The [Lahore Durbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire#Government) underwent a quick succession of rulers after the death of Ranjit Singh. His son [Kharak Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharak_Singh) died on 6 November 1840, soon after taking the throne. On that same day, the next appointed successor to the throne, [Nau Nihal Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nau_Nihal_Singh), died in an accident at the gardens of Hazuri Bagh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83] Maharaja [Sher Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Singh) was then selected as Maharajah, though his claim to the throne was quickly challenged by [Chand Kaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chand_Kaur), widow of Kharak Singh and mother of Nau Nihal Singh, who quickly seized the throne.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83] Sher Singh raised an army that attacked Chand Kaur's forces in Lahore on 14 January 1841. His soldiers mounted weaponry on the minarets of the Badshahi Mosque to target Chand Kaur's forces in the Lahore fort, destroying the fort's historic *Diwan-e-Aam*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESidhwa2005\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-97] Kaur quickly ceded the throne, but Sher Sing was then assassinated in 1843 in Lahore's *Chah Miran* neighbourhood along with his wazir Dhiyan Singh.[^cite_note-bansal-93] Dhyan Singh's son, Hira Singh, sought to avenge his father's death by laying siege to Lahore to capture his father's assassins. The siege resulted in the capture of his father's murderer, Ajit Singh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83]



Maharaja [Sher Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Singh) attended by his council in [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort)

[Duleep Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duleep_Singh) was then crowned Maharajah, with Hira Singh as his *wazir*, but his power would be weakened by the continued infighting among Sikh nobles,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83] as well as confrontations against the British during the two [Anglo-Sikh wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Sikh_War_\(disambiguation\)).

After the conclusion of the two Anglo-Sikh wars, the Sikh Empire fell into disarray, resulting in the fall of the *Lahore Durbar*, and commencement of British rule after they captured Lahore and the wider Punjab region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-83]

The [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) seized control of Lahore in February 1846 from the collapsing Sikh state and occupied the rest of Punjab in 1848.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] Following the defeat of the Sikhs at the [Battle of Gujrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gujrat), British troops formally deposed Maharaja Duleep Singh in Lahore that same year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] Punjab was then annexed to the British Indian Empire in 1849.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

At the commencement of British rule, Lahore was estimated to have a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_\(human_biology\)) of 120,000.[^cite_note-Univ_Of_Minnesota_Press-101] Prior to annexation by the British, Lahore's environs consisted mostly of the [Walled City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) surrounded by plains interrupted by settlements to the south and east, such as [Mozang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozang_Chungi) and [Qila Gujar Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qila_Gujar_Singh), which have since been engulfed by modern Lahore. The plains between the settlements also contained the remains of Mughal gardens, tombs, and Sikh-era military structures.[^cite_note-102]

The British viewed Lahore's Walled City as a bed of potential social discontent and disease epidemics, and so largely left the inner city alone, while focusing development efforts in Lahore's suburban areas and Punjab's fertile countryside.[^cite_note-103] The British instead laid out their capital city in an area south of the Walled City that would first come to be known as "Donald's Town" before being renamed "Civil Station".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]

Under early British rule, formerly prominent Mughal-era monuments that were scattered throughout Civil Station were also re-purposed and sometimes desecrated – including the [Tomb of Anarkali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Anarkali), which the British had initially converted to clerical offices before re-purposing it as an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) church in 1851.[^cite_note-104] The 17th-century [Dai Anga Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Anga_Mosque) was converted into railway administration offices during this time, the tomb of Nawab Bahadur Khan was converted into a storehouse, and the tomb of Mir Mannu was used as a wine shop.[^cite_note-105] The British also used older structures to house municipal offices, such as the Civil Secretariat, Public Works Department, and Accountant General's Office.[^cite_note-106]



Constructed in the aftermath of the 1857 [Sepoy Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy_Mutiny), the design of the [Lahore Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Junction_railway_station) was highly militarised to defend the structure from further uprisings against British rule.

The British built the [Lahore Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Junction_railway_station) just outside the Walled City shortly after the [Sepoy Mutiny of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857); the station was therefore styled as a mediaeval castle to ward off any potential future uprisings, with thick walls, turrets, and holes to direct gun and cannon fire for the defence of the structure.[^cite_note-107] Lahore's most prominent government institutions and commercial enterprises came to be concentrated in Civil Station in a half-mile wide area flanking [The Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mall,_Lahore), where unlike in Lahore's military zone, the British and locals were allowed to mix.[^cite_note-Mall_Road-108] The Mall continues to serve as the epicentre of Lahore's civil administration, as well as one of its most fashionable commercial areas. The British also laid the spacious [Lahore Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Cantonment) to the southeast of the Walled City at the former village of Mian Mir, where unlike around The Mall, laws did exist against the mixing of different races.

Lahore was visited on 9 February 1870 by [Prince Alfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Alfred,_Duke_of_Edinburgh), [Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Edinburgh) – a visit in which he received delegations from the [Dogras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogra_dynasty) of [Jammu](43066157.md), Maharajas of [Patiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patiala), the Nawab of [Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur), and other rulers from various Punjabi states.[^cite_note-\:0-109] During the visit, he visited several of Lahore's major sights.[^cite_note-\:0-109] British authorities built several important structures around the time of the [Golden Jubilee of Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Queen_Victoria) (1887) in the distinctive [Indo-Saracenic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Saracenic_Revival_architecture), including the [Lahore Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Museum) and [Mayo School of Industrial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_College_of_Arts).[^cite_note-Making_Lahore_Modern_1894-110]

The British carried out a census of Lahore in 1901, and counted 20,691 houses in the Walled City.[^cite_note-111] An estimated 200,000 people lived in Lahore at this time.[^cite_note-Univ_Of_Minnesota_Press-101] Lahore's posh [Model Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Town,_Lahore) was established as a "garden town" suburb in 1921, while [Krishan Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishan_Nagar) locality was laid in the 1930s near The Mall and Walled City.



[The Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mall,_Lahore), Lahore's pre-independence commercial core, features many examples of colonial architecture.

Lahore played an important role in the independence movements of both India[^cite_note-Mall_Road-108] and Pakistan. The [Declaration of the Independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_the_Independence_of_India) was moved by [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru) and passed unanimously at midnight on 31 December 1929 at Lahore's [Bradlaugh Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradlaugh_Hall).[^cite_note-112] The Indian *[Swaraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_flag)* [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_flag) was adopted this time as well. Lahore's jail was used by the British to imprison independence activists such as [Jatin Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatin_Das), and was also where [Bhagat Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagat_Singh) was hanged in 1931.[^cite_note-113] Under the leadership of [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah), the [All India Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Muslim_League) passed the [Lahore Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Resolution) in 1940, demanding the creation of Pakistan as a separate homeland for the Muslims of India.[^cite_note-114]

### Partition

The future of the city of Lahore was [fiercely contested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Line#Lahore) during partition.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004175-115] According to the 1941 census, the city of Lahore had a population of 671,659, of which was 64.5% Muslim, with the remainder 35% being Hindu and Sikh, alongside a small Christian community.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004176-116][^cite_note-117] This population figure was disputed by Hindus and Sikhs before the Boundary Commission that would draw the [Radcliffe Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Line) to demarcate the border of the two new states based on religious demography, who argued that the city was only 54% Muslim based on 1945 ration card figures, and that Hindu and Sikh domination of the city's economy and educational institutions should trump Muslim demography.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004176-116] Two-thirds of shops, and 80% of Lahore's factories belonged to the Hindu and Sikh community.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004176-116] Indian journalist Kuldip Nayar claimed that [Cyril Radcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Radcliffe) had told him in 1971 that he originally had planned to give Lahore to the new [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India),[^cite_note-Dabas2017-118][^cite_note-Nayar2018-119][^cite_note-Kaul1991-120] but decided to place it within the [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan), which he saw as lacking a major city as he had already awarded [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta) to India.[^cite_note-Nayar-121][^cite_note-Dabas2017-118][^cite_note-Nayar2018-119]

As tensions grew over the city's uncertain fate, Lahore experienced [Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India)'s worst riots.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004176–177-122] Carnage ensued in which all three religious groups were both victims and perpetrators.[^cite_note-123] Early riots in March and April 1947 destroyed 6,000 of Lahore's 82,000 homes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004177-124] Violence continued to rise throughout the summer, despite the presence of armoured British personnel.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004177-124] Hindus and Sikhs began to leave the city *en masse* as their hopes that the Boundary Commission would award the city to India came to be regarded as increasingly unlikely. By late August 1947, 66% of Hindus and Sikhs had left the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004177-124] The Shah Alami Bazaar, once a largely Hindu quarter of the Walled City, was entirely burnt down during subsequent rioting.[^cite_note-dejonge-125]

When Pakistan's independence was declared on 14 August 1947, the Radcliffe Line had not yet been announced, and so cries of "Long live Pakistan" and "God is greatest" were heard intermittently with "Long live [Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan)" throughout the night.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004177-124] On 17 August 1947, Lahore was awarded to Pakistan on the basis of its Muslim majority in the 1941 census and was made capital of the Punjab province in the new state of Pakistan. The city's location near the Indian border meant that it received large numbers of refugees fleeing eastern Punjab and northern India, though it was able to accommodate them given the large stock of abandoned Hindu and Sikh properties that could be re-distributed to newly arrived refugees.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004177–178-126] In post-partition India, the loss of Lahore catalyzed the development of the new [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_architecture) capital city of [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh).[^cite_note-127]

[First Lady](10847.md) [Jacqueline Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Kennedy_Onassis) and President [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_\(President_of_Pakistan\)) travelled by car in Lahore, 1962

[Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) left Lahore with a much-weakened economy, and a stymied social and cultural scene that had previously been invigorated by the city's Hindus and Sikhs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004178-128] Industrial production dropped to one-third of pre-Partition level by the end of the 1940s, and only 27% of its manufacturing units were operating by 1950, and usually well-below capacity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004178-128] [Capital flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_flight) further weakened the city's economy while [Karachi](17123.md) industrialised and became more prosperous.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004178-128] The city's weakened economy, and proximity to the Indian border, meant that the city was deemed unsuitable to be the Pakistani capital after independence. Karachi was therefore chosen to be the capital on account of its relative tranquility during the Partition period, stronger economy, and better infrastructure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004178-128]



Sections of the [Walled City of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) have been under restoration since 2012 in conjunction with the [Agha Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Khan) Trust for Culture.

After independence, Lahore slowly regained its significance as an economic and cultural centre of western Punjab. Reconstruction began in 1949 of the Shah Alami Bazaar, the former Hindu-dominated commercial heart of the Walled City prior to its destruction in the 1947 riots.[^cite_note-dejonge-125] The [Tomb of Allama Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Allama_Iqbal) was built in 1951 to honour the philosopher-poet who provided the spiritual inspiration for the Pakistan movement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004178-179-129] In 1955, Lahore was selected to be the capital of all [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) during the single-unit period that lasted until 1970.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004178-128] Shortly afterwards, Lahore's iconic [Minar-e-Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minar-e-Pakistan) was completed in 1968 to mark the spot where the [Pakistan Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Resolution) was passed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKudaisyaYong2004179-130] With support from the [United Nations](31769.md), the [government](1897833.md) was able to rebuild Lahore, and most scars from the communal violence of Partition were ameliorated.

The second [Islamic Summit Conference](47725.md) was held in the city in 1974.[^cite_note-oic_conf-131] In retaliation for the destruction of the [Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babri_Masjid) in India, riots erupted in 1992 in which several non-Muslim monuments were targeted, including the tomb of Maharaja [Sher Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Singh),[^cite_note-bansal-93] and the former Jain temple near The Mall. In 1996, the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) [Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Cricket_World_Cup) final match was held at the [Gaddafi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddafi_Stadium) in Lahore.[^cite_note-132]

The [Walled City of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) restoration project began in 2009, when the Punjab government restored the Royal Trail from [Akbari Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbari_Gate) to the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort) with money from the [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-133]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Lahore)



Lahore as seen from International Space Station. The [Ravi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River) flows from north to west. The city is rapidly growing towards the south.

Lahore is in northeastern portion of Pakistan, lying between 31°15′—31°45′ N and 74°01′—74°39′ E. The city is bounded on the north and west by the [Sheikhupura District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikhupura_District), on the east by [Wagah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagah), and on the south by [Kasur District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasur_District). The [Ravi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River) flows on the northern side of Lahore. Lahore city covers a total land area of 404 square kilometres (156 sq mi).

Lahore has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*), bordering on a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate). The hottest month is June, where temperatures routinely exceed 45 °C (113 °F). The monsoon season starts in late June, and the wettest months are July, August and September.[^cite_note-HKO-134] with heavy rainfalls and evening thunderstorms with the possibility of cloudbursts and flash floods. The coolest month is January, with dense fog.[^cite_note-135]

The city's record high temperature was 50.4 °C (122.7 °F), recorded on 5 June 2003.[^cite_note-136] On 10 June 2007, a temperature of 48 °C (118 °F) was recorded;[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] this was in the shade, and the meteorological office recording the figure reported a heat index in direct sunlight of 55 °C (131 °F).[^cite_note-139] The highest rainfall in a 24-hour period is 337 millimetres (13.3 in), recorded on 1 August 2024.[^cite_note-140]

### Population

The results of the [2017 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Census_of_Pakistan) determined the population of Lahore to be 11,126,285,[^cite_note-census2017-146] with an annual growth rate of 4.07% since [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Pakistan_Census).[^cite_note-census2017-district-147] Gender-wise, 52.35% of the population are male, 47.64% are female, and 0.01% are transgender.[^cite_note-census2017-district-147] Lahore is a demographically young city, with over 40% of its inhabitants below the age of 15.[^cite_note-148]

### Tribes and background

#### Controversy about the city's Pashtun population

Some [Pashtun nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_nationalism) parties argue that the [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) population of Lahore in the 2017 census has been underestimated, with Ameer Bahadur Khan, provincial general secretary of [Awami National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_National_Party), putting their numbers at 1.5 million, while Gul Muhammad Regwal, a member of [Pakhtunkhwa Milli Awami Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtunkhwa_Milli_Awami_Party), claiming more than 1 million including 300,000 in the [Walled City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) alone, most of these having moved over the last 20 years, including due to military operations, but remaining uncounted as they're not considered permanent residents of Lahore.[^cite_note-151]

The [Punjabi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) is the most-widely spoken native language in Lahore, with 73.58% of Lahore counting it as their first language according to the 2023 Census.[^cite_note-173] Lahore is the largest Punjabi-speaking city in the world. According to the [2023 Pakistani census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census) 21.1% speak [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), 2.06% [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto), 2.01% [Mewati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewati) and 2.78% other mother tongues.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

[Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) and English are used as official languages and as mediums of instruction and media administration. However, Punjabi is also taught at graduation level and used in theatres, films, and newspapers from Lahore.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] Several Lahore-based prominent educational leaders, researchers, and social commentators have demanded that the Punjabi language should be declared as the medium of instruction at the primary level and be used officially in the [Punjab Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_the_Punjab), Lahore.[^cite_note-The_Nation_2015-178][^cite_note-Business_Recorder_2015-179]

## Cityscape

### Old City



Cityscape of Lahore

Lahore's modern cityscape consists of the historic [Walled City of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) in the northern part of the city, which contains several [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) and national heritage sites. Lahore's urban planning was not based on geometric design but was instead built piecemeal, with small cul-de-sacs, as *katrahs* and *galis* developed in the context of neighbouring buildings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] Though certain neighbourhoods were named for particular religious or ethnic communities, the neighbourhoods themselves typically were diverse and were not dominated by the namesake group.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59]



The area around the [Wazir Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque) exemplifies the [Walled City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore)'s urban form

Lahore's urban typology is similar to other ancient cities in South Asia, such as [Peshawar](66230.md), [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan) and [Delhi](37756.md) – all of which were founded near a major river, and included an old walled city and royal citadel.



Lahore's [Hazuri Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazuri_Bagh) is at the centre of an ensemble of Mughal and Sikh era monuments, including the [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque), [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort), [Roshnai Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshnai_Gate), and the [Samadhi of Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi_of_Ranjit_Singh).

By the end of the Sikh rule, most of Lahore's massive [haveli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haveli) compounds had been occupied by settlers. New neighbourhoods occasionally grew up entirely within the confines of an old Mughal haveli, such as the Mohallah Pathan Wali, which grew within the ruins of a haveli of the same name, built by Mian Khan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] By 1831, all Mughal Havelis in the Walled City had been encroached upon by the surrounding neighbourhood,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlover2008\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-59] leading to the modern-day absence of any Mughal Havelis in Lahore.

A total of thirteen gates once surrounded the historic walled city. Some of the remaining gates include the Raushnai Gate, Masti Gate, Yakki Gate, Kashmiri Gate, Khizri Gate, Shah Burj Gate, Akbari Gate, and Lahori Gate. Southeast of the walled city is the spacious British-era [Lahore Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Cantonment).

### Architecture

Further information: [Architecture of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Lahore)



Built in 2012, [Grand Jamia Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Jamia_Mosque,_Lahore) in southern Lahore is a blend of [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture) and modern architecture.

Lahore is home to numerous monuments from the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Dynasty), [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire), and the [British Indian Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj). The architectural style of the [Walled City of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) has traditionally been influenced by Mughal and Sikh styles.[^cite_note-181]

#### Sikh period

By the arrival of the Sikh Empire at the end of the 18th century, rebuilding efforts under Ranjit Singh and his successors were influenced by Mughal practices, and Lahore was known as the 'City of Gardens' during the Ranjit Singh period.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] Later, British maps of the area surrounding Lahore dating from the mid-19th century show many walled private gardens which were confiscated from the Muslim noble families bearing the names of prominent Sikh nobles – a pattern of patronage which was inherited from the Mughals.

While much of Lahore's Mughal-era fabric lay in ruins by the time of his arrival, Ranjit Singh's army's plundered most of Lahore's most precious Mughal monuments, and stripped the white marble from several monuments to send to different parts of the Sikh Empire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESidhwa2005\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (August_2024)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-97] Monuments plundered of their marble include the [Tomb of Asif Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Asif_Khan) and the [Tomb of Nur Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Nur_Jahan); the [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens,_Lahore) was plundered of much of its marble, and its costly [agate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agate) gate was stripped.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. The [Lava Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_Temple) at the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort) dates from the Sikh period,

#### British period



A syncretic architectural style that blends Islamic, Hindu, and Western motifs took root during the colonial era, as shown at [Aitchison College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitchison_College).



Much of old Lahore features colonial-era buildings, such as the Tollinton Market.

As the capital of British Punjab, the city derived much of its architecture from British colonialists. Structures were built predominantly in the [Indo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gothic) style – a syncretic architectural style that blends elements of [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture) and [Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture) or in the distinct [Indo-Saracenic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Saracenic_Revival_architecture). The British also built [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) Montgomery Hall, which today serves as the [Quaid-e-Azam Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaid-e-Azam_Library).[^cite_note-186]

[Lawrence Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagh-e-Jinnah,_Lahore) were also laid near Civil Station, and were paid for by donations solicited from both Lahore's European community, as well as from wealthy locals. The gardens featured over 600 species of plants, and were tended to by a horticulturist sent from London's [Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Botanic_Gardens,_Kew).[^cite_note-187]

The leafy suburbs to the south of the Old City, as well as the Cantonment southwest of the Old City, were largely developed under British colonial rule, and feature colonial-era buildings built alongside leafy avenues.

The British authorities built several important structures around the time of the [Golden Jubilee of Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Queen_Victoria) in 1887 in the distinctive Indo-Saracenic style, such as the [Lahore Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Museum) and [Mayo School of Industrial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_College_of_Arts).[^cite_note-Making_Lahore_Modern_1894-110] Other prominent examples of the Indo-Saracenic style in Lahore include Lahore's prestigious [Aitchison College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitchison_College), the Punjab Chief Court (today the [Lahore High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_High_Court)), [Lahore Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Museum), and the [University of the Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Punjab).

Many of Lahore's most important buildings were designed by civil engineer and architect [Sir Ganga Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Ram), who is considered "the father of modern Lahore".[^cite_note-Ahmed2001-188][^cite_note-HaroonKhalid-189][^cite_note-thenews.com.pk-190][^cite_note-191]

### Parks and gardens

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of parks and gardens in Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_and_gardens_in_Lahore).



Lahore's [Bagh-e-Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagh-e-Jinnah,_Lahore) was laid in 1862 as the [Lawrence Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagh-e-Jinnah_\(Lahore\)).

Lahore is also known as "the city of gardens" due to its large number of gardens. The [Shahdara Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahdara_Bagh) was one of the earliest [Mughal gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_garden), laid out in 15th century, and contains the [Tomb of Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Jahangir). The [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens_\(Lahore\)) were laid out during the reign of [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan) and were designed to mimic the [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic) paradise of the afterlife described in the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an). The gardens follow the familiar [charbagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charbagh) layout of four squares, with three descending terraces. In 1818, [Hazuri Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazuri_Bagh) was built during reign of Ranjit Singh to celebrate his capture of the [Koh-i-Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koh-i-Noor) diamond from Shuja Shah Durrani.

The [Lawrence Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagh-e-Jinnah_\(Lahore\)) was established in 1862 and was originally named after [Sir John Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Lawrence), late 19th-century British Viceroy to India. The Circular Garden, which surrounds the Walled City on three sides, was established by 1892.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELatif1892\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_August_2024\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. ## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Lahore)

  [Emporium Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporium_Mall)

As of 2008, the city's gross domestic product (GDP) by [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) (PPP) was estimated at \$40 billion with a projected average growth rate of 5.6 per cent. This is on par with Karachi, Pakistan's economic hub, with Lahore (having half the population) fostering an economy that is 51% of the size of Karachi's (\$78 billion in 2008).[^cite_note-pwc1-194] It is estimated that Lahore contributes 11.5% to the national economy, and 19% to the provincial economy of Punjab.[^cite_note-195] As a whole, Punjab has a \$115 billion economy, making it the first (and to date, only) Pakistani Subdivision with an economy of more than \$100 billion, at the rank 144.[^cite_note-pwc1-194] Lahore's GDP is projected to be \$102 billion by 2025, with a slightly higher growth rate of 5.6% per annum, as compared to Karachi's 5.5%.[^cite_note-pwc1-194][^cite_note-196]

A major industrial agglomeration with about 9,000 industrial units, Lahore has shifted in recent decades from manufacturing to service industries.[^cite_note-adb-197] Some 42% of its workforce is employed in finance, banking, real estate, community, cultural, and social services.[^cite_note-adb-197] The city is Pakistan's largest software and hardware producing centre,[^cite_note-adb-197] and hosts a growing computer-assembly industry.[^cite_note-adb-197] The city has always been a centre for publications; 80% of Pakistan's books are published in Lahore, and it remains the foremost centre of literary, educational, and cultural activity in Pakistan.[^cite_note-2005_pg7_15-33]

The [Lahore Expo Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_Centre_Lahore) is one of the biggest projects in the history of the city and was inaugurated on 22 May 2010.[^cite_note-expolahore.com-198] Defence Raya Golf Resort, now fully operational (as of the 2024), boasts Pakistan's largest and Asia's premier golf course. This luxurious project is a result of a partnership between DHA Lahore and BRDB Malaysia, offering world-class residential, recreational, and commercial facilities. The development of such large-scale projects continues to elevate Lahore's profile, contributing significantly to the national economy.[^cite_note-199] Ferozepur Road of the [Central business districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) of Lahore contains high-rises and skyscrapers including Kayre International Hotel and [Arfa Software Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arfa_Software_Technology_Park).

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Lahore)



[Kalma Underpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalma_Underpass)

### Public transportation



[Lahore Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metrobus)

Lahore's main public transportation system is operated by the [Lahore Transport Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Transport_Company) (LTC) and Punjab Mass Transit Authority (PMTA). The backbone of its public transport network is the PMTA's [Lahore Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metrobus) and the [Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_\(Lahore_Metro\)) of the [Lahore Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metro) train. LTC and PMTA also operates an extensive network of buses, providing bus service to many parts of the city and acting as a feeder system for the Metrobus. The Orange Line metro spans 27.1 km (16.8 mi) around the city and operates at a speed of 80 km/h (50 mph).

#### Metrobus

Main article: [Lahore Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metrobus)

The [Lahore Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metrobus) is a [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) service operating in Lahore, [Punjab, Pakistan](24751.md).[^cite_note-200] Lahore Metrobus service is integrated with [Lahore Transport Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Transport_Company)'s local bus service to operate as one urban transport system, providing a connected transit service across [Lahore District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_District) with connections to neighbouring suburban communities.

#### Low occupancy vehicles

Low occupancy vehicle (LOVs)—functionally a medium-sized van or wagon—run on routes throughout the city. They function like buses and operate on many routes throughout the city.[^cite_note-201]

### Metro Train



The [Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_\(Lahore_Metro\)) is Pakistan's first metro rail line.

#### Orange Line

Main article: [Orange Line (Lahore Metro)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_\(Lahore_Metro\))

The [Orange Line Metro Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_\(Lahore_Metro\)) is an automated [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system in Lahore.[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203] The Orange line is the first of the three proposed rail lines proposed for the [Lahore Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metro). As of 2020, it is the primary metro rail line in the city. The line spans 27.1 km (16.8 mi), with 25.4 km (15.8 mi) elevated and 1.72 km (1.1 mi) underground,[^cite_note-Norinco-204] and had a cost of 251.06 billion [rupees](588111.md) (\$1.6 billion). The line consists of 26 subway stations (Ali Town Station to Dera Gujran Station) and is designed to carry over 250,000 passengers daily. [CRRC Zhuzhou Locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRRC_Zhuzhou_Locomotive) rolled out the first of 27 trains for the metro on 16 May 2017.[^cite_note-205] The train has speed up to 80 km/h (50 mph). For improved durability, its [bogies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogie) are heat-resistant, can manage unstable voltage, and feature energy-saving air-conditioning.[^cite_note-206] Successful initial test trials were run in mid-2018,[^cite_note-207] and commercial operations began on 25 October 2020.[^cite_note-Lahore_Metro_Train-208]

#### Blue Line

The Blue Line is a proposed 24 kilometres (15 mi) line from Chauburji to College Road Township. Along the way, it will connect places like Mozang Chungi, Shadman Chowk, Jail Road, Mian Boulevard Gulberg, Mian Boulevard Garden Town, and Faisal Town.[^cite_note-railway-technology.com-209]

#### Purple Line

The Purple Line is a proposed 19 kilometres (12 mi) line from Bhaati Chowk to the Allama Iqbal International Airport. Along the way, it will connect places like Brandreth Road, Railway Station, Allama Iqbal Road, Dharampura, and Ghazi Road.[^cite_note-railway-technology.com-209]

### Taxi and rickshaw

Ride-sharing services such as [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) and [Careem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Careem) are available in the city. Motorcycle rides are also available in the city, which have been introduced by private companies.

[Auto rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_rickshaws) play an important role of public transport in Lahore. As of 2019, there were approximately 82,000 auto rickshaws and 65,000 motorcycle rickshaws in the city.[^cite_note-The_Express_Tribune_2019_c467-210] Motorcycle rickshaws, usually called *[chingchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Qingqi)*[^cite_note-Khan_2023_j517-211] (after the Chinese company Jinan Qingqi Motorcycle Co. Ltd, who first introduced these to the market) or *chand gari* ('moon car')[^cite_note-BOL_News_2023_a520-212] are cheaper than auto rickshaws and provide a shared ride experience for multiple passengers and fares, whereas auto rickshaws cater to only one passenger or group for a fare. Since 2002, all auto rickshaws have been required to use [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) as fuel,[^cite_note-213] and all-electric rickshaws were introduced in 2023.[^cite_note-Sheikh_2023_x607-214]

## Government

### Metropolitan Corporation

Under Punjab Local Government Act 2013, Lahore is a [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) under the authority of the Metropolitan Corporation Lahore.[^cite_note-222] The Metropolitan Corporation Lahore is a body consisting of nine deputy mayors (one from each zone in the district) and the city's mayor – all of whom are elected in popular elections. The Metropolitan Corporation approves zoning and land use, coordinates urban design and planning, sets environmental protection laws, and provides municipal services.

#### Mayor

Main article: [Mayor of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Lahore)

As per the Punjab Local Government Act 2013, the [Mayor of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Lahore) is the elected head of the Metropolitan Corporation of Lahore. The mayor is directly elected in municipal elections every four years alongside 9 deputy [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_in_Lahore) mayors. Mubashir Javed of the [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)) was elected mayor of Lahore in 2016. The mayor is responsible for the administration of government services, the composition of councils and committees overseeing [Lahore City District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_District) departments and serves as the chairperson for the meeting of the Lahore Council. The mayor also functions to help devise long-term development plans in consultation with other stakeholders and bodies to improve the condition, livability, and sustainability of urban areas.

## Festivals



[Lahore Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Canal) during the spring [Basant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basant_Kite_Festival) festival

The people of Lahore [celebrate many festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festivals_in_Lahore) and events throughout the year, including Islamic, traditional Punjabi, Christian, and national holidays and festivals.

Some people decorate their houses and light candles to illuminate the streets and houses during public holidays; roads and businesses may be lit for days. Many of Lahore's dozens of [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) shrines hold annual festivals called *[urs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urs)* to honour their respective saints. For example, the mausoleum of [Ali al-Hujwiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_al-Hujwiri) at the [Data Darbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_Darbar) shrine has an annual *urs* that attracts up to one million visitors per year.[^cite_note-linus-225] The [Mela Chiraghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mela_Chiraghan) festival in Lahore takes place at the shrine of [Madho Lal Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madho_Lal_Hussain), while other large *urs* take place at the shrines of [Bibi Pak Daman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibi_Pak_Daman), and at the [Shrine of Mian Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine_of_Mian_Mir).[^cite_note-226] [Eid ul-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Fitr) and [Eid ul-Adha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Adha) are celebrated in the city with public buildings and shopping centres decorated in lights. The people of Lahore also commemorate the martyrdom of [Imam Husayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Husain) at [Karbala](210957.md) with massive processions that take place during the first ten days of the month of [Muharram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muharram).[^cite_note-227]

[Basant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basant_\(season\)) is a traditional [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_culture) festival that marks the coming of spring. Basant celebrations in Pakistan are centred in Lahore, and people from all over the country and abroad come to the city for the annual festivities. [Kite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kite)-flying competitions traditionally take place on city rooftops during Basant, while the [Lahore Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Canal) is decorated with floating lanterns. Courts have banned kite-flying because of casualties and power installation losses. The ban was lifted for two days in 2007, then immediately reimposed when 11 people were killed by [celebratory gunfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebratory_gunfire), sharp kite-strings, electrocution, and falls related to the competition.[^cite_note-228]

Lahore's churches are elaborately decorated for [Christmas](6237.md) and [Easter](9325.md) celebrations.[^cite_note-229] Shopping centres and public buildings also feature Christmas installations to celebrate the holiday, even though Christians only constitute 5.1% of the total population of Lahore in 2024. See [Religion in Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Lahore)[^cite_note-230]

Lahore remains a major tourist destination in Pakistan. The [Walled City of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore) was renovated in 2014 and is popular due to the presence of [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-231] Among the most popular sights are the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort), adjacent to the Walled City, and home to the [Sheesh Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheesh_Mahal_\(Lahore\)), the [Alamgiri Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamgiri_Gate), the [Naulakha Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naulakha_Pavilion), and the [Moti Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moti_Masjid_\(Lahore\)). The fort and adjoining [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens,_Lahore) have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981.[^cite_note-232]

The city is home to Muslim historical religious sites, most prominently the [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque), constructed in 1673; it was the largest mosque in the world upon construction. Another popular sight is the [Wazir Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque),[^cite_note-233] constructed in 1635 and known for its extensive *faience* tile work.[^cite_note-234] [Data Darbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_Darbar), near the Old City of Lahore, is the largest Sufi shrine in South Asia.[^cite_note-a-235] Apart from Islam, two functional Hindu temples exist: the [Krishna Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Mandir_demolition_reports) and [Valmiki Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiki_Mandir,_Lahore). The [Samadhi of Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi_of_Ranjit_Singh), also located near the Walled City, houses the [funerary urns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urn) of the [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) ruler [Maharaja Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Ranjit_Singh_\(Punjab\)).

Whereas the Walled City reflects Lahore's historic grandeur, Defence Raya Golf Resort contributes to the city's modern side. Other posh areas include [Gulberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulberg,_Lahore), [Iqbal Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iqbal_Town,_Lahore) and the [Nahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahr,_Lahore).[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

Lahore is sometimes considered Pakistan's educational capital, with more colleges and universities than any other city in Pakistan. The literacy rate of Lahore is 74%. The city is Pakistan's largest producer of professionals in the fields of science, technology, IT, law, engineering, medicine, nuclear sciences, pharmacology, telecommunication, biotechnology, microelectronics, and nanotechnology, and has the only future hyper high-tech centre in Pakistan.[^cite_note-238] Most of the reputable universities are public, but in recent years there has also been an upsurge in the number of private universities. Lahore University of Management Sciences (LUMS) is the only [AACSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_to_Advance_Collegiate_Schools_of_Business) accredited business school in Pakistan. Lahore has successfully hosted many international sports events, including the finals of the [1990 Men's Hockey World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup) and the [1996 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Cricket_World_Cup). The headquarters of all major sports governing bodies in Pakistan are located in Lahore, including cricket, hockey, rugby, and football. Lahore is also home to the head office of the [Pakistan Olympic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Olympic_Association).

[Gaddafi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddafi_Stadium) is a [Test cricket ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Test_cricket_grounds) in Lahore. It was completed in 1959, and renovations were carried out by Pakistani architect [Nayyar Ali Dada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayyar_Ali_Dada) in the 1990s.

The multi purpose [Punjab Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Stadium,_Pakistan) located near the headquarters of the [Pakistan Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Football_Federation), is mainly used for [football](10568.md) matches and has hosted several events including [AFC President's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_President's_Cup) and [FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). [Wohaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wohaib_FC) club from Lahore was one of the leading football clubs in Pakistan in the 1990s.[^cite_note-\:02-239]

Lahore also had its own football league, the [Lahore District Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_District_Football_League), in which local teams would participate.

Lahore is home to several [golf](19568112.md) courses, including the [Lahore Gymkhana Golf Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Gymkhana_Club), the Lahore Garrison Golf and Country Club, the Royal Palm Golf Club, and newly built Defence Raya Golf & Country Club. Lake City, a 9-hole course, opened in nearby [Raiwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raiwind) Road in 2011. The newly opened Oasis Golf and Aqua Resort is a state-of-the-art resort, featuring golf, water parks, and leisure activities like horse riding and archery.

The [Lahore Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Marathon) is part of an annual package of six international marathons sponsored by [Standard Chartered Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chartered_Bank) across Asia, Africa, and the Middle East. More than 20,000 athletes from Pakistan and all over the world participate in this event. It was first held on 30 January 2005, and again on 29 January 2006. More than 22,000 people participated in the 2006 race. The third marathon was held on 14 January 2007.[^cite_note-240] Plans exist to build Pakistan's first sports city in Lahore, on the bank of the [Ravi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River).[^cite_note-241]

## Awards

In 1966, the [Government of Pakistan](1897833.md) awarded a special flag, the [Hilal-i-istaqlal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilal-i-istaqlal), to the cities of Lahore, [Sargodha](1738747.md), and [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot) for showing severe resistance to the enemy during the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965), as these cities were targets of the Indian aggression.[^cite_note-249] Every year on [Defence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Day) (6 September), this flag is hoisted in these cities in recognition of the will, courage, and perseverance of their people.[^cite_note-250]
