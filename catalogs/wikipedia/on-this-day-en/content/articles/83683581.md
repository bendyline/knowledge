**Darline Lettie Graham Nordone**[^cite_note-2] (born **Darlene Lettie Graham**;[^cite_note-3] June 12, 1964) is an American politician and [public administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration) serving as the [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seniority_in_the_United_States_Senate) [United States senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_senator) from [South Carolina](27956.md), a seat she has held since July 14, 2026. A member of the [Republican Party](32070.md), she was appointed to fill the vacancy created by the death of her older brother, [Lindsey Graham](363279.md). She is the [first woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_first_women_holders_of_political_offices_in_the_United_States) to represent South Carolina in the United States Senate.

Graham is the [Republican](32070.md) nominee in the [2026 United States Senate election in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina), facing [Democratic](5043544.md) nominee [Annie Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Andrews_\(pediatrician\)).

## Early life and education

Darlene Lettie Graham[^cite_note-The_Atlantic_Tea_Party-4][^cite_note-The_State_marriage-5] was born in [Central, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central,_South_Carolina), on June 12, 1964.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-NYTimes_Bond-7][^cite_note-8] Her parents, Florence James "F. J." Graham and Millie Graham (née Walters), operated a restaurant, bar, liquor store, and pool hall, and the family lived in rooms behind the business.[^cite_note-NYTimes_Bond-7] Her mother died of cancer when Graham was 11, and her father died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack) 15 months later.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] After their deaths, Graham's brother, [Lindsey](363279.md), arranged for her to live with her aunt and uncle in [Seneca, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca,_South_Carolina). Before joining the [Air Force](32090.md) in 1982, Lindsey—who was nine years her senior—became Graham's [legal guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_guardian).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-The_State_Ads-12] He taught her to spell her first name "Darline", a spelling she continues to use in adulthood.[^cite_note-The_Atlantic_Tea_Party-4]

Graham graduated from the [College of Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Charleston) with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) in 1989.[^cite_note-Post_and_Courier_2026-07-13-13] She also earned a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in [rehabilitation counseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehabilitation_counseling) from [South Carolina State University](574357.md) in 2009.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Career

### Public administration

In 2008, Graham began working for the South Carolina Vocational Rehabilitation Department. She helped coordinate a [remote work](158555.md) program for adults with severe disabilities for [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner).[^cite_note-16] By 2015, she was the department's public information director.[^cite_note-17]

In 2019, Graham became commissioner and agency head of the [South Carolina Commission for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Commission_for_the_Blind), which administers [vocational rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_rehabilitation), [independent living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_living), and blindness-prevention programs for South Carolinians who are blind or have [low vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_vision).[^cite_note-18] She also serves on the South Carolina State Workforce Development Board and its SC Works Management Committee. In 2026, she was elected president of the National Council of State Agencies for the Blind, with her term scheduled to start in January 2027.[^cite_note-Post_and_Courier_2026-07-13-13]

### Political involvement

In 2002, Graham appeared in a television advertisement for Lindsey's campaign in that year's [United States Senate election in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-The_State_2002-10-14-1] She continued to assist in his subsequent campaigns, discussing her childhood during the [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina)[^cite_note-The_State_Ads-12] and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina) Senate races.[^cite_note-19] Graham supported her brother's [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsey_Graham_2016_presidential_campaign) for [president of the United States](24113.md) in the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), introducing him in his first campaign speech.[^cite_note-20]

## U.S. Senate (2026–present)



Graham was ceremonially sworn in by [Chuck Grassley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Grassley) on July 14, 2026

### Appointment

South Carolina governor [Henry McMaster](3482397.md) appointed Graham on July 13, 2026, to fill her brother's [vacant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_vacancy_\(United_States\)) senate seat,[^cite_note-21] the same day President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) endorsed her.[^cite_note-22] She was sworn in on July 14.[^cite_note-23] She is the first woman to represent South Carolina in the Senate and the first senator to replace a sibling.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Her appointment marked the first time 27 women have served concurrently in the Senate, including 11 Republican women, both records.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### 2026 election

Main article: [2026 United States Senate election in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina)

President Trump publicly encouraged Graham on July 17 to run in the [2026 United States Senate election in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina) and promised to endorse her if she did.[^cite_note-29] Graham told [Sean Hannity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Hannity) three days later that she intended to run for a full term.[^cite_note-30] She came in first in the special primary election on August 11, advancing to a runoff with [Ralph Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Norman).[^cite_note-31] On August 25, Graham won the runoff,[^cite_note-32] advancing to the general election against [Annie Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Andrews_\(pediatrician\)), with the winner set to become the first woman elected to the Senate from South Carolina.

When Graham was asked questions about [national security](240468.md) issues in [Taiwan](25734.md) and the [South China Sea](74209.md) during the debate with Norman, she could not answer them.[^cite_note-NBC_News_2026-08-22-33] Her debate performance was called "embarrassing".[^cite_note-NBC_News_2026-08-22-33][^cite_note-34] Although Trump endorsed Graham, some of his supporters refused to vote for her.[^cite_note-35]

During the special Republican primary runoff, Andrews asked Graham and Norman to have a debate with the winner. After winning the runoff, Graham refused to debate Andrews.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In response, the [South Carolina Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Democratic_Party) created a website to pressure her to debate Andrews. Eventually, Graham agreed to a debate on October 11.[^cite_note-38] In her statement agreeing to the debate, she said: "I'm in. On October 11, I will take the debate stage and expose Annie Andrews' radical positions she's spent the last year hiding from South Carolina families. This is your seat, South Carolina, and it will be my honor to show you why I am ready to continue serving you and representing our conservative values in Washington."[^cite_note-39]

### Tenure



Graham and Trump in the Oval Office after signing the [Sanctioning Russia Act](79879253.md), September 18, 2026

On July 16, Graham introduced the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, legislation to impose sanctions on [Russia](25391.md). It is based on the [Sanctioning Russia Act](79879253.md), a bill Lindsey Graham authored and supported, and had bipartisan support.[^cite_note-40] It passed the Senate in August[^cite_note-41] and passed in the House in mid-September with bipartisan support. President Trump signed it into law two days after.[^cite_note-42]

Graham voted to confirm the nomination of [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) as [United States Attorney General](44002.md), in a 50–49 vote.[^cite_note-43]

## Personal life

Graham was previously married to Terry Lee Boggs; the couple had two daughters together before divorcing in 2003.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Graham married Larry Nordone circa 2006.[^cite_note-The_State_marriage-5] They live in [Lexington, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-NYTimes_Bond-7]
