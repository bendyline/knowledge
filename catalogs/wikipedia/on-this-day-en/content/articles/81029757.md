The **first Lecornu government** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *gouvernement Lecornu I*) was the forty-seventh government of [France](5843419.md). It was formed in September 2025 after President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) appointed [Sébastien Lecornu](54364868.md) as [Prime Minister](218751.md) on 9 September,[^cite_note-3] replacing [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou), who had been removed from office by a failed [motion of confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_confidence).[^cite_note-4] The list of ministers was announced on 5 October.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]&#x20;

Lecornu resigned less than 14 hours after presenting his [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)) on 6 October, 27 days after his appointment,[^cite_note-\:10-7] making his first government the shortest-lived one in French history. On 10 October, Lecornu was re-appointed as Prime Minister. He then formed the **second Lecornu government** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *gouvernement Lecornu II*), the 48th and incumbent government of France.[^cite_note-\:2-8]&#x20;

## Formation

### First government

See also: [2024–2025 French political crisis](77741666.md)

On 9 September 2025, President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) appointed Lecornu as Prime Minister following the fall of the [Bayrou government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayrou_government).[^cite_note-9] After 26 days, the composition of the government was announced on 5 October, with most of the ministers retaining their portfolios from the Bayrou government. As well as the continuing ministers, some other deputies from President Macron's [Ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_Citoyens) coalition were added, with former ministers [Eric Woerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Woerth) and [Bruno Le Maire](20472835.md) receiving portfolios again.[^cite_note-\:3-10][^cite_note-\:4-11] Out of 18 nominations, 4 are members of [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)), and 8 of them are former ministers from the more right-leaning factions of the Ensemble coalition. Despite this, [Bruno Retailleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Retailleau) retaining the [Ministry of the Interior](871703.md), declared the same evening that the composition of the government "doesn't reflect the break" desired by The Republicans, who demanded a third of the ministries.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]&#x20;

Shortly after the nomination, the various leaders of the left-wing parties making up the [New Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Popular_Front) expressed their outrage against the new cabinet: [Fabien Roussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabien_Roussel) ([French Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party)) called it a "middle finger to the French" whilst [Jean-Luc Mélenchon](20298042.md) described it as a "parade of LR and former LR revenants"; Marine Tondelier ([Les Écologistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_%C3%89cologistes)) described the nominations as a "bonus for incompetence" and a "contempt for democracy", and [Olivier Faure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Faure) ([Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\))) spoke of it as a complete "[Panini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panini_Group) album of the [Sarkozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarkozy)/[Fillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillon) government".[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:3-10][^cite_note-\:4-11] For the [far-right](9701975.md), [Jordan Bardella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Bardella) ([National Rally](67119.md)) criticised the composition of the government, whilst [Éric Ciotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Ciotti) ([Union of the Right for the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_the_Right_for_the_Republic)) also invoked the image of a "middle finger".[^cite_note-\:3-10]&#x20;

#### Resignation

On 6 October 2025, Prime Minister Lecornu and his cabinet resigned just one day after being formally appointed by President [Emmanuel Macron.](43671127.md)[^cite_note-15] The resignation came amid widespread political backlash over the composition of the new government, which included several figures closely associated with Macron's previous administrations, and escalating tensions with [Les Républicains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)), led by [Bruno Retailleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Retailleau). Lecornu had pledged to avoid using Article 49.3 of the French Constitution to push the 2026 budget through Parliament, instead advocating for a negotiated compromise. He also faced criticism for proposing a scaled-down version of the planned "Zucman tax" on wealth, limited to certain financial holdings. In his resignation statement, Lecornu cited the "absence of conditions necessary to govern" in a fragmented parliament. Following his departure, opposition parties called for early legislative elections, and reports suggested that President Macron was considering dissolving the National Assembly.[^cite_note-16] The Lecornu government became the shortest in French history, lasting only 14 hours between the appointment of its ministers and its resignation.[^cite_note-\:10-7] It notably beat the previous records held by [Frédéric François-Marsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Fran%C3%A7ois-Marsal) in 1924, [Édouard Herriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Herriot) in 1926, and [Henri Queuille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Queuille) in 1950, which all only lasted two days before resigning or being removed by [Parliament](1009776.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]&#x20;

### Re-appointment and reactions

Macron reappointed Lecornu as Prime Minister on 10 October.[^cite_note-\:2-8] [La France Insoumise](52520313.md)'s [Manuel Bompard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Bompard) said that he considered the reappointment humiliating and vowed to [impeach](15328.md#France) President Macron and file a [motion of no confidence](208101.md) against Lecornu's second government.[^cite_note-\:02-20] The [French Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party)'s [Fabien Roussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabien_Roussel) called for a [snap election](462915.md).[^cite_note-\:0-21] [The Ecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ecologists), along with La France Insoumise and the French Communist Party, announced that they would support the motion.[^cite_note-\:1-22] The [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\))'s [Pierre Jouvet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Jouvet) said that a motion of no confidence was not ruled out because no agreement was reached with the government.[^cite_note-\:0-21]&#x20;

[The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)) and the [Union of Democrats and Independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Democrats_and_Independents) said they would not join the coalition.[^cite_note-23] As a result, ministers appointed by Lecornu from The Republicans were expelled from the party.[^cite_note-24] [National Rally](67119.md)'s [Jordan Bardella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Bardella) deemed the reappointment shameful for France and democracy, saying that the National Rally would file a motion of no confidence.[^cite_note-25] The [Union of the Right for the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_the_Right_for_the_Republic)'s [Éric Ciotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Ciotti) would support the motion.[^cite_note-\:1-22] He called on his former right-wing colleagues to join him to bring about great change, and welcomed them like a family.[^cite_note-26]&#x20;
