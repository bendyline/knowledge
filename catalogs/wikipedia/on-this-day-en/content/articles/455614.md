[United States president](24113.md) [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) signs the declaration of war against Japan on December 8, 1941

A [declaration of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_war) is a formal declaration issued by a national government indicating that a state of war exists between that nation and another. A document by the [Federation of American Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_American_Scientists) gives an extensive listing and summary of statutes which are automatically engaged upon the [United States](3434750.md)' declaring war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElseaWeed2014-1]

For the United States, [Article One, Section Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_One_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Section_8:_Powers_of_Congress) of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) says "[Congress](31756.md) shall have power to ... declare War." However, that passage provides no specific format for what form legislation must have in order to be considered a "[declaration of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_war)" nor does the Constitution itself use this term. In the courts, the [United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_First_Circuit), in *[Doe v. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doe_v._Bush)*, said: "\[T]he text of the [October Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Resolution) itself spells out justifications for a war and frames itself as an 'authorization' of such a war,"[^cite_note-2] in effect saying that an authorization suffices for declaration and that what some may view as a formal congressional "[Declaration of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_war)" was not required by the Constitution.

The last time the United States formally declared war, using specific terminology, on any nation was on June 5, 1942, when war was declared against Axis-aligned [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_\(1920-1946\)), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria), and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania), because President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) thought it was improper to engage in hostilities against a country without a formal declaration of war. Yet, since then, every American president has used military force without a declaration of war.[^cite_note-3] However, [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) observed in [*Federalist* No. 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._25) that formal declarations of war were already falling into disuse by 1787.[^cite_note-4]

This article will use the term "formal declaration of war" to mean congressional legislation that uses the phrase "declaration of war" in the title. Elsewhere, this article will use the terms "authorized by Congress," "funded by Congress" or "[undeclared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undeclared_war)" to describe other such conflicts.

## History

The United States has formally declared war against foreign nations in five separate wars, each upon prior request by the president of the United States. Four of those five declarations came after hostilities had begun.[^cite_note-Henderson2000-5] James Madison reported that in the [Federal Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(United_States\)) of 1787, the phrase "make war" was changed to "declare war" in order to leave to the executive the power to repel sudden attacks but not to commence war without the explicit approval of Congress.[^cite_note-6] Debate continues as to the legal extent of the president's authority in this regard.

Public opposition to American involvement in foreign wars, particularly during the 1930s, was expressed as support for a [Constitutional Amendment](261586.md) that would require a [national referendum on a declaration of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_referendum).[^cite_note-7] Several constitutional amendments, such as the [Ludlow Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludlow_Amendment), have been proposed that would require a national referendum on a declaration of war.

After Congress repealed the [Gulf of Tonkin Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin_Resolution) in January 1971 and President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) continued to wage war in Vietnam, Congress passed the [War Powers Resolution](209756.md) ([Pub. L.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Congress#Public_law,_private_law,_designation) [93–148](https://uslaw.link/citation/us-law/public/93/148)) over the veto of Nixon in an attempt to rein in some of the president's claimed powers. The War Powers Resolution prescribes the only power of the president to wage war which is recognized by Congress.[^cite_note-8]

## Declarations of war

The United States has formally declared war in five separate conflicts, issuing declarations against ten different foreign nations. The only country to have been the subject of multiple U.S. war declarations is Germany, which the United States formally declared war against twice, once in [World War I](4764461.md) and again in [World War II](32927.md). A case could also be made for Hungary, as it was a [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state) to [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), against which the U.S. declared war in World War I.

### Formal declarations of war

Under the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution), [Congress](31756.md) holds the sole authority to issue formal war declarations. Since the nation's founding, Congress has exercised this power on a limited number of occasions.

The first formal declaration occurred on [June 18, 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_declaration_of_war_on_the_United_Kingdom), when the United States declared war against the [United Kingdom](31717.md), citing British [impressment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressment) of American sailors and violations of U.S. neutrality during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars). This conflict, known as the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), concluded with the [Treaty of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ghent) in 1814.[^cite_note-9]

On [May 13, 1846](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_declaration_of_war_on_Mexico), Congress declared war on [Mexico](3966054.md) at the request of President [James K. Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_K._Polk), following territorial disputes and military clashes in [Texas](29810.md). The [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) resulted in the [Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo) (1848), which expanded U.S. territory to include present-day [California](5407.md), [Arizona](21883824.md), and other southwestern states.[^cite_note-10]

The [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) marked another instance of a formal declaration of war. The conflict was precipitated by the sinking of the [USS *Maine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Maine_\(1890\)) in [Havana Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana_Harbor) and growing tensions over Spanish colonial rule in [Cuba](5042481.md). The war ended later that year with the [Treaty of Paris (1898)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1898\)), which granted the United States control over former Spanish territories, including [Puerto Rico](23041.md), [Guam](11974.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElseaWeed20142-11]

During [World War I](4764461.md), the United States declared war on [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) on [April 6, 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_\(1917\)), citing [unrestricted submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrestricted_submarine_warfare) and the [Zimmermann telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimmermann_telegram), in which Germany attempted to persuade Mexico to join the war against the United States. Later that year, on [December 7, 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_declaration_of_war_on_Austria-Hungary), Congress also declared war on [Austria–Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), a German ally. The conflict formally ended for the United States with the signing of separate peace treaties with Germany, [Austria](26964606.md), and [Hungary](13275.md) in 1921.[^cite_note-12]

[World War II](32927.md) saw the most formal war declarations by the United States. Following the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) on December 7, 1941, Congress declared war on [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) the next day, with near-unanimous approval. In response, [Nazi Germany](21212.md) and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), led by [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) and [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), declared war on the United States on [December 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_declaration_of_war_against_the_United_States), prompting reciprocal declarations by Congress against both nations. In 1942, the United States further declared war on [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_\(1920%E2%80%9346\)), and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania), all [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers)-aligned nations.[^cite_note-13] The war concluded in 1945 with the [unconditional surrender](415242.md) of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender) and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan), followed by formal peace treaties in the years after.[^cite_note-14]

Each of these declarations was issued through a Congressional resolution, signed into law by the president, signifying the commencement of hostilities under the authority of the U.S. government. Since [World War II](32927.md), the United States has engaged in numerous military conflicts, but none have been accompanied by a formal declaration of war. Instead, Congress has authorized the use of military force through resolutions, such as those in [Korea](16772.md), [Vietnam](32611.md), and the [war on terror](13425800.md), including conflicts in [Afghanistan](19666611.md) and [Iraq](5043324.md).

### Other undeclared wars

On at least 125 occasions, the president has acted without prior express military authorization from Congress.[^cite_note-49] These include instances in which the United States fought in the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War) from 1898 to 1903, in [Nicaragua](21362.md) in 1927, as well as the [NATO bombing campaign](322473.md) of [Yugoslavia](34244.md) in 1999, and the [2018 missile strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_missile_strikes_against_Syria) on [Syria](7515849.md).

The [Indian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Wars) comprise at least 28 conflicts and engagements. These localized conflicts, with [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), began with European colonists coming to North America, long before the establishment of the United States. For the purpose of this discussion, the Indian Wars are defined as conflicts with the United States of America. They begin as one front in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in 1775 and had concluded by 1918. The [United States Army](32087.md) still maintains a [campaign streamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_streamers) for [Pine Ridge 1890–1891](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wounded_Knee_Massacre) despite opposition from certain Native American groups.[^cite_note-50]

The [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) was not an international conflict under the laws of war, because the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) (CSA) was not a government that had been granted full diplomatic recognition as a [sovereign nation](28791.md) by other sovereign states[^cite_note-history-state-gov-51][^cite_note-McPherson-52] or by the government of the United States.[^cite_note-53] As such, the statutory authority for the use of the military during the American Civil War was the [Insurrection Act of 1807](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrection_Act_of_1807).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

The U.S. briefly joined the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) between Israel and Iran on the night of June 22, 2025, without a declaration of war or congressional approval. On February 28, 2026, the U.S. and Israel jointly launched [airstrikes on Iran](82533582.md), killing [several Iranian officials](82542668.md), including Supreme Leader [Ali Khamenei](82537558.md), without a declaration of war or congressional approval.

Main article: [War Powers Resolution](209756.md)
