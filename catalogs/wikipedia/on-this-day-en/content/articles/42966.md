The **Montreal Canadiens**[^cite_note-12] (French: *Canadiens de Montréal*, lit. 'Canadians of Montreal'), officially ***_Club de hockey Canadien_*** (lit. 'Canadian hockey club')[^cite_note-Canadiens-Privacy-13] and colloquially known as the **Habs**,[^cite_note-14] are a professional [ice hockey](14790.md) team based in [Montreal](7954681.md). The Canadiens compete in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) as a member of the [Atlantic Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Division_\(NHL\)) in the [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_\(NHL\)). Since 1996, the team has played its home games at the [Bell Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Centre), originally known as the Molson Centre.[^cite_note-Renamed-15] The Canadiens previously played at the [Montreal Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Forum), which housed the team for seven decades and all but their first two [Stanley Cup](66968.md) championships.[^cite_note-16]

Founded in 1909, the Canadiens are the oldest continuously operating professional ice hockey team worldwide, and the only existing NHL club to predate the [founding of the league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_National_Hockey_League). One of the earliest [North American professional sports franchises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_teams_in_the_United_States_and_Canada), the Canadiens' history predates that of every other Canadian franchise outside the [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League)'s [Toronto Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Argonauts), as well as every American franchise outside [baseball](3850.md) and the [National Football League](21211.md)'s [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals). The franchise is one of the "[Original Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Six)", the teams that made up the NHL from 1942 until the [1967 expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_NHL_expansion). The team's championship season in [1992–93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_NHL_season) marked the last time a Canadian team won the Stanley Cup.[^cite_note-CanadaCup-17][^cite_note-18]

The Canadiens have won the Stanley Cup 24 times, more times than any other franchise, having earned 23 victories since the founding of the NHL, and 22 since 1927, when NHL teams became the only ones to compete for the Stanley Cup.[^cite_note-MostCups-19] The Canadiens also had the most championships by a team of any of the [major North American sports leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada#%22Big_Four%22_leagues) until the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) won their 25th [World Series](23489161.md) title in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Series).[^cite_note-20]

## History

Main article: [History of the Montreal Canadiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Montreal_Canadiens)

The Canadiens were founded by [J. Ambrose O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose_O'Brien) on December 4, 1909, as a charter member of the [National Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_Association) (NHA),[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Gazette-OlympicStadiumNegotiations-22] the forerunner to the [National Hockey League](21809.md). It was to be the team of the [francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone) community in Montreal, composed of francophone players, and under francophone ownership as soon as possible.[^cite_note-23] The founders named the team "Les Canadiens", a term identified at the time with French speakers.[^cite_note-24] The team's [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1909%E2%80%9310_Montreal_Canadiens_season) was not a success, as they placed last in the league. After their first year, ownership was transferred to [George Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Kennedy_\(sports_promoter\)) of Montreal and the team's record improved over the next seasons.[^cite_note-Canadian_Dictionary_of_Biography_online-25] The team won its first Stanley Cup championship in the [1915–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1915%E2%80%9316_NHA_season).[^cite_note-26] In 1917, with four other NHA teams, the Canadiens formed the NHL,[^cite_note-27] and they won their first NHL Stanley Cup during the [1923–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923%E2%80%9324_NHL_season), led by [Howie Morenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howie_Morenz).[^cite_note-28] The team moved from the [Mount Royal Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Royal_Arena) to the [Montreal Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Forum) for the [1926–27 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926%E2%80%9327_NHL_season).[^cite_note-29]

The club began the 1930s decade successfully, with back-to-back Stanley Cup wins in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_Stanley_Cup_Final) and [1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_Stanley_Cup_Final). However, the Canadiens, along with cross-town rivals the [Montreal Maroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Maroons), declined both on the ice and economically during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_Canada) era. Losses grew to the point where team owners considered selling interest to [Cleveland](5951.md), Ohio, though local investors were ultimately found to finance the Canadiens.[^cite_note-30] After the Maroons suspended operations following the [1937–38 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937%E2%80%9338_NHL_season), several of their players joined the Canadiens.[^cite_note-31]

Led by the "[Punch Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_line_\(hockey\))" of [Maurice "Rocket" Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Richard), [Toe Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toe_Blake) and [Elmer Lach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Lach) in the 1940s, the Canadiens enjoyed success again atop the NHL. From [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Stanley_Cup_Final) to [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Stanley_Cup_Final), the franchise won six Stanley Cups, including a record five straight from [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Stanley_Cup_Final) to 1960, with a new set of stars coming to prominence: [Jean Béliveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_B%C3%A9liveau), [Dickie Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickie_Moore_\(ice_hockey\)), [Doug Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Harvey_\(ice_hockey\)), [Bernie "Boom Boom" Geoffrion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Geoffrion), [Jacques Plante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Plante) and Richard's younger brother, [Henri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Richard).[^cite_note-32]

The Canadiens added ten more championships in 15 seasons from [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Stanley_Cup_Final) to [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Stanley_Cup_Final), with another [dynastic run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty_\(sports\)#Ice_hockey) of four-straight Cups from [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Stanley_Cup_Final) to 1979.[^cite_note-33] In the [1976–77 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_NHL_season), the Canadiens set three [still-standing team records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NHL_records_\(team\)) – fewest losses (8) in an 80-game season,[^cite_note-\:0-34] the longest home unbeaten streak (34),[^cite_note-35] and best goal differential (+216)[^cite_note-36] – with one additional record that lasted almost half a century for the most points (132) in an 80-game season until it was surpassed by the [2022–23 Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Boston_Bruins_season).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] In the [1977–78 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_NHL_season), the team had a 28-game unbeaten streak, the [second-longest in NHL history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NHL_longest_winning_streaks).[^cite_note-streaks-39] The next generation of stars included [Guy Lafleur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Lafleur), [Yvan Cournoyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvan_Cournoyer), [Ken Dryden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Dryden), [Pete Mahovlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Mahovlich), [Jacques Lemaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Lemaire), [Pierre Larouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Larouche), [Steve Shutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Shutt), [Bob Gainey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Gainey), [Serge Savard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Savard), [Guy Lapointe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Lapointe) and [Larry Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Robinson). [Scotty Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotty_Bowman), who would later set a record for most NHL victories by a coach, was the team's head coach for its last five Stanley Cup victories in the 1970s.[^cite_note-40]

The Canadiens won Stanley Cups in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Stanley_Cup_Final), led by rookie star goaltender [Patrick Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Roy),[^cite_note-41] and in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Stanley_Cup_Final), continuing their streak of winning at least one championship in every decade from the 1910s to the 1990s (this streak came to an end in the 2000s).[^cite_note-42] In March 1996, the team moved from the Montreal Forum, its home during 70 seasons and 22 Stanley Cups, to the Molson Centre (now called [Bell Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Centre)).[^cite_note-43]

Following Roy's departure in 1995, the Canadiens fell into an extended stretch of mediocrity,[^cite_note-44] missing the playoffs in four of their next ten seasons and failing to advance past the second round of the playoffs until [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-45] By the late 1990s, with both an ailing team and monetary losses exacerbated by a record-low value of the [Canadian dollar](101846.md), Montreal fans feared their team would end up relocated to the [United States](3434750.md). Team owner [Molson Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molson_Brewery) sold control of the franchise and the Molson Centre to American businessman [George N. Gillett Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_N._Gillett_Jr.) in 2001, with the [right of first refusal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_first_refusal) for any future sale by Gillett and a condition that the NHL Board of Governors must unanimously approve any attempt to move to a new city.[^cite_note-Gazette-sale-Gillett-46] Led by club president [Pierre Boivin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Boivin), the Canadiens returned to being a lucrative enterprise, earning additional revenues from broadcasting and arena events. In 2009, Gillett sold the franchise to a [consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium) led by the [Molson family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molson_family) which included [The Woodbridge Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Woodbridge_Company), [BCE/Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Canada), the [Fonds de solidarité FTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fonds_de_solidarit%C3%A9_FTQ), [Michael Andlauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Andlauer), [Luc Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Bertrand) and the [National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Canada) Financial Group for \$575 million, more than double the \$275 million he spent on the purchase eight years prior.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

During the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_NHL_season), the Canadiens celebrated their [100th anniversary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Canadiens_centennial) with various events,[^cite_note-100th-commemorativeLogo-49] including hosting both the [2009 NHL All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_National_Hockey_League_All-Star_Game),[^cite_note-AllStar2009-50] and the [2009 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-PR-2009Draft-51]

The Canadiens became the first team in NHL history to reach 3,000 victories with their 5–2 victory over the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) on December 29, 2008.[^cite_note-52]

Façade of the Bell Centre. On the wall is a banner celebrating the Canadiens centennial, featuring two players, one in black and white and one in colour, and the Canadiens logo in front of a "100".

The [Bell Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Centre) with banners celebrating the [Montreal Canadiens centennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Canadiens_centennial).

For the [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NHL_season), the league moved the Canadiens along with the other six teams from Canada to the [North Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Division_\(NHL\)). Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the Canadiens only played against teams in the division in the regular season to avoid [travel restrictions between the United States and Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_restrictions_related_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic). All teams in the division played without fans to begin the season.[^cite_note-53] The Canadiens advanced through the [2021 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), beating the [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) in the first round of the playoffs 4–3, [overcoming a 3–1 Maple Leafs lead in the series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_teams_to_overcome_3%E2%80%931_series_deficits#National_Hockey_League). The Canadiens then [swept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewash_\(sport\)#Ice_hockey) the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) in the second round, advancing to the Stanley Cup semifinals.[^cite_note-54] After defeating the [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md) in the penultimate round, clinching an overtime victory in game 6 of the series, they reached their first [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_Final) in 28 years, whilst also being the first Canadian team to do so since the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-55] Montreal eventually lost the Stanley Cup Final to the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning), 4–1.[^cite_note-56]

In [2021–22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NHL_season), the Canadiens were unable to replicate their success from the prior season, ultimately finishing last in the league for the first time since the [1939–40 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939%E2%80%9340_NHL_season) and the first time in the NHL's expansion era, in what was one of the worst seasons in the team's history.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] In the process, they set team records for most regulation losses (49), most goals against (319), fewest wins (22),[^cite_note-min70games-60] and fewest points (55),[^cite_note-min70games-60] while their .335 point percentage was the team's third-worst ever, after only the [1925–26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1925%E2%80%9326_NHL_season) (.319) and [1939–40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939%E2%80%9340_NHL_season) (.260) campaigns. As a result, team owner [Geoff Molson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Molson) authorized a "rebuild" of the roster over an extended period, a first in the modern history of the franchise.[^cite_note-61] The Canadiens finished fifth-last in the subsequent [2022–23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NHL_season) and [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NHL_season) seasons.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

## Team identity

Further information: [History of the Montreal Canadiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Montreal_Canadiens)

The Canadiens organization operates in both English and French. For many years, public address announcements and press releases have been given in both languages, and the team website and social media outlets are in both languages as well. At home games, the first stanza of [O Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Canada) is sung in French, and the chorus is sung in English.

Original design of the "CHC" logo (1917–1919, 1921–1922)

The classic "C" and "H" in the Canadiens' logo, one of the sport's oldest and most recognizable, were first used together in the [1917–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917%E2%80%9318_NHL_season), when the club changed its name from "Club Athlétique Canadien" to "Club de Hockey Canadien",[^cite_note-64] before evolving to its current form in [1952–53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952%E2%80%9353_NHL_season). Previous logo iterations had simply a "C" for "Canadiens" in 1909–10 and a "C" with an "A" inside from 1913 to 1916, with the "A" standing for "Athlétique".[^cite_note-\:1-65] The "H" stands for "Hockey" in the 1917–18 team name ("Club de Hockey Canadien"), not "Habitants", a popular misconception.[^cite_note-\:1-65] In Quebec history, [habitants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitants) were francophone settlers who farmed the land along both shores of the St. Lawrence River and the Gulf of St. Lawrence. As a nickname for the hockey team, "Habitants" is attested in French on 9 February 1914 in a *[Le Devoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Devoir)* report of a 9–3 win over Toronto.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] According to *[About.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/About.com)*, the first man to refer to the team as "the Habs" was [Tex Rickard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex_Rickard), owner of the [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1925\)), in 1924. Rickard apparently told a reporter that the "H" on the Canadiens' sweaters was for "Habitants".[^cite_note-theH-68]

Since 1911, the team's primary colours have been blue, white and red. The home [sweater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_jersey) is predominantly red, with four blue-and-white stripes: one across each arm, one across the chest, and one across the waistline. The main road sweater is white with a red-and-blue stripe across the waist, red at the end of both sleeves, and red shoulder yokes. The basic design has been in use since 1914 and took its current form in 1925, evolving as materials changed.[^cite_note-jerseys-69] Because of the team's long history and significance in Quebec, the sweater has been called *'La Sainte-Flanelle'* ("the holy flannel sweater").

As of 2015, the Canadiens' home red sweater is the league's only uniform to feature the [French language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) version of the NHL shield logo (LNH) on the neck collar, in acknowledgment of Montreal's French Canadian heritage. The road white sweater has the English NHL shield logo.[^cite_note-70]

The Canadiens used multiple other designs before 1914. The original shirt of the 1909–10 season was blue with a white C. The second season had a red shirt featuring a green [maple leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_leaf) with the C logo and green pants. In the season before they adopted the current look, the Canadiens wore a "[barber pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_pole)" jersey with red, white, and blue stripes, and a logo with a white maple leaf reading "CAC" ("Club athlétique Canadien").[^cite_note-jerseys-69] All three designs were worn during the [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_NHL_season) as part of the Canadiens' [centenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary).[^cite_note-cent-71]

In the 2020–21 season, the Canadiens unveiled a "[Reverse Retro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NHL_season#%22Reverse_Retro%22_jerseys)" alternate uniform in collaboration with [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas). The uniform was essentially the same as their regular red uniform, but with blue as the primary colour and red as the stripe colour.[^cite_note-72] A second iteration was released in the 2022–23 season, again using the same template but with red relegated to the logo only and featuring a light blue base with white/dark blue/white stripes.[^cite_note-73]

This period also saw the introduction of [corporate sponsor advertising](21809.md#Corporate_sponsors) across NHL-sanctioned equipment, starting with helmet ads and followed by front jersey patches on gameday uniforms. Contextually, the Canadiens' away jerseys feature the [Air Canada](145623.md) logo[^cite_note-74] in the upper right chest area, while the name and shield of the [Royal Bank of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bank_of_Canada) (RBC) is stitched onto its home counterpart.[^cite_note-75]

The Canadiens' colours are a readily identifiable aspect of French Canadian culture. In the short story "[The Hockey Sweater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hockey_Sweater)", [Roch Carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roch_Carrier) described the influence of the Canadiens and their jersey in rural Quebec communities during the 1940s.[^cite_note-Civilization-HockeySweater-76] The story was adapted into an animated short, *The Sweater*, narrated by Carrier.[^cite_note-NFB-HockeySweater-77] A passage from the short story appears on the [2002 issuance of the Canadian five-dollar bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Journey_Series#\$5_note).[^cite_note-CBC-SpiritHockey-78][^cite_note-HockeyPeoplesHistory-VirtualHotStove-79]

### Motto

> *Nos bras meurtris vous tendent le flambeau, à vous toujours de le porter bien haut.*

> To you from failing hands we throw the torch. Be yours to hold it high.

The motto is from the poem "[In Flanders Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Flanders_Fields)" by [John McCrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCrae), which was written in 1915, the year before the Canadiens won their first Stanley Cup championship. The motto appears on the wall of the Canadiens' dressing room as well as on the inside collar of the new [Adidas Adizero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_uniform#Adidas_ADIZERO_\(2017%E2%80%932024\)) jerseys introduced in 2017.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

Following the last game at the Forum, every living former Canadiens captain passed down a torch until it reached then-current captain [Pierre Turgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Turgeon), who became the first to wear the C at the then-Molson Centre. During the 2026 playoffs, a former Canadiens great carried a torch into the Bell Centre to symbolically "ignite" the arena during pregame festivities.[^cite_note-82]

### Mascot



The Canadiens mascot, Youppi!, poses for photographs at a Rogers Media event

Beginning in the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_season), the Canadiens adopted [Youppi!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youppi!) as their official mascot, the first costumed mascot in their long history. The foregoing was the longtime mascot for [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB)'s [Montreal Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos) but was dropped from the franchise when they moved to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) prior to the [2005 MLB season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Major_League_Baseball_season) and became the [Washington Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Nationals).[^cite_note-NBC-83] With the changeover, Youppi! became the first mascot in professional sports to switch leagues. He is also the first mascot in professional sports to get ejected from a game dating back to his time with the Expos.[^cite_note-84] In June 2020, Youppi! became the first mascot from a Canadian-based club to be inducted into the [Mascot Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascot_Hall_of_Fame).[^cite_note-85]

In November 2022, the Canadiens introduced METAL!, an "unofficial official mascot", for the team's Reverse Retro series of games that season.[^cite_note-86] METAL! was retired ahead of the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NHL_season).[^cite_note-87]

### Rivalries

#### Toronto Maple Leafs

Main article: [Canadiens–Maple Leafs rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadiens%E2%80%93Maple_Leafs_rivalry)

The Canadiens have developed strong rivalries with two fellow Original Six franchises, with whom they frequently shared divisions and competed in postseason play. The oldest is with the [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs), who first faced the Canadiens as the [Toronto Arenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Arenas) in 1917. The teams met 16 times in the playoffs, including five [Stanley Cup Finals](3314237.md). Featuring the two largest cities in Canada and two of the largest fanbases in the league, the rivalry is sometimes dramatized as being emblematic of Canada's [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadian) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadian) linguistic divide.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] From 1938 to 1970, they were the only two Canadian teams in the league.

#### Boston Bruins

Main article: [Bruins–Canadiens rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruins%E2%80%93Canadiens_rivalry)

The team's other Original Six rivals are the [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins), who, since their NHL debut in 1924 have played the Canadiens more than any other team in both regular season play and [the playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_most_frequent_NHL_playoff_series) combined. The teams have played 34 playoff series, seven of which were in the finals.[^cite_note-Canadiens.com-90][^cite_note-Games-91]

#### Quebec Nordiques (1979–1995)

Main article: [Battle of Quebec (ice hockey)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Quebec_\(ice_hockey\))

The Canadiens also had an intraprovincial rivalry with the [Quebec Nordiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Nordiques) during their existence from 1979 to 1995, nicknamed the "[Battle of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Quebec_\(ice_hockey\))".[^cite_note-92]

## Broadcasting

Main article: [List of Montreal Canadiens broadcasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Montreal_Canadiens_broadcasters)

Montreal Canadiens games are broadcast locally in both the French and English languages. [CHMP 98.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHMP-FM) is the Canadiens' French-language radio flagship.[^cite_note-fagstein-habs15-93] The station began simulcasting Canadiens games with sister station [CKAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKAC) in 2007 due to the latter's weaker nighttime signal, and became sole flagship in 2011 after CKAC switched to an all-to an all-traffic format in 2011. Martin McGuire is the French play-by-play voice, with Dany Dubé on colour commentary.

As of the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NHL_season), the team's regional television rights in both languages, and its English-language radio rights, are held by [Bell Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Media).[^cite_note-tsn-newrights2017-94] [CKGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKGM), *[TSN Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSN_Radio) 690*, is the English-language radio flagship; it acquired the rights under a seven-year deal which began in the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NHL_season).[^cite_note-95] In June 2017, Bell Media reached a five-year extension.[^cite_note-tsn-newrights2017-94]

Regional television rights in French are held by [Réseau des sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_des_sports) (RDS) under a 12-year deal that began in the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_NHL_season).[^cite_note-tsn-rdscanadienscontract-96] A sister to the English-language network [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network), RDS was the only French-language sports channel in Canada until the 2011 launch of [TVA Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVA_Sports),[^cite_note-tvasports-gazette-97] and was also the previous national French rightsholder of the NHL; as a result, the Canadiens forwent a separate regional contract, and allowed all of its games to be televised nationally in French as part of RDS's overall NHL rights.[^cite_note-gandm-tvarogers-98]

With TVA Sports becoming the national French rightsholder in the 2014–15 season through a sub-licensing agreement with [Sportsnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet),[^cite_note-gandm-tvarogers-98] RDS subsequently announced a 12-year deal to maintain regional rights to Canadiens games not shown on TVA Sports. As a result, games on RDS are [blacked out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackout_\(broadcasting\)) outside the Canadiens' home market of Quebec, [Atlantic Canada](71095.md) and Ontario from [Pembroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke,_Ontario) eastward shared with the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators).[^cite_note-tsn-rdscanadienscontract-96] At least 22 Canadiens games per season (primarily through its Saturday night *[La super soirée LNH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Soir%C3%A9e_du_hockey#Present_day)*), including all playoff games, are televised nationally by TVA Sports.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-nhl-tvasports-100]

[TSN2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSN2) assumed the English-language regional television rights in the 2017–18 season, with [John Bartlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bartlett_\(sportscaster\)) on play-by-play, and [Dave Poulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Poulin), [Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson_\(ice_hockey\)) and [Craig Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Button) on colour commentary.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-tsn-newrights2017-94] All other games, including all playoff games, are televised nationally by [Sportsnet or CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_Sportsnet).[^cite_note-sn-habs2015-102] Bartlett returned to Sportsnet over the 2018 off-season, and was succeeded by [Bryan Mudryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Mudryk).[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

English-language regional rights were previously held by [Sportsnet East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet_East) (with [CJNT *City Montreal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJNT-DT) as an overflow channel), under a three-year deal that expired following the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NHL_season); the games were called by Bartlett and [Jason York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_York). Prior to this deal, TSN held the rights from 2010 through 2014; the games were broadcast on a part-time channel with [Dave Randorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Randorf) on play-by-play.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-fagstein-habs15-93][^cite_note-106]

#### Hall of Fame

The Montreal Canadiens have an affiliation with a number of inductees to the [Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame). Thirty-seven of these players are from three separate notable dynasties: 12 from 1955 to 1960, 11 from 1964 to 1969, and 13 from 1975 to 1979. [Howie Morenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howie_Morenz) and [Georges Vézina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_V%C3%A9zina) were the first Canadiens given the honour in 1945, while [Shea Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Weber) was the most recently inducted, in 2024. Along with players, a number of inductees from the builders category are affiliated with the club. The first inductee was vice president [William Northey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Northey_\(ice_hockey\)) in 1945. The most recent inductee was [Pat Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Burns) in 2014.[^cite_note-112]

In addition to players and builders, seven broadcasters for the Montreal Canadiens have been awarded the [Foster Hewitt Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_Hewitt_Memorial_Award) from the Hockey Hall of Fame. The first two recipients of the award were [Danny Gallivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Gallivan) and [René Lecavalier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lecavalier) in 1984. The other five award recipients are [Doug Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Smith_\(sportscaster\)) (1985), [Dick Irvin Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Irvin_Jr.) (1988), [Richard Garneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Garneau) (1999), [Gilles Tremblay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Tremblay_\(ice_hockey\)) (2002), and [Pierre Houde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Houde) (2024).[^cite_note-113]
