**French Polynesia** ([/ˌpɒlɪˈniːʒə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/En-us-French_Polynesia.ogg/En-us-French_Polynesia.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/86/En-us-French_Polynesia.ogg/En-us-French_Polynesia.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*POL-ih-NEE-zhə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Polynésie française* [\[pɔlinezi fʁɑ̃sɛːz\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cb/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Polyn%C3%A9sie_fran%C3%A7aise.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Polyn%C3%A9sie_fran%C3%A7aise.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cb/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Polyn%C3%A9sie_fran%C3%A7aise.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Polyn%C3%A9sie_fran%C3%A7aise.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Tahitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_language): *Pōrīnetia farāni*) is an [overseas collectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_collectivity) of [France](5843419.md) and its sole [overseas country](#Governance); it is classified as a [non-self-governing territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories) by the [United Nations](31769.md). It comprises 121 geographically dispersed islands and [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll)[^cite_note-islands-9] stretching over more than 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi) in the [South Pacific Ocean](23070.md). French Polynesia is associated with the [European Union](9317.md) as an [overseas country and territory (OCT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Countries_and_Territories).[^cite_note-10] The total land area of French Polynesia is 3,521 square kilometres (1,359 sq mi),[^cite_note-land_area-6] with a population of 282,596 as of September 2025[^cite_note-11] of which at least 205,000 live in the [Society Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_Islands) and the remaining population lives in the rest of the archipelago.

French Polynesia is divided into five island groups: the [Austral Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austral_Islands); the [Gambier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambier_Islands); the [Marquesas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesas_Islands); the [Society Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_Islands) (comprising the [Leeward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Islands_\(Society_Islands\)) and [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands_\(Society_Islands\))); and the [Tuamotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotus). Among its 121 islands and atolls, 75 were inhabited at the 2017 census.[^cite_note-islands-9] [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti), which is in the Society Islands group, is the most populous island, home to nearly 69% of the population of French Polynesia as of 2017. [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete), located on Tahiti, is the capital of French Polynesia. Although not an integral part of its territory, [Clipperton Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipperton_Island) was administered from French Polynesia until 2007.

Hundreds of years after the [Great Polynesian Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia#History_of_the_Polynesian_people), European explorers began traveling through the region, visiting the islands of French Polynesia on several occasions. Traders and whaling ships also visited. In 1842, the French took over the islands and established a French [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) that they called *_Établissements français d'Océanie_* (EFO; French Establishments/Settlements of Oceania).

In 1946, the EFO became an [overseas territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_territory_\(France\)) under the constitution of the [French Fourth Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fourth_Republic), and [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians) were granted the right to vote through citizenship. In 1957, the territory was renamed French Polynesia. In 1983, it became a member of the [Pacific Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Community), a regional development organization. Since 28 March 2003, French Polynesia has been an overseas collectivity of the French Republic under the constitutional revision of article 74, and later gained, with law 2004-192 of 27 February 2004, an administrative autonomy, two symbolic manifestations of which are the title of the [President of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_French_Polynesia) and its additional designation as an [overseas country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_country_of_France).[^cite_note-12]

## History

Anthropologists and historians believe the Great Polynesian Migration commenced around 1500 BC as [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) went on a journey using [celestial navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_navigation) to find islands in the South Pacific Ocean. The first islands of French Polynesia to be settled were the Marquesas Islands in about 200 BC. The [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians) later ventured southwest and discovered the Society Islands around AD 300.[^cite_note-KMLA1797-13]

European encounters began in 1521 when Portuguese explorer [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan), sailing at the service of the [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Spain), sighted [Puka-Puka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puka-Puka) in the [Tuāmotu-Gambier Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Tuamotu-Gambier). In 1606 another Spanish expedition under [Pedro Fernandes de Queirós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Fernandes_de_Queir%C3%B3s) sailed through Polynesia sighting an inhabited island on 10 February[^cite_note-14] which they called Sagitaria (or Sagittaria), probably the island of [Rekareka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rekareka) to the southeast of Tahiti.[^cite_note-15] In 1722, Dutchman [Jakob Roggeveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Roggeveen) while on an expedition sponsored by the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company), charted the location of six islands in the Tuamotu Archipelago and two islands in the Society Islands, one of which was [Bora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Bora).

British explorer [Samuel Wallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wallis) became the first European navigator to visit Tahiti in 1767. French explorer [Louis Antoine de Bougainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Antoine_de_Bougainville) also visited Tahiti in 1768, while British explorer [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook) arrived in 1769,[^cite_note-KMLA1797-13] and [observed the transit of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1769_transit_of_Venus_observed_from_Tahiti). He would stop in Tahiti again in 1773 during his second voyage to the Pacific, and once more in 1777 during his third and last voyage before being killed in Hawaii.

In 1772, the Spanish [Viceroy of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru) Don [Manuel de Amat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_de_Amat_y_Junient) ordered a number of expeditions to Tahiti under the command of [Domingo de Bonechea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domingo_de_Bonechea) who was the first European to explore all of the main islands beyond Tahiti.[^cite_note-Paradise_Past_book_(page_43:_The_De_Boenechea_Expedition)-16] A short-lived Spanish settlement was created in 1774,[^cite_note-KMLA1797-13] and for a time some maps bore the name *Isla de Amat* after Viceroy Amat.[^cite_note-17] [Christian missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) began with Spanish priests who stayed in Tahiti for a year. Protestants from the [London Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Missionary_Society) settled permanently in Polynesia in 1797.



Society Island kingdoms

King [Pōmare II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomare_II) of Tahiti was forced to flee to [Mo'orea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorea) in 1803; he and his subjects were converted to Protestantism in 1812. French Catholic missionaries arrived on Tahiti in 1834; their expulsion in 1836 caused France to send a gunboat in 1838. In 1842, Tahiti and [Tahuata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahuata) were declared a [French protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate), to allow Catholic missionaries to work undisturbed. The capital of [Papeetē](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete) was founded in 1843. In 1880, France annexed Tahiti, changing the status from that of a protectorate to that of a [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empires). The island groups were not officially united until the establishment of the French protectorate in 1889.[^cite_note-KMLA1889-18]

After France declared a protectorate over Tahiti in 1842 and [fought a war with Tahiti (1844–1847)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Tahitian_War), the British and French signed the [Jarnac Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarnac_Convention) in 1847, declaring that the kingdoms of [Raiatea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raiatea), [Huahine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huahine) and [Bora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Bora) were to remain independent from both powers and that no single chief was to be allowed to reign over the entire archipelago. France eventually broke the agreement, and the islands were annexed and became a colony in 1888 (eight years after the Windward Islands) after many native resistances and conflicts called the [Leewards War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leewards_War), lasting until 1897.[^cite_note-Craig-19][^cite_note-Matsuda-20]

In the 1880s, France claimed the [Tuamotu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotu_Archipelago), which formerly belonged to the [Pōmare Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomare_Dynasty), without formally annexing it. Having declared a protectorate over Tahuata in 1842, the French regarded the entire Marquesas Islands as French. In 1885, France appointed a governor and established a general council, thus giving it the proper administration for a colony. The islands of [Rimatara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimatara) and [Rūrutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurutu) unsuccessfully lobbied for British protection in 1888, so in 1889 they were annexed by France. Postage stamps were first issued in the colony in 1892. The first official name for the colony was *Établissements de l'Océanie* (Establishments in Oceania); in 1903 the general council was changed to an advisory council, and the colony's name was changed to [Établissements français de l'Océanie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tablissements_fran%C3%A7ais_de_l'Oc%C3%A9anie?action=edit\&redlink=1) (French Establishments in Oceania).[^cite_note-KMLA1918-21]



A two-franc [World War II](32927.md) emergency-issue banknote (1943), printed in [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete), and depicting the outline of [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti) on the reverse

In 1940, the administration of French Polynesia recognised the [Free French Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_Forces), and many Polynesians served in World War II, primarily in North Africa and parts of Italy. Unknown at the time to the French and Polynesians, the [Konoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumimaro_Konoe) Cabinet in [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japan) on 16 September 1940 included French Polynesia among the many territories which were to become [Japanese possessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_East_Asia_Co-Prosperity_Sphere#Japanese-governed), as part of the "[Eastern Pacific Government-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_East_Asia_Co-Prosperity_Sphere)" in the [post-war world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothetical_Axis_victory_in_World_War_II).[^cite_note-22] However, in the course of the war in the Pacific the Japanese were not able to launch an actual invasion of the French islands.



The French frigate [*Floréal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Flor%C3%A9al) in November 2002, at anchor in [Bora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Bora) lagoon

In 1946, Polynesians were granted French citizenship and the islands' status was changed to an overseas territory. The islands' name was changed in 1957 to *Polynésie Française* (French Polynesia). In 1962, France's early [nuclear testing](589091.md) ground in [Algeria](358.md) was no longer usable when Algeria became independent and the [Moruroa atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moruroa) in the Tuamotu Archipelago was selected as the new testing site. Nuclear tests were conducted underground after 1974.[^cite_note-KMLA1977-23] In 1977, French Polynesia was granted partial internal autonomy; in 1984, the autonomy was extended. French Polynesia became a full overseas collectivity of France in 2003.[^cite_note-cia-24]

In September 1995, France stirred up widespread protests by resuming nuclear testing at [Fangataufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangataufa) atoll after a three-year moratorium. The last test was on 27 January 1996. On 29 January 1996, France announced that it would accede to the [Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Test_Ban_Treaty), and no longer test nuclear weapons.[^cite_note-NYT-25]

French Polynesia was relisted in the [United Nations list of non-self-governing territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories) in 2013, making it eligible for a UN-backed independence referendum. The relisting was made after the indigenous opposition was voiced and supported by the Polynesian Leaders Group, Pacific Conference of Churches, Women's International League for Peace and Freedom, Non-Aligned Movement, World Council of Churches, and Melanesian Spearhead Group.[^cite_note-26]

## Governance

Main article: [Politics of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_French_Polynesia)

Under the terms of Article 74 of the [French constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_France) and the [Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Law) 2014–192 on the statute of autonomy of French Polynesia, politics of French Polynesia takes place in a framework of a [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) French overseas collectivity, whereby the President of French Polynesia is the [head of government](37570.md), and of a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the government and the [Assembly of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_French_Polynesia) (the territorial assembly).

Political life in French Polynesia was marked by great instability from the mid-2000s to the mid-2010s. The anti-independence right-wing president of French Polynesia, [Gaston Flosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Flosse), who had been in power since 1991, had supported the resumption of the [French nuclear weapons tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_nuclear_weapons_tests_of_France) in 1995. Furthermore, he had obtained from his longtime friend and political ally [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac), then president of France, a status of expanded autonomy for French Polynesia in 2004. He failed to secure an absolute majority in the [2004 French Polynesian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_French_Polynesian_legislative_election), resulting in deadlock at the Assembly of French Polynesia. Flosse's longtime opponent, the pro-independence leader [Oscar Temaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Temaru), whose pro-independence coalition had won one less seat than Flosse's party in the Assembly, was elected president of French Polynesia by the Assembly in June 2004 thanks to the votes of two non-aligned Assembly members. This resulted in several years of political instability, as neither the pro- nor the anti-independence camps were assured of a majority, depending on the votes of smaller non-aligned parties representing the interests of the distant islands of French Polynesia (as opposed to Tahiti). Several [constructive votes of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_vote_of_no_confidence) were passed against the presidency, resulting in it shuffling between Temaru and Flosse until December 2006, when Temaru was succeeded by [Gaston Tong Sang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Tong_Sang), a close ally of Flosse.

On 14 September 2007, Temaru was elected president of French Polynesia for the third time in three years (with 27 of 44 votes cast in the Assembly).[^cite_note-27] He replaced anti-independence leader [Gaston Tong Sang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Tong_Sang), who on 31 August had lost a no confidence vote in the Assembly, after Flosse, hitherto opposed to independence, supported Temaru to topple the Tong Sang government. Temaru, however, had no stable majority in the Assembly, and new elections were held in February 2008 in an attempt to solve the political crisis.



The [Assembly of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_French_Polynesia)

Tong Sang's political party, [Tāhōʻēraʻa Huiraʻatira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81h%C5%8D%CA%BB%C4%93ra%CA%BBa_Huira%CA%BBatira), won the territorial elections. The two minority parties of Temaru and Flosse, who together had one more member in the Assembly than did Tāhōʻēraʻa Huiraʻatira, formed an alliance to prevent Tong Sang from becoming president again. Flosse was then elected president of French Polynesia by the territorial assembly on 23 February 2008 with the support of the pro-independence party led by Temaru, while Temaru was elected speaker of the territorial assembly with the support of the anti-independence party led by Flosse. Both formed a coalition cabinet. Many observers doubted that the alliance between anti-independence Flosse and pro-independence Temaru could last very long.[^cite_note-28]

At the [French municipal elections held in March 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_French_municipal_elections), several prominent mayors who were members of the Flosse–Temaru coalition lost their offices in key municipalities of French Polynesia, which was interpreted as a disapproval of how the alliance had prevented Tong Sang (whose party French Polynesian voters had placed first in the territorial elections the month before) from becoming president of French Polynesia. Only a month later, on 15 April 2008, the coalition government was toppled—by a [constructive vote of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_vote_of_no_confidence) in the territorial assembly—when two members of the Flosse–Temaru coalition left the coalition and sided with Tong Sang's party. Tong Sang's majority in the territorial assembly was very narrow, and he was toppled in February 2009, succeeded again by Temaru, who was still supported by Flosse.

Temaru's return to power was brief as he fell out with Flosse and was toppled in November 2009, succeeded by his predecessor, Tong Sang. Tong Sang remained in power for a year and a half before being toppled in a vote of no confidence in April 2011, and succeeded again by Temaru. Temaru's fifth stint as president of French Polynesia lasted two years, during which time he campaigned for the re-inscription of French Polynesia on the [United Nations list of non-self-governing territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories). Temaru's party, [Tāvini Huiraʻatira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81vini_Huira%CA%BBatira), lost the [2013 French Polynesian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_Polynesian_legislative_election) by a wide margin, only two weeks before the [United Nations](31769.md) re-registered French Polynesia on its list of non-self governing territories. This was interpreted by political analysts as a rejection by French Polynesian voters of Temaru's push for independence as well as the consequence of the socioeconomic crisis affecting French Polynesia after years of political instability and corruption scandals.

Flosse, whose anti-independence party won a majority of seats in the 2013 election, succeeded Temaru as president of French Polynesia in May 2013, but he was removed from office in September 2014 due to a corruption conviction by France's highest court. Flosse was replaced as president of French Polynesia by his second-in-command in the anti-independence camp, [Édouard Fritch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Fritch), who was also Flosse's former son-in-law (divorced from Flosse's daughter). Fritch fell out with Flosse in 2015 as both leaders were vying for control of the anti-independence camp, and Fritch was excluded from Flosse's party in September 2015, before founding his own anti-independence party, [Tāpura Huiraʻatira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81pura_Huira%CA%BBatira), in February 2016. His new party managed to keep a majority in the Assembly, and Fritch remained as president.

Political stability returned to French Polynesia following the split of the anti-independence camp in 2016. Tāpura Huiraʻatira won 70% of the seats in the Assembly of French Polynesia at the [2018 French Polynesian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Polynesian_legislative_election)—defeating both Temaru's pro-independence party and Flosse's anti-independence party—and Édouard Fritch was re-elected president of French Polynesia by the Assembly in May 2018. By 2022, Édouard Fritch was the longest-serving president of French Polynesia since his former father-in-law Flosse in the 1990s and early 2000s.

### Legal status



[Bora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Bora)

Between 1946 and 2003, French Polynesia had the status of an overseas territory (*_[territoire d'outre-mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territoire_d'outre-mer)_*, or *TOM*). In 2003, it became an overseas collectivity (*_[collectivité d'outre-mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivit%C3%A9_d'outre-mer)_*, or COM). Its statutory law of 27 February 2004 gives it the particular designation of "[overseas country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_country) within the Republic" (*_pays d'outre-mer au sein de la République_*, or POM), but without legal modification of its status.[^cite_note-29]

### Relations with mainland France



High Commission of the French Republic in Papeete

Despite having a local assembly and government, French Polynesia is not in a [free association](346414.md) with France, like the [Cook Islands](7067.md) with [New Zealand](4913064.md). As a French overseas collectivity, the [French central government](3398091.md) has direct responsibility for justice, university education, defense (including the [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md) and [French military forces](10724.md)) and foreign affairs. The local government of French Polynesia retains control over primary and secondary education, health, town planning, and the environment.[^cite_note-30] The [High Commissioner of the Republic in French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner_of_the_Republic_in_French_Polynesia) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Haut commissaire de la République en Polynésie française*) represents the French state in the territory.

French Polynesia sends three [deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(France\)) to the [French National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_France) in three constituencies, [the 1st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Polynesia's_1st_constituency) representing Papeete and its north-eastern suburbs, plus the [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) (municipality) of [Mo'orea-Mai'ao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorea-Maiao), the [Tuāmotu-Gambier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Tuamotu-Gambier) administrative division, and the Marquesas Islands administrative division, [the 2nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Polynesia's_2nd_constituency) representing much of Tahiti outside Papeete and the Austral Islands administrative subdivision, and [the 3rd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Polynesia's_3rd_constituency) representing the [Leeward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Islands_\(Society_Islands\)) [administrative subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_French_Polynesia) and the south-western suburbs of Papeete. French Polynesia also sends two senators to the [French Senate](567323.md).

#### Defence

The defence of the collectivity is the responsibility of the [French Armed Forces](10724.md). Some 900 military personnel are deployed in the territory — incorporating the Pacific-Polynesian Marine Infantry Regiment (RIMaP-P)[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-fpolynesia-32] — along with modest air transport and surveillance assets.[^cite_note-33] The latter included three [Falcon 200 Gardian maritime surveillance aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_20) from [French Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Naval_Aviation), which as of 2025/26 are being replaced, on an interim basis, by the more advanced [Falcon 50 aircraft](3098320.md). These aircraft will in turn be replaced by the new [Falcon 2000 Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_2000) starting in about 2030.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] The former is composed of two [CN-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA/IPTN_CN-235) tactical transport aircraft drawn from the Air Force's ET 82 "Maine" transport squadron.[^cite_note-fpolynesia-32]

As of mid-2024, three principal [French Navy](383256.md) vessels are based in the territory, including the surveillance frigate [*Prairial*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Prairial), the patrol and support ship [*Bougainville*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Entrecasteaux-class_patrol_ship), and *Teriieroo to Teriierooiterai*, a vessel of the new [*Félix Éboué* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrouilleur_Outre-mer) of patrol vessels.[^cite_note-39] In 2027 a second vessel of the *Félix Éboué* class, *Philip Bernardino*, is to be deployed in Tahiti to further reinforce France's maritime surveillance capabilities in the region.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] As of 2025, the coastal tug (RPC), *Manini*, was operational in the territory[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] and she is eventually to be replaced and/or complemented by two *RP10*-class harbour tugboats.[^cite_note-45]

As of 2025, Flottilla 35F of [French Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Naval_Aviation) deploys a detachment of two [AS 365N Dauphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_AS365_Dauphin) helicopters in Tahiti.[^cite_note-46] The helicopters carry out a variety of roles in the territory, and may be embarked on the frigate *Prairial* as needed.[^cite_note-47]

The [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md) (which also polices rural and border areas in metropolitan France) deploys some 500 active personnel and civilians, plus around 150 reservists, in French Polynesia.[^cite_note-48] The patrol boat *Jasmin* of the [Maritime Gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Gendarmerie) is also based in the territory and is to be replaced by a new PCG-NG patrol boat in the latter 2020s.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_French_Polynesia)



Map of French Polynesia



[Bora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Bora), Leeward Islands

The islands of French Polynesia make up a total land area of 3,521 square kilometres (1,359 sq mi),[^cite_note-land_area-6] scattered over more than 2,000 km (1,200 miles) of ocean. There are 121 islands in French Polynesia and many more islets or *motu*s around [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll).[^cite_note-islands-9] The highest point is [Mount Orohena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Orohena) on [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti).

It is made up of five archipelagos. The largest and most populated island is Tahiti, in the Society Islands.

Aside from Tahiti, some other important atolls, islands, and island groups in French Polynesia are: [Ahē](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahe), [Bora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Bora), [Hiva ʻOa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiva_Oa), [Huahine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huahine), [Maiʻao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai%CA%BBao), [Maupiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maupiti), [Mehetiʻa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meheti%CA%BBa), [Moʻorea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo%CA%BBorea), [Nuku Hiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuku_Hiva), [Raʻiātea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%CA%BBi%C4%81tea), [Tahaʻa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha%CA%BBa), [Tetiʻaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teti%CA%BBaroa), [Tubuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubuai) and [Tūpai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupai).

French Polynesia is home to four terrestrial ecoregions: [Marquesas tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesas_tropical_moist_forests), [Society Islands tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_Islands_tropical_moist_forests), [Tuamotu tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotu_tropical_moist_forests), and [Tubuai tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubuai_tropical_moist_forests).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-52]

## Administrative divisions

Main article: [Administrative divisions of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_French_Polynesia)



The 5 administrative subdivisions and 48 [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) of French Polynesia.

French Polynesia is divided in five administrative subdivisions (*subdivisions administratives*):

- [Marquesas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesas_Islands) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *les îles Marquises* or officially *la subdivision administrative des îles Marquises*)

- [Leeward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Islands_\(Society_Islands\)) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *les îles Sous-le-Vent* or officially *la subdivision administrative des îles Sous-le-Vent*) (the two *subdivisions administratives* Windward Islands and Leeward Islands are part of the Society Islands)

- [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands_\(Society_Islands\)) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *les îles du Vent* or officially *la subdivision administrative des îles du Vent*) (the two *subdivisions administratives* Windward Islands and Leeward Islands are part of the Society Islands)

- [Tuāmotu-Gambier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Tuamotu-Gambier) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *les Îles Tuamotu-Gambier* or officially *la subdivision administrative des îles Tuamotu-Gambier*) (the Tuamotus and the Gambier Islands)

- [Austral Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austral_Islands) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *les îles Australes* or officially *la subdivision administrative des îles Australes*) (including the [Bass Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_Islands_\(French_Polynesia\)))

The five administrative subdivisions are not local councils; they are solely [deconcentrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralisation) subdivisions of the French central State. At the head of each administrative subdivision is an *administrateur d'État* ('State administrator'), generally simply known as *administrateur*, also sometimes called *chef de la subdivision administrative* ('head of the administrative subdivision'). The *administrateur* is a civil servant under the authority of the High Commissioner of the French Republic in French Polynesia in [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete).

Four administrative subdivisions (Marquesas Islands, Leeward Islands, Tuamotu-Gambier, and Austral Islands) each also form a deconcentrated subdivision of the government of French Polynesia. These are called *circonscriptions* ('districts'). The head of a *circonscription* is the *tavana hau*, known as *administrateur territorial* in French ('territorial administrator'), but the [Tahitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_language) title *tavana hau* is most often used. The [president of French Polynesia's government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_French_Polynesia) appoints the *tavana hau*, who directly reports to the president. The Windward Islands, due to their proximity to Papeete, do not form a deconcentrated subdivision of the government of French Polynesia.

The 5 administrative subdivisions are themselves divided in 48 [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France). Like all other communes in the French Republic, these are municipalities in which local residents with either a French or another [EU](9317.md) citizenship elect a municipal council and a mayor in charge of managing local affairs within the commune. Municipal elections occur every six years on the same date as in the rest of the French Republic (the last municipal elections [took place in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_municipal_elections)).

30 communes are further subdivided in 98 [associated communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_communes_of_France) which have each a delegate mayor and a registry office. These 30 communes were subdivided in associated communes either because they have a large land territory (particularly in the larger islands such as [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti) or [Nuku Hiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuku_Hiva)) or because they are made up of [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll) distant from each other (particularly in the Tuamotu archipelago), which led to the creation of associated communes for each inhabited atoll.

17 communes (out of French Polynesia's 48 communes) have banded together in three separate [communities of communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communaut%C3%A9_de_communes). These indirectly elected [intercommunal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France#Intercommunality) are still relatively new in French Polynesia, and unlike in [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) and its [overseas regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_regions_of_France) it is not mandatory for the communes in French Polynesia to join an intercommunal council. The three intercommunal councils in existence as of 2022, all formed on a voluntary basis, were:

- community of communes of the Marquesas Islands ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *communauté de communes des îles Marquises*, or *CODIM*), formed in 2010 by all the communes in the administrative subdivision of the Marquesas Islands

- community of communes Havaʻi ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *communauté de communes Havaʻi*, or *CCH*), formed in 2012 by all the communes in the administrative subdivision of the Leeward Islands, with the exception of [Bora-Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora-Bora_\(commune\)) which preferred to remain separate for financial reasons

- community of communes Terehēamanu ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *communauté de communes Terehēamanu*), formed in 2021 by 5 [exurban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurb) and rural communes on the eastern side of the island of [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti): [Hitiaa O Te Ra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitiaa_O_Te_Ra), [Taiarapu-Est](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiarapu-Est), [Taiarapu-Ouest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiarapu-Ouest), [Teva I Uta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teva_I_Uta), and [Papara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papara).

These communities of communes, as elsewhere in the French Republic, are not full-fledged [territorial collectivities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_collectivity), but only federations of communes. From a legal standpoint, the only territorial collectivities in French Polynesia are the overseas collectivity of French Polynesia and the 48 communes.

## Demographics



Tahitian girls, c. 1860–1879

Main article: [Demographics of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_French_Polynesia)

Total population was 278,786 according to the 18 August 2022 census,[^cite_note-pop-7] 68.7% of whom lived on the island of [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti) alone. The urban area of Papeete, the capital city, has 136,771 inhabitants (2017 census).[^cite_note-55]

At the 2017 census, 89.0% of people living in French Polynesia had been born there (up from 87.3% in 2007); 8.1% had been born in [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) (down from 9.3% in 2007); 1.2% were born elsewhere in [overseas France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_France) (down from 1.4% in 2007); and 1.7% were from foreign countries (down from 2.0% in 2007).[^cite_note-Census_2017-56] The population of natives of Metropolitan France living in French Polynesia has declined in relative terms since the 1980s, but in absolute terms their population peaked at the 2007 census, when 24,265 lived in French Polynesia (not counting their children born there).[^cite_note-migration-57] With the local economic crisis, their population declined to 22,278 at the 2012 census,[^cite_note-migration-57] and 22,387 at the 2017 census.[^cite_note-Census_2017-56]

All the indigenous languages of French Polynesia are [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_languages). French Polynesia has been linguistically diverse since ancient times, with each community having its own local speech variety. These dialects can be grouped into seven languages on the basis of [mutual intelligibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility): [Tahitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_language), [Tuamotuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotuan_language), [Rapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapa_language), [Austral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austral_language), [North Marquesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Marquesan), [South Marquesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Marquesan), and [Mangarevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangareva_language). Some of these, especially Tuamotuan, are really [dialect continua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect_continua) formed by a patchwork of different dialects. The [distinction between languages and dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_and_dialect) is notoriously difficult to establish, and so some authors may view two varieties as dialects of the same language, while others may view them as distinct languages. In this way, North and South Marquesan are often grouped together as a single Marquesan language, and Rapa is often viewed as part of Austral subfamily. At the same time, Ra'ivavae is often viewed as distinct from them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECharpentierFrançois201573–76-64]

[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) is the sole official language of French Polynesia.[^cite_note-65] An [organic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_law) of 12 April 1996 states that "French is the official language, Tahitian and other Polynesian languages can be used". At the 2017 census, among the population whose age was 15 and older, 73.9% of people reported that the language they spoke the most at home was French (up from 68.6% at the 2007 census), 20.2% reported that the language they spoke the most at home was [Tahitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_language) (down from 24.3% at the 2007 census), 2.6% reported [Marquesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesan_language) and 0.2% the related [Mangareva language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangareva_language) (same percentages for both at the 2007 census), 1.2% reported any of the [Austral languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austral_language) (down from 1.3% at the 2007 census), 1.0% reported [Tuamotuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotuan_language) (down from 1.5% at the 2007 census), 0.6% reported a [Chinese dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Chinese) (41% of which was [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese)) (down from 1.0% at the 2007 census), and 0.4% another language (more than half of which was [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)) (down from 0.5% at the 2007 census).[^cite_note-languages-66]

At the same census, 95.2% of people whose age was 15 or older reported that they could speak, read and write French (up from 94.7% at the 2007 census), whereas only 1.3% reported that they had no knowledge of French (down from 2.0% at the 2007 census).[^cite_note-languages-66] 86.5% of people whose age was 15 or older reported that they had some form of knowledge of at least one Polynesian language (up from 86.4% at the 2007 census but down from 87.8% at the 2012 census), whereas 13.5% reported that they had no knowledge of any of the Polynesian languages (down from 13.6% at the 2007 census but up from 12.2% at the 2012 census).[^cite_note-languages-66]

### Music

Main article: [Music of French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_French_Polynesia)

French Polynesia appeared in the [world music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_music) scene in 1992, recorded by French musicologist [Pascal Nabet-Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Nabet-Meyer?action=edit\&redlink=1) with the release of [The Tahitian Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tahitian_Choir)'s recordings of unaccompanied vocal Christian music called [himene tārava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himene_tarava).[^cite_note-67] This form of singing is common in French Polynesia and the Cook Islands, and is notable for a unique drop in pitch at the end of the phrases, a characteristic formed by several different voices, accompanied by a steady grunting of staccato, [nonlexical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-lexical_vocables_in_music) syllables.[^cite_note-68]

### Dance

Tahitian dance as a movement art evolved alongside Tahitian oral transmission of cultural knowledge.[^cite_note-69] In fact, dance movement or gesture has significance that supported the transmission of cultural knowledge.[^cite_note-70] Dance styles include ['Aparima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Aparima) and ['upa'upa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'upa'upa).

However, after the [London Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Missionary_Society) brought their religion to the islands, they pressured King [Pōmare II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%8Dmare_II) (whom they had converted from traditional beliefs to their [Reformed tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_tradition)) to introduce a new legal code.[^cite_note-71] This code, now known as the Pōmare Code, came into effect in 1819[^cite_note-72] and banned numerous traditional practices including dancing, chants, floral costumes, tattooing and more.[^cite_note-73]

### Religion



Cemetery in the [Tuāmotu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Tuamotu-Gambier)

[Christianity](5211.md) is the main religion of the islands. A majority of 54% belongs to various [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) churches, especially the [Maohi Protestant Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maohi_Protestant_Church), which is the largest and accounts for more than 50% of the population.[^cite_note-tahitipresse-74] It traces its origins to [Pōmare II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%8Dmare_II), the king of Tahiti, who converted from traditional beliefs to the Reformed tradition brought to the islands by the London Missionary Society.

[Catholics](606848.md) constitute a large minority of 38.3%\[^cite_note-[www.catholic-hierarchy.org-75](http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org-75/)] of the population (2019)\[^cite_note-[www.catholic-hierarchy.org-75](http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org-75/)] which has its own [ecclesiastical province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province), comprising the Metropolitan [Archdiocese of Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Papeete) and its only [suffragan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan), the [Diocese of Taiohae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Taiohae).[^cite_note-76] The number and proportion of Catholics has increased significantly since 1950, when they represented 21.6% of the total population.\[^cite_note-[www.catholic-hierarchy.org-75](http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org-75/)]

Data from 1991 revealed that Catholics were in the majority in the [Tuamotu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotu_Islands),[^cite_note-Saura-1991-77] [Gambier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambier_Islands)[^cite_note-Saura-1991-77] and the [Marquesas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesas_Islands),[^cite_note-Saura-1991-77] while Protestants formed the majority in the [Austral Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austral_Islands)[^cite_note-Saura-1991-77] and several of the [Society Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_Islands) such as Tahiti.[^cite_note-Saura-1991-77] This diversity is due to the fact that Protestant missionaries (from England and the United States) first came to one group of islands, and after French colonisation the Catholic Church spread to several more scattered islands, but also to the main island of Tahiti.[^cite_note-Saura-1991-77]

[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_in_French_Polynesia) had 30,350 members as of 2025.[^cite_note-78] [Community of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Christ), another denomination within the [Latter-Day Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) tradition, claimed 9,256 total French Polynesian members as of 2018[^cite_note-79] including Mareva Arnaud Tchong who serves in the church's governing [Council of Twelve Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Twelve_Apostles_\(Community_of_Christ\)). There were about 3,000 [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) in Tahiti as of 2014,[^cite_note-80] and an estimated 500 Muslims in French Polynesia.[^cite_note-81]

### Cuisine

French Polynesia produces a significant array of fruits and vegetables as natural local produce (especially coconut), which feature in many of the dishes of the islands, as does fresh seafood. Foods like [faraoa 'ipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraoa_'ipo), [poisson cru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_cru) and [rēti'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%93ti'a) are commonly eaten. The islands of Tahiti and the Marquesas indulge in a unique food made by preserving [breadfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadfruit), known as [*ma*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fermented_foods#Fermented_fruits_and_vegetables), which either can be baked further in the [earth oven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_oven), or consumed directly mixed with fresh breadfruit as *popoi mei* (as is in the Marquesas).[^cite_note-82]

### Sports



Va'a (traditional Polynesian outrigger canoe) during the Hawaiki Nui Va'a race

#### Football

Further information: [Football in Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Tahiti)

The [sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport) of [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)) in the island of [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti) is run by the [Fédération Tahitienne de Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Tahitienne_de_Football).

#### Va'a

The Polynesian traditional sport [va'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Va'a) is practiced in all the islands.[^cite_note-83] French Polynesia hosts the [Hawaiki nui va'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiki_nui_va'a?action=edit\&redlink=1) an international race between Tahiti, Huahine and Bora Bora.

#### Surfing

French Polynesia is famous for its [reef break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_break) waves. [Teahupo'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teahupo'o) is probably the most renowned, regularly ranked in the best waves of the world.[^cite_note-84] This site hosts the annual [Billabong Pro Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billabong_Pro_Teahupoo) surf competition, the 7th stop of the [World Championship Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_championship_tour_\(WCT\)_surfing),[^cite_note-85] and hosted the surfing events of the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-86]

#### Kitesurfing

There are many spots to practice kitesurfing in French Polynesia, with Tahiti, Moorea, Bora-Bora, Maupiti and Raivavae being among the most iconic.[^cite_note-87]



Fakarava atoll, south pass

#### Diving

French Polynesia is internationally known for diving. Each archipelago offers opportunities for divers. [Rangiroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangiroa) and [Fakarava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakarava) in the [Tuamotu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotus) islands are the most famous spots in the area.[^cite_note-88]

#### Rugby

Main article: [Rugby union in French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_French_Polynesia)

Rugby is also popular in French Polynesia, specifically rugby union.[^cite_note-89]

### Television

Television channels with local programming include [Polynésie la 1ère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyn%C3%A9sie_la_1%C3%A8re) (established in 1965) and [Tahiti Nui Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti_Nui_Television) (established in 2000). Channels from metropolitan France are also available.

Tourism is an important source of income for French Polynesia.

The legal tender of French Polynesia is the [CFP franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFP_franc) which has a fixed exchange rate with the [euro](9472.md). The nominal [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of French Polynesia in 2019 was 6.02 billion [U.S. dollars](18717338.md) at market exchange rates, the seventh-largest economy in Oceania after [Australia](4689264.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [Hawaii](13270.md), [Papua New Guinea](22943.md), [New Caledonia](21342.md), and [Guam](11974.md).[^cite_note-GDP-8] The GDP per capita was US\$21,673 in 2019 (at market exchange rates, not at [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity)), lower than in Hawaii, Australia, New Zealand, Guam, New Caledonia, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the Cook Islands, but higher than in all other independent insular states and dependent territories of [Oceania](22621.md).[^cite_note-GDP-8]

French Polynesia was severely affected by the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) and subsequent [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), and experienced as a result 4 years of [recession](25382.md) from 2009 to 2012. French Polynesia renewed with economic growth in 2013, and experienced strong economic growth in the 2nd half of the 2010s, with an average [real GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_gross_domestic_product) growth rate of +2.8% per year from 2016 to 2019, before being affected by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in 2020, which has led to another recession.[^cite_note-96]

French Polynesia has a moderately developed economy, which is dependent on imported goods, tourism, and the financial assistance of mainland France. Tourist facilities are well developed and are available on the major islands. Main agricultural productions are [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) ([copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra)), vegetables and fruits. French Polynesia exports [noni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noni) juice, a high quality [vanilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_production_in_French_Polynesia), and the famous [black Tahitian pearls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_pearl)[^cite_note-Taaroa_Bijoux-97] which accounted for 55% of exports (in value) in 2008.[^cite_note-FT-98]

French Polynesia's seafloor contains rich deposits of [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel), [cobalt](24580536.md), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), and [copper](828834.md) that are not exploited.[^cite_note-99]

In 2008, French Polynesia's imports amounted to 2.2 billion U.S. dollars and exports amounted to 0.2 billion U.S. dollars.[^cite_note-FT-98]

### Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_French_Polynesia)

There are 53 airports in French Polynesia; 46 are paved.[^cite_note-cia-24] [Fa'a'ā International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fa'a'%C4%81_International_Airport) is the only international airport in French Polynesia. Each island has its own airport that serves flights to other islands. [Air Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Tahiti) is the main airline that flies around the islands.

### Communication

In 2017, Alcatel Submarine Networks, a unit of [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia), launched a project to connect many of the islands in French Polynesia with underwater fiber optic cable. The project, called NATITUA, is intended to improve French Polynesian broadband connectivity by linking Tahiti to 10 islands in the Tuamotu and Marquesas archipelagos.[^cite_note-100] In August 2018, a celebration was held to commemorate the arrival of a submarine cable from [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete) to the atoll of Hao, extending the network by about 1000 kilometres.[^cite_note-101]
