**Najjariyeh** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): النجارية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *an-Najjāriyah*) also known as Al-Zahrani in the Iqlim Al-Shoumar area of the greater [Jabal Amil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amil) region of [Lebanon](17771.md), is a municipality in the [Sidon District](11166784.md) of the [South Governorate](1283570.md). The [Zahrani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahrani_River) reaches the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) at Najjariyah and forms the boundary with the town of [Ghazieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazieh) to the north. The village extends inland from the mouth of the Zahrani River to the Hajjeh Valley. It borders the municipalities and villages of [Al-Ghaziyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghaziyah), [Al-Aaddoussiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aaddoussiyah), [Al-Marwaniyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Marwaniyah), [Al-Maamriyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maamriyah), and [Hajjeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjeh,_Lebanon). The village administers three [hamlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)): [Msayleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msayleh), Kherbet Basal and Brak Ez-Zahrani. What is also strategic about the municipality that it is located between three major cities which are, [Sidon](28366.md), [Nabatieh](43794798.md) and [Tyre](30796.md). The village contains The Coastal Highway that connects the two cities of [Sidon](28366.md) and [Tyre](30796.md), and also contains a major road connecting the cities of [Nabatieh](43794798.md) and [Sidon](28366.md). The Municipality also includes the Zahrani Bridge that connects the Zahrani Region and The [Sidon District](11166784.md) together.&#x20;

The village's population is predominantly [Shiite Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiite_Muslim), while most [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslims) live in a separate neighborhood known as Hayy al-Arab, meaning "Arab Quarter". Most Sunni Muslims in Najjariyeh are Lebanese Bedouin Arabs, some of whom live in the Msayleh area of the municipality. The municipality has two mosques, one Sunni and one Shiite, with the Shiite mosque regarded as the main mosque.[^cite_note-1] The municipality's main [husayniyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayniyya), a building used for Shia religious practice, is the Husayniyya of Ahlul Bayt Alayhuma al-Salam, which is also a stronghold of the [Amal movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_movement) and the Islamic Risala Scout Association.

The municipality includes the Hajjeh Valley, where a cultural complex belonging to [Nabih Berri](592775.md) is located. Najjariyeh also has a monument in the Zahrani area near Al-Ghaziyah, by the Zahrani River, erected by the Amal movement in memory of Bilal Fahes, one of its members, who carried out a suicide bomb attack against an Israeli convoy there in 1987.

## History

In 1875, in the late [Ottoman](22278.md) era, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) described it: "Nedjarieh \[...] is a fairly considerable farm, worked by [Maronites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite), [Metawilehs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metawileh), and "schismatic Greeks", and which has replaced an ancient agricultural establishment, as is proved by [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) and presses hewn in the rock. Fig, olive and mulberry trees surround it."[^cite_note-2]

Known by the residents of the village, It was known that Islamic prophet [Khidr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khidr) has passed and stayed in the area, which is modern day Najjariyah. A [shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine) was built onto the place he stayed in. It was also historically known that during the exile of [Abu Dharr al-Ghifari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dharr_al-Ghifari) to [Jabal Amil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amil) or modern day [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md) due to the disagreements with the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate). He has passed by and helped spread [Shia Islam](26961.md) within the area Najjariyeh was in. Historical residents then followed the teaching of [Abu Dharr al-Ghifari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dharr_al-Ghifari).[^cite_note-3]

## Involvement in Hezbollah-Israel conflict

[Hezbollah](13919.md) is known for its control and activity within the Village, which lead to the bombing of [Hezbollah](13919.md) strongholds in Najjariyeh on may 17th, 2024. This attack was targeted towards [Hezbollah](13919.md) senior, “Hussain Khodor Mahdi”, which lead to his death in the attack with two other [Syrians](8862873.md). Another [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel) attack also occurred on the hamlet of [Msayleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Msayleh) belonging to Najjariyeh on October 11th, 2025. It was an attack on heavy machinery and vehicles, [Israel](9282173.md) claiming that the machinery all belonged to [Hezbollah](13919.md), though that many [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) sources rebuke the claim, saying it was an unlawful attack and a violation of human rights.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

On 31 March 2026, during the [2026 Lebanon War](82549840.md), Israeli warplanes targeted a building in the village housing a family of four two brothers and two sisters, all of whom were killed in the strike. The primary target of the attack was senior [Hezbollah](13919.md) leader “Abbas Khalil Hallal”, who was later pronounced dead in the aftermath of the strike.

## Families

Most families in Najjariyeh consist of Twelver [Shiite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims). They all live together closely in the hillside of the village. The Major families named are: Tarraf - Hussain - Othman - Hallal - Kawtharani - Kachakech - Mahdi - Mjallal - Abbas - Nasser - Jaber - Issa - Mahmoud - Diab - Zarnaji - Al-Ali - Kassem - Najjar - Ayyad - Khalil - Khalife - Mkhayber - Atwi - Younes - Hijazi - Ramadan - Asaad.[^cite_note-6]

## Demographics

In 2014, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) made up 95.61% of registered voters in Najjariyeh. 83.05% of the voters were [Shiite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims) and 12.56% were [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Sunni_Muslims).[^cite_note-7]
