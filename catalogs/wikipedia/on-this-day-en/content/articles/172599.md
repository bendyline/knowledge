# Search and rescue

For other uses, see [Search and rescue (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_and_rescue_\(disambiguation\)).

"S\&R" redirects here. For the membership-only retail warehouse club chain, see [S\&R Membership Shopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&R_Membership_Shopping).

**Search and rescue** (**SAR**) is the search for and provision of aid to people who are in distress or imminent danger. The general field of search and rescue includes many specialty sub-fields, typically determined by the type of terrain the search is conducted over. These include [mountain rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_rescue); ground search and rescue, including the use of [search and rescue dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_and_rescue_dog) (such as [K9 units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_dog)); [urban search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_search_and_rescue) in cities; [combat search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_search_and_rescue) on the battlefield and [air-sea rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-sea_rescue) over water.

The [International Search and Rescue Advisory Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Search_and_Rescue_Advisory_Group) (INSARAG) is a UN organisation that promotes the exchange of information between national urban search and rescue organisations. The [duty to render assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty_to_render_assistance) is covered by Article 98 of the [UNCLOS](31940.md).

## Definitions

There are many different definitions of search and rescue, depending on the agency involved and country in question.

- [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md) and [Canadian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard): "Search and Rescue comprises the search for, and provision of aid to, persons, ships or other craft which are, or are feared to be, in distress or imminent danger."

  [^cite_note-CF-1]

  [^cite_note-2]

- [United States Coast Guard](32223.md): "The use of available resources to assist persons or property in potential or actual distress."

  [^cite_note-3]

- [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md): A search is "an operation normally coordinated by a Rescue Coordination Center (RCC) or rescue sub-center, using available personnel and facilities to locate persons in distress" and rescue is "an operation to retrieve persons in distress, provide for their initial medical or other needs, and deliver them to a place of safety".

  [^cite_note-DoD-4]

## History

One of the world's earliest well-documented SAR efforts ensued following the 1656 wreck of the [Dutch](21148.md) merchant ship *[Vergulde Draeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vergulde_Draeck)* off the west coast of [Australia](4689264.md). Survivors sought help, and in response three separate SAR missions were conducted, without success.[^cite_note-5]

On 29 November 1945, a Sikorsky R-5 performed the first civilian helicopter rescue operation in history, with Sikorsky's chief pilot Dmitry "Jimmy" Viner in the cockpit, using an experimental hoist developed jointly by [Sikorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Aircraft) and [Breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeze-Eastern). All five crew members of an oil barge, which had run aground on [Penfield Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penfield_Reef), were saved before the barge sank.[^cite_note-6]

In 1983, [Korean Air Lines Flight 007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_007) with 269 occupants was shot down by a Soviet aircraft near [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin_Island). The Soviets sent SAR helicopters and boats to Soviet waters, while a search and rescue operation was initiated by U.S., South Korean, and Japanese ships and aircraft in international waters, but no survivors were found.[^cite_note-7]

In July 2009, [Air France Flight 447](23042123.md) was lost in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean. An international SAR effort was launched, to no avail. A third effort nearly two years later discovered the crash site and recovered the [flight recorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_recorder).

In early 2014, [Malaysia Airlines Flight 370](42142305.md) crashed under mysterious circumstances. Many nations contributed to the initial SAR effort, which was fruitless. In June 2014, the [Australian Transport Safety Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Transport_Safety_Bureau) commissioned the MV *[Fugro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugro) Equator* to lead a three-month survey of the ocean bed. As of 2014, the search for Flight 370 had become the largest SAR to date.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Types of search and rescue

### Ground (lowland) search and rescue

Main article: [Missing person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missing_person)

Ground search and rescue is the search for persons who are lost or in distress on land or inland waterways. People may go missing for a variety of reasons. Some may disappear voluntarily, due to issues like [domestic abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_abuse). Others disappear for involuntary reasons such as mental illness, getting lost, an accident, death in a location where they cannot be found or, less commonly, due to [abduction](169376.md). Ground search and rescue missions that occur in urban areas should not be confused with "urban search and rescue", which in many jurisdictions refers to the location and extraction of people from collapsed buildings or other entrapments.[^cite_note-FEMAUSR-10]

In some countries, the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) are the primary agency for carrying out searches for a missing person on land. Some places have voluntary search and rescue teams that can be called out to assist these searches.

Search and rescue agencies may contain small specialist teams for executing operations where there are specific environmental risks. Examples include swift water rescue, flood response, technical rope rescue, confined space rescue, over-snow rescue, and thin ice rescue.

### Mountain rescue

Main article: [Mountain rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_rescue)

Mountain rescue relates to search and rescue operations specifically in rugged and mountainous terrain.

### Cave rescue

Main article: [Cave rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_rescue)

Cave rescue is a highly specialised form of rescue for rescuing injured, trapped or lost [cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) explorers.

### Urban search and rescue

Main article: [Urban search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_search_and_rescue)

Urban search and rescue (US\&R or USAR), also referred to as Heavy Urban Search and Rescue (HUSAR), is the location and rescue of persons from collapsed buildings or other urban and industrial entrapments. Due to the specialised nature of the work, most teams are multi-disciplinary and include personnel from police, fire and emergency medical services. Unlike traditional ground search and rescue workers, most US\&R responders also have basic training in structural collapse and the dangers associated with live electrical wires, broken natural gas lines and other hazards. While [earthquakes](10106.md) have traditionally been the cause of US\&R operations, [terrorist attacks](30636.md) and extreme weather such as [tornadoes](37530.md) and [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane) have also resulted in the deployment of these resources.[^cite_note-FEMAAboutUSR-11]

### Combat search and rescue

Main article: [Combat search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_search_and_rescue)

Combat search and rescue (CSAR) is search and rescue operations that are carried out during war that are within or near combat zones.[^cite_note-ArmyTech-12]

### Maritime search and rescue

See also: [Air-sea rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-sea_rescue) and [Maritime safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_safety)



A [US Navy](20518076.md) rescue swimmer swims toward a stranded boat near [Guam](11974.md) in 2019

Maritime search and rescue is carried out at sea to save sailors and passengers in distress, or the survivors of downed aircraft. The type of agency which carries out maritime search and rescue varies by country; it may variously be the [coast guard](146790.md), [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) or voluntary organisations. When a distressed or missing vessel is located, these organisations deploy helicopters, rescue vessels or any other appropriate vessel to return them to land. In some cases, the agencies may carry out an [air-sea rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-sea_rescue) (ASR). This refers to the combined use of aircraft (such as [flying boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boat), [floatplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floatplane), [amphibious helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_helicopter) and non-amphibious helicopters equipped with hoists) and surface vessels.[^cite_note-Algeo-13] Another type of Maritime search and rescue is [Submarine rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_rescue). The [International Convention on Maritime Search and Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_Maritime_Search_and_Rescue) (SAR Convention) is the legal framework that applies to international maritime and air-sea rescue.[^cite_note-UNHCR-14]

## By nation

### Australia

National

The Australian search and rescue service is provided by three authorities; the Joint Rescue Coordination Centre (JRCC) at the [Australian Maritime Safety Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Maritime_Safety_Authority) (AMSA), the Australian Defence Force (ADF) and the State/Territory Police Jurisdictions. In a very broad sense, the JRCC respond to national and international registered aircraft, off shore marine incidents and beacon activations. The ADF are responsible for Australian and foreign military personnel, vehicles, vessels and aircraft while within the Australian SRR. Police are responsible for coastal marine incidents, lost persons, unregistered aircraft, inland waterways, ports and identified beacons.[^cite_note-AusSAR-15] The JRCC operates a 24-hour Rescue Coordination Centre (RCC) in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) and is responsible for the national coordination of both maritime and aviation search and rescue. The JRCC is also responsible for the management and operation of the Australian [ground segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_segment) of the [Cospas-Sarsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cospas-Sarsat) distress beacon detection system. The JRCC's jurisdiction spans Australia and as well as covering 52.8 million square kilometres of the [Indian](14580.md), [Pacific](23070.md) and [Southern Oceans](20611428.md)[^cite_note-AusSAR-15] constituting about 11% of the Earth's surface.[^cite_note-16]

The JRCC is staffed by SAR specialists who have a naval, [merchant marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_marine), air force, [civil aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation) or police service background. The JRCC also coordinates medical evacuations, broadcasts maritime safety information and operates the Australian Ship Reporting System (AUSREP).[^cite_note-AusSAR-15] In coordinating search and rescue missions, the JRCC will call on assistance from organisations as appropriate,[^cite_note-17] such as the Defence forces, [Border Protection Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Protection_Command), trained aviation organisations (Civil SAR Units), emergency medical helicopters, state Police services and trained Air Observers from the [State Emergency Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Emergency_Service).[^cite_note-SA-SES_AirObs-18] There are also other organisations, such as the non-profit [Westpac Life Saver Rescue Helicopter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westpac_Life_Saver_Rescue_Helicopter_Service) that is based at a number of sites around Australia and contracted by various authorities to deliver search and rescue services.

State



[BSAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_Search_and_Rescue_Victoria) searchers at [Mount Dom Dom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Dom_Dom).

[State Police](964240.md) in many states operate state-based search and rescue squads, such as the [Victoria Police Search and Rescue Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Police_Search_and_Rescue_Squad), which provides specialist expertise, advice and practical assistance in land search and rescue on most terrain including snow and vertical cliff search and rescue.[^cite_note-VictoriaSAR-19] There are also state-based volunteer search and rescue groups such as the [NSW SES Bush Search and Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSW_SES_Bush_Search_and_Rescue) in [New South Wales](21654.md) and [Bush Search and Rescue Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_Search_and_Rescue_Victoria)[^cite_note-BSARAus-20] in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)). These state-based groups draw searchers from bushwalking, mountaineering and specialist rescue clubs within their State. A few groups respond on horseback as [mounted search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mounted_search_and_rescue). The [State Emergency Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Emergency_Service) is a collection of volunteer-based emergency organisations established in each state or territory which are responsible for many rescue efforts in urban and rural areas and in any rescue that results from flood or storm activity. In rural areas the SES conducts most bush search, vertical and road traffic rescues. In urban areas they assist the police and fire services with USAR.[^cite_note-SESabout-21]

Air and marine search and rescue (SAR) in Canada is delivered through the National Search and Rescue Program, a federal framework involving several government departments and volunteer organizations. Aeronautical and maritime SAR operations are coordinated by [Joint Rescue Coordination Centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Rescue_Coordination_Centre_\(disambiguation\)) (JRCCs) located in Halifax, Trenton, and Victoria.

Primary responsibility for maritime SAR lies with the [Canadian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard), while the [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md) provide aircraft, crews, and specialized rescue personnel. The Department of National Defence has overall responsibility for the coordinated SAR system.[^cite_note-CF-1]

Volunteer marine search and rescue services are provided by units of the [Canadian Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary), a nationwide network of community-based organizations that assist the Canadian Coast Guard in responding to maritime incidents. The Auxiliary includes more than 4,000 volunteers operating over 1,000 vessels across Canada's coastal and inland waters. Canada records roughly 7,000 marine and humanitarian search and rescue incidents annually, and Auxiliary units respond to approximately one quarter of these calls while working alongside federal and local emergency services.[^cite_note-25]

Ground and inland water search and rescue (GSAR) is the responsibility of provinces and territories, with the [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) (RCMP) and other police forces coordinating operations, often using volunteer GSAR teams operating under provincial coordinating bodies.[^cite_note-RCMPSAR-26]

The Canada Shipping Act, most recently passed in 2001, is the framework document that funds international SAR activities.[^cite_note-27]

### Cyprus



A [Cyprus Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Air_Forces) [AW139](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW139) SAR helicopter during a search and rescue demonstration

The [Cyprus Republic](5593.md) Search and Rescue (SAR) system is organised by the [Cyprus Joint Rescue Coordination Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Joint_Rescue_Coordination_Center) (JRCC Larnaca).

The JRCC (Greek: Κέντρο Συντονισμού Έρευνας και Διάσωσης) is an independent agency of the Ministry of Defence of the Republic of Cyprus that started its operations on a 24h basis on 7 August 1995 as a unit of the [Cyprus Air Force Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Air_Forces).[^cite_note-cjrcc06-55]

On 1 March 2002, the JRCC took full responsibility for investigating, organising, coordinating and executing every SAR incident-operation in the Republic of Cyprus Search & Rescue Region (SRR).[^cite_note-cjrcc06-55] JRCC Larnaca operated as a military unit until 26 July 2010, when JRCC was transformed to an independent agency under the Ministry of Defence with the Minister being responsible for its operational aspects. Logistic and technical support is the responsibility of the Ministry of Communications & Works.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-56] Its primary mission is to organise the Cyprus Republic Search And Rescue system, to co-ordinate, control and direct SAR operations in its area of responsibility (which is identical to the Nicosia [FIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_region)), in order to find and rescue people whose lives are at risk, as a result of an air or naval accident, in the least possible time.[^cite_note-57] This is achieved by coordinating all the different agencies involved such as the Cyprus Police Aviation Unit, the [Cyprus Port and Naval Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Port_and_Marine_Police), the [Cyprus National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_National_Guard) [Naval Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Navy), the [Cyprus National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_National_Guard) [Air Force Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Air_Forces), the [Cyprus Civil Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Civil_Defence) and other secondary units.[^cite_note-58]

The JRCC reports directly to the operational control of the Ministry of Defence and it is staffed by qualified personnel of the [Cyprus National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_National_Guard), mainly from the branches of the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Navy) and the [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Air_Forces).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-56]

### Denmark



[Royal Danish Air Force](548819.md) [S-61A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH-3_Sea_King) with its rescue swimmer

Search and rescue operators in [Denmark](76972.md) are primarily: [Danish air force](548819.md) Squadron 722, [Danish navy air squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Navy_Air_Squadron), naval home guard and the [Danish Maritime Safety Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Maritime_Safety_Administration), coordinated by the Joint Rescue Coordination Centre, operated by the navy and air force in the [Danish Naval Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Danish_Fleet) facilities near [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus). Internationally the Danish works mainly with [Germany](11867.md), [Norway](21241.md) and [Sweden](5058739.md). With the two latter, the annual exercises *Baltic SAREX*[^cite_note-SAREX07-65] and *Scan-SAR*[^cite_note-SCANSAR06-66] are conducted.

SAR services in Denmark started in 1957 with seven [Sikorsky S-55s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_H-19). Their [piston engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_engine) produced only 550 hp (410 kW) and they had limited fuel capacity, so their operational range was short. To increase the operational area, [Pembroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percival_Pembroke) twin-engined fixed-wing aircraft were employed for search. These aircraft would localise the distressed person(s) and the S-55s would then rescue them. The SAR service was started for respond to fighter-plane crashes as 79 aircraft crashed, with 62 dead, in the period 1950–1955.,[^cite_note-DAFHIS-67] but civilian SAR duties are also conducted.

In 1962, eight ship-based [Aérospatiale Alouette IIIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_III) were received. These were primarily meant for the ships patrolling the North Atlantic, but also supported the S-55s. In 1964–1965 the seven S-55s were replaced with eight Sikorsky [S-61A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH-3_Sea_King) helicopters.[^cite_note-DAFS61-68]

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Rescue helicopters in Denmark](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category\:Rescue%20helicopters%20in%20Denmark).

In 2007, the [Danish Defence](8038.md) held a public display in [Horsens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsens), to raise awareness about rescue services and maritime safety. Maritime SAR is important because Denmark has a relative long coast line to its land mass.[^cite_note-DAPD1-69][^cite_note-DAPD2-70]

In 2008, the SAR forces in Denmark were equipped with eight EH-101, one or two Lynx, 34 naval home guard vessels and 21 rescue vessels,[^cite_note-DAPD3-71] as well as the naval vessels at sea. The EH-101s operate from bases in [Aalborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_Airport), [Skrydstrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skrydstrup?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Roskilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_Airport). When the sea water temperatures are low a helicopter is also deployed to the island of [Bornholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornholm_Airport) in the [Baltic Sea](3335.md). The Lynx operates from [Karup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karup_Airport). Maritime vessels are spread out through the entire coastline and on islands. The S-61s and EH-101s have a crew of six: two [pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator), a [navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigator), a [flight engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_engineer), a [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) and a [rescue swimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_swimmer#Denmark).

### New Zealand

[New Zealand](4913064.md)'s Search and Rescue Region extends from the [South Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole) to the southern border of the [Honolulu](13887.md) region, including Norfolk, Tonga, Samoa, and [Cook Islands](7067.md).[^cite_note-NZAIP-122]

These teams draw together numerous specialists and organisations to achieve an integrated multi-agency response.[^cite_note-126]

Among those organisations that act in a support capacity for FENZ are Response Teams (NZRTs). These are regional rescue groups of professional volunteers that train to a minimum industry standard of USAR Category 1R (USAR Responder), which is also standard for FENZ firefighters. Their additional capabilities, which vary among different teams, include: high angle rope rescue, storm response, swift water response, medics, welfare, and rural fire support.[^cite_note-127] Many Response Teams were deployed to assist in the rescue and recovery effort of the [2011 Christchurch earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Christchurch_earthquake).

### United States

Main article: [Search and rescue in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_and_rescue_in_the_United_States)



US Coast Guard [Eurocopter HH-65 Dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_HH-65_Dolphin) SAR helicopter



US Coast Guard [Sikorsky HH-60 Jayhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_HH-60_Jayhawk) SAR helicopter

In the United States there are many organisations with SAR responsibilities at the national, state and local level. Most day-to-day SAR missions in the US are run by the County Sheriffs,[^cite_note-165] except in states like [Alaska](624.md), where the State Highway Patrol oversees SAR, or in other areas where SAR services are part of [fire/rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_department), [EMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services), or a wholly separate, [non-profit](72487.md) organisation. They in turn, can request help from other departments, as well as state and national resources if they think they need them. A typical Sheriff's Office has a volunteer SAR team that matches the terrain and population of that county. SAR members are typically trained in the Incident Command System (ICS),[^cite_note-166] first aid, and the outdoor skills needed in that terrain and climate. Most of this article is about the federal response to assist large complicated SAR missions.

In January 2008, the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) released the [National Response Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Response_Framework) which serves as the guiding document for a federal response during a national emergency. Search and Rescue is divided into four primary elements, while assigning a federal agency with the lead role for each of the four elements.[^cite_note-167]

- Structural Collapse-USAR: [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) [Federal Emergency Management Agency](73797.md)

- Waterborne: [United States Coast Guard](32223.md), [United States Coast Guard Auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary)

- Inland-wilderness: [United States Department of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Interior), [National Park Service](161535.md)

- Aeronautical: [United States Air Force](32090.md) via the [Air Force Rescue Coordination Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Rescue_Coordination_Center) and USAF rescue wings, groups and squadrons in the [Air Combat Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Combat_Command), [Pacific Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Air_Forces) (for Alaska and Hawaii), [Air Education and Training Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Education_and_Training_Command), [Air Force Reserve Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Reserve_Command) and the [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard); the [Civil Air Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Air_Patrol) in its role as the USAF Auxiliary; and the [United States Navy](20518076.md) and [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md), both Active and Reserve (secondary missions for land-based USN maritime patrol and reconnaissance squadrons and land-based and sea-based USN/USMC helicopter squadrons)

SAR standards adopted by agencies having jurisdiction are developed primarily by non-governmental organisations, including [ASTM International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASTM_International) and [National Fire Protection Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fire_Protection_Association). These standards are adopted also by training and certification organisations such as [Mountain Rescue Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Rescue_Association) and [National Association for Search and Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_Search_and_Rescue) to develop training that will meet or exceed those standards.[^cite_note-ASTM-168] Within ASTM International, standards specific to SAR are developed by Technical Committee F32 on Search and Rescue. Formed in 1988, the committee had 85 current members and jurisdiction of 38 approved standards.[^cite_note-ASTM-168]
