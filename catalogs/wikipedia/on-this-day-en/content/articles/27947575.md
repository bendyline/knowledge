# Canal del Dique



&#x20;Sunset in the Levee Channel in [Mahates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahates) town

The **Canal del Dique** ([English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language): Levee Channel) is a 118 km [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) that connects [Cartagena Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartagena_Bay?action=edit\&redlink=1) (at the [corregimiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corregimiento) of [Pasacaballos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasacaballos?action=edit\&redlink=1)) to the [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River) in the [Bolívar Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar_Department) in northern [Colombia](5222.md). The canal is a bifurcation or artificial arm of the Magdalena River, and its eastern portion forms most of the border between the departments of Bolívar and [Atlántico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A1ntico_Department). Its port on the Magdalena River is [Calamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calamar,_Bol%C3%ADvar).

## History

The canal was built by the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in 1582 to bypass the shoals and sandbars of the Magdalena River [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) and thereby connect the region's main colonial ports ([Cartagena](63927.md) and [Santa Marta](547214.md)) to the interior of what is today Colombia.[^cite_note-EL-1] It quickly fell into disrepair, and was rebuilt in 1650.[^cite_note-GB-2]

However, by the end of the 18th century, it had become impassable except during times of high runoff, and by 1821 it was completely blocked.[^cite_note-GB-2] Trade moved increasingly away from Cartagena to Santa Marta and Sabanilla[^cite_note-GB-2], a port near the mouth of the Magdalena, later eclipsed by [Puerto Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Colombia) and [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla). By 1831, traders in the city began to lobby for the canal's reopening, but repeated efforts to redredge the channel failed.[^cite_note-GB-2] In the mid-19th century, U.S. engineer [George M. Totten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Totten) helped rebuild part of the canal.[^cite_note-3] By the end of the 19th century, a railroad had largely replaced it.

In 1923 and 1952, the canal was improved, but use then began to decline due to increased sedimentation of the Magdalena River.[^cite_note-4] In 1972 an extensive study was made of the morphology of the Rio Magdalena and the Canal del Dique.[^cite_note-5]

As of 2009, a modernization of the channel was being considered to boost trade in the port of Cartagena.[^cite_note-6]

Since 2013, [river diversions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentation_enhancing_strategies) have been planned with Dutch company [Royal Haskoning DHV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_HaskoningDHV) to control [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) and [water flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)) along the canal.[^cite_note-7] This has led to creation of a new [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) [wetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) area, land building and [ecological restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_ecology) in the region.

### In the literature

The canal figures prominently in [Gabriel García Márquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Garc%C3%ADa_M%C3%A1rquez)'s novel *[Love in the Time of Cholera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_in_the_Time_of_Cholera)*.
