**Lombardy**[^cite_note-pron-9] ([Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) and [Italian](14708.md): *Lombardia*;[^cite_note-orth-1][^cite_note-13] [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *Lumbardia*) is an administrative [region of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) that covers 23,844 km<sup>2</sup> (9,206 sq mi);[^cite_note-area-3] it is located in [northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) and with a population of about 10 million is the most populous region, constituting more than one-sixth of Italy's population.[^cite_note-population-4] Lombardy is located between the [Alps](981.md) mountain range and tributaries of the river [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)), and includes [Milan](36511.md), its capital and largest city, [whose metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_metropolitan_area) is the largest in the country and among the [largest in the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Europe).[^cite_note-14]

Its territory is divided into 1,501 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities) (the region with the largest number of municipalities in the entire national territory),[^cite_note-comuni-15] distributed among 12 administrative subdivisions (11 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Italy) plus the [Metropolitan City of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan)). The region ranks first in Italy in terms of population, population density, and number of municipalities, while it is fourth in terms of surface area, after [Sicily](27619.md), [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont), and [Sardinia](29376.md).[^cite_note-tuttitalia-16]

It is the second-most populous [region of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_\(Europe\)) (EU),[^cite_note-pop-17] and the [second region of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Union_regions_by_GDP) by nominal GDP.[^cite_note-GDP-18] Lombardy is the leading[^cite_note-first-19] region of Italy in terms of economic importance,[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-20] contributing to approximately one-fifth of the national [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] It is also a member of the [Four Motors for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Motors_for_Europe), an international economic organization whose other members are [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) in Germany, [Catalonia](6822.md) in Spain, and [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](45093325.md) in France. Milan is the economic capital of Italy[^cite_note-prologis-23] and a global centre for business, fashion, and finance.

Of the 58 [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](44940.md) in Italy, 11 are in Lombardy, tying it with [Castile and León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n) in northwest-central Spain.[^cite_note-24]

## Etymology

See also: [Lombardy (historical region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy_\(historical_region\))



Map of [Historical Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy_\(historical_region\))

The [name *Lombardy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy_\(historical_region\)) comes from *Lombard*, which is derived from [Late Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Latin) *_Longobardus, Langobardus_* ("a Lombard"), which derived from the [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic) elements \**langaz* + \**bardaz*; equivalent to *long beard*. According to some scholars, the second element derives from [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) *\*bardǭ*, *\*barduz* ("axe"), related to German *_Barte_*.[^cite_note-Partridge_2009-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

The name of the region derives from the name of the people of the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) who arrived in Italy in 568 and made [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) their capital. During the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages), "Lombardy" referred to the [Kingdom of the Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards) ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Regnum Langobardorum*). It was ruled by the Germanic Lombard raiders, who controlled most of early Christian Italy since the [Lombard invasion of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_invasion_of_Italy) of [Byzantine Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorian_prefecture_of_Italy) in 568, until the fall of Pavia, in 774 by [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) on the Pope's behalf. As such, "Lombardy" and "Italy" were nearly interchangeable; by the mid-8th century, the Lombards ruled everywhere except the [Papal possessions around Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States)—roughly modern [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio) and northern [Umbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria)—[Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and some [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) possessions in the south—southern [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) and [Calabria](44772.md); some coastal settlements including [Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Amalfi), [Gaeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Gaeta), [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Naples) and [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Sorrento); [Sicily](27619.md) and [Sardinia](29376.md); their culture is foundational to [Italy in the Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_in_the_Middle_Ages).[^cite_note-28] The term was also used until around 965 in the form Λογγοβαρδία (*_Longobardia_*) as the name for [the territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longobardia) roughly covering modern Apulia, which the Byzantines had recovered from the Lombard [rump state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rump_state) [Duchy of Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento).

## History

### Prehistory and antiquity

Further information: [Cisalpine Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Gaul)



A couple in duel with a "symbol" in the middle depicted in the [Rock Drawings in Valcamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Drawings_in_Valcamonica). The Rock Drawings in Valcamonica are the largest collections of prehistoric [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) in the world.

[^cite_note-unesco2-29]

From archaeological findings of ceramics, arrows, axes, and carved stones, the area of current-day Lombardy has been settled at least since the second millennium BC. Well-preserved [rock drawings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Drawings_in_Valcamonica) left by ancient [Camuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camuni) in the [Valcamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valcamonica) depicting animals, people, and symbols were made over 8,000 years before the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age),[^cite_note-adorno-30] based on about 300,000 records.[^cite_note-archeointro-31]

The many artefacts found in a [necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necropolis) near [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) and the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) demonstrate the presence of the [Golasecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golasecca) [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) culture that prospered in western Lombardy between the ninth and the 4th centuries BC. In the following centuries, Lombardy was inhabited by different peoples; the [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) founded the city of [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua)[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] and spread the use of writing. It was the seat of the Celtic [Canegrate culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canegrate_culture) starting from the 13th century BC, and later of the Celtic [Golasecca culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golasecca_culture). From the 5th century BC, the area was invaded by more [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) [Gallic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls) tribes coming from north of the Alps. These people settled in several cities including Milan and extended their rule to the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md). Celtic development was halted by the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) expansion in the Po Valley from the 3rd century BC. After centuries of struggle, at the end of the 2nd century B.C.,[^cite_note-34] the entirety of modern-day Lombardy became a Roman province called [Gallia Cisalpina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallia_Cisalpina)—"[Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul) on the inner side (with respect to Rome) of the [Alps](981.md)".

The Roman culture and language overwhelmed the former civilization in the following years, and Lombardy became one of the most developed and richest areas of Italy with the construction of roads and the development of agriculture and trade. Important figures were born here, such as [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) (in [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como)) and [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil) (in Mantua). In [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity) the strategic role of Lombardy was emphasised by the move of the capital of the [Western Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) to [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum) (Milan). Here, in 313 AD, Roman Emperor [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I) issued the famous *[Edict of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Milan)*, which gave freedom of confession to all religions within the Roman Empire.

### Kingdom of the Lombards

Further information: [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) and [Kingdom of the Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards)



The [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Holy_Roman_Empire\)) within the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 962

During and after the fall of the Western Empire, Lombardy heavily suffered from destruction brought about by a series of invasions by tribal peoples. After 540, [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) became the permanent capital of the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom), the fixed site of the court and the royal treasury.[^cite_note-35] The last and most effective invasion was that of the Germanic [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) or Longobards, whose nation migrated to the region from the [Carpathian basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_basin) in fear of the conquering [Pannonian Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars) in 568. The Lombards' long-lasting reign, with its capital in [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia), gave the current name to the region. There was a close relationship between the [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria) and Lombard nobility for many centuries.

After the initial struggles, relationships between the Lombard people and the [Gallo-Roman peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Roman_culture)[^cite_note-36] improved. The Lombard language and culture was integrated with the Latin culture, leaving evidence in many names, the legal code and laws. The Lombards became intermixed with the [Roman population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_people) owing to their relatively smaller number.[^cite_note-37] The end of Lombard rule came in 774, when the Frankish king [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) [conquered Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Pavia_\(773%E2%80%93774\)), deposed [Desiderius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desiderius) the last Lombard king, and annexed the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kingdom_of_the_Lombards)—mostly northern and central present-day Italy—to his newly established [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) was crowned by the Pope on 25 December 800. The former Lombard dukes and nobles were replaced by other German vassals, prince-bishops and marquises. The entire northern part of the Italian peninsula continued to be called "Lombardy" and its population "Lombards" throughout the following centuries.

### Communes and the Empire



[San Michele Maggiore, Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Michele_Maggiore,_Pavia), where almost all the [kings of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Italy) were crowned up to [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor)

In the 10th century, Lombardy, although formally under the rule of the Holy Roman Empire, was included in the [kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Holy_Roman_Empire\)), of which [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) remained the capital until 1024.[^cite_note-38] Starting gradually in the late-11th century, Lombardy became divided into many small, autonomous city-states, the [medieval communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_communes). Also in the 11th century, the region's economy underwent a significant boom due to improved trading, sartorial manufacturing of silk and wool, and agricultural conditions; arms manufacturing for the purpose of defensive army development, by the German imperial divisions of *Guelphs* (*Welfen*) defending the Pope and *Ghibellins* (*Wibellingen*) defending the Emperor, became a significant factor. As in other areas of Italy, this led to a growing self-acknowledgement of the cities, whose increasing wealth made them able to defy the traditional feudal supreme power that was represented by the German emperors and their local legates.



Member cities of the first and second [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League)

This process peaked in the 12th and 13th centuries, when [Lombard Leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League) formed by allied cities of Lombardy, usually led by Milan, defeated the [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen) Emperor [Frederick I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa), at [Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano) but not his grandson [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) at [Battle of Cortenuova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cortenuova). Although having the military purpose as preponderant, the Lombard League also had its own stable government, considered one of the first examples of [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation) in Europe.[^cite_note-39] Subsequently, among the local city-states, a process of consolidation took place, and by the end of the 14th century, two *[signoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signoria)* emerged as rival [hegemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemon) in Lombardy; [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan) and [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Mantua).

### Renaissance duchies of Milan and Mantua

Further information: [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan) and [Duchy of Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Mantua)



The [Viscontis'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_of_Milan) dominions in the 14th century, before the foundation of the [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan)

In the 15th century, the [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan) was one of the wealthiest states during the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).[^cite_note-40] Milan and Mantua became centres of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), whose culture with people such as [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) and [Andrea Mantegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Mantegna), and works of art such as da Vinci's *[The Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_\(Leonardo\))* were highly regarded. The enterprising class of the communes extended its trade and banking activities well into northern Europe; the metonym "Lombard" designated a merchant or banker from northern Italy, for example [Lombard Street, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_Street,_London).

The name "Lombardy" came to denote the whole of northern Italy until the 15th century and sometimes later.[^cite_note-41] From the 14th century onward, the instability created by the internal and external struggles ended in the creation of noble *seigniories*, the most significant of whom were the [Viscontis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_of_Milan) (later [Sforzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Sforza)) in Milan and of the [Gonzagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Gonzaga) in Mantua. This wealth, however, attracted the now-more-organised armies of national powers such as France and Austria, which waged a lengthy battle for Lombardy in the late 15th to early 16th centuries.

### Late-Middle Ages, Renaissance and Enlightenment



The [Napoleonic Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)) in 1807, having Milan as its capital, with [Istria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istria) and [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia), shown in yellow

After the [Battle of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pavia), the Duchy of Milan became a possession of the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburgs) of Spain. The eastern part of modern-day Lombardy, including the cities [Bergamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamo) and [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia), was controlled by the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice), which had begun to extend its influence in the area from the 14th century onwards. Between the mid-15th century and the [battle of Marignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marignano) in 1515, the northern part of east Lombardy from [Airolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airolo) to [Chiasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiasso) (modern [Ticino](84147.md)), and the [Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina) valley came under possession of the [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy).

[Pestilences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1629%E2%80%931631_Italian_plague) like that of 1628–1630,[^cite_note-42] which [Alessandro Manzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni) described in his *[I promessi sposi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Betrothed)* (*The Betrothed*), and the general decline of Italy's economy in the 17th and 18th centuries halted further development of Lombardy. In 1706 the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) came to power.

Austrian rule was interrupted in the late-18th century by the French; under [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon), Lombardy became the centre of the [Cisalpine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic) and of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)), both of which were [puppet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) of France's [First Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire), with Milan as capital and Napoleon as head of state. During this period, Lombardy regained Valtellina from Switzerland.

### Modern era

Further information: [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy%E2%80%93Venetia) and [Unification of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy)



The [Five Days of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Days_of_Milan), 1848

The restoration of Austrian rule in 1815 as the [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy%E2%80%93Venetia) was characterised by a struggle with the new ideals introduced by the Napoleonic era. Lombardy was then an important centre of the *[Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento)*, with the [Five Days of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Days_of_Milan) in March 1848, the [Ten Days of Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Days_of_Brescia) in 1849, the [Belfiore martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfiore_martyrs) in Mantua in the years between 1851 and 1853. The annexation of Lombardy to the [Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Piedmont-Sardinia) occurred following the [Second Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italian_War_of_Independence) in 1859, a war during which Lombardy was the main theatre of battle (battles of [Montebello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Montebello_\(1859\)), [Palestro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Palestro), [Magenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Magenta), [Solferino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Solferino) and [San Fermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Fermo)).

In 1861, with the [proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy), Lombardy became part of the modern Italian state, except for the central-eastern part of the [province of Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua) which was [annexed in 1866](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite_of_Veneto_of_1866) after the [Third Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Italian_War_of_Independence).[^cite_note-43] Regarding the battle of Solferino, it was during this conflict that [Henry Dunant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dunant) took the initiative to create the [Red Cross](15487.md). After the annexation of Mantua, Lombardy achieved its present-day territorial shape by adding the [Oltrepò Pavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltrep%C3%B2_Pavese), formerly the southern part of the [Province of Novara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Novara), to the [Province of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pavia).

### Contemporary era



[Italian partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement) in Milan during the occupation of Italy, April 1945



Skyscrapers and large buildings in the 1960s, such as that of the [Centro Direzionale di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_Direzionale_di_Milano), iconographically represent the [Italian economic miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_economic_miracle).

The Alpine front of [World War I](4764461.md) crossed the eastern Lombardy Alpine side, and in the post-war period Milan was the centre of the [Italian Fasces of Combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasci_Italiani_di_Combattimento). Milan then became the [Gold Medal of Military Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Medal_of_Military_Valor) for the [Italian resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement)[^cite_note-44] during the [Italian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Civil_War) after its liberation from fascism during the [World War II](32927.md), while the partisan resistance spread across the valleys and provinces.

Following the historical borders, in 1948 the administrative region of Lombardy was prefigured as part of the newly formed [Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Republic). In the years of the [Italian economic miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_economic_miracle), Milan was one of the poles of the "industrial triangle" of northern Italy formed by the cities of [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin)-[Milan](36511.md)-[Genoa](47332321.md). The [Years of Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_\(Italy\)) had wide relevance in Lombardy, with the [Piazza Fontana bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Fontana_bombing) in Milan in 1969 and the [Piazza della Loggia bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_della_Loggia_bombing) in [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia) in 1974.

In the 1980s, Milan became a symbol of the country's economic growth, and a symbol of the economic-financial rampantism of the so-called *"Milano da bere"*, literally "Milan to be drunk",[^cite_note-45] while the Milanese socialist group of [Bettino Craxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettino_Craxi) was in the national government. The city of Milan, in the early 1990s, was the origin of the series of scandals known as *Tangentopoli* which emerged from the judicial investigations of the Milanese prosecutor's office known as [Mani pulite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_pulite), which then spread to the rest of the country.

In early 2020, Lombardy was severely affected by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), in which [Italy was one of the worst-affected countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Italy) in Europe. Several towns were quarantined from 22 February after community transmission was documented in Lombardy and [Veneto](43780.md) the previous day. The entirety of Lombardy was placed under lockdown on 8 March,[^cite_note-46] followed by all of Italy the following day,[^cite_note-47] making Italy the first country to implement a nationwide lockdown in response to the epidemic, which the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) declared a [pandemic](24255.md) on 11 March. The lockdown was extended twice, and the region toughened restrictions on 22 March, banning outdoor exercise and the use of vending machines,[^cite_note-48] but from the beginning of May, following a reported decrease in the number of active cases, restrictions were gradually relaxed.[^cite_note-49]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Lombardy)

Lombardy has a surface area of 23,861 km<sup>2</sup> (9,213 sq mi), and is the fourth-largest [region of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy), after [Sicily](27619.md), [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont), and [Sardinia](29376.md).[^cite_note-tuttitalia-16] It is bordered by [Canton Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_Ticino) and [Canton Grisons](169850.md) of [Switzerland](26748.md) to the north, and by the Italian regions of [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md) and [Veneto](43780.md) to the east, [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md) to the south and [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) to the west.

Lombardy's northern border lies between the [Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina) and the valleys of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) and the [Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inn_\(river\)). To the east, [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda) and the [Mincio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincio) separate Lombardy from the other Italian regions, as does the Po River in the south, with the exception of the [province of Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua) and [Oltrepò Pavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltrep%C3%B2_Pavese). The western boundary is formed by the [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) and the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) river, except for [Lomellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomellina). Lombardy has three natural zones: mountains, hills and plains—the last being divided into *Alta* (high plains) and *Bassa* (low plains).

### Soils



[Pizzo Coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzo_Coca) is the highest peak in the [Orobic Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamasque_Alps) (3,050 m (10,010 ft)).

The surface area of Lombardy is divided almost equally between the plains (which represent approximately 47% of the territory) and the mountainous areas (which represent 41%). The remaining 12% of the region is hilly.[^cite_note-50]

The [orography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orography) of Lombardy is characterised by three distinct belts; a northern mountainous belt constituted by the Alpine relief, a central piedmont area of mostly alluvial pebbly soils, and the Lombard section of the [Padan Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padan_Plain) in the south of the region. The main valleys are [Val Camonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Camonica), [Val Trompia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Trompia), [Valle Sabbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_Sabbia), [Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina), [Val Seriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Seriana), [Val Brembana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Brembana), [Valsassina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valsassina), and [Valassina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valassina).[^cite_note-51]

The most important mountainous area is the [Alpine](981.md) zone, which includes the [Lepontine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepontine_Alps) and [Rhaetian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhaetian_Alps) (4,020 m (13,190 ft)), which derive their name, respectively, from the [Raeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhaetian_people), a population of [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscans) origin who took refuge in the Central Alps during the Celtic invasion of the Italian peninsula, and from the [Ligurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligures) population of the [Lepontii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepontii), who were settled in this area and then subjugated by the Roman emperor [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus),[^cite_note-.montagnadilombardia-52] the [Orobic Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamasque_Alps) (3,050 m (10,010 ft)) which derive their name from the [Orobii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orobii), population of Ligurian or perhaps Celtic origin,[^cite_note-.montagnadilombardia-52] the [Ortler Alps](2002978.md) and the [Adamello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamello) massif. It is followed by the Alpine foothills zone [Prealps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prealps), which are followed by hills that smooth the transition from the mountain to the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), the main peaks of which are the [Grigna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigna) Group (2,410 m (7,910 ft)), [Resegone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resegone) 1,875 m (6,152 ft), and [Presolana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presolana) (2,521 m (8,271 ft)).[^cite_note-53]

The plains of Lombardy, which are formed by alluvial deposits, can be divided into the *Alta*—an upper, permeable ground zone in the north—and the *Bassa*, a lower zone dotted by the line of *fontanili*, where spring waters rise from impermeable ground. Inconsistent with the three distinctions above is the small sub-region of [Oltrepò Pavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltrep%C3%B2_Pavese), which is formed by the [Apennine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_Mountains) foothills beyond the [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)),[^cite_note-oltresentieri-54] and [Lomellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomellina), an area particularly renowned for its [rice paddies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_paddies).

### Hydrography



[Ponte Coperto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponte_Coperto) in [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) over the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) river



[Naviglio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_Grande) in [Gaggiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaggiano)



Panoramic view of [Lake Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como) with the [Alps](981.md) and [Bellagio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellagio,_Lombardy)

The Po marks the southern border of the region for approximately 210 km (130 mi); its major tributaries are the [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)), which rises in the Val Bedretto in [Switzerland](26748.md) and joins the Po near Pavia,[^cite_note-55] the [Olona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olona), the [Lambro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambro), the [Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_\(river\)), the [Oglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oglio) and the [Mincio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincio).

The numerous lakes of Lombardy are all of glacial origin and are located in the northern highlands. From west to east, these lakes are: [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore), [Lake Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano) (both shared with Switzerland), [Lake Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como), [Lake Iseo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Iseo), [Lake Idro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Idro), and [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda) (the largest lake in Italy).[^cite_note-56] South of the Alps lies a succession of low hills of [morainic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) origin that were formed during the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period), as well as small, barely fertile plateaux with typical heaths and conifer woods. A minor mountainous area, the [Oltrepò Pavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltrep%C3%B2_Pavese) lies in the [Apennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennines) range south of the Po.[^cite_note-oltresentieri-54]

The [navigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigli) are a system of interconnected [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) in and around [Milan](36511.md), dating back as far as the Middle Ages.[^cite_note-57] The system consists of five canals: [Naviglio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_Grande), [Naviglio Pavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_Pavese), [Naviglio Martesana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_Martesana), [Naviglio di Paderno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_di_Paderno), and [Naviglio di Bereguardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_di_Bereguardo). The first three were connected through Milan via the *Fossa Interna*, also known as the Inner Ring. The urban section of the [Naviglio Martesana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviglio_Martesana) was covered over at the beginning of the 1930s, together with the entire Inner Ring, thus sounding the death knell for the northeastern canals.

### Alpine passes



The northern side of the [Splügen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spl%C3%BCgen_Pass)

The Lombard Alpine valleys are wider than those found in the [Alps](981.md) in [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) and [Aosta Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aosta_Valley). Most of them are crossed by streams that descend towards the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), forming rivers that then flow into the Po on the hydrographic left. Thanks to the width of their valleys, the Lombard Alpine passes, although they are at a high altitude, are easily accessible.

The most important international passes found in the Lombard Alps, which connect the region with [Switzerland](26748.md) are the [Splügen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spl%C3%BCgen_Pass) (2,118 m (6,949 ft)), the [Maloja Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloja_Pass) (1,815 m (5,955 ft)) and the [Bernina Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_Pass) (2,323 m (7,621 ft)), with the latter two which are located in Swiss territory. The most important national passes are the [Stelvio Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stelvio_Pass) (2,759 m (9,052 ft)) and the [Tonale Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonale_Pass) (1,883 m (6,178 ft)), which connect Lombardy with [Trentino-Alto Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino-Alto_Adige). These Alpine passes are also of great importance from a historical point of view, given that they have always allowed easy communication between Lombardy and its bordering territories. This has resulted in constant commercial traffic, which contributed to the development of the region.[^cite_note-58]

### Flora and fauna



The protected areas of Lombardy

The plains have been intensively cultivated for centuries, and little of the original environment remains. The most common trees are [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm), [alder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alder), [sycamore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycamore), [poplar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus), [willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow), and [hornbeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbeam). In the area of the foothills lakes, however, [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), [cypresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress), and [larches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larch) grow, as do varieties of subtropical flora such as [magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia), [azalea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azalea) and [acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia). Numerous species of endemic flora in the Prealpine area include some species of [saxifrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxifrage), [Lombardy garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allium_insubricum),[^cite_note-RHSPF-59] [groundsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundsel), and [bellflowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanula_isophylla).

The highlands are characterised by the typical vegetation of the [Italian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Alps). At and below approximately 1,100 m (3,600 ft), oaks or broadleaf trees grow; on the mountain slopes between 2,000 and 2,100 m (6,600 and 6,900 ft), beech trees grow at the lowest limits, with conifer woods higher up. Shrubs such as [rhododendron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhododendron), dwarf pine, and [juniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniper) are native to the summit zone beyond 2,200 m (7,200 ft).

Lombardy includes many protected areas. The most important is [Stelvio National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stelvio_National_Park), established in 1935[^cite_note-60]—the fourth-largest Italian natural park, with typically alpine wildlife such as [red deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_deer), [roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer), [ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibex), [chamois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamois), foxes, [ermine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoat), and [golden eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_eagle); and the [Parco naturale lombardo della Valle del Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_naturale_lombardo_della_Valle_del_Ticino), which was instituted in 1974 on the Lombard side of the River [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino_\(river\)) to protect one of the last major examples of fluvial forest in northern Italy. There have also been efforts to protect the endangered [Italian agile frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_agile_frog). The [Parco naturale lombardo della Valle del Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_naturale_lombardo_della_Valle_del_Ticino) is the first [Italian regional park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regional_parks_of_Italy) to be established[^cite_note-61] as well as the first European river park. In 2022, the two parks were included by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in the [World Network of Biosphere Reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Network_of_Biosphere_Reserves).[^cite_note-62]

Other parks in the region are the [Campo dei Fiori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_dei_Fiori_di_Varese) and the [Cinque Vette Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinque_Vette_Park), both of which are located in the [Province of Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Varese). The system of protected areas in Lombardy consists of one national park, 24 regional parks, 65 [natural reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reserve)[^cite_note-63] and 30 [natural monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_monument).[^cite_note-64] In total, protected areas cover more than 27% of the regional territory.[^cite_note-65]

### Climate



Fog at the [Visconti Castle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visconti_Castle_\(Legnano\))



An [olive tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_tree) on the shores of [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda). On Lake Garda, there is a "Mediterranean" microclimate which allows the cultivation of olive trees and the production of olive oil.

Lombardy has a wide array of climates due to variations in elevation, proximity to inland water basins, and large metropolitan areas. The climate is mainly [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Cfa), especially in the plains, though with significant variations from the Köppen model, especially in the normally long, damp, and cold winters. There is high seasonal temperature variation; in Milan, the average temperature is 2.5 °C (36.5 °F) in January and 24 °C (75 °F) in July. The plains are often subject to fog during the coldest months.[^cite_note-66]

In the Alpine foothills with an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Cfb), numerous lakes have a mitigating influence, allowing typically Mediterranean crops (such as [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) and [citrus fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus_fruit)) to grow. In the hills and mountains, the climate is [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Dfb). In the valleys, it is relatively mild, while it can be severely cold with copious snowfalls above 1,500 m (4,900 ft).

Precipitation is more intense in the [Prealpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prealpi_Orobiche) zone, with up to 1,500 to 2,000 mm (59.1 to 78.7 in) annually, but it is also abundant in the plains and alpine zones, with an average of 600 to 850 mm (23.6 to 33.5 in) annually. The average annual rainfall is 827 mm (32.6 in).[^cite_note-67] [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda), thanks to its size and position, mitigates the climate of its coasts,[^cite_note-68] creating a "[Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate)" [microclimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate) that makes the cultivation of olive trees and the production of [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) possible; the so-called "Lombard oil" is also produced in other Lombard lake areas.[^cite_note-69]

### Geology



[Val Brembana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Brembana)



[Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley) in [Origgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origgio), in the [Alto Milanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto_Milanese). In the background [Mount Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rosa)

The geological structure of Lombardy derives from the [orogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orogeny) of the [Alps](981.md), resulting from the collision between the [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Plate) and [Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate) plates, which generated the Alpine chain from the [Upper Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Cretaceous) to the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene).[^cite_note-70]

The [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), on the other hand, is of more recent origin, formed by the deposit of detrital material on the continental shelf, coming from the erosion caused by surface waters that accompanied the lifting of the Alpine chain, which rose to the west and north of the plain, and of the [Apennine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_Mountains) chain to the south, filling the marine gulf that existed in the [Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliocene), created by the uplift of the two mountain chains.[^cite_note-71]

### Pollution

Lombardy is one of the most air-polluted areas of Europe.[^cite_note-72] Because of high levels of industrialisation and the lack of wind due to the region being enclosed between mountain ranges, air pollution remains a severe problem in Lombardy and northern Italy.

In March 2019, the [European Space Agency](10363.md) (ESA)[^cite_note-73] published images taken from its satellites, showing a large stain composed of [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide) and fine particles above the Po Valley area. Lombardy is the geographic and economic centre of this area, with more than 10 million residents and the highest GRP per inhabitant in the country. Most of its major cities are located in the Po River basin, which crosses the region. The stain analysed by ESA is the main reason Po Valley air pollution levels are so high. Milan also has high levels of [ozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone) and [nitrogen oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_oxides), which are mainly produced by diesel and petrol engines in cars.

According to the Chicago Energy Policy Institute,[^cite_note-74] which has recently developed the Air Quality Life Index (AQLI), Po Valley air pollution reduces [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) by approximately six months. Air pollution in the Po Valley is linked to livestock and factories. The use of [NPK fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPK_fertilizer), composed of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, along with manure emissions from intensive breeding and high levels of nitrogen dioxide released by diesel and petrol engines are all contributing factors to pollution in Northern Italy. Lombardy also produces vast amounts of animal waste, a significant contributor to pollution. Lombardy produces more than 40% of Italy's milk, and over half of the Italian pig production is located in the Po Valley.[^cite_note-75]

According to research published in [The Lancet Planetary Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lancet_Planetary_Health),[^cite_note-76] in January 2021, Brescia and Bergamo had the highest death rate from [fine particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_particulate_matter) (PM<sub>2.5</sub>) in Europe.

The data show that many cities in Lombardy and the Po Valley suffer from the most serious impact of poor air quality in [Europe](9239.md), primarily the metropolitan area of Milan, which ranks 13th in terms of fine particulate impact, with an annual premature death rate of 3,967, accounting for approximately 9% of the total.

One-sixth of the Italian population, about 10 million people, live in Lombardy (17.1% of the national population; 2.2% of the [European Union](9317.md) population). The population is highly concentrated in the Milan metropolitan area (2,029 inh./km<sup>2</sup>) and the Alpine foothills that compose the southern section of the provinces [Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Varese), [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Como), [Lecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lecco), [Monza and Brianza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Monza_and_Brianza) and [Bergamo](987207.md), (1,200 inh./km<sup>2</sup>). A lower average population density (250 inh./km<sup>2</sup>) is found in the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padan_Plain) and the lower Brescia valleys; much lower densities (fewer than 60 inh./km<sup>2</sup>) inhabit the northern mountain areas and the southern [Oltrepò Pavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oltrep%C3%B2_Pavese) subregion.[^cite_note-Regional_Statistical_Yearbook_2014-79]

The growth of the regional population was particularly sustained during the 1950s–1960s, due to a prolonged economic boom, high birth rates and strong migration inflows—especially from southern Italy. Since the 1980s, Lombardy has become the destination of a large number of international migrants, to the point where they now make up 14.9% of the population.[^cite_note-foreignborn-80]

### Religion



Palazzo Arcivescovile in Milan, the official residence of the [Archbishop of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan)

The primary religion is [Roman Catholicism](606848.md). Over the centuries, the Catholic [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of Lombardy have given birth to ten [popes](23056.md): [Pope John XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XIV), [Pope Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II), [Pope Urban III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_III), [Pope Celestine IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Celestine_IV), [Pope Pius IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IV), [Pope Gregory XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIV), [Pope Innocent XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI), [Pope Pius XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI), [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII) and [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI).

The structure of the Lombard Catholic dioceses is historical. Of the ten dioceses, eight date back to the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), only [Crema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crema,_Lombardy) and [Vigevano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigevano) were created in the 16th century to reflect political needs,[^cite_note-81] and it does not appear that any diocesan seat was ever suppressed. The [diocese of Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Lugano) was created in the 19th century to separate the Swiss parishes which since ancient times had been dependent on the dioceses of [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como) and [Milan](36511.md).

In Lombardy there are two main Catholic liturgical rites: the [Ambrosian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite) (used in the [Milanese archdiocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan), but also used in the parishes of [Val Taleggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Taleggio) in the [province of Bergamo](987207.md)) and the [Roman Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rite).[^cite_note-82] The Milanese diocese, comprising approximately half of the faithful of the region, is the [metropolitan see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province), while the others are its [suffragans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_diocese).

Significant religious minorities in Lombardy include [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), [Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity), as well as [Jews](25955086.md), [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist), [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) and [Muslims](6037917.md).[^cite_note-83]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Lombardy)

### Government



[Palazzo Lombardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Lombardia) in [Milan](36511.md), headquarters of the regional government of Lombardy

Lombardy has a system of [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) in which the President of the Region (*_Presidente della Regione_*) is the [head of government](37570.md) and of a pluriform [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is vested in the regional government (*_Giunta Regionale_*) and [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in the [Regional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Lombardy) (*_Consiglio Regionale_*).

Like the other regions of Italy with ordinary statutes, the region has been provided for since 1948 by articles 114 and 115 of the [Constitution of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy), but only with law no. 281 of 16 May 1970 having as its object "Financial measures for the implementation of the Regions with ordinary statute", did it implement its functions. The law, which aimed to implement the process of administrative decentralization envisaged by article 5 and article 118 of the Constitution, started the process.

The Council is elected for a five-year term, but, if the President suffers a vote of no confidence, resigns or dies, under the *simul stabunt, simul cadent* clause introduced in 1999 (literally *they will stand together or they will fall together*), also the Council is dissolved and a snap election is called.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] The Regional Cabinet (*Giunta Regionale*) is presided by the President of the Region (*Presidente della Regione*), who is elected for a five-year term, and is currently composed by 17 members: the President and 16 regional [Assessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assessor_\(Italy\)), including a Vice President (*Vice Presidente*), while 4 under-secretaries (*Sottosegretari*) help the President but have no voting rights in the cabinet meetings.[^cite_note-86]

### Politics



Internal view of Palazzo Lombardia

From 1945 to the early 1990s, the moderate [Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democracy_\(Italy\)) maintained a large majority of the popular support and control of the most important cities and provinces from the end of the Second World War. The opposition [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party) was a considerable presence only in southern Lombardy and in the working-class districts of Milan; their base, however, was increasingly eroded by the rival centrist [Italian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Socialist_Party) until the *[Mani Pulite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Pulite)* corruption scandal, which spread from Milan to the whole of Italy, almost completely erased the old political class.[^cite_note-87]

This, together with general disaffection for the central government, led to the sudden growth of the secessionist [Northern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Nord). Since 2002, Lombardy has remained strongly conservative in six general elections. The regional capital Milan elected progressive [Giuliano Pisapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Pisapia) at the 2011 municipal elections and the 2013 regional elections saw a narrow victory for the centre-right coalition.[^cite_note-88]

On 22 October 2017, a non-binding [autonomy referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_autonomy_referendum,_2017) took place in Lombardy. The turnout was 38.3%, of which 95.3% voted in favour.

### Administrative divisions

See also: [List of municipalities of Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Lombardy)

Lombardy is divided into 1,501 municipalities (the region with the largest number of municipalities in the entire national territory[^cite_note-comuni-15]), distributed in twelve administrative subdivisions (eleven [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Italy) plus the [Metropolitan City of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan)). The largest province is that of [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brescia), the smallest that of [Monza and Brianza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Monza_and_Brianza). The [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of [Campione d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campione_d'Italia) also belongs to the region, a municipality entirely surrounded by [Swiss territory](26748.md) and part of the [province of Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Como).

### Symbols



The [flag of Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Lombardy)



One of the [Camunian roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camunian_rose) depicted in the [Rock Drawings in Valcamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Drawings_in_Valcamonica), in the [province of Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brescia)

The symbols of Lombardy are, pursuant to the region's statute of autonomy, the flag, the coat of arms, the banner and the celebration of 29 May.[^cite_note-89]

The official coat of arms of Lombardy consists of a [Camunian rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camunian_rose), an ancient solar symbol common to some proto-[Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celt) peoples, present in 94 of the approximately 140,000 [Rock Drawings in Valcamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Drawings_in_Valcamonica), in the [province of Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brescia). These engravings were made from the [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) (approximately 8th-6th millennium BC) to the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) (1st millennium BC) by various ancient peoples, including the [Camunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camunni). The engravings made by the latter, including the rose of the same name, were made during the Iron Age.[^cite_note-90]

The Camunian rose on the region's coat of arms is made of [argent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argent), symbolizing light. In the background, the green colour represents the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley). Officially adopted together with the banner with regional law n. 85 of 12 June 1975,[^cite_note-simboli-91] the coat of arms was introduced on the proposal of the then councilor for culture [Alessandro Fontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Fontana)[^cite_note-92] and was designed in the same year by [Pino Tovaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pino_Tovaglia), [Bob Noorda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Noorda), [Roberto Sambonet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Sambonet?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Bruno Munari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Munari).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-simboli2-94]

The banner consists of a reproduction of the [Carroccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroccio), a large four-wheeled chariot bearing the city insignia around which the militias of the [Medieval communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_commune) of [northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) gathered and fought, whose autonomy it represented, and of the coat of arms of the region. The dimensions of the Lombardy banner are 3 m × 2 m (9.8 ft × 6.6 ft) and the ribbons and tie are in the [national colours of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_colours_of_Italy).[^cite_note-simboli2-94]

Since 29 January 2019[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] the Lombardy region has adopted the coat of arms with the Camunian rose as its official flag, thus attesting to the established practice in public offices and events.

The regional festival of Lombardy, which was established with regional law n. 15 of 26 November 2013,[^cite_note-simboli-91] is celebrated on 29 May in memory of the victory of the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League) over the imperial troops of [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) in the [battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano), an armed clash which took place on 29 May 1176 in the surroundings of the [city of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legnano) which ended to the hegemonic plan of the German emperor over the medieval municipalities of northern Italy.[^cite_note-97] After the decisive defeat of Legnano, the emperor accepted a six-year armistice (the so-called "[Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) truce"), until the [Peace of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance), following which the medieval municipalities of northern Italy agreed to remain faithful to the Empire in exchange for full local jurisdiction over their territories.[^cite_note-98]

## Culture

### Cuisine

Main article: [Lombard cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_cuisine)



A crockpot of *[cassœula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassoeula)*. It is a typical dish of the popular tradition, a main dish of many Lombard *[sagre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagra_\(festival\))*.



[Polenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polenta) porridge, one of the typical dishes of Lombardy, served with [salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salami) and mushrooms

[Lombard cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_cuisine) is the style of cooking in the [Northern Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) region of Lombardy. The historical events of its provinces and the diversity of its territories resulted in a varied culinary tradition. First courses in Lombard cuisine range from [risottos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risotto) to [soups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup) and [stuffed pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filled_pasta) (in [broth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broth) or not), and a large choice of second-course meat or fish dishes, due to the many lakes and rivers of Lombardy.[^cite_note-99]

The cuisine of the various Lombardy provinces can be united by the following traits: prevalence of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and stuffed pasta over dry pasta, both [butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter) and [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) for cooking, dishes cooked for a long time, as well as the widespread use of [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork), [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk) and [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product), and [egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg)-based preparations; to which is added the consumption of [polenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polenta), common to the whole [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy).[^cite_note-100]

Rice is popular in Lombardy; the region is the largest in Europe for rice production and in particular the province of Pavia, where over 84,000 ha (210,000 acres) are cultivated.[^cite_note-Riso_italiano,_dove_si_coltiva-101] Rice is often used in soups and *[risotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risotto)*, such as "risotto alla milanese", with [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron). In Monza, a popular recipe adds pieces of sausages to the risotto, while in Pavia they eat Carthusian risotto, according to the legend created by the monks of the [Certosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certosa_di_Pavia), which is based on crayfish, carrots and onions. They also eat risotto with eye beans, a version with sausage and [bonarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonarda_Piemontese), and risotto with [common hops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humulus_lupulus) (ürtis in Pavese dialect). [Polenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polenta) is common throughout the region.

Regional cheeses include *[Robiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robiola)*, *[Crescenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescenza)*, *[Taleggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taleggio_\(cheese\))*, *[Gorgonzola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgonzola_\(cheese\))* and *[Grana Padano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano)*. Butter and cream are used. Single-pot dishes, which take little work to prepare, are popular. Common types of pasta include [Casoncelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casoncelli) in Brescia and Bergamo and [Pizzoccheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzoccheri) in Valtellina. In Mantua, festivals feature *tortelli di zucca* ([ravioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravioli) with pumpkin filling) accompanied by melted butter and followed by [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_meat) stuffed with chicken or other stewed meats.[^cite_note-102] Among typical regional desserts is [Nocciolini di Canzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canzo#Cuisine)—dry biscuits. Common in the whole [Insubria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubria) area are *[bruscitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruscitti)*, originating from [Altomilanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto_Milanese), which consist of a braised meat dish cut very thin and cooked in wine and [fennel seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennel), historically obtained by stripping leftover meat.

### Languages

Main article: [Lombard language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language)



[Lombard language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) distribution in Europe:

&#x20; Areas where Lombard is spoken

&#x20; Areas where Lombard is spoken alongside other languages ([Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German), [Ladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladin_language) and [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language)) and areas of linguistic transition (with [Piedmontese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmontese_language), with [Emilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language) and with [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language))

&#x20; Areas of influence of Lombard (Tridentine dialect)

[Lombard language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) is widely used in Lombardy, in [diglossia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diglossia) with Italian. Lombard is a language[^cite_note-103] belonging to the [Gallo-Italic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic) group within the [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) characterized by a [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_language) [linguistic substratum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_substratum) and a [Lombardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardic_language) [linguistic superstratum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstratum).[^cite_note-\:0-104] It is a [cluster of homogeneous varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect#Dialect_and_language_clusters) used by at least 3,500,000 native speakers in Lombardy and some areas of neighbouring regions, such as the eastern part of [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) and the southern Switzerland cantons of [Ticino](84147.md) and [Grisons](169850.md).[^cite_note-\:0-104] The language is also spoken in [Santa Catarina](256305.md) in [Brazil](3383.md) by Lombard immigrants from the [Province of Bergamo](987207.md), in [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

The Celtic [linguistic substratum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_substratum) of modern Lombard and the neighbouring languages of Northern Italy is self-evident and so the Lombard language is classified as a [Gallo-Italic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_languages) (from the ancient Roman name for the Celts, [Gauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls)).[^cite_note-auto2-107] [Roman domination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) shaped the dialects spoken in the area, which was called [Cisalpine Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Gaul) by the Romans, and much of the [lexicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicon) and [grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar) of the Lombard language have their origin in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin).[^cite_note-108] However, that influence was not homogeneous[^cite_note-auto2-107] since idioms of different areas were influenced by previous linguistic substrata, and each area was marked by a stronger or weaker Latinisation or the preservation of ancient Celtic characteristics.[^cite_note-auto2-107]

The main varieties of the Lombard language are [Western Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Lombard) (spoken in the provinces of [Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Varese), [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Como), [Lecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lecco), [Sondrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Sondrio), [Monza and Brianza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Monza_and_Brianza), [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan), [Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lodi) and [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pavia)), [Eastern Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lombard) (in the provinces of [Bergamo](987207.md) and [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brescia), in [Cremasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crema,_Italy), in [Upper Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mantua) and in the [Lecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lecco) municipalities of Val San Martino), the Alpine Lombard (in [Ticino](84147.md) and in [Italian Grisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Grisons), in the north of Lombardy and [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) and in some areas of [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino)) and the Southern Lombard, in transition with the [Emilian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language) (in the provinces of [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Cremona) and [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua)); in the southern part of the [province of Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua) and in Casalasco (south-eastern area of the [province of Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Cremona)) Emilian language is spoken.[^cite_note-109]

The Lombard language should not be confused with that of the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) – [Lombardic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardic_language), a [Germanic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) extinct since the Middle Ages. Lombard is considered a [minority language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_language) that is structurally separate from [Italian](14708.md) by both [Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue) and the [UNESCO](21786641.md) *Red Book on Endangered Languages*. However, [Italy](14532.md) and [Switzerland](26748.md) do not recognize Lombard speakers as a linguistic minority. Traditionally, the Lombard dialects have been classified into the Eastern, Western, Alpine and Southern Lombard dialects.[^cite_note-110]

### Art and architecture

#### From prehistory to the classical era



The remains of the [Milan amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_amphitheatre), which can be found inside the archaeological park of the Antiquarium in Milan

The first artistic evidence in Lombardy dates back to the [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) period when, at the end of the [Würm glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrm_glaciation), the historical cycle of the [Rock Drawings in Valcamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Drawings_in_Valcamonica) began, which continued and subsequently expanded in the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and the [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Age) to end only in Roman and medieval times.[^cite_note-111] The Camunian cycle is considered one of the most important testimonies of prehistory worldwide[^cite_note-112] and is therefore included in the list of [World Heritage Site](44940.md).

Furthermore, further finds have been found of the presence of prehistoric populations in the Lombardy territory, also included in the world heritage of humanity with the serial site of the "[Prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_pile_dwellings_around_the_Alps)", with several locations located in Lombardy.[^cite_note-113]

The [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) left evidence scattered throughout the archaeological museums of the region, while the [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscans) presence is attested in the [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua) area. Following the [Roman conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_expansion_in_Italy), the artistic evolution of the region veered towards the styles of the conquerors from the late republican period to the Roman imperial era: monumental remains of this historical era can be seen in [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia) ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Brixia*) and [Milan](36511.md) ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *[Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum)*).

#### From late antiquity to the modern era



Early Christian chapel located inside the [Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant'Ambrogio) in Milan

In the late ancient period, the Lombardy territory acquired importance, with Milan being the capital of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), and consequently, the artistic production also increased, of which evidence remains especially in sacred architecture with the construction of [Early Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christian_art_and_architecture) churches, [particularly in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christian_churches_in_Milan).

The subsequent early medieval period, coeval with and following the [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period), will be of capital importance for the development of regional art: the stylistic features of barbarian art introduced by the new populations in fact brought a decisive contribution, merging with late ancient models (which are maintained with continuity) as well as thanks to [Byzantine influences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_art),[^cite_note-114] for the creation of a truly Lombard art. In fact, upon leaving the early medieval period, we began to talk about artistic styles specific to Lombardy such as the [Lombard Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Romanesque).



[Basilica of Sant'Abbondio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant'Abbondio) in [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como)

Noteworthy examples of the Lombard Romanesque style are the work of the [Comacine masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comacine_masters), in particular in the [Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant'Ambrogio) and [Basilica of San Michele Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Michele_Maggiore,_Pavia) and in the [Basilica of Sant'Abbondio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant'Abbondio), in the Como area. The most important contribution between the 6th and 8th centuries came from the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) who, occupying a large part of Italy, established their capital in [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) and made Lombardy the fulcrum of their kingdom bringing their art with them, of which there remain both significant testimonies (in particular in [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia), [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza), Pavia and [Castelseprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelseprio,_Lombardy)) and a substantial influence on subsequent artistic developments.

In the Lombard area, the [Carolingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_art) period saw substantial artistic continuity with the previous Lombard period. The lower production of monumental buildings typical of these centuries is counterbalanced by numerous minor artefacts of great value, such as the [Cross of Agilulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Agilulf?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Cross of Desiderius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Desiderius) and the [Gospel Book of Theodelinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_Book_of_Theodelinda?action=edit\&redlink=1). Also in Lombardy are some of the greatest expressions of Lombard sculpture, such as the slab with peacock in the Museum of Santa Giulia in [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia)[^cite_note-115] or the [Plutei of Theodota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutei_of_Theodota) in the [Pavia Civic Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia_Civic_Museums). The following centuries, as already mentioned, were characterized by artistic styles typical of Lombardy such as the Lombard Romanesque, the [Lombard Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Gothic_architecture), the [Lombard Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_in_Lombardy) and the Lombard [Seicento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seicento). Finally, we must not forget, especially during the Renaissance, the contributions and stimuli left in local art by some great Renaissance masters who worked in Milan at the [Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Sforza) court, such as [Filarete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filarete), [Donato Bramante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donato_Bramante) and [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) and in Mantua at the [Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Gonzaga) court, like [Andrea Mantegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Mantegna) and [Giulio Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Romano_\(painter\)).

#### Contemporary age



[Umberto Boccioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_Boccioni), *[Unique Forms of Continuity in Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_Forms_of_Continuity_in_Space)*, example from 1949 exhibited at the [Museo del Novecento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_del_Novecento) in Milan

In February 1910 the painters [Umberto Boccioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_Boccioni), [Carlo Carrà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Carr%C3%A0), [Giacomo Balla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Balla), [Gino Severini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gino_Severini) and [Luigi Russolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Russolo) signed the *Manifesto dei pittori futuristi* in Milan and in April of the same year the *Manifesto tecnico della pittura futurista,*,[^cite_note-116] which they contributed, together with others posters signed in other Italian cities, to found the artistic movement of [Futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism). Upon the death of Umberto Boccioni in 1916, Carrà and Severini found themselves in a phase of evolution towards [cubist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism) painting, consequently, the Milanese group disbanded, moving the headquarters of the movement from Milan to Rome, with the consequent birth of the "second Futurism".

Lombardy was the birthplace of another important artistic movement of the 20th century, the [Novecento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novecento_Italiano), which was born in Milan at the end of 1922. It was started by a group of artists composed of [Mario Sironi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Sironi), [Achille Funi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achille_Funi), [Leonardo Dudreville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Dudreville), [Anselmo Bucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anselmo_Bucci), [Emilio Malerba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Malerba), [Pietro Marussig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Marussig) and [Ubaldo Oppi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubaldo_Oppi) who, at the Pesaro Gallery in Milan, joined together in the new movement baptized Novecento by Bucci.[^cite_note-117] These artists, who felt they were translators of the spirit of the 20th century, came from different experiences and artistic currents, but linked by a common sense of "return to order" in art after the avant-garde experiments especially of Futurism: in this sense this artistic movement also adopted the name of [simplified Neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumentalism). The Novecento movement also manifested itself in literature with [Massimo Bontempelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Bontempelli) and above all in architecture with the famous architects [Giovanni Muzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Muzio), [Giò Ponti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gi%C3%B2_Ponti), [Paolo Mezzanotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Mezzanotte) and others. Some of the works of the major Lombard artists of the 20th century are exhibited at the [Museo del Novecento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_del_Novecento) in Milan.[^cite_note-118]

### Literature

Main article: [Western Lombard literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Lombard_literature)



[Carlo Maria Maggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Maria_Maggi)

The first texts written in the vernacular [Lombard language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) date back to the 13th century. These are mainly works of a didactic-religious nature; an example is the *Sermon Divin* by [Pietro da Barsegapè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_da_Barsegap%C3%A8?action=edit\&redlink=1), which narrates the [Passion of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_of_Jesus). Very important is the contribution to Lombard literature of [Bonvesin de la Riva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonvesin_de_la_Riva), who wrote, among other works, the *[Liber di Tre Scricciur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_di_Tre_Scricciur)*, the *De magnalibus urbis Mediolani* ("The Wonders of Milan"), and an [etiquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiquette), the *De quinquaginta curialitatibus ad mensam* ("Fifty table courtesies").[^cite_note-122]

From the 15th century, the prestige of literary [Tuscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_dialect) began to supplant the use of northern vernaculars which had been used, although influenced by the Florentine vernacular, also in chancellor and administrative contexts.[^cite_note-123] Despite this, starting from this century, there began to be the first signs of a true Lombard literature, with literary compositions in the Lombard language both in the western part of the region and in the eastern one.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]



[Carlo Porta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Porta)

The 17th century also saw the emergence of the figure of the playwright [Carlo Maria Maggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Maria_Maggi), who created, among other things, the Milanese mask of [Meneghino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meneghino).[^cite_note-126] Also in the 17th century, the first *[bosinade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosinada)* were born, occasional popular poems written on loose sheets of paper and posted in squares or read (or even sung) in public; they had great success and widespread diffusion until the first decades of the 20th century.[^cite_note-127] Milanese literature had a strong development in the 18th century: some important names emerged, including the famous poet [Giuseppe Parini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Parini), who wrote some compositions in the Lombard language.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

The beginning of the 19th century was dominated by the figure of [Carlo Porta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Porta), recognized by many as the most important author of Lombard literature, and also included among the greatest poets of Italian national literature. With him, some of the highest peaks of literary expressiveness in the Lombard language were reached, which clearly emerged in works such as *La Ninetta del Verzee*, *Desgrazzi de Giovannin Bongee*, *La guerra di pret* e *Lament del Marchionn de gamb avert*.[^cite_note-130] Milanese poetic production took on such important dimensions that in 1815 the scholar [Francesco Cherubini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Cherubini) published a four-volume [anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology) of Lombard literature, which included texts written from the 17th century to his day.[^cite_note-131]

### Music

Main articles: [Music of Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Lombardy) and [Music of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Milan)



Founded in 1778, [La Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Scala) in [Milan](36511.md) is the world's most famous opera house.

[^cite_note-Griffin-133]

Each of Lombardy's 12 provinces has its own musical traditions. Bergamo is famous for being the birthplace of [Gaetano Donizetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Donizetti) and home of the Teatro Donizetti; Brescia hosts the 1709 Teatro Grande; Cremona is regarded as the origin of the violin and is home to several of the most prestigious [luthiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luthier);[^cite_note-134] and Mantua was one of the founding and most important cities in 16th- and 17th-century opera and classical music.

Other cities such as Lecco, Lodi, Varese and Pavia ([Teatro Fraschini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Fraschini)) also have rich musical traditions, but Milan is the centre of the Lombard musical scene. It was the workplace of [Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi), one of the most famous and influential 19th-century opera composers. The province has acclaimed theatres, such as the [Piccolo Teatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccolo_Teatro_\(Milan\)) and the Teatro Arcimboldi; however, the most famous is the 1778 [Teatro alla Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_alla_Scala) (popularly La Scala), the world's most famous opera house.[^cite_note-Griffin-133]

[Remains of Roman forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia#UNESCO_World_Heritage_monuments) in Brescia



[Sacro Monte di Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacro_Monte_di_Varese)



[Sabbioneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbioneta), a town and *[comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune)* in the [province of Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua)

There are ten [UNESCO World Heritage sites](44940.md) wholly or partially located in Lombardy.[^cite_note-135] Some of these comprise several individual objects in different locations. One of the entries has been listed as natural heritage and the others are cultural heritage sites.

At [Monte San Giorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_San_Giorgio) on the border with Swiss canton Ticino just south of [Lake Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano), a wide range of marine [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic) fossils have been found. During the Triassic period, 245–230 million years ago, the area was a shallow tropical lagoon. Fossils include reptiles, fish, [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean) and insects.[^cite_note-136]

The [Rock Drawings in Valcamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Drawings_in_Valcamonica) date to between 8000 BC and 1000 BC, covering prehistoric periods from the [Epipaleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipaleolithic) and [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) to the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), and constitute the largest collections of prehistoric [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) in the world.[^cite_note-unesco2-29] The collection was recognized by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 1979 and was Italy's first recognized World Heritage Site. UNESCO has formally recognized more than 140,000 figures and symbols,[^cite_note-unesco2-29] but new discoveries have increased the number of catalogued incisions to between 200,000[^cite_note-adorno-30] and 300,000.[^cite_note-137] The petroglyphs are spread on all surfaces of the valley, but concentrated in the areas of [Darfo Boario Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfo_Boario_Terme), [Capo di Ponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capo_di_Ponte), [Nadro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadro), [Cimbergo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimbergo) and [Paspardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paspardo).

[Prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_pile_dwellings_around_the_Alps) are a series of prehistoric pile dwelling (or [stilt house](1879233.md)) settlements in and around the [Alps](981.md) built from about 5000 to 500 BC on the edges of lakes, rivers or wetlands. In 2011, 111 sites located variously in [Switzerland](26748.md) (56), [Italy](14532.md) (19), [Germany](11867.md) (18), [France](5843419.md) (11), [Austria](26964606.md) (5) and [Slovenia](27338.md) (2) were added to the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) list.[^cite_note-UNESCO3-138] In Slovenia, these were the first World Heritage Sites to be listed for their cultural value.[^cite_note-139] Excavations conducted at some of the sites have yielded evidence regarding prehistoric life and the way communities interacted with their environment during the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and [Bronze Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) in Alpine Europe. These settlements are a unique group of exceptionally well-preserved and culturally rich archaeological sites, which constitute one of the most important sources for the study of early agrarian societies in the region.[^cite_note-UNESCO3-138]

Another multi-centred site, [Longobards in Italy, Places of Power (568–774 A.D.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longobards_in_Italy,_Places_of_Power_\(568%E2%80%93774_A.D.\)) consists of seven locations across mainland Italy which illustrate the history of the Lombard period. Two of the sites are in modern-day Lombardy: the fortifications (the *castrum* and the [Torba Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torba_Tower)), and the church of Santa Maria *foris portas* ("outside the gates") has Byzantinesque frescoes at [Castelseprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelseprio_\(archaeological_park\)), and the [monastic complex of San Salvatore-Santa Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvatore,_Brescia) at Brescia. The UNESCO site at Brescia also includes the [remains of its Roman forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia#UNESCO_World_Heritage_monuments), the best-preserved in northern Italy.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

The Church and [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) Convent of [Santa Maria delle Grazie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_delle_Grazie_\(Milan\)) in Milan with *[The Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_\(Leonardo\))* by Leonardo da Vinci represent architectural and painting styles of the 15th-century Renaissance period.[^cite_note-142] The towns Mantua and [Sabbioneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbioneta) are also listed as a combined World Heritage site relating to this period, here focussing more on town-planning aspects of the time than on architectural detail. While Mantua was rebuilt in the 15th and 16th centuries, according to Renaissance principles, Sabbioneta was planned as a new town in the 16th century.[^cite_note-143]

The [Sacri Monti of Piedmont and Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacri_Monti_of_Piedmont_and_Lombardy) are a group of nine sites in northwest Italy, two of which are in Lombardy. The concept of holy mountains can be found elsewhere in Europe. These sites were created as centres of pilgrimage by placing chapels in the natural landscape and were loosely modelled on the topography of Jerusalem. In Lombardy, Sacro Monte del Rosario di Varese and Sacro Monte della Beata Vergine del Soccorso, which were built in the early-to mid-17th century, mark the architectural transition from the late Renaissance to the Baroque style.[^cite_note-144]

[Crespi d'Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crespi_d'Adda) is a historic settlement and an outstanding example of the 19th and early 20th-century ["company towns"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_village) built in Europe and North America by enlightened industrialists to meet the workers' needs. The site is still intact and is partly used for industrial purposes, although changing economic and social conditions now threaten its survival.[^cite_note-145] Since 1995 it has been on [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s list of [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites).

[Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua) and [Sabbioneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbioneta) represent two approaches of [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) period town planning. Mantua (pictured), originating in Roman times and preserving structures from the 11th century, was renovated in the 15th and 16th centuries. On the other hand, Sabbioneta was founded in the second half of the 16th century by [Vespasiano I Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasiano_I_Gonzaga) and built with a [grid plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan), according to the period's vision of an ideal city.[^cite_note-146]

The [Rhaetian Railway in the Albula/Bernina Landscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_Railway) is mostly located in the Swiss canton [Grisons](169850.md), but extends over the border into [Tirano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirano). The site is listed because of the complex railway engineering (tunnels, viaducts and avalanche galleries) necessary to take the narrow-gauge railway across the main chain of the Alps.[^cite_note-147] The two railway lines were opened in several stages between 1904 and 1910.[^cite_note-148]

The [Venetian Works of Defence between the 16th and 17th centuries: Stato da Terra – western Stato da Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Works_of_Defence_between_the_16th_and_17th_centuries:_Stato_da_Terra_%E2%80%93_western_Stato_da_Mar) is a transnational system of fortifications that were built by the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) on its [mainland domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domini_di_Terraferma) (*Stato da Terra*) and its territories stretching along the [Adriatic](23275478.md) coast (*[Stato da Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stato_da_Mar)*). This site includes the fortified city [Bergamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamo).[^cite_note-149]

### Traditions and folklore



Typical masks of the [Bagolino Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagolino_Carnival?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the [province of Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brescia)

There are numerous traditional festivals and meetings in Lombardy: cities and towns offer calendars full of events, some of which have ancient origins.

The [Oh bej! Oh bej!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_bej!_Oh_bej!) is held in Milan on 7 and 8 December each year and commemorates the appointment of Saint [Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose) as bishop of the city.

The [Ambrosian Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrosian_Carnival?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Italian](14708.md): *Carnevale Ambrosiano*) is celebrated in the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan) and in the territories of some of the neighbouring [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese). It lasts until the first Saturday of Lent.[^cite_note-AA.VV-150]

The [Bagolino Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagolino_Carnival?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Italian](14708.md): *Carnevale di Bagolino*), of very ancient tradition, is celebrated in [Bagolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagolino), an ancient village that retains its medieval architectural characteristics and located in the Caffaro Valley, in the [province of Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brescia).[^cite_note-151]



The horse race of the [Palio di Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palio_di_Legnano) 2014

The [Palio di Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palio_di_Legnano) is held in [the city of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legnano) and in the [Metropolitan City of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan), on the last Sunday of May. It is the historical re-enactment which celebrates the victory of the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League) over the emperor [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) in the [battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano) on 29 May 1176. The event includes a [medieval pageant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_pageant) through the streets of the city and finally a [horse race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_race) between the [eight contrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrade_of_Legnano) in which Legnano is divided.

The historical re-enactment of the [oath of Pontida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_Pontida) is held in [Pontida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontida), in the [province of Bergamo](987207.md), on 7 March and commemorates the legendary oath of the Lombard League which should have been the prelude to the victorious war of the Lombard municipalities against Barbarossa.[^cite_note-AA.VV-150]

## Economy

Lombardy is the first[^cite_note-first-19] region of Italy in terms of economic importance.[^cite_note-huffingtonpost-20] As of 2021, the [gross regional product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_product) (GRP) of Lombardy was equal to over €366 billion and accounted for about 22% of Italy's total GDP. Lombardy's 2021 GRP was €36,500 per person, more than 25% higher than the national average of €25,729.[^cite_note-Regional_Statistical_Yearbook_2014-79] Lombardy is the [second region of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Union_regions_by_GDP) by nominal GDP.[^cite_note-GDP-18]

Lombardy's [services sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Services_sector) has grown since the 1980s, led by innovative activities in business services, credit and financial services. Lombardy also remains the main industrial area of Italy. Milan, its capital, is the economic capital of Italy,[^cite_note-prologis-23] is a global financial and business centre and is widely regarded as a global capital in industrial design, fashion and architecture.[^cite_note-161]

Lombardy has cultural and economic relationships with many foreign countries including [Azerbaijan](746.md),[^cite_note-162] Austria,[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] France,[^cite_note-166] Hungary,[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Switzerland (especially the cantons of [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Ticino) and [Grisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Grisons)),[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] Canada (the [Province of Quebec](7954867.md)),[^cite_note-177] Germany (the States of [Bavaria](3764.md), [Saxony](28395.md), and [Saxony-Anhalt](38138.md)),[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180] [Kuwait](7515890.md),[^cite_note-181] the Netherlands ([Province of Zuid-Holland](28687.md)),[^cite_note-182] and Russia.[^cite_note-183]

The region can be broadly divided into three economic areas: Milan, where the services sector comprises 65.3% of employment; the provinces of [Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varese), Como, [Lecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecco), [Monza and Brianza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Monza_and_Brianza), Bergamo and Brescia, the latter having the highest value added in industry in Europe,[^cite_note-205] where there is a highly industrialised economy and a rich agricultural sector; and the provinces of [Sondrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Sondrio), [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pavia), [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Cremona), [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua) and [Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lodi), where there is consistent agricultural activity and an above-average development of the services sector.

In the tertiary sector, the weight of trade and finance is significant. The [Italian Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Stock_Exchange) is based in Milan, one of the main European financial centres.[^cite_note-206] In the early 21st century, two new business districts, [Porta Nuova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Nuova_\(Milan\)) and [CityLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityLife_\(Milan\)), were built in Milan in the space of a decade, radically changing the skyline of the city. Banking, transport, communication and business services activities are also important. Milan is also a global hub for event management and trade fairs. [Fiera Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiera_Milano) operates the most important trade fair organiser in Italy and the world's fourth largest[^cite_note-Fiera-204] exhibition hall in [Rho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho,_Lombardy), were international exhibitions like [Milan Furniture Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Furniture_Fair), [EICMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EICMA), [EMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMO_\(trade_show\)) take place on 400,000 square metres of exhibition areas with more than 4 million visitors in 2018.[^cite_note-207] Milan hosted the [Universal Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair) in [1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_International) and [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2015).

### Agriculture

Main article: [Lombardia (wine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardia_\(wine\))



Cultivated fields in [Upper Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mantua)

The productivity of agriculture is enhanced by the use of fertilisers and the traditional abundance of water, which has been boosted since the Middle Ages by the construction of irrigation systems that were partly designed by Leonardo da Vinci. Lower plains are used for fodder crops, cereals (rice, wheat and maize) and sugar beet. Lombardy is one of the main European regions for rice production and together with Piedmont, produces 93% of Italian rice. Cultivation is concentrated in the provinces of Pavia (84,000 ha (210,000 acres), Milan (14,000 ha (35,000 acres)), Lodi 2,000 ha (4,900 acres) and Mantua (1,200 ha (3,000 acres)).[^cite_note-Riso_italiano,_dove_si_coltiva-101] Produce of the higher plains includes cereals, vegetables, fruit trees and mulberries. Fruits and wine are produced in upland areas such as the [Prealps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prealpi_Orobiche) and [Alps](981.md) sectors in the north.

Lombardy is a centre of animal breeding, which includes dairy cows (36%) and pigs (50%). The region's dairy industry produces 30% of Italian milk,[^cite_note-milk-208] which is used to produce different types of cheese, totalling about 4,715,130 tonnes, 36% of Italian cheese production.[^cite_note-milk-208]

A variety of cured sausages is produced in Lombardy, like Salame Milano, Salame bergamasco, Salame mantovano, Salame di Varzi, Bastardei, Salam casalin, Salame Brianza, Salame pancettato.

Lombardy ranks 9 of 20 in the production of DOC and DOCG wines with 877,351 hl (19,299,024.00 imp gal; 23,177,161.43 US gal).[^cite_note-Feder-209] Lombardy also produces still red, white and [rosé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ros%C3%A9) wines made from a variety of [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_grape_varieties), including [Nebbiolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebbiolo) wines in the [Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina) region and [Trebbiano di Lugana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebbiano_di_Lugana) white wines produced with the *Chiaretto*-style rosé along the shores of [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda). ### Fashion

Main article: [Fashion in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_in_Milan)



[Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prada) shop at [Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Vittorio_Emanuele_II) in [Milan](36511.md)

Milan is a [fashion capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_capital) of the world. Lombardy has always been an important centre for silk and textile production, notably the cities of Pavia, [Vigevano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigevano) and [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona). Milan is one of the fashion capitals of the world; the city has approximately 12,000 companies, 800 showrooms and 6,000 sales outlets; the city hosts the headquarters of global fashion houses. The best-known high-class shopping district is [Quadrilatero della moda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilatero_della_moda).

In 2009, Milan was regarded as the world fashion capital, surpassing New York, Paris and London.[^cite_note-languagemonitor.com-230] Most of the major Italian fashion brands, such as [Luxottica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxottica), [Valentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_SpA), [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace), [Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prada), [Armani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armani), [Dolce & Gabbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_&_Gabbana) and [Zegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zegna) are currently headquartered in Milan. Buttons are manufactured in the industrial districts of [Grumello del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumello_del_Monte) and [Palosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palosco).[^cite_note-231]

### Furniture

Furniture is manufactured in the industrial district around [Brianza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brianza), which has an annual turnover of about €2 billion from 1,700 companies.[^cite_note-brianza-232] The furniture factories, which have about 40,000 employees, are mainly concentrated in [Lissone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lissone), [Meda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meda,_Lombardy), [Cantù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cant%C3%B9) and [Mariano Comense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Comense). This district has close relations with Milan's design industry. A number of large furniture exhibitions take place in Milan, including "Salone del Mobile Milano".

### Rail



[Milano Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Centrale_railway_station) is the second-busiest railway station in Italy for passenger flow

[^cite_note-269]

&#x20;and the largest railway station in Europe by volume.

[^cite_note-unitremilano-270]

The Lombardy railway network has 428 stations and extends for approximately 2,000 km (1,200 mi).[^cite_note-271] The network is mainly managed by [RFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rete_Ferroviaria_Italiana); 320 km (200 mi) of railway lines are under concession to [Ferrovienord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovienord),[^cite_note-272] while the [Parma](24231.md)-[Suzzara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzzara) and Suzzara-[Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara) lines, although partially falling within Lombardy (55 km (34 mi) and 11 stations), are under concession to [Ferrovie Emilia Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_Emilia_Romagna).[^cite_note-273]

The national and international railway service is mainly ensured by [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia) and to a lesser extent by [Nuovo Trasporto Viaggiatori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuovo_Trasporto_Viaggiatori), [TGV](57212.md) and [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn). The regional territory has three [high-speed lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail): [Turin–Milan high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin%E2%80%93Milan_high-speed_railway), [Milan–Bologna high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93Bologna_high-speed_railway) and [Milan–Verona high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93Verona_high-speed_railway).[^cite_note-274]

Milan is the core of Lombardy's regional train network. The regional service is the responsibility of [Trenord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenord), a company founded on 3 May 2011 and jointly owned by the [Ferrovie Nord Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_Nord_Milano) and Trenitalia, which operates on both the RFI and Ferrovie Nord Milano networks, which have the [Milan Passerby railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Passerby_Railway) as their main interconnection hub.[^cite_note-275] The [Trenitalia Tper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia_Tper) company operates on the FER network.

[Milano Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Centrale_railway_station), with 110 million passengers per year, is the largest and [eighth busiest railway station in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_railway_stations_in_Europe) and the second busiest in Italy after [Roma Termini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini).[^cite_note-grandistazioni-276] [Milano Cadorna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Cadorna_railway_station) and [Milano Porta Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Porta_Garibaldi_railway_station) stations are respectively the seventh and the eleventh busiest stations in Italy.[^cite_note-grandistazioni-276] Milano Centrale railway station is the largest railway station in Europe by volume.[^cite_note-unitremilano-270]

As of September 2025, the [Milan suburban railway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_suburban_railway_service), operated by [Trenord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenord), comprises 12 [S lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-train) connecting the metropolitan area with the city centre, with possible transfers to all the metro lines. Most S lines run through the [Milan Passerby railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Passerby_Railway), commonly referred to as "il Passante" and served by double-decker trains every 4/8 minutes in the central underground section.[^cite_note-277]

[^cite_note-285]

The cities of Milan and Brescia have [underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) systems. Milan has the most extensive metro network in Italy, with 5 lines in operation ([M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_1), [M2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_2), [M3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_3), [M4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_4) and [M5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro_Line_5)). [Milan Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Metro) has a daily ridership of 1.15 million,[^cite_note-rep3sep-286] the largest in Italy as well as one of the largest in Europe. It is operated by [Azienda Trasporti Milanesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azienda_Trasporti_Milanesi).

The architectural project of the Milan Metro, created by [Franco Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Albini) and [Franca Helg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franca_Helg), and the signs, designed by [Bob Noorda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Noorda), received the [Compasso d'Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compasso_d'Oro) award in 1964.[^cite_note-287] Within the [European Union](9317.md) it is the seventh largest network in terms of kilometres.[^cite_note-288]

### Navigation



Car ferry across [Lake Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) from [Verbania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbania) ([Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont)) to [Laveno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laveno) (Lombardy). In the background [Monte Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Rosa) mountain chain and Verbania

Even though it is not surrounded by the sea, the region has a naval system that develops on the lakes, along the rivers and [Navigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigli). The most important waterway system in Lombardy is part of the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley)-[Veneto](43780.md) one which allows navigation from [Casale Monferrato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casale_Monferrato) to [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) along the [Po river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_river).[^cite_note-292] In this waterway system, the most important ports in Lombardy are those of [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona)[^cite_note-293] and [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua).[^cite_note-294]

Navigation on the lakes has a predominantly tourist function and takes place regularly on scheduled routes. The scheduled shipping routes cover 460 km (290 mi) and are frequented by over 10 million travellers annually.[^cite_note-295] The connections are managed by the [Gestione Governativa Navigazione Laghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestione_Governativa_Navigazione_Laghi).
