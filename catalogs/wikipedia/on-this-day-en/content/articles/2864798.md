**Kramatorsk** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Краматорськ, pronounced [\[krɐmɐˈtɔrʲsʲk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e0/Uk-%D0%9A%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg/Uk-%D0%9A%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e0/Uk-%D0%9A%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg/Uk-%D0%9A%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Краматорск) is a city and the administrative centre of [Kramatorsk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramatorsk_Raion) in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) of the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) region of eastern [Ukraine](31750.md). Prior to 2020, Kramatorsk was a [city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)). Since October 2014, Kramatorsk has been the provisional seat of Donetsk Oblast, following the events surrounding the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas).[^cite_note-2] Its population is 147,145 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-3] It is an important industrial and [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) center in Ukraine. Kramatorsk is on the banks of the [Kazennyi Torets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazennyi_Torets), a right tributary of the [Siverskyi Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets).

During the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md), Kramatorsk has been hit by Russian shelling multiple times.[^cite_note-4]

## Etymology

According to Doctor of Philology Yevhen Otin from [Dnipro](989795.md) who specializes in [onomastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomastics), the name of the city comes from a toponymic phrase that has not been preserved: *Krom Torov* or *Krom Torskaya* 'border along the Tor River'. The word *kroma* means 'edge, frontier, border', and *Tor* is the old name for the [Kazennyi Torets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazennyi_Torets) River. However, it can be stated succinctly, it's most likely from the train station, the settlement was built around. The original name of the train station, *Kram-na-Tore* translates as Border-on-Tor (lit. 'on the bank of the Tor river'). Similar to the Russian city [Rostov-on-Don](317562.md), which is located at the mouth of the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River).

During the Soviet period, it was thought that the name comes from a French word "crématoire" connecting it with a local factory. Such version was proposed by a Russian linguist Vladimir Nikonov from [Simbirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simbirsk) (Ulyanovsk). However, Dr. Otin pointed out that settlement of Kramatoroka existed before establishment of any factories.

## History

### Early history

Scarce historical documents do not provide information of previous settlement in the area, but archaeological excavations in the area show that it was populated. In the second half of the 17th and early 18th centuries, the area in which Kramatorsk was to develop was heavily populated by Cossacks from the [Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate), particularly Oril Palatinate of [Zaporizhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhian_Sich) which was liquidated in 1775.[^cite_note-eou-5] Here were finding a refuge from their landowners serfs from the southern regions of Muscovy and Mordovia. In 1767 the Tsarist government granted 10,000 [dessiatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dessiatin) to a Russian count Taranov who established a village of serfs Petrovka.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6][^cite_note-7] In 1799 it was added to [Izyum uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izyum_uyezd?action=edit\&redlink=1) (county) of [Sloboda Ukraine Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine_Governorate).[^cite_note-ukrssr-6]

Kramatorsk came into being in the second half of the 19th century when a station on the [Kursk-Kharkiv-Azov Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk-Kharkiv-Azov_Railway) was built.[^cite_note-9] The station was originally called Kram-na-Tore in 1868 but this was later contracted into Kramatorsk and the town of Kramatorsk developed around the railway station,[^cite_note-eou-5] becoming a major urban settlement in the north of [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) with several heavy machine production facilities. In 1885 in area were found deposits of chaulk, sand, sandstone, valuable clays, mineral dyes which provided construction of building materials.[^cite_note-eou-5] In 1887 Belgian investors (Stertzer and Sons) built a factory of [refractory materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refractory_materials) near Kramatorsk rail station. In 1896 the German firm "W\.Fizner and K.Hamper"[^cite_note-ukrssr-6] opened a machine factory manufacturing its products for local mining and rail industries.[^cite_note-eou-5][^cite_note-ukrssr-6] The factory was also producing its own metal. Just before the [1905 Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Russian_Revolution), during the 1901–1904 production of [cast iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast_iron) at the factory tripled and accounted for 4.5 millions of [poods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pood). Number of employed workers at the factory has doubled during that period (1900–1904) to some 1,460. The workers of the factory were peasants hired from nearby villages, while managing personnel was predominantly foreigners.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6] During the Soviet period that factory was known as the Old Kramatorsk Machine-Building Plant.[^cite_note-eou-5] A factory worker for one 11–12 hour work day was receiving around 70 kopecks to 1 ruble and 10 kopecks. A primary medical institution for the factory was a regional hospital in [Sloviansk](2533448.md) (about 15 km (9.3 mi)), while in Kramatorsk was a small local clinic with no more than 12 beds. At that time around the settlement appeared smaller iron shops that belonged to Vykov merchants. Employed at least 70 craftsmen, they were producing [plowshares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plowshare), horseshoes, shovels. The settlement accounted for two steam-powered mills, blacksmith shops, grain crusher mill.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6]

The first educational institution was opened in 1869 by the Kramatorsk rail station administration as a single grade school for children of the rail station servicemen, clerks of telegraph and post office, local police. About 20 years later a gubernatorial government established a primary 2-year parochial school which had a small library (part of the [Ministry of National Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Education_\(Russian_Empire\)) program). In 1897 the "W\.Fizner and K.Hamper" company established the 3-year factory school for children of the administration and skilled workers.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6]

### 20th century

Already in spring of 1903, the Old Kramatorsk Machine-Building Plant came under influence of the [Russian Social Democratic Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Social_Democratic_Labour_Party) (RSDLP) of [Kharkiv](2788558.md) and Ekaterinoslav ([Dnipro](989795.md)) party cells and at the factory was organized first "revolutionary" group. The group had intention to join the political strike that took place in summer of 1903 at the Russian South (including today's Ukraine), but in August members of the group were all arrested. In spring of 1904 RSDLP established its party cell within the town of Kramatorsk itself. In mid February 1905 the RSDLP organized a short strike that ended after arrival of couple of companies of the [Lebedyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedyn) regiment. Some 600 workers were fired right away. In the beginning of 1905 Kramatorsk accounted for over 12,000 residents.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6]

Later that year RSDLP organized scandal at the factory turning prayer for the [October Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Manifesto) into an unsanctioned gathering which called for armed uprising. During that time, at the factory was organized a local council of workers' deputies, a soviet (before [Bolshevization of the Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevization_of_the_Soviets)). Under influence of [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks), in 1905 a renegade [Bolshevik Military Organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik_Military_Organizations) was established in Kramatorsk which organized the purchase of arms. There also was established a communication with the Kharkiv Locomotive Factory council of workers' deputies. On 12 December 1905 there was a huge strike at the factory calling for overthrowing the Russian [Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar) ([Nicholas II of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia)) and talked about the need to provide the peasants with land. In 1906 Kramatorsk was visited by members of the Central Committee of the [Russian Social Democratic Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Social_Democratic_Labour_Party), while financially they were supported by the Ekaterinoslav party headed by [Grigory Petrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Petrovsky). On 11 June 1906 Kramatorsk joined the Bolshevik mutiny which included factories of [Yuzivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzivka), [Kostiantynivka](2879020.md), [Druzhkivka](6862714.md), [Horlivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horlivka) factories and accounted for some 3,000 workers calling for armed uprising and support of peasants for their rights on land. The demonstrators marched to the neighboring village of Petrovka. The [Russian Minister of Internal Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_interior_ministers_of_Russia) [Pyotr Stolypin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Stolypin) was calling the [Kiev Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Military_District) commander to immediately dispatch a military detachment to Izyum uyezd (county) and Kramatorsk, in particular. The Kramatorsk factory was forced to be closed and workers were released, while those who participated in the mutiny were prosecuted.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6] Later the factory was reopened.

The town of Kramatorsk gave a jump start to a political career of Ukrainian Bolshevik [Vlas Chubar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlas_Chubar) who organized at the factory a school of political propaganda. Following the [Lena massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_massacre), on 1 May 1912 Chubar organized protest with red banners which gathered some 2,000 workers. Many participants were fired, while some were sent to military service. Vlas Chubar was imprisoned in the Bakhmut jail ([Bakhmut](3889507.md)). During that time Bolsheviks were carrying out a fundraising at the factory for their "[Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravda)" newspaper (est. 5 May 1912). A very close relations local Bolsheviks kept with a member of the Russian [State Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Duma_\(Russian_Empire\)) from [Kharkov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_Governorate) and a local Bolshevik from the [Poltava](38581.md)'s suburbs [Matvei Muranov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matvei_Muranov) who visited Kramatorsk in 1913.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6]

Before [World War I](4764461.md), the town remained an unorganized workers' settlement.[^cite_note-ukrssr-6] It accounted for some 4,000 residents (1914).[^cite_note-eou-5]

In April 1918 troops loyal to the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic) took control of Kramatorsk.[^cite_note-10]



Mass grave of local victims of World War II

During [World War II](32927.md), it was occupied by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) between 20 October 1941 and 6 February 1942 and again between 27 February 1942 and 6 September 1943.[^cite_note-EMU-11]

Between 1980 and 1989, several people [were exposed to a radiological source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramatorsk_radiological_accident) in one of the apartment buildings, resulting in 6 deaths and at least 17 cases of [radiation sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_sickness).

### Russo-Ukrainian War

Further information: [Battle of Kramatorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kramatorsk), [February 2015 Kramatorsk rocket attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2015_Kramatorsk_rocket_attack), [Kramatorsk railway station attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramatorsk_railway_station_attack), and [2023 Kramatorsk restaurant missile strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Kramatorsk_restaurant_missile_strike)

On 12 April 2014, at the same time as [events in nearby Sloviansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sloviansk), the police station in Kramatorsk [was seized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kramatorsk) by armed pro-Russian militants in military uniform, and later the city council was too.[^cite_note-12] Kramatorsk remained under pro-Russian separatist control, until July 2014. There would be a series of incidents in those three months.[^cite_note-13] Located only 13 km from [Sloviansk](2533448.md), which became a separatist stronghold, Kramatorsk would also be under separatist control, yet in a different situation. Unlike Sloviansk, which had high profile figures [Vyacheslav Ponomarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Ponomarev_\(public_figure\)) and [Igor Girkin/Strelkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Girkin) associated with it, Kramatorsk was not notable in this sphere. Further, while the Ukrainian army did not enter Sloviansk in its months under separatist control, they made several entries into Kramatorsk in this time.[^cite_note-14] On 5 July, after almost three months, Ukrainian forces finally retook control of Kramatorsk, as part of sweeping territorial gains at that time, including nearby [Sloviansk](2533448.md).[^cite_note-15] In October 2014, Kramatorsk was made the provisional [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of Donetsk Oblast after the administration relocated from [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol). On 10 February 2015, Kramatorsk was [shelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2015_Kramatorsk_rocket_attack) by pro-Russian forces, leaving 17 people dead and 60 injured.[^cite_note-16]



Destructions after a Russian missile strike on 25 August 2024

On 8 April 2022, during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Kramatorsk was subjected to missile strikes.[^cite_note-17] The Kramatorsk railway station [was hit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramatorsk_railway_station_attack) by [Tochka-U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTR-21_Tochka) missiles which killed at least 57 people and wounded at least 109 others.[^cite_note-18] [Pavlo Kyrylenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Kyrylenko), the governor of the Donetsk Oblast, said thousands of people had been at the station at the time the two missiles struck. On 19 April 2022, Russian troops launched rocket attacks on Kramatorsk, as a result of which one person was killed and three were injured.[^cite_note-19] On 21 April 2022, British Defence Ministry intelligence reported that Russian troops in the Donetsk Oblast were advancing towards Kramatorsk.[^cite_note-20] However, in early September 2022, Ukraine launched [a major counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kharkiv_counteroffensive), regaining several settlements in the Kharkiv Oblast. This relieved the pressure on Kramatorsk with the recapture of [Lyman](6862925.md) by Ukrainian forces on 1 October 2022.

On 27 June 2023, [a Russian missile strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Kramatorsk_restaurant_missile_strike) killed at least 11 people and injured over 60 in a pizza restaurant in the center of the city.[^cite_note-Sky_News-21]

As part of broader offensive efforts in Donetsk Oblast, Russian forces advanced in direction of Kramatorsk, making it a frontline city. On 10 September 2025, Ukrainian authorities ordered the evacuation of children and their guardians from some areas in and around the city, citing increased drone activity.[^cite_note-22]

#### Transportation

Between 1937 and 1 August 2017, Kramatorsk had a tram network. However, it is now closed and public transportation is provided by buses and trolley-buses.[^cite_note-26]

## Healthcare

The city has 18 medical and preventive care facilities, employing 634 doctors and 1,750 nursing staff. The hospital bed ratio is 85.7 per 10,000 residents, with a total of 1,730 beds. The occupancy rate per bed is 338.5 days (2007).

The main causes of mortality are circulatory diseases (75.8% of the total number of deaths in the city), neoplasms (17.4%), and gastrointestinal diseases (2.9%). Neoplasms are the leading cause of death among the working-age population.

The infant mortality rate per 1,000 live births was: 11.6 in 2005; 12.7 in 2006; 2007—10.7. (The regional average is over 13.9).

From 1996 to November 2008, 836 HIV-positive individuals were identified in the city, 88 of whom were children. 179 infected individuals were diagnosed with AIDS. During this time, 229 HIV-infected individuals died in the city, 136 of whom were due to AIDS. 91.6% of those infected were aged 18 to 49. Over the past year, the number of blood-borne infections has decreased (from 49% to 45%), while sexually transmitted infections have increased (from 41% to 45%).

## Sports



Ice Arena



Prapor Stadium



[Motocross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motocross) in Kutovaya Balka

The first sports society was founded in 1912.

The city has two stadiums: the [Bliuminh Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bliuminh_Stadium) (1937, 1956, owned by NKMZ) in Pushkin Park with a grandstand capacity of 8,055, and the [Prapor Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prapor_Stadium_\(Kramatorsk\)) in Bernatsky Garden (1936, 1968, owned by SKMZ) with a capacity of 6,000. The largest indoor football pavilion in Ukraine was also built in Bernatsky Garden.

The football team [FC Kramatorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kramatorsk) played at the "Prapor" stadium.[^cite_note-28] The [VVS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VVS_\(football_club,_Kramatorsk\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) club was also previously based in the city.

Kramatorsk has two swimming pools: one in the NKMZ Palace of Culture (built in 1963, the first in the Donetsk region[^cite_note-29]) and one in the sports complex of Vocational School No. 28.

An annual [motocross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motocross) race is held in Kutova Balka on City Day in September.

A new ice rink was officially opened in 2021. Since July 2021, the city has been home to two hockey teams: HC Kramatorsk and [HC Donbass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC_Donbass).
