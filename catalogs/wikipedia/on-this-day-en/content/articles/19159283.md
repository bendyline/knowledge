**Seoul**,[^cite_note-9] officially **Seoul Special Metropolitan City**,[^cite_note-11] is the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of [South Korea](27019.md), and the largest city in the whole of [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea). The broader [Seoul Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Area), encompassing Seoul, [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md) and [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon),[^cite_note-12] emerged as the world's [sixth largest metropolitan economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP) in 2022, behind [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), [Tokyo](354951.md), [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles), [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_metropolitan_area), and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_metropolitan_area), and hosts more than half of South Korea's population. Although Seoul's population peaked at over 10 million, it has gradually decreased since 2014, standing at about 9.6 million residents as of 2024.[^cite_note-4-population-4]

Seoul's history traces back to 18 BC when it was founded by the people of [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje), one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea. During the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) dynasty, Seoul was officially designated as the capital, surrounded by the Fortress Wall of Seoul. In the early 20th century, Seoul was occupied by the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), temporarily renamed "[Keijō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keij%C5%8D)" ("Gyeongseong" in Korean). The [Korean War](16772.md) brought fierce battles, with Seoul changing hands four times and leaving the city mostly in ruins. Nevertheless, the city has since undergone significant reconstruction and rapid urbanization.

The *de facto* administrative capital has gradually switched to the [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_city) of [Sejong City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejong_City), which opened in 2012[^cite_note-13] and is located about 121 kilometers further south.[^cite_note-14] As of 2019, Sejong hosts 10 out of the [South Korean government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_government)'s 18 ministries.[^cite_note-15] Despite this, the [Constitutional Court of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Korea) declared in 2004 that Seoul must remain the national capital due to it being an "unwritten constitutional custom", unless otherwise changed by a nationwide referendum.[^cite_note-16]

Seoul was rated Asia's most livable city, with the second-highest quality of life globally according to [Arcadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadis) in 2015[^cite_note-Arcadis2015-19] and a GDP per capita (PPP) of approximately \$40,000.[^cite_note-GDPinfo-22] 15 [*Fortune* Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) companies, including industry giants such as [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung),[^cite_note-23] [LG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG), and [Hyundai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_Group), are headquartered in the Seoul Capital Area, which has major technology hubs, such as Gangnam and Digital Media City.[^cite_note-24] Seoul is ranked seventh in the [Global Power City Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) and the [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index), and is one of the five leading hosts of global conferences.[^cite_note-25] The city has also hosted major events such as the [1986 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Asian_Games), the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics), and the [2010 G20 Seoul summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_G20_Seoul_summit), in addition to three matches at the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup).

Seoul is geographically set in a mountainous and hilly terrain, with [Bukhansan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhansan) positioned on its northern edge. Within the Seoul Capital Area lie five UNESCO World Heritage Sites: [Changdeokgung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changdeokgung), [Hwaseong Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_Fortress), [Jongmyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jongmyo), [Namhansanseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namhansanseong), and the [Royal Tombs of the Joseon dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Tombs_of_the_Joseon_dynasty).[^cite_note-26] Furthermore, Seoul has witnessed a surge in modern architectural development, with iconic landmarks including the [N Seoul Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_Seoul_Tower), the [63 Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63_Building), the [Lotte World Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_World_Tower), the [Dongdaemun Design Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongdaemun_Design_Plaza), [Lotte World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_World), the [Trade Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_Tower), [COEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfield_COEX_Mall), [IFC Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Finance_Center_Seoul), and [Parc1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc1). Seoul was named [World Design Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Design_Capital) 2010 and has served as the national hub for the music, entertainment, and cultural industries that have propelled [K-pop](629945.md) and the [Korean Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Wave) to international prominence.

## Toponymy

Main article: [Names of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Seoul)

Traditionally, *seoul* (서울) has been a native Korean (as opposed to [Sino-Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Korean_vocabulary)) [common noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_noun) simply meaning 'capital city.' The word *seoul* is believed to have descended from *Seorabeol* (서라벌; historically transliterated into the Hanja form 徐羅伐), which originally referred to [Gyeongju](426047.md), the capital of [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

[Wiryeseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiryeseong) (위례성; 慰禮城), the capital settlement of [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje), was located within the boundaries of modern-day Seoul. Seoul was also known by other various historical names, such as Bukhansan-gun (북한산군; 北漢山郡, during the [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo) era), Namcheon (남천; 南川,[^cite_note-Jinheung_Taewang_Stele_Seoul-29] during the Silla era), [Hanyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Seoul) (한양; 漢陽, during the [Northern and Southern States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_States_period)), Namgyeong (남경; 南京, during the [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo) era), and Hanseong (한성; 漢城, during the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) era). The word *seoul* was used colloquially to refer to the capital as early as the 17th century.[^cite_note-30] Thus, the Joseon capital of *Hanseong* was widely referred to as the *seoul.*[^cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-31] Due to its common usage, French missionaries called the Joseon capital *Séoul* (/se.ul/) in their writings, hence the common romanization *Seoul* in various languages today.

Under subsequent [Japanese colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), *Hanseong* was renamed *Keijō* (京城, literally 'capital city')[^cite_note-32] by the Imperial authorities to prevent confusion with the [hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja) [漢](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%BC%A2) (a transliteration of a native Korean word 한; *han*; lit. 'great'), which may also refer to the [Han people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) or the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) in Chinese and is associated with China in Japanese context.[^cite_note-33] After [World War II](32927.md) and the [liberation of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Korea), *Seoul* became the official name for the Korean capital. The [Standard Korean Language Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Korean_Language_Dictionary) still acknowledges both common and proper noun definitions of *seoul*.[^cite_note-34]

Unlike most place names in Korea, as it is not a Sino-Korean word, *Seoul* has no inherently corresponding Hanja ([Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters) used in the Korean language). Instead of [phonetically transcribing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_into_Chinese_characters) *Seoul* to Chinese, in the [Chinese-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinophone) world, Seoul was called *Hànchéng* (汉城; 漢城), which is the Chinese pronunciation of *Hanseong*. On 18 January 2005, the [Seoul Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Government) changed Seoul's official Chinese name from the historic *Hànchéng* to *Shǒu'ěr* (首尔; 首爾). *Shǒu'ěr* is a [phono-semantic match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phono-semantic_matching) incorporating both sound and meaning (through 首 meaning 'head', 'chief', 'first').[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## History

Main article: [History of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Seoul)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Seoul).

### Prehistory



Reconstructed *umjip* houses at [Amsa-dong Prehistoric Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsa-dong_Prehistoric_Site)

There is evidence of human habitation in the area now corresponding to Seoul from 30,000 to 40,000 years before the present.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._2201513-37] Around 4,000 B.C., people of the area lived in huts with lowered floors called *[umjip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umjip)*. There is evidence of the consumption of cooked grain and fish by 3,000 B.C. Around 1,500 B.C., communities began transitioning into the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and farming at scale.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._22015122–123,_155-38]

Due to modern Seoul's significant urbanization, [Amsa-dong Prehistoric Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsa-dong_Prehistoric_Site) is the only known major archaeological site in Seoul where [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) materials have been found,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._2201571,_121-39] although such materials have also been found in minor sites throughout the city (and all around the surrounding [Han River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Korea\)) basin),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._2201577-40] often through [rescue archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_archaeology).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._2201513–16,_70-41]

### Samhan and Baekje periods

See also: [Samhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhan) and [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje)

Around the collapse of [Wiman Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiman_Joseon) (194–108 B.C.) in the northern part of Korea, numerous refugees went south to the Han River basin, which was then controlled by [Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_\(Korean_state\)) (4th–2nd century B.C.). These diverse peoples brought with them culture and technology of the [Chinese Warring States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period) that accelerated the region's progress into the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). Their arrival destabilized the region; Jin disintegrated, and dozens of [statelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate) emerged that competed for influence in the Han River basin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._3201549–52,_114-42]

Baekje (18 B.C. – 660 A.D.), once one of the statelets in the [Mahan confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahan_confederacy), became the dominant local power by the 2nd century A.D.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._3201552–53,_66,_68–69-43] Its capital was in [Wiryeseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiryeseong); Wiryeseong specific location is not known with certainty, but it is believed to have been within the bounds of the ramparts [P'ungnapt'osŏng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pungnaptoseong) and [Mongchontoseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongchontoseong).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._32015114-44] This area is now in southeastern Seoul.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeoul:_A_2,000-Year_History_Vol._32015136-45][^cite_note-46]

### Silla period

In July or August 553, [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla) took the control of the region from Baekje, and the city became a part of newly established Sin Province (신주; 新州).[^cite_note-Samguk_Sagi_Silla_Jinheung_19-47][^cite_note-Samguk_Sagi_Baekje_Seong_19-48] Sin (新) has both meaning of "New" and "Silla", thus literally means New Silla Province.

In November 555, Jinheung Taewang made a royal visit to Bukhansan, and inspected the frontier.[^cite_note-49] In 557, Silla abolished Sin Province, and established Bukhansan Province (북한산주; 北漢山州).[^cite_note-50] The word Hanseong (한성; 漢城; lit. 'Han Fortress') appears on the stone wall of "Pyongyang Fortress", which was presumably built in the mid to late 6th century AD over period of 42 years, located in Pyongyang, while there is no evidence that Seoul had name Hanseong dating the three kingdoms and earlier period.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

In 568, Jinheung Taewang made another royal visit to the northern border, visited Hanseong, and stayed in Namcheon on his way back to the capital. During his stay, he set Jinheung Taewang Stele, abolished Bukhansan Province, and established Namcheon Province (남천주; 南川州; lit. 'South River Province'), appointing the city as the provincial capital.[^cite_note-Jinheung_Taewang_Stele_Seoul-29][^cite_note-56] Based on the naming system, the actual name of Han River during this time was likely Namcheon (Nam River) itself or should have the word ending with "cheon" (천; 川) not "gang" (강; 江) nor "su" (수; 水). In addition, "Bukhansan" Jinheung Stele clearly states that Silla had possession of Hanseong (modern day [Pyongyang](47948.md)), thus Bukhansan has to be located north of Hanseong. Modern day Pyongyang was not Pyongyang, [Taedong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taedong_River) was likely Han River, and Bukhansan was not Bukhansan during the three kingdoms period.[^cite_note-Jinheung_Taewang_Stele_Seoul-29][^cite_note-57] Moreover, Pyongyang was a common noun meaning capital used by Goguryeo and Goryeo dynasties, similar to Seoul.[^cite_note-58]

In 603, Goguryeo attacked [Bukhansanseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhansanseong), which Silla ended up winning.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In 604, Silla abolished Namcheon Province, and reestablished Bukhansan Province in order to strengthen the northern border. The city lost its provincial capital position and was put under Bukhansan Province once again.[^cite_note-61] This further proves that Bukhansan was located in the North of modern-day Pyongyang as changing the provincial name and objective would not be required if Bukhansan was located within Seoul.

In the 11th century [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo), which succeeded [Unified Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Silla), built a summer palace in Seoul, which was referred to as the "Southern Capital". It was only from this period that Seoul became a larger settlement.[^cite_note-Britannica-62]

### Joseon dynasty

Further information: [Joseon dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon_dynasty), [Downtown Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seoul), and [Seongjeosimni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongjeosimni)



The [Fortress Wall of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul)



[Seongjeosimni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongjeosimni) was included in the five administrative divisions of [Hanseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Seoul), in the late period of the Joseon dynasty

Seoul became the [planned capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_purpose-built_national_capitals) of Korea by [Yi Seong-gye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Seong-gye), the founding father of the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) dynasty. After enthroning himself as [King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Joseon) at the capital of old [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo) in 1392, Yi Seong-gye changed the name of his Kingdom from Goryeo to Joseon in 1393 and began his search for a place for a new capital. After several governmental debates, Yi Seong-gye chose Hanyang (Sindo) instead of Muak in September 1394. As Joseon's new capital, Hanyang was planned as a geographic embodiment of [Korean Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Confucianism). Construction of the city began in October 1394. During its early construction stages, some major palaces, including [Gyeongbokgung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbokgung), were finished in 1395. The [Fortress Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul) surrounding Hanyang was partially finished around 1396.[^cite_note-63]<sup>: 96–111 </sup>

The city of Hanyang was governed by the [Hanseongbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseongbu?action=edit\&redlink=1) (한성부; 漢城府), an agency of the national government dedicated to affairs on the administration of the capital city. The Hanseongbu divided Hanyang into two major categories: areas inside the Fortress Wall, which were typically named *Seong-jung* (성중; 城中) or *Doseong-an* (도성 안; lit. 'Inside the fortress'), and areas 10 [Ri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_\(unit\)) (Korean mile) around the Fortress Wall, which were named as *[Seongjeosimni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongjeosimni)* (성저십리; 城底十里; lit. '10 Ris around the fortress'). The *Doseong-an* area later gained the informal but popular name *Seodaemun-an* (사대문 안), which literally means 'areas inside of the [Four Great Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eight_Gates_of_Seoul)', and became the one and only [downtown (city center) of Hanyang city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seoul).[^cite_note-64]<sup>: 90–100 </sup>

In the late 19th century, after hundreds of years of isolation, Seoul opened its gates to foreigners and began modernization. Seoul became the first city in [East Asia](19605700.md) to introduce electricity in the palace, which was established by the [Edison Illuminating Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_Illuminating_Company).[^cite_note-65] A decade later the city also implemented electrical street lights.[^cite_note-66]

### Korean Empire

Main article: [Korean Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Empire)



Seoul, capital city of the Korean Empire, in 1905

After [Gojong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gojong_of_Joseon)'s proclamation of Korea as the [Korean Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Empire) in 1897, Seoul was temporarily called **Hwang Seong** (황성; 皇城; lit. 'the imperial city'). Much of modern development around this era was propelled by trade with foreign countries like France and the United States. For example, the [Seoul Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Electric_Company), Seoul Electric Trolley Company, and Seoul Fresh Spring Water Company were all joint Korean–U.S. owned enterprises.[^cite_note-67] Through these efforts, there were some modernization efforts by the late 19th century prior to annexation. Seoul became the first city in East Asia to have electricity, trolley cars, water, telephone, and telegraph systems all at the same time,[^cite_note-Summer_Institute_Summaries-68]

### Japanese annexation of Korea

Main articles: [Keijō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keij%C5%8D) and [Korea under Japanese rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule)



[March First Movement](430481.md) in Seoul, 1919

After the [annexation treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Korea_Annexation_Treaty) in 1910, [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) annexed Korea and renamed the city [Gyeongseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongseong) ("Kyongsong" in Korean and "[Keijō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keij%C5%8D)" in Japanese). The city saw significant transformation under Japanese colonial rule. Imperial Japan removed the city walls, paved roads, and built Western-style buildings.

Seoul was deprived of its special status as the capital city and downsized under imperial Japan, compared to the traditional notion among people of the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) dynasty that Seoul included the area of approximately 4 km (2.5 miles) radius surrounding the [Fortress Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul) (i.e., Seongjeosimni; 성저십리; 城底十里). On October 1, 1910, Imperial Japan demoted Seoul as no different than any other city within the [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md). After Imperial Japan's redistricting, Seoul only included the area inside the [Fortress Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul) and present-day [Yongsan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_District). In the 1930s, as part of Imperial Japan's war efforts leading up to the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), [Yeongdeungpo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdeungpo_District) was annexed into Seoul on April 1, 1936, to function as an industrial complex for steel and other [metalworking](266443.md) factories.

The city was liberated by U.S. forces at the end of [World War II](32927.md).

### Contemporary history



Commemoration of the [Liberation of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Korea) in front of [Seoul Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Station), 15 August 1945

In 1945, following the liberation from Japanese colonial rule, the American military assumed control of Korea, including its capital city, then referred to as Kyeongseongbu in line with Japanese nomenclature. The [U.S. military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Military_Government_in_Korea) published the Charter of the City of Seoul in the official gazette on October 10 of the following year. The charter declared Seoul as the name of the city and established it as a [municipal corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation). Seoul's status as a municipal corporation mirrored the [independent cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)) in the United States that do not belong to any county, and Seoul was established as an independent administrative unit, separate from the existing provinces.[^cite_note-\:11-69] The Korean version of the Charter translated "*municipal corporation*" as "*special free city*" (특별자유시; 特別自由市),[^cite_note-70] which later became [special metropolitan city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_cities_of_South_Korea) (or special metropolitan city; 특별시; 特別市) in the [Local Autonomy Act of 1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Autonomy_Act_of_1949?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-71] Seoul has retained its status as the only special metropolitan city in South Korea.[^cite_note-72]

> The City of Seoul is hereby constituted a municipal corporation to be known as SEOUL. The boundaries of the municipal corporation are the present limits of the City of Seoul consisting of the following eight districts: [Chong Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jung_District,_Seoul), [Chong No Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jongro_District), [Sur Tai Moon Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seodaemun_District), [Tong Tai Moon Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongdaemun_District), [Sung Tong Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongdong_District), [Ma Po Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapo_District), [Yong San Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_District), and [Yang Doung Po Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdeungpo_District), and as such may be extended as provided by law.

— *U.S. Army Military Government in Korea*, Charter of the City of Seoul[^cite_note-\:11-69]

Seoul under the U.S. military government between 1945 and 1948 was much smaller than it is today. It only covered the [Fortress Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul), marked by the [Eight Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Gates_of_Seoul), and the districts incorporated during Japanese rule to prosecute imperial Japan's war efforts.[^cite_note-73]



[U.S. Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marines) engaged in [urban warfare](319027.md) during the [battle for Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Seoul), part of the [Korean War](16772.md), in late September 1950.

During the [Korean War](16772.md), Seoul changed hands between the Soviet- and Chinese-backed North Korean forces and the American-backed South Korean forces four times: falling to the North Koreans in the June 1950 [First Battle of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Seoul), recaptured by UN forces in the September 1950 [Second Battle of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Seoul), falling to a combined Chinese-North Korean force in the January 1951 [Third Battle of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Seoul), and finally being recaptured once more by UN forces in [Operation Ripper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ripper) during the spring of 1951.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] The extensive fighting left the city heavily damaged after the war. The capital was temporarily relocated to [Busan](68115.md).[^cite_note-Britannica-62] One estimate of the extensive damage states that after the war, at least 191,000 buildings, 55,000 houses, and 1,000 factories lay in ruins. In addition, a flood of refugees had entered Seoul during the war, swelling the population of the city and its metropolitan area to an estimated 1.5 million by 1955.[^cite_note-hamnett-76]



Bird's eye view of Seoul from the [Namsan cable car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_cable_car), 1962

Following the war, Seoul began to focus on reconstruction and modernization. As [South Korea's economy started to grow rapidly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_on_the_Han_River) from the 1960s, [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) also accelerated and workers began to move to Seoul and other larger cities.[^cite_note-hamnett-76] In 1963, Seoul went through two major expansions that established the shape and size of the present-day Seoul—barring minor adjustments to the borders later in 1973 and 2000. In August 1963, Seoul annexed parts of [Yangju-gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangju), [Gwangju-gun](323128.md), [Siheung-gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siheung), [Gimpo-gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo), and [Bucheon-gun](967682.md), expanding the northeastern borders of Seoul. In September, Seoul again annexed present-day [Gangnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangnam).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The two consecutive expansions more than doubled the size of Seoul from approximately 268 km<sup>2</sup> (103 mi<sup>2</sup>) to 613 km<sup>2</sup> (237 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-79]

After annexation, [Gangnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangnam)'s development was spurred by key infrastructure projects: the construction of the [Hannam Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannam_Bridge) (1966–1969) and [Gyeongbu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_Expressway) (1968–1970). As Seoul's population kept growing, [Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee)'s regime focused its development plans on Gangnam. The main hurdle for Gangnam's development was floods because the area is low-lying and prone to flooding. Then Seoul mayor Kim Hyun-ok ordered construction of an expressway that doubled as [embankment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embankment_\(earthworks\)), which became the present-day [Gangbyeon Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangbyeonbuk-ro). The construction started in March 1967 and completed in September of the same year. Similar projects transformed previously flood-prone areas into usable land for development. Such areas include the current [Ichon-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichon-dong), the [Banpo apartment complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banpo-dong), [Apgujeong-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apgujeong-dong) and [Jamsil-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamsil-dong).



Young people watching the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup)

Until 1972, Seoul was claimed by North Korea as its *de jure* capital, being specified as such in Article 103 of the [1948 North Korean constitution](4872690.md).[^cite_note-80]

Seoul was the host city of the [1986 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Asian_Games) and [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) as well as one of the venues of the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

[South Korea's 2019 population](27022.md) was estimated at 51.71 million, and according to the 2018 Population and Housing Census, 49.8% of the population resided in the Seoul metropolitan area. This was up by 0.7% from 49.1% in 2010, showing a distinct trend toward the concentration of the population in the capital.[^cite_note-84] Seoul has become the economic, political and cultural hub of the country,[^cite_note-Britannica-62] with several [Fortune Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) companies, including [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung), [SK Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Holdings), [Hyundai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Group), [POSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POSCO) and [LG Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Group) headquartered there.[^cite_note-85]

## Geography



Satellite picture of Seoul

Seoul is in the northwest of South Korea. Seoul proper comprises 605.25 km<sup>2</sup> (233.69 sq mi),[^cite_note-seoul1-3] with a radius of approximately 15 km (9 mi), roughly bisected into northern and southern halves by the [Han River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Korea\)). The river is no longer actively used for navigation, because its [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) is located at the borders of the two Koreas, with civilian entry barred. There are four main mountains in central Seoul: [Bugaksan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugaksan), [Inwangsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inwangsan), [Naksan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naksan) and [Namsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan). The [Seoul Fortress Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul), which historically bounded the city, goes over these mountains. The city is bordered by eight mountains, as well as the more level lands of the Han River plain and western areas.

### Parks

Main article: [List of parks in Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_in_Seoul)



[Han River Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangang_Park)

Seoul has a large number of parks. One of the most famous parks is [Namsan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_\(Seoul\)), which offers recreational hiking and views of the [downtown Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seoul) skyline, especially via its [N Seoul Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_Seoul_Tower). [Seoul Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Park,_Seoul), located in [Songpa District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songpa_District) and built to host the 1988 Summer Olympics, is the largest park. The areas near the stream [Tancheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tancheon) are popular for exercise. [Cheonggyecheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonggyecheon) also has spaces for recreation. In 2017 the [Seoullo 7017 Skypark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoullo_7017_Skypark) opened, spanning diagonally overtop Seoul Station.

There are also many parks along the [Han River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\(Korea\)), such as Ichon Hangang Park, Yeouido Hangang Park, Mangwon Hangang Park, Nanji Hangang Park, Banpo Hangang Park, Ttukseom Hangang Park and Jamsil Hangang Park. The Seoul National Capital Area also contains a [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt) aimed at preventing the city from [sprawling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) out into neighboring Gyeonggi Province. These areas are frequently sought after by people looking to escape from urban life on weekends and during vacations.

### Air quality



The Seoul metropolitan area, [Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus) [Sentinel-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-2) Satellite image taken on 3 December 2023. Air quality in Seoul's metropolitan area can be monitored in detail by the satellite.

#### Atmospheric pollution

See also: [Air pollution in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution_in_South_Korea)

[Air pollution](10934212.md) is a major issue in Seoul.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-npr-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] According to the 2016 [World Health Organization](33583.md) Global Urban Ambient Air Pollution Database,[^cite_note-who-90] the annual average [PM2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM2.5) concentration in 2014 was 24 micrograms per cubic meter (1.0×10<sup>−5</sup> gr/cu ft), which is 2.4 times higher than that recommended by the WHO [Air Quality Guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_quality_guideline)[^cite_note-guidelines-91] for the annual mean PM2.5. The [Seoul Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Government) monitors and publicly shares real-time air quality data.[^cite_note-92]



[Smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smog) and [Asian dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_dust) affecting Seoul in 2008.

Since the early 1960s, the [Ministry of Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Environment_\(South_Korea\)) has implemented a range of policies and air pollutant standards to improve and manage air quality for its people.[^cite_note-policy-93] The "Special Act on the Improvement of Air Quality in the Seoul Metropolitan Area" was passed in December 2003. Its 1st Seoul Metropolitan Air Quality Improvement Plan (2005–2014) focused on improving the concentrations of [PM10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM10) and [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide) by reducing emissions.[^cite_note-94] As a result, the annual average PM10 concentrations decreased from 70.0 μg/m<sup>3</sup> in 2001 to 44.4 μg/m<sup>3</sup> in 2011[^cite_note-95] and 46 μg/m<sup>3</sup> in 2014.[^cite_note-who-90] As of 2014, the annual average PM10 concentration was still at least twice than that recommended by the WHO Air Quality Guidelines.[^cite_note-guidelines-91] The 2nd Seoul Metropolitan Air Quality Improvement Plan (2015–2024) added PM2.5 and [ozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone) to its list of managed pollutants.[^cite_note-96]

Investment in air quality improvement between 2007 and 2020 in the order of US\$9 billion on the part of three key local authorities, namely Gyeonggi, Incheon and Seoul, delivered a clear legal framework of responsibility, publicly checkable results and a major focus on reduction of transport pollutants.[^cite_note-97] In July 2020, South Korea, then the 11th largest world economy, announced a US\$35 billion position on ending investment in coal.[^cite_note-auto-98] In November 2020, South Korea committed to a carbon-neutral economy by 2050.[^cite_note-auto-98] Between 2005 and 2021 annual concentration levels of small particulate matter (PM10) fell by 30-40 % in Seoul, whilst concentrations of larger particulate matter (PM 2.5) in the same period fell by 19% across the country and more in Seoul and Gyeonggi.[^cite_note-99]

[Asian dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_dust), emissions from Seoul and in general from the rest of South Korea, as well as emissions from China, all contribute to Seoul's air quality.[^cite_note-npr-87][^cite_note-100] Besides air quality, greenhouse gas emissions represent hot issues in South Korea since the country is among top-10 strongest emitters in the world. Seoul is the strongest hotspot of greenhouse gas emissions in the country and according to satellite data, the persistent carbon dioxide anomaly over the city is one of the strongest in the world.[^cite_note-101] Air quality is monitored by geo-stationary satellite measurements centred on Korea and its immediate neighbours.[^cite_note-102]

#### Air pollution inside the Metro system

In January 2024 [Seoul Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metro), whose passengers at the time numbered approximately 7 million a day, announced plans for extensive pollution reduction measures across the network. The target was to cut pollution to over 30% below the legal limit of 50 μg/m3. It was 32 μg/m3 by 2026. The outset actuality was 38.8 μg/m3 average concentration of pollution. Starting in 2024, ₩100 billion annually for three years was earmarked for air pollution reduction measures. These included installation of air conditioning, better ventilation systems and filters, replacement of dust-inducing gravel rail tunnel beds with concrete ones, dust-capture matting at turnstiles, and constant public readings for pollution within the system.[^cite_note-103]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Seoul)

Seoul has a [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dwa*) or [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cwa*, by −3 °C or 26.6 °F isotherm), influenced by the [monsoons](57630.md); there is great variation in temperature and precipitation throughout the year.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] The suburbs of Seoul are generally cooler than the center of Seoul because of the [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) effect.[^cite_note-106] Summers are hot and humid, with the [East Asian monsoon](4399268.md) taking place from June until July and [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) taking place any time between July and September. August, the hottest month, has average high and low temperatures of 30.0 and 22.9 °C (86 and 73 °F) with higher temperatures possible. [Heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) values can surpass 40 °C (104.0 °F) at the height of summer. Winters are usually cold to freezing with average January high and low temperatures of 2.1 and −5.5 °C (35.8 and 22.1 °F), and are generally much drier than summers, with an average of 24.9 days of snow annually. Sometimes, temperatures drop dramatically to below −10 °C (14 °F), and on some occasions as low as −15 °C (5 °F) in the mid winter period of January and February. Temperatures below −20 °C (−4 °F) have been recorded.

## Government

Main article: [Seoul Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Government)



[Seoul City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_City_Hall)

The Seoul Metropolitan Government is the local government for Seoul, and is responsible for the administration and provision of various services to the city, including correctional institutions, education, libraries, public safety, recreational facilities, sanitation, water supply, and welfare services. It is headed by a mayor and three vice mayors, and is divided into 25 autonomous districts and 522 administrative neighborhoods.[^cite_note-encyber-112][^cite_note-113]

### Administrative districts

Main article: [List of districts of Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Seoul)



Seoul is divided into 25 "*[gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_South_Korea#Gu_\(District\))*" (구; 區) (district).[^cite_note-Administrative_Districts-114] The *gu* vary greatly in area (from 10 to 47 km<sup>2</sup> or 3.9 to 18.1 sq mi) and population (from fewer than 140,000 to 630,000). [Songpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songpa_District) has the most people, while [Seocho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seocho_District) has the largest area. The government of each gu handles many of the functions that are handled by city governments in other jurisdictions. Each *gu* is divided into "*[dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_\(neighbourhood\))*" (동; 洞), or neighborhoods. Some gu have only a few *dong*s while others like Jongno District have a very large number of distinct neighborhoods. Seoul has 467 administrative dongs (행정동; 行政洞) in total.

### Education

Further information: [Education in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_South_Korea) and [List of universities in Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Seoul)



Entrance of [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University)

Compulsory education lasts from grade 1–9 (six years of elementary school and three years of middle school).[^cite_note-127] Students spend six years in elementary school, three years in middle school, and three years in high school. Secondary schools generally require students to wear uniforms. There is an exit exam for graduating from high school and many students proceeding to the university level are required to take the [College Scholastic Ability Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Scholastic_Ability_Test) that is held every November. Although there is a test for non-high school graduates, called school qualification exam, most Koreans take the test.

Seoul is home to various [specialized schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialized_school), including three science high schools, and six foreign language High Schools. Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education comprises 235 College-Preparatory High Schools, 80 Vocational Schools, 377 Middle Schools, and 33 Special Education Schools as of 2009.

Seoul is home to the majority of South Korea's most prestigious universities, including the three [SKY Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKY_\(universities\)): [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University), [Korea University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_University), and [Yonsei University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonsei_University). In the [QS Best Student Cities Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QS_World_University_Rankings#QS_Best_Student_Cities_Ranking), Seoul ranked 2nd place in 2023 and 1st place in 2025.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

## Culture

### Architecture

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Seoul) and [Architecture of South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_South_Korea)

The traditional heart of Seoul is the old Joseon dynasty city, now the [downtown area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seoul), where most palaces, government offices, corporate headquarters, hotels, and traditional markets are located. [Cheonggyecheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonggyecheon), a stream that runs from west to east through the valley before emptying into the Han River, was for many years covered with concrete, but was recently restored by an urban revival project in 2005.[^cite_note-161] [Jongno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jongno) street, meaning "Bell Street", has been a principal street and one of the earliest commercial streets of the city,[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] on which one can find [Bosingak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosingak), a pavilion containing a large bell.

Seoul has many historical and cultural landmarks. In [Amsa-dong Prehistoric Settlement Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsa-dong), [Gangdong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangdong_District), neolithic remains were excavated and accidentally discovered by a flood in 1925.[^cite_note-164]



[Gyeonghoeru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonghoeru), a royal banquet pavilion of Gyeongbokgung Palace

Urban and civil planning was a key concept when Seoul was first designed to serve as a capital in the late 14th century. The Joseon dynasty built the "Five Grand Palaces" in Seoul—[Changdeokgung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changdeokgung), [Changgyeonggung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changgyeonggung), [Deoksugung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deoksugung), [Gyeongbokgung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbokgung) and [Gyeonghuigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonghuigung)—all of which are located in the Jongno and Jung Districts. Among them, Changdeokgung was added to the UNESCO [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage) List in 1997 as an "outstanding example of Far Eastern palace architecture and garden design". The main palace, Gyeongbokgung, underwent a large-scale restoration project.[^cite_note-165] UNESCO also recognizes Seoul as a "[Design City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_Cities_\(UNESCO\))".

Seoul has been surrounded by walls that were built to regulate visitors from other regions and protect the city in case of an invasion. [Pungnap Toseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pungnap_Toseong) is a flat earthen wall built at the edge of the Han River, which is widely believed to be the site of [Wiryeseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiryeseong). [Mongchon Toseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongchontoseong) is another earthen wall built during the Baekje period that is now located inside the [Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Park,_Seoul).[^cite_note-toseong-166] The [Fortress Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_Wall_of_Seoul) of Seoul was built early in the Joseon dynasty for protection of the city. After many centuries of destruction and rebuilding, about 2⁄3 of the wall remains, as well as six of the original [eight gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eight_Gates_of_Seoul). These gates include the south gate [Namdaemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namdaemun) and the east gate Dongdaemun. Namdaemun was the oldest wooden gate until a 2008 arson attack, and was re-opened after complete restoration in 2013.[^cite_note-167]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Seoul)

Seoul has a well developed transportation network. Its system dates back to the era of the [Korean Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Empire), when the first streetcar lines were laid and a railroad linking Seoul and Incheon was completed.[^cite_note-182] Seoul's most important streetcar line ran along Jongno until it was replaced by Line 1 of the subway system in the early 1970s. Other notable streets in [downtown Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seoul) include [Euljiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euljiro), [Teheranno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teheranno), [Sejongno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejongno), [Chungmuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chungmuro), Yulgongno, and Toegyero. There are nine major subway lines stretching for more than 250 km (155 mi), with one additional line planned. As of 2010, 25% of the population has a commute time of an hour or longer.

### Bus

Main article: [Seoul Buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Buses)



Seoul buses

Seoul's bus system is operated by the Seoul Metropolitan Government (S.M.G.), with four primary bus configurations available servicing most of the city. Seoul has many large intercity/express bus terminals. These buses connect Seoul with cities throughout South Korea. The Seoul Express Bus Terminal, Central City Terminal and Seoul Nambu Terminal are located in the district of [Seocho District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seocho_District). In addition, East Seoul Bus Terminal in [Gwangjin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangjin_District) and Sangbong Terminal in [Jungnang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungnang_District) handles traffics mainly from Gangwon and Chungcheong provinces.

Seoul has a comprehensive [urban railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_rail_transit) network of 21 [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit), [light metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_metro) and [commuter](6286.md) lines that interconnects every district of the city and the surrounding areas of [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon), [Gyeonggi province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonggi_province), western [Gangwon Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangwon_Province,_South_Korea), and northern [South Chungcheong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Chungcheong_Province). With more than 8 million passengers per day, the subway is one of the busiest subway systems in the world and the largest in the world, with a total track length of 940 km (580 mi). In addition, in order to cope with the various modes of transport, Seoul metropolitan government employs several mathematicians to coordinate the subway, bus, and traffic schedules into one timetable. The various lines are run by [Korail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korail), [Seoul Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metro), [NeoTrans Co. Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NeoTrans_Co._Ltd.), [AREX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AREX), and Seoul Metro Line 9 Corporation.

### Train



[KTX Sancheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX_Sancheon)

Seoul is connected to every major city in South Korea by rail. Most major South Korean cities are linked via the [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Train_Express) high-speed train, which has a normal operation speed of more than 300 km/h (186 mph). The Mugunghwa and Saemaul trains also stop at all major stations. Major railroad stations include:[^cite_note-183]

- [Seoul Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Station), [Yongsan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_District): Gyeongbu line ([KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX)/[ITX-Saemaeul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITX-Saemaeul)/[Nuriro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuriro)/[Mugunghwa-ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugunghwa-ho))

- [Yongsan station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_station), [Yongsan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_District): Honam line (KTX/ITX-Saemaeul/Nuriro/Mugunghwa), Jeolla/Janghang lines (Saemaul/Mugunghwa)

- [Yeongdeungpo station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdeungpo_station), [Yeongdeungpo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdeungpo_District): Gyeongbu/Honam/Janghang lines (KTX/ITX-Saemaeul/Saemaul/Nuriro/Mugunghwa)

- [Cheongnyangni station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheongnyangni_station), [Dongdaemun District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongdaemun_District): Gyeongchun/Jungang/Yeongdong/Taebaek lines ([ITX-Cheongchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITX-Cheongchun)/ITX-Saemaul/Mugunghwa)

- [Suseo station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suseo_station) (HSR), [Gangnam District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangnam_District): [Suseo HSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suseo_High_Speed_Railway) (SRT)

### Airports

Seoul is served by two international airports, [Incheon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport) and [Gimpo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport).



[Incheon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport)

[Gimpo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport) opened in 1939 as an airfield for the [Japanese Imperial Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_Army) and opened for civil aircraft in 1957. Since the opening of Incheon International, Gimpo International handles domestic flights along with some short haul international flights to [Tokyo Haneda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport), [Osaka Kansai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Kansai_International_Airport), [Taipei Songshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Songshan_Airport), [Shanghai Hongqiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Hongqiao_International_Airport), and [Beijing Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Capital_International_Airport) although flights to [Osaka Kansai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Kansai_International_Airport) and [Beijing Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Capital_International_Airport) also operate from Incheon International.

[Incheon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport) opened in March 2001 in [Yeongjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongjong) island. It is now responsible for major international flights. Incheon International Airport is Asia's eighth busiest airport in terms of passengers, the world's [fourth busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_cargo_traffic) by cargo traffic, and the world's [eighth busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_international_passenger_traffic) in terms of international passengers in 2014. In 2016, 57,765,397 passengers used the airport. Incheon International Airport opened terminal 2 on 18 January 2018.

Incheon and Gimpo are linked to Seoul by [expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport_Expressway), and to each other by the [AREX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_International_Airport_Railroad) to [Seoul Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Station). Intercity bus services are available to various destinations around the country.

### Cycling

[Cycling](5931.md) is becoming increasingly popular in Seoul and in the entire country. Both banks of the Han River have cycling paths that run all the way across the city along the river. In addition, Seoul introduced in 2015 a [bicycle-sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-sharing_system) named [Ddareungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ddareungi) (and named Seoul Bike in English).[^cite_note-184]
