The **United States men's national soccer team** (**USMNT**), designated as **USA** by [FIFA](11049.md), represents the [United States](3434750.md) in men's international [soccer](10568.md), which is governed by the [United States Soccer Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation). The team has been an affiliate member of FIFA since 1914 and is a founding member of [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF), which was founded in 1961. Regionally, it is an affiliate member of [NAFU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Football_Union) in the North American Zone. From 1946 to 1961, it was a member of [NAFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Football_Confederation), the former governing soccer body in North America and a predecessor confederation of CONCACAF, and also a member of [PFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamerican_Championship#Panamerican_Football_Confederation), the former unified confederation of the Americas.

The United States has qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) twelve times. It is one of five CONCACAF teams to have advanced to the knockout stage, finishing in third place in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), reaching the quarterfinals in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), and also reaching the round of 16 in 7 editions, 6 of them since 1994. Their third place finish in Uruguay 1930 is the best result by a CONCACAF team in the competition and is the only non-European or South American team to achieve a World Cup podium finish. They returned in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), defeating [England](9904.md) [1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v_England_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\)) in the latter, but did not qualify again until [1990](157232.md). As host in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), the U.S. received an automatic berth and lost to [Brazil](149286.md) in the round of 16. They qualified for the next five World Cups, a feat shared with only seven other nations.[^cite_note-11] The United States have hosted the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), with Mexico and Canada as co-hosts. It has qualified for the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) four times, finishing as runners-up in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Confederations_Cup), defeating top ranked European champions [Spain](609825.md) 2–0 in the semifinal and losing to [Brazil](149286.md) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final), and also finishing in third place twice.

The [United States](3434750.md) has participated twenty times in CONCACAF's premier continental competition. It is the second most successful team in its confederation, winning 10 CONCACAF continental titles (seven [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) titles and three [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League) titles). It has participated five times in the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica); hosting the [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) editions, and finished fourth place twice in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario). In defunct regional competitions, the team finished as runners-up in the [NAFC Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Football_Confederation#NAFC_Championship) in [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_NAFC_Championship), and also finished as runners-up in the [North American Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Nations_Cup) as hosts in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_North_American_Nations_Cup).

## History

Main article: [History of the United States men's national soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_men's_national_soccer_team)

### Early years

A first United States national soccer team (controlled by the [American Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_Association_\(1884%E2%80%931924\)), established 1884)[^cite_note-Murray-12][^cite_note-Timeline-13] was constituted on November 28, 1885, when it played [Canada](723688.md) in the first international match held outside the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-Timeline-13] Canada defeated the U.S. 1–0 at Clark Field in the [East Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Newark,_New_Jersey) neighborhood of [Kearny, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kearny,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] A second match in East Newark the following year resulted in the U.S. defeating Canada 1–0, although neither match was officially recognized.[^cite_note-RSSSF-7] The U.S. earned both silver and bronze medals in [men's tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1904_Summer_Olympics) at the [1904 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics) in [St. Louis](27687.md) through [Christian Brothers College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Brothers_College_High_School) and St. Rose Parish, though the tournament is declared official only by the [IOC](15147.md); FIFA does not endorse tournaments held before 1908.[^cite_note-16]



The first official U.S. formation in 1916, [Stockholm Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Olympic_Stadium), [Sweden](5058739.md)

The U.S. played its first official international match under the auspices of [U.S. Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation) on August 20, 1916, at Stockholm Olympic Stadium, defeating [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) 3–2.[^cite_note-17] The U.S. fielded a team in the [1930 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Uruguay](31843.md), the first edition of the World Cup. The U.S. began group play by beating [Belgium](679716.md) 3–0, and then earned a 3–0 victory over Paraguay, with FIFA crediting [Bert Patenaude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Patenaude) with two of the goals.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In November 2006, FIFA announced that it had accepted evidence that Patenaude scored all three goals against Paraguay, and was thus the first person to score a [hat-trick](411541.md) in a World Cup.[^cite_note-21] In the semifinals, the U.S. lost to [Argentina](454699.md) 6–1 and were eliminated. There was no third place game; however, using the overall tournament records in 1986, FIFA credited the Americans with a third-place finish ahead of fellow semifinalist [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-22] This remains the U.S. team's best World Cup result, and is the highest finish of any team from outside of South America and Europe.

The U.S. qualified for the [1934 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) by defeating [Mexico](808402.md) 4–2 in Italy a few days before the tournament opened.[^cite_note-Guardian-Mexico-23] In a straight knock-out format, the team first played host [Italy](362466.md) and lost 7–1, eliminating the U.S. from the tournament.[^cite_note-24] At the [1936 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) in Berlin, the U.S. lost 1–0 to Italy in the first round and were eliminated. Italy went on to win both tournaments.[^cite_note-25]

The [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) in Brazil was the next World Cup appearance for the United States, as it withdrew in 1938 and the tournament was not held again until 1950 due to [World War II](32927.md). The U.S. lost its first match 3–1 against [Spain](609825.md), but then [won 1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v_England_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\)) against [England](9904.md) at [Independência Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independ%C3%AAncia_Stadium) in [Belo Horizonte](82198.md). Striker [Joe Gaetjens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Gaetjens) was the lone goalscorer in the match, which was called "The Miracle on Grass" and considered one of the greatest upsets in the history of the World Cup.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The U.S. were eliminated from the tournament in their third game, a 5–2 defeat to [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team).[^cite_note-28]

### 1960s–1980s

The national team spent the mid-to-late 20th century in near complete irrelevance in both the international game and the domestic sporting scene. CONCACAF had only one World Cup berth until 1982.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-Carlisle-Days-30] Playing only two matches from 1981 to 1983, U.S. Soccer targeted the [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) in Los Angeles and the [1986 World Cup](157231.md) to rebuild the national team and its fan base. The International Olympic Committee declared that teams from outside Europe and South America could field full senior teams. The U.S. finished with a 1–1–1 record in the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics) of the tournament but did not qualify for the second round, losing to Egypt on a tiebreaker.[^cite_note-31]

To provide a more stable national team program and renew interest in the [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%931984\)), U.S. Soccer entered the national team into the NASL league schedule for the 1983 season as [Team America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_America_\(NASL\)). This team lacked the continuity and regularity of training that conventional clubs enjoy, and many players were unwilling to play for the national team instead of their own clubs when conflicts arose.[^cite_note-Carlisle-Days-30] Team America finished the season at the bottom of the league, with U.S. Soccer canceling the experiment and withdrawing the national team from the NASL after one season. By the end of 1984, the NASL had folded, leaving the U.S. without a single professional-level outdoor soccer league.[^cite_note-32]

The 1986 World Cup was hosted by Mexico after [Colombia withdrew from contention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_withdrawal_from_the_1986_FIFA_World_Cup) due to economic concerns and the United States lost their subsequent bid to host. In the last game of CONCACAF qualifying for the 1986 World Cup, the U.S. needed only a tie against Costa Rica to reach the final qualification group against Honduras and Canada. U.S. Soccer scheduled the game to be played in [Torrance, California](107690.md), an area with many Costa Rican expatriates, and marketed the game almost exclusively to the Costa Rican community.[^cite_note-33] Costa Rica won the match 1–0, and kept the U.S. from reaching its fourth World Cup finals.[^cite_note-Carlisle-Days-30][^cite_note-34]

### 1990s

On July 4, 1988, FIFA named the U.S. as the host of the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) under significant international criticism given the perceived weakness of the national team and the lack of a professional outdoor league. The success of the 1984 Olympics played a role in FIFA's decision. The U.S. qualified for the [1990 World Cup](157232.md) with a [1–0 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_heard_round_the_world_\(soccer\)) against [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_national_football_team) in the last match of the [1989 CONCACAF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_CONCACAF_Championship). Mexico had been disqualified from the CONCACAF Championship for using ineligible players in a youth tournament, which allowed a chance for the U.S. to qualify for their first World Cup in 40 years.[^cite_note-35]

The team was coached by [Bob Gansler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Gansler), [Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Panthers) and U20 national team coach, in preparation for the 1990 World Cup in Italy. Two of the team's more experienced players, [Rick Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Davis) and [Hugo Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_P%C3%A9rez_\(soccer,_born_1963\)), were unavailable for selection while recovering from injuries. Rather than fill out his team with veteran professionals from U.S. [indoor soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_soccer) leagues, Gansler and his assistant Stejem Mark chose to select many younger players with better conditioning for the outdoor game, including several collegiate players such as [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Cavaliers_men's_soccer) goalkeeper [Tony Meola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Meola). The U.S. entered the tournament as major underdogs and lost all three of its group games to Czechoslovakia, Italy, and Austria. Defenders [Jimmy Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Banks) and [Desmond Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Armstrong) became the first African Americans to appear in a World Cup match for the United States.[^cite_note-36]

During the [1993 U.S. Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_U.S._Cup), a tournament designed to prepare for the upcoming World Cup, the U.S. beat England 2–0.[^cite_note-37] After qualifying automatically as the host of the 1994 World Cup under [Bora Milutinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bora_Milutinovi%C4%87), the U.S. opened the tournament schedule with a 1–1 tie against Switzerland in the [Pontiac Silverdome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac_Silverdome) in the suburbs of [Detroit](8687.md), the first World Cup game played indoors. In its second game, the U.S. faced [Colombia](680175.md), then ranked fourth in the world, at the [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)) near [Los Angeles](18110.md). Aided by an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) from [Andrés Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Escobar), the U.S. won 2–1;[^cite_note-38] Escobar was later murdered in his home country, possibly in retaliation for this mistake.[^cite_note-39] Despite a 1–0 loss to Romania in its final group game, the U.S. made it past the initial round for the first time since 1930. In the round of 16, the U.S. lost 1–0 to the eventual champion [Brazil](149286.md).[^cite_note-40] U.S. Soccer later fired Milutinović in 1995 because he was reportedly not interested in administrative duties in addition to coaching.[^cite_note-41]

The U.S. were invited to play in the [1995 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), where they finished first in their group after defeating [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) and [Argentina](454699.md) in an upset victory.[^cite_note-42] In the quarterfinals, the U.S. defeated [Mexico](808402.md) on penalties, and then lost to Brazil 1–0 in the semifinals. The United States finished fourth after losing to Colombia in the third-place match.[^cite_note-43]

In the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) in France, the team lost all three group matches, 2–0 to [Germany](250204.md), 2–1 to [Iran](272865.md), and 1–0 to the [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro_national_football_team), finishing last in the field of 32. The tournament was marred by disputes between the players and head coach [Steve Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Sampson), who resigned shortly after the tournament.[^cite_note-44]

### 2000s



[Claudio Reyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Reyna) during practice

Under former [D.C. United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.C._United) head coach [Bruce Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Arena), the U.S. qualified for the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) and reached the quarterfinals, their best finish in a World Cup since 1930. The team earned four points in the group stage, beginning with a 3–2 win over [Portugal](576328.md), followed by a 1–1 tie with co-host and eventual semifinalist [South Korea](1018627.md). The third and final match was a 3–1 loss to Poland; the team still advanced to the round of 16 when South Korea defeated Portugal. The U.S. met continental rivals [Mexico](808402.md) for the first time in a World Cup, and won the game 2–0, with goals from [Brian McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_McBride) and [Landon Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Donovan). In the quarterfinals, the USMNT met [Germany](250204.md) and lost 1–0 after being controversially denied a penalty when [Torsten Frings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsten_Frings) handled the ball to prevent a [Gregg Berhalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Berhalter) goal.[^cite_note-45] Donovan won the Best Young Player for the tournament.[^cite_note-46]

In the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), after finishing top of the CONCACAF qualification tournament, the U.S. was drawn into Group E along with the [Czech Republic](729739.md), [Italy](362466.md), and [Ghana](1029620.md). The United States opened the tournament with a 3–0 loss to the Czech Republic. The team then tied 1–1 against eventual winners Italy,[^cite_note-47] and then were knocked out of the tournament when they were beaten 2–1 by Ghana in its final group match, with [Clint Dempsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Dempsey) scoring the U.S.'s only goal in the tournament (the goal against Italy had been an own goal by Italian defender [Cristian Zaccardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Zaccardo)).[^cite_note-48] Following the tournament, Arena's contract was not renewed; his assistant, former [Chicago Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Fire_FC) and [MetroStars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroStars) head coach [Bob Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Bradley), became interim head coach in December 2006 and was selected for the full-time role in May 2007.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

After winning the [2007 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) against Mexico, the USMNT qualified for the [2009 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Confederations_Cup).[^cite_note-51] The U.S. defeated top-ranked Spain, who were on a 35-game undefeated streak, 2–0. BBC Sport journalist Saj Chowdhury referred to the victory as "one of the biggest upsets in world football."[^cite_note-52] With the win, the United States advanced to its first-ever final in a men's FIFA tournament. The team lost 3–2 to Brazil after leading 2–0 at half-time.[^cite_note-53] Hosting the [2009 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), the United States was beaten by Mexico 5–0 in the final; this defeat broke the U.S. team's 58-match home unbeaten streak against CONCACAF opponents, and was the first home loss to Mexico since 1999 and their first home loss in a competitive match since 1998.[^cite_note-54]

In the [fourth round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_fourth_round) of the 2010 World Cup qualification, [Jozy Altidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozy_Altidore) became the youngest U.S. player to score a hat-trick, in a 3–0 victory over Trinidad and Tobago.[^cite_note-55] On October 10, 2009, the U.S. secured qualification to the 2010 World Cup with a 3–2 win over Honduras. Four days later, the U.S. finished in first place in the final round of qualification with a 2–2 tie against Costa Rica.[^cite_note-56]

### 2010s



[Landon Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Donovan) at the [2010 World Cup](168079.md)

In the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), the USMNT was drawn into [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C) against [England](9904.md), [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team) and [Algeria](1029528.md). After drawing against England 1–1 and Slovenia 2–2, the U.S. defeated Algeria 1–0 with a stoppage-time goal from Landon Donovan, taking first place in a World Cup group for the first time since 1930. In the round of 16, the U.S. was eliminated by [Ghana](1029620.md), 2–1.[^cite_note-57]



[Clint Dempsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Dempsey) with the U.S. in 2011

After losing to Mexico 4–2 in the final of the [2011 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), Bob Bradley was relieved of his duties and former [Germany](250204.md) manager [Jürgen Klinsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klinsmann) was hired as head coach.[^cite_note-58] The U.S. won 1–0 in [Genoa](47332321.md), Italy on February 29, 2012, the team's first-ever win over Italy.[^cite_note-59] In July 2013, the United States became North American champions for the fifth time after winning the [Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) with a 1–0 victory over Panama in the final, with Landon Donovan winning the tournament's golden ball award.[^cite_note-60] A 4–3 victory over [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) in an international friendly match in [Sarajevo](26786.md) represented the 12th consecutive win for the USMNT, the longest active winning streak for any team in the world at that time.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] The winning streak ended September 6, when the U.S. lost to Costa Rica 3–1 in [San José](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9,_Costa_Rica) in [the final round of qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_fourth_round).[^cite_note-63] The U.S. eventually clinched a spot in the [2014 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_World_Cup).[^cite_note-64]

The Americans were drawn into [Group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G), along with Ghana, Germany, and Portugal.[^cite_note-65] The U.S. won 2–1 in their rematch with Ghana,[^cite_note-66] and then tied their second group game against Portugal 2–2. In the final game of the group stage, the U.S. fell to Germany 1–0, but moved on to the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage) on goal difference.[^cite_note-67] This was the first time that the team made two consecutive trips to the knockout stage of the FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-68] In the round of 16, the U.S. lost 2–1 to Belgium in extra time, despite goalkeeper [Tim Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Howard) making a World Cup record 15 saves[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] during the match.[^cite_note-71]

In the [2015 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), the U.S. were eliminated by Jamaica 2–1 in the semifinals, before losing to Panama on penalties in the third place match. It marked the first time the team failed to make the tournament final since 2003.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] In the 2015 [CONCACAF Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Cup) playoff to determine the region's entry to the [2017 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), the U.S. were defeated 3–2 by Mexico at the Rose Bowl.[^cite_note-74] The results led to criticism of Klinsmann's coaching style, particularly the lack of a cohesive identity.[^cite_note-75]

In June 2016, the U.S. hosted the [Copa América Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario), a special edition of the Copa América to commemorate the centenary of the tournament and its first to be played outside of South America.[^cite_note-Athletic-Copa-76] In their third Copa América appearance,[^cite_note-Athletic-Copa-76] the U.S. topped [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario_Group_A) on goal difference against Colombia despite losing to them, and then beat [Ecuador](1016797.md) 2–1 in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The team lost 4–0 to [Argentina](454699.md) in the semifinals and 1–0 to Colombia again in the third place match. The U.S. finished fourth overall, tying their best finish ever in 1995.[^cite_note-79]

Following consecutive losses to Mexico and Costa Rica in the opening games of the [final round of qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_fifth_round) for the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md), Klinsmann was removed as national team coach and technical director and replaced by previous U.S. head coach Bruce Arena.[^cite_note-80] World Cup qualification resumed on March 24, 2017, where Arena and his team achieved a record 6–0 win over [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team).[^cite_note-81]



[Jozy Altidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozy_Altidore) in 2019

The U.S. earned their third ever result in World Cup qualification at the [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md) when they drew 1–1 against Mexico. In July 2017, the U.S. won their sixth [Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) with a 2–1 win over Jamaica in the final.[^cite_note-82] Following a [2–1 defeat to Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_v_United_States_\(2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification\)) on October 10, 2017, the U.S. failed to qualify for the 2018 World Cup, missing the tournament for the first time since 1986.[^cite_note-83] Many pundits and analysts called this the worst result and worst performance in the history of the national team.[^cite_note-84]

Following Arena's resignation on October 13, 2017, assistant coach [Dave Sarachan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sarachan) was named interim head coach during the search for a permanent replacement.[^cite_note-85] The search for a permanent head coach was delayed by the [USSF presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_Soccer_Federation_presidential_election) in February 2018 and the hiring of [Earnie Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnie_Stewart) as general manager in June 2018.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] [Gregg Berhalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Berhalter), coach of the [Columbus Crew](245695.md) and a former USMNT defender, was announced as the team's new head coach on December 2, 2018.[^cite_note-auto-88][^cite_note-89]

Under Berhalter the team lost in the [2019 Gold Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_final) 1–0 against [Mexico](808402.md) and were unable to defend their title.[^cite_note-90]

### 2020s

An influx of new young talent playing for top European clubs, widely described as America's [golden generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_generation),[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] entered the national team in the late 2010s and early 2020s, including [Christian Pulisic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Pulisic), [Weston McKennie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston_McKennie), [Tyler Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Adams), [Timothy Weah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Weah), [Sergiño Dest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergi%C3%B1o_Dest), and [Gio Reyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Reyna). This new group won the inaugural [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League) in 2021 with a 3–2 victory against Mexico in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Final). A different roster won the [Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) against Mexico later that summer, contributing to a record for wins in a calendar year, with 17 wins, three ties, and two losses in 2021.[^cite_note-94]



[Christian Pulisic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Pulisic) at the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md)

The United States qualified for the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) by finishing third in the [final qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round). Grouped with England, Iran, and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B), the team advanced to the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage) after beating [Iran](272865.md) 1–0 in their critical final group stage matchup with a [Christian Pulisic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Pulisic) goal, finishing the group as runners-up with five points and without losing a game.[^cite_note-95] At the knockouts, they faced the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) and were defeated 3–1 in the Round of 16.[^cite_note-96] Midfielder [Kellyn Acosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellyn_Acosta) became the first [Asian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American) player to appear for the U.S. at a World Cup.[^cite_note-97]

After Berhalter's contract expired in December 2022, the U.S. searched for a new head coach. Under interim manager [B.J. Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.J._Callaghan) in June 2023, the United States successfully defended their Nations League trophy by winning the [2022–23 CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_CONCACAF_Nations_League). The team conceded no goals in the [finals tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals), winning 3–0 against Mexico and 2–0 against Canada in the [final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final).[^cite_note-98] Callaghan remained the team's manager for the [2023 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) the following month, which was played with a different roster. The U.S. were eliminated in the semifinals by Panama after losing a penalty shootout.[^cite_note-99]

The United States automatically qualified for the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md) as co-host in February 2023, and secured a spot at the U.S.-hosted [2024 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) by defeating Trinidad and Tobago 4–2 over two legs in [November 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_CONCACAF_Nations_League#Quarter-finals).[^cite_note-100] Gregg Berhalter was reappointed as coach of the United States on June 16, 2023.[^cite_note-101] In March 2024, the United States won their [third CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_CONCACAF_Nations_League) title by defeating Mexico 2–0 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final).[^cite_note-102] At the Copa América, the United States were eliminated in the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Group_C), with a 1–0 loss in the final group stage match against [Uruguay](679782.md) involving several controversial decisions against the Americans from referee [Kevin Ortega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ortega_\(football_referee\)), including a potentially offside Uruguay goal and handling of yellow cards.[^cite_note-103] Following the team's elimination, Berhalter was fired as head coach on July 10, 2024.[^cite_note-104]

On September 10, 2024, [Todd Boehly](35675191.md) and the USMNT's delegates agreed to an undisclosed exit fee for former [Chelsea](7473.md) manager [Mauricio Pochettino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Pochettino), and Pochettino would subsequently be announced as the new head coach of the United States, replacing Berhalter.[^cite_note-pochettino-105] In March [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Nations_League_Finals) the U.S. lost to [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team) 0–1 in stoppage time, marking the first time they've been eliminated from a [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League) title.[^cite_note-106] At the [2025 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), a newer inexperienced squad was called up rather than its regular starters due to club commitments or fatigue. The squad ultimately made it to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_final), controversially losing to [Mexico](808402.md) 2–1 despite [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball_\(association_football\)) shouts.[^cite_note-107]

The U.S. was the primary host of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) with [Mexico](808402.md) and [Canada](723688.md). In the opening group stage match against [Paraguay](804025.md), [in Inglewood](107640.md), [California](5407.md), the U.S. achieved its biggest national team win in World Cup history with a 4–1 scoreline, as [Folarin Balogun](60360233.md) scored a brace to become the first American in 96 years to have a multi-goal World Cup game since [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), and [Christian Pulisic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Pulisic) became the all-time U.S. World Cup assist leader with 3.[^cite_note-108] They won their second game against [Australia](4792249.md) 2–0, [in Seattle](11388236.md), also marking the first time the U.S. had won multiple group stage games since the 1930 World Cup.[^cite_note-109] The U.S. clinched first place in Group D after just two matches, becoming the tournament's second nation to advance to the [knockout stage](81667005.md). With their final group stage opponent [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) already eliminated, the Americans rotated 10 bench players for the final group match, narrowly losing 2–3 back in Inglewood, due to a late Turkish goal scored in stoppage time.[^cite_note-110] In the [round of 32](81667041.md), the U.S., down a man from a controversial red card to Balogun, beat [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) 2–0,[^cite_note-111] [in Santa Clara, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara,_California), breaking their national team's record of most victories at any World Cup with three wins. In the round of 16, the U.S. team was eliminated by [Belgium](679716.md) after a 4–1 defeat, once again in Seattle, repeating the eventual tournament elimination result from 2014.[^cite_note-112]

In August 2026, head coach [Mauricio Pochettino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Pochettino) signed a new contract, extending through 2030.[^cite_note-113]

## Rivalries

### Mexico

Main article: [Mexico–United States soccer rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_soccer_rivalry)

The United States and Mexico have been the most successful teams in CONCACAF and are major rivals.[^cite_note-Guardian-Mexico-23]

The two countries first met in 1934 during a [World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#Final_round) in [Rome](25458.md) that the U.S. won; Mexico won the following 24 meetings and were perennial qualifiers for the World Cup. Since 1980, the U.S. has had more wins against Mexico, particularly in home matches and major tournaments.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] They faced off in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Mexico_vs_United_States) of the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), with the United States winning 2–0.[^cite_note-123] On August 15, 2012, the U.S. defeated Mexico at [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md) in the first victory for the Americans on Mexican soil in 75 years.[^cite_note-124] On October 10, 2015, Mexico defeated the U.S. 3–2 in the [CONCACAF Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Cup) in [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)) to qualify for the [2017 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup). Thirteen months later, on November 11, 2016, El Tri defeated the U.S. in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower.com_Field) for their second consecutive victory on American soil, in [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_fifth_round) for the 2018 World Cup.[^cite_note-125] In 2021, the United States defeated Mexico three times in a calendar year for the first time, winning the [CONCACAF Nations League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final), the [Gold Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_final), and in [2022 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round).[^cite_note-126]

Ever since their first meeting in 1934, the two teams have met 79 times, with Mexico leading 38W–17T–24L, outscoring the U.S. 149–93. However, because of recent growth of [soccer in the U.S.](579105.md), since the beginning of the 21st century, the U.S. leads the series 19W–8T–11L. Either the United States or Mexico has won every edition of the [Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) except one (the [2000 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) was won by Canada).

### Canada

See also: [Canada–United States sports rivalries § Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_sports_rivalries#Soccer)

The U.S. has a secondary rivalry with [Canada](723688.md), with the rivalry stemming from a generally [friendly rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_sports_rivalries) between the two countries. The United States has historically been the stronger side, having qualified for 11 World Cups while Canada has qualified for three.

On October 15, 2019, Canada defeated the United States for the first time in 34 years, at [BMO Field](3163911.md) in [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-127] In [2022 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF\)), Canada tied 1–1 with the U.S. in [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md), and defeated the USMNT 2–0 in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario). On June 18, 2023, the United States defeated Canada 2–0 in the [CONCACAF Nations League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Nations_League_final) in [Las Vegas](47737.md).[^cite_note-128] The U.S. defeated Canada yet again in a [2023 Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup_knockout_stage) quarterfinal matchup, this time 3–2 on penalties, in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati), Ohio.

On September 7, 2024, the United States lost 2–1 to Canada in an international friendly in [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), marking Canada’s first win over the United States on American soil in 67 years.[^cite_note-ussoccer.com-129][^cite_note-130] On March 23, 2025, the United States again lost 2–1 to Canada in the [2024–25 CONCACAF Nations League third-place match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_CONCACAF_Nations_League#Third_place_play-off), giving Canada consecutive wins over the United States for the first time since 1985.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] In stoppage time, a scuffle involving players from both teams broke out after [Gio Reyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Reyna) brought down [Jacob Shaffelburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Shaffelburg) near the touchline.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

The United States currently leads the series at 17W–11T–14L.[^cite_note-ussoccer.com-129][^cite_note-135]

### Costa Rica

In the 2010s, the United States began to develop a rivalry with [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team).[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] The impetus for the rivalry was a [2014 World Cup qualifying match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_fourth_round) played on March 22, 2013, at [Dick's Sporting Goods Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick's_Sporting_Goods_Park) in [Commerce City, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_City,_Colorado) under blizzard conditions. Costa Rica filed a protest with FIFA due to field conditions after the United States won the game 1–0, but were denied recourse. The game was dubbed "[Snow Clasico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_Clasico)" because of weather conditions.[^cite_note-139] The United States have never won an away match in Costa Rica, losing 10 meetings and drawing twice.[^cite_note-140]

## Supporters

Main articles: [Sam's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam's_Army) and [The American Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Outlaws)

U.S. soccer fans, dressed in red, cheer in bleachers as they hold a large U.S. flag over themselves at a soccer match.

[Sam's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam's_Army) at a U.S. vs [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_football_team) match

There have been two main [supporter groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporter_groups) backing U.S. Soccer teams, [Sam's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam's_Army) and [The American Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Outlaws). Sam's Army started shortly after the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup)[^cite_note-141] and were active through 2014. Sam's Army members wore red to matches and sung or chanted throughout the match, and often brought huge U.S. flags and other banners to the game.

The American Outlaws began in [Lincoln, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska), in 2007 as a local supporters' group.[^cite_note-142] The group's membership attempted to address a lack of consistency from game to game in supporter organization and social events on match days.[^cite_note-143]

The U.S. men's national team has a major following on [social media](5897742.md) platforms. Interest in young American players and the attention they bring has led to an increase in foreign investment in U.S. players,[^cite_note-144] soccer development programs, and sports clubs.[^cite_note-145]

## Home stadium

See also: [United States men's national team results by home stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_soccer_team_results#Results_by_home_stadium)



The United States opening its first match at the [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in [Los Angeles](18110.md) during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)

The United States does not have a dedicated [national stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_stadium) like some other national teams; instead, the team has played its home games at various venues across the country. As of 2026, the team has played in 125 stadiums in 30 states and the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia). [Robert F. Kennedy Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Memorial_Stadium), located in the national capital of Washington, D.C., has hosted 24 matches, the most of any stadium.[^cite_note-146] The state of [California](5407.md) has hosted 123 matches, the most of any state, and [greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area) has hosted 83 matches at several venues in and around the city of LA. The [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum) hosted 20 matches from 1965 to 2000. The [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)), a 92,000-seat venue in [Pasadena](92408.md), has hosted 17 national team matches.[^cite_note-147]

As mentioned, as the main co-host of the 2026 World Cup, the team played all five of its games on the West Coast (albeit not at any venues from 1994), starting with beating Paraguay at Inglewood, California's [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md), then beating Australia at Seattle's [Lumen Field](206812.md), before losing to Turkey once again playing at SoFi, as well as beating Bosnia & Herzegovina at Santa Clara's [Levi's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi's_Stadium), and getting eliminated by Belgium back at Lumen Field.

### Media coverage

[TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Sports_\(United_States\)) has the main English language rights for U.S. Soccer broadcasts from 2022 to 2030, including all friendlies, CONCACAF Nations League group play matches and home World Cup qualifiers. All matches stream live on [Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_\(streaming_service\)) with matches also on [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_\(American_TV_network\)), [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(American_TV_channel\)) or [TruTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TruTV). [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_on_CBS_Sports) airs USMNT away World Cup qualifiers and the Nations League finals, semifinals and third place game on [CBS Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_Network) or the [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+) streaming service. [Fox Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports) holds the rights to USMNT matches in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) and [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), with matches airing on [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) or [Fox Sports 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_1). [Telemundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemundo) has the Spanish language rights to all U.S. Soccer broadcasts from 2023 to 2030. These agreements do not apply to World Cup away qualifiers, whose rights are distributed by the host country.[^cite_note-148]

## Competitive record

Further information: [Category\:United States men's national soccer team results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:United_States_men's_national_soccer_team_results)

See also: [United States men's national soccer team results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_soccer_team_results) and [United States men's national soccer team records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_soccer_team_records_and_statistics)

The United States has competed at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), the [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League), and the [Summer Olympics](27566.md). The team has also played in the now-defunct [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup), the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), as well as several minor tournaments.

The best result for the United States in a World Cup tournament came in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) when it reached the semifinals. The team included six naturalized internationals, five of them from Scotland and one from England.[^cite_note-156] In the modern era, the team's best result came in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), when it reached the quarterfinals. Its worst World Cup tournament results in were group-stage eliminations in [1990](157232.md), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup); the team failed to qualify altogether for the final tournament in [2018](13327177.md). The United States reached the round of 16 in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](168079.md), [2014](656933.md), [2022](17742072.md), and [2026](36581929.md).

In the [Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup), the United States finished in third place in both [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) and [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), and were runner-up in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup). The team appeared in its first intercontinental tournament final at the 2009 Confederations Cup,[^cite_note-157] where it lost to [Brazil](149286.md) 3–2 after leading 2–0 at halftime.

The U.S. men's soccer team first played in the Summer Olympics in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics). From that tournament to [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics), only amateur and state-sponsored Eastern European players were allowed on Olympic teams. The Olympics became a full international tournament in 1984 after the [IOC](15147.md) allowed full national teams from outside [FIFA](11049.md)'s strongest confederations of [UEFA](32332.md) and [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL). Since [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics) the men's Olympic event has been age-restricted, to those 23 years and younger, with the exception (since 1996) of up to three overage players; participation has been by the [United States men's national under-23 soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_under-23_soccer_team).

In regional competitions, the United States has won the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) seven times, with its most recent title in 2021.[^cite_note-158] The team has won three of the four [CONCACAF Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Nations_League) editions that have been played so far, including the inaugural 2021 final.[^cite_note-159] Its best finish at the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) was fourth place at the [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Copa_America) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario) editions,[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] while it competed as the host nation in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), failing to advance past the group stage.
