The **price of oil**, or the **oil price**, generally refers to the [spot price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_price) of a [barrel](594043.md) (42 U.S. gallons; 159 liters) of [benchmark crude oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmark_crude_oil)—a reference price for buyers and sellers of crude oil such as [West Texas Intermediate](797304.md) (WTI), [Brent Crude](1150532.md), [Dubai Crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Crude), [OPEC Reference Basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPEC_Reference_Basket), [Tapis crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapis_crude), [Bonny Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonny_Light), [Urals oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urals_oil), [Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus-34_Light), and [Western Canadian Select](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canadian_Select) (WCS) among others.[^cite_note-Benchmarks_2011-1][^cite_note-pricingdifferences-2] Oil prices are determined by global [supply and demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand), rather than any country's domestic production level, although the [Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries](166346.md) (OPEC) [cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel) increases oil prices.[^cite_note-s032-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Through the years



The price of oil has been affected internationally at the times of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the [2022- Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) and the 2026 Iran war.

Oil traders, Houston, 2009

Before oil, [whale oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_oil) was used in lamps, as lubrication, etc.[^cite_note-NBER-7] It was very expensive.[^cite_note-8] In 1804, its price was \$0.5/gallon or \$21/barrel,[^cite_note-NBER-7] which is \$575 per barrel in 2025 dollars.[^cite_note-9] Beginning in the 1850s, [petroleum](23195.md) quickly replaced whale oil use.[^cite_note-NBER-7]

The global price of crude oil was relatively consistent in the nineteenth century and early twentieth century.[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] This changed in the 1970s, with a significant increase in the price of oil globally.[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] There have been a number of structural drivers of global oil prices historically, including oil supply, demand, and storage shocks, and shocks to global [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) affecting oil prices.[^cite_note-BoC_2019-11]

Notable events driving significant price fluctuations include the 1973 [OPEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Arab_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries) oil [embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo) targeting nations that had supported Israel during the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War),[^cite_note-12]<sup>: 329 </sup> resulting in the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) in the [1979 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_oil_crisis), the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), and the [2010s oil glut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010s_oil_glut) that led to the "largest oil price declines in modern history" in 2014 to 2016. The 70% decline in global oil prices was "one of the three biggest declines since World War II, and the longest lasting since the supply-driven collapse of 1986."[^cite_note-Stocker_20180118-13] By 2015, the United States had become the third-largest producer of oil and resumed exporting oil upon repeal of its 40-year export ban.[^cite_note-CIA_2015-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Conflict

The [2020 Russia–Saudi Arabia oil price war](63341569.md) resulted in a 65% decline in global oil prices at the beginning of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-JPT_Jacobs_2020-17][^cite_note-OECD_2020-18] In 2021, the [record-high energy prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_global_energy_crisis) were driven by a global surge in demand as the world recovered from the [COVID-19 recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_recession).[^cite_note-Al-Jazeera-19][^cite_note-CNBC-20][^cite_note-21] By December 2021, an unexpected rebound in the demand for oil from United States, China and India, coupled with U.S. shale industry investors' "demands to hold the line on spending", has contributed to "tight" oil inventories globally.[^cite_note-22] On 18 January 2022, as the price of Brent crude oil reached its highest since 2014—\$88, concerns were raised about the rising cost of gasoline—which hit a record high in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-23]

In March 2026, international oil prices surpassed the \$100 per barrel threshold for the first time since 2022, primarily driven by the outbreak of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). On 9 March, the brent crude benchmark surged to an intraday high of \$119.50,[^cite_note-24] a 29% increase, following strikes on energy infrastructure near Tehran and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Prices subsequently moderated to approximately \$100 per barrel after G7 finance ministers announced a potential coordinated release of strategic petroleum reserves. During the crisis, Iranian officials suggested prices could exceed \$200 per barrel if hostilities continued, while the United States executive branch characterized the price spike as a short-term consequence of regional security measures.

As of June 14, 2026, the conflict in the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) has caused uncertainty in oil markets. According to The Economist magazine, refined oil prices are between 35 percent and 70 percent higher than before the war. "The International Energy Agency, an official forecaster, warns that the crunch will get much worse unless flows through Hormuz resume."[^cite_note-25]

## Structural drivers of global oil price

According to [Our World in Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_World_in_Data), in the nineteenth and early twentieth century the global crude oil prices were "relatively consistent."[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] In the 1970s, there was a "significant increase" in the price of oil globally,[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] partially in response to the [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) and [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_oil_crisis) oil crises. In 1980, globally averaged prices "spiked" to US\$107.27.[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10]



Oil production cost per barrel, by country, 2015

Historically, there have been a number of factors affecting the global price of oil. These have included the [Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Arab_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries) led by [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) resulting in the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) in the [1979 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_oil_crisis), [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) (1980–88), the 1990 [Invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kuwait) by [Iraq](7515928.md), the 1991 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), the [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis), the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), the 2002–03 national strike in [Venezuela](32374.md)'s state-owned oil company [Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. (PDVSA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA), [Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC)](166346.md), the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), OPEC's 2009 cut in oil production,[^cite_note-Mouawad-26] the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) 2010s uprisings in Egypt and Libya, and the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) (2011–24).

The 2013 oil supply glut that led to the "largest oil price declines in modern history" in 2014 to 2016. The 70% decline in global oil prices was "one of the three biggest declines since World War II, and the longest lasting since the supply-driven collapse of 1986."[^cite_note-27]

By 2015 the United States was the 3rd-largest producer of oil moving from importer to exporter.[^cite_note-CIA_2015-14] The [2020 Russia–Saudi Arabia oil price war](63341569.md) resulted in a 65% decline in global oil prices at the beginning of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-JPT_Jacobs_2020-17][^cite_note-OECD_2020-18]

Structural drivers affecting historical global oil prices include are "oil [supply shocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_shock), oil-market-specific [demand shocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand_shock), storage demand shocks", "shocks to global [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth)",[^cite_note-BoC_2019-11] and "speculative demand for oil stocks above the ground".[^cite_note-JIMF_Lutz_201404-28]

## Analyses of oil price fluctuations



Weekly reports on crude oil inventories or total stockpiles in storage facilities like these tanks have a strong bearing on oil prices.

Oil prices are determined by global forces of [supply and demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand), according to the classical economic model of price determination in microeconomics.[^cite_note-IEA_20040614-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The demand for oil is highly dependent on global macroeconomic conditions.[^cite_note-IEA_20040614-29] According to the [International Energy Agency](285292.md), high oil prices generally have a large negative impact on global [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth).[^cite_note-IEA_20040614-29]

In 1974, in response to the previous year's oil crisis, the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation) presented a new economic model of the global oil market that included four sectors—"crude production, transportation, refining, and consumption of products"—analyzed separately for six regions: the United States, Canada, Latin America, Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and Asia.[^cite_note-RAND_Kennedy_1974-33] The study listed exogenous variables that can affect the price of oil: "regional supply and demand equations, the technology of refining, and government policy variables". Based on these exogenous variables, their proposed economic model would be able to determine the "levels of consumption, production, and price for each commodity in each region, the pattern of world trade flows, and the refinery capital structure and output in each region".[^cite_note-RAND_Kennedy_1974-33]

A system dynamics economic model of oil price determination "integrates various factors affecting" the dynamics of the price of oil, according to a 1992 *[European Journal of Operational Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journal_of_Operational_Research)* article.[^cite_note-Morecroft_19920526-34]

A widely cited 2008 *[The Review of Economics and Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Review_of_Economics_and_Statistics)* article by Lutz Killian examined the extent to which "exogenous oil supply shocks"—such as the Iranian revolution (1978–1979), Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988), Persian Gulf War (1990–1991), Iraq War (2003), Civil unrest in Venezuela (2002–2003), and perhaps the Yom Kippur War/Arab oil embargo (1973–1974)"—explain changes in the price of oil."[^cite_note-Kilian_200080418-35] Killian stated that, by 2008, there was "widespread recognition" that "oil prices since 1973 must be considered endogenous with respect to global macroeconomic conditions".[^cite_note-Kilian_200080418-35]

Kilian added that these "standard theoretical models of the transmission of oil price shocks that maintain that everything else remains fixed, as the real price of imported crude oil increases, are misleading and must be replaced by models that allow for the endogenous determination of the price of oil." Killian found that there was "no evidence that the 1973–1974 and 2002–2003 oil supply shocks had a substantial impact on real growth in any G7 country, whereas the 1978–1979, 1980, and 1990–1991 shocks contributed to lower growth in at least some G7 countries."[^cite_note-36]

A 2019 [Bank of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Canada) (BOC) report described the usefulness of a [structural vector autoregressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_autoregression#Structural_VAR) (SVAR) model for conditional forecasts of global GDP growth and oil consumption in relation to four types of oil shocks.[^cite_note-BoC_2019-11] The structural vector autoregressive model was proposed by the American [econometrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrician) and [macroeconomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomist) [Christopher A. Sims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Sims) in 1982 as an alternative statistical framework model for macroeconomists. According to the BOC report—using the SVAR model—"oil supply shocks were the dominant force during the 2014–15 oil price decline".[^cite_note-BoC_2019-11]

By 2016, despite improved understanding of oil markets, predicting oil price fluctuations remained a challenge for economists according to a 2016 article in the *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, which was based on an extensive review of academic literature by economists on "all major oil price fluctuations between 1973 and 2014".[^cite_note-JEP_Baumeister_20160101-37]

A 2016 article in the [Oxford Institute for Energy Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Institute_for_Energy_Studies) describes how analysts offered differing views on why[^cite_note-OIES+Bassam_2016-38] the price of oil had decreased 55% from "June 2014 to January 2015"[^cite_note-WB_Baffes_201503-39]<sup>: 10 </sup> following "four years of relative stability at around US\$105 per barrel".[^cite_note-WB_Baffes_201503-39]<sup>: 41 </sup> A 2015 World Bank report said that the low prices "likely marks the end of the commodity supercycle that began in the early 2000s" and they expected prices to "remain low for a considerable period of time".[^cite_note-WB_Baffes_201503-39]<sup>: 4 </sup>

[Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs), for example, has called this structural shift the "New Oil Order"—created by the [U.S. shale revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_gas_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-GS_viaQuartz_20150508-40] Goldman Sachs said that this structural shift was "reshaping global energy markets and bringing with it a new era of [volatility](11930108.md)" by "impacting markets, economies, industries and companies worldwide" and will keep the price of oil lower for a prolonged period.[^cite_note-goldmansachs_20150508-41] Others say that this cycle is like previous cycles and that prices will rise again.[^cite_note-OIES+Bassam_2016-38]

A 2020 *Energy Economics* article confirmed that the "supply and demand of global crude oil and the financial market" continued to be the major factors that affected the global price of oil. The researchers, using a new Bayesian structural time series model, found that shale oil production continued to increase its impact on oil price but it remained "relatively small".[^cite_note-42]

Major benchmark references, or pricing markers, include [Brent](1150532.md), [WTI](797304.md),[^cite_note-Mabro-43] the [OPEC Reference Basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPEC_Reference_Basket) (ORB)—introduced on 16 June 2005 and is made up of [Saharan Blend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_Blend?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Algeria](358.md)), [Girassol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girassol?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Angola](701.md)), [Oriente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriente_\(Ecuador\)) (from [Ecuador](9334.md)), [Rabi Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Light?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Gabon](12027.md)), [Iran Heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Heavy?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Iran](14653.md)), [Basra Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra_Light?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Iraq](7515928.md)), [Kuwait Export](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_Export?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Kuwait](7515890.md)), [Es Sider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Es_Sider?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Libya](17633.md)), [Bonny Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonny_Light_oil) (from [Nigeria](21383.md)), [Qatar Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Marine?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Qatar](8238258.md)), [Arab Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Light?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)), [Murban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murban?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [UAE](69328.md)),[^cite_note-44] and [Merey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merey_\(benchmark\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (from [Venezuela](32374.md)),[^cite_note-45] [Dubai Crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Crude), and [Tapis Crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapis_crude) (from [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_in_Malaysia)).

In North America the benchmark price refers to the [spot price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_price) of [West Texas Intermediate](797304.md) (WTI), also known as Texas Light Sweet, a type of crude oil used as a benchmark in oil pricing and the underlying commodity of New York Mercantile Exchange's oil futures contracts. WTI is a light [crude oil](23195.md), lighter than [Brent Crude](1150532.md) oil. It contains about 0.24% sulfur, rating it a sweet crude, sweeter than Brent.[^cite_note-46]

Its properties and production site make it ideal for being refined in the United States, mostly in the Midwest and Gulf Coast regions. WTI has an API gravity of around 39.6 (specific gravity approx. 0.827) per barrel (159 liters) of either WTI/[light crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_crude) as traded on the [New York Mercantile Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mercantile_Exchange) (NYMEX) for delivery at [Cushing, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushing,_Oklahoma).[^cite_note-47] Cushing, Oklahoma, a major oil supply hub connecting oil suppliers to the Gulf Coast, has become the most significant trading hub for crude oil in North America.

In Europe and some other parts of the world, the price of the oil benchmark is [Brent Crude](1150532.md) as traded on the [Intercontinental Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Exchange) (ICE, into which the [International Petroleum Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Petroleum_Exchange) has been incorporated) for delivery at [Sullom Voe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullom_Voe). Brent oil is produced in coastal waters ([North Sea](21179.md)) of UK and Norway. The total consumption of crude oil in [UK](31717.md) and [Norway](21241.md) is more than the oil production in these countries.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] So Brent crude market is very opaque with very low oil trade physically.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Brent price is used widely to fix the prices of crude oil, [LPG](361384.md), [LNG](832128.md), natural gas, etc. trade globally including Middle East crude oils.[^cite_note-RBN_Fielden_20150324-53]

There is a differential in the price of a barrel of oil based on its grade—determined by factors such as its specific gravity or [API gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API_gravity) and its sulfur content—and its location—for example, its proximity to [tidewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidewater_\(marketing\)) and refineries. Heavier, [sour crude oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sour_crude_oil) lacking in tidewater access—such as Western Canadian Select—are less expensive than lighter, [sweeter oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_crude_oil)—such as WTI.[^cite_note-54]

The [Energy Information Administration](1704673.md) (EIA) uses the imported refiner acquisition cost, the [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_mean) cost of all oil imported into the US, as its "world oil price".

## Global oil prices: a chronology

Main article: [World oil market chronology from 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_oil_market_chronology_from_2003)



Oil prices in USD, 1861–2015. Measured are 1861–1944 averaged US crude oil, 1945–1983 Arabian Light, 1984–2015 Brent. Red line adjusted for inflation, blue not adjusted.

The price of oil remained "relatively consistent" from 1861 until the 1970s.[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] In [Daniel Yergin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Yergin)'s 1991 Pulitzer prize-winning book *[The Prize: The Epic Quest for Oil, Money, and Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prize:_The_Epic_Quest_for_Oil,_Money,_and_Power)*, Yergin described how the "oil-supply management system"—which had been run by "international oil companies"—had "crumbled" in 1973.[^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55]<sup>: 599 </sup>

Yergin states that the role of [Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries) (OPEC)—which had been established in 1960, by [Iran](14653.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Venezuela](32374.md)[^cite_note-OPEC-56][^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55]<sup>: 499 </sup>[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-NYT_Martin_19820316-58]— in controlling the price of oil, was dramatically changed. Since 1927, a [cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel) known as the "[Seven Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sisters_\(oil_companies\))"—five of which were headquartered in the United States—had been controlling posted prices since the so-called 1927 [Red Line Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_Agreement) and 1928 [Achnacarry Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achnacarry_Agreement), and had achieved a high level of price stability until 1972, according to Yergin.[^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55]

There were two major [energy crisis in the 1970s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis): the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) and the [1979 energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_energy_crisis) that affected the price of oil. Starting in the early 1970s—when domestic production of oil was insufficient to satisfy increasing domestic demands—the US had become increasingly dependent on oil imports from the Middle East.[^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55] Until the early 1970s, the price of oil in the United States was regulated domestically and indirectly by the Seven Sisters. The "magnitude" of the increase in the price of oil following OPEC's 1973 embargo in reaction to the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War) and the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), was without precedent.[^cite_note-JEP_Baumeister_20160101-37]

In the 1973 [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War), a coalition of Arab states led by [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Syria](7515849.md) attacked [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55]<sup>: 570 </sup> During the ensuing [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), the Arab oil-producing states began to embargo oil shipments to Western Europe and the United States in retaliation for supporting Israel. Countries, including the United States, Germany, Japan,[^cite_note-59] and Canada[^cite_note-Budget_1980-60] began to establish their own national energy programs that were focused on security of supply of oil,[^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55]<sup>: 607 </sup> as the newly formed Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) doubled the price of oil.[^cite_note-Yergin_1991-55]<sup>: 607 </sup>

During the [1979 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_oil_crisis), the global oil supply was "constrained" because of the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md)—the price of oil "more than doubled",[^cite_note-economist_20141209-61] then began to decline in "real terms from 1980 onwards, eroding OPEC's power over the global economy," according to *The Economist*.[^cite_note-economist_20141209-61]

The 1970s oil crisis gave rise to speculative trading and the WTI crude oil futures markets.[^cite_note-Qiang_201307-62][^cite_note-Ji_Qiang_201312-63]

In the early 1980s, concurrent with the OPEC embargo, oil prices experienced a "rapid decline."[^cite_note-NYT_Martin_19820316-58][^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] In early 2007, the price of oil was US\$50. In 1980, globally averaged prices "spiked" to US\$107.27,[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10] and reached its all-time peak of US\$147 in July 2008.

The [1980s oil glut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_oil_glut) was caused by non-OPEC countries—such as the United States and Britain—increasing their oil production, which resulted in a decrease in the price of oil in the early 1980s, according to *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*.[^cite_note-economist_20141209-61] When OPEC changed their policy to increase oil supplies in 1985, "oil prices collapsed and remained low for almost two decades", according to a 2015 [World Bank](36752.md) report.[^cite_note-WB_Baffes_201503-39]<sup>: 10 </sup>[^cite_note-worldbank_2009-64]

In 1983, the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX) launched crude oil futures contracts, and the London-based [International Petroleum Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Petroleum_Exchange) (IPE)—acquired by [Intercontinental Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Exchange) (ICE) in 2005— launched theirs in June 1988.[^cite_note-Petroleum_Science_Tian_20191201-65]

The price of oil [reached a peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_price_increase_of_1990) of c. US\$65 during the [1990 Persian Gulf crisis and war.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) The [1990 oil price shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_oil_price_shock) occurred in response to the [Iraqi invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_invasion_of_Kuwait), according to the Brookings Institution.[^cite_note-Hamilton-66]

There was a period of global recessions and the price of oil hit a low of c. \$15 before it peaked at a high of \$45 on 11 September 2001, the day of the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md),[^cite_note-NYT_2008-67] only to drop again to a low of \$26 on 8 May 2003.[^cite_note-CNCB_2016_oilfutures-68]

The price rose to \$80 with the U.S.-led [invasion of Iraq.](201936.md)

There were [major energy crises in the 2000s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000s_energy_crisis) including the [2010s oil glut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010s_oil_glut) with [changes in the world oil market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_oil_market_chronology_from_2003).



[West Texas Intermediate](797304.md) (WTI) oil prices and gas prices, 1991 to 2017

Starting in 1999, the price of oil rose significantly. It was explained by the rising oil demand in countries like China and India.[^cite_note-69] A dramatic increase from US\$50 in early 2007, to a peak of US\$147 in July 2008, was followed by a decline to US\$34 in December 2008, as the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) took hold.[^cite_note-AB_Evans_2009-70]<sup>: 46 </sup>

By May 2008, The United States was consuming approximately 21 million bpd and importing about 14 million bpd—60% with OPEC supply 16% and Venezuela 10%.[^cite_note-UPI_Westenskow_20080526-71] During the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), the price of oil underwent a significant decrease after the record peak of US\$147.27 it reached on 11 July 2008. On 23 December 2008, WTI crude oil spot price fell to US\$30.28 a barrel, the lowest since the 2008 financial crisis began. The price sharply rebounded after the crisis and rose to US\$82 a barrel in 2009.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-cnbc_2015-73]

On 31 January 2011, the [Brent](1150532.md) price hit \$100 a barrel briefly for the first time since October 2008, on concerns that the [2011 Egyptian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_protests) would "lead to the closure of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) and disrupt oil supplies".[^cite_note-BBC_2011-74] For about three and half years the price largely remained in the \$90–\$120 range.

From 2004 to 2014, OPEC was setting the global price of oil.[^cite_note-Fortune_Tully_20200430-75] OPEC started setting a target price range of \$100–110/bbl before the 2008 financial crisis[^cite_note-WB_Baffes_201503-39]<sup>: 10 </sup> —by July 2008 the price of oil had reached its all-time peak of US\$147 before it plunged to US\$34 in December 2008, during the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-AB_Evans_2009-70]<sup>: 46 </sup> Some commentators including *[Business Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Week),* the *[Financial Times](136566.md)* and the *[Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Post)*, argued that the rise in oil prices prior to the 2008 financial crisis was due to [speculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculation) in [futures markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_market).[^cite_note-Wallace2008-76][^cite_note-ftgateway_2008-77][^cite_note-washingtonpost_2008-78][^cite_note-money_2008-79][^cite_note-CFTC_2008-80][^cite_note-WTRG_Williams_nd-81]

Up until 2014, the dominant factor on the price of oil was from the demand side—from "China and other emerging economies".[^cite_note-WSJ_Yergin_20141130-82][^cite_note-macroeconomics_Ratti_20160901-83]

By 2014, production from [unconventional reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_\(oil_&_gas\)_reservoir) through [hydraulic fracturing in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing_in_the_United_States) and oil production in Canada, caused oil production to surge globally "on a scale that most oil exporters had not anticipated" resulting in "turmoil in prices."[^cite_note-WSJ_Yergin_20141130-82] The United States oil production was greater than that of Russia and Saudi Arabia, and according to some, broke OPEC's control of the price of oil.[^cite_note-Fortune_Tully_20200430-75] In the middle of 2014, price started declining due to a significant increase in oil production in USA, and declining demand in the emerging countries.[^cite_note-84] According to [Ambrose Evans-Pritchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose_Evans-Pritchard), in 2014–2015, Saudi Arabia flooded the market with inexpensive crude oil in a failed attempted to slow down US shale oil production, and caused a "positive supply shock" which saved consumers about US\$2 trillion and "benefited the world economy".[^cite_note-telegraph_EvansPritchard_20200309-85]

During 2014–2015, OPEC members consistently exceeded their production ceiling, and China experienced a marked slowdown in economic growth. At the same time, U.S. oil production nearly doubled from 2008 levels, due to substantial improvements in [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_oil) "[fracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fracking)" technology in response to record oil prices. A combination of factors led a plunge in U.S. oil import requirements and a record high volume of worldwide oil [inventories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventory) in storage, and a collapse in oil prices that continues into 2016.[^cite_note-NYT_2014-86][^cite_note-glut-87] Between June 2014 and January 2015, according to the [World Bank](36752.md), the collapse in the price of oil was the third largest since 1986.[^cite_note-OIES+Bassam_2016-38]

In early 2015, the US oil price fell below \$50 per barrel dragging Brent oil to just below \$50 as well.[^cite_note-BBC_below_50-88]

The [2010s oil glut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010s_oil_glut)—caused by multiple factors—spurred a sharp downward spiral in the price of oil that continued through February 2016.[^cite_note-telegraph_2015-89] By 3 February 2016 oil was below \$30—[^cite_note-calgaryherald_feb_3_2016_-90] a drop of "almost 75% since mid-2014 as competing producers pumped 1–2 million barrels of crude daily exceeding demand, just as China's economy hit lowest growth in a generation."[^cite_note-CNCB_2016_oilfutures-68] The [North Sea oil and gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_oil) industry was financially stressed by the reduced oil prices, and called for government support in May 2016.[^cite_note-91] According to 15 February 2016 report by [Deloitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte) LLP—the audit and consulting firm—with global crude oil at near ten-year low prices, 35% of listed E\&P oil and gas companies are at a high risk of bankruptcy worldwide.[^cite_note-G\&M_Deloitte_2016-92][^cite_note-reuters_2016_Feb16-93] Bankruptcies "in the oil and gas industry could surpass levels seen in the Great Recession."[^cite_note-G\&M_Deloitte_2016-92][^cite_note-fortune_2016-94]

The global average price of oil dropped to US\$43.73 per barrel in 2016.[^cite_note-WID_Ritchie_20171002-10]

By December 2018, OPEC members controlled approximately 72% of total world proved oil reserves, and produced about 41% of the total global crude oil supply.[^cite_note-IEA_OilPrices_2020-95] In June 2018, OPEC reduced production.[^cite_note-theguardian_Vaughan_20181112-96] In late September and early October 2018, the price of oil rose to a four-year high of over \$80 for the benchmark Brent crude[^cite_note-theguardian_Vaughan_20181112-96] in response to concerns about constraints on global supply. The production capacity in Venezuela had decreased. [United States sanctions against Iran](9941859.md), OPEC's third-biggest oil producer, were set to be restored and tightened in November.[^cite_note-economist_20180929_oil-97]

The price of oil dropped in November 2018 because of a number of factors, including "rising petro-nations' oil production, the U.S. shale oil boom, and swelling North American oil inventories," according to *Market Watch*.[^cite_note-marketwatch_Saefong_20181116-98]



[Brent](1150532.md) barrel petroleum [spot prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_price) from May 1987 to 2016, in [United States dollars](18717338.md) (USD)

The 1 November 2018 [U.S. Energy Information Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Energy_Information_Administration) (EIA) report announced that the US had become the "leading crude oil producer in the world" when it hit a production level of 11.3 million barrels per day (bpd) in August 2018, mainly because of its [shale oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_oil) production.[^cite_note-EIA_20181101-99] US exports of petroleum—crude oil and products—exceeded imports in September and October 2019, "for the first time on record, based on monthly values since 1973."[^cite_note-EIA_French_20200107-100]

When the price of Brent oil dropped rapidly in November 2018 to \$58.71,[^cite_note-marketwatch_Saefong_20181130-101] more than 30% from its peak,[^cite_note-CNN_Egan_2018121-102]—the biggest 30-day drop since 2008—factors included increased oil production in Russia, some OPEC countries and the United States, which deepened global over supply.[^cite_note-marketwatch_Saefong_20181130-101]

In 2019 the average price of Brent crude oil in 2019 was \$64, WTI crude oil was \$57,[^cite_note-EIA_French_20200107-100] the [OPEC Reference Basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPEC_Reference_Basket) (ORB) of 14 crudes was \$59.48 a barrel.[^cite_note-OPEC_20190110-103]



Movement of [WTI](797304.md) price from January 2019 to June 2020. The crash started in mid-February 2020.

On 8 March 2020, global oil prices fell precipitously when Saudi Arabia announced unexpected price cuts at the onset of the [COVID-19 recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_recession). In the face of cratering demand Russia responded in kind, resulting in a sudden [price war](63341569.md).[^cite_note-Kellogg-104] The resulting low prices represented a threat to the fiscal health of oil-exporting countries.[^cite_note-OECD_20200930-105] The IHS Market reported that the "COVID-19 demand shock" represented a bigger contraction than that experienced during the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession) during the late 2000s and early 2010s.[^cite_note-telegraph_EvansPritchard_20200309-85]

As demand for oil dropped to 4.5m million bpd below forecasts, tensions rose between OPEC members.[^cite_note-telegraph_EvansPritchard_20200309-85] At a 6 March OPEC meeting in Vienna, major oil producers were unable to agree on reducing oil production in response to the global [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-NYT_20200306-106] The spot price of WTI benchmark crude oil on the NYM on 6 March 2020 dropped to US\$42.10 per barrel.[^cite_note-businessinsider_20200306-107]

On 8 March, the [2020 Russia–Saudi Arabia oil price war](63341569.md) was launched, in which Saudi Arabia and Russia briefly flooded the market, also contributed to the decline in global oil prices.[^cite_note-108] Later on the same day, oil prices had decreased by 30%, representing the largest one-time drop since the 1991 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War).[^cite_note-WaPo_Long_20200309-109] Oil traded at about \$30 a barrel.[^cite_note-WaPo_Long_20200309-109] Very few energy companies can produce oil when the price of oil is this low. Saudi Arabia, Iran, and Iraq had the lowest production costs in 2016, while the United Kingdom, Brazil, Nigeria, Venezuela, and Canada had the highest.[^cite_note-WSJ_20160415-110] On 9 April, Saudi Arabia and Russia agreed to oil production cuts.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

By April 2020 the price of WTI dropped by 80%, down to a low of about \$5.[^cite_note-NYT_Krauss_20200331-113] As the demand for fuel decreased globally with pandemic-related lockdowns preventing travel,[^cite_note-BBC_20200420-114] and due to excessive demand for storage of the large surplus in production, the price for future delivery of US crude in May became [negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_pricing) on 20 April 2020, the first time to happen since the [New York Mercantile Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mercantile_Exchange) began trading in 1983.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] In April, as the demand decreased, concerns about inadequate storage capacity resulted in oil firms "renting tankers to store the surplus supply".[^cite_note-BBC_20200420-114] An October *Bloomberg* report on slumping oil prices—citing the EIA among others—said that, with the increasing number of virus cases, the demand for gasoline—particularly in the United States—was "particularly worrisome", while global inventories remained "quite high".[^cite_note-Bloomberg_Rigzone_20201021-117]

With the price of WTI at a record low, and 2019 Chinese 5% import tariff on U.S. oil lifted by China in May 2020, China began to import large quantities of US crude oil, reaching a record high of 867,000 bpd in July.[^cite_note-Forbes_Eberhart_20200920-118]

In a January 2020 EIA report, the average price of Brent crude oil in 2019 was \$64 per barrel compared to \$71 per barrel in 2018. The average price of WTI crude oil was \$57 per barrel in 2019 compared to \$64 in 2018.[^cite_note-EIA_French_20200107-100] On 20 April 2020, WTI Crude [futures contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_contracts) dropped below \$0 for the first time in history,[^cite_note-119] and the following day Brent Crude fell below \$20 per barrel. The substantial decrease in the price of oil was caused by two main factors: the [2020 Russia–Saudi Arabia oil price war](63341569.md)[^cite_note-Kellogg-104] and the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), which lowered demand for oil because of lockdowns around the world.[^cite_note-Kellogg-104]

In the fall of 2020, against the backdrop of the resurgent pandemic, the [U.S. Energy Information Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Energy_Information_Administration) (EIA) reported that global oil inventories remained "quite high" while demand for gasoline—particularly in the United States—was "particularly worrisome."[^cite_note-Bloomberg_Rigzone_20201021-117] The price of oil was about US\$40 by mid-October.[^cite_note-120] In 2021, the [record-high energy prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_global_energy_crisis) were driven by a global surge in demand as the world quit the economic recession caused by COVID-19, particularly due to strong energy demand in Asia.[^cite_note-Al-Jazeera-19][^cite_note-CNBC-20][^cite_note-121]

The ongoing 2019–2021 Persian Gulf crisis, which includes the use of drones to attack Saudi Arabia's oil infrastructure, has made the Gulf states aware of their vulnerability. Former US President "Donald Trump's 'maximum pressure' campaign led Iran to sabotage oil tankers in the Persian Gulf and supply drones and missiles for a surprise strike on Saudi oil facilities in 2019."[^cite_note-122] In January 2022, Yemen's Houthi rebels drone attacks destroyed oil tankers in Abu Dhabi prompting concerns about further increases in the price of oil.[^cite_note-123]

The oil prices were seen rising to hit \$71.38 per barrel in March 2021, marking the highest since the beginning of the pandemic in January 2020.[^cite_note-124] The oil price rise followed a missile drone attack on [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)'s [Aramco](290527.md) oil facility by [Yemen](350939.md)'s [Houthi rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement).[^cite_note-125] The [United States](3434750.md) said it was committed to defending Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-126]

On 5 October 2021, crude oil prices reached a multiyear high but retreated by 2% the following day. The price of crude was on the rise since June 2021, after a statement by a top US diplomat that even with a nuclear deal with Iran, hundreds of economic sanctions would remain in place.[^cite_note-127] Since September 2021, Europe's energy crisis has been worsening, driven by high crude prices and a scarcity of Russian gas on the continent.[^cite_note-128]

The high price of oil in late 2021, which resulted in US gasoline pump prices that rose by over \$1 a gallon—a seven-year high—added pressure to the United States, which has extensive reserves of oil and has been one of the world's largest producers of oil since at least 2018.[^cite_note-Forbes_Eberhart_2021-129] One of the major factors in the US refraining from increased oil production is related to "investor demands for higher financial returns".[^cite_note-Forbes_Eberhart_2021-129] Another factor as described by Forbes, is 'backwardation'—when oil futures markets see the current price of \$85+ as higher than what they can anticipate in the months and years in the future. If investors perceive lower future prices, they will not invest in "new drilling and fracking."[^cite_note-Forbes_Eberhart_2021-129]



The start price, end price, highs and lows of WTI oil prices for each year of the decade

By mid-January 2022, *Reuters* raised concerns that an increase in the price of oil to \$100—which seemed to be imminent—would worsen the inflationary environment that was already breaking 30-year-old records.[^cite_note-Reuters_20220119-130] Central banks were concerned that higher energy prices would contribute to a "wage-price spiral." The [European Union](9317.md) (EU) embargo of Russian seaborne oil, in response to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) in February, 2022, was one—but not the only—factor in the increase in the global price of oil, according to *The Economist*.[^cite_note-Economist_202205-131]

When the EU added new restrictions to Russia's oil on May 30, there was a dramatic increase in the price of Brent crude to over \$120 a barrel.[^cite_note-Economist_202205-131] Other factors affecting the surge in the price of oil included the tight oil market combined with a "robust demand" for energy as travel increased following the easing of coronavirus restrictions.[^cite_note-Economist_202205-131] At the same time, the United States was experiencing decreased refinery capacity which led to higher prices for petrol and diesel.[^cite_note-Economist_202205-131]

In an effort to lower energy prices and to curb inflation, President Biden announced on March 31, 2022, that he would be releasing a million bbl/d from the [Strategic Petroleum Reserve](167198.md) (SPR).[^cite_note-Puko_Ferek_20220331-132][^cite_note-Krauss_20220401-133] Bloomberg described how the price of oil, gas and other commodities had risen driven by a global "resurgence in demand" as COVID-19 restrictions were eased, combined with supply chains problems, and "geopolitical tensions".[^cite_note-Bloomberg_20220224-134]

In March 2023, oil prices dropped over \$2 a barrel on the 14th following the [Collapse of Silicon Valley Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_Silicon_Valley_Bank). The bank's collapse sent a tremor through various financial sectors, from banking to the oil industry.[^cite_note-135]

In May 2024, [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer), along with 22 other Democrats, urged the Department of Justice to take robust action against alleged collusion and price-fixing in the oil industry. In a letter to [Merrick Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrick_Garland), the senators referenced a FTC investigation revealing price-fixing by oil executives, which had increased energy costs for Americans. The FTC found that [Scott D. Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_D._Sheffield), colluded with OPEC to raise crude oil prices. Although the FTC cleared Exxon Mobil's \$60 billion acquisition of Pioneer, it barred Sheffield from joining the new company's board. The senators called for a comprehensive DOJ investigation into potential Sherman Antitrust Act violations, citing concerns over national security and economic burdens on lower-income families due to inflated fuel costs.[^cite_note-136]

On 10 October 2024, oil prices surged over 3% due to escalating tensions in the Middle East, raising concerns about potential disruptions to crude supplies. Brent crude reached \$75.98 per barrel, and U.S. WTI climbed to \$72.30. Fears of retaliatory strikes on oil facilities and possible U.S. involvement grew, while OPEC+ ministers met without expected changes to production policies.[^cite_note-137]

On 13 January 2026, oil prices rose by over 2% due to potential disruptions to Iranian crude oil exports overshadowing increased supply from Venezuela.[^cite_note-138] This came after President Donald Trump cancelled all meetings with Iranian officials and pledged support to protesters of anti-government demonstrations in Iran.[^cite_note-139]

## Oil-storage trade (contango)

Main article: [Oil-storage trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil-storage_trade)



The [Knock Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawise_Giant) (1979–2010, used for [floating storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_production_storage_and_offloading) in 2004–2009), a ULCC supertanker compared to the longest ships ever built

The oil-storage trade, also referred to as contango, a market strategy in which large, often vertically integrated oil companies purchase oil for immediate delivery and storage—when the price of oil is low— and hold it in storage until the price of oil increases. Investors bet on the future of oil prices through a [financial instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_instrument), [oil futures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_futures) in which they agree on a contract basis, to buy or sell oil at a set date in the future. Crude oil is stored in salt mines, tanks and oil tankers.[^cite_note-oil_glut-140]

Investors can choose to take profits or losses prior to the oil-delivery date arrives. Or they can leave the contract in place and physical oil is "delivered on the set date" to an "officially designated delivery point", in the United States, that is usually [Cushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushing,_Oklahoma), Oklahoma. When delivery dates approach, they close out existing contracts and sell new ones for future delivery of the same oil. The oil never moves out of storage. If the forward market is in "[contango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contango)"—the [forward price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_price) is higher than the current [spot price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_price)—the strategy is very successful.

Scandinavian Tank Storage AB and its founder Lars Jacobsson introduced the concept on the market in early 1990.[^cite_note-Yglesias_2014-141] But it was in 2007 through 2009 the oil storage trade expanded,[^cite_note-NPR-142] with many participants—including [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) giants, such as [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley), [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs), and [Citicorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp)—turning sizeable profits simply by sitting on tanks of oil.[^cite_note-WSJ6oct2007-143] By May 2007 Cushing's inventory fell by nearly 35% as the oil-storage trade heated up.[^cite_note-WSJ6oct2007-143]

By the end of October 2009 one in twelve of the largest oil tankers was being used more for temporary storage of oil, rather than transportation.[^cite_note-FT2009-144]

From June 2014 to January 2015, as the price of oil dropped 60% and the supply of oil remained high, the world's largest traders in crude oil purchased at least 25 million barrels to store in supertankers to make a profit in the future when prices rise. [Trafigura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafigura), [Vitol](17518274.md), [Gunvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunvor_\(company\)), [Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_Industries), [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) and other major energy companies began to book oil storage supertankers for up to 12 months. By 13 January 2015 At least 11 [Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCC) and Ultra Large Crude Carriers (ULCC)](15923603.md#Size_categories)" have been reported as booked with storage options, rising from around five vessels at the end of last week. Each VLCC can hold 2 million barrels."[^cite_note-Reuters_2015-145]

In 2015 as global capacity for oil storage was out-paced by global oil production, and an oil glut occurred. Crude oil storage space became a tradable commodity with [CME Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CME_Group)— which owns [NYMEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYMEX)— offering oil-storage futures contracts in March 2015.[^cite_note-oil_glut-140] Traders and producers can buy and sell the right to store certain types of oil.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

By 5 March 2015, as oil production outpaces oil demand by 1.5 million bpd, storage capacity globally is dwindling.[^cite_note-oil_glut-140] In the United States alone, according to data from the [Energy Information Administration](1704673.md), U.S. crude-oil supplies are at almost 70% of the U. S. storage capacity, the highest to capacity ratio since 1935.[^cite_note-oil_glut-140]

In 2020, rail and road tankers and decommissioned oil pipe lines are also being used to store crude oil for contango trade.[^cite_note-148] For the WTI crude to be delivered in May 2020, the price had fallen to -\$40 per bbl (i.e. buyers would be paid by the sellers for taking delivery of crude oil) due to lack of storage/expensive storage.[^cite_note-149] [LNG carriers](12378960.md) and LNG tanks can also be used for long duration crude oil storage purpose since LNG can not be stored long term due to evaporation. Frac tanks are also used to store crude oil deviating from their normal use.[^cite_note-150]

## Comparative cost of production

In their May 2019 comparison of the "cost of supply curve update" in which the Norway-based [Rystad Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rystad_Energy)—an "independent energy research and consultancy"—ranked the "worlds total recoverable liquid resources by their breakeven price", they listed the "Middle East onshore market" as the "cheapest source of new oil volumes globally" with the "North American tight oil"—which includes onshore [shale oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_oil) in the United States—in second place.[^cite_note-Rystad_20200509-151]

The breakeven price for North American shale oil was US\$68 a barrel in 2015, making it one of the most expensive to produce. By 2019, the "average Brent breakeven price for tight oil was about US\$46 per barrel. The lowest average WTI breakeven price for tight oil in 2022-2024 was in the Permian Delaware basin at \$48.50/Bbl followed by Williston at \$54.00/Bbl and Permian Midland at \$59.00/Bbl.[^cite_note-152] The breakeven price of oil from Saudi Arabia and other Middle Eastern countries was US\$42, in comparison.[^cite_note-Rystad_20200509-151]

Rystad reported that the average breakeven price for oil from the oil sands was US\$83 in 2019, making it the most expensive to produce, compared to all other "significant oil producing regions" in the world.[^cite_note-Rystad_20200509-151] The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) made similar comparisons.[^cite_note-nationalobserver_20190522-153]

In 2016, the *Wall Street Journal* reported that the United Kingdom, Brazil, Nigeria, Venezuela, and Canada had the costliest production.[^cite_note-WSJ_20160415-110] Saudi Arabia, Iran, and Iraq had the cheapest.[^cite_note-WSJ_20160415-110]

## Future projections

Main articles: [Oil depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_depletion) and [Peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil)

Peak oil is the period when the maximum rate of global [petroleum](23195.md) [extraction](8082027.md) is reached, after which the rate of production enters terminal decline. It relates to a long-term decline in the available supply of petroleum. This, combined with increasing demand, will significantly increase the worldwide prices of petroleum-derived products. Most significant will be the availability and price of liquid fuel for transportation.[^cite_note-journal-154]

&#x20;The US Department of Energy in the [Hirsch report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirsch_report) indicates that&#x20;

> The problems associated with world oil production peaking will not be temporary, and past "energy crisis" experience will provide relatively little guidance. The challenge of oil peaking deserves immediate, serious attention, if risks are to be fully understood and mitigation begun on a timely basis.[^cite_note-155]

Global annual crude oil production (including shale oil, [oil sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_sands), lease condensate and gas plant condensate but excluding liquid fuels from other sources such as [natural gas liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_liquids), biomass and derivatives of coal and natural gas) increased from 75.86 million [barrels](594043.md#Oil_barrel) (12.1 million [cubic metres](30125638.md)) in 2008 to 83.16 million bbl (13.2 million m<sup>3</sup>) per day in 2018 with a marginal annual growth rate of 1%.[^cite_note-156]

## Impact of rising oil price

The rising oil prices could negatively impact the [world economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy).[^cite_note-157] One example of the negative impact on the world economy, is the effect on the supply and demand. High oil prices indirectly increase the cost of producing many products thus causing increased prices to the consumer.[^cite_note-158] Since supplies of petroleum and natural gas are essential to [modern agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_agriculture) techniques, a fall in global oil supplies could cause spiking [food prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_prices) in the coming decades.[^cite_note-journal-154][^cite_note-159] One reason for the [increase in food prices in 2007–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_world_food_price_crisis) may be the [increase in oil prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000s_energy_crisis) during the same period.[^cite_note-160]

Bloomberg warned that the world economy, which was already experiencing an inflationary "shock", would worsen with oil priced at \$100 in February 2022.[^cite_note-Bloomberg_20220224-134] The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) described how a combination of the "soaring" price of commodities, imbalances in supply and demand, followed by pressures related to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, resulted in monetary policies being tightened by central banks, as some inflation in some countries broke 40-year-old record highs.[^cite_note-Gourinchas_20220419-161][^cite_note-Bloomberg_20220224-134] The IMF also cautioned that there was a potential for social unrest in poorer nations as the price of food and fuel increases.[^cite_note-Gourinchas_20220419-161]

## Impact of declining oil price

A major rise or decline in oil price can have both economic and political impacts. The decline on oil price during 1985–1986 is considered to have contributed to the fall of the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-162] Low oil prices could alleviate some of the negative effects associated with the [resource curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_curse), such as authoritarian rule[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] and gender inequality.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] Lower oil prices could however also lead to domestic turmoil and [diversionary war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversionary_foreign_policy). The reduction in food prices that follows lower oil prices could have positive impacts on violence globally.[^cite_note-\:0-170]

Research shows that declining oil prices make oil-rich states less bellicose.[^cite_note-171] Low oil prices could also make oil-rich states engage more in international cooperation, as they become more dependent on foreign investments.[^cite_note-172] The influence of the United States reportedly increases as oil prices decline, at least judging by the fact that "both oil importers and exporters vote more often with the United States in the United Nations General Assembly" during oil slumps.[^cite_note-\:0-170]

The macroeconomics impact on lower oil prices is lower inflation. A lower inflation rate is good for the consumers. This means that the general price of a basket of goods would increase at a bare minimum on a year to year basis. Consumer can benefit as they would have a better purchasing power, which may improve real gdp.[^cite_note-auto-173] However, in recent countries like Japan, the decrease in oil prices may cause deflation and it shows that consumers are not willing to spend even though the prices of goods are decreasing yearly, which indirectly increases the real debt burden.[^cite_note-auto-173] Declining oil prices may boost consumer oriented stocks but may hurt oil-based stocks.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] It is estimated that 17–18% of S\&P would decline with declining oil prices.

It has also been argued that the collapse in oil prices in 2015 should be very beneficial for developed western economies, who are generally oil importers and aren't over exposed to declining demand from China.[^cite_note-176] In the Asia-Pacific region, exports and economic growth were at significant risk across economies reliant on commodity exports as an engine of growth. The most vulnerable economies were those with a high dependence on fuel and mineral exports to China, such as: Korea DPR, Mongolia and Turkmenistan—where primary commodity exports account for 59–99% of total exports and more than 50% of total exports are destined to China.[^cite_note-177]

The decline in China's demand for commodities also adversely affected the growth of exports and GDP of large commodity-exporting economies such as Australia (minerals) and the Russian Federation (fuel). On the other hand, lower commodity prices led to an improvement in the trade balance—through lower the cost of raw materials and fuels—across commodity importing economies, particularly Cambodia, Kyrgyzstan, Nepal and other remote island nations (Kiribati, Maldives, Micronesia (F.S), Samoa, Tonga, and Tuvalu) which are highly dependent on fuel and agricultural imports.[^cite_note-178]

The oil importing economies like EU, Japan, China or India would benefit, however the oil producing countries would lose.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181] A Bloomberg article presents results of an analysis by Oxford Economics on the GDP growth of countries as a result of a drop from \$84 to \$40. It shows the GDP increase between 0.5% to 1.0% for India, USA and China, and a decline of greater than 3.5% from Saudi Arabia and Russia. A stable price of \$60 would add 0.5 percentage point to global gross domestic product.

Katina Stefanova has argued that falling oil prices do not imply a recession and a decline in stock prices.[^cite_note-182] Liz Ann Sonders, Chief Investment Strategist at Charles Schwab, had earlier written that that positive impact on consumers and businesses outside of the energy sector, which is a larger portion of the US economy will outweigh the negatives.[^cite_note-183]

While President Trump said in 2018, that the lower price of oil was like a "big Tax Cut for America and the World",[^cite_note-CNN_Egan_2018121-102] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* said that rising oil prices had a negative impact on oil-importing countries in terms of international trade.[^cite_note-economist_20180929_oil-97] Import prices rise in relation to their exports.[^cite_note-economist_20180929_oil-97] The importing country's [current account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_account_\(balance_of_payments\)) deficits widen because "their exports pay for fewer imports".[^cite_note-economist_20180929_oil-97]

## Speculative trading and crude oil futures

In the wake of the 1970s oil crisis, speculative trading in crude oil and crude oil futures in the [commodity markets](48190.md) emerged.[^cite_note-Qiang_201307-62][^cite_note-Ji_Qiang_201312-63]

NYMEX launched crude oil futures contracts in 1983, and the IPE launched theirs in June 1988.[^cite_note-Petroleum_Science_Tian_20191201-65] Global crude oil prices began to be published through NYMEX and IPE crude oil futures market.[^cite_note-Petroleum_Science_Tian_20191201-65] Volatility in crude oil prices can cause problems for the global economy. These crude oil futures contracts helped mitigate the "economic hazards of international crude oil spot price fluctuations".[^cite_note-Petroleum_Science_Tian_20191201-65] By 2019, NYMEX and ICE had become "representative of the world crude oil futures market"—an important factor in the world economy.[^cite_note-Petroleum_Science_Tian_20191201-65] Crude oil futures bring some uncertainty to the market and contribute to crude oil price fluctuations.[^cite_note-Petroleum_Science_Tian_20191201-65]

By 2008, there were a number of widely traded oil futures market listings.[^cite_note-Bloomberg_2008-184] Some of the big multinational oil companies actively participate in crude oil trading applying their market perception to make profit.[^cite_note-185]

### Speculation during the 2008 financial crisis

According to a U.S. [Commodity Futures Trading Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_Futures_Trading_Commission) (CFTC) 29 May 2008 report the "Multiple Energy Market Initiatives" was launched in partnership with the United Kingdom [Financial Services Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Services_Authority) and [ICE Futures Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IntercontinentalExchange) in order to expand surveillance and information sharing of various futures contracts. Part 1 is "Expanded International Surveillance Information for Crude Oil Trading."[^cite_note-CFTC_2008-80] This announcement received wide coverage in the financial press, with speculation about oil futures price manipulation.[^cite_note-ftgateway_2008-77][^cite_note-washingtonpost_2008-78][^cite_note-money_2008-79] In June 2008 *Business Week* reported that the surge in oil prices prior to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) had led some commentators to argue that at least some of the rise was due to [speculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculation) in the [futures markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_exchange).[^cite_note-Wallace2008-76]

The July 2008 interim report by the Interagency Task Force found that [speculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculation) had not caused significant changes in oil prices and that the [increase in oil prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_price_increases_since_2003) between January 2003 and June 2008 \[were] largely due to fundamental supply and demand factors."[^cite_note-ITF_200807-186]<sup>: 3 </sup> The report found that the primary reason for the price increases was that the [world economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy) had [expanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_expansion) at its fastest pace in decades, resulting in substantial increases in the demand for oil, while the [oil production](8082027.md) grew sluggishly, compounded by production shortfalls in oil-exporting countries.[^cite_note-ITF_200807-186]<sup>: 3 </sup>

The report stated that as a result of the imbalance and low [price elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_elasticity), very large price increases occurred as the [market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_\(economics\)) attempted to balance scarce [supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_elasticity_of_supply) against growing [demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_elasticity_of_demand), particularly from 2005 to 2008.[^cite_note-ITF_200807-186]<sup>: 14 </sup> The report forecast that this imbalance would persist in the future,[^cite_note-ITF_200807-186]<sup>: 4 </sup> leading to continued upward pressure on oil prices, and that large or rapid movements in oil prices are likely to occur even in the absence of activity by speculators.[^cite_note-ITF_200807-186]<sup>: 4 </sup>

### Hedging using oil derivatives

The use of [hedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)) using commodity [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\)) as a risk management tool on price exposure to liquidity and earnings, has been long established in North America. Chief Financial Officers (CFOS) use derivatives to dampen, remove or mitigate price uncertainty.[^cite_note-ogfj_2012-187] Bankers also use hedge funds to more "safely increase leverage to smaller oil and gas companies."[^cite_note-ogfj_2012-187] However, when not properly used, "derivatives can multiply losses"[^cite_note-ogfj_2012-187] particularly in North America where investors are more comfortable with higher levels of risk than in other countries.[^cite_note-ogfj_2012-187]

With the large number of bankruptcies as reported by Deloitte[^cite_note-fortune_2016-94] "funding \[for upstream oil industry] is shrinking and hedges are unwinding."[^cite_note-G\&M_Deloitte_2016-92] "Some oil producers are also choosing to liquidate hedges for a quick infusion of cash, a risky bet."[^cite_note-reuters_2016_Feb16-93]

According to John England, the Vice-chairman Deloitte LLP, "Access to capital markets, bankers' support and derivatives protection, which helped smooth an otherwise rocky road, are fast waning...The roughly 175 companies at risk of bankruptcy have more than \$150 billion in debt, with the slipping value of secondary stock offerings and asset sales further hindering their ability to generate cash."[^cite_note-188]

To finance exploration and production of the [unconventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_\(oil_&_gas\)_reservoir) oil industry in the United States, "hundreds of billions of dollars of capital came from non-bank participants \[non-bank buyers of bank energy credits] in leveraged loans that were thought at the time to be low risk.[^cite_note-ft_2015-189] However, with the oil glut that continued into 2016, about a third of oil companies are facing bankruptcy.[^cite_note-fortune_2016-94] While investors were aware that there was a risk that the operator might declare bankruptcy, they felt protected because "they had come in at the 'bank' level, where there was a senior claim on the assets \[and] they could get their capital returned."[^cite_note-ogfj_2012-187]

According to a 2012 article in *Oil and Gas Financial Journal*, "the combination of the development of large resource plays in the US and the emergence of business models designed to ensure consistent dividend payouts to investors has led to the development of more aggressive hedging policies in companies and less restrictive covenants in bank loans."[^cite_note-ogfj_2012-187]

### Institutional investors divesting from oil industry

At the fifth annual World Pensions Forum in 2015, [Jeffrey Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Sachs) advised [institutional investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_investor) to divest from carbon-reliant [oil industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_industry) firms in their [pension fund](43615.md)'s portfolio.[^cite_note-Sachs_2015-190]
