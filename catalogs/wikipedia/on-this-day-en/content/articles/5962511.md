&#x20; **Kim Jong Un**[^cite_note-7] (born 8 January c. 1982–1984)[^cite_note-birthdate-8] is a North Korean politician and [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator) who has been serving as the [supreme leader](25084215.md) of [North Korea](21255.md) since 2011, following [the death of his father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Kim_Jong_Il), [Kim Jong Il](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Il). He is the third ruler from the [Kim family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_family_\(North_Korea\)) to lead the country, which was founded by his grandfather [Kim Il Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung). Kim holds the positions of [general secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_secretary_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea) (WPK)[^cite_note-9] and [president of the State Affairs Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_State_Affairs_Commission), as well as the highest offices in the [Korean People's Army](21263.md).[^cite_note-10]

The second of three children of Kim Jong Il and his mistress [Ko Yong Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Yong-hui), Kim studied at the [International School of Berne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_of_Berne) in [Switzerland](26748.md) before attending [Kim Il Sung University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung_University) between 2002 and 2007. From 2009, Kim was viewed as the successor to the North Korean leadership. Following his father's death in 2011, [state television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Central_Television) announced Kim as the "great successor to the revolutionary cause". He assumed various leadership posts, and also became member of the [Presidium of the WPK Politburo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Politburo_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea), the highest decision-making body in the country. In July 2012, Kim was promoted to the highest rank of [marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonsu) in the [Korean People's Army](21263.md), consolidating his positions as [commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief_of_North_Korea) and Chairman of the [Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Military_Commission_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea).

Like his father and grandfather before him, Kim Jong Un rules North Korea as a repressive and totalitarian dictatorship, and his leadership has maintained the [Kim family cult of personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_cult_of_personality). [State media](4540969.md) often refer to him as "[Respected Comrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kim_Jong_Un's_titles)", "[Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taewonsu)", and "Head of State". Kim's government has been accused of [human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_North_Korea). He reportedly ordered the purge and execution of several North Korean officials including his uncle, [Jang Song-thaek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jang_Song-thaek), in 2013, and [the assassination of his half-brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Kim_Jong-nam), [Kim Jong-nam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong-nam), in [Malaysia](3607937.md) in 2017. [Kim Jong Un's ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimjongunism) departed from his father's military-first *[Songun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songun)* policy, professing a "people-first policy" and renewed commitment to [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism). Kim has promoted the [Byungjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byungjin) policy of parallel development of [the country's nuclear weapons program](106424.md) alongside [the country's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_North_Korea), named after the similar policy of Kim Il Sung. He has revived the structures of the WPK, expanding the party's power at the expense of the military leadership.

Kim oversaw the world's [four most recent nuclear weapons tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_nuclear_weapons_tests_of_North_Korea)[^cite_note-11] and [extensive missile tests](5831012.md), including [North Korea's first intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-14). These heightened tensions [with South Korea](5882788.md), the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea%E2%80%93United_States_relations), and [China](15980883.md), culminating in the [2017–2018 North Korea crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%932018_North_Korea_crisis). In 2018 and 2019, Kim took part in summits with Chinese President [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) and South Korean president [Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in) and U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md), the first meetings between a North Korean and a U.S. head of state. Despite the brief [thaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9tente) in relations, the negotiations ultimately broke down without progress on [reunification of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Korea) or nuclear disarmament. He claimed success in combating the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_North_Korea), with the country not reporting confirmed cases until May 2022, although independent observers questioned this claim. In December 2023, Kim declared that North Korea formally abandoned efforts to reunify Korea and proposed the [two hostile states theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_hostile_states_theory), symbolically demolishing the [Arch of Reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Reunification). In June 2024, Kim signed [a security and defense treaty with Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean%E2%80%93Russian_Treaty_on_Comprehensive_Strategic_Partnership), while supplying Russia with material for the Russo-Ukrainian war. In October, [North Korea sent soldiers to assist Russian units](78396607.md) in the [Kursk campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_campaign).

## Early life

North Korean authorities and state-run media state that Kim Jong Un was born on 8 January 1982.[^cite_note-Birth-1982-12] [South Korean intelligence](991544.md) officials believe that the actual date was 1983.[^cite_note-NYTRodman-13] The US government lists his birth year as 1984, based on the passport he used while studying in Switzerland.[^cite_note-14] [Ko Yong Suk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Yong_Suk), Kim's aunt who defected to the United States in 1998, also claimed the 1984 birthdate, saying Kim was the same age as her son, who was a playmate from a young age.[^cite_note-15] It is thought that Kim's official birth year was changed for symbolic reasons; 1982 marked the seventieth birthday of his grandfather Kim Il Sung, and forty years after the official birth of his father Kim Jong Il.[^cite_note-indy-16]

Kim Jong Un is the second of three children of [Ko Yong Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Yong-hui) and Kim Jong Il; his elder brother, [Kim Jong Chul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Chul), was born on 25 September 1981, while his younger sister, [Kim Yo Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yo_Jong), is believed to have been born in 1987.[^cite_note-Lee2012-17][^cite_note-18] He is a grandson of Kim Il Sung, who was the founder of and led North Korea from its establishment in 1948 until [his death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Kim_Il_Sung) in 1994.[^cite_note-19] Kim is the first leader of North Korea to have been born a North Korean citizen, his father having been born in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and his grandfather having been born during the [Japanese colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule).

All of Kim Jong Il's children are said to have lived in Switzerland, as well as the mother of the two youngest sons, who lived in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) for some time.[^cite_note-Figaro-20] First reports said that Kim Jong Un attended the private [International School of Berne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_of_Berne) in [Gümligen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCmligen) in Switzerland under the name "Chol-pak" or "Pak-chol" from 1993 to 1998.[^cite_note-blaineharden-21][^cite_note-22] In April 2012, new documents came to light indicating that Kim Jong Un had lived in Switzerland since 1991 or 1992, earlier than previously thought.[^cite_note-23] The Laboratory of Anthropology and Anatomy at the [University of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lyon), analyzed a picture of Kim Jong Un from 2012 and the picture of a student believed to be Kim, taken at the Liebefeld Steinhölzli school in 1999. [Anthropometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropometric) facial comparison produced a 95% match, and the [forensic anthropologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_anthropologist) involved at the occasion expressed being convinced that the two photos depict the same person.[^cite_note-24]



The Liebefeld-Steinhölzli public school in [Köniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6niz), Switzerland, reportedly attended by Kim Jong Un

Then-councilor Ueli Studer, representing the municipal school board of [Köniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6niz), Switzerland, confirmed that a North Korean student had attended the Liebefeld-Steinhölzli public school from August 1998 until 2000 fall. Kim was registered under the name "Pak-un" or "Un-pak" as the son of an employee of the North Korean embassy in Bern. He first attended a special class for foreign-language children and later attended the regular classes of the 6th, 7th, 8th and part of the final 9th year, leaving the school abruptly in the autumn of 2000. Former school headmaster Peter Burri described the North Korean teenager as "well-integrated, hardworking, and ambitious".[^cite_note-NZZ1.2748091-25] However, his grades and attendance rating were reported to have been poor.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The ambassador of North Korea in Switzerland, [Ri Chol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ri_Su-yong), had a close relationship with Kim Jong-un and acted as his mentor,[^cite_note-Figaro-20] and Kim was chaperoned by an older student, thought to be his bodyguard.[^cite_note-Henckel2009-28]

Kim was described by classmates as a shy child who was awkward with girls and indifferent to political issues. He distinguished himself in sports and had a fascination with the American [National Basketball Association](22093.md) and [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan); a friend said he had been shown pictures of Kim with [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) and [Toni Kukoč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Kuko%C4%8D).[^cite_note-Chosun-29][^cite_note-Guardian2009-30] Another classmate said Kim had told him that he was the son of the leader of North Korea.[^cite_note-Shubert2010-31][^cite_note-ta2909-32] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported in 2009 that Kim Jong Un's schoolfriends recalled he "spent hours doing meticulous pencil drawings" of Jordan.[^cite_note-Higgins2009-33] He was also obsessed with computer games,[^cite_note-Chosun-29][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] and was a fan of [Jackie Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Chan) action movies.[^cite_note-kimpersonality-36] His elder brother Kim Jong Chul attended the school with him.[^cite_note-37]

Most analysts agree that Kim Jong Un attended [Kim Il Sung University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung_University) in [Pyongyang](47948.md) from 2002 to 2007.[^cite_note-NYTProfile-38] Kim obtained two degrees, one in physics at Kim Il Sung University and another as an Army officer at the [Kim Il Sung Military University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung_Military_University).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Powell2011-40]

In late February 2018, [Reuters](18998750.md) reported that Kim and his father had used forged passports—supposedly issued by Brazil and dated 26 February 1996—to apply for visas in various countries. Both 10-year passports carry a stamp saying "Embassy of Brazil in Prague". Kim Jong Un's passport records the name "Josef Pwag" and a date of birth of 1 February 1983.[^cite_note-2018-02-28_Reuters-41]

For many years, only one confirmed photograph of Kim was known to exist outside North Korea, apparently taken in the mid-1990s, when he was eleven.[^cite_note-42] Occasionally, other supposed images of him surfaced but were often disputed.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] In June 2010, shortly before he was given official posts and publicly introduced to the North Korean people, more pictures were released, taken when he was attending school in Switzerland.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The first official image of him as an adult was a group photograph released on 30 September 2010, at the end of the party conference that effectively anointed him, in which he is seated in the front row, two places from his father. This was followed by newsreel footage of him attending the conference.[^cite_note-47]

## Succession

### Pre-2010 Party Conference speculation

Kim Jong Un's eldest half-brother, [Kim Jong Nam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong-nam), had been the favorite to succeed, but reportedly fell out of favor after 2001, when he was caught attempting to enter Japan on a [fake passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_passport) to visit [Tokyo Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Disneyland).[^cite_note-concert-48] Kim Jong Nam was [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Kim_Jong-nam) in Malaysia in 2017 by suspected North Korean agents.[^cite_note-independent-49]

Kim Jong Il's former personal chef, [Kenji Fujimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenji_Fujimoto), revealed details regarding Kim Jong Un, with whom he had a good relationship,[^cite_note-50] stating that he was favored to be his father's successor. Fujimoto also said that Jong Un was favored by his father over his elder brother, Kim Jong Chul, reasoning that Jong Chul is too feminine in character, while Jong Un is "exactly like his father".[^cite_note-nyt-51] Furthermore, Fujimoto stated that "if power is to be handed over then Jong Un is the best for it. He has superb physical gifts, is a big drinker and never admits defeat." Also, according to Fujimoto, Jong Un smokes [Yves Saint Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yves_Saint_Laurent_\(brand\)) cigarettes, loves [Johnnie Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnnie_Walker) whisky and has a [Mercedes-Benz 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_600) luxury sedan.[^cite_note-52] When Jong Un was 18, Fujimoto described an episode where Jong Un once questioned his lavish lifestyle and asked, "we are here, playing basketball, riding horses, riding jet skis, having fun together. But what of the lives of the average people?"[^cite_note-nyt-51] On 15 January 2009, the South Korean news agency [Yonhap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonhap) reported that Kim Jong Il had appointed Kim Jong Un to be his successor.[^cite_note-concert-48][^cite_note-53]

On 8 March 2009, [BBC News](1139893.md) reported that Kim Jong Un was on the ballot for [2009 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_North_Korean_parliamentary_election) to the [Supreme People's Assembly](695507.md), the [rubber stamp parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_stamp_\(politics\)) of North Korea.[^cite_note-54] Subsequent reports indicated that his name did not appear on the list of lawmakers,[^cite_note-55] but he was later elevated to a mid-level position in the [National Defense Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Commission_\(North_Korea\)), the highest decision-making body of the state and highest organisation of the [North Korean military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_military) at that time.[^cite_note-Rosen2009-56]

From 2009, it was understood by foreign diplomatic services that Kim was to succeed his father Kim Jong Il as the head of the Korean Workers' Party and *de facto* leader of North Korea.[^cite_note-successor-57] He has been named as "Brilliant Comrade".[^cite_note-58] His father had also asked embassy staff abroad to pledge loyalty to his son.[^cite_note-huff-59] There have also been reports that citizens in North Korea were encouraged to sing a newly composed "song of praise" to Kim Jong Un, in a similar fashion to that of [praise songs relating to Kim Jong Il](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_General_Kim_Jong_Il) and Kim Il Sung.[^cite_note-Willacy2009-60] Later, in June, Kim was reported to have visited [China](5405.md) secretly to "present himself" to the Chinese leadership.[^cite_note-61] The Chinese foreign ministry has strongly denied that this visit occurred.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-blaineharden-21]

In September 2009, it was reported that Kim Jong Il had secured support for the succession plan, after a propaganda campaign.[^cite_note-Lim2009-63] It is believed by some that Kim Jong Un was involved in the [*Cheonan* sinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROKS_Cheonan_sinking)[^cite_note-Gayathri2011-64] and the [bombardment of Yeonpyeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Yeonpyeong)[^cite_note-65] to strengthen his military credentials and facilitate a successful transition of power from his father.[^cite_note-Chun2011-66]

### Vice Chairman of the Central Military Commission

Kim Jong Un was made a *[daejang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daejang)* (대장), the equivalent of a four-star [general](2965165.md) in the [United States](3434750.md),[^cite_note-BBCmodel-67] on 27 September 2010, a day ahead of a rare Workers' Party of Korea conference in Pyongyang, the first time North Korean media had mentioned him by name and despite him having no previous military experience.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] Despite the promotion, no further details, including verifiable portraits of Kim, were released.[^cite_note-71] On 28 September 2010, he was named vice chairman of the [Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Military_Commission_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea) and appointed to the [Central Committee of the Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea), in an apparent nod to become the successor to Kim Jong Il.[^cite_note-72]

On 10 October 2010, Kim Jong Un was alongside his father when he attended the ruling Workers' Party's 65th-anniversary celebration. This was seen as confirming his position as the next leader of the Workers' Party. Unprecedented international press access was granted to the event, further indicating the importance of Kim Jong Un's presence.[^cite_note-73] In January 2011, the government reportedly began purging around 200 protégés of both Kim Jong Un's uncle-in-law [Jang Song Thaek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jang_Song-thaek) and [O Kuk Ryol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Kuk-ryol), the vice chairman of the National Defence Commission, by either detention or execution to further prevent either man from rivaling Jong Un.[^cite_note-74]

## Leader of North Korea



People bowing to the [statues of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansudae_Grand_Monument), April 2012



Portraits of Kim Jong Un's father and grandfather ([Arirang Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arirang_Festival) mass games in Pyongyang)

On 17 December 2011, Kim Jong Il died. Despite the elder Kim's plans, it was not immediately clear after his death whether Kim Jong Un would in fact take full power, and what his exact role in a new government would be.[^cite_note-Branigan2011-75] Following his father's death, Kim Jong Un was hailed as the "great successor to the revolutionary cause of *[Juche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juche)*",[^cite_note-76] "outstanding leader of the party, army and people",[^cite_note-77] and "respected comrade who is identical to Supreme Commander Kim Jong Il",[^cite_note-78] and was made chairman of the Kim Jong Il funeral committee. The [Korean Central News Agency](632399.md) (KCNA) described Kim Jong Un as "a great person born of heaven", a propaganda term only his father and grandfather had enjoyed.[^cite_note-79] The ruling Workers' Party of Korea also said in an editorial, "We vow with bleeding tears to call Kim Jong Un our supreme commander, our leader."[^cite_note-Lee2011-80]

He was publicly declared [Supreme Commander of the Korean People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_North_Korea) on 24 December 2011,[^cite_note-bbc24-81] and formally appointed to the position on 30 December 2011 when the Politburo Workers' Party of Korea "courteously proclaimed that the dear respected Kim Jong Un, vice chairman of the Central Military Commission of the WPK, assumed the supreme commandership of the Korean People's Army".[^cite_note-AFP2011-82] The message also declared that it was Kim Jong Il's will on 8 October 2011 that Kim Jong Un be appointed KPA supreme commander after his death.[^cite_note-83] On 26 December 2011, the leading North Korean newspaper *[Rodong Sinmun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodong_Sinmun)* reported that Kim Jong Un had been acting as chairman of the Central Military Commission,[^cite_note-84] and supreme leader of the country, following his father's demise.[^cite_note-85]



North Korean soldiers saluting at the [Revolutionary Martyrs' Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Martyrs'_Cemetery) in Pyongyang, 2012

On 27 March 2012, Kim was elected to the 4th Conference of the Workers' Party of Korea. On 11 April, that conference wrote the post of general secretary out of the party charter and instead designated Kim Jong Il as the party's "Eternal General Secretary". The conference then elected Kim Jong Un as leader of the party under the newly created title of First Secretary. Kim Jong Un also took his father's post as Chairman of the Central Military Commission, as well as his father's old seat on the [Politburo Presidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Politburo_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea).[^cite_note-86] In a speech made prior to the Conference, Kim Jong Un declared that "Imbuing the whole society with [Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimilsungism%E2%80%93Kimjongilism) is the highest programme of our Party".[^cite_note-87] On 11 April 2012, the 5th Session of the 12th Supreme People's Assembly appointed Kim Jong Un as "[First Chairman of the National Defence Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_National_Defence_Commission)", as the office of "Chairman of the National Defence Commission" was also abolished.[^cite_note-Sang-Hun_Correction-88]

In July 2012,[^cite_note-Green2012-89] Kim Jong Un was promoted to [*konghwaguk wonsu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonsu_\(North_Korea\)) (공화국원수; lit. '[marshal of the republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal)'), the highest active rank in the military. The decision was jointly issued by the Central Committee and the Central Military Commission of the Workers' Party of Korea, the National Defence Commission, and the Presidium of the Supreme People's Assembly, the KCNA subsequently announced.[^cite_note-marshal-90] The only higher rank is [Taewonsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taewonsu) (roughly translated as [Grand Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Marshal) or [Generalissimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalissimo)) which was held by Kim's grandfather, Kim Il Sung, and which was awarded posthumously to his father, Kim Jong Il, in February 2012.[^cite_note-Green2012-89][^cite_note-marshal-90] The promotion confirmed Kim's role as top leader of the North Korean military and came days after the replacement of Chief of General Staff [Ri Yong Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ri_Yong-ho_\(general\)) by [Hyon Yong Chol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyon_Yong-chol).[^cite_note-marshal-90]

On 9 March 2014, Kim Jong Un was elected to a seat in the Supreme People's Assembly, the country's [unicameral](204304.md) [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature). He ran unopposed, but voters had the choice of voting yes or no. There was a record turnout of voters and, according to government officials, all voted "yes" in his symbolic home district of [Mount Paektu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Paektu).[^cite_note-91] The Supreme People's Assembly subsequently re-elected him the First Chairman of the National Defence Commission.[^cite_note-92]

### Leadership style

Analysts are divided about how much actual power Kim has.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] After he assumed power, some analysts had predicted that when Kim Jong Il died, Jang Song Thaek would act as [regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent), as Kim Jong Un was too inexperienced to immediately lead the country.[^cite_note-Wallace2013-96][^cite_note-Shim2011-97]

In July 2012, Kim Jong Un showed a change in cultural policy from his father by attending a [Moranbong Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moranbong_Band) concert. The concert contained several elements of pop culture from [the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture), particularly the United States. Kim used this event to introduce his wife to the public, an unprecedented move in North Korea.[^cite_note-98] In the same year, Kim Jong Il's personal chef Kenji Fujimoto visited North Korea and said, "Stores in Pyongyang were brimming with products and people in the streets looked cheerful. North Korea has changed a lot since Kim Jong Un assumed power. All of this is because of leader Kim Jong Un."[^cite_note-McCurry2012-99]

According to analysts, Kim Jong Un has used his resemblance to his grandfather to harness Kim Il Sung's personality cult and popular nostalgia for earlier times.[^cite_note-Pak-100] In 2013, Kim copied his grandfather's style when he gave his first New Year's address, a break from the approach of his father, Kim Jong Il, who never made a televised address during his 17 years in power.[^cite_note-101] He has also appeared more accessible and open than his father, hugging and linking arms with young and old.[^cite_note-Pak-100] In his public appearances, he appears more active than his father or grandfather, for example, weeding, riding a horse, driving a tank, riding a rollercoaster, or using information technology.[^cite_note-Pak-100]



People in Pyongyang watch Kim Jong Un on [North Korean TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Central_Television), 2015

In April 2012, when a satellite launch failed, the government admitted this publicly, the first time it had ever done so.[^cite_note-Pak-100] In May 2014, following the [collapse of an apartment building in Pyongyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Pyongyang_apartment_building_collapse), Kim Jong Un was said to be very upset at the loss of life that resulted. A statement issued by the country's official news agency the Korean Central News Agency used the rare expression "profound consolation and apology". An unnamed government official was quoted by the [BBC](19344654.md) as saying Kim Jong Un had "sat up all night, feeling painful".[^cite_note-102] While the height of the building and the number of casualties was not released, media reports described it as a 23-story building and indicated that more than a hundred people may have died in the collapse.[^cite_note-103]

### Cult of personality

Further information: [North Korean cult of personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_cult_of_personality)

Kim Jong Un frequently performs symbolic acts that associate him with the [personality cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_cult_of_personality) of his father and grandfather.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Like them, Kim Jong Un regularly tours the country, giving "[on-the-spot guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-the-spot_guidance)" at various sites.[^cite_note-106] North Korean state media often refers to him as "Respected Comrade Kim Jong Un"[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] or "Marshal Kim Jong Un".[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]



A selection of [Kim Jong Un's works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Un_bibliography), translated to different languages

On 9 January 2012, a large rally was held by the Korean People's Army in front of the [Kumsusan Palace of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumsusan_Palace_of_the_Sun) to honor Kim Jong Un and to demonstrate loyalty.[^cite_note-111] On 15 April 2012, during a military parade to commemorate Kim Il Sung's centenary, Kim Jong Un made his first public speech, *[Let Us March Forward Dynamically Towards Final Victory, Holding Higher the Banner of Songun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_Us_March_Forward_Dynamically_Towards_Final_Victory,_Holding_Higher_the_Banner_of_Songun)*.[^cite_note-112] That speech became the basis of a hymn dedicated to him, "[Onwards Toward the Final Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onwards_Toward_the_Final_Victory)".[^cite_note-Branigan2012-113] In November 2012, satellite photos revealed a half-kilometer-long (1,600 ft) propaganda message carved into a hillside in [Ryanggang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryanggang_Province), reading "Long Live General Kim Jong Un, the Shining Sun!"[^cite_note-114]

### Political developments

Kim has changed North Korea's internal power dynamics, increasing the influence of the WPK while decreasing the power of the military.[^cite_note-Aoki-2021-115] In May 2016, he organized the WPK's [7th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Congress_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea), its first congress since 1980.[^cite_note-116] In the congress, Kim was reelected as leader of the WPK, though his title of First Secretary was changed to Chairman[^cite_note-117] (a title held by his grandfather from 1949 to 1966).

The Supreme People's Assembly (SPA) subsequently amended the [constitution](4872690.md) in June 2016, abolishing the National Defence Commission (NDC) except in times of war, and replacing it with the [State Affairs Commission](50991731.md) (SAC), which was named the "supreme policy-oriented leadership body of state power". Kim became the [Chairman of the State Affairs Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_State_Affairs_of_North_Korea) on 29 June 2016. These amendments marked the decrease of the military's influence, with the newly established SAC including more civilian and less military members than the NDC.[^cite_note-Aoki-2021-115]

During the [2019 North Korean parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_North_Korean_parliamentary_election), Kim Jong Un became the first North Korean leader to not stand as a candidate for the SPA.[^cite_note-118] The constitution was further amended in 2019 by the SPA.[^cite_note-Atsuhito-2019-119] References to *Juche* and *Songun* were replaced by Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism, and the constitution stipulated that the mission of North Korea's armed forces was to "defend unto death the Party Central Committee headed by the great Comrade Kim Jong Un"; this made Kim the first North Korean leader to be named in the constitution while currently holding power. The chairman of the State Affairs Commission's status was amended to be the "supreme leader who represents the state", effectively making Kim Jong Un both *de jure* and *de facto* head of state;[^cite_note-Atsuhito-2019-119] previously the president of the Presidium of the SPA was the *de jure* head of state. The chairman also was named as the commander-in-chief of the armed forces as well as the "supreme representative of all the Korean people".[^cite_note-120]

In January 2021, the WPK held its [8th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Congress_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea), where Kim delivered a nine-hour-long report in which he admitted failures in carrying out the economic plan and lambasted leading officials' shortcomings.[^cite_note-121] He also praised the country's nuclear capability and addressed the United States as the DPRK's main enemy.[^cite_note-122] Kim Jong Un was reelected as party leader, with his title changed to General Secretary, a post held by his father and grandfather.[^cite_note-123] Previously, the title general secretary was awarded "eternally" to Kim Jong Il in 2012.[^cite_note-124] The congress also saw the WPK reassert its commitment to communism.[^cite_note-125] The [Control Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_Commission_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea) was abolished, with the [Central Auditing Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Auditing_Commission_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea) taking up its duties.[^cite_note-kcnawatch-126] The Congress was also unique in that its backdrop did not include the [Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il portraits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung_and_Kim_Jong_Il_portraits) where they would normally have been put, suggesting Kim's desire to make his mark on the country's politics.[^cite_note-Aoki-2021-115]

The congress marked the consolidation of WPK control over the army, as well as a further decrease in the army's power; the number of military delegates dropped from 719 in the 7th Congress to 408.[^cite_note-Lee-2021-127] Politburo members increased from 28 to 30, though incumbent military elite membership decreased from eight to six. The influence of the KPA Party Committee and the [General Political Bureau](44059604.md) (GPB) was decreased; with the committee's ranking now equal to provincial party committees, while it was above them previously. The GPB was also no longer equal to the Central Committee, while the Central Military Commission was given effective command of the armed forces.[^cite_note-Lee-2021-127] "Military-first policy" was also removed from the charter, being replaced by "people-first politics".[^cite_note-Aoki-2021-115]

Under Kim, North Korea has changed the names of several offices and institutions in what has been named by [Yonhap News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonhap_News_Agency) as an effort to present itself as a "normal state". The Ministry of People's Armed Forces was renamed to the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(North_Korea\)) in January 2021, while state media began referring to Kim as "President of the State Affairs" rather than "chairman" in English language articles starting from February 2021.[^cite_note-128] In November 2021, the South Korean NIS reported that the North Korean government has begun using the term [Kimjongunism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimjongunism), in an effort to establish an independent ideological system centered on Kim. Analyst Ken Gause described this as Kim "now ready to put his stamp firmly on the regime".[^cite_note-129]

### Economic policies

Kim Jong Un has been promoting a policy of *[byungjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byungjin)* (병진), similar to his grandfather Kim Il Sung's policies from the 1960s, developing the national economy in parallel with the country's [nuclear weapons program](106424.md).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] A set of comprehensive economic measures, the "Socialist Corporate Responsible Management System", were introduced in 2013.[^cite_note-IFES1-133] The measures increase the autonomy of enterprises by granting them "certain rights to engage in business activities autonomously and elevate the will to labor through appropriately implementing the socialist distribution system". Another priority of economic policies that year was agriculture, where the *pojon* (vegetable garden) responsibility system was implemented. The system reportedly achieved a major increase in output in some collective farms.[^cite_note-IFES1-133]

North Korean media described the economy as a "flexible collectivist system" where enterprises were applying "active and evolutionary actions" to achieve economic development. These reports reflect Kim's general economic policy of reforming management, increasing the autonomy and incentives for economic actors. This set of reforms known as the "May 30th measures" reaffirms both socialist ownership and "objective economic laws in guidance and management" to improve living standards. Other objectives of the measures are to increase the availability of domestically manufactured goods on markets, introduction of defence innovations into the civilian sector and boost international trade.[^cite_note-IFES2-134]

There has been a construction boom in Pyongyang, bringing colour and creative architectural styles to the city. While in the past there was a concentration on building monuments, Kim Jong Un's government has constructed amusement parks, aquatic parks, skating rinks, a [dolphinarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphinarium) and a ski resort.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Kim has been actively promoting a consumer culture, including entertainment and cosmetics.[^cite_note-Pak-100] He has also overseen the construction of [tourist attractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_North_Korea) in the country.[^cite_note-137]

Kim has attempted to ease North Korea's food shortages, though the food situation deteriorated during the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-138] In March 2023, during a WPK plenary session, he called for boosting agricultural production, saying that it is "important to concentrate on increasing the yield at all the farms".[^cite_note-139]

### Purges and executions

As with all reporting on North Korea, reports of purges and executions are difficult to verify.[^cite_note-140] Allegations in 2013 that Kim Jong Un had his ex-girlfriend, singer [Hyon Song Wol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyon_Song-wol), executed for violating pornography laws turned out to be false.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] In May 2016, analysts were surprised to find that General [Ri Yong Gil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ri_Yong-gil), reported by South Korea to have been executed earlier in the year, was, in fact, alive and well.[^cite_note-143]

In December 2013, Kim Jong Un's uncle Jang Song Thaek was arrested and executed for treachery.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] Jang is believed to have been executed by firing squad. Yonhap has stated that, according to multiple unnamed sources, Kim Jong Un has also put to death members of Jang's family, to completely destroy all traces of Jang's existence through "extensive executions" of his family, including the children and grandchildren of all close relatives. Those reportedly killed in Kim's purge include Jang's sister Jang Kye Sun, her husband and ambassador to Cuba, Jon Yong Jin, and Jang's nephew and ambassador to Malaysia, Jang Yong Chol. The nephew's two sons were also said to have been killed.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] At the time of Jang's removal, it was announced that "the discovery and purge of the Jang group ... made our party and revolutionary ranks purer ..."[^cite_note-148] and after his execution on 12 December 2013 state media warned that the army "will never pardon all those who disobey the order of the Supreme Commander".[^cite_note-149]

In April 2015, the U.S.-based Committee for Human Rights in North Korea and AllSource Analysis released an analysis of satellite imagery from the Kanggon military training area, about 13 miles north of Pyongyang, which they said showed six ZPU-4 anti-aircraft guns positioned near a group of standing figures on or about 7 October 2014. The report concluded that the "most plausible explanation" was a public execution, although the identities of the people shown and the reason for the event were unknown. Writing in *The Washington Post*, Adam Taylor noted that the imagery appeared to support earlier reports that North Korea had used anti-aircraft weapons in executions, but also emphasized that the scene could not be confirmed.[^cite_note-HRNK2015-150][^cite_note-Taylor2015-151]

### Human rights violations

See also: [Human rights in North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_North_Korea)

Like his father and grandfather, Kim rules North Korea as a [totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian) state.[^cite_note-indy-16][^cite_note-Willacy2009-60] In January 2013, the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights [Navi Pillay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navi_Pillay) said that the North Korean human rights situation had not improved since Kim had taken power and called for an investigation.[^cite_note-152] A report on the situation of human rights in North Korea in February 2013[^cite_note-153] by [United Nations Special Rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Special_Rapporteur) [Marzuki Darusman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzuki_Darusman) proposed a UN commission of inquiry.[^cite_note-154] The [report of the commission of inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Report_of_the_Commission_of_Inquiry_on_Human_Rights_in_the_Democratic_People's_Republic_of_Korea)[^cite_note-155] was published in February 2014 and suggested Kim could "possibly" be made accountable for crimes against humanity at the [International Criminal Court](14880.md).[^cite_note-156]

In July 2016, the [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md) imposed personal sanctions on Kim. Although his involvement in human rights abuses was cited as the reason,[^cite_note-157] officials said the sanctions target the country's nuclear and missile programs.[^cite_note-158] In June 2017, U.S. President Donald Trump condemned Kim Jong Un's "brutal" government and described Kim as a "madman" after the death of American student [Otto Warmbier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Warmbier) who had been imprisoned during a visit to North Korea.[^cite_note-159] However, in 2019, President Trump said that he believed Kim was not responsible for Warmbier's death.[^cite_note-160]

### Alleged assassination attempts

In 2012, a gun was discovered beneath a juniper tree in Ryugyeongwon, located near a route that Kim was going to travel. It was assumed this was part of an assassination attempt.[^cite_note-161]

In May 2017, the North Korean government stated that the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA) of the United States and the South Korean NIS hired a North Korean [lumberjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumberjack) who worked in Russia to assassinate Kim with a "biochemical weapon" that was both [radioactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive) and nano-poisonous, and whose effect would have been delayed by a few months.[^cite_note-162] North Korea said that it would seek extradition of anyone involved in the assassination attempt.[^cite_note-163]

### Nuclear weapons development

See also: [North Korea and weapons of mass destruction](106424.md)



Model of an [Unha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unha)-9 rocket on display at a floral exhibition in Pyongyang, 30 August 2013

Under Kim Jong Un, North Korea has continued to develop nuclear weapons, testing bombs in February 2013, January and September 2016, and September 2017.[^cite_note-Albert-164] As of 2021, North Korea had conducted nearly 120 missile tests under Kim Jong Un, compared with roughly 33 tests during the combined tenures of his father and grandfather.[^cite_note-Pak-100][^cite_note-Aoki-2021-115] By 2023, this climbed up to a total of 226.[^cite_note-165] According to several analysts, North Korea sees the nuclear arsenal as vital to deter an attack, and it is unlikely that North Korea would launch a nuclear war.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] According to a [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation) senior researcher, Kim Jong Un believes that nuclear weapons are his guarantee of the survival of North Korea and its government.[^cite_note-168] In 2022, it was estimated that North Korea had around 45–55 nuclear weapons.[^cite_note-169]

In 2012, on the 100th anniversary of Kim Il Sung's birth, he said: "the days are gone forever when our enemies could blackmail us with nuclear bombs".[^cite_note-Pak-100] At a plenary meeting of the Central Committee of the Workers' Party held on 31 March 2013, he announced that North Korea would adopt "a new strategic line on carrying out economic construction and building nuclear armed forces simultaneously".[^cite_note-170] In December 2015, Kim stated that his family "turned the DPRK into a powerful nuclear weapons state ready to detonate a self-reliant A-bomb and H-bomb to reliably defend its sovereignty and the dignity of the nation".[^cite_note-Kim_has_H-bomb?-171]

During the 7th WPK Congress in 2016, Kim Jong Un stated that North Korea would "not use nuclear weapons first unless aggressive hostile forces use nuclear weapons to invade on our sovereignty".[^cite_note-172] However, on other occasions, North Korea has threatened ["pre-emptive" nuclear attacks](384984.md) against a US-led attack.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] In his New Year's Day speech on 2 January 2017, Kim Jong Un said that the country was in the "last stage" of preparations to test-fire an [intercontinental ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBM).[^cite_note-KCNA_Jan_2,_20172-175] On 4 July, North Korea conducted the first publicly announced flight test of its ICBM [Hwasong-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-14), timed to coincide with the U.S. [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)) celebrations.[^cite_note-176] On 3 September, the country conducted its [sixth nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_North_Korean_nuclear_test).[^cite_note-177] On 28 November 2017, North Korea tested the [Hwasong-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-15) missile, which became the first ballistic missile developed by North Korea that is theoretically capable of reaching all of the US mainland.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-Albert-164] In response, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) enacted a series of [sanctions against North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_North_Korea) for its nuclear program and missile tests.[^cite_note-179]

Until 2022, North Korea's stated policy position was that nuclear weapons "will never be abused or used as a means for preemptive strike", but if there is an "attempt to have recourse to military force against us" North Korea may use their "most powerful offensive strength in advance to punish them". This was not a full [no first use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_first_use) policy.[^cite_note-38north-20201013-180] This policy changed in 2022 with a law approved by the SPA, which states that in the case of an attack against the top leadership or the nuclear command and control system, nuclear attacks against the enemy would be launched automatically. Additionally, the new law indicates that if Kim Jong Un was killed, the authorization of nuclear strikes would pass to a senior official.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

### Foreign relations

See also: [List of international trips made by Kim Jong Un](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_trips_made_by_Kim_Jong_Un)

#### China



Kim Jong Un with CCP General Secretary [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) and Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) prior to the start of the [2025 China Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China_Victory_Day_Parade)

[Relations with China](15980883.md) initially deteriorated under Kim due to his nuclear weapons program. On 30 November 2012, Kim met with [Li Jianguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Jianguo), member of the [Politburo](51851.md) of the ruling [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) and first-ranking [vice chairman of the National People's Congress Standing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairperson_of_the_Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress), who "briefed Kim on the [18th CCP National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_National_Congress_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party)", according to the KCNA.[^cite_note-letter_Dec_3-183] A letter from [General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) was hand-delivered during the discussion.[^cite_note-letter_Dec_3-183]

China condemned North Korea's nuclear tests in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_North_Korean_nuclear_test),[^cite_note-184] [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2016_North_Korean_nuclear_test),[^cite_note-185] and 2017[^cite_note-186] China also banned imports of North Korean coal in February 2017,[^cite_note-187] while enforcing UN Security Council sanctions on North Korea put in 2017.[^cite_note-188] In response, the Korean Central News Agency published an unprecedented criticism of China, accusing it of "big-power chauvinism";[^cite_note-189] KCNA later published articles criticizing Chinese state media.[^cite_note-190]

The relationship started improving in March 2018, when [Kim visited Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim%E2%80%93Xi_meetings), meeting with CCP General Secretary Xi Jinping,[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] marking his first foreign trip since assuming power.[^cite_note-193] From 7–8 May, Kim made a second visit to China, meeting with Xi in [Dalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian).[^cite_note-Surprise-194] A further third meeting happened on 19–20 June, when Kim travelled to Beijing to meet Xi.[^cite_note-195] Kim again met Xi in Beijing on 7–10 January 2019.[^cite_note-196] On 20–21 June 2019, Xi travelled to Pyongyang, the first visit by a Chinese leader to North Korea since CCP General Secretary [Hu Jintao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Jintao) visited in 2005.[^cite_note-197] On 2 September 2025, North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, accompanied by many other North Korean dignitaries, traveled to Beijing by special train to attend the 80th anniversary of the [China Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China_Victory_Day_Parade), marking his first visit to China since 2019, as well as the first time he attended a multilateral event.[^cite_note-198] In June 2026, Xi [visited North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_state_visit_by_Xi_Jinping_to_North_Korea), marking his first visit since 2019.[^cite_note-199]

#### South Korea and the United States

In his 2018 New Year Speech, Kim announced that he was open to dialogue with [South Korea](27019.md) with a view to take part in the [upcoming Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_at_the_2018_Winter_Olympics) in the South.[^cite_note-200] The [Seoul–Pyongyang hotline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul%E2%80%93Pyongyang_hotline) was reopened after almost two years.[^cite_note-201] North and South Korea marched together in the Olympics opening ceremony, and fielded a [united women's ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_women's_national_ice_hockey_team).[^cite_note-202] In addition to the athletes, Kim sent an unprecedented high-level delegation including his sister, Kim Yo Jong, and President of the Presidium, [Kim Yong Nam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yong-nam), and performers such as the [Samjiyon Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samjiyon_Orchestra).[^cite_note-Delegation-203] On 5 March, he had a meeting with South Korea's Chief of the National Security Office, Chung Eui Yong, in Pyongyang.[^cite_note-204]

At the [April 2018 inter-Korean summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2018_inter-Korean_summit), Kim and South Korean President [Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in) signed the [Panmunjom Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panmunjom_Declaration), pledging to convert the [Korean Armistice Agreement](30076555.md) into a full peace treaty, formally ending the [Korean War](16772.md), by the end of the year.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] On 26 May, Kim had a second and unannounced meeting in the North Korean side of [Panmunjom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panmunjom), meeting with Moon to discuss his proposed summit with US President Donald Trump in Singapore.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209]

Kim and U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) shake hands at the start of the [2019 Koreas–United States DMZ Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Koreas%E2%80%93United_States_DMZ_Summit) in July 2019

On 10 June, Kim arrived in [Singapore](27318.md) and met with Prime Minister [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong).[^cite_note-Singapore-210] On 12 June, Kim held his [first summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_North_Korea%E2%80%93United_States_Singapore_Summit) with Trump and signed a declaration, affirming a commitment to peace, nuclear disarmament, and the [repatriation of the remains of U.S. war dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovery_of_US_human_remains_from_the_Korean_War).[^cite_note-Rosenfeld-2018-211] This marked the first-ever meeting between leaders of North Korea and the United States.[^cite_note-Rosenfeld-2018-211]

In September, Kim held another [summit with Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2018_inter-Korean_summit) in Pyongyang. Kim agreed to dismantle North Korea's nuclear weapons facilities if the United States took reciprocal action. The two governments also announced that they would establish buffer zones on their borders to prevent clashes.[^cite_note-212]

In February 2019, Kim held [another summit with Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_North_Korea%E2%80%93United_States_Hanoi_Summit) in [Hanoi](56667.md), Vietnam, which Trump cut short on the second day without an agreement. The Trump administration said that the North Koreans wanted complete sanctions relief, while the North Koreans said that they were only asking for partial sanctions relief.[^cite_note-213] On 30 June 2019, in the Korean DMZ, Kim again met with Trump, shaking hands warmly and expressing hope for peace. Kim and Trump then joined Moon Jae-in for a brief chat.[^cite_note-214] Talks in Stockholm began on 5 October 2019 between US and North Korean negotiating teams but broke down after one day.[^cite_note-215] During this period, Trump and Kim established a personal relationship and exchanged at least 27 letters in which the two men described a warm personal friendship.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217]

By 2020, however, negotiations almost completely stalled without progress on denuclearization,[^cite_note-218] with both Trump and Kim focusing on domestic issues.[^cite_note-219] North Korean foreign ministry further criticized the Trump administration that year for "empty promise\[s]",[^cite_note-220] and further took action by demolishing the four-story joint-liaison office building it shared with South Korea on 17 June.[^cite_note-USA-Today-demolition-joint-office-221] North Korea further ignored attempts at outreach by the administration of president [Joe Biden](145422.md),[^cite_note-222] and Kim said in October 2021 that the "US has been frequently sending signals that they are not hostile towards our country, but there is no single evidence that they are not hostile",[^cite_note-223] and also criticized South Korea for "destroying the military balance in the Korean peninsula and increasing military instability and danger".[^cite_note-224]

In December 2023, during a speech at the 9th plenum of the [8th Central Committee of the Workers' Party of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Central_Committee_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea), Kim called for a "fundamental turnabout" in North Korea's stance towards South Korea, calling the South the "enemy".[^cite_note-Seo-2023-225] He stated "the party's comprehensive conclusion after reviewing decades-long inter-Korean relations is that reunification can never be achieved with those ROK riffraffs that defined the 'unification by absorption' and 'unification under liberal democracy' as their state policy", which he said is in "sharp contradiction with what our line of national reunification was: one nation, one state with two systems".[^cite_note-Kim-2024-226]

Kim cited [South Korean constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_South_Korea)'s claims over the entire Korean Peninsula and South Korean President [Yoon Suk Yeol](62877952.md)'s policy towards the north as evidence that South Korea is an unsuitable partner for reunification.[^cite_note-Seo-2023-225] He said the relations between the two Koreas currently were "states hostile to each other and the relations between two belligerent states" and no longer ones that are "consanguineous or homogeneous",[^cite_note-227] continuing by saying it is "unsuitable" to discuss the issue of reunification "with this strange clan \[ROK], who is no more than a colonial stooge of the U.S. despite the rhetorical word \[we used to use]—'the fellow countrymen." Kim also instructed the WPK on reforming organizations related to inter-Korean relations, including the WPK's [United Front Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Front_Department_of_the_Workers'_Party_of_Korea).[^cite_note-Kim-2024-226]

#### Russia



Kim meeting with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) during the [North Korea–Russia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_North_Korea%E2%80%93Russia_summit), April 2019

On 25 April 2019, Kim held his [first meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_visit_by_Kim_Jong_Un_to_Russia) with Russian president Vladimir Putin in [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok). Kim Jong Un requested Russian leader Vladimir Putin's help in resolving a nuclear stalemate with the U.S.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229]

North Korea under Kim [supported](78396607.md) [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)) in 2022, blaming the "hegemonic policy" of the US for the war, recognizing the independence of the breakaway states of [Donetsk](42425747.md) and [Luhansk People's Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk) in [Eastern Ukraine](13065940.md)[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-Yoon-2023-231] as well as recognizing [Russia's unilateral annexation of Donetsk, Kherson, Luhansk and Zaporizhzhia oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts) on 30 September.[^cite_note-232] In September 2022, US intelligence said that Russia was buying millions of artillery shells and rockets from North Korea due to the sanctions caused by Russia's invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-233] A February 2023 report by the [Center for Strategic International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies) (CSIS) stated that after dropping to nearly zero during the COVID-19 era, trade between North Korea and Russia rebounded back to pre-pandemic levels.[^cite_note-Yoon-2023-231]



Kim with Russian Minister of Defence [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu) at a [parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_parades_in_North_Korea#2023_3) in Pyongyang, on the occasion of the [Day of Victory in the Great Fatherland Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Victory_in_the_Great_Fatherland_Liberation_War), 27 July 2023



Kim with [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at [Vostochny Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostochny_Cosmodrome), September 2023

In July 2023, Russian Minister of Defence [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu) and a Chinese delegation led by CCP Politburo member [Li Hongzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Hongzhong) arrived in North Korea for the 70th anniversary of the end of the Korean War.[^cite_note-234] Shoigu met with Kim Jong Un and North Korean Defense Minister [Kang Sun Nam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kang_Sun-nam).[^cite_note-235] In September 2023, Kim Jong Un visited Russia in his first foreign trip since 2019. [The meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_North_Korea%E2%80%93Russia_summit) lasted over four hours at the [Vostochny Cosmodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostochny_Cosmodrome) in the [Amur Oblast](87627.md), and was described as underpinning how the two countries' interests are aligning. During the meeting, Kim once again gave his support for Russia's "sacred fight" against the west, expressing his "...support for all the measures taken by the Russian government, and \[he] takes this opportunity again to affirm that \[he] will always be with Russia." When asked if Russia would help North Korea build satellites, presumably in return for ammunition, Putin said "that's why \[they] came here."[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

In November 2023, the NIS reported that North Korea had exported over one million shells to Russia since August 2023,[^cite_note-238] compared to a combined 2 million shells provided by NATO members to Ukraine since the start of the Russian invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-239] In February 2024, Kim received a car as a gift from Putin.[^cite_note-240] Four months later Putin arrived in Pyongyang and held talks with Kim. It was Putin's second visit to North Korea, since 2000.[^cite_note-241] Both sides signed the [North Korean–Russian Treaty on Comprehensive Strategic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean%E2%80%93Russian_Treaty_on_Comprehensive_Strategic_Partnership) as an agreement of close mutual defence corporation and militant friendship.[^cite_note-242] By the end of 2024, Russia and North Korea had exchanged delegations on AI, cyber-operations, intelligence, science and technology, sports, agriculture, and culture.[^cite_note-\:0-243]

In October 2024 North Korea sent military personnel to Russia who fought against Ukraine for Russia in Russian uniforms and under Russian command.[^cite_note-244] According to the United States and its allies, by the end of 2024 North Korea had sent 11,000 troops to fight for Russia, along with munitions including at least six million heavy artillery rounds and 100 ballistic missiles. Although North Korea initially denied that it was involved in the Russo-Ukrainian war, in April 2025 Kim publicly acknowledged that North Korean troops were fighting alongside Russian forces to "annihilate and wipe out the Ukrainian neo-Nazi occupiers." According to US historian and former diplomat [Jung H. Pak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jung_H._Pak), in addition to payments for arms and soldiers Russia likely provided North Korea with various commodities and assisted it in accessing the global financial system to pay its workers, circumventing UN sanctions.[^cite_note-\:0-243]

### COVID-19 pandemic

During 2020, Kim claimed success in combating the [COVID-19 pandemic in North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_North_Korea), after putting the country in isolation and limiting public gatherings.[^cite_note-245] In April 2020, a three-week absence from public view led to speculation that Kim was seriously ill or dead, but no clear evidence of any health problem came to light.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] He appeared rarely in public over the following months, possibly because of health problems or the risk of COVID-19.[^cite_note-248] In August, it was reported that Kim had ceded a degree of authority to his sister, Kim Yo Jong, giving her responsibility for relations with South Korea and the United States and making her his *de facto* second-in-command.[^cite_note-249]

On 5 September 2020, Kim toured the areas hit by [Typhoon Maysak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Maysak_\(2020\)). He also replaced the local provincial party committee chairman and ordered Pyongyang officials to lead a recovery effort.[^cite_note-250] His ruling party also pledged harsh punishment for the city and provincial officials, stating that they failed to protect the residents from the disaster.[^cite_note-251] Kim fired [Kim Song Il](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Song-il_\(North_Korean_politician\)), who was chairman of the [South Hamgyong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hamgyong_Province) Workers' Party of Korea Committee.[^cite_note-252] In January 2022, a North Korean [KCTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Central_Television) documentary, "2021, A Great Victorious Year", was released, which appeared to address Kim's sudden weight loss and infrequent public appearances. It said that Kim's body had "completely withered away" as he "suffered" for the people during 2021, completing tasks hitherto unpublicized while North Korea faced "challenges" and "worst-ever hardships".[^cite_note-253]

North Korea claimed to have detected no cases of COVID-19 until May 2022, although several South Korean academics doubt this claim, pointing to restrictions and economic data.[^cite_note-254] In May 2022, North Korea announced that its first COVID-19 outbreak had started in April. In a meeting with the WPK, Kim ordered "all the cities and counties of the whole country to thoroughly lock down", and called for the mobilization of emergency reserve medical supplies.[^cite_note-255] In the days that followed the country's announcement, hundreds of thousands of new cases of fevers were reported, as well as 27 related deaths related to fever of unidentified origins, among which one death was confirmed as from the [Omicron variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omicron_variant) according to state-media KCNA.[^cite_note-Yang-2022-256] Kim spoke further at a subsequent WPK meeting, stating that the virus had brought "great turmoil" to his country, and urged the party and people to remain unified and organized in their efforts to combat the virus.[^cite_note-Yang-2022-256] Kim went on to blame the crisis on incompetence and irresponsibility on the part of the party organizations, and also cast blame on "negligence including drug overdose due to lack of knowledge of treatment methods" as the reason for most of the deaths since the outbreak.[^cite_note-Yang-2022-256] As part of the country's response to the COVID-19 outbreak, Kim stated that he looked to learn from the response mounted by China.[^cite_note-257] By the end of May, North Korean state media reported the COVID-19 outbreak was "controlled and improved across the country" following a re-evaluation by Kim and the WPK.[^cite_note-258] In August 2022, Kim Yo Jong indicated that Jong Un had contracted the virus.[^cite_note-259]

## Personal life

### Personality

Kenji Fujimoto, a Japanese chef who was Kim Jong Il's personal cook, described Kim Jong Un as "a chip off the old block, a spitting image of his father in terms of face, body shape, and personality".[^cite_note-McCurry2011-260] Kim is a fan of basketball, and his favorite teams include the Chicago Bulls and the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers).[^cite_note-kimpersonality-36][^cite_note-261] On 26 February 2013, Kim Jong Un met basketball player [Dennis Rodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Rodman),[^cite_note-262] which led many reporters to speculate that Rodman was the first American whom Kim had met.[^cite_note-263] During Rodman's trip, *[Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_\(magazine\))* magazine correspondent [Ryan Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Duffy_\(correspondent\)) said that Kim was "socially awkward" and avoided eye contact.[^cite_note-Vice_2013-264]

According to Cheong Seong-chang of the [Sejong Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejong_Institute), Kim Jong Un has greater visible interest in the welfare of his people and engages in greater interaction with them than his father did.[^cite_note-265] South Koreans who saw Kim at the summit in April 2018 described him as straightforward, humorous, and attentive.[^cite_note-266] After meeting him, Donald Trump said, "I learned he was a talented man. I also learned he loves his country very much." He added that Kim had a "great personality" and was "very smart".[^cite_note-267]

### Public image



Kim and Vladimir Putin watching a military parade in Pyongyang.

*[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine ranked Kim as the [36th most powerful person in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_World's_Most_Powerful_People) in 2018, the highest amongst Koreans.[^cite_note-268]

In a 2013 poll, 61.7% of North Korean defectors in South Korea said that Kim Jong Un was probably supported by most of his countrymen, an increase from the 55.7% approval rating for his father in a similar survey done two years earlier.[^cite_note-269] In a poll of South Koreans conducted following the [May 2018 inter-Korean summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2018_inter-Korean_summit), 78% of respondents said they trusted Kim, compared with 10% approval a couple months prior.[^cite_note-270]

The nickname "Kim Fatty the Third" (Chinese: 金三胖; pinyin: *Jīn Sān Pàng*) began trending among Chinese users of the websites [Baidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidu) and [Weibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sina_Weibo) in late 2016. In response, the North Korean government successfully petitioned the Chinese government to censor the nickname on all Chinese websites.[^cite_note-271]

### Wealth

See also: [Residences of North Korean leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residences_of_North_Korean_leaders)

The *[International Business Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Business_Times)* reported Kim to have 17 luxury palaces around North Korea, a fleet of 100 (mostly European) luxury cars, a private jet, and a 100-foot (30 m) yacht.[^cite_note-Villasanta2019-272] Dennis Rodman described his trip to a private island owned by Kim Jong Un: "It's like [Hawaii](13270.md), [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza), or [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) but he's the only one that lives there."[^cite_note-273]

In 2012, *[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider)* reported that there were "\[s]igns of a rise in luxury goods ... creeping out of North Korea since Kim Jong Un took over" and that his "wife [Ri Sol Ju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ri_Sol-ju) was photographed holding what appeared to be an expensive [Dior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dior) handbag, worth almost \$1,594 – an average year's salary in North Korea".[^cite_note-Taylor2012-274] According to diplomatic sources, "Kim Jong Un likes to drink and party all night like his father and ordered the \[imported sauna] equipment to help him beat [hangovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangover) and fatigue."[^cite_note-275]

In 2018, Kim reportedly received delivery of two armored [Mercedes-Maybach S600s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Maybach_S600), each valued at \$500,000, through an illicit shipping network in violation of international sanctions.[^cite_note-276]

### Health



A growth on Kim's cheek, June 2024.

In 2009, reports suggested that Kim Jong Un was a [diabetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetic) and suffered from [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension).[^cite_note-huff-59] Like his grandfather and father, he is also known to [smoke cigarettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_in_North_Korea).[^cite_note-277]

Kim Jong Un did not appear in public for six weeks in September and October 2014. State media reported that he was suffering from an "uncomfortable physical condition". Previously, he had been [limping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limping).[^cite_note-278] When he reappeared, he was using a walking stick.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280] In September 2015, the South Korean government commented that Kim appeared to have gained 30 kg (66 lb) in body mass over the previous five years, reaching an estimated weight of 130 kg (290 lb).[^cite_note-281]

In April 2020, Kim was not seen in public for 20 days, leading to rumours that he was critically ill or dead.[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] In June 2021, following a one-month-long absence from the public eye, outside observers noted that Kim had lost a considerable amount of weight.[^cite_note-284] It was speculated that he had lost 10 to 20 kg (22 to 44 lb).[^cite_note-285] In July 2024, South Korea's spy agency reported that Kim had regained weight and was suffering from obesity-related health issues, including high blood pressure and diabetes. North Korean officials were reportedly seeking new medications abroad for his conditions.[^cite_note-286] A growth, seen on his cheek as early as late 2020, has been noted to appear to be growing.[^cite_note-287]

### Family

Main article: [Kim family (North Korea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_family_\(North_Korea\))

See also: [Kim (Korean surname) § Jeonju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_\(Korean_surname\)#Jeonju)



Kim (holding envelope) with [Chung Eui-yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chung_Eui-yong). Kim's sister [Kim Yo Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yo_Jong) (on the right) is said to be very close to him.

On 25 July 2012, North Korean state media reported for the first time that Kim Jong Un was married to Ri Sol Ju.[^cite_note-\:1-288][^cite_note-fox-289] Ri, who was believed to be in her early 20s, had been accompanying Kim Jong Un to public appearances for several weeks prior to the announcement.[^cite_note-fox-289] According to a South Korean analyst, Kim Jong Il had hastily arranged the marriage after suffering a stroke in 2008, the two married in 2009, and they had a son in 2010.[^cite_note-Sky-children-290][^cite_note-\:1-288] Dennis Rodman, after visiting in 2013, reported that they had a second newborn child, a daughter named [Kim Ju Ae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Ju_Ae).[^cite_note-Sky-children-290][^cite_note-NationalPost2013-291] According to South Korean intelligence sources, the couple is believed to have had a third child, a daughter, in February 2017.[^cite_note-Sky-children-290][^cite_note-292]

On 18 November 2022, Kim Jong Un was seen reviewing key military arsenals with his daughter Ju Ae.[^cite_note-293] The two were seen together again at a gathering with missile scientists later in the same month.[^cite_note-294] She has since routinely accompanied him.[^cite_note-295] In February 2026 South Korea's NIS assessed that Ju Ae, then aged approximately 13, was his designated successor. This was based on information including her more prominent appearances in state media, alleged policy input, and visit to the 2025 China Victory Day Parade.[^cite_note-296]

Kim is sometimes accompanied by his younger sister Kim Yo Jong,[^cite_note-Lee2012-17] who is said to be instrumental in creating his public image and organising public events for him.[^cite_note-297] According to Kim Yong-hyun, a professor of North Korean studies at [Dongguk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguk_University) in Seoul, and others, the promotion of Yo Jong and others is a sign that "the Kim Jong Un government has ended its co-existence with the remnants of the previous Kim Jong Il government by carrying out a generational replacement in the party's key elite posts".[^cite_note-298]

On 13 February 2017, Kim Jong-nam, the exiled half-brother of Kim Jong Un, was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Kim_Jong-nam) with the [nerve agent VX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VX_\(nerve_agent\)) while walking through Terminal 2 at [Kuala Lumpur International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur_International_Airport).[^cite_note-299] Kim Jong Un is widely believed to have ordered the assassination.[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-delusional-301]
