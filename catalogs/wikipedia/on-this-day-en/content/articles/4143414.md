**Pre-industrial society** refers to social attributes and forms of political and cultural organization that were prevalent before the advent of the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), which occurred from 1750 to 1850. *Pre-industrial* refers to a time before there were [machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine) and [tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool) to help perform tasks *en masse*. Pre-industrial civilization dates back to centuries ago, but the main era known as the pre-industrial society occurred right before the [industrial society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_society). Pre-Industrial societies vary from region to region depending on the culture of a given area or history of social and political life. Europe was known for its [feudal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) and the [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance).

The term "pre-industrial" is also used as a benchmark for environmental conditions before the development of industrial society: for example, the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md), adopted in Paris on 12 December, 2015 and in force from 4 November, 2016, "aims to limit [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) to well below 2, preferably to 1.5 degrees [celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius_scale), compared to *pre-industrial* levels."[^cite_note-1] The date for the end of the "pre-industrial era" is not defined.[^cite_note-2]

## Common attributes



George Walker, 1814

- Limited [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_production)

- Extreme [agricultural economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_economics)

- Limited [division of labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_labor). In pre-industrial societies, production was relatively simple and the number of specialized crafts was limited.

- Limited variation of [social classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class)

- [Parochialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parochialism)—[Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication) were limited between communities in pre-industrial societies. Few had the opportunity to see or hear beyond their own village. Industrial societies grew with the help of faster means of communication, having more [information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information) at hand about the world, allowing [knowledge transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_transfer) and [cultural diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_diffusion) between them.

- Populations grew at substantial rates

  [^cite_note-3]

- Social classes: peasants and lords

  [^cite_note-Persson-4]

- [Subsistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_economy) level of living

  [^cite_note-Persson-4]

- Population dependent on [peasants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasant) for food

  [^cite_note-Persson-4]

- People were located in [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village) rather than in [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City)

- A large amount of the population is peasants (usually around 98%)

### Labor conditions

Harsh working conditions had been prevalent long before the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) took place, especially for [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaves). Pre-industrial society was very static, with [child labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labour) and dirty living conditions being common. However, long [working hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_time#Hunter-gatherer) were not as equally prevalent before the Industrial Revolution.[^cite_note-industrial4-5]
