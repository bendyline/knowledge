&#x20;A **cost-of-living crisis** is a situation or period of high [inflation](38286.md) with increases in the nominal [cost of living](522934.md) while [real wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_wages) decrease. As a result, [real income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_income) and living standards are squeezed to the point that people cannot afford the [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) that they were previously accustomed to. The population becomes 'poorer' than it used to be in real terms. This is in contrast to a situation in which wages are rising with [inflation](38286.md) and workers' standard of living remains unchanged.[^cite_note-1]

During the 2020s, a cost-of-living crisis impacted many countries around the world amid [global inflation](69605317.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-nz-ng-3] In February 2023, 3 out of 4 consumers globally were worried about the rising cost of everyday expenses.[^cite_note-4] *[The Big Issue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Issue)* defines a cost of living crisis as "a situation in which the cost of everyday essentials like groceries and [bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invoice) are rising faster than average household incomes".[^cite_note-tcolc-5]

## Effects on society

### Health

Cost-of-living crises have had significant and wide-ranging negative consequences for mental and physical [well-being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-being).[^cite_note-colhtc-6] For example, high food prices force people to choose to eat cheaper foods with less nutritional value or less food in general. Worse nutrition also leads to a higher likelihood of getting sick from infectious diseases. In poorer countries, there is a higher risk of starvation.[^cite_note-Kilfoyle-2023-7][^cite_note-8] [Public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) can be threatened.[^cite_note-amm-9] Reduced access to household heating and cooling has been demonstrated to cause a range of health issues including respiratory and cardiovascular illness, heat-related illness, poorer mental health and in some cases premature death.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Cold-11]

Mental health also declines across the board due to the stress of being unable to afford to live properly. Rates of depression and anxiety increase. People are also more likely to lose sleep, forego meeting with friends, not engage in their hobbies, and skip out on exercising. A cost-of-living crisis will lead to a higher demand for social and health services. However, higher operating expenses and a lack of staff to meet the higher demand will result in squeezed budgets and worse service. Overworked and underpaid staff will also be more likely to quit, creating a vicious cycle.[^cite_note-Kilfoyle-2023-7][^cite_note-12]

### Job market

Workers are more likely to quit their job and switch to a higher-paying one because their current job no longer pays enough to cover their [lifestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifestyle_\(social_sciences\)) expenses.[^cite_note-13] Workers are most likely to strike in an effort to improve their circumstances.[^cite_note-14]

### Business and investments

Small businesses will be negatively affected due to higher material and energy prices.[^cite_note-15] Customers also have less purchasing power, and so will purchase fewer items from companies who do not sell essential goods.[^cite_note-16] It becomes more difficult to predict investment returns because of market [volatility](11930108.md) and uncertainty. People are also less likely to invest in businesses or the stock market because they have less [disposable income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_household_and_per_capita_income).[^cite_note-17]

### Spending and debt

Consumer confidence drops.[^cite_note-18] Non-essential spending is reduced, with [luxury goods](858665.md), [travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel) and [fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion) seeing the greatest declines.[^cite_note-19] People are more likely to take on [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt) in order to keep up with bills and living expenses.[^cite_note-20]

## By region



[2012 Aysén protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Ays%C3%A9n_protests) due to the high cost of living in [Patagonia](65433.md)

In 2022, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that numerous countries were being impacted by a cost of living crisis, including [Belgium](3343.md), [Germany](11867.md), [India](14533.md), [Nigeria](21383.md), [Philippines](23440.md), [South Africa](17416221.md) and [Taiwan](25734.md).[^cite_note-nz-ng-3]

### Australia

In the 2020s, [Australia](4689264.md) faced a cost of living crisis, impacting [food security](216361.md) and increasing demands for food banks.[^cite_note-21]

### Brazil

In 2022, [Brazil](3383.md) saw double digit inflation rates.[^cite_note-colcro-22] Despite Brazil being an [agricultural powerhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Brazil), [food prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_prices) are rising.[^cite_note-colcro-22]

### Canada

In 2024, [CBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News) reported many Canadians were struggling amidst rising living costs and working multiple jobs.[^cite_note-23]

### Ireland

[Ireland](14560.md) is currently experiencing a cost-of-living crisis, resulting in Ireland becoming the most expensive country in the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-24] This crisis has been accompanied by crises in housing, [fuel](82911459.md) and healthcare. A reported 36% of Irish families were not able to pay at least one essential household bill.[^cite_note-25] This economic strain has contributed to public dissatisfaction and protests against the government.

### New Zealand

New Zealand is currently experiencing a cost-of-living crisis, with increasing unemployment and a contracting GDP alongside record numbers leaving the country, but The Treasury and Reserve Bank government departments expect New Zealand's economy to make an economic turnaround sometime between 2026 and 2030.

### United Kingdom

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [United Kingdom cost-of-living crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_cost-of-living_crisis).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=United_Kingdom_cost-of-living_crisis\&action=edit)]

In late 2021, the prices of many essential goods in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) began increasing faster than [household incomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_in_the_United_Kingdom), resulting in a fall in [real incomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_income). The phenomenon has been termed a cost-of-living crisis. It is due in part to the [economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic), including [a global surge in inflation](69605317.md), [wage stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wage_stagnation) attributed to the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\))'s [austerity programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_government_austerity_programme), as well as the economic instability and [mass migration](1247786.md#Post-withdrawal_from_the_EU)[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] caused by [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_effects_of_Brexit) and the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70188989.md).

While all in the UK are affected by rising prices, the crisis most substantially affects people living in [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_United_Kingdom). The British government has responded with measures including grants, tax rebates, and subsidies to electricity and gas suppliers. Regular pay began to outpace inflation beginning in May 2023,[^cite_note-28] but living costs have remained at elevated levels, and have continued to increase faster than headline inflation into 2025.[^cite_note-United_Kingdom_cost-of-living_crisis_\:0-29] The [Joseph Rowntree Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Rowntree_Foundation) projected in 2025 that disposable incomes would continue to decline for the rest of the decade.[^cite_note-30]
