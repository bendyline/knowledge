**Heilongjiang**[^cite_note-6] is an inland [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China). It is the northernmost and easternmost province of the country and contains China's northernmost point (in [Mohe City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohe_City) along the Amur) and easternmost point (at the confluence of the [Amur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur) and [Ussuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ussuri) rivers).

The province is bordered by [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin) to the south and [Inner Mongolia](55064.md) to the west. It also [shares a border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Russia_border) with [Russia](25391.md) ([Amur Oblast](87627.md), [Jewish Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Autonomous_Oblast), [Khabarovsk Krai](87626.md), [Primorsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai) and [Zabaykalsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabaykalsky_Krai)) to the north and east. The capital and the largest city of the province is [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin). Among Chinese provincial-level [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_China), Heilongjiang is the [sixth-largest by total area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_area), the [20th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population), and the [second-poorest by GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) after only [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) province.

The province takes its name from the [Amur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur) river (in Chinese: Heilong River), which marks [the border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Russia_border) between the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) and [Russia](25391.md).

Heilongjiang has significant agricultural production,[^cite_note-Stratfor-7] and raw materials, such as timber, oil, and coal.

## Etymology

The province takes its name from the [Amur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur) river, whose name in [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) is *Heilongjiang* ('black [dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_dragon) river').[^cite_note-\:12-8]

Ancient Chinese records and other sources state that Heilongjiang was inhabited by people such as the [Sushen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushen), [Buyeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyeo), the [Mohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohe_people), and the [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people). [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) [Donghu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghu_people) lived in [Inner Mongolia](55064.md) and the western part of Heilongjiang.[^cite_note-9] Some names are Manchu or Mongolian.[^cite_note-10] The eastern portion of Heilongjiang was ruled by the [Bohai Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balhae) between the 7th and 10th centuries, followed by the Khitan [Liao dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty). The Jurchen [Jin dynasty (1115–1234)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(1115%E2%80%931234\)) that subsequently ruled much of north China arose within the borders of modern Heilongjiang.



Heilongjiang and Jilin Provinces on a French map dated to 1734

Heilongjiang as an administrative entity was created in 1683, during the [Kangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor) era of the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu) [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), from the northwestern part of the [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin) province.[^cite_note-edmonds-11] This Heilongjiang Province only included the western part of today's Heilongjiang Province, and was under the supervision of the General of Heilongjiang (Sahaliyan Ula i Jiyanggiyūn) (the title is also translated as the Military Governor of Heilongjiang; *jiyanggiyūn* is the Manchu reading of the Chinese word 將軍 *jiāngjūn*; "military leader, general" and is cognate with [Japanese](15606.md) *[shōgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgun)*), whose power extended, according to the [Treaty of Nerchinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nerchinsk), as far north as the [Stanovoy Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanovoy_Mountains). The eastern part of what's today Heilongjiang remained under the supervision of the General of Jilin (Girin i Jiyanggiyūn), whose power reached the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan). These areas deep in [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria) were closed off to [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) migration.



Seal of the Guard General of Heilongjiang at the Heilongjiang General Mansion

The original seat of the Military Governor of Heilongjiang, as established in 1683, was in Heilongjiang City (also known as [Aigun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aigun) or [Heihe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heihe), or, in [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_language), Saghalien Ula), located on the Amur river. However, already in 1690 the seat of the governor was transferred to [Nenjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenjiang_County) (Mergen) on the [Nen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nen_River), and, in 1699, further south to [Qiqihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiqihar). According to modern historians, the moves may have been driven by supply considerations: Nenjiang and Qiqihar are connected by a convenient waterway (Nen River) with southern Manchuria, whereas accessing Aigun (Heihe) would require either sailing all the way down the [Sungari River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungari_River) until its confluence with the Amur and then up the Amur to Heihe, or using a [portage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage) over the Lesser Xing'an Mountains between the Nen River valley and the Amur valley. An additional advantage of Qiqihar may have been its location at the junction of a northbound road (to Nenjiang) and a westbound one (to Mongolia), enabling its garrison to defend both against the Russians and the Ölöt [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols).[^cite_note-12]

Little Qing Military presence existed north of Aigun. According to the 18th- and early-20th-century European sources and the reports of the Russians in the 1850s, the farthest Qing "advance guard" post was at Ulusu-Modon (Ulussu-Mudan) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 乌鲁苏穆丹 *Wūlǔsūmùdān*), near the Amur river's famous S-shaped meander. (The post was on the left (north) bank of the river, lost to the Russians in 1860.)

In 1858 and 1860, the [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_Dynasty) government was [forced to give up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_Annexation) all land beyond the Amur and Ussuri rivers to the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), cutting off the Qing Empire from the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) and giving Heilongjiang its present northern and eastern borders. At the same time, [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria) was opened to [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) migration by the Qing government. By the early twentieth century, due to the *[Chuang Guandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuang_Guandong)*, the Han Chinese had become the dominant ethnic group in the region.[^cite_note-13]

In 1931, Japanese forces [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria) Heilongjiang. In 1932, the Japanese completed their conquest of the province, which became part of the [Japanese](15573.md) [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo).

In 1945, Japanese forces in Manchuria were [defeated by the Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Manchuria). During the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), Soviet forces aided the Chinese communists. Heilongjiang became the first province to be completely controlled by the communists and [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin) the first major city to be controlled by them.

At the beginning of communist rule, Heilongjiang included only the western portion of the present-day province, and had its capital at Qiqihar. The remaining area was the [province of Songjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songjiang_Province); its capital was Harbin. In 1954, these two provinces were merged into present-day Heilongjiang. During the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), Heilongjiang was also expanded to include [Hulunbuir League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulunbuir_League) and some other areas previously in [Inner Mongolia](55064.md); this has since mostly been reversed.

## Geography

Heilongjiang is a land of varied topographies. Much of the province is dominated by mountain ranges such as the [Greater Khingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khingan) Range and [Lesser Khingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Khingan) Range, [Zhangguangcai Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangguangcai_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Laoye Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laoye_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Wanda Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Mountains). The highest peak is [Datudingzi Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datudingzi_Mountain) at 1,690 metres (5,540 ft), located on the border with [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin) province.[^cite_note-14] The Greater Khingan Range contains China's largest remaining virgin forest and is an important area for China's forestry industry.

The east and southwest of the province, which are relatively flat and low in altitude, feature the [Muling River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muling_River), the [Naoli River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoli_River), the [Songhua River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhua_River), the [Nen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nen_River), and the [Mudan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudan_River), all tributaries of the Amur, while the northern border forms part of the Amur valley. [Xingkai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xingkai_Lake) (or [Khanka Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanka_Lake)) is found on the border with [Russia](25391.md)'s [Primorsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai).

A [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dwa* or *Dwb*) predominates in the province, though areas in the far north are [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate) (Köppen *Dwc*).[^cite_note-15] Winters are long and bitter, with an average of −31 to −15 °C (−24 to 5 °F) in January, and summers are short and warm to very warm with an average of 18 to 23 °C (64 to 73 °F) in July. The annual average rainfall is 400 to 700 millimetres (16 to 28 in), concentrated heavily in summer. Clear weather is prevalent throughout the year, and in the spring, the [Songnen Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songnen_Plain) and the [Sanjiang Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjiang_Plain) provide abundant sources of wind energy.

The province's largest cities include [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin), [Qiqihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiqihar), [Mudanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudanjiang), [Jiamusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiamusi), [Daqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daqing), [Jixi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jixi), [Shuangyashan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuangyashan), [Hegang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegang), [Qitaihe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qitaihe), [Yichun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichun,_Heilongjiang), and [Heihe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heihe).

## Transport

### Roads

Heilongjiang boasts an extensive road network, As of October 2020. it has 165,989 km (103,141 mi) of expressways,[^cite_note-16] highways and other roads. The [Beijing – Harbin Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G1_Beijing%E2%80%93Harbin_Expressway) is the most significant expressway corridor to the province, which begins at the Heilongjiang – Jilin border and ends within the [Harbin Ring Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G1001_Harbin_Ring_Expressway). The [Harbin – Tongjiang Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G1011_Harbin%E2%80%93Tongjiang_Expressway) runs northeast and it links far-flung counties within the jurisdiction of Harbin, Jiamusi and other major counties in Northeast Heilongjiang. Near the end of Harbin – Tongjiang Expressway, [Jiansanjiang–Heixiazi Island Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G1012_Jiansanjiang%E2%80%93Heixiazi_Island_Expressway) branches off the main expressway at Jiansanjiang and connects many state-owned farms at the far east of the province before ending near the Sino-Russian border. The [Suifenhe – Manzhouli Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G10_Suifenhe%E2%80%93Manzhouli_Expressway) is another major corridor it runs southeast to northwest and connects some of the most significant population centers of the province, including Mudanjiang, Harbin, Daqing and Qiqihar, before ending at the Heilongjiang – Inner Mongolia border. The [Hegang – Dalian Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G11_Hegang%E2%80%93Dalian_Expressway), another major expressway that facilitates the transportation of lumber and coal, runs between Hegang and the Heilongjiang – Jilin border in East Heilongjiang.

### Railways

There are 60 railway lines of around 5,300 kilometres (3,300 miles) including a section of the [Eurasian Land Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Land_Bridge). The [Harbin–Dalian high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin%E2%80%93Dalian_high-speed_railway), completed in 2012, stretches from Harbin, Heilongjiang's capital, to [Dalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian) in Liaoning province via [Changchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changchun) and [Shenyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang) comprising 23 stops. It is expected to transport 37 million passengers per year by 2020 and 51 million by 2030.

### Airports

Major airports include [Harbin Taiping International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Taiping_International_Airport), [Qiqihar Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiqihar_Airport), [Mudanjiang Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudanjiang_Airport), [Jiamusi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiamusi_Airport) and [Heihe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heihe_Airport). Harbin International Airport is capable of handling six million passengers every year and connects to over 70 domestic and international cities.

### Tongjiang-Nizhneleninskoye railway bridge

Main article: [Tongjiang-Nizhneleninskoye railway bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongjiang-Nizhneleninskoye_railway_bridge)

The [Tongjiang-Nizhneleninskoye railway bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongjiang-Nizhneleninskoye_railway_bridge) was proposed in 2007 by [Valery Solomonovich Gurevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valery_Solomonovich_Gurevich), the vice-chairman of the [Jewish Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Autonomous_Oblast) in [Russia](25391.md). The railway bridge over the Amur river will connect Tongjiang with [Nizhneleninskoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhneleninskoye), a village in the Jewish Autonomous Oblast.[^cite_note-17]

The Chinese portion of the bridge was finished in July 2016.[^cite_note-NYT71616-18] In December 2016, work began on the Russian portion of the bridge. Completion of structural link between the two sides of the bridge was completed in March 2019.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Opening to rail traffic has been repeatedly delayed, with the December 2019 estimate being "the end of 2020",[^cite_note-21] and then 3rd quarter of 2021.[^cite_note-22]

## Politics

Further information: [List of provincial leaders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_leaders_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)

Like all [governing institutions in mainland China](23239.md), Heilongjiang has a parallel party-government system,[^cite_note-PoliticalSystem2-40] in which the [CCP Heilongjiang Provincial Committee Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Heilongjiang) outranks the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Heilongjiang).[^cite_note-41] The [CCP Heilongjiang Provincial Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilongjiang_Provincial_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) acts as the top policy-formulation body, and has control over the Heilongjiang Provincial People's Government.

In 2022, Heilongjiang's GDP was 1.59 trillion [RMB](66958.md) (\$236 billion in GDP nominal), with a per capita GDP of CN¥51,906 (US\$7,717 in nominal).[^cite_note-GDPdata-3] Its primary, secondary, and tertiary industries contributed ¥360 billion (22.7%), ¥465 billion (29.2%), and ¥764 billion (48%) to GDP, respectively.[^cite_note-GDPdata-3]

Heilongjiang's GDP has been rising steadily since 2003, growing 37% from 2003 to 2007. The value of the private economy reached RMB234 billion in 2006 and accounted for 37.6 percent of the GDP. In that year, the tax revenue from private enterprises hit RMB20.5 billion.

Private enterprises in Heilongjiang led the overall economic growth of the province. Many leading private enterprises have begun to emerge.

### Agriculture

Heilongjiang is home to China's largest plantations of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans), with a total of 14.37 million ha (35.5 million acres) of grain plantation area, including 4 million ha (9.9 million acres) of rice plantation and 5.5 million ha (14 million acres) of corn.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Heilongjiang has vast tracts of black soil ([chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem)), one of the most fertile soil types.[^cite_note-Wen_Dazhong-44][^cite_note-45] Since the early 20th century, cultivation in the black soil belt has expanded by almost 100-fold, and after the 1960s agriculture in the region transformed to modern agriculture with heavy mechanization and an increase of fertilizer use.[^cite_note-Wen_Dazhong-44] Heilongjiang is one of the Asia's leading production areas for [japonica rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japonica_rice), known for high quality brand rice varieties.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-Harbin_rice-47] The introduction of cold-resistant varieties, favorable policies and climate change have all contributed to a significant increase in rice production in recent years.[^cite_note-48] Commercial crops grown include [beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beet), [flax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flax), [sunflowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower).[^cite_note-Harbin_rice-47]

Heilongjiang is also an important source of lumber for China. Pine, especially the [Korean pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_pine) and [larch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larch) are the most important forms of lumber produced in Heilongjiang. Forests are mostly found in the [Greater Khingan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khingan_Mountains) and [Lesser Khingan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Khingan_Mountains), which are also home to protected animal species such as the [Siberian tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_tiger), the [red-crowned crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-crowned_crane), and the [lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynx).

Herding in Heilongjiang is centered upon horses and cattle; the province has the largest number of milk cows and the highest production of milk among all the province-level divisions of China.

### Industry

Heilongjiang is part of [northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China), the country's traditional industrial base. Industry is focused upon coal, petroleum, lumber, machinery, and food. Due to its location, Heilongjiang is also an important gateway for trade with [Russia](25391.md). Since a wave of privatization led to the closure of uncompetitive factories in the 1990s, Manchuria has suffered from stagnation. As a result, the government has started the [Revitalize Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Area_Revitalization_Plan) campaign to deal with this problem, promoting the private sectors as the preferred method of the [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up).

[Petroleum](23195.md) is of great importance in Heilongjiang, and the [Daqing oilfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daqing_oilfields) are an important source of [petroleum for China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_China).[^cite_note-\:1-49] Coal, gold, and [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite) are other important [minerals to be found](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_exploration_in_China) in Heilongjiang. Heilongjiang also has great potential for [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), with potential capacity for 134 gigawatts of power production.[^cite_note-50]

Suifenhe BECD is the largest among the three state-level border-trade zones of Heilongjiang, in terms of investor numbers. Suifenhe BECD has a convenient transport network. The Binzhou-Suifenhe Railway, which connects the Russian Far East Railway, is an important port for export. The railway distance between Suifenhe and Harbin is 548 km (341 mi). Buguranikinai, the corresponding Russian port city, is 21 km (13 mi) away.

By the end of 2022, Heilongjiang Province had a permanent resident population of 30.99 million.[^cite_note-65] This represented a decrease of 260,000 from the previous year. - Ages 0–14: 9.3% of the total population

- Ages 65 and above: 17.8% of the total population

In addition, out-migration has contributed to a continuous population decline since 2014.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### Ethnic groups

The majority of Heilongjiang's population is [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), while [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_nationalities) include the [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu), [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Xibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xibe_people), and [Hezhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezhen).

In Heilongjiang Province, the Han Chinese constitute an overwhelming majority, many of whom are descendants of [immigrants from Shandong and Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuang_Guandong) during the Qing dynasty. Ten indigenous ethnic minorities are officially recognized: the Manchu, Korean, Mongol, Hui, Daur, Xibe, Hezhen, [Oroqen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oroqen_people), [Evenki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenki_people), and [Kyrgyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuyu_Kyrgyz_people) peoples.[^cite_note-mzw-71] Among these, the Hezhen are unique to Heilongjiang Province.[^cite_note-72] Within the province, there is the [Dorbod Mongol Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorbod_Mongol_Autonomous_County), the Meilisi Daur District in Qiqihar, as well as several ethnic townships.

Among the province's permanent residents, the Han population numbers 30,728,612, accounting for 96.48% of the total; all ethnic minorities together total 1,121,476, or 3.52%. Compared with the 2010 Sixth National Population Census, the Han population decreased by 6,210,569 people (−16.81%) but their proportion rose by 0.07 percentage points. Minority populations decreased by 253,334 (−18.43%), and their proportion declined by 0.07 percentage points. Specifically:

- The [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people) population decreased by 164,213 (−21.95%), down 0.12 percentage points in proportion.

- The [Korean Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Chinese) population decreased by 57,683 (−17.6%), down 0.01 percentage points.

- The [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China) population decreased by 13,273 (−10.58%), but their proportion rose by 0.02 percentage points.

- The [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) population decreased by 26,285 (−25.83%), down 0.03 percentage points.

- The [Daur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daur_people) population decreased by 6,607 (−16.4%), with no change in proportion.

- The [Xibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xibe_people) population decreased by 1,349 (−17.73%), with no change in proportion.

- The [Hezhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezhe_people) population increased by 192 (+5.31%), with no change in proportion.

## Tourism



A [Siberian tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_tiger) at [Harbin Siberian Tiger Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Siberian_Tiger_Park)

Harbin, the provincial capital, is a city of contrasts, with Chinese, [Russian](25391.md), and eclectic worldwide influences clearly apparent. [Bukui Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukui_Mosque), a national heritage site, is the largest glazed-tile building in the province.[^cite_note-74] [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox), [Roman Catholic](606848.md), and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) churches as well as [synagogues](48559.md) dot the city.[^cite_note-China_Expat_City_Guide:_Harbin-75]

The long, cold winter is the site of its famed [ice sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_sculpture) exhibitions. In 2007 the 8th Ice and Snow World opened to visitors in Harbin. More than 2,000 ice sculptures were on display at the annual event.[^cite_note-76]

Wudalianchi Lakes are a series of five lakes formed between 1719 and 1721 when volcanic eruption shaped one section of a tributary of the Amur into five interconnected lakes. The second lake in particular is renowned for its irregular geological sights. [Lake Jingbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Jingbo), in [Ning'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ning'an) County, is a section of the [Mudan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudan_River) that has been narrowed and shaped by volcanic eruption into a series of sights, including the [Diaoshuilou Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaoshuilou_Falls).

The province has a [zoological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo) park called "[Harbin Siberian Tiger Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Siberian_Tiger_Park)".[^cite_note-DK2014-77]
