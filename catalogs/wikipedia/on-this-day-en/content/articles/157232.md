The **1990 FIFA World Cup** was the 14th [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the quadrennial [football](10568.md) tournament for men's senior national teams. It was held from 8 June to 8 July 1990 in Italy, the second country to host the event for a second time (after Mexico in 1986). Teams representing 116 national football associations entered and [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) began in April 1988. Twenty-two teams qualified from this process, along with the host nation itself [Italy](362466.md) and defending champions [Argentina](454699.md).

The tournament was won by [West Germany](250204.md), for the third time. They [beat defending champions Argentina 1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) at the [Stadio Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Olimpico) in [Rome](25458.md), a rematch of [the previous final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) four years earlier. Italy ended up finishing third and [England](9904.md) fourth, after both lost their semi-finals in [penalty shoot-outs](511302.md). This was the last tournament to feature a team from West Germany, with the country being [reunified with East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) a few months later in October, as well as teams from the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) before the end of the [Cold War](325329.md) in 1991, as the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_national_football_team), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team), and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) teams all made their final appearances. [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) made their first appearances in the finals. As of 2026, this was the only time the United Arab Emirates qualified for a FIFA World Cup finals. [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) went on an unexpectedly strong run in the tournament, becoming the first African team to reach the quarter-finals of the World Cup. The official match ball was the [Adidas Etrusco Unico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Etrusco_Unico).

The 1990 World Cup is widely regarded as one of the poorest World Cups in terms of the games.[^cite_note-Italy_1990-1][^cite_note-World_Cup_1990-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] It generated an average 2.21 goals per game – a record low that still stands[^cite_note-Record-5] – and a then-record 16 red cards, including the first dismissal in a final. However, the tournament had a significant lasting influence on the game as a whole. In England, the team's success in this tournament led to the resurgence of the [domestic top-flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division), which had suffered from violence on the pitch and hooliganism by spectators throughout the 1980s.[^cite_note-Impact-6] It saw the introduction of the pre-match Fair Play Flag (then inscribed with "Fair Play Please") to encourage fair play. Overly defensive tactics of many teams led to the introduction of the [back-pass rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-pass_rule) in 1992 and [three points for a win](4960100.md) instead of two, both of which have encouraged attacking play, increasing spectator interest in the sport. The tournament also produced some of the World Cup's best remembered moments and stories, including the emergence of African nations, and [Luciano Pavarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti)'s performance of "[Nessun dorma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nessun_dorma)" during the first ever joint concert of [The Three Tenors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Tenors) on July 7, shortly before the match for third place which featured the host team.[^cite_note-Impact-6]

The 1990 World Cup stands as one of the most watched events in television history, garnering an estimated 26.69 billion non-unique viewers over the course of the tournament.[^cite_note-7] This was the first World Cup officially recorded and transmitted in [HDTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDTV), which was a joint effort of the Italian broadcaster [RAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI) and Japan's [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK).[^cite_note-8] The huge success of the broadcasting model also had a lasting impact on the sport around the world.[^cite_note-Impact-6] At the time, it was the most watched World Cup in history in non-unique viewers, later bettered only by the [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) and the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) editions of the competition.[^cite_note-9]

## Host selection

Main article: [FIFA World Cup hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts)

The vote to choose the hosts of the 1990 tournament was held on 19 May 1984 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), Switzerland. Here, the FIFA Executive Committee chose Italy ahead of the only rival bid, the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), by 11 votes to 5.[^cite_note-Hosting-10] This awarding made Italy only the second nation to host two World Cup tournaments after Mexico had also achieved this with their [1986 staging](157231.md), having agreed to replace Colombia as hosts the year before the 1990 hosts were chosen. Italy had previously held the event in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), where they had won their first championship.

Austria, England, France, Greece, West Germany and Yugoslavia also submitted initial applications for the 31 July 1983 deadline.[^cite_note-11] A month later, only England, Greece, Italy and the Soviet Union remained in the hunt after the other contenders all withdrew.[^cite_note-12] All four bids were assessed by FIFA in late 1983, with the final decision over-running into 1984 due to the volume of paperwork involved.[^cite_note-13] In early 1984, England and Greece also withdrew, leading to a two-horse race in the final vote. The [Soviet boycott of the 1984 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics_boycott), announced on the eve of the World Cup decision, was speculated to have been a major factor behind Italy winning the vote so decisively,[^cite_note-14] although this was denied by the FIFA President [João Havelange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Havelange).[^cite_note-Hosting-10] The Soviet state media responded by accusing FIFA of political corruption, and blamed the organisation's American sponsors (chiefly [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola)) for influencing the decision.[^cite_note-15]

Iran also planned to host the competition. They proposed it to FIFA in 1977, but a few months later they withdrew their request.[^cite_note-16]

## Qualification

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification)

116 teams entered the 1990 World Cup, including Italy as host nation and Argentina as reigning World Cup champions, who were both granted automatic qualification. Thus, the remaining 22 finals places were divided among the continental confederations, with 114 initially entering the qualification competition. Due to rejected entries and withdrawals, 103 teams eventually participated in the qualifying stages.

Thirteen places were contested by [UEFA](32332.md) teams (Europe), two by [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) teams (South America), two by [CAF](715265.md) teams (Africa), two by [AFC](715249.md) teams (Asia), and two by [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) teams (North and Central America and Caribbean). The remaining place was decided by a play-off between a CONMEBOL team and a team from the [OFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) (Oceania).

After finishing in third place in [1986](157231.md), [France](250197.md) missed the tournament for the first time since the [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) edition.[^cite_note-17]

[Mexico](808402.md) was disqualified during the qualification process for [fielding four overage players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachirules) in [a prior youth tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_CONCACAF_U-20_Tournament).[^cite_note-18]

Three teams made their debuts; this was the first World Cup to feature [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team), the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team).

Returning after long absences were Egypt, which appeared for the first time since 1934; the [United States](582488.md) (which would not miss a World Cup again until 2018), which competed for the first time since 1950; Colombia, which appeared for the first time since 1962; Romania, which last appeared at the Finals in 1970; and Sweden and the Netherlands, both of which last qualified in 1978. Austria, Cameroon, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia also returned after missing the 1986 tournament.

## Venues

Twelve stadiums in twelve cities were selected to host matches at the 1990 World Cup. The [Stadio San Nicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_San_Nicola) in [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari) and [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin)'s [Stadio delle Alpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_delle_Alpi) were completely new venues opened for the World Cup. Of the twelve stadiums used, only four (San Siro, Luigi Ferraris, Comunale of Florence, and Renato Dall'Ara) had been used for the [1934 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), while [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) was the only host city from 1934 not to be selected for the 1990 tournament.

The ten existing venues all underwent extensive programmes of improvements in preparation for the tournament, forcing many of the club tenants of the stadia to move to temporary homes. Additional seating and roofs were added to most stadia, with further redevelopments seeing running tracks removed and new pitches laid. Due to structural constraints, several of the existing stadia had to be virtually rebuilt to implement the changes required.

Like España '82 and México '86, the group stage of this tournament was organised so an individual group only played in two cities in close proximity to each other. Group A only played in Rome and Florence (hosts [Italy](362466.md) played all but two competitive matches in Rome: their semi-final match was played in Napoli, and their match for third place in Bari), Group B played their matches in Naples and Bari (except for Argentina vs. Cameroon, which was the opening match of the tournament, played in Milan), Group C played their matches in Turin and Genoa, Group D played all their matches in Milan and Bologna, Group E played only in Udine and Verona, and Group F played in the insular cities of Cagliari and Palermo. The cities that hosted the most World Cup matches were the two biggest cities in Italy: Rome and Milan, each hosting six matches, and Bari, Naples, and Turin each hosted five matches. Cagliari, Udine and Palermo were the only cities of the 12 selected that did not host any knockout round matches.

The England national team, at the British government's request, played all 3 of their group stage matches in Cagliari on the island of Sardinia.[^cite_note-ENGseed-20] Hooliganism, [rife in English football in the 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_hooliganism_in_the_United_Kingdom#1980s) had spilled over onto the European continent when 39 mostly Italian Juventus supporters [were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_disaster) and 600 were injured at the 1985 European Cup Final in [Brussels](3708.md) while trying to flee from an attack by Liverpool supporters. This hooliganism had followed the England national team while they played friendlies on the European continent – the distrust of English fans was high enough that the English Football Association's reputation and even diplomatic relations between the UK and Italy were seen to be at risk if England played any group stage matches on the Italian mainland. Thanks largely to British Sports Minister Colin Moynihan's negative remarks about English fans weeks before the match, security around Cagliari during England's three matches there was heavy – in addition to 7,000 local police, the [Carabinieri](45173.md) and [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_special_forces) of the [Italian military](14705.md) were also there patrolling the premises. The Italian authorities' presence proved to be justified as there were several riots during the time England were playing their matches in Cagliari, leading to a number of injuries, arrests and even deportations.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Most of the construction cost in excess of their original estimates and total costs ended up being over £550 million (approximately \$935 million). Rome's [Stadio Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Olimpico) which hosted the final was the most expensive project overall, while Udine's [Stadio Friuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Friuli), the newest of the existing stadia (opened 14 years prior), cost the least to redevelop.

## Squads

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_squads)

Squads for the 1990 World Cup consisted of 22 players, as for the previous tournament in 1986. Replacement of injured players was permitted during the tournament at FIFA's discretion. Two goalkeepers – Argentina's [Ángel Comizzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Comizzo) and England's [Dave Beasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Beasant) – entered their respective squads during the tournament to replace injured players ([Nery Pumpido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nery_Pumpido) and [David Seaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Seaman)).

## Match officials

41 match officials from 34 countries were assigned to the tournament to serve as referees and assistant referees. Officials in italics were only used as assistants during the tournament. Referees dressed only in traditional black jerseys for the final time at a World Cup (a red change shirt was used for two Group C games in which Scotland wore their navy blue shirts).

## Draw

### Seedings

The six [seeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_\(sports\)) teams for the 1990 tournament were announced on 7 December 1989, two days ahead of the draw. The FIFA Organising Committee also decided ahead of the draw, to allocate the six seeded teams into the first position of the six groups, in the alphabetic order of their seeding rank: first seed to Group A, second seed to Group B, etc.[^cite_note-Seedings-25] The seeds were decided by FIFA, primarily based on the nations' [ranked position in the 1986 World Cup](157231.md#FIFA_retrospective_ranking) (counting double), with the [ranked position in the 1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup#FIFA_retrospective_ranking) also considered as a secondary influence (counting normal).[^cite_note-26]

Six of the [final eight in 1986](157231.md#FIFA_retrospective_ranking) had qualified for the 1990 tournament, the missing nations being Mexico (quarter-final in 1986) and France (third place). Italy did not reach the final eight in 1986, but despite this were still seeded first in 1990 as hosts. In total, the primary seeding criteria hereby resulted in seven countries to be potentially seeded. As the tournament structure only had place for the selection of six seeded teams, this left FIFA needing to exclude one of the six teams qualifying for a seed based on the primary sporting criteria. FIFA first decided, that all qualified teams having played the 1986 semi-finals (Argentina, Germany, Belgium), should be guaranteed a seeding place behind the first seeded host nation Italy. Consequently, one of the three qualified nations who were eliminated in the 1986 quarter-finals (Brazil, England or Spain), should not be seeded based on the secondary sporting criteria – looking at their [1982 FIFA World Cup results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup#FIFA_retrospective_ranking).

Owing to their performance in 1982, but also to their overall World Cup record, Brazil were seeded third, and not considered to drop out of the seedings. FIFA therefore had to choose if England or Spain should be granted the last seeding slot, and ultimately opted to seed England ahead of Spain.[^cite_note-Seedings-25]

Spain had only been eliminated in their 1986 quarter-final on penalties, albeit by fourth-placed Belgium, while England had been defeated in their 1986 quarter-final after 90 minutes by eventual winners Argentina. If the only criteria had been the [1986 ranking](157231.md#FIFA_retrospective_ranking), then Spain should have been seeded as #6 instead of England; a viewpoint being supported by FIFA President [João Havelange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Havelange) as late as 29 November 1989.[^cite_note-27] If taking the [1982 ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup#FIFA_retrospective_ranking) into consideration, England however had performed slightly better than Spain – as they had finished ahead of them in [Group B of the second group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup#Group_B). If only considering the results of the [1978 event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), it could on the other hand have been counter argued, that Spain by their group stage performance and Netherlands by their silver medal accomplishment, should have been seeded rather than [England and Belgium who both had failed to qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup#Qualification).

Spanish officials believed the seeding was contrived to ensure England would be placed in Group F, the group to be held off the Italian mainland, in a bid to contain England's hooliganism problems.[^cite_note-ENGseed-20] Their coach [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_Miramontes) said, "We feel we've been cheated...they wanted to seed England and to send it to Cagliari at all costs. So they invented this formula".[^cite_note-Seedings-25] FIFA countered that "the formula was based on the teams' respective showings during the previous two World Cups. England merited the sixth position. This is in no way a concession to English hooliganism".[^cite_note-Seedings-25]

Meanwhile, the Netherlands also had an argument, that on grounds of recent footballing form, they should be seeded, as the winners of the [1988 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988), in which both [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team) and [England](9904.md) had been eliminated in the group stages, while [Belgium](679716.md) (fourth in the 1986 World Cup after beating Spain, and thus seeded in 1990) had failed to even qualify. However, this argument was countered by the fact that Netherlands had themselves failed to qualify for both the 1982 and 1986 World Cups, which was considered the most important factor in the decision not to seed them.[^cite_note-28]

The arguments whether or not seeding positions were justified, mainly existed ahead of the draw, and rapidly fizzled out after the draw, as the two teams considered the most unlucky not to be seeded (Spain and Netherlands), were both drawn in groups against the two teams considered the weakest of the seeded nations (Belgium and England). After the group stage had been completed, England could be said to have justified their seeded position by winning their [Group F](#Group_F) ahead of Ireland and Netherlands; while Spain seemed to have made their own point about being worth a seeded position, by defeating Belgium to top their own [Group E](#Group_E).

### Final draw

On 9 December 1989, the draw was conducted at the [Palazzo dello Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PalaLottomatica) in Rome,[^cite_note-30] where the teams were drawn out from the three pots to be placed with the seeded teams in their predetermined groups, in alphabetic order. A special draw decided teams should first be drawn from Pot 1, then from Pot 3, and finally from Pot 2. Each drawn team also had their group position number drawn from a separate group bowl, in order to decide their exact match schedule. The only stipulation of the draw was that no group could feature two South American teams. Hence, a special rule was set up that:[^cite_note-Pots-29][^cite_note-Draw_part1-31][^cite_note-Draw_part2-32]

- **Pot 2 rule (final version):** *If the two South American teams (Colombia and Uruguay) are drawn as the second/third team from Pot 2, then they will not go into group B/C – as those two groups are led by the two seeded South American teams (Argentina and Brazil); but instead they will be placed in the next open group in the alphabet led by a seeded European team (Group D/E).*

  [^cite_note-Pots-29]

  [^cite_note-Draw_part1-31]

  [^cite_note-Draw_part2-32]

The procedure for the draw, was presented by FIFA at a press meeting the day before the draw. However, after having received objections from Italian officials, FIFA opted to slightly change their special Pot 2 rule, during the last 24 hours ahead of the draw. Before this rule was changed into the final wording given in the paragraph above, it had been intended to say:[^cite_note-Pot2rule-33]

- **Pot 2 rule (dropped initial version):** *First two drawn European teams from Pot 2 shall, irrespectively of the alphabet order for the open groups, first be drawn into the two groups led by a seeded South American team (Argentina's Group B and Brazil's Group C); while the two drawn South American teams (Colombia and Uruguay) can not join Group B+C and shall instead be drawn into the first still open group being led by a seeded European team.*

  [^cite_note-Pot2rule-33]

This dropped initial wording, would have increased the risk for Italy in Group A to draw one of the difficult two South American teams, instead of one of the easier European teams from Pot 2; because for Italy to get paired with a European Pot 2 team it would have required the less likely event that all three first drawn teams from Pot 2 should be European. When FIFA accepted to change the Pot 2 rule into the final version presented at the televised draw, this lowered the chance for the Italian Group A to draw a South American Pot 2 team, from the 80% chance created by the initial wording to only a 33% chance (two out of six teams).[^cite_note-Pot2rule-33]

The ceremony was hosted by Italian television presenter [Pippo Baudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pippo_Baudo), with Italian actress [Sophia Loren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Loren) and opera singer [Luciano Pavarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti) conducting the draw alongside FIFA general secretary [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter).[^cite_note-ENGseed-20] The draw show was FIFA's most ambitious yet with the appearances of [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9), [Bobby Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Moore), [Karl-Heinz Rummenigge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Heinz_Rummenigge), [Daniel Passarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Passarella), [Rubén Sosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Sosa) and [Bruno Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Conti) as well as a performance of the Italian version of the [tournament's official song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_official_songs) "To Be Number One" by Giorgio Moroder, performed as "[Un'estate italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un'estate_italiana)" by [Edoardo Bennato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Bennato) and [Gianna Nannini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianna_Nannini).[^cite_note-34]

The event also featured the [official mascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_mascots) of this World Cup, *Ciao*, a [stick figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stick_figure) player with a football head and an [Italian tricolour body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy) that formed the word "ITALIA" when deconstructed and reconstructed.[^cite_note-35] Its name is a greeting in Italian.

In each group, the teams would play three matches, one against each of the other teams. Victories would be granted 2 points, while a draw awarded 1 point. After completion of the group stage, the best two teams of each group as well as the [four best ranked third places](#Ranking_of_third-placed_teams), advanced to round 16 in the [knockout stage](#Knockout_stage). A total of 52 games would be played, including the final and a match for third place between the two semi-final losers.

## Summary of tournament

### Negative tactics

The tournament generated a record low goals-per-game average and a then-record of 16 red cards were handed out.[^cite_note-Record-5] In the knockout stage, many teams played defensively for 120 minutes, with the intention of trying their luck in the penalty shoot-out, rather than risk going forward. Two exceptions were the eventual champions West Germany and hosts Italy, the only teams to win three of their four knockout matches in normal time. There were four penalty shoot-outs, a record equalled with the [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2014](656933.md), and [2018](13327177.md) tournaments, until it was surpassed by the [2022](17742072.md) tournament, with five.[^cite_note-36] Eight matches went to extra time, also a record tied with the 2014 tournament.

Losing finalists Argentina were prime examples of this trend of cautious defensive play, choosing to do so because 3 of their best players were left off the squad due to injury. They scored only five goals in the entire tournament (a record low for a finalist). Argentina also became the first team to advance twice on penalty shoot-outs and the first team to fail to score and have a player sent off in a World Cup final.[^cite_note-Italy_1990-1]

Largely as a result of this trend [IFAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Football_Association_Board) introduced the [back-pass rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-pass_rule) in time for the [1994 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) to make it harder for teams to time-waste by repeatedly passing the ball back for their goalkeepers to pick up. [Three, rather than two points](4960100.md) would be awarded for victories at future tournaments to help further encourage attacking play.

### Emergence of Cameroon

[Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) reached the quarter-finals, where they were narrowly defeated by England.[^cite_note-Italy_1990-1] They opened the tournament with a shock victory over reigning champions Argentina, before topping the group ahead of them, [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team) and [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988) runners-up the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team). Their success was fired by the goals of [Roger Milla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Milla), a 38-year-old forward who came out of international retirement to join the national squad at the last moment after a personal request from Cameroonian President [Paul Biya](392319.md). Milla's four goals and flamboyant goal celebrations made him one of the tournament's biggest stars as well as taking Cameroon to the last eight.[^cite_note-Italy_1990-1] Most of Cameroon's squad was made up of players who played in France's premier football league, [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) – French is one of the officially spoken languages in Cameroon, it being a former French territory. In reaching this stage, they had gone further than any African nation had managed in a World Cup before; a feat not surpassed until [Morocco](1024865.md) reached the semi-final in [2022](17742072.md). Cameroon's feat (equalled by [Senegal](1017436.md) in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) and [Ghana](1029620.md) in [2010](168079.md)) remains the best by a Sub-Saharan African team. Their success was African football's biggest yet on the world stage and FIFA subsequently decided to allocate the [CAF qualifying zone](715265.md) an additional place for the next World Cup tournament.

### All-champion final four

Despite the performances of nations such as Cameroon, Colombia, Ireland, Romania and Costa Rica, the semi-finalists consisted of Argentina, England, Italy and West Germany, all previous World Cup winners, with eight previous titles between them. After the [1970 tournament](157227.md), this is only the second time in the history of the World Cup this has occurred. The teams which finished first, second and third had also contested both the two previous World Cup Finals between themselves.

The Group stage saw the twenty-four teams divided into six groups of four teams. Each group was a [round-robin](939742.md) of six games, in which each team played one match against each of the other teams in the same group. Teams were awarded two points for a win, one point for a draw and none for a defeat. The teams coming first and second in each group qualified for the Round of 16. The four best third-placed teams would also advance to the next stage.

As was typical of a World Cup staged in Europe, the matches all started at either 5\:00 or 9\:00 in the evening; this allowed for the games to avoid being played in the heat of an Italian summer, which would soar past 86F (30C) all over Italy.

If teams finished level on points, they were to be ranked on the following criteria in order:

> 1. Greatest total goal difference in the three group matches
>
> 2. Greatest number of goals scored in the three group matches
>
> 3. Most points earned in matches against other teams in the tie
>
> 4. Greatest goal difference in matches against other teams in the tie
>
> 5. Greatest number of goals scored in matches against other teams in the tie
>
> 6. Drawing of lots

### Group A

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_A)

Hosts [Italy](362466.md) won Group A with a 100 percent record. They beat [Austria](695226.md) 1–0 thanks to substitute [Salvatore 'Totò' Schillaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Schillaci), who had played only one international before but would become a star during the tournament. A second 1–0 victory followed against a [United States team](582488.md) already thumped 5–1 by [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team). The Czechoslovaks ended runners-up in the group, while the USA's first appearance in a World Cup Finals since [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) ended with three consecutive defeats.&#x20;

### Group B

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B)

[Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) defeated world champions [Argentina](454699.md) 1–0. Despite ending the match with only nine men, the African team held on for a shock 1–0 win, with contrasting fortunes for the Biyik brothers: [François Omam-Biyik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Omam-Biyik) scoring the winning goal with a downward header, shortly after seeing [André Kana-Biyik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Kana-Biyik) sent off for a serious foul. Cameroon's second red card was awarded to [Benjamin Massing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Massing) for up-ending Caniggia in the last few minutes as Argentina pressed for an equalizer.[^cite_note-43] In Cameroon's second game, the introduction of [Roger Milla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Milla) was the catalyst for a 2–1 win over [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team), Milla scoring twice from the bench (making him the oldest goalscorer in the tournament). With progression assured, Cameroon slumped to a 4–0 defeat in their final group game to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_national_football_team) (in what would be their last World Cup due to the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union)), who were striving to stay in the tournament on goal difference after successive 2–0 defeats. Argentina lost their veteran goalkeeper, [Nery Pumpido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nery_Pumpido), to a broken leg during their victory over the USSR: his replacement, [Sergio Goycochea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Goycochea), proved to be one of the stars of their tournament. In the final match, a 1–1 draw between Romania and Argentina sent both through, equal on points and on goal difference but with Romania having the advantage on goals scored; Romania were thus second, and Argentina qualified as one of the best third-placed teams. &#x20;

### Group C

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C)

[Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) beat [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) 1–0 in their first match, lost 1–0 to [Brazil](149286.md) in their second, then saw off [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) 2–1 to claim a place in the second round. Brazil took maximum points from the group. They began with a 2–1 win over Sweden, then beat both Costa Rica and Scotland 1–0. Scotland's 2–1 win over Sweden was not enough to save them from an early return home as one of the two lowest-ranked third-placed teams. &#x20;

### Group F

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F)

Group F featured [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), [England](9904.md), the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) and [Egypt](1022953.md). In the six group games, no team managed to score more than once in a match. England beat Egypt 1–0—the only match with a decisive result—and that was enough to win the group. England took the lead with an early goal for Lineker against Ireland, but Sheedy's late equaliser secured a draw. The Netherlands drew with Egypt: they had taken a 1–0 lead, but Egypt equalised with a penalty by Abdelghani. England then drew 0–0 with the Netherlands; a goal from a free-kick by Pearce was disallowed, Pearce having failed to register that it was an *indirect* free kick, and scoring directly. Lineker also had a goal disallowed for offside. For the second World Cup in succession, however, England lost their captain Bryan Robson to an injury which put him out of the tournament, just over halfway through their second match: he was replaced by David Platt in the side, and by Shilton as captain. Ireland missed a number of scoring opportunities in the second half of the other 0–0 draw against Egypt.[^cite_note-68] After the first four matches all four teams had equal records with two draws, one goal for and one goal against. England's victory over Egypt, thanks to a 58th-minute goal from [Mark Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Wright_\(footballer,_born_1963\)), put them top of the group: in the other match, Gullit gave the Netherlands the lead against Ireland, but Niall Quinn scored a second-half equaliser and the two teams finished in second and third, still with identical records. Both teams qualified, but they had to draw lots to place the teams in second and third place. &#x20;

## Knockout stage

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage)

The [knockout stage](939680.md) involved the 16 teams that qualified from the group stage of the tournament. There were four rounds of matches, with each round eliminating half of the teams entering that round. The successive rounds were: round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals and the final. There was also a match for third place. For each game in the knockout stage, any tied score at 90 minutes was followed by 30 minutes of [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtime_\(association_football\)); if scores were still level there would be a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) (five penalties each, if neither team already had a decisive advantage, and more if necessary) to determine who progressed to the next round. Scores after extra time are indicated by (a.e.t.) and penalty shoot-outs are indicated by (pen.).

### Round of 16

Two of the ties—Brazil vs Argentina and Italy vs Uruguay—pitted former champion countries against each other and West Germany met the Netherlands in a rematch of the [1974 World Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_Final). The all-South American game was won for Argentina by a goal from [Claudio Caniggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Caniggia) with 10 minutes remaining after a run through the Brazilian defence by [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) and a strong performance from their goalkeeper [Sergio Goycochea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Goycochea). It would later come to light that [Branco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branco_\(footballer\)) had been offered water spiked with tranquillisers by Maradona and [Ricardo Giusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Giusti) during half time, to slow him down in the second half. Initially discredited by the press, Branco would be publicly proven right years later, when Maradona confessed the episode on a TV show in Argentina.[^cite_note-77] Hosts Italy beat Uruguay 2–0, thanks to goals from Schillaci and [Aldo Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Serena).

The match between West Germany and the Netherlands was held in Milan, and both sides featured players from the two Milanese clubs (Germans [Andreas Brehme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Brehme), [Lothar Matthäus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothar_Matth%C3%A4us) and [Jürgen Klinsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klinsmann) for [Internazionale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.C._Internazionale_Milano), and Dutchmen [Marco van Basten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_van_Basten), [Ruud Gullit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_Gullit) and [Frank Rijkaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard) for [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C._Milan)). After 22 minutes [Rudi Völler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudi_V%C3%B6ller) and Rijkaard were both dismissed after a number of incidents between the two players, including Rijkaard spitting on Völler. As the players walked off the pitch together, Rijkaard spat on Völler a second time. Early in the second half, Jürgen Klinsmann put the West Germans ahead and Andreas Brehme added a second with eight minutes left. A [Ronald Koeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Koeman) penalty for the Netherlands in the 89th minute narrowed the score to 2–1 but the Germans saw the game out to gain some revenge for their [exit to the Dutch in the previous European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988#Semi-finals).

Meanwhile, in Cameroon v. Colombia, Roger Milla was introduced as a second-half substitute with the game goalless, eventually breaking the deadlock midway in extra time. Three minutes later he netted a second after Colombian goalkeeper, [René Higuita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Higuita) was dispossessed by Milla while well out of his goal, leaving the striker free to slot the ball into the empty net. Though the deficit was soon reduced to 2–1, Cameroon held on to become the first African team to reach the World Cup quarter-finals. Costa Rica were beaten 4–1 by Czechoslovakia, for whom [Tomáš Skuhravý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Skuhrav%C3%BD) scored the tournament's second and final [hat-trick](411541.md).

The Republic of Ireland's match with Romania remained goalless after extra time and the Irish side won 5–4 on penalties. [David O'Leary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_O'Leary) converted the penalty that clinched Ireland's place in the quarter-finals. Ireland thus became the first team since Sweden in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) to reach the last eight in a World Cup finals tournament without winning a match outright. Yugoslavia beat Spain 2–1 after extra time, with [Dragan Stojković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_Stojkovi%C4%87) scoring both the Yugoslavs' goals. England were the final qualifier against Belgium, as midfielder [David Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Platt_\(footballer\))'s swivelling volley broke the stalemate with the game moments away from a penalty shoot-out.

### Quarter-finals

The first game of the last 8 saw Argentina and a Yugoslav side, reduced to 10 men after only half an hour, play out a goalless stalemate. The holders reached the semi-finals after winning the penalty shoot-out 3–2, despite Maradona having his penalty saved. A second Argentine miss (by [Pedro Troglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Troglio)) looked to have eliminated them until goalkeeper [Sergio Goycochea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Goycochea) – playing because first choice [Nery Pumpido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nery_Pumpido) broke his leg during the group stage – rescued his side by stopping the Yugoslavs' final two spotkicks.

The Republic of Ireland's World Cup run was brought to an end by a single goal from Schillaci in the first half of their quarter-final with hosts Italy, thus becoming the team who advanced the furthest in a World Cup without winning a single match. West Germany beat Czechoslovakia with a 25th minute [Lothar Matthäus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothar_Matth%C3%A4us) penalty.

The quarter-final between England and Cameroon was the only quarter-final to produce more than one goal. Despite Cameroon's heroics earlier in the tournament, [David Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Platt_\(footballer\)) put England ahead in the 25th minute. At half-time, Milla was brought on. In the second half, the game was turned on its head during a five-minute stretch: first Cameroon were awarded a penalty from which [Emmanuel Kunde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Kunde) scored the equaliser; then in the 65th minute [Eugene Ekeke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Ekeke) put Cameroon ahead. Cameroon came within eight minutes of reaching the semi-finals before they conceded a penalty, which Gary Lineker converted. Midway through extra time, England were awarded another penalty and Lineker again scored from the spot. England were through to the semi-finals for the first time since 1966.

### Semi-finals

The first semi-final featured the host nation, Italy, and the world champions, Argentina in Naples. 'Toto' Schillaci scored yet again to put Italy ahead in the 17th minute, but Claudio Caniggia equalised midway through the second half, breaking [Walter Zenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Zenga)'s [clean sheet](313346.md) streak throughout the tournament. There were no more goals in the 90 minutes or in extra time despite Maradona (who played for Naples in Serie A at the time) showing glimpses of magic, but there was a sending-off: [Ricardo Giusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Giusti) of Argentina was shown the red card in the 13th minute of extra time. Argentina went through on penalties, winning the shoot-out 4–3 after more heroics from Goycochea.

The semi-final between West Germany and England at [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC)' home stadium in Turin was goalless at half-time. Then, in the 60th minute, a free-kick tapped to Andreas Brehme resulted in a shot which was deflected off [Paul Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Parker_\(footballer\)) into his own net. England equalised with ten minutes left; Gary Lineker was the scorer, Parker somewhat redeeming himself by providing the assist. The game ended 1–1. Extra time yielded more chances. Klinsmann was guilty of two glaring misses and both sides struck a post. England had another Platt goal disallowed for offside. The match went to penalties, and West Germany went on to win the shoot-out 4–3, Pearce having his penalty saved and Waddle firing over the bar.[^cite_note-90]

The two matches had the exact same score at 1–1, an identical penalty shoot-out score at 4–3, and the same order of penalties scored.

### Match for third place

The game saw three goals in a 15-minute spell near the end of the match. [Roberto Baggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Baggio) opened the scoring after a mistake by England's goalkeeper Peter Shilton, in his final game before international retirement, presented a simple opportunity. A header by [David Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Platt_\(footballer\)) levelled the game 10 minutes later but [Schillaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Schillaci) was fouled in the penalty area five minutes later, leading to a penalty. Schillaci himself got up to convert the kick to win him the tournament's [Golden Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cup_Golden_Boot) for his six-goal tally. [Nicola Berti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Berti) had a goal ruled out minutes later, but the hosts claimed third place. England had the consolation prize of the Fair Play award, having received no red cards and the lowest average number of yellows per match.

### Final

Main article: [1990 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_final)

The final between West Germany and Argentina has been cited as one of the lowest-quality World Cup finals.[^cite_note-Italy_1990-1][^cite_note-World_Cup_1990-2][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] In the 65th minute, Argentina's [Pedro Monzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Monzon) – himself only recently on as a substitute – was sent off for a foul on [Jürgen Klinsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klinsmann). Monzon was the first player sent off in a World Cup final.

Argentina, weakened by suspension and injury, offered little attacking threat throughout a contest dominated by the West Germans, who struggled to create many clear goalscoring opportunities. The only goal of the contest arrived in the 85th minute when Mexican referee [Edgardo Codesal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgardo_Codesal) awarded a [penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_\(association_football\)) to West Germany, after a foul on [Rudi Völler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudi_V%C3%B6ller) by [Roberto Sensini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Sensini) leading to Argentinian protests. An earlier possible foul by Goycoecha, who tripped [Klaus Augenthaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Augenthaler) in the box, had not been given. German commentators speculated that the subsequent penalty was therefore a concession by the referee.[^cite_note-Germany_finally_awarded_penatyy-97] [Andreas Brehme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Brehme) converted the spot kick to settle the contest. In the closing moments, Argentina were reduced to nine after [Gustavo Dezotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Dezotti), who had already been given a yellow card earlier in the match, received a red card when he hauled [Jürgen Kohler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Kohler) to the ground during a stoppage in play. The 1–0 scoreline provided another first: Argentina were the first team to fail to score in a World Cup final.

With its third title (and three second-place finishes) West Germany – in its final tournament before [national reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) – became the most successful World Cup nation at the time along with Italy and Brazil (who had also won three titles each then). West German manager [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) became the first man to both captain (in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup)) and manage a World Cup winning team, and only the second man (after [Mário Zagallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Zagallo) of Brazil) to win the World Cup as a player and as team manager. It was also the first time a team from UEFA won the final against a non-European team.

- Lowest attendance: **27,833** – **[Yugoslavia vs United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D#Yugoslavia_vs_United_Arab_Emirates)** (Group D, 19 June)

- Average attendance: **48,391** (5th highest in World Cup history)

- Oldest player: **[Peter Shilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Shilton)** ([England](9904.md)) (40 years 292 days)

- Youngest player: **[Rónald González Brenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B3nald_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Brenes)** ([Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team)) (19 years 307 days)

- Italy's performance of 6 wins, 1 draw and 0 losses is the highest winning percentage for a team that did not win the World Cup.

  [^cite_note-Italy0-108]

- The Republic of Ireland became the second team in World Cup history to reach the last eight without winning a match (Sweden progressed to the last eight by default in 1938 when Austria withdrew).
