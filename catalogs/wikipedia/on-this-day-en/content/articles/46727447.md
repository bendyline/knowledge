The **Lagos–Ibadan Expressway** is a 127.6-kilometre-long (79.3 mi) [expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-access_road) connecting [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan), the capital of [Oyo State](2227658.md) and [Lagos](85232.md), Nigeria's largest city.[^cite_note-1] It is also the major route to the northern, southern and eastern parts of Nigeria.[^cite_note-2] The expressway is the oldest in Nigeria, commissioned in August 1978 during the Military era, under the administration of Lieutenant-General [Olusegun Obasanjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olusegun_Obasanjo).[^cite_note-3]

The expressway is one of the busiest inter-state routes in Nigeria and handles more than 250,000 PCUs daily and constitutes one of the largest road networks in Africa.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] It is part of the [Federal Roads Maintenance Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Roads_Maintenance_Agency) (FERMA) projects, concerned with road improvement and connectivity between the [States of Nigeria](226734.md).[^cite_note-6]

## Reconstruction

The reconstruction of the expressway was flagged off in July 2013 by President [Goodluck Ebele Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Ebele_Jonathan) then president of the [Federal Republic of Nigeria](21383.md), to help reduce the travel time of hundreds of thousands of commuters and international air passengers.[^cite_note-7] The contract was awarded to [Julius Berger Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Berger_Nigeria) and Reynolds Construction Company Limited at a sum of 167 billion Naira, equivalent to \$838,986,290.[^cite_note-8] Two sections of the expressway will be reconstructed and this includes Section I (Lagos to Sagamu Interchange) and section II (Sagamu Interchange to Ibadan).[^cite_note-9]
