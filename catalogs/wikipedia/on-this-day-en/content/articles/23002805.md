# Drug lord

"Drug king" redirects here. For the film, see [The Drug King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Drug_King).

A **drug lord**, **drug baron**, **kingpin**, or **lord of drugs** is a type of [crime boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_boss) in charge of a [drug trafficking](218449.md) network, organization, or enterprise.[^cite_note-1] When a group of independent drug lords [collude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collude) with each other, in order to improve their profits and dominate the [illegal drug trade](218449.md), they form an organization called a [drug cartel](197015.md).[^cite_note-2]

### Pablo Escobar

Main article: [Pablo Escobar](161570.md)



[Pablo Escobar](161570.md)

Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria (December 1, 1949 – December 2, 1993) was a [Colombian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_People) drug overlord. Often referred to as the "World's Greatest Outlaw", Escobar was perhaps the most elusive cocaine trafficker to have ever existed. He is considered the 'King of Cocaine' and is known as the lord of all drug lords.[^cite_note-colombialink.com-3] In 1989, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine declared Escobar as the seventh-richest man in the world, with an estimated personal fortune of US\$30 billion.[^cite_note-4] In 1986, he attempted to enter Colombian politics. It is said that Pablo Escobar once burnt two million dollars in cash to keep his daughter warm while on the run.[^cite_note-5] Escobar was the boss of the famous [Medellín Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_Cartel), the most powerful drug empire to exist and is said to have had over twice the power and money of their [rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry), the [Cali Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali_Cartel). Pablo was known as *Paisa* [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood), for his contributions to the poor, but was also known for murdering anyone who got in his way. His [carrot-and-stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrot_and_stick) strategy of [bribing](260979.md) public officials in the Colombian government, and sending [hitmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_killing) to murder the ones who rejected his bribes, came to be known as "silver or lead" or "money or bullets". When the Colombian government launched a [manhunt](11592326.md) for Escobar, it needed assistance from the [DEA](146720.md), the [CIA](5183633.md), the Cali Cartel, and [Los Pepes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pepes). On December 2, [Search Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_Bloc) killed Escobar on a rooftop.

### Amado Carrillo Fuentes

Main article: [Amado Carrillo Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amado_Carrillo_Fuentes)

As a top drug lord in Mexico, Amado Carrillo (1956–1997) was transporting four times more cocaine to the U.S. than any other trafficker, building a fortune of over \$25 billion.[^cite_note-6] He was called El Señor de Los Cielos ("The Lord of the Skies") for his use of over 22 private [727 jet airliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) to transport Colombian cocaine to municipal airports and dirt airstrips around Mexico, including Juárez. He was a member of the [Guadalajara Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara_Cartel) and worked for [Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_F%C3%A9lix_Gallardo). After Félix was arrested, Amado formed the [Juarez Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juarez_Cartel). In the months before his death, the DEA described Carrillo as the most-powerful drug trafficker of his era, and many analysts claimed profits neared \$25 billion.

### Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán

Main article: [El Chapo](4176378.md)



[El Chapo](4176378.md)

Guzman is the most notorious drug lord in history, according to the U.S. [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md) (DEA).[^cite_note-7] In the 1980s, he was a member of the [Guadalajara Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara_Cartel) and used to work for [Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_F%C3%A9lix_Gallardo). After Félix's arrest in 1989, Guzmán formed the [Sinaloa Cartel](16881931.md) along with [Ismael Zambada García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismael_Zambada_Garc%C3%ADa) and [Héctor Luis Palma Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Luis_Palma_Salazar). He is well known for his use of sophisticated tunnels—similar to the one located in [Douglas, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas,_Arizona)—to smuggle cocaine from Mexico into the United States in the early 1990s. In 1993, a 7.3-ton shipment of his [cocaine](7701.md), concealed in cans of chili peppers and destined for the United States, was seized in [Tecate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecate), [Baja California](199793.md). That same year he barely escaped an ambush by the [Tijuana Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_Cartel) led by Ramon Arellano Felix and his gunmen. After being captured in Guatemala, he was jailed in 1993 and in 1995 he was moved to the maximum-security prison called Puente Grande, but paid his way out of prison and hid in a laundry van as it drove through the gates. On 22 February 2014, Guzmán was arrested again. He is considered a [folk hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_hero) in the narcotics world, celebrated by musicians who write and perform *[narcocorridos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcocorrido)* (drug ballads) extolling his exploits.[^cite_note-WSJ2009-06-13-8] For example, Los Traviezos recorded a ballad extolling his life on the run.[^cite_note-9] In July 2015, Guzman escaped a second time from a maximum-security prison through a hole in a shower floor that led to a mile-long tunnel, ending at a nearby house.[^cite_note-10] A large-scale manhunt ensued. On 8 January 2016, Guzmán was captured by the [Mexican Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Marines).[^cite_note-11] He was sentenced to life imprisonment, and incarcerated in [ADX Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADX_Florence), Colorado, United States.[^cite_note-SentenceBBC-12][^cite_note-13]

### Osiel Cárdenas Guillén

Main article: [Osiel Cárdenas Guillén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiel_C%C3%A1rdenas_Guill%C3%A9n)

[Osiel Cárdenas Guillén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiel_C%C3%A1rdenas_Guill%C3%A9n) (born 18 May 1967) is a former Mexican drug lord who was the leader of the [Gulf Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Cartel) and [Los Zetas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Zetas). Originally a [mechanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanic) in [Matamoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matamoros,_Tamaulipas), Guillén eventually became involved in the [illegal drug trade](218449.md) through the Gulf Cartel before becoming its leader in 1997 by assassinating drug lord [Salvador Gómez Herrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_G%C3%B3mez_Herrera?action=edit\&redlink=1). Guillén recruited over 30 deserters from the [Mexican Army](2948082.md)'s special forces unit, the *[Cuerpo de Fuerzas Especiales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuerpo_de_Fuerzas_Especiales)*, to form the cartel's armed wing; this group would go on to be among the founding members of Los Zetas, another Mexican drug cartel. In 1999, Guillén and a group of Gulf Cartel gunmen [threatened two U.S. federal agents at gunpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Matamoros_standoff), which triggered a massive combined effort from American and Mexican law enforcement agencies to crack down on the leadership structure of the Gulf Cartel and led to Guillén becoming one of the most wanted criminals in the world. Guillén was arrested in Mexico in 2003 and deported to the U.S. in 2007, where he remains incarcerated to this day.[^cite_note-Ehtab-14][^cite_note-\:14-15]

### Jorge Alberto Rodriguez

Jorge Alberto Rodriguez, also known as Don Cholito, is a notorious Argentine-born, Trinidadian and Colombian mixed drug lord from New York, who headed the 400 criminal organization, a dismantled secret cell of the [Cali Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali_Cartel). Pulled into the drug trade at age 12, he left home at age 14 to begin working for his Father, [Gilberto Rodríguez Orejuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilberto_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Orejuela), who headed the Cali Cartel. Within six years he had amassed a fortune exceeding over US\$300 million by shipping drugs from Colombia to nearly every state in the U.S. He was one of the most ruthless international drug lords unknown to law enforcement or governments. During that time, the murder rate and cocaine-related hospital emergencies in the United States doubled. He was arrested on July 6, 1990, in Tallahassee, Florida and sentenced to a 25-year prison term for a number of federal violations. Following his conviction, Rodriguez continued to operate his illicit business from behind bars, importing as much as 12,500 kilograms of cocaine into the U.S. each month and ordering numerous murders of informants, witnesses, in the U.S. and Colombia. He reigned and flourished while incarcerated until he was placed in court-ordered high-security isolation in 1994. ### Marcola

Main article: [Marcola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcola)

Born on April 13, 1968, in [Osasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osasco) (a city located in the [state of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo), [Brazil](3383.md)), Marcos Willians Herbas Camacho, known as "**Marcola**" is one of the founders and the current leader of [Primeiro Comando da Capital](4711820.md) (PCC), the largest and the most powerful [Brazilian](3383.md) [criminal organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_organization).[^cite_note-19] A [Brazilian with Bolivian origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivians_in_Brazil), Marcola (whose criminal career began when he was a child, at the age of nine and currently serving a sentence of 234 years in prison for murder, drug trafficking and other crimes) is the current leader of PCC and commands this powerful crime syndicate within inside the [Taubaté Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taubat%C3%A9_Prison), one of the most infamous prisons in Brazil. Under the command of Marcola, the PCC expanded its influence outside Brazil, to having a presence in [Bolivia](3462.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [Venezuela](32374.md), [Paraguay](23420.md) and [Peru](170691.md). The PCC is notorious for its use of violence, for its [numerous confrontations with police officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_S%C3%A3o_Paulo_violence_outbreak) and because of [their violent conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Brazil_prison_riots) [over territory control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Brazil_prison_riots) and [the control of drug trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Altamira_prison_riot) against another powerful Brazilian criminal organization, the [Comando Vermelho](5172177.md) (or CV), a powerful crime syndicate based in the [city of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Rio_de_Janeiro).[^cite_note-20] Marcola is currently serving his sentence at the Brasília Federal Penitentiary, which was inaugurated in 2018 to isolate the country’s most dangerous inmates.[^cite_note-21]

### Griselda Blanco

Main article: [Griselda Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griselda_Blanco)



[Griselda Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griselda_Blanco)

Griselda Blanco (1943–2012), known as the "Godmother of Cocaine", was a drug lord who operated between Miami and Colombia during the 1970s and 1980s. During the height of her operation, she smuggled nearly 1,600 kilograms (3,500 lb) of cocaine into the U.S. every month through a network in south Florida.[^cite_note-cowboys2-22] She was noted for her ruthlessness and use of extreme violence, employing tactics such as publicly assassinating people in broad daylight, bayoneting a [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry) trafficker inside [Miami International Airport](128968.md), and inventing the [drive-by motorcycle shooting](65683.md) execution method.[^cite_note-vice-23][^cite_note-24] It was estimated that she was responsible for the homicides of 200 people in Colombia, Florida, New York, and California.[^cite_note-vice-23] Arrested in 1985 for drug-trafficking charges, she was subsequently convicted and spent almost 20 years in a U.S. prison.[^cite_note-Villarreal2012-25] She was killed by motorcycle hitmen in Colombia on 3 September 2012 as she was coming out of a butcher's shop.[^cite_note-Godmother-26]

### Roberto Suárez Gómez

Main article: [Roberto Suárez Goméz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Su%C3%A1rez_Gom%C3%A9z)

Pablo Escobar started to buy cocaine from Roberto Suárez in the 1970s when he had just created the [Medellín cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_cartel). Suárez started building cocaine laboratories in the middle of the Bolivian Amazon jungle and in the zone of "Los Yungas" in the end of the 1960s and created the first cocaine cartel in Bolivia called "La Corporación". At first, the Medellin cartel bought cocaine at \$8,000 per kilogram (\$3,600/lb). La Corporación then sold cocaine-based paste to Colombian cartels, and they finished and distributed it in the east of the United States. The finished cocaine was sold directly to Mexican cartels for distribution in the west of the United States. Suárez received untold amounts of money, but as detectives and journalists discovered the corruption between Bolivia and the U.S., the empire Suárez built began to fall. Suárez was arrested by the DEA in 1988, and Escobar took over of the production and distribution of 80% of the world's cocaine.

### Rick Ross



["Freeway" Rick Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Freeway%22_Rick_Ross)

Main article: ["Freeway" Rick Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Freeway%22_Rick_Ross)

Rick Ross (born January 28, 1960),[^cite_note-FBPRoss-27] a.k.a. "Freeway" Ricky Ross, is a convicted drug-trafficker best known for the drug empire he presided over in [Los Angeles](18110.md) in the early 1980s.[^cite_note-28] The nickname "Freeway" came from Ross growing up next to the 110 [Harbor Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Freeway).[^cite_note-29] During the height of his drug dealing, Ross was said to have made "\$2 million in one day." According to the *Oakland Tribune*, "In the course of his rise, prosecutors estimate that Ross transported several tons of cocaine to New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and elsewhere, and made more than \$600 million in the process."[^cite_note-30]

In 1998, Ross was sentenced to [life imprisonment](148681.md) after being convicted of purchasing more than 100 kilograms of cocaine from a federal agent during a sting operation. Ross became the subject of controversy later that year when a series of articles by journalist [Gary Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Webb) in the *[San Jose Mercury News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Mercury_News)* revealed a connection between Ross's main cocaine source, [Danilo Blandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilo_Blandon), and the [CIA](5183633.md) as part of the [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair).[^cite_note-31] Ross's case went before the [federal](195149.md) [court of appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_appeals) and his sentence was reduced to 20 years. He was later moved to a [halfway house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halfway_house) in March 2009 and released from custody on September 29, 2009.[^cite_note-FBPRoss-27]

In June 2014, Ross released his book *[Freeway Rick Ross: The Untold Autobiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_Rick_Ross_\(book\))*, co-written by crime-writer [Cathy Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathy_Scott).[^cite_note-32]

### Manuel Noriega



[Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega), following his arrest by U.S. authorities

Main article: [Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega)

By the early 1970s, American law enforcement officials had reports of Noriega's possible involvement with narcotics trafficking.[^cite_note-Frantz-33] No formal criminal investigations were begun, and no indictment was brought: according to Dinges, this was due to the potential diplomatic consequences.[^cite_note-Frantz-33][^cite_note-34] This evidence included the testimony of an arrested boat courier, and of a drug smuggler arrested in New York.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinges199058–60-35] Though Torrijos frequently promised the U.S. cooperation in dealing with drug smuggling, Noriega would have headed any effort at enforcement, and the U.S. began to see Noriega as an obstacle to combatting drug smuggling.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinges199061–64-36] Dinges writes that the U.S. government considered several options to move Noriega out of the drug trafficking business, including assassinating him, and linking him to a fictional plot against Torrijos. Though no assassination attempt was made, the other ploys may have been tried in the early 1970s, according to Dinges.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinges199061–64-36] Dinges wrote that beginning in 1972 the U.S. relaxed its efforts at trapping individuals involved with smuggling within the Panama government, possibly as a result of an agreement between Torrijos and U.S. President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDinges199068–70-37]

### Ramon Arellano Felix

Main article: [Ramon Arellano Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Arellano_Felix)

Ramón Arellano Félix was a Mexican drug lord who was a founding member of the [Tijuana drug cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_Cartel) (a.k.a. the Arellano-Félix Organization) alongside his brothers.[^cite_note-azstar1-38] Arellano Félix was allegedly one of the most ruthless enforcers in the organization and was a suspect in various murders. He had been linked by Mexican police to the 1997 massacre of twelve members of a family outside of [Ensenada, Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada,_Baja_California). The family was related to a drug dealer that had an unpaid debt to the Arellano Félix Cartel. On September 18, 1997, Arellano Félix was placed on the [FBI's ten most-wanted list](237871.md).[^cite_note-39] In a sealed indictment in the [United States District Court for the Southern District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_California), he was charged with conspiracy to import cocaine and marijuana.. Ramon and his brothers, primarily [Benjamin Arellano Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Arellano_Felix) were the undisputed leaders of the [Tijuana Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_Cartel) and developed an intense rivalry with [Joaquin Guzman Loera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Guzman_Loera)'s [Sinaloa Cartel](16881931.md). The war between both organizations lasted more than 10 years until Ramón was killed in [Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n) on February 10, 2002, by policemen allegedly on the payroll of the [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry), Sinaloa Cártel.

### Ismael Zambada García

Main article: [Ismael Zambada García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismael_Zambada_Garc%C3%ADa)

Ismael Zambada García is a drug smuggler in Mexico and co-founder of the [Sinaloa Cartel](16881931.md).[^cite_note-40] Mexico's top anti-drug prosecutor, [José Santiago Vasconcelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Santiago_Vasconcelos), called Zambada "drug dealer No. 1" and said the fugitive has become more powerful as his fellow kingpins have fallen, including one who was allegedly killed on Zambada's orders.

On July 25, 2024, Zambada was arrested in Texas after arriving in a private plane with one of Joaquin Guzmán's sons, Joaquín Guzmán López.[^cite_note-41] As of August 2025, he is in federal custody in the USA.[^cite_note-42]

### Arturo Beltran Leyva

Main article: [Arturo Beltran Leyva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Beltran_Leyva)

Marcos Arturo Beltrán Leyva (September 27, 1961 – December 16, 2009) was the leader of the [Mexican](3966054.md) drug-trafficking organization known as the [Beltrán-Leyva Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltr%C3%A1n-Leyva_Cartel), which is headed by the Beltrán Leyva brothers: Marcos Arturo, [Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Beltr%C3%A1n_Leyva), [Alfredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Beltr%C3%A1n_Leyva) and [Héctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Beltr%C3%A1n_Leyva).[^cite_note-43] The cartel was engaged in cocaine, [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana), [heroin](14034.md), and [methamphetamine](11038318.md) production, transportation, and wholesaling. It controlled numerous drug-trafficking corridors into the United States. and was also responsible for [human smuggling](68714.md), [money laundering](19390.md), [extortion](162449.md), [kidnapping](169376.md), [murder](20171.md), [contract killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_killing), [torture](47702.md), [gun-running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun-running), and other acts of violence in Mexico.[^cite_note-DOS-44] The organization was connected with the assassinations of numerous Mexican law-enforcement officials.[^cite_note-DOS-44]

### Frank Lucas



[Frank Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lucas_\(drug_lord\))

Main article: [Frank Lucas (drug lord)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lucas_\(drug_lord\))

Frank Lucas was a former heroin dealer and organized crime boss who operated in [Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem), [New York](8210131.md) during the late 1960s and early 1970s. He was known for cutting out middlemen in the drug trade and buying heroin directly from his source in the [Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_\(Southeast_Asia\)). Lucas boasted that he smuggled heroin using the coffins of dead American servicemen,[^cite_note-Drugss-45][^cite_note-Drugs-46] but this claim is denied by his South Asian associate, [Leslie "Ike" Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_%22Ike%22_Atkinson).[^cite_note-Drugsss-47] His career was dramatized in the 2007 feature film *[American Gangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gangster_\(film\))* starring [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington).

### Zhenli Ye Gon

Part of the currency seized from [Ye Gon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhenli_Ye_Gon)'s home

Zhenli Ye Gon (traditional Chinese: 葉真理;[^cite_note-Gilberto-51] born January 31, 1963, Shanghai, People's Republic of China) is a Mexican businessman of Chinese origin accused of trafficking [pseudoephedrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoephedrine) into Mexico from [Asia](689.md). ### Christopher Coke



[Christopher Coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Coke)

Main article: [Christopher Coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Coke)

Michael Christopher Coke (born 13 March 1969),[^cite_note-aol-55] a.k.a. Dudus,[^cite_note-nyr-56] is a [Jamaican](15660.md) drug lord and the leader of the [Shower Posse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shower_Posse) [gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_Posse). He is the youngest son of drug lord [Lester Lloyd Coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Lloyd_Coke) whose extradition had also, prior to his 1992 death in a Jamaican prison cell, been requested by the U.S.[^cite_note-nyr-56] Until the younger Coke's handover to U.S. forces on 24 June 2010, "Dudus" served as the *[de facto](43594.md)* leader of [Tivoli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_Gardens,_Kingston) in the city of [Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Jamaica);[^cite_note-nyr-56] prior to his 2010 capture Jamaican police were unable to enter this neighborhood without community consent.[^cite_note-nyr-56]

The son of a prominent drug lord, Coke grew up wealthy, going to school with children of the country's political elite. Ruling the gang where his father left off, he became a leader in the community of Tivoli Gardens, distributing money to the area's poor, creating employment, and setting up community centers.[^cite_note-extradite-57]

In 2009, the U.S. began requesting his [extradition](239648.md), and in May 2010, a recalcitrant Government of Jamaica issued a warrant.[^cite_note-nyr-56] That same month the government took steps to capture Coke. In a run-up to Coke's arrest, more than 70 people–all but one of them civilians–died in a 24 May 2010 raid of Tivoli Gardens.[^cite_note-nyr-56] He was arrested at a Jamaican checkpoint on 22 June 2010.[^cite_note-nyr-56]

## Modern drug lords

Following the death of [Pablo Escobar](161570.md) in 1993, significant changes occurred in the structure of the drug trade, departing from massive cartels such as [Juan David Ochoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_David_Ochoa)'s [Medellín cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_cartel). Drug lords have begun breaking the large cartels into much smaller organizations. In so doing, they decreased the number of people involved and shrank their role as targets—most likely in an attempt to avoid the fate of their predecessors. With newer technology, drug lords are able to manage their operations more effectively from behind the scenes, keeping themselves out of the spotlight and off the FBI and DEA wanted lists. These smaller cartels are slowly proving to be safer and more profitable for those involved.[^cite_note-NORQUIST-76]

In [developing countries](78449.md), it is still common for drug lords to control local and regional governments, although this phenomenon is not as common as it was in the past. For example, [Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismael_%22El_Mayo%22_Zambada) held de facto control over the Mexican state of [Durango](222234.md), by contributing donations to political campaigns during gubernatorial elections, along with assassinating political candidates who rejected his donations, and through bribing and intimidating the Durango [State Police](964240.md).[^cite_note-77] As of early 2023, he had never been arrested or incarcerated.[^cite_note-78] He was, however, arrested in 2024.

In [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country), drug lords seldom control local and regional governments; they also have less influence over their surroundings, and their ability to continue to run their businesses, upon being arrested and incarcerated. Unlike developing countries, developed countries have stronger rule of law and do not suffer from nearly as much corruption. Hence, it is difficult for drug lords to operate in developed countries, such as the United States or Canada, in modern times. However, it is still relatively common for drug lords and drug cartels to operate with certain levels of [impunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impunity) in developing countries, especially Latin American countries, such as Colombia, Venezuela, Brazil, and Mexico, in modern times.[^cite_note-Drugss-45][^cite_note-Drugssss-79]

Another trend that has been emerging in the last decade is a willingness of local authorities to cooperate with foreign nations, most notably the U.S., in an effort to apprehend and incarcerate drug lords. Some countries have been more willing to extradite their drug lords to face charges in other countries, an act that not only benefits them directly but also gives them favor with foreign governments. Mexico extradited 63 drug dealers to the U.S. in 2006. However, extradition may be prohibited if the person faces either the death penalty or a life sentence without the possibility of parole. Nevertheless, such efforts have failed to curb the rise of new drug lords because of widespread corruption in foreign countries, especially Latin American countries. Today, there are also many drug lords in Latin America, who have never been extradited, and continue to operate with impunity.[^cite_note-Drugs-46]

## Organizational role

Since 1967, research on [organized crime](22625.md) leadership (and, by extension, drug lords) has evolved. Where once studies emphasised the importance of the leader's human capital (e.g. individual traits), it has now developed to focus upon the leader's social capital (e.g. information and resource brokers, social status, access to information).[^cite_note-80]
