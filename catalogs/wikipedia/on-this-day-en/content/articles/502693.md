**Suwon** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 수원; Korean pronunciation: [\[sʰu.wʌn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)) is the largest city and capital of [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md), South Korea's most populous province. The city lies approximately 30 km (19 mi) south of the national capital, [Seoul](19159283.md). With a population of 1.2 million,[^cite_note-Population-3] Suwon has more inhabitants than the metropolitan city of [Ulsan](323111.md),[^cite_note-Population-3][^cite_note-UlsanPopulation-4] though it enjoys a lesser degree of self-governance as a 'special city'.[^cite_note-SpecialCaseKBS-5]

Traditionally known as the 'City of [Filial Piety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filial_piety)',[^cite_note-Piety-6] modern Suwon retains a variety of historical landmarks. As a walled city, it is a popular destination for day-trippers from Seoul,[^cite_note-NightTourismInitiative-7] with the wall itself—Hwaseong Fortress—receiving 1½ million visits in 2015.[^cite_note-HwaseongNumbersJoongang-8]

Suwon plays an important economic role as it is home to [Samsung Electronics](46445426.md), Korea's largest and most profitable company.[^cite_note-SamsungSales-9] The company's research and development centre is in Yeongtong District in eastern Suwon, where its headquarters have also been located since 2016.[^cite_note-SamsungHQ-10] Samsung's prominence in Suwon is clear: the company is partnered with Sungkyunkwan University,[^cite_note-SamsungSKKU-11] which has a campus in the city; it also owns the professional football team [Suwon Samsung Bluewings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Samsung_Bluewings). This team has won the [K League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_League) four times and the Asian Super Cup twice.[^cite_note-AsianSuperCupHistory-12][^cite_note-AsianSuperCup2002BBC-13] The city is also home to the K League 2 team [Suwon FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_FC) and the KBO League baseball team KT Wiz.

Suwon houses several well-known universities, most notably Sungkyunkwan University and Ajou University.[^cite_note-Suwon_Universities-14] It is served by three expressways, the national railway network, and three lines on the Seoul Metropolitan Subway.

## Name

*Suwon* literally means "water source".[^cite_note-LiteralMeaning-15] While the area has gone by different names since antiquity, almost all share this meaning.[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-\:1-17] The name *Suwon* descends from the [Proto–Three Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto%E2%80%93Three_Kingdoms_period) statelet [Mosuguk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosuguk?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:0-16] After this period, the area (including modern-day [Hwaseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong,_Gyeonggi)) was called *Maehol*, *Maetkol*, or *Mulgol* (매홀; 맷골; 물골; 買忽).[^cite_note-OldNames-18][^cite_note-\:0-16] In 757 CE, the name was changed to *Susŏng-gun* (수성군; 水城郡; lit. 'Susŏng County')[^cite_note-OldNames-18][^cite_note-\:0-16] to distinguish it from another territory with a similar-sounding name.[^cite_note-\:1-17] In 940, the name was changed again to *Su-ju* (수주; 水州; lit. 'Su Province').[^cite_note-OldNames-18][^cite_note-\:0-16] In the 11th century, it went by either *Susŏng* (different Hanja: 隋城) or *Hannam* (한남; 漢南; lit. 'south of Han'),[^cite_note-\:0-16] and in 1310, it finally received the name *Suwon*.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

In English, *Suwon* often used to be spelt 'Sou-wen'.[^cite_note-SouwenEncBrit-19]

## History

### Early history

Suwon has been inhabited since at the latest the early Bronze Age: artifacts such as pottery, sculptures, and arrowheads dating from the Bronze Age to the early Iron Age have been found in the area,[^cite_note-\:0-16] including on [Yeogisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeogisan?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is now a [monument of Gyeonggi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monuments_of_Gyeonggi_Province?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:0-16]

During the [Three Kingdoms of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_of_Korea) period, the area was a territory of the statelet Mosuguk, part of the [Mahan confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahan_confederacy).[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-\:1-17] It came under the influence of [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo) in the late 5th century CE before becoming part of [Unified Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Silla) (668–935).[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-OldNames-18] Suwon then fell to [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo) after a military campaign led by King [Taejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Goryeo).[^cite_note-\:0-16]

In the 13th and 14th centuries, the area was promoted, demoted, merged, and made part of various administrative districts. It then became a part of [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) upon the dynasty's founding, and in 1395 it was made an administrative centre of [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md).[^cite_note-\:0-16]

Until the late 18th century, Suwon was administered from modern-day Annyeong-dong at the foot of Hwasan—a hill in Hwasan-dong, [Hwaseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong,_Gyeonggi).[^cite_note-Relocation-20][^cite_note-\:0-16] In 1796, [King Jeongjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeongjo_of_Joseon) relocated Central Suwon to its current location at the foot of Paldalsan,[^cite_note-Relocation-20][^cite_note-\:0-16] and to protect this new city, he commanded the building of [Hwaseong Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_Fortress)—a protective wall around the town.[^cite_note-UNESCO-21]

The town steadily grew, and an 1899 administrative report recorded a population of 49,708 people in 12,579 households.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

### Japanese colonial period

See also: [Korea under Japanese rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule) and [Korean independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_independence_movement)

During the 1910–1945 Japanese colonial period, a number of prominent [Korean independence activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Korean_independence_activists) came from or operated in Suwon. [Kim Se-hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Se-hwan?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Yi Sŏn-gyŏng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Son-gyong?action=edit\&redlink=1) (이선경; 李善卿) were both arrested for their activities.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

### Liberation to Korean War

On 15 August 1949, Suwon was promoted from a county to a city, with some of its former territory made into Hwaseong County.[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-HwaseongSplit-22]



Hwaseong Fortress in the Korean War

When the [Korean War](16772.md) began, the United States Air Force [49th Fighter Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_Fighter_Wing), then stationed in Japan, was sent to Korea[^cite_note-49JapanKorea-23] with an initial mission of evacuating civilians from Suwon and [Gimpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo).[^cite_note-SuwonUSArmy-24] While on this mission, on 27 June 1950, US planes in Suwon were attacked by North Korean fighters. The ensuing [Battle of Suwon Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Suwon_Airfield) became the first aerial combat of the war.[^cite_note-FirstBattleMilitary-25] Suwon Airfield was attacked again two days later while [General Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Douglas_MacArthur) was on site.[^cite_note-MacArthurAttack-26] Though the US repelled both attacks, Suwon fell to the advancing North Koreans one week later, on 4 July 1950.[^cite_note-SuwonCaptureWestAustralian-27] The following day saw the first land conflict between United States and North Korean forces, the [Battle of Osan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Osan).



North Korean [T-34-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-34-85) caught on a bridge south of Suwon by US attack aircraft in the Korean War

North Korean troops were not the only threat to life: in the early days of the war, southern authorities feared left-leaning civilians, and many were killed.[^cite_note-AdmissionSydneyHerald-28] Suwon was a site of such killings: eyewitness account from US intelligence officer [Donald Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Nichols_\(spy\)) places Suwon as the location of a massacre of approximately 1,800 in late June 1950.[^cite_note-MassacreNichols-29][^cite_note-MassacreKim-30][^cite_note-MassacreCBS-31]

Suwon was retaken, fell again to the North, before being recaptured for the final time. In total, the city changed hands four times during the war.[^cite_note-ChangedHandsFourTimesManchesterRegiment-32]

While under southern authority, Suwon hosted forces from several countries. For example, on 16 December 1950, the [Greek Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Expeditionary_Force_\(Korea\)) relocated from Busan to Suwon, attached to the [US 1st Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-GreekForce-33] The city also appeared strategically important, as in late 1951, the US Air Force's top fighter pilot [Gabby Gabreski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabby_Gabreski) was placed in charge of [Suwon Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Air_Base).[^cite_note-SuwonUSArmy-24][^cite_note-Gabreski-34]



US 51st Fighter Interceptor Wing at Suwon Air Base, 1952

A memorial to French forces was erected in 1974 near the Yeongdong Expressway's North Suwon exit.[^cite_note-FrenchMemorial-35] A 2013 renovation was funded by the French government.[^cite_note-FrenchMemorialDetails-36]

### Recent history

In 1964, the headquarters of Gyeonggi Province began a process of relocation from Seoul to Suwon.[^cite_note-CouncilHistory-37][^cite_note-\:0-16] Seoul had left the province in 1949.[^cite_note-SeoulSeparationLaw-38] When the construction of the headquarters was completed on 23 June 1967, the date was set as a new annual holiday: Suwon Citizen's Day (수원시민의 날). The Hwahong Cultural Festival (now [Hwaseong Cultural Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_Cultural_Festival?action=edit\&redlink=1)) was established to celebrate the occasion.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

Suwon has experienced a number of administrative territory changes since the 1960s. In 1963, Suwon expanded greatly as 20 villages were incorporated from Hwaseong-gun.[^cite_note-1963Law-39] In 1983, two more villages were acquired from Yongin.[^cite_note-1983Law-40][^cite_note-\:0-16] In 1987, Suwon expanded westwards, acquiring another two villages from Hwaseong.[^cite_note-1987Law-41][^cite_note-HomaesilTransfer-42][^cite_note-\:0-16] [Gwonseon District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwonseon_District) and [Paldal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paldal_District) were established in 1988.[^cite_note-\:0-16] It received more territory from Hwaseong and Yongin in 1994,[^cite_note-1994Law-43][^cite_note-\:0-16] and again from Hwaseong in 1995.[^cite_note-\:0-16] It established [Yeongtong District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongtong_District) in 2003.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

In preparation for the construction of a new planned city [Gwanggyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanggyo_New_Town), there were two-way exchanges of land between Suwon and Yongin in 2007[^cite_note-2007Law-44][^cite_note-GwanggyoExchange-45] and 2019.[^cite_note-2019Law-46][^cite_note-SecondGwanggyoExchange-47] Suwon's most recent land exchange occurred in 2020, when it swapped some land parcels with Hwaseong.[^cite_note-2020Law-48]

## Geography

Suwon lies in the north of the Gyeonggi plain, 30 km (19 mi) south of the national capital, Seoul. It is bordered by the cities of [Uiwang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uiwang) to the north-west, [Yongin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongin) to the east, [Hwaseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_City) to the south-west, and [Ansan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansan) to the west.[^cite_note-49] Suwon is near the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea) coast: at its closest point, on the 239-metre (784 ft) [Chilbosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilbosan_\(Gyeonggi\)) ridge to the west, Suwon lies 18.2 km (11.3 mi) from Ueumdo[^cite_note-CoastDistanceFreeMapTools-50] in Sihwa Lake, a coastal inlet cordoned off to drive the [world's largest tidal power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihwa_Lake_Tidal_Power_Station).[^cite_note-Chilbosan-51] Suwon is primarily situated in a basin-like terrain surrounded by low hills such as Gwanggyosan to the north, with the historic Suwon Hwaseong Fortress serving as a central landmark that reflects the city's unique radial urban structure developed since the late 18th century.

### Geology and topography

Suwon is primarily composed of [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) metamorphic rock. It has [amphibolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibolite) that intrude through these, and also granites from the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) Era.[^cite_note-GeologyOverview-52]

Most of Suwon is composed of biotite granite (Jbgr) from the [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) period. This granite is centred on Paldalsan. A form of Daebo granite, this rock is distributed through Homaesil-dong, Geumgok-dong, Dangsu-dong, Seryu-dong, Seodun-dong, Gwonseon-dong, and other areas. Its main constituent minerals are quartz, [plagioclase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagioclase), orthotic, biotite, and amphibole.[^cite_note-GeologyOverview-52]

Precambrian biotite gneiss (PCEbgn) is found in northern Suwon, specifically Pajang-dong, Gwanggyo-dong, Woncheon-dong, and Maetan-dong. Visible rocks here are composed of [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar), [biotite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotite), [amphibole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibole), and [muscovite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscovite); and are generally dark grey or dark green. Mesozoic biotite granite intrudes through these.[^cite_note-GeologyOverview-52]

Precambrian quartzo-feldspathic gneiss (PCEqgn) is distributed in some mountainous areas in Hagwanggyo-dong and Sanggwanggyo-dong in northern Suwon. This gneiss has primarily undergone [silicification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicification), and is mainly composed of quartz, feldspar, biotite, and muscovite. It is grey, grey-brown, and white.[^cite_note-GeologyOverview-52]

Suwon's single tectonic fault splits from the Singal Fault in Iui-dong, creating the Woncheonri Stream. The stream follows the fault through Ha-dong, Woncheon-dong, and Maetan-dong till it joins the Hwangguji Stream in Annyeong-dong, [Hwaseong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong,_Gyeonggi). This is a 20 km-long vertical fault running SSW, eventually to the Yellow Sea. In Suwon, biotite gneiss and biotite granite are brought into contact by the fault.[^cite_note-GeologyOverview-52]

While the low-lying fault sits in the south of Suwon, the north is hillier: the city's highest point is [Gwanggyosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanggyosan) (582 m (1,909 ft)) on the border with Yongin.[^cite_note-Gwanggyosan-53]

### Streams and lakes

Most of Suwon's streams originate on Gwanggyosan or other nearby peaks. Since the city is bounded to the north by Gwanggyosan, to the west by Chilbosan, and to the east by other hills, the streams, chiefly the Hwanggujicheon, [Suwoncheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwoncheon), [Seohocheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seohocheon), and Woncheollicheon, flow southwards.[^cite_note-Streams-54] After merging, they eventually empty into the Yellow Sea at [Asan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asan_Bay). The entirety of Suwon is drained in this manner.[^cite_note-WaterFlowJinwi-55]

Several of Suwon's streams feature lakes. Since there are few natural lakes on the Korean mainland,[^cite_note-FewNaturalLakesSeoEtAl-56] Suwon's lakes are small reservoirs. These 11 reservoirs are [Chungmanje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chungmanje?action=edit\&redlink=1), otherwise known as Seoho (서호) near Hwaseo Station;[^cite_note-ChungmanjeEKC-57] Irwol Reservoir (일원 저수지) near Sungkyunkwan University; Bambat Reservoir (밤밭 저수지) near Sungkyunkwan University Station;[^cite_note-BambatGGN-58] [Manseokkeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manseokkeo?action=edit\&redlink=1), otherwise known as Irwang Reservoir (일왕 저수지) in Manseok Park;[^cite_note-ManseokkeoEKC-59] Pajang Reservoir (파장 저수지) near the North Suwon exit of the Yeongdong Expressway; [Gwanggyo Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanggyo_Reservoir?action=edit\&redlink=1) (광교 저수지) and Hagwanggyo Reservoir (하광교 소류지) at the foot of Gwanggyosan; Woncheon and Sindae Reservoirs (원천 저수지, 신대 저수지) in Gwanggyo Lake Park; and Geumgok Reservoir (금곡 저수지), a small lake at the foot of Chilbosan. Irwang Reservoir (Manseokkeo) has been designated a world heritage site for irrigation.[^cite_note-IrwangIrrigationYonhap-60] Wangsong Reservoir (왕송 저수지), on the border with Uiwang, used to be partly in Suwon, but after controversial boundary changes, it is now entirely in Uiwang.[^cite_note-BoundaryChangeControversy-61]

### Climate

Suwon has both a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfa*), and a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen: *Cwa*).[^cite_note-ClimateData-62]

The city is prone to occasional flooding: the 1998 flood caused the death of a US soldier,[^cite_note-1998FloodBBC-63] and 145 mm (5.7 in) of rain fell in 24 hours in 2010.[^cite_note-2010FloodAlJazeera-64]

### Administrative divisions

Districts of Suwon

The city is composed of four *[gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_South_Korea#Gu_\(District\))* (districts).[^cite_note-Suwon_Statistics-68] [Jangan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jangan_District) (장안구) and [Gwonseon District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwonseon_District) (권선구) were established on 1 July 1988. Suwon has several new 'towns', e.g., Homaesil[^cite_note-HomaesilTraffic-70] and [Gwanggyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanggyo_New_Town). The latter is perhaps the most notable of these: the first stage of its construction was completed in 2011.[^cite_note-GwanggyoStart-71]

## Economy

The largest employer in Suwon is [Samsung Electronics](46445426.md), which was founded in the city in 1969.[^cite_note-SamsungCambridge-93] Its headquarters remain in Suwon, located today with the company's large R\&D complex in Maetan-dong. Samsung's presence in the city can be seen through its sponsorship of local sports teams such as Suwon Samsung Bluewings Football Club[^cite_note-BluewingsHistory-94] and two of the oldest domestic basketball teams—Samsung Thunders and Samsung Life Blueminx—both of which have since left Suwon.[^cite_note-ThundersMoveChosun-95][^cite_note-ThundersMoveKyeonggi-96][^cite_note-SamsungLifeMoveChosun-97][^cite_note-SamsungLifeMoveKyeonggi-98] Other major companies in Suwon include [SK Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Corporation),[^cite_note-SKC-99] [Samsung SDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_SDI),[^cite_note-SDIBusinessKorea-100] and [Samsung Electro-Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Electro-Mechanics).[^cite_note-SamsungElectroMechanicsForbes-101]

### Samsung Electronics

On September 1, 1973, Samsung Electronics moved its headquarters to Suwon from Euljiro, Seoul. This was to establish an electronic components facility process with Japan's SANYO Electric Co., Ltd. As a result, the status of Suwon City improved along with the growth of Samsung Electronics.

## Landmarks

### Hwaseong Fortress

Main article: [Hwaseong Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_Fortress)

[Hwaseong Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwaseong_Fortress), built under the orders of King Jeongjo in 1796, is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.[^cite_note-UNESCO-21] The entire city used to be encircled by the fortress walls,[^cite_note-HwaseongKOCIS-102] but Suwon has long since expanded far beyond this boundary. There are four main gates in the walls,[^cite_note-UNESCO-21] and Haenggung Palace lies in the centre of the fortress.[^cite_note-HaenggungLocationSWCF-103]

Hwaseong was built under the guidance of philosopher [Chŏng Yagyong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%8Fng_Yagyong).[^cite_note-UNESCO-21] Workers were paid for their labor for one of the first times in Korea's history, [corvée labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corv%C3%A9e) having been common previously.[^cite_note-PaidLabor-104] Construction details were meticulously recorded in the text *Hwaseong Seongyeok Uigwe* (화성 성역 의궤).[^cite_note-Uigwe-105] This document was invaluable after the Korean War: reconstruction efforts from 1964 to the present day have relied heavily on this.[^cite_note-UNESCO-21]

### Hyanggyo

Suwon [Hyanggyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyanggyo) (수원향교; 水原鄕校) was a government-run school and [Confucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Confucianism) ceremonial centre during the Goryeo and Joseon periods. During the Joseon Dynasty, it was the largest and oldest state school in Gyeonggi Province.[^cite_note-AHA-106] The school was originally built in 1291 beside Hwasan in Wau-ri, Hwaseong-gun. It was moved to its current location at 107–9 Hyanggyo-ro, Gyo-dong around 1795, when Hwaseong Fortress was built.[^cite_note-VisitKorea-107] The school houses memorial tablets to [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius), [Mencius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mencius), and 25 Korean figures noteworthy to Confucianism.[^cite_note-Trippose-108] It is open to the public on weekdays from 9 a.m. till 5 p.m., but it is closed at weekends.[^cite_note-VisitKorea-107]

### Bugugwon

Bugugwon (부국원), built prior to 1923, is a cultural centre at 130 Hyanggyo-ro, Gyo-dong. There is no record of the 85.95 m<sup>2</sup> building's construction, but exterior photographs were published in 1923. Under Japanese rule, the building was the headquarters of Bugugwon Co., Ltd., which sold agricultural products such as fertilizers. After liberation, from 1952 to 1956, it temporarily housed the Suwon Court and the Public Prosecutor's Office. From 1957 to 1960, it was used as the Suwon City Education Support Office, and in 1974 the Republican Party used it as their Gyeonggi Province base. In 1979, the Suwon Arts Foundation was based here, and in 1981 it became an internal medicine clinic.[^cite_note-KNH-109] Since 2018, it has been a public cultural space.[^cite_note-BugugwonCulturalSpace-110]

### Adams Memorial Hall

[Adams Memorial Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_Memorial_Hall?action=edit\&redlink=1) served as a focal point for the [independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_independence_movement). The building was constructed in 1923 under Pastor [William Noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Noble_\(missionary\)) with funding from various sources, including a church in the United States, Suwon Jongno Church, and residents. Here, independence activists including Park Seon-tae and Lee Deuk-su met weekly to discuss their activities.[^cite_note-Adams-111]

## Culture and contemporary life

### Housing

As is typical of urban South Korea,[^cite_note-ApartmentLiving-112] Suwon has many apartment complexes. The city has been affected greatly by real estate price fluctuations.[^cite_note-PriceFluctuationsHankyung-113]

### Food

Suwon is known for Suwon [galbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galbi), a variation on beef ribs enjoyed throughout Korea.[^cite_note-GalbiGyeonggiGrandTour-114]

### Sport

[Suwon World Cup Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_World_Cup_Stadium) was built for the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-WCStadium-115] Today, it is home to the second-tier [K League 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_League_2) team [Suwon Samsung Bluewings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Samsung_Bluewings). Their city rivals [Suwon FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_FC) also field a men's team in K League 2, while [Suwon FC Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_FC_Women) play in the [WK League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WK_League). Both Suwon FC teams play home matches at [Suwon Sports Complex](3958897.md).[^cite_note-SuwonFCGroundKLeagueUnited-116][^cite_note-SuwonFCWomenChosun-117]



[KT Wiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KT_Wiz) Stadium

Since 2013, Suwon has been home to the professional baseball team [KT Wiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KT_Wiz). The team played at Sungkyunkwan University until [Suwon Baseball Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Baseball_Stadium) remodeling was completed in time for their elevation to the [KBO League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBO_League) in 2015.[^cite_note-KTWizSKKUSBS-118] The stadium was previously the home of the [Hyundai Unicorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Unicorns), who folded after the 2007 season.[^cite_note-Unicorns_KoreaTimes-119]

Two of the Korean Basketball League and Women's Korean Basketball League's oldest teams, [Samsung Thunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Samsung_Thunders) and [Samsung Life Blueminx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Life_Blueminx) respectively, used to be based in Suwon. Samsung Thunders relocated to Jamsil Arena in Seoul in 2001[^cite_note-ThundersMoveChosun-95][^cite_note-ThundersMoveKyeonggi-96] while Samsung Life moved to Yongin in 2005.[^cite_note-SamsungLifeMoveChosun-97][^cite_note-SamsungLifeMoveKyeonggi-98] Top-flight men's basketball returned to Suwon in 2021, when [KT Sonicboom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_KT_Sonicboom) relocated from Busan to the renamed Suwon KT Sonicboom Arena (formerly Seosuwon Chilbo Gymnasium).[^cite_note-SonicBoomBusanSuwon-120]

The 5,145-capacity [Suwon Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Gymnasium) is home to the men's and women's [V-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-League_\(South_Korea\)) volleyball teams [Suwon KEPCO Vixtorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_KEPCO_Vixtorm) and [Suwon Hyundai Engineering & Construction Hillstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Hyundai_Engineering_&_Construction_Hillstate) respectively.[^cite_note-VolleyballVolleybox-121] This gymnasium staged the [handball events in the 1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-SuwonGymnasium1988-122] It also hosted [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball_at_the_2014_Asian_Games) and [table tennis at the 2014 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis_at_the_2014_Asian_Games)[^cite_note-AsianGames-123] and hosted the 2010 Judo World Cup.[^cite_note-Judo2010IJF-124][^cite_note-Judo2010BBC-125][^cite_note-Judo2010KBS-126]

### Museums

Suwon has two national museums. The National Map Museum of Korea houses a collection of 33,598 maps.[^cite_note-MapMuseum-127] This museum is located at 92 Worldcup-ro, Woncheon-dong. Admission is free, and the museum opens daily from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m.[^cite_note-MapMuseum-127] Suwon's other national museum, the National Agricultural Museum of Korea, opened by Seoho Lake in December 2022.[^cite_note-AgrMuseumYonhap-128] This museum is located at 154 Suin-ro, Seodun-dong, admission is free, and it is open daily from 10 a.m. till 6 p.m.[^cite_note-AgrMuseumEntry-129]

There are also a number of smaller museums in Suwon. For example, Suwon Hwaseong Museum, at 21 Changnyong-daero, Maehyang-dong, features exhibits contextualizing and explaining the construction of Hwaseong.[^cite_note-HwaseongMuseumNews-130] A smaller museum, which focuses on the history of toilets, [Haewoojae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haewoojae), has gathered some international attention.[^cite_note-ToiletMuseumBBC-131] Built privately in 2007 at 463 Jangan-ro, Imok-dong, this museum is dedicated to the history of toilets.[^cite_note-ToiletMuseumBBC-131] Ownership of Haewoojae was transferred to the city council in 2009.[^cite_note-ToiletMuseumTourist-132]

### Libraries

Suwon Central Library opened in 1980 at 318 Paldalsan-ro, Gyo-dong.[^cite_note-Libraries-133][^cite_note-CentralLibrary-134] Today, the city has 27 public libraries: seven in Gwonseon District, five in Jangan District, six in Paldal District, and nine in Yeongtong District.[^cite_note-Libraries-133] The council also plans to build another in Imok-dong.[^cite_note-ImokLibraryPlan-135]

### Parks and gardens

Suwon has two municipal arboreta: Irwol Arboretum (일월 수목원) and Yeongheung Arboretum (영흥 수목원). These opened simultaneously on 19 May 2023 beside Irwol Reservoir and Yeongheung Park respectively.[^cite_note-ArboretaKBS-136][^cite_note-ArboretaSeoulNews-137] The 10.15-hectare (25.1-acre) Irwol Arboretum features 429,000 plants of 52,000 species, while Yeongheung Arboretum hosts 118,000 plants of 42,000 species over 14.6 hectares (36 acres).[^cite_note-ArboretaSeoulNews-137] There are also 338 parks scattered through the city.[^cite_note-138] Some of these, e.g., [Gwanggyo Lake Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwanggyo_Lake_Park), Seoho Park, Irwol Park, and [Manseok Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manseok_Park), contain sizeable lakes.[^cite_note-LakesChosun-139]

### Street art

Haenggung-dong and Ji-dong in central Suwon are known for their murals,[^cite_note-MuralsLonelyPlanet-140] while Haenggung-dong streets have a variety of other artistic features such as optical illusions.[^cite_note-HaenggungdongSWCF-141]

Suwon Station

[Suwon Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Station) is served by [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX) and other trains on the [Gyeongbu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_Line), which connects [Seoul](19159283.md) to [Busan](68115.md).[^cite_note-SuwonStationKTXKoreaTrains-159] From 1930 to 1972, the [Suryeo Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suryeo_Line) also connected Suwon to Yeoju,[^cite_note-SuryeoSuinHistory-160] and from 1937 to 1996, the [Suin Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suin_Line) ran from Suwon to Incheon.[^cite_note-SuryeoSuinHistory-160] The Suin Line has since been reconstructed as part of the [Seoul Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Subway).[^cite_note-SuryeoSuinHistory-160][^cite_note-SuinCompletion-161] Today, three Seoul Metro lines (14 stations) serve Suwon,[^cite_note-MetroStationsCityCouncil-162] and there are plans for network expansion. Construction of an extension of the Sinbundang Line to Homaesil is scheduled to begin in 2024.[^cite_note-Sinbundang2024-163] Another planned line—the [Dongtan–Indeogwon Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongtan%E2%80%93Indeogwon_Line)—should create several new stations in Suwon, but this has been delayed, prompting affected cities to call for urgent action.[^cite_note-DongtanIndeogwonDelay-164]

Gwanggyo Jungang Underground Transfer Centre

Suwon is also served by two inter-city bus terminals with nationwide connections: [Suwon Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Bus_Terminal) near Seryu Station,[^cite_note-SuwonTerminal-165] and [West Suwon Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Suwon_Bus_Terminal) in Guun-dong.[^cite_note-WestSuwonTerminal-166] Nevertheless, bus terminal passenger numbers are decreasing.[^cite_note-167] Suwon is also connected to Seoul and other nearby cities by city and express buses with departure points across the city.[^cite_note-GyeonggiBuses-168] In 2017, a new bank of bus stops opened at Suwon Station Transfer Center. This was built to alleviate pressure on existing bus and taxi stands across the tracks.[^cite_note-SuwonStationTransferCenter-169] Another transfer centre is incorporated into Gwanggyo Jungang Station; this is underground, and bus stands feature screen doors.[^cite_note-UndergroundBusStation-170] Suwon has invested heavily in electric buses—in 2019, it built the country's largest bus charging station at 46 Gyeongsu-daero 1220beon-gil, Pajang-dong.[^cite_note-BusCharging-171]

Suwon is served by several expressways. The [Yeongdong Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdong_Expressway) (50) passes through the city, with two exits within the city limits: North Suwon and East Suwon.[^cite_note-172] The Gwanggyo Sanghyeon exit on the [Yongin–Seoul Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongin%E2%80%93Seoul_Expressway) (171) is on Suwon's border with Yongin,[^cite_note-SanghyeonIC-173] and the [Pyeongtaek–Paju Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyeongtaek%E2%80%93Paju_Expressway) (17) also has an exit in Suwon (Geumgok).[^cite_note-GeumgokIC-174] The Suwon exit of the [Gyeongbu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbu_Expressway) (1) was renamed *Suwon Singal* in 2014 to reflect its actual location in Singal in neighboring Yongin.[^cite_note-SuwonSingal-175]

Suwon has invested in ecological transport.[^cite_note-EcocapitalKoreaTimes-176] The city was the first place in Korea to introduce dockless public bicycles.[^cite_note-Dockless-177] Traversing Suwon by bicycle is facilitated by numerous cycle paths beside the streams that cut through the city. In 2013, Suwon hosted the [EcoMobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EcoMobility) World Festival. For one month, streets in Haenggung-dong were closed to cars as a car-free experiment. Residents used non-motorized vehicles provided by the festival organizers.[^cite_note-178] The experiment was not unopposed.[^cite_note-EcomobilityOppositionJournal-179]

## Military

[Suwon Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_Air_Base) in Jangji-dong, Gwonseon District was used by the United States Air Force during the Korean War, when it was the scene of the conflict's first aerial combat.[^cite_note-FirstBattleMilitary-25] Today the base is under Republic of Korea Air Force jurisdiction, though it is still managed and maintained by the US military.[^cite_note-AirBaseGlobalSecurity-180] The US military also maintains Madison Site—a small signals unit with nearby helipad on Gwanggyosan.[^cite_note-MadisonUSArmy-181]

## Fauna

While much of Suwon's wildlife can be expected to be similar to that in the surrounding province, two species are worth noting specifically in regard to the city. Firstly, an undisclosed location in Suwon appears to be Korea's first recorded breeding site of the [white-breasted waterhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-breasted_waterhen).[^cite_note-Waterhen-182] Secondly, the [Suwon tree frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwon_tree_frog)—one of three tree frogs to inhabit the Korean peninsula—[^cite_note-TreeFrogNumbersBorzee-183][^cite_note-TreeFrogRangeDonga-184] was discovered in Suwon around 1980, but due to urban sprawl it is no longer found in the city. It has, however, been found recently in Paju, Ansan, and Pyeongtaek (Gyeonggi Province); Eumseong (Chungcheongbuk-do); Gangwon-do; and North Korea.[^cite_note-SuwonTreeFrogKBS-185] The species is considered endangered.[^cite_note-TreeFrogEndangered-186][^cite_note-TreeFrogNumbersBorzee-183][^cite_note-TreeFrogRangeDonga-184]
