The **Washington DC Open**, branded as the **Mubadala DC Open** for sponsorship reasons, is an annual professional outdoor [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt) [tennis](29773.md) tournament played at the [William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H.G._FitzGerald_Tennis_Center) in [Rock Creek Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Creek_Park) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) The event is categorized as an [ATP 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500_tournaments) event on the [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour) and a [WTA 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_500_tournaments) event on the [WTA Tour](1398173.md) and is the only combined 500 event in the world.[^cite_note-History-2][^cite_note-DCOpen-3]

Organized annually in the summer schedule of events on North American hardcourts leading up to the [US Open](82729.md), the Washington Open was first held in 1969 as the **[Washington Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Star) International**.[^cite_note-History-2] It was held on [clay courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court) until 1986, when the surface was changed to hardcourts. In 2011, the event expanded to include its first women's tournament, a [WTA International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_International_tournaments) (now [WTA 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_250_tournaments)) competition held in a separate venue in [College Park, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Park,_Maryland). The following year, the men's and women's events were consolidated at the Washington venue.&#x20;

In 2023, the WTA 500-level [Silicon Valley Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley_Classic) was discontinued and merged with the Citi DC Open, forming the first joint-500-level event on the ATP and WTA tours. The tournament is owned and managed by [Mark Ein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ein) in partnership with [IMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_\(company\)).[^cite_note-4]

## History



The tournament's center court

The tournament was first held on the men's tour in 1969, known as the Washington Star International from 1969 to 1981, the Sovran Bank Classic from 1982 to 1992, the Newsweek Tennis Classic in 1993, the Legg Mason Tennis Classic from 1994 to 2011, and the Citi Open from 2012 to 2022. Competition was held on outdoor clay courts until 1986 when it switched to the current hard courts. Co-founders John A. Harris and [Donald Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Dell), founder of ProServ International, have since remained closely involved. The location of the event in Washington, D.C., was chosen at the urging of [Arthur Ashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe), an early supporter.[^cite_note-History-2][^cite_note-DCOpen-3]

The women's event was first held in 2011 in [College Park, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Park,_Maryland), as the Citi Open, and for the 2012 season, the ATP and WTA decided to merge their Maryland and Washington spots into a joint tournament, with the women's event moving to the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center, and [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup) replacing [Legg Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legg_Mason) as title sponsor of the joint event.[^cite_note-5]

In 2015, the Washington Open dropped out of the [US Open Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_Series) because of disagreements with [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), which that year took over broadcast rights to the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_\(Tennis\)) and US Open Series events. ESPN would only commit to airing four hours of coverage from the tournament on [ESPN2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN2), with the rest relegated to streaming on [ESPN3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN3); by contrast, coverage had been split between ESPN2 and [Tennis Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Channel) in 2014.[^cite_note-6] Donald Dell criticized ESPN for using ESPN3 to acquire sports rights without any intent to broadcast them on linear television, arguing that "if you're running a tournament, and it's \$2 million, and sponsorship money in the \$6 million-to-\$8 million range, you've got sponsors that don't want to be having only four or six hours on television." A four-year, \$2.1 million agreement was reached with Tennis Channel, promising 171 hours of television coverage. The broadcast revenue was also used to fund additional amenities at the tournament venue.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In 2019, the Washington Open was acquired by venture capitalist and USTA board member [Mark Ein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ein). It returned to the US Open Series, and also signed a five-year extension of its media rights with Tennis Channel.[^cite_note-9] The 2020 tournament was cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The men's event returned for 2021, but the women's event remained cancelled; the WTA did not reinstate its sanctioning of the tournament due to conflicts with the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The tournament instead organized a women's invitational, featuring [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff), [Jessica Pegula](32944701.md), and [Jennifer Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Brady).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-GauffInterview-14]

In June 2023, Ein and [IMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_\(company\)) announced that the Washington Open would merge with the [Silicon Valley Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley_Classic) to form a single tournament in Washington, D.C.; this therefore promoted the Washington Open from a [WTA 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_250_tournaments) event to a [WTA 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_500_tournaments) event. Players had usually been divided between the two tournaments, as the Silicon Valley Classic was more prestigious, but the Washington Open was in closer proximity to the other tournaments of the North American hardcourt season. As a result of the merger, the Silicon Valley Classic's title sponsor [Mubadala Investment Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubadala_Investment_Company) became a co-title sponsor of the event, rebranding it as the Mubadala Citi DC Open. The tournament was the first-ever joint 500-level event on the ATP and WTA tours, and remains the only joint 500 event in the world.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Citigroup did not renew its sponsorship, and Mubadala became the sole title sponsor of the tournament beginning in 2026 under a five-year deal.[^cite_note-18]

## Past finals



A night match in 2018.



Grandstand in 2017.



A side court in 2017.

In the men's singles, [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi) (1990–91, 1995, 1998–99) holds the records for most titles (five) and most finals overall (six, runner-up in 2000). He also shares with [Michael Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chang) (1996–97), [Juan Martín del Potro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro) (2008–09) and [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) (2017–18) the record for most consecutive titles, with two. In the women's singles, [Magdaléna Rybáriková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdal%C3%A9na_Ryb%C3%A1rikov%C3%A1) (2012–13) holds the record for most titles (two) and co-holds the record for most finals (two) with [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova) (runner-up in 2012, 2015). In the men's doubles, [Marty Riessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Riessen) (1971–72, 1974, 1979) and [the Bryan brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bryan_brothers) (2005–07, 2015) hold the record for most titles (four), with the Bryans also holding the record for most consecutive titles (three). The Bryans co-hold the record for most finals (six, runners-up in 2001–02) with [Raúl Ramírez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Ram%C3%ADrez) (winner in 1976, 1981–82, runner-up in 1975, 1978–79). In the women's doubles, [Shuko Aoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuko_Aoyama) (2012–14) holds alone the record for most titles, most consecutive titles and most finals (three).
