[Presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_elections_in_France) were held in [France](5843419.md) on 21 and 22 April 2007 to elect the successor to [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) as [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_French_Republic) of France (and *ex officio* [Co-Prince of Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-Prince_of_Andorra)) for a five-year term. As no candidate received a majority of the vote, a second round was held on 5 and 6 May 2007 between the two leading candidates, [Nicolas Sarkozy](557007.md) and [Ségolène Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal). Sarkozy was elected with 53% of the vote.[^cite_note-1]

Sarkozy and Royal both represented a generational change. Both main candidates were born after World War II,[^cite_note-2] along with the first to have seen adulthood under the [Fifth Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fifth_Republic), and the first not to have been in politics under [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle). In addition, Royal was the first woman in France's history to reach the second round in a presidential election.

The election result has been interpreted as an example of [center squeeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_squeeze), a kind of [spoiler effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoiler_effect) common to the [plurality-rule family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality-rule_family) of voting rules, since Sarkozy, a conservative, and Royal, a socialist, eliminated moderate liberal [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou) in the first round, despite polls showing a majority of voters preferred Bayrou in a one-on-one match with either of his opponents.[^cite_note-\:4-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Sarkozy ran for [re-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_French_presidential_election) in 2012, but was defeated by Royal's lifelong partner at the time, [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande).[^cite_note-6] Since the death of [Lionel Jospin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Jospin) in 2026, it is the earliest French presidential election in which either of the second round candidates is still alive.

## Electoral system



Transparent ballot boxes have been required since 1988.



Some French cities used [voting machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_machine).

For the first time in a presidential election, [electronic voting](371301.md) was introduced in some areas, having been authorised in 2004. They were introduced in only 82 of 36,000 [voting districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_communes), and were criticised by a number of people, both on the left and on the right.[^cite_note-11] A [petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petition) against them has also been made (see [Wikinews\:Electronic voting disputed in France](https://en.wikinews.org/wiki/Electronic%20voting%20disputed%20in%20France)).

Using the three colours of the national flag (blue, white, and red) on electoral advertisements or partisan documentation was prohibited by electoral regulations.[^cite_note-12] Ségolène Royal contended that the book *Ensemble* ("together") published by Nicolas Sarkozy, whose cover is blue, white and red,[^cite_note-13] was effectively an electoral partisan documentation and should have been covered by this prohibition.[^cite_note-14]

## Candidates

### Requirements

*Reference: [Constitutional Council, FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20070126071151/http://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/dossier/presidentielles/2007/faq/p01.htm)*

Further information: [President of the French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_French_Republic)

The requirements for being [successfully nominated as a candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomination_rules) are defined by the organic law of 6 November 1962.[^cite_note-15]

All candidates had to be of French nationality and at least 23 years old (the same requirement as for the candidates to the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_France)).

Candidates must obtain signatures from 500 elected officials (mayors, members of Parliament, elected representatives) supporting their candidacy. These signatures from elected officials (informally known in French as *parrainages*, but legally known as "presentations") must be from at least 30 [departments](38523.md) or overseas territories, and no more than 10 percent can be from any individual department. A presentation from an elected official does not imply the official supports the policies of the candidate, but rather that this official considers the candidate to be a serious candidate.

Candidates must also submit a statement with details of their personal assets.

The [Constitutional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Council_of_France) published the official candidate list on 20 March 2007. The candidates were listed in a [randomised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomisation) order. This order was used for the official campaign: thus, posters for Olivier Besancenot were to always be displayed on the No. 1 board, those for Marie-George Buffet on the No. 2 board, etc., regardless of where in France the boards were located.

There were 12 candidates for the 2007 election.[^cite_note-cc-9]

### Leading candidates

Four candidates consistently registered over 10% in the opinion polls and were regarded as having a reasonable chance of reaching the second round.[^cite_note-16]

- [Nicolas Sarkozy](557007.md) was nominated by the [Union for a Popular Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement) on 14 January 2007. He was the leader of the UMP and was [Interior Minister](871703.md) until stepping down to focus on his campaign on 26 March 2007.

- [Ségolène Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal) was selected by the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Socialist_Party) on [17 November 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_French_Socialist_Party_presidential_primary) to be the party's candidate for the election. She won 60.6% of the votes in a ballot of party members to choose their candidate, against 20.8% for [Dominique Strauss-Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Strauss-Kahn) and 18.5% for [Laurent Fabius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Fabius). She was the first woman to represent a major French party in a presidential contest.

- [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou) was nominated by the [centrist](39619035.md) [Union for French Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_French_Democracy) (UDF) on 2 December 2006.

- [Jean-Marie Le Pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Le_Pen) ran for the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_\(France\)), a far-right party which promotes policies of strong law enforcement, economic [protectionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism) and strong measures to control immigration. As during previous presidential campaigns, Le Pen raised the question of whether he would be able to obtain the necessary 500 signatures on a number of occasions, which he claims is the result of pressure placed on elected officials by the major parties to support their own candidate (he has often claimed, during past elections, that "political elites" have sabotaged his campaigns); however, on 14 March 2007 his party said that he had obtained the necessary signatures.

## Campaign

The election campaign raised a number of issues:

- **Jobs and unemployment** – France long had an [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) officially close to 10%, down to below 9% in 2007. Employment, and employment conditions, are a perennial concern for the French (see [Economy of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_France) and [Poverty in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_France)).

- **European disunity** – The presidential election followed the [EU Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe) [rejection vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_referendum_on_the_European_Constitution) in 2005, which threw into question the future direction of the European Union.

- **International politics** – A majority in France approved of President [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac)'s opposition to the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md) by the United States. Sarkozy was considered as pro-Washington, while Royal, although seen as probably also in line with Washington, is thought to be more moderate. Left-wing intellectual [Régis Debray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9gis_Debray)'s metaphor was that Sarkozy was like a direct flight to Washington, D.C., while Royal was a flight to Washington with a stop-off in [Oslo](22309.md), referring to her European inclinations.

  [^cite_note-Debray-24]

  &#x20;Chirac's public opposition to the [Iraq War](5043324.md) should however be relativised, as he toned down his criticisms after a while. Furthermore, he involved French [troops in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) and agreed, immediately after the [11 September 2001 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_September_2001_attacks), on increased cooperation between Western intelligence services, materialised by the creation of [Alliance Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Base) in the centre of Paris, a joint international [Counterterrorist Intelligence Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterterrorist_Intelligence_Center).

- **Law and order** – During the 2002 campaign, [law and order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_order_\(politics\)) came to the forefront, especially with respect to unruly youths from poor suburbs. In late 2005, in some of these suburbs [significant unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_civil_unrest_in_France) erupted. Again, law and order was a forefront issue, mainstream candidates tackled the problem of reining in unruly youngsters. Sarkozy proposed measures for harsher criminal procedure for youngsters, while Royal proposed to send unruly youths to centres under military discipline.

  [^cite_note-25]

  &#x20;Sarkozy's attitude was widely criticised on the left, and also by the UMP minister [Azouz Begag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azouz_Begag), who defected his party to support Bayrou following Sarkozy's management of the autumn 2005 crisis.

  [^cite_note-26]

- **Immigration** – The issue of [immigration in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_France) split France. The number of [deportations](412990.md) more than doubled since 2002, with Sarkozy as Minister of the Interior from 2002 to 2004 and 2005 to 2007.

  [^cite_note-27]

  &#x20;Sarkozy declared in April 2006 that immigrants who did not "like France" should "leave it."

  [^cite_note-SarkozyRFI-28]

  &#x20;Opponents have labelled Sarkozy's attitude as repressive, in particular towards [illegal immigrants](7124412.md),

  [^cite_note-29]

  [^cite_note-30]

  &#x20;materialised by numerous police raids against [illegal aliens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\)), strongly opposed by the left.

  [^cite_note-31]

  &#x20;The main problem concerns illegal immigrants (*sans-papiers*, "without documents") who cannot obtain [work permits](14666962.md) without proper immigration documents and are therefore mostly found in the [informal economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy) – construction, restaurants, etc. Although the [right of foreigners to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_foreigners_to_vote) was a classic claim of the left-wing, it was not an important issue of the campaign. On the other hand, Sarkozy declared himself in favour of [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action) which was widely contested both on the left and on the right, on grounds that it would favour *communautarisme* – separation of communities – along ethnic lines, and that it means taking into account ethnic alleged memberships in statistics, which is legally prohibited and not done by the [INSEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSEE). Left-wingers argued in favour of social actions not based on ethnic factors, but on geographical situation and equality of territory; however, the traditional [Universalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_universalism) of the French republicans has also been criticised on the left-wing by some intellectuals supporting a middle-ground between Republican universalism and [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism).

  [^cite_note-Balibar-32]

- **The Far Right** – The [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Front), long dismissed as a fringe party, stunned many when its leader, [Jean-Marie Le Pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Le_Pen), reached the second round of the [2002 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_French_presidential_election). Le Pen's points of focus – law and order and immigration – were later taken up by politicians such as Sarkozy. Le Pen on 12 April 2007 criticised Sarkozy for being Hungarian and asked if he should run for the [president of Hungary](380393.md).

  [^cite_note-33]

  [^cite_note-34]

  &#x20;Le Pen was 79 years old, the same age which [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) was when he stepped down from the presidency in 1969. Le Pen's approval rating in opinion polls markedly increased after France's [riots in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_civil_unrest_in_France). As a counterweight, the Left and the cultural elite and athletes (like [Lilian Thuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Thuram)) rose to oppose Sarkozy's response to the riots.

- **Anti-neoliberalism and Disarray of left-wing parties** – During the [2002 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_French_presidential_election), a number of left-wing candidates ran for office, which, according to commentators, was one reason for the defeat of Socialist candidate [Lionel Jospin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Jospin). Jospin blamed in particular [Jean-Pierre Chevènement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Chev%C3%A8nement)'s candidacy, as well as [Christiane Taubira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiane_Taubira)'s one. However, others commentators have criticised Jospin's attitude and the policies of the PS, which account, according to them, for the low score of Jospin. Inheritor of the [French Section of the Workers' International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Section_of_the_Workers'_International) (SFIO), the PS is historically [social-democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social-democrat), while the [French Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party) (PCF) is a governmental party, which participated in Jospin's *[Gauche plurielle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauche_plurielle)* (Plural Lefts) government (with ministers such as [Jean-Claude Gayssot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Gayssot), etc.). In this sense, the PCF does not consider itself a far-left party, to the contrary of the Revolutionary Communist League (LCR) or [Workers' Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Struggle) (LO). But the PCF does consider itself part of the "[anti-liberal](221220.md)" coalition, which opposed the [Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe) (TCE). Inside the [French Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Socialist_Party) (PS, *Parti socialiste*) itself, [Laurent Fabius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Fabius) headed the minority who opposed the treaty. However, the victory of the "No" during the [May 2005 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_European_Constitution_referendum) on the TCE hasn't been exploited yet by the left parties or organisations which supported it. The main topic of the non-PS left-wings was to try to choose a single candidate for the "anti-liberal" Left, which opposes [neo-liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-liberalism). This eventually failed, and the far-left was represented by [four competing candidates](#Other_candidates), Marie-George Buffet, Olivier Besancenot, José Bové and Arlette Laguiller.

Abroad, [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi), the former [prime minister of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Italy), gave his support to Sarkozy immediately following the first round, while [Romano Prodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Prodi), the then Italian premier and leader of the centre-left [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Union_\(political_coalition\)) coalition, called for an alliance between Bayrou and Royal.[^cite_note-55]

Spanish Prime Minister [José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero](19614411.md) has shown his support for Royal.[^cite_note-56]

[European commissioner](9974.md) and [Vice-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_the_European_Commission) [Margot Wallström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Wallstr%C3%B6m) was criticised after she informally suggested support for Royal on her blog, celebrating the fact that a woman got into the second round. She said: "J'étais si contente de voir qu'une femme participera au deuxième tour de l‘élection présidentielle!" (I was so happy to see that a woman would be participating in the second round of the presidential election!)[^cite_note-57] Commissioners are not meant to be politically biased in elections under their code of conduct.[^cite_note-58] Wallström is a social-democrat, like Royal. [José Manuel Barroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Barroso), the head of the European Commission, has privately discussed the idea of forming a "strategic partnership" with Mr. Sarkozy.[^cite_note-59]

Many U.S. [pundits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundit_\(expert\)) and western economists expressed support for [Nicolas Sarkozy](557007.md). [Steve Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Forbes) devoted several columns in the influential financial publication *[FORBES Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*.[^cite_note-FORBES-60] The London-based magazine *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* also expressed support for Sarkozy's economic platform .

## Opinion polls



A pro-Sarkozy sticker, after being defaced, in Paris, France. (Translation: "*Together ~~everything~~ NOTHING becomes possible.*")

French law prohibits publishing the results of opinion polls related to the election during the day of the election and the preceding day, so as to prevent undue influencing of the vote.[^cite_note-64] No estimate can be given before Sunday 20\:00, when the last voting office closes and official counts begin to be released. However, media from neighbouring countries, which are not bound by these regulations, have long broadcast estimates ([Télévision Suisse Romande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9l%C3%A9vision_Suisse_Romande) in particular). In 2007, the issue took a particular importance because of the generalisation of blogs and Internet pages. Journalist [Jean-Marc Morandini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Morandini) stirred turmoil when he announced his intention of publishing results on his blog as soon as 18\:00.[^cite_note-65] Another problem was that the results from the voting offices in the Americas (consulates and French overseas possessions) were counted on Saturday night,[^cite_note-66] and some began circulating rumours as to these results.

## Results

The first round saw a very high turnout of 83.8% – 36.7 million of the 44.5 million [electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) voted from a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_France) of 64.1 million (not including French people living abroad).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-Libe-68][^cite_note-69] The results of that round saw Sarkozy and Royal qualify for the second round with Sarkozy getting 31% and Royal 26%. [François Bayrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Bayrou) came third (19%) and [Jean-Marie Le Pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Le_Pen) fourth (10%), unlike [in 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_French_presidential_election) when Le Pen got a surprising 16.9% and qualified for the second round.[^cite_note-MondeResults-70]

Immediately after the first round's results were made official, four defeated left-wing candidates – [José Bové](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bov%C3%A9), [Marie-George Buffet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-George_Buffet), [Arlette Laguiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlette_Laguiller) and [Dominique Voynet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Voynet) – urged their supporters to vote for Royal.[^cite_note-71] This was the first time since 1981 that Laguiller had endorsed the Socialist Party's candidate.[^cite_note-72] [Olivier Besancenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Besancenot) called his supporters to vote against Sarkozy.[^cite_note-73] [Frédéric Nihous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Nihous) and [Gérard Schivardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Schivardi) never officially supported either Royal or Sarkozy. [Philippe de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_de_Villiers) called for a vote for Sarkozy.[^cite_note-74] Le Pen told his voters to "abstain massively" in the second round.[^cite_note-75]

On 25 April, Bayrou declared he would not support either candidate in the runoff,[^cite_note-76] and announced he would form a new political party called the [Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Movement_\(France\)). He criticised both major candidates, and offered to debate them. Royal agreed to hold a televised debate, while Sarkozy offered to have a private discussion but not a televised [debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_presidential_debates).[^cite_note-77]

By around 18\:15 local time on 6 May, Belgian and Swiss news sources such as *[Le Soir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Soir)*,[^cite_note-78] [RTBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTBF),[^cite_note-79]&#x20;*&#x20;[La Libre Belgique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Libre_Belgique)*[^cite_note-80] and *[La Tribune de Genève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribune_de_Gen%C3%A8ve)*[^cite_note-81] had announced Nicolas Sarkozy as the winner of the second round, citing preliminary exit poll data. The final CSA estimate showed him winning with 53% of the votes cast. Royal conceded defeat to Sarkozy that evening.[^cite_note-82]

### First round and analysis

Nationwide, Nicolas Sarkozy obtained 31% and Ségolène Royal 26% – while in 2002, [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) had obtained 20%, and [Lionel Jospin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Jospin) 16.18%. The right-of-centre François Bayrou obtained 18.6% this time, nearly tripling his 2002 result (6.8%). National Front (FN) candidate, Jean-Marie Le Pen, made only 10.4%, compared to his stunning 16.9% finish in 2002. Along with the April–May shift to the far right made by Sarkozy, this has led many commentators to allege that traditional voters of the FN had been tempted by Sarkozy.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-SarkozyRFI-28] Overall, the left-wing reached 36% of the votes, against 19% for the "centre", 33% for the right wing and 11% for the [far right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_far-right_movements_in_France).

Other candidates received a much lower share of the vote than they had in 2002, with [Olivier Besancenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Besancenot) ([Revolutionary Communist League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Communist_League_\(France\)), LCR) failing to achieve the 5% necessary to have his political campaign reimbursed by the state. Besancenot received 4.1%, compared to 4.3% in 2002. He was followed by the traditionalist [Philippe de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_de_Villiers) (2.2%), Communist [Marie-George Buffet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-George_Buffet) (1.9%, compared to 3.4% for [Robert Hue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hue) in 2002), Green candidate [Dominique Voynet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Voynet) (1.6%, compared to 5.3% for [Noël Mamère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Mam%C3%A8re) in 2002), [Workers' Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Struggle)'s candidate [Arlette Laguiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlette_Laguiller) (1.3%, compared to 5.7% in 2002), [alter-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter-globalisation) candidate [José Bové](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bov%C3%A9) (1.3%), [Frédéric Nihous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Nihous) (1.2% , against 4.2% for [Jean Saint-Josse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Saint-Josse) in 2002) and finally [Gérard Schivardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Schivardi) with 0.3% ([Daniel Gluckstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Gluckstein) had achieved 0.5% in 2002). The [abstention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstention) rate was 15.4%.

With an overall record [turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) of 83.8%, a level not achieved since the [1965 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_French_presidential_election) when turnout was 84.8%, the vast majority of the electorate decided not to stay home. Most of them decided against [protest votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_vote), and chose the *vote utile* ([tactical voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_voting), literally "useful vote"), that is, a vote for one of the purported leaders of the electoral race (Nicolas Sarkozy, Ségolène Royal and/or François Bayrou). The "Anyone But Sarkozy" push benefited both Bayrou and Royal,[^cite_note-Clivage-84] while the tactical voting, on the right or on the left, explains the low score of the other candidates, in contrast with the last presidential election's first round.

The electoral campaign saw a polarisation of the political scene, encapsulated by the "Anyone But Sarkozy" slogan on the left. But it also saw a reconfiguration of the political chessboard, with various left-wing figures and voters deciding to support Sarkozy against Royal, who saw opposition inside her own party. [Bernard Tapie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Tapie), a former Socialist, [Max Gallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Gallo), who had supported left-wing Republican [Jean-Pierre Chevènement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Chev%C3%A8nement) in 2002, [Eric Besson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Besson),[^cite_note-85] etc., passed on Sarkozy's side. On the other hand, some right-wing voters, upset by Sarkozy's attitude on law and order, immigration, and even genetics (his recent declarations on [paedophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paedophilia), homosexuality and suicides as [genetically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) induced, denounced by the geneticist [Axel Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Kahn)[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]), decided to vote for Bayrou. Centrist figures of the Socialist party, such as [Michel Rocard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Rocard) and [Bernard Kouchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Kouchner), called for an alliance between Bayrou and Royal, which might have had consequences in the [June 2007 legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_French_legislative_elections) – these determined the [parliamentary majority](465640.md), and decided that France would not see another *[cohabitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohabitation_\(government\))* between the President, head of state, and the Prime minister, leader of the government. Former socialist minister [Claude Allègre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_All%C3%A8gre) stated such an alliance was "entirely conceivable", while Royal herself strongly criticised Rocard's comments. [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande), the national secretary of the Socialist Party and Ségolène Royal's partner, excluded any alliance with the centre-right, along with others left-wing leaders, such as [Laurent Fabius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Fabius) or [Dominique Voynet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Voynet).[^cite_note-91]

Voting booth in [Vaulnaveys-le-Haut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaulnaveys-le-Haut).

In urban areas, most lower and middle-income neighbourhoods and cities voted largely for Ségolène Royal. In the [tenth arrondissement of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_arrondissement_of_Paris), Royal obtained 42% against 25% for Sarkozy, and 20.35% for Bayrou; in the [11th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_arrondissement_of_Paris), Royal obtained more than 40.8% to 25.8% for Sarkozy and 20.9% for Bayrou. In the [18th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVIIIe_arrondissement), Royal obtained 41.1% against 23.4% for Sarkozy; in the [19th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_arrondissement_of_Paris), Royal obtained more than 39%, against almost 28% for Sarkozy; and in the [20th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_arrondissement_of_Paris), Royal obtained 42.4% against 23.2% for Sarkozy, and 18.3% for Bayrou. Royal also narrowly beat Sarkozy in the normally conservative city of [Bordeaux](4097.md) (31.4% against 30.8%, and 22% for Bayrou), as well as in [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_France), [Caen](92443.md), [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand), [Grenoble](50972.md), [Nantes](67453.md), [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen), [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans), [Montpellier](73369.md), [Saint-Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-%C3%89tienne), [Limoges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limoges), [Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens), [Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Atlantiques) (where Bayrou finished first), [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes) and [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) (the historical base of the former [Radical-Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical-Socialist_Party_\(France\))). Working-class Paris suburbs (called *les [banlieues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banlieue)*) also massively voted for Royal. This was more or less expected, in particular with the high level of voter registration by suburban youths, who had been strongly opposed to Sarkozy since the [2005 riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_civil_unrest_in_France) during which he had made controversial remarks. Meanwhile, a large number of university students had participated in the [protests against the CPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_labor_protests_in_France), proposed by Sarkozy's UMP party, in the spring of 2006; they also strongly backed Royal. She consequently came first in [Nanterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanterre), with almost 36% against 23% for Sarkozy. She reached 41.6% in [Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis), against 19.6% for Sarkozy and 15.5% for Bayrou. In [Évry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vry,_Essonne), she also passed the 40% line, while Sarkozy received only 23.6%. In [Créteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9teil), she won a closer race, gaining 35% to Sarkozy's 30% and 18% for Bayrou. In the department of [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis), home to many people of immigrant origin, Royal obtained 34.2% to 26.8% for Sarkozy and 16.7% for Bayrou.

In contrast, wealthy [arrondissements of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Paris) voted for Sarkozy. The prosperous [16th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_arrondissement_of_Paris) gave him 64% of its vote, against 16.4% for Bayrou and only 11.27% for Royal; the [seventh arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_arrondissement_of_Paris) voted for 56% in favour of Sarkozy, to 20.35 for Bayrou and 15.35% for Royal; the [eighth arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_arrondissement_of_Paris) voted at more than 58% for Sarkozy to 18.65% for Bayrou and 14% for Royal; the [15th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_arrondissement_of_Paris) voted 41.5% for Sarkozy against 24.3% for Royal and 22.9% for Bayrou. The mostly wealthy Paris suburbs of the [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine) department, home of [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine) where Sarkozy is [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_\(France\)), voted 38.3% for him, against 26% for Royal and 21.3% for Bayrou. Sarkozy also won in the [Essonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essonne) department (more than 31% against 27% for Royal), in the [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne) (33.5% to almost 24% for Royal) as well as in the [Yvelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvelines) (37.7% against 23% for Royal and 22% for Bayrou).

[Marseille](40888948.md), the second-largest city of France, went Sarkozy's way overall as he won 34.25% of the vote to 27.1% for Royal and only 14.1% for Bayrou (putting a close third ahead of Le Pen, who obtained 13.4%). However, in working-class neighbourhoods of the north of Marseille, such as Savine ([15th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_arrondissement_of_Marseille)) and the Busserine ([14th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_arrondissement_of_Marseille)), Royal received overwhelming support, receiving 60% of the vote in Busserine.

France's third-largest city, [Lyon](8638634.md), also was won by Sarkozy, who received 34.5% of the vote to 27.3% for Royal and 22% for Bayrou. He triumphed as well in the wealthy city of [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence) with 36.8%, against 25.4% for Royal and 19.8% for Bayrou. In [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), a conservative stronghold, Sarkozy obtained more than 41% against 20.4% for Royal and less than 15% for Bayrou. Sarkozy also narrowly beat Royal in the industrial port of [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre) (29% against 26.8%), as well as in [Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon), [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%AEmes), [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metz), [Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy,_France), and [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) (these last three cities belonging to the [Alsace-Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine) region).

### Regional votes

A map of France's departments shows the candidate of the Socialist Party, Ségolène Royal, came first in the South-West and the [Massif Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif_Central), which were traditional bases of the [Radical-Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_\(France\)) during the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic). She also topped the poll in [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany_\(administrative_region\)), except in the department of [Morbihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbihan), but a fifth of electors in Brittany voted for Bayrou.[^cite_note-92] [Nièvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%A8vre) and [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) were other departments where she came first, as well as the overseas departments of [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) and [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) and the overseas territory of [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon). Sarkozy came first everywhere else, except for [Pyrénées-Atlantiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Atlantiques), where Bayrou topped the poll in the department of his birth.

The left regressed, compared 2002, in the [Nord-Pas-de-Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord-Pas-de-Calais) region, which has traditionally favored Socialist and Communist candidates. The [Nord department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord_\(French_department\)), hit hard during the 1980s by an industrial crisis, gave a plurality to Sarkozy (29.3%), while Royal won 24.8% (and won the city of [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille)) and Bayrou received 15.6%. [Marie-George Buffet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-George_Buffet) barely received 5% in the constituency of the Communist deputy [Alain Bocquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Bocquet).

The [Haute-Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Garonne), traditional Radical-Socialist territory, voted (including its capital, Toulouse), for Ségolène Royal, giving her 33%, against less than 27% for Sarkozy and slightly more than 19% for Bayrou. The [Corrèze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corr%C3%A8ze), where Jacques Chirac began his political career as the deputy of [Ussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ussel,_Corr%C3%A8ze), also voted slightly in favour of Royal, as did the [Creuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creuse), one of the least-populated departments of France.



Results of candidates with over 3% of votes in the first round, by [departments](38523.md) of [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France).

The [Alpes-Maritimes](80989.md), part of the [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) region where the National Front won several cities in the 1990s ([Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon) of the [Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_\(department\)), [Marignane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marignane) of the [Bouches-du-Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouches-du-Rh%C3%B4ne) and [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_Vaucluse) of the [Vaucluse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaucluse)) voted for Sarkozy at 43.6%, while Royal received only 17.9%, Bayrou 15.0%, and Jean-Marie Le Pen 13.5%. The [Vaucluse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaucluse) department gave 32.8% of its votes to Sarkozy, 20.9% to Royal, 16.8% to Le Pen and 15.5% to Bayrou.

The [Vendée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vend%C3%A9e) voted 29.7% for Sarkozy, 21.7% for Royal, 20.8% for Bayrou, and 11.3% for [Philippe de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_de_Villiers), deputy of the department. Le Pen. meanwhile, managed only 6.5%.

Le Pen's highest departmental tallies occurred in [Aisne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisne) (17.3%) and [Haute-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Marne) (17%). Other departments to give him more than 15% were the [Vaucluse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaucluse) (16.8%), [Haute-Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Sa%C3%B4ne) (16.5%), [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse_\(department\)) (16.3%), [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_\(department\)) (16.2% – where left-wing candidate Besancenot received 5.35%), [Pas-de-Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pas-de-Calais) (16%), [Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oise) (15.9%), [Corse-du-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corse-du-Sud) (15.9%), [Vosges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vosges_\(department\)) (15.7%), and [Gard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gard) (15.4%),

Departments where Besancenot obtained more than 5% of the vote include [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_\(department\)), Aisne (where Le Pen also achieved a strong results), [Ariège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari%C3%A8ge_\(department\)), Allier (where Sarkozy obtained 28% against nearly 26% for Royal), [Calvados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvados_\(department\)) (where Sarkozy finished first with 29% to 25% for Royal), [Finistère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finist%C3%A8re), Cher, [Côtes d'Armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4tes_d'Armor), Creuse, [Indre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre), Meurthe-et-Moselle, [Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord_\(French_department\)), Meuse, [Moselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moselle_\(department\)), Pas-de-Calais (6.2%), [Sarthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarthe), Nièvre, [Puy-de-Dôme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puy-de-D%C3%B4me), Somme, [Territoire-de-Belfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territoire-de-Belfort), Seine-Maritime, [Haute-Vienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Vienne) and the [overseas collectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_collectivity) of [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon) (6.5%, along with 5.1% for José Bové; only 6.7% for Le Pen).

The [overseas department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_territories_of_France) of [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) has been strongly opposed to Sarkozy; [Aimé Césaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aim%C3%A9_C%C3%A9saire), [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_\(France\)) of [Fort-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort-de-France) and leader of the *[Négritude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9gritude)* movement, refused to see him during his visit there in December 2005 (due to the UMP vote of the [2005 law on colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_law_on_colonialism)[^cite_note-93]). In the first round, it heavily supported Royal (48.5%, against 33.8% for Sarkozy and only 8.6% for Bayrou; the next highest total was received by Besancenot, with 2.5%). [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) also strongly supported Royal (46.2%, to 25% for Sarkozy and 13% for Bayrou). Meanwhile, Sarkozy won in [New Caledonia](21342.md) (with 49.7% of the vote) and in [Guadeloupe](12343.md) (with 42.6%, against 38.3% for Royal), as well as in [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) and the overseas territories of [French Polynesia](10737.md) and [Wallis and Futuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallis_and_Futuna).

### Demographic breakdown of the first-round vote

*Source: IPSOS, see [Sociologie du vote du 1er tour](https://web.archive.org/web/20070514215841/http://www.humanite.fr/journal/2007-05-05/2007-05-05-850796), *[L'Humanité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Humanit%C3%A9)*, 5 May 2007.*

30% of men voted for Sarkozy, 24% of them for Royal. 32% of women voted Sarkozy, 27% Royal. 29% of 18- to 24-year-olds voted Royal, against 26% for Sarkozy. Sarkozy also made a higher score for 35- to 44-year-olds and 60- to 69-year-olds, but a lesser score in the 45- to 59-year-old category.

36% of farmers voted Sarkozy against 8% for Royal. Workers voted at similar levels for both Sarkozy and Royal (21% for each), while public servants voted at 34% for Royal (18% for Sarkozy). 19% of unemployed people voted for Sarkozy, 32% of them for Royal. Students also voted in majority for Royal (32% against 21%), while pensioned elders voted at 41% for Sarkozy (23% for Royal).

### Second round

Supporters of Ségolène Royal awaiting the results, 8 pm, in front of the headquarters of the Socialist Party in Paris

The second round of the 2007 French presidential election started in [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon) on Saturday 5 May 2007 at 8 am local time (2007-05-05 10\:00 UTC) and ended in the large cities of [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) on Sunday, 6 May 2007 at 8 pm local time (2007-05-06 18\:00 UTC). [Turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) in the second round of the election was 84.0%, higher than in the first round. Nicolas Sarkozy got 53.06% of the votes and Ségolène Royal got 46.94%.

The [left-right division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_and_right_in_politics) was reinforced, according to many observers, by the election of Nicolas Sarkozy.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] 91% of the electors self-identifying as members of the centre-left voted for Royal, and 92% of those who self-identified as centre-right voted for Sarkozy.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] The center thus appears to have been polarized.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] The vast majority of the dissident left also voted for Royal, while the extreme right strongly supported Sarkozy.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] Although Jacques Chirac was successful among young electors [in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_French_presidential_election), mostly owing to his discourse on the "social rupture" (*fracture sociale*), Sarkozy's electorate was more traditionally right-wing and focused on older people. The only age group that gave him a majority was the over-50, who accounted for 52% of his voters, compared to only 37% of Royal's.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] Sarkozy obtained only 40% among those 18–24 years old, while Chirac had obtained 55% in the same category in 1995.[^cite_note-Clivage-84]

In social categories, Sarkozy won majorities among pensioned and inactive elders (58%), business leaders, shopkeepers and craftworkers (82% ), categories which are traditionally conservative.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] Sarkozy lost votes, compared to Chirac, among workers (59% for Royal) and employees (57% for Royal).[^cite_note-Clivage-84]

The general electoral geography did not significantly change from the first Chirac election. However, Sarkozy received a lesser score in [Corrèze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corr%C3%A8ze), Chirac's home department, and bettered Chirac's score in the North-East, where Le Pen had obtained some of his better scores in 2002.[^cite_note-Clivage-84] Overall, the increase in votes for Sarkozy between the two rounds occurred mostly in departments where the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_\(France\))'s presence is strong.[^cite_note-Clivage-84]

Spoilt votes represented 4.2% of the electors (as much as in 2002 and 1995).[^cite_note-Clivage-84]

### Abstention and spoilt votes

[Abstention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstention) was exceptionally low, as well as [protest votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_vote). [Blank vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_vote) (going to vote, but deliberately cancelling one's ballot, by any means possible – tearing it in two, writing [Tintin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tintin_\(character\)) on it, or anything absurd as such) is not included in official counts – i.e. it is considered a [spoilt vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoilt_vote), counted as equivalent to abstention. A very small party, the *[Parti Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_Blanc?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (White Party, for "white vote", i.e. blank vote) called for the official count of white votes by the state (as in [None of the above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/None_of_the_above) systems). It organised a march in Paris on Wednesday 18 March 2007 in which only thirty people participated.[^cite_note-94]

## Aftermath

### Riots



Pro-Ségolène Royal youth chanted against Nicolas Sarkozy



A gathering of opponents to Sarkozy on [Place de la Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_de_la_Bastille) in Paris, on 6 May evening, quickly ended in confrontations between the far-left and the riot control forces



Bastille tear-gassed

Thousands of youths took to the streets Sunday night following the final presidential election results. While many simply expressed their discontent at the election of Nicolas Sarkozy, others chose to engage in violent action. Riots erupted in several urban centers including the capital Paris where some of the most intense clashes were reported in the [Place de la Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_de_la_Bastille).[^cite_note-95] A gathering of opponents to Sarkozy there quickly ended in confrontations between the youth and the riot control forces, who [tear gassed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_control_agent) the whole place.

732 cars were torched according to estimates of the [DGPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DGPN) (direction of the police) and government buildings and property came under attack. Police clashed with protesters who were described by French media as members of the [ultra-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-left) and of the [autonome movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism) or youth from the suburbs.[^cite_note-UG-96] During the fighting dozens of officers were injured and 592 alleged rioters were arrested.[^cite_note-Libe592-97] 70 people were arrested in the North department and 79 in Paris.[^cite_note-98] Overall the situation remained calm.

Some clashes continued on the night of Monday to Tuesday, with 365 torched cars and 160 alleged rioters [detained by the police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_detention).[^cite_note-UG-96] Ten people were in court already by Monday. Two of them were given firm prison sentences of six and three months respectively, and two others to 120 hours of [TIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_service) (General Interest Labour, an alternative sentence to prison).[^cite_note-UG-96] Another one has been given a two-month firm prison sentence and two others TIG hours.[^cite_note-99] Some of the people judged in [Lyon](8638634.md) have denied any involvement in the riots (two of them received 120 hours of TIG and a 200 euros fine).[^cite_note-100]

300 to 400 people demonstrated on the [Boulevard Saint-Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_Saint-Michel) on Wednesday 9 May, in opposition to a demonstration of [white supremacists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacist). By 9 pm that night 118 of them had been arrested.[^cite_note-101] A 31-year-old engineer took legal action following his release from custody claiming he had been a victim of [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality). He claimed that he had not taken part to the demonstrations, but had been arrested nonetheless.[^cite_note-102]

### Sarkozy's detention March 2018

On 20 March 2018, Sarkozy was arrested by the French Police because of the suspect having received 50 million euros for his presidential campaign from [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md).[^cite_note-103]
