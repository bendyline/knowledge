**Macclesfield Football Club** is an [association football](10568.md) club based in [Macclesfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macclesfield), [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire), England.[^cite_note-MaccWeb-2] The club currently competes in the [National League North](645482.md), the sixth tier of the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), and plays its home games at the [Moss Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_Rose) (known as the Leasing.com Stadium for sponsorship reasons).

Macclesfield is a [phoenix club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_club_\(sports\)) of the former [Macclesfield Town F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macclesfield_Town_F.C.) founded in 1874, which was [wound-up](565034.md) after a [High Court](26569402.md) ruling on 16 September 2020.[^cite_note-BBC-16Sep2020-3] The club joined the [North West Counties Premier Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Counties_Football_League), the fifth step of the [National League system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_System) ahead of the 2021–22 season.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Macclesfield recorded three promotions in the four subsequent seasons, winning the North West Counties Premier Division in 2022,[^cite_note-\:1-5] the [NPL Division One West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Premier_League) in 2023,[^cite_note-\:2-6] and the [NPL Premier Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Premier_League) in 2025.[^cite_note-\:3-7] The team also reached the semi-final of the [FA Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Trophy) in 2024.[^cite_note-\:4-8] In 2026, they reached the fourth round of the [ FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025-26_FA_Cup) by becoming the first sixth-tier team to beat a top-tier team ([Premier League](11250.md) side [Crystal Palace](385313.md), the reigning FA Cup holders).[^cite_note-BBC-10Jan2026-9]

## History

Macclesfield F.C. is a successor to [Macclesfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macclesfield_Town_F.C.).[^cite_note-10] Founded in 1874, Macclesfield Town was liquidated and expelled from the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(English_football\)) in 2020 because of debts of over £500,000.[^cite_note-11] Its assets were put up for sale, including the [Moss Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_Rose) stadium on the [Rightmove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rightmove) property website.[^cite_note-12] In October 2020, the stadium and all available assets were purchased by Robert Smethurst, the owner of [Stockport Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport_Town_F.C.), with the aim of returning professional football to Macclesfield by creating the phoenix club as Macclesfield F.C.[^cite_note-13] Smethurst appointed the Welsh former professional footballer [Robbie Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Savage) as a member of the board for the new phoenix club with [Danny Whitaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Whitaker) as manager.[^cite_note-14] He also invested in upgrading the stadium to improve the commercial viability of the new club.[^cite_note-15]

In May 2021, it was announced that Macclesfield would be placed in the [NWCFL Premier Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Counties_Football_League), the fifth step of the [National League system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_System) (ninth tier in the [English football pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Football_League_System)), for their first season.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Savage announced that the club would be joining with his foundation to offer free tickets to foundation participants to encourage youth attendance at the club's matches.[^cite_note-16] Macclesfield entered the [FA Vase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Vase) for the first time in [2021–22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_FA_Vase).[^cite_note-17]

### 2021–22 season

On 31 July 2021 Macclesfield played their first competitive match, a 1–0 win at home against [Burscough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burscough_F.C.) in the NWCFL Premier Division played in front of a restricted capacity sell-out crowd of 2,018; Leon Arnasalam scored the only goal.[^cite_note-18] The club's first away match was on 3 August, a 4–4 draw at [Winsford United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winsford_United), also in the NWCFL Premier Division, with Tom Clare scoring all four Macclesfield goals.[^cite_note-19] On 7 August Macclesfield played their first [FA Cup](11237.md) match, beating Burscough 4–0 in the Extra Preliminary Round, but were then knocked out of in the Preliminary Round losing 6–4 at Squires Gate.[^cite_note-20]

On 30 August Macclesfield defeated local rivals [Congleton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congleton_Town_F.C.) 1–0 in the NWCFL Premier Division in front of a record crowd of 3,502, [James Berry-McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Berry_\(footballer\)) scoring the only goal.[^cite_note-21] The match was suspended for several minutes late in the second half due to crowd trouble.[^cite_note-22] It was the first time the neighbouring towns had met in a league match since the 1964–65 season.[^cite_note-23]

On 11 September, Macclesfield played in the [FA Vase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Vase) for the first time, losing 3–2 at [Northern Counties East League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Counties_East_Football_League) Premier Division side [Winterton Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterton_Rangers_F.C.) in the first qualifying round.

Macclesfield F.C. was featured in a [BBC Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Sport) documentary film, *Robbie Savage: Making Macclesfield FC*, broadcast on 13 November 2021, which followed the launch of the new club following the collapse of Macclesfield Town.[^cite_note-Golby-13Nov2021-24]

Despite a relatively poor run of form at the start of the new year (L2, D1, W2 in January), a run of ten consecutive wins followed, in which 37 goals were scored and only four conceded.[^cite_note-25] On 12 March 2022, Macclesfield secured the [North West Counties Premier Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Counties_Football_League) title and promotion to the [NPL Division One West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Premier_League) after a 4–0 win against [Ashton Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashton_Athletic_F.C.), becoming the first club in England's top nine tiers in 2022 to guarantee promotion.[^cite_note-\:1-5] Macclesfield finished their league campaign on 2 April, 15 points clear of second-placed [Skelmersdale United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skelmersdale_United_F.C.).[^cite_note-26]

### 2022–23 season

In October 2022, with the club three points clear at the top of the NPL West Division, [Danny Whitaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Whitaker) was replaced as Macclesfield manager by David McNabb.[^cite_note-NPL-31Oct2022-27] McNabb stepped down in December 2022, with midfielder [Neil Danns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Danns) being appointed interim manager.[^cite_note-Barnes-13Dec2022-28] A 3–1 win against [Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafford_F.C.) on 1 April 2023 saw the club crowned champions and achieve back-to-back promotions.[^cite_note-\:2-6]

### 2023–24 season

During the summer of 2023, manager Neil Danns left to take up an assistant role at [Tranmere Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranmere_Rovers_F.C.), and his assistant [Mark Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Duffy_\(footballer\)) was named as the new head coach.[^cite_note-29] Record goal-scorer [James Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Berry_\(footballer\)) departed for a club record fee to join [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(English_football\)) side [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield_F.C.).[^cite_note-30] Luke Duffy joined for a record fee from [Warrington Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrington_Town_F.C.),[^cite_note-31] and [John Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rooney_\(footballer\)), [Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney)'s younger brother, returned to the Silkmen having started his career with 41 league appearances for Macclesfield Town.[^cite_note-32]

Macclesfield entered the [FA Cup](11237.md) in the [first qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_FA_Cup_qualifying_rounds). Victories over [Whickham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whickham_F.C.), [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton_F.C.), and [Warrington Rylands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrington_Rylands_1906_F.C.) secured a place in the fourth qualifying round against [Alfreton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfreton_Town_F.C.).[^cite_note-33] After four defeats in five games, including a 3–1 FA Cup defeat at Alfreton,[^cite_note-ATFC-15Oct2023-34] Duffy was sacked as head coach on 27 October 2023,[^cite_note-Hall-26Oct2023-35] and was replaced by [Alex Bruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bruce_\(footballer,_born_1984\)).[^cite_note-BBC-30Oct2023-36] The Silkmen entered the [FA Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_FA_Trophy) in the third qualifying round; in the fourth round proper they defeated [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(English_football\)) opposition [Dorking Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorking_Wanderers_F.C.) 5–0.[^cite_note-\:4-8] Macclesfield were the only side outside of the National League to reach the semi-finals, but were knocked out of the competition in a 2–1 defeat at [Gateshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateshead_F.C.) on 7 April.[^cite_note-37]

On 21 February 2024, Bruce stepped down as Macclesfield manager to take up a coaching role at [Salford City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salford_City_F.C.),[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-BBC-21Feb2024-39] and was replaced by Michael Clegg, previously manager at Warrington Rylands.[^cite_note-WG-22Feb2024-40] Under Clegg, Macclesfield finished second, qualifying for the NPL play-offs, but lost the play-off final 2–1 to [Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_F.C.).[^cite_note-Gordon-06May2024-41]

### 2024–25 season

Ahead of the 2024–25 season prolific forward [Kane Drummond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Drummond) departed to [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield_F.C.)[^cite_note-42] and [Luke Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Murphy_\(English_footballer\)) was released.[^cite_note-Retained-43] On 17 June 2024, Clegg left his role as manager by mutual consent,[^cite_note-44] and Robbie Savage was appointed head coach on the same day.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Notable summer signings included winger [Justin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Johnson_\(footballer\)),[^cite_note-47] midfielder [Danny Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Whitehead), who had four spells at Macclesfield Town,[^cite_note-48] striker [Danny Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Elliott_\(footballer\)) following his departure from [National League North](645482.md) side [Scunthorpe United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scunthorpe_United_F.C.),[^cite_note-49] and goalkeeper Max Dearnley from [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton_F.C.).[^cite_note-50]

The team began the season with a 1–0 win away at [Worksop Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop_Town_F.C.) and remained undefeated until a 2–1 loss away to [Mickleover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickleover_F.C.) on 28 November.[^cite_note-\:5-51] 35 league goals from golden boot winner Danny Elliott and 23 clean sheets from Max Dearnley, helped the team to 109 points - a division and club record.[^cite_note-\:5-51] Macclesfield's promotion to the [National League North](645482.md) was confirmed with six matches still to play after defeating [Bamber Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamber_Bridge_F.C.) 2–1 on 22 March 2025, becoming [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Northern_Premier_League#Premier_Division) [Northern Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Premier_League) Premier Division champions, and ending two seasons in the seventh tier.[^cite_note-\:3-7][^cite_note-52]

### 2025–26 season

Savage signed a new two-year deal to stay as Macclesfield manager, but two weeks later, on 1 July 2025, opted to become [Forest Green Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Green_Rovers_F.C.)' new manager and was replaced as Silkmen manager by [John Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rooney_\(footballer\)).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] On 16 December 2025, forward Ethan McLeod died in a car accident while returning from a game against [Bedford Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Town).[^cite_note-55] Following the accident, the club retired the number 20 shirt as a tribute to the former player.[^cite_note-mcleod_shirt_retirement-56]

The club made history on 10 January 2026 by [beating FA Cup holders Crystal Palace 2–1](82078681.md) in the third round to become the first non-league team to defeat the defending champions since 1909 (when [Crystal Palace](385313.md) themselves, playing in the [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League), beat the holders [Wolverhampton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.))[^cite_note-57] and the first sixth-tier team to defeat a first-tier team in the history of the FA Cup.[^cite_note-58] The result was widely described at the time as the biggest shock in the competition's history.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

In the [National League North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_National_League), Macclesfield finished 4th to reach the play-offs, beating [Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_F.C.) 2–1 in their quarter-final,[^cite_note-63] but subsequently lost 3–1 to [Kidderminster Harriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidderminster_Harriers_F.C.) at the semi-final stage.[^cite_note-64] In June 2026, Macclesfield temporarily suspended co-owner Robert Smethurst following his May 2026 arrest on suspicion of controlling and coercive behaviour.[^cite_note-65] On 5 August 2026, Smethurst resigned as a director but remained a shareholder.[^cite_note-66]

## Stadium

Macclesfield play their home games in the south of the town at the Leasing.com Stadium, previously home to Macclesfield Town from 1891 until 2020. The stadium's current capacity is 5,350, of which 2,095 is seated.[^cite_note-67] The club's joint highest home attendance was 5,348 on 10 January 2026 for the FA Cup third round tie against Crystal Palace and 16 February 2026 for the FA Cup fourth round tie against Brentford.

The *ID Unlimited Main Stand* (traditionally known as the London Road or Main Stand) runs along one side of the pitch and consists of a seated grandstand with open air terracing to either side, and the opposite side is the seated *Besseges Stand*. The club's most vociferous supporters congregate in the *WRS Star Lane End*, which is a mixture of terracing and seating. Visiting supporters are housed in the open air *Silkmen Terrace North* (named after a [public house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_house) which formerly adjoined the terrace) and part of the *Besseges Stand*.
