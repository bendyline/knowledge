**Sanok**,[^cite_note-2] in full the **Royal Free City of Sanok**,[^cite_note-3] is a town in the [Subcarpathian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcarpathian_Voivodeship) of southeastern [Poland](22936.md)[^cite_note-TERYT-4] with 38,397 inhabitants, as of June 2016.[^cite_note-polskawliczbach.pl-1] Located on the [San River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River) and around 52 km (32 mi) south of [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl), Sanok lies directly by the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains).

The town's history goes back over a thousand years to when it was part of a [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) trade route.[^cite_note-5] The [Museum of Folk Architecture](12812780.md) as well as the refurbished [Sanok Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Castle) and Old Town are popular points of interest.[^cite_note-auto-6] The region also features a 70 km (43 mi) trail for [hikers](147313.md) and cyclists.[^cite_note-auto-6]

## Geography

The city of Sanok is the capital of [Sanok County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_County) in the [Subcarpathian Voivodeship](345194.md) in Poland. Previously, it was in the [Krosno Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno_Voivodeship) (1975–1998) and in the [Ruthenian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_Voivodeship) (1340–1772), which was part of the [Cherven Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherven_Cities)/[Red Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia) region, and in wider sense, of the [Lesser Poland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Province,_Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) (not of [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland) proper). Historically, it was part of the *[Land of Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Land)*.

This historic city is situated on the [San River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River) at the foot of [Castle Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Castle) in the [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland) (*Małopolska*) region. It lies in a wooded, hilly area near the national road number 28, which runs along southern Poland, from [Ustrzyki Dolne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustrzyki_Dolne) to [Wadowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadowice) (340 km or 211 mi away). It is located in the heartland of the [Pogórze Bukowskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pog%C3%B3rze_Bukowskie) part of [Doły (Pits)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C5%82y_Jasielsko_Sanockie), and its average elevation is 300 m (984 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level), although there are some hills located within the confines of the city.

Sanok is located on the bank of the river San. The area surrounding mountain range stretches between the [Wisłok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wis%C5%82ok), [Osława](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Os%C5%82awa) and [San Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River) in the [Salt Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82onne_Mountains_Landscape_Park) ([Central Beskidian Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Beskidian_Piedmont)), in the inland with [temperateness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) climate. The hills of the [Bieszczady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieszczady_Mountains) [mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) are typical for this countryside. Sanok County is bordered by [Krosno County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krosno_County) to the west, [Brzozów County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brzoz%C3%B3w_County) to the north, [Przemyśl County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl_County) to the north-east and [Lesko County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesko_County) to the east. It also borders [Slovakia](26830.md) to the south. Before [World War II](32927.md), the Oslawa and San Rivers line was designated the *wild frontier* between Poles and [Lemkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemko).

The city is a member of [Carpathian Euroregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Euroregion), which is designed to bring together the people who inhabit the region of the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) and to facilitate their cooperation in the fields of science, culture, education, trade, tourism and economy.

## History

### Middle Ages



An 1847 drawing of [Sanok Royal Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Castle) overlooking the [San River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River)

The earliest mention of the region where Sanok is located comes from [Nestor the Chronicler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestor_the_Chronicler), who described how in 981, the surrounding areas, along with several [Cherven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherven_Cities) [gords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grad_\(Slavic_settlement\)), then inhabited by the [West Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs) tribe of [Lendians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lendians), was made a part of [Red Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia), when [Vladimir I of Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_I_of_Kiev) invaded the land and took it over from the [Duchy of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Poland). In 1018, the settlement along with the surrounding region returned to Poland, and in 1031, was again recaptured by Rus'. Sanok and the region then again returned to Poland in 1069, only to be made part of Rus' in 1085. In 1339, the town was given the [Magdeburg law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg_rights) by [Boleslaw-Yuri II of Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boleslaw-Yuri_II_of_Galicia).[^cite_note-7]

Sanok was mentioned in the Ruthenian [Hypatian Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypatian_Codex) chronicle, where one can read that in the year 1150: *The Hungarian King [Géza II of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9za_II_of_Hungary) crossed the mountains and seized the stronghold of Sanok with its governor as well as many villages in the [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl) area*. The same chronicle refers to Sanok twice more, stating that in 1205 it was the meeting place of a Ruthenian princess Anna and a Hungarian king, and that in 1231 a Ruthenian prince made an expedition to "Sanok - *Hungarian Gate*".

During the [Galicia–Volhynia Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia_Wars), Sanok was seized by King [Casimir III of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_III_the_Great), who reconfirmed its municipal status on 25 April 1366, and made it a [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Poland) of the Polish Crown. At that time Sanok became the centre of a new administrative district called [Sanok Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Land), a part of the [Ruthenian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_Voivodeship). Several courts of justice operated in the town, including the municipal and rural courts of lower instance and also the higher instance court for the entire Sanok Land, based on the [German town law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_town_law).[^cite_note-8] Germans settled in the territory of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_\(1385%E2%80%931569\)) (territory of present-day [Subcarpathian Voivodeship](345194.md)) from the 14th to 16th centuries (*see _[Ostsiedlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_German_settlement_in_Central_and_Eastern_Europe)_*), mostly after the region returned to Poland in 1340, when [Casimir III of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_III_of_Poland) took the [Czerwień towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherven_Cities).

[Marcin Bielski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcin_Bielski) states that [Bolesław I the Brave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_I_the_Brave) had settled some Germans in the region to defend the borders against Hungary and Kievan Rus', who later turned to farming. [Maciej Stryjkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maciej_Stryjkowski) mentions German peasants near [Przeworsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przeworsk), [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl), Sanok, and [Jarosław](386732.md), describing them as good farmers. The region was also traditionally inhabited by subgroups of the [Rusyn people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyn_people): [Lemkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemko) and [Boykos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boykos).

### Modern period



Main Market Square in Sanok

As early at the 17th century, an important [trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route) went across Sanok connecting the interior of Hungary with Poland through the [Łupków Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81upk%C3%B3w_Pass). As a result of the [First Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Partition_of_Poland) (Treaty of St-Petersburg dated 5 July 1772), Sanok was annexed by the Austrian [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy).[^cite_note-9] At that time, Sanok and the surrounding region (including west and east of Subcarpathian Voivodship) became known as the [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Central_Europe\)) province, within the Habsburg [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria).

In the mid-18th century, 47.7% of the town's population was Roman Catholic (Polish), 36.4% Jewish, and 14.7% Greek Catholic (Ruthenian).[^cite_note-10] The [Galician peasant revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_slaughter) took place in the region during the [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848).



Courtyard of the Sanok Castle

The course of the river [Dunajec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunajec) and that of the [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_\(river\)), both in West Galicia, marked the two successive stages in the breakthrough battle which initiated the Austro-German offensive of 1915 on the eastern front. An attempt to hold the line of the Wisłok river and the [Łupków Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81upk%C3%B3w_Pass) failed before renewed Austro-German attacks on 8 May 1915. [Wisłok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wis%C5%82ok) Valley was one of the strategically important Carpathian rivers bitterly contested in battles on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_I\)) of World War I during the winter of 1914–1915.[^cite_note-11]

During [World War I](4764461.md), the Russian army occupied the town from May until July, 1915 and significantly damaged the town. The town was subsequently occupied by troops of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire).

In 1918 Poland regained independence and control of the town and within the interwar [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) it was the seat of the Sanok County in the [Lwów Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w_Voivodeship). Sanok was known as a centre of [Ukrainian](145144.md) movement in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)), and of [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage) of the [Lemkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemkos) and other [Rusyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyns).

### World War II



Mass grave of Poles massacred by the Germans at the Gruszka mountain

The Jewish population of Sanok may have comprised nearly 30% of the total population in the early 20th century. During the joint German–Soviet [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), which started [World War II](32927.md), in September 1939, it was invaded by Germany, and the *[Einsatzgruppe I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzgruppen)* entered the town on September 25, 1939, to commit various [atrocities against the populace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation).[^cite_note-12] In 1939–1940, the Germans imprisoned many Poles in the local prison, especially those who tried to escape occupation to take refuge in [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-mw-13] The Germans then massacred 112 Poles at the Gruszka mountain near [Tarnawa Dolna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnawa_Dolna,_Podkarpackie_Voivodeship).[^cite_note-mw-13] The victims are buried at the Central Cemetery in Sanok. At the beginning of the [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) during [World War II](32927.md), the Jewish population was around 5,000. During the occupation, most of the Jews were either executed or killed in [Nazi death camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_death_camps) or [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps) during [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust). Some of the actions against the Jews were assisted by local auxiliaries and hundreds of the deaths occurred in Sanok itself,[^cite_note-14] while the [Polish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) established the secret [Polish Council to Aid Jews "Żegota"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBegota), which operated in the town.[^cite_note-15] Buildings that had been owned by Jews were taken over by the local population. The local Jewish cemetery still exists. Several hundred Jews are thought to have survived, most of whom fled to the Soviet Union at the beginning of the war. Some of the Jews emigrated to [Canada](5042916.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) in the early 1900s with Sanoker Burial Societies spreading throughout New York and other regions where they settled.

In 1942, Sanok was the location of the Stalag 327 [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II), following its relocation from [Jarosław](386732.md) and before its further relocation to [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl).[^cite_note-ushm-16] Afterwards the present-day district of Olchowce was the location of a subcamp of Stalag 327, in which some 7,000-10,000 POWs died.[^cite_note-ushm-16]

In 1943 the foundation of the [Waffen-SS Division Galizien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Waffen_Grenadier_Division_of_the_SS_Galicia_\(1st_Ukrainian\)) took place among the Ukrainian minority in Sanok, with many locals volunteering in the ethnic Ukrainian [Waffen-SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waffen-SS). Because of material support and assistance provided by the Ukrainian minority to the Ukrainian Insurgent Army, which was waging a battle for Ukrainian [separatism](286527.md) against the Polish state, new Soviet-installed [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Poland) authorities deported the Ukrainian (and Lemko) population of Sanok and its region to the [Recovered Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovered_Territories) attached to Poland after World War II during [Operation Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vistula) (1946–1947). Some of the Lemkos expelled returned to Sanok in 1957-58 and others after 1989.

Sanok contains an [open-air museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzeum_Budownictwa_Ludowego_w_Sanoku) called a [skansen](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/skansen#Polish) in the Biała Góra district, where examples of architecture from all of the region's main [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) have been moved and carefully reassembled in a skansen evoking everyday rural life in the 19th century.

## Archaeology



[Lusatian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusatian_culture) pots on display at the Sanok's archaeology museum

Settled in [prehistoric times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory), the south-eastern Poland region that is now [Subcarpathia](345194.md) was overrun in pre-Roman times by various tribes, including the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) ([Anarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarti)), [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) and [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) ([Przeworsk culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przeworsk_culture) and [Púchov culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%BAchov_culture)). In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), the area was inhabited by the [Polish tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_tribes) of [Lendians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lendians), and the area was also invaded by [Hungarians](17106358.md), before it eventually became part of the emerging Polish state in the 10th century.

The region subsequently became part of the [Great Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Moravia) state. Upon the invasion of the [Hungarian tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) into the heart of the Great Moravian Empire around 899, the [Lendians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lendians) of the area declared their allegiance to [Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary). The region then became a site of contention between Poland, [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') and Hungary starting in at least the 9th century.



A 12th century earring found in Sanok

The first traces of settlement in the area of modern Sanok date back to at least the 9th century. The following century a Slavic fortified town (*[gord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grad_\(Slavic_settlement\))*) was created there and initially served as a center of pagan worship. The etymology of the name is unclear, though most scholars derive it from the Celtic river-name *[San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River)*.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Certain [archaeological excavations](18951655.md) performed on the castle hill and on *Fajka hill* near Sanok-[Trepcza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trepcza), not only confirm the written resources, but date the Sanok stronghold origin to as early as the 9th century. On Fajka hill, where probably the first settlement of Sanok was situated, some remains of an ancient sanctuary and a cemetery were found, as well as numerous decorations and encolpions in Kievan type. Also found were two [seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_\(emblem\)) of the Great Kievan Prince [Rurik Rostislavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurik_Rostislavich) from the second half of the 12th century.

## Economy



Sanok bus station

Sanok has a strong industry base - home to [Stomil Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Rubber_Company)[^cite_note-20] (established in 1932) and Pass Gummiwerke plants, producers of various rubber and metal-rubber seals, strings and laggings for automotive sector, construction industries and electrical household goods sector, [PGNiG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_G%C3%B3rnictwo_Naftowe_i_Gazownictwo)[^cite_note-21] and Sanok Bus [Car Factory](163778.md) "[Autosan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosan)"[^cite_note-22] (established in 1832), a producer of high capacity buses, cabins for the [Polish Army](40153898.md) and bodies for rail-vehicles. Stomil is next to the main train station in Sanok and Autosan is a 10-minute walk from the station, while the town centre is a 15-minute walk in the other direction.

## Culture and education



Library and the [Grzegorz of Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grzegorz_of_Sanok) Monument

The town has several public schools and a branch of the Polish High School of Technology. The town also has a football club called [Stal Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stal_Sanok) and some other [sport clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club) (including volleyball, swimming, handball, [ice hockey](14790.md)). The [Sanok Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok_Castle) near the centre of the old town houses a museum displaying over 300 fine icons. The [Museum of Folk Architecture](12812780.md) is one of the biggest [open-air museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-air_museum) in [Poland](22936.md) and show cases 19th and early 20th century life in this area of Poland.

## Sport

The city has two professional sports teams. The local [ice hockey](14790.md) team is [STS Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Sanok),[^cite_note-23] which has won the [Polska Hokej Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polska_Liga_Hokejowa) league title twice, in the [2011/2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Polska_Liga_Hokejowa_season) and [2013/2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Polska_Liga_Hokejowa_season) season. They won the [Polish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Cup_\(ice_hockey\)) twice, in 2010-11 and 2011–12. The local football club is [Stal Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stal_Sanok), which competes in the lower leagues.



[STS Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Sanok) celebrating the Polish championship in 2014

There many sports facilities in Sanok and the main complex of those facilities is The Civic Sports and Recreation Centre, situated near the [San River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_River). The Centre includes: the artificial speed skating oval Tor Błonie, a complex of indoor and outdoor swimming pools, a hotel, a tourist hostel, a camp-site, a sports stadium with technical facilities, etc. There is also another artificial ice rink in the centre of the town, designed for ice hockey and managed by the ice hockey club [STS Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS_Sanok). There are two more sports facilities at Stróżowska street: a stadium of sports club [Stal Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stal_Sanok), and a gymnasium of the Technical Schools Complex.

In winter, a ski-lift operates in the nearby [Karlików](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlik%C3%B3w).

In the mid-18th century, Roman Catholics constituted 48.7% of the population, people of Jewish faith 36.5%, and 14.7% of the inhabitants belonged to the Greek Catholic Church.[^cite_note-25]

In 1900, the town had 6123 inhabitants, 57% [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), 30% [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), and the remainder of various [Rusyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyns) ethnicities ([Boyko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko), [Lemkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemkos), etc.), and others. The town's large population of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) were almost all murdered during [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust).
