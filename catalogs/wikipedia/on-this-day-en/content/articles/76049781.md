# List of exomoon candidates

As of 2026, there have been no positive confirmations of [satellites of extra-solar planets](2505466.md) (exomoons); however, some evidence in favour of their existence has been produced.

## Timeline

This is a [dynamic list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia\:WikiProject_Lists#Dynamic_lists) and may never be able to satisfy particular standards for completeness. You can help by [editing the page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special\:EditPage/List_of_exomoon_candidates) to add missing items, with references to [reliable sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia\:Reliable_sources).

- 2012 — It has been surmised that [J1407b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J1407b), a possibly [planetary-mass object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary-mass_object) that eclipsed the star [V1400 Centauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V1400_Centauri) (aka. J1407) in 2007, may have a few moons based on gaps observed in its [circumstellar disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumstellar_disk) or [ring system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_system).

  [^cite_note-1]

  &#x20;Later studies have since found that J1407b is most likely a free-floating [sub-brown dwarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-brown_dwarf) or [rogue planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_planet), possibly less than 6 [Jupiter masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_masses).

  [^cite_note-Kenworthy2020-2]

- 2012 — The confirmed [hot Jupiter](961349.md) planet [WASP-12b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WASP-12b) may also possess a moon.

  [^cite_note-3]

- December 2013, April 2014 — A candidate exomoon of a [free-floating planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-floating_planet) [MOA-2011-BLG-262L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOA-2011-BLG-262L), was announced, but due to degeneracies in the modelling of the microlensing event, the observations can also be explained as a Neptune-mass planet orbiting a low-mass red dwarf, a scenario the authors consider to be more likely.

  [^cite_note-AXV-2014-4]

  [^cite_note-NASA-20140410-5]

  [^cite_note-New_Scientist-6]

  &#x20;In 2024 the latter scenario was confirmed.

  [^cite_note-Terry2024-7]

- October 2018 — researchers using the [Hubble Space Telescope](40203.md) published observations of the candidate exomoon [Kepler-1625b I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-1625b_I), which suggest that the host planet is likely several [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter) masses, while the exomoon may have a mass and radius similar to [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune). The study concluded that the exomoon hypothesis is the simplest and best explanation for the available observations, though warned that it is difficult to assign a precise probability to its existence and nature.

  [^cite_note-Teachey2017-8]

  [^cite_note-Teachey2018-9]

  - April 2019 — reanalysis concluded that the data was fit better by a planet-only model. According to this study, the discrepancy was an artifact of the data reduction, and [Kepler-1625b I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-1625b_I) likely does not exist.

    [^cite_note-Kreidberg-10]

- September 2019 – A hypothesis involving potential transits of large exomoons being detached from their planets (see [ploonet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ploonet)) was posited to explain the light flux-variations of the [Tabby's Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabby's_Star), which were identified from the data collected by [Kepler space telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler_space_telescope).

  [^cite_note-ploonets-11]

- August 2020 — A paper by Chris Fox and Paul Wiegert examined the Kepler dataset for indications of exomoons solely from transit timing variations. Eight candidate signals were found that were consistent with an exomoon, however the signals could also be explained by the presence of another planet. Fox and Wiegert's conclusion was more and higher quality transit timing data would be required to establish whether these are truly moons or not.

  [^cite_note-FoxWiegert2020-12]

  &#x20;[David Kipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kipping) re-derived the timings of six of the eight targets (based on a pre-peer review version) and evaluated the TTV evidence as uncompelling. The same study finds that [Kepler-1625b I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-1625b_I) remains an exomoon candidate.

  [^cite_note-Kipping2020-13]

- August 2021 — astronomers reported an habitable-zone 1.7 [R<sub>🜨</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_radius) exomoon candidate transiting one of the components in the planetary-mass binary [2MASS J1119-1137AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2MASS_J11193254%E2%80%931137466_AB).

  [^cite_note-\:0-14]

- January 2022 — an exomoon candidate was reported around the planet [Kepler-1708b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-1708b), and because it is orbiting a planet at approximately 1.6 AU from a star that is slightly more luminous than the Sun, it too could be within the habitable zone.

  [^cite_note-Kipping2022-15]

  &#x20;However, this candidate is based on limited observations (only two transits) and some consider the data to be non-convincing.

  [^cite_note-16]

- November 2022 — another exomoon candidate was reported around the planet [Kepler-1513b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-1513b) (KOI-3678.01). Unlike the previous giant exomoon candidates of Kepler-1625 and Kepler-1708, this exomoon would be terrestrial-mass, ranging from 0.76 [Lunar masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_mass) to 0.34 [Earth masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_mass) depending on the planet's mass and moon's orbital period.

  [^cite_note-Kipping2023-17]

  - October 2023 — a follow-up study by the same team found that the observed TTVs are caused by a second planet in the system, and not by a moon.

    [^cite_note-Yahalomi2023-18]

- December 2023 — The exomoon candidate around Kepler-1625b was again challenged, along with the Kepler-1708b candidate. This study argues that the statistical significance of these exomoon candidates is lower than previously claimed (with false positive probabilities of 10.9% and 1.6%, respectively) and that true giant exomoons would have stronger evidence. Evidence for exomoon transits may be caused by stellar activity in the Kepler light curves.

  [^cite_note-Heller2023-19]

  &#x20;Kipping's team published a response arguing that these exomoon candidates remain possible.

  [^cite_note-Kipping2024-20]

- October 2024 — New measurements with the [Very Large Telescope](151648.md) of the star [WASP-49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WASP-49) gave more evidences favoring the presence of a possible volcanically active-moon around the hot Jupiter WASP-49b.

  [^cite_note-oza2024-21]

  - A similar study in 2025 with [JWST](434221.md) suggested that the sulfur dioxide detection in the atmosphere of [WASP-39b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WASP-39b) is due to the presence of a volcanically active-exomoon.&#x20;

    [^cite_note-Oza#2025-22]

- 2025 — While investigating HD 206893 B with the [GRAVITY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRAVITY_\(Very_Large_Telescope\)) instrument at the [Very Large Telescope](151648.md) (VLT), signs of a potential very massive [exomoon](2505466.md) was detected around HD 206893 B. If confirmed, the exomoon's mass should be 40% of [Jupiter's mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_mass).

  [^cite_note-\:1-23]

  [^cite_note-\:2-24]

- July 2026 — The [European Southern Observatory](175184.md)'s [Very Large Telescope](151648.md) reported evidence of a possible [exomoon](2505466.md)-like object orbiting the [brown dwarf](44401.md) [CD-35 2722 B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-35_2722) in the [CD-35 2722](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-35_2722) system, in a study published in the *[Nature](43427.md)* journal.

  [^cite_note-25]

  [^cite_note-Kevin2026-27]
