# South Lawn



[Marine One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_One) descending to its landing on the South Lawn in July 1987



[State Arrival Ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visits_to_the_United_States) for President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) of the Philippines in May 2003

The&#x20;**&#x20;South Lawn** at the [White House](33057.md) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), is directly south of the house and is bordered on the east by East Executive Drive and the [Treasury Building](53667.md), on the west by West Executive Drive and the [Old Executive Office Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower_Executive_Office_Building), and along its curved southern perimeter by South Executive Drive and a large circular public lawn called [The Ellipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ellipse).

Since the address of the White House is 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, and the [North Lawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Lawn) faces Pennsylvania Avenue, the South Lawn is sometimes described as the back lawn of the White House.

## Description and use



The Easter [Egg Roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_rolling) on the South Lawn in April 2017

The South Lawn presents a long north–south vista from the White House. Open to the public until [World War II](32927.md), it is now a closed part of the White House grounds that provides a setting for official events like the [State Arrival Ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Arrival_Ceremony) as well as informal gatherings including the annual White House [Egg Rolling Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_rolling) and staff barbecues. [Marine One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_One), the presidential helicopter, departs from and lands on the South Lawn.[^cite_note-WP_2026-06-30-1]

## History and design



A [stereograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoscopy) showing the earliest fountain on the South Lawn, c. 1868



[Sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep) graze on the South Lawn during the [administration of Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Woodrow_Wilson), 1918

When the White House was first occupied in 1800 the site of the South Lawn was an open meadow gradually descending to a large marsh, the Tiber Creek, and Potomac River beyond.[^cite_note-2] [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson) completed grading of the South Lawn, building up mounds on either side of a central lawn. Jefferson, working with architect [Benjamin Henry Latrobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Henry_Latrobe) located a [triumphal arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumphal_arch) as a main entry point to the grounds, just southeast of the White House.[^cite_note-3] [Pierre-Charles L'Enfant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Charles_L'Enfant)'s 1793 plan of the city of Washington, indicates a setting of terraced formal gardens descending to Tiber Creek. Later in 1850, landscape designer [Andrew Jackson Downing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson_Downing) attempted to soften the geometry of the L'Enfant plan, incorporating a semicircular southern boundary and meandering paths.[^cite_note-4] Andrew Jackson Downing's changes included enlarging the South Lawn, creating a large circular lawn he termed the "Parade or President's Park" and bordered by densely planted shrubs and trees.[^cite_note-5] During the administration of [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) the marsh to the south was drained, and the South Lawn received additional grading and 8 to 10 feet (2.4 to 3.0 m) of fill to make the descent to the Potomac more gradual.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-6]

During the administrations of [Rutherford B. Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_B._Hayes) and [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland)'s first term, the [U.S. Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Corps_of_Engineers) were engaged to reconfigure the South Lawn, reducing the size of Downing's circular parade, and creating the current boundaries much as they presently are.[^cite_note-7] [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt), who had engaged the architectural firm of [McKim, Mead, and White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKim,_Mead,_and_White) to reconfigure and rebuild parts of the White House in 1902, was influenced to remove the complex of Victorian era glass houses built up the West Colonnade and the site of the present [West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Wing). In 1934, President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) engaged Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. to evaluate the grounds and recommend changes. Olmsted understood the need to offer presidents and their families a modicum of privacy balancing with the requirement for public views of the White House.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-6] The Olmsted plan presented the landscape largely as seen today: retaining or planting large specimen trees and shrubs on the perimeter to create boundaries for visual privacy, but punctuated with generous sight lines of the house from north and south.[^cite_note-8]

The [1974 White House helicopter incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_White_House_helicopter_incident) ended on the South Lawn.[^cite_note-9]

In 2025, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) had an [88-foot tall flagpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_at_the_White_House) installed on the South Lawn.[^cite_note-10]

A temporary 5,000-seat arena was erected on the South Lawn for [UFC Freedom 250](80931969.md) in 2026, the only professional sporting event to be held on White House grounds.[^cite_note-Dezeen-11] After the arena was dismantled, construction crews began work on a granite [helipad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_helipad) to accommodate the new [Sikorsky VH-92 Patriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_VH-92_Patriot), which scorched and uprooted the grass beneath it.[^cite_note-WP_2026-06-30-1][^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-\:1-13]

## Horticulture

### Grass

The lawn is planted with a grass variety called [tall fescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca_arundinacea).[^cite_note-14]

### Specimen trees



Snow on the [Atlas Cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus_atlantica) on the South Lawn in January 2007

Trees on the South Lawn include southern magnolias (*[Magnolia grandiflora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_grandiflora)*) on either side of the South portico, Japanese threadleaf maple (*[Acer palmatum dissectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_palmatum)*), American elm (*[Ulmus americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulmus_americana)*), white oak (*[Quercus alba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_alba)*), white saucer magnolia (*[Magnolia × soulangeana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_%C3%97_soulangeana)*), Atlas cedar (*[Cedrus atlantica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus_atlantica)*), sugar maple (*[Acer saccharum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_saccharum)*), and northern red oak (*[Quercus rubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_rubra)*).[^cite_note-15]

### Seasonal plantings

The South Lawn pool and fountain is planted seasonally with borders of [tulips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulipa) edged by grape hyacinth (*[Muscari armeniacum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscari_armeniacum)*) for spring, red geranium (*[Pelargonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelargonium)*) and Dusty Miller (*[Senecio cineraria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senecio_cineraria)*) in summer, and chrysanthemum (*[Chrysanthemum cinerariaefolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysanthemum)*) in fall.[^cite_note-16]

## Amenities

### Ceremonial gardens

The two ceremonial gardens of the White House (the [Rose Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Rose_Garden) and the [Jacqueline Kennedy Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Kennedy_Garden)) face the South Lawn. The Rose Garden (sometimes referred to as "The presidents Garden") is located south-west of the main residence along the west colonnade, just outside the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office). The Jacqueline Kennedy Garden is located south-east of the main residence along the east colonnade. The garden was dedicated by [Lady Bird Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Johnson) as the Jacqueline Kennedy Garden on April 22, 1965, although it has been called the "First Lady's Garden" by some later administrations.

### Tennis and basketball court

Main article: [White House tennis court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_tennis_court)



Tennis Pavilion on the South Lawn in 2020

A tennis court was first installed during the [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) administration on the south lawn. Since then, the court has been moved several times, eventually landing in its current position in the south-west area. [President Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) had basketball court lines and removable baskets installed so he could play full court basketball. Located just west of the tennis and basketball court is a half-court basketball area that also housed a horseshoe pit. During [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s first term, [Melania Trump](1209075.md) oversaw the design and construction of the White House Tennis Pavilion by the firm [Steven W. Spandle Architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_W._Spandle_Architect) in 2020.[^cite_note-Melania_Work-17]

### Swimming pool

Main article: [White House swimming pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_swimming_pool)



[President Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) swimming in the [White House swimming pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_swimming_pool) in July 1975

In 1975, the outdoor swimming pool was installed by President [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] It is located directly south of the West Wing, surrounded by a thick row of trees for privacy. A cabana was later added. The original pool at the White House was indoors, located in between the main residence and the West Wing. However, President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) turned it into an area for the press, now known as the [James S. Brady Press Briefing Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_S._Brady_Press_Briefing_Room).[^cite_note-Association2002-20]

### Putting green

Main article: [White House putting green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_putting_green)



[Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and [Joe Biden](145422.md) on the White House Putting Green in April 2009

The [putting green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putting_green) was first installed in 1954 by [President Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) who was an avid golfer. It was removed by President Nixon[^cite_note-21] and later reinstalled by President [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) in 1991.[^cite_note-22] President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) moved it to its current location just south of the Rose Garden, a short walk from the Oval Office.[^cite_note-23]

### Horseshoe pit

Main article: [White House horseshoe pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_horseshoe_pit)

A [horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe) pit was created on the site of the present putting green by [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman), and later re-established near the swimming pool by [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) who was an avid player. Two month long horseshoe tournaments were held bi-annually during Bush's presidency, with teams consisting of maintenance and house staff and family members and administration personnel. Bush would frequently demonstrate his prowess at horseshoes for foreign dignitaries.[^cite_note-WHM-24][^cite_note-NR18-25]

### Children's garden

The children's garden is located between the tennis court and basketball court to the south-west area of the property. The garden was a gift to the White House in 1968 from [President Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) and his [wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Bird_Johnson). The garden supplies a secluded location for children to play in private. The garden features a goldfish pond in the sitting area. Footprints and handprints of various President's children and grandchildren are embedded in various stones making up the walkway.

### Helipad

Main article: [White House helipad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_helipad)



[Sikorsky VH-92 Patriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_VH-92_Patriot) initial takeoff from the helipad in September 2026



[VH-3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH-3D) on South Lawn with the [Washington Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Monument) and the [Jefferson Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Memorial) (background) in September 2018

The South Lawn includes a [helipad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helipad) for the president's helicopter, [Marine One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_One), to land directly on the White House grounds.

The lawn originally did not require a permanent helipad, but three removable aluminum discs were used to accommodate the landing gear of previous helicopters since the 1960s and recently the [VH-3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH-3D).[^cite_note-26] The helicopter took off and landed in a grassy area directly south of the main residence.[^cite_note-27]

The new Marine One, a [Sikorsky VH-92 Patriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_VH-92_Patriot) whose powerful [rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_rotor) uprooted the lawn and created hazardous flying debris was removed for White House flights. To accommodate the aircraft, President Trump ordered construction of a [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_granite) with white [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) helipad on the former landing area in June 2026. The helipad, completed in early September 2026,[^cite_note-TheHill-28] measures roughly 100 feet (30 m) in diameter and contains a depiction of the [presidential seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_the_president_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-\:1-13]

### Running track

[President Clinton](3356.md) had a quarter-mile (0.4 km) long jogging track installed during his presidency in 1993. An avid runner, Clinton would regularly want to go on runs, but his doing so greatly disrupted Washington traffic; thus the track was built adjacent to the South Lawn driveway. At a distance, its spongy surface is virtually indistinguishable from the driveway's asphalt.

### Playground

A playground was installed in 2009 under President Obama for his two children, Sasha and Malia Obama. The playground was located just south of the Oval Office near the [Rose Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Rose_Garden), but removed at the end of the Obama administration.[^cite_note-29]

### Vegetable garden

Main article: [White House vegetable garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_vegetable_garden)



[First Lady Michelle Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Obama) harvests vegetables with [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) students in 2011.

[Michelle Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Obama) installed a vegetable garden during her husband's presidency on the far south area of the property. The vegetables grown are used at the White House for meals as well as donated to area shelters.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Beehives



Artisan beehive installed in 2026

[^cite_note-Beehive-32]

In 2009, Michelle Obama moved two beehives to the South Lawn that White House Carpenter Charlie Brandt began as a hobby on the complex a few years prior.[^cite_note-Moved_Hives-33] The organic honey is used in food preparation for the [First Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_family_of_the_United_States), state dinners, and gatherings while serving as official gifts.[^cite_note-People_Edwards-34]

In 2026, Melania Trump announced the expansion of the White House honey program with the addition of a newly installed beehive. Hand-crafted by a local artisan in the image of the White House, the new hive added two bee colonies, bringing the number of colonies to four.[^cite_note-People_Edwards-34][^cite_note-Melania_Honey-35]

### Belleau Wood tree



[President Trump](4848272.md) and French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) plant the Belleau Wood tree on the South Lawn in commemoration of the [Battle of Belleau Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Belleau_Wood) as [First Lady Melania Trump](1209075.md) and [Brigitte Macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Macron) look on in April 2018.

During French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md)'s 2018 state visit to the United States, President Donald Trump, First Lady Melania Trump, French President Macron and his wife [Brigitte Macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Macron) planted a "European [sessile oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sessile_oak) from the Belleau Wood" on the South Lawn to commemorate the [Battle of Belleau Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Belleau_Wood).[^cite_note-nytimestrumpsfirststatedinner-36][^cite_note-marinecorpstime-37] The oak was originally sprouted at the [World War I Battle of Belleau Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Belleau_Wood) grounds in northern France. The tree was removed to [quarantine](25237.md) soon after planting.[^cite_note-BelleauWood-38][^cite_note-39] The [Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture)'s [Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_and_Plant_Health_Inspection_Service) said at least two years of monitoring and testing may be required before the tree and the backup tree can be planted.[^cite_note-CBS_SF-40] The tree died in quarantine in 2019.[^cite_note-Le_Monde_2019-41]
