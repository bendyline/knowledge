**Susumu Kitagawa** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (北川 進, *Kitagawa Susumu*; born 4 July 1951) is a Japanese [Nobel Prize](25416893.md)-winning [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist) specializing in [coordination chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_chemistry), with a focus on organic–inorganic hybrid compounds and the chemical and physical properties of [porous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porous_medium) coordination polymers, particularly [metal-organic frameworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal-organic_framework).[^cite_note-AngewChem2009-1] He is Distinguished Professor at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University)'s Institute for Integrated Cell-Material Sciences (iCeMS), which he co-founded. In 2025, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](25416893.md) jointly with [Richard Robson](49469415.md) and [Omar M. Yaghi](17344326.md).

## Life



Kitagawa attended an online meeting with [Shigeru Ishiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Ishiba) (October 9, 2025)

Kitagawa was born on 4 July 1951 in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto).[^cite_note-Nobel-2]

He earned his PhD in hydrocarbon chemistry from [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) in 1979, having completed his undergraduate studies at the same institution. That year, he was appointed assistant professor at [Kindai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindai_University), where he was promoted to lecturer in 1983 and associate professor in 1988.[^cite_note-AngewChem2009-1][^cite_note-KitagawaCV2016-3]

In 1992, he became professor of [inorganic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_chemistry) at [Tokyo Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_University).[^cite_note-AngewChem2009-1] He returned to Kyoto University in 1998 as a professor of inorganic functional chemistry in the Department of Synthetic Chemistry and [Biological Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry).[^cite_note-KitagawaCV2016-3][^cite_note-Trager2025-4] In 2007, he co-founded the [Institute for Integrated Cell-Material Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Integrated_Cell-Material_Sciences?action=edit\&redlink=1) (iCeMS), serving as founding deputy director, then as director from 2013 to 2023.[^cite_note-MEXT2009-5][^cite_note-icemsTimeline-6] In 2024, he was appointed Executive Vice-President for Research Promotion at Kyoto University.[^cite_note-KUIAS-profile-7]

His overseas academic postings include a postdoctoral fellowship at [Texas A\&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_University) with [F. Albert Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Albert_Cotton) (1986–1987) and a guest professorship at the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) (1996).[^cite_note-AngewChem2009-1][^cite_note-Trager2025-4][^cite_note-Kitagawa2001-8] He received an honorary doctorate from the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) in 2018.[^cite_note-TUM2025-9]

Kitagawa served as a member and associate member of the [Science Council of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Council_of_Japan) from 2011 to 2023.[^cite_note-KitagawaCV2016-3][^cite_note-SCJ2025-10]

In 2025, Kitagawa, along with [Richard Robson](49469415.md) and [Omar M. Yaghi](17344326.md), was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](25416893.md) for their foundational work on [molecular building blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_block_\(chemistry\)) for metal-organic frameworks.[^cite_note-NYT2025ChemistryNobel-11]

## Research

[Scholia](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata\:Scholia) has a profile for [**Susumu Kitagawa (Q11401680)**](https://iw.toolforge.org/scholia/Q11401680).

Following the discoveries of [Makoto Fujita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Fujita_\(chemist\)) (1994) and [Omar M. Yaghi](17344326.md) (1995), Kitagawa demonstrated in 1997 that coordination polymer structures possess gas adsorption properties, a key finding for the development of functional porous materials.[^cite_note-Trager2025-4]

His most influential works include:

- A 1997 seminal report on a porous coordination polymer (MOF) for small molecule adsorption.
