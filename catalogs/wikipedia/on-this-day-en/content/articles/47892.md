The **Bay of Bengal** forms the northeastern part of the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), located between the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) and the [Indochinese peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia), south of the [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) region. Spread across an area of 2,600,000 km<sup>2</sup> (1,000,000 sq mi), it is bordered by many of the [countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries_of_the_Bay_of_Bengal) in South Asia and Southeast Asia.

Geopolitically, the bay is bound by mainland [India](14533.md) on the west and northwest, [Bangladesh](3454.md) in the north, [Myanmar](19457.md) in the northeast and the [Andaman and Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_and_Nicobar_Islands) of India in the east. Its southern limit is a line between [Sangaman Kanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangaman_Kanda) in [Sri Lanka](26750.md), and the northwesternmost point of [Sumatra](28679.md) in [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-world-1][^cite_note-asiatic-2]

Major river systems of the Indian subcontinent such as the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges), [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra), [Irrawaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_River), [Godavari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari), [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River), [Brahmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmani), [Baitarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baitarani_River), [Rushikulya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushikulya), [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi), and [Kaveri](177299.md) flow into the Bay of Bengal. The coast consist of many beaches including some of the world's longest natural beaches, such as [Cox's Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Beach) and [Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Beach), and varied ecosytems such as the [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans), the largest [mangrove forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest) in the world.

## Background

### Extent

### Etymology

The bay gets its name from the [historical Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bengal) region (modern-day [Bangladesh](3454.md) and the Indian states of [West Bengal](34040.md), [Tripura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura) and the [Barak Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Valley) of Southern [Assam](186162.md)). In Ancient Indian [scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shastra), this water body may have been referred to as Mahodadhi.[^cite_note-5] Ancient Romans called it *Sinus Gangeticus* or *Gangeticus Sinus* whilst Ancient Greeks called it in [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Κόλπος Γαγγητικός, meaning "Gulf of the Ganges",[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] and old European maps continued to use this name.[^cite_note-8]

### History



Ross Island, in the [Andamans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Islands), was one of the main naval bases of India during [World War II](32927.md).

The Bay of Bengal was historically known by several names reflecting the significant maritime kingdoms that bordered it. In the region corresponding to modern [Odisha](250724.md) and parts of [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md), it was widely referred to as Kalinga Sagar (Kalinga Sea), derived from the ancient kingdom of [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(region\)).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-reemerge-10]

In ancient [Classical India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_India), the Bay of Bengal was known as Vaṅgasāgara ([Vanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanga_Kingdom) Sea).[^cite_note-11] Another name used mainly in ancient [Tamil literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_literature) was Vanga Kadal ([Vanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanga_kingdom) Sea or Ocean).&#x20;

[Northern Circars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Circars) occupied the western coast of the Bay of Bengal and is now considered to be India's [Odisha](250724.md) and [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) state. [Chola dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty) (9th century to 12th century) when ruled by [Rajaraja Chola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajaraja_Chola_I) and [Rajendra Chola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Chola_I) occupied and controlled the Bay of Bengal with [Chola Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_Navy) [circa AD 1014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Cholas), the Bay of Bengal was also called the Chola Sea or Chola Lake.[^cite_note-reemerge-10]

The [Kakatiya dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakatiya_dynasty) reached the western coastline of the Bay of Bengal between the [Godavari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari) and the [Krishna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River). [Kushanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) about the middle of the [1st century AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India#Early_classical_period_\(c._200_BCE_%E2%80%93_c._320_CE\)) invaded northern India perhaps extending as far as the Bay of Bengal. [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya) extended the [Maurya Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_India#Maurya_Empire) across northern India to the Bay of Bengal. [Hajipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Harbour) was a stronghold for Portuguese Pirates. In the 16th century, the Portuguese built trading posts in the north of the Bay of Bengal at Chittagong (Porto Grande) and [Satgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saptagram) (Porto Pequeno).[^cite_note-12]

The earliest sign of Muslims in the region came from the textile trade routes where one targeted the east Arabian Sea influencing migration of Arabs and Persians and another to the west causing Buddhist Bengalis to culturally mix with Islam.[^cite_note-13]

#### Marine archaeology

[Maritime archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_archaeology) or marine archaeology is the study of how ancient peoples interacted with the sea and waterways. A specialized branch, [archaeology of shipwrecks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_shipwrecks), studies the salvaged artifacts of ancient ships. Stone anchors, amphorae shards, elephant tusks, hippopotamus teeth, ceramic pottery, a rare wood mast and lead ingots are examples which may survive submerged for centuries for archaeologists to discover, study, and place their salvaged findings into the timeline of history. Coral reefs, tsunamis, cyclones, mangrove swamps, battles, and a criss-cross of sea routes in a high trading area combined with piracy have all contributed to shipwrecks in the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-18]

## Significance

### Economic importance

See also: [Countries of the Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries_of_the_Bay_of_Bengal)

One of the first trading ventures along the Bay of Bengal was The Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies, more commonly referred to as the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company). [Gopalpur-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopalpur-on-Sea) was one of their main trading centers. Other trading companies along the Bay of Bengal shorelines were the [English East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_East_India_Company) and the [French East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Law's_Company).[^cite_note-23]

[BIMSTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIMSTEC) (Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation) supports free trade internationally around the Bay of Bengal between [Bangladesh](3454.md), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), India, Myanmar, [Nepal](171166.md), Sri Lanka, and [Thailand](30128.md).

The [Sethusamudram Shipping Canal Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sethusamudram_Shipping_Canal_Project) is a new venture proposed which would create a channel for a shipping route to link the [Gulf of Mannar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mannar) with the Bay of Bengal. This would connect India from east to west without the necessity of going around Sri Lanka.

Thoni and catamaran fishing boats of fishing villages thrive along the Bay of Bengal shorelines. Fishermen can catch between 26 and 44 species of marine fish.[^cite_note-24] In one year, the average catch is two million tons of fish from the Bay of Bengal alone.[^cite_note-25] Approximately 31% of the world's coastal fishermen live and work on the bay.[^cite_note-26]

### Geostrategic importance

See also: [Eastern South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_South_Asia)

The Bay of Bengal is centrally located in South and Southeast Asia. It lies at the center of two huge economic blocks, the [SAARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAARC) and [ASEAN](106539.md). It influences China's southern landlocked region in the north and major sea ports of Bangladesh and India. China, India, and Bangladesh have forged naval cooperation agreements with Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia to increase cooperation in checking terrorism in the high seas.[^cite_note-atimes.com-27] The Bay of Bengal's connection of South Asia to East Asia has aided in Bangladesh's efficiency of distributing natural gas to the Asia Pacific.[^cite_note-28]



Image of United States ships participating in the [Malabar 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_2007) naval exercise. Aegis cruisers from the navies of Japan and Australia, and logistical support ships from Singapore and India in the Bay of Bengal took part.

Its outlying islands (the Andaman and Nicobar Islands) and, most importantly, major ports such as [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Port), [Mongla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongla_Port), [Payra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Payra), [Paradip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradip_Port), [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Port), [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Port), [Visakhapatnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visakhapatnam_Port), and [Tuticorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuticorin_Port), along its coast with the Bay of Bengal added to its importance.[^cite_note-29]

China has made efforts to project influence into the region through tie-ups with Myanmar and Bangladesh.[^cite_note-30] The United States has held major exercises with Bangladesh, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and recently India.[^cite_note-globalpolitician.com-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The largest ever [wargame](1225714.md) in Bay of Bengal, known as Malabar 2007, was held in 2007 and naval warships from the United States, India, Singapore, Japan and Australia took part.[^cite_note-35]

Large deposits of natural gas in the areas within Bangladesh's sea zone incited a serious urgency by India and Myanmar into a territorial dispute.[^cite_note-atimes.com-27] Disputes over rights of some oil and gas blocks have caused brief diplomatic spats between Myanmar and India with Bangladesh.

The disputed [maritime boundary](29613061.md) between Bangladesh and Myanmar resulted in military tensions in 2008 and 2009. The maritime dispute between Bangladesh and Myanmar settled in 2012 through the judgement of ITLOS.[^cite_note-36] In 2014, the dispute between India and Bangladesh was also settled in which the UN tribunal awarded Bangladesh 19,467 km<sup>2</sup> of the 25,602 km<sup>2</sup> sea area of the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-37]

### Religious importance



Samudra arati or worship of the sea by disciples of the Govardhan Matha at Puri

The Bay of Bengal in the stretch of [Swargadwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancha_Tirtha,_Puri), the gateway to heaven in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), in the Indian town of [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(city\)) is considered holy by [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus).[^cite_note-DeccanHerald-06Mar16-38][^cite_note-39]

The Samudra [arati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arati) is a daily tradition started by the present [Shankaracharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankaracharya) of Puri 9 years ago to honour the [sacred sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_sea).[^cite_note-DeccanHerald-06Mar16-38] The daily practise includes prayer and fire offering to the sea at Swargadwar in Puri by disciples of the [Govardhana matha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govardhana_matha) of the Shankaracharya.[^cite_note-DeccanHerald-06Mar16-38][^cite_note-40] On Paush Purnima of every year the Shankaracharya himself comes out to offer prayers to the sea.[^cite_note-41]

### Islands



[Havelock Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havelock_Island), Andaman Islands

The islands in the bay are numerous, including the [Andaman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Islands) and [Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_Islands) of India. The Cheduba group of islands, in the north-east, off the Burmese coast, are remarkable for a chain of mud volcanoes, which are occasionally active.[^cite_note-EB1911-50]

[Great Andaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Andaman) is the main archipelago or island group of the Andaman Islands, whereas [Ritchie's Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie's_Archipelago) consists of smaller islands. Only 37, or 6.5%, of the 572 islands and islets of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands are inhabited.[^cite_note-51]

### Rivers

Many major rivers of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_major_rivers_of_India) and [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Bangladesh) flow west to east before draining into the Bay of Bengal. The [Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) is the northernmost of these rivers. Its main channel enters and flows through Bangladesh, where it is known as the [Padma River](1538264.md), before joining the [Meghna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghna_River). However, the [Brahmaputra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River) flows from east to west in [Assam](186162.md) before turning south and entering Bangladesh where it is called the [Jamuna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamuna_River). This joins the Padma whereupon the Padma joins the Meghna River that finally drains into Bay of Bengal. The [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans) is a mangrove forest in the southern part of the [Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Delta) which lies in the Indian state of West Bengal and in Bangladesh. The Brahmaputra at 2,948 km (1,832 mi) is the 15th [longest River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_by_length) in the world. It originates in [Tibet](31516.md). The [Hooghly River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooghly_River), another channel of the [Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga) that flows through [Kolkata](47905.md) drains into Bay of Bengal at [Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagar_Island) in West Bengal, India.

The Ganga–Brahmaputra-Barak rivers deposit nearly 1000 million tons of [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) every year. The sediment from these three rivers form the Bengal Delta and the [submarine fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_fan), a vast structure that extends from [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) to south of the Equator, is up to 16.5 kilometres (10.3 mi) thick, and contains at least 1,130 trillion tonnes of sediment, which has accumulated over the last 17 million years at an average rate of 665 million tons per annum.[^cite_note-52] The fan has buried organic carbon at a rate of nearly 1.1 trillion mol/yr (13.2 million t/yr) since the early [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) period. The three rivers currently contribute nearly 8% of the [total organic carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_organic_carbon) (TOC) deposited in the world's oceans. Due to high TOC accumulation in the deep sea bed of the Bay of Bengal, the area is rich in oil and natural gas and [gas hydrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_hydrate) reserves. Bangladesh can [reclaim land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation) substantially and economically gain from the sea area by constructing sea [dikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee), bunds, [causeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causeways) and by trapping the sediment from its rivers.

Further southwest of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), the [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi_River), [Godavari](47988.md), [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River) and [Kaveri Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaveri_River) also flow from west to east across [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) in Peninsular India and drain into the Bay of Bengal forming [deltas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta). Many small rivers also drain directly into the Bay of Bengal forming [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary); the shortest of them is the [Cooum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooum_River) at 64 km (40 mi).

While Myanmar's [Irrawaddy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_River) flows into the [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea), sediment from the river is found in the eastern Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-53]

## Oceanography

### Geology

#### Lithosphere and plate tectonics

The [lithosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithosphere) of the earth is broken up into what are called [tectonic plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics). Underneath the Bay of Bengal, which is part of the great [Indo-Australian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Australian_Plate) and is slowly moving north east. This plate meets the [Burma Microplate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Plate) at the [Sunda Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Trench). The [Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_Islands) and the [Andaman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Islands) are part of the Burma Microplate. The India Plate subducts beneath the Burma Plate at the Sunda Trench or Java Trench. Here, the pressure of the two plates on each other increase pressure and temperature resulting in the formation of volcanoes such as the [volcanoes in Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_volcanoes_in_Myanmar), and a [volcanic arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_arc) called the [Sunda Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Arc). The [Sumatra-Andaman earthquake and Asian tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami) was a result of the pressure at this zone causing a [submarine earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_earthquake) which then resulted in a destructive tsunami.[^cite_note-55]

#### Marine geology



Bay of Bengal near [Tenneti Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenneti_Park), [Visakhapatnam](238760.md)

A zone 50 m wide extending from the island of Sri Lanka and the [Coromandel coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coromandel_Coast) to the head of the bay, and thence southwards through a strip embracing the Andaman and Nicobar islands, is bounded by the 100 fathom line of sea bottom; some 50 m. beyond this lies the 500-fathom limit. Opposite the mouth of the Ganges, however, the intervals between these depths are very much extended by deltaic influence.[^cite_note-EB1911-50]

[Swatch of No Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swatch_of_No_Ground) is a 14 km-wide deep sea canyon of the Bay of Bengal. The deepest recorded area of this valley is about 1340 m.[^cite_note-56] The submarine canyon is part of the [Bengal Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Fan), the largest submarine fan in the world.[^cite_note-mpgCurray-57][^cite_note-whoi-bf-mar2000-58]

#### Submarine fans

Submarine fan is also known as [abyssal fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssal_fan). Bay of Bengal fan, known as **Bengal Fan**, also known as the **Ganges Fan** is world's largest abyssal fan, also known as deep-sea fans, underwater deltas, and submarine fans. The fan is about 3,000 km (1,900 mi) long, 1,430 km (890 mi) wide with a maximum thickness of 16.5 km (10.3 mi).[^cite_note-59] The fan resulted from the uplift and erosion of the [Himalayas](37966.md) and the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau) produced by the collision between the [Indian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate) and the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate). Most of the sediment is supplied by the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) and [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra) rivers which supply the Lower [Meghna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghna_River) [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Delta) in Bangladesh and the [Hoogly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooghly_River) delta in [West Bengal](34040.md) (India). Several other large rivers in Bangladesh and India provide smaller contributions.[^cite_note-mpgCurray-57] [Turbidity currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbidity_current) have transported the sediment through a series of [submarine canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_canyons), some of which are more than 2,400 kilometres (1,500 mi) in length, to be deposited in the Bay of Bengal up to 30 degrees [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude) from where it began. To date, the oldest sediments recovered from the Bengal fan are from [Early Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Miocene) age.[^cite_note-mpgCochran-60] Their mineralogical and geochemical characteristics allow to identify their Himalayan origin and demonstrate that the [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) was already a major mountain range 20 million years ago.[^cite_note-mpgFrance-Lanord-61]

The fan completely covers the floor of the Bay of Bengal. It is bordered to the west by the continental slope of eastern India, to the north by the continental slope of Bangladesh and to east by the northern part of [Sunda Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Trench) off Myanmar and the [Andaman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Islands), the [accretionary wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretionary_wedge) associated with [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) of the [Indo-Australian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Australian_Plate) beneath the [Sunda Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Plate) and continues along the west side of the [Ninety East Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninety_East_Ridge). The Nicobar Fan, another lobe of the fan, lies east of the Ninety East Ridge.[^cite_note-sepm92-62][^cite_note-whoi-bf-mar2000-58]

The fan is now being explored as a possible source of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels) for the surrounding [developing nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_nation).

The fan was first identified by bathymetric survey in the sixties by Bruce C. Heezen and Marie Tharp which identified the abyssal cone and canyon structures. It was delineated and named by Joseph Curray and David Moore following a geological and geophysical survey in 1968.[^cite_note-whoi-bf-mar2000-58][^cite_note-curray1971-63]

### Oceanographic chemistry

[Coastal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal) regions bordering the Bay of Bengal are rich in minerals. Sri Lanka, [Serendib](26750.md), or *Ratna – Dweepa* which means Gem Island. [Amethyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amethyst), [beryl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryl), [ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby), [sapphire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire), [topaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topaz), and [garnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet) are just some of the [gems of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gems_of_Sri_Lanka). Garnet and other precious gems are also found in abundance in the [Indian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_India) of [Odisha](250724.md) and [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md).[^cite_note-64] A 2014 study found that as a result of ocean acidification, there was reduced shell thickness of marine animals and breaking strength compared to normal shells. The study also showed that the pH in Bay of Bengal fell to 7.75 compared in 1994 when it averaged 7.95.[^cite_note-65]

### Oceanographic climate

From January to October, the current is northward flowing, and the clockwise circulation pattern is called the "East Indian Current". The Bay of Bengal [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_India) moves in a northwest direction striking the Nicobar Islands, and the Andaman Islands first end of May, then coast of [Mainland India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_India) by end of June.

The remainder of the year, the counterclockwise current is southwestward flowing, and the circulation pattern is called the East Indian Winter Jet. September and December see very active weather, season varsha (or monsoon), in the Bay of Bengal producing severe cyclones which affect eastern India. Several efforts have been initiated to cope with [storm surge](449744.md).[^cite_note-66]

### Marine biology, flora and fauna



A [spinner dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinner_dolphin) in Bay of Bengal



*[Tachypleus gigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachypleus_gigas)* in [Odisha](250724.md)

The Bay of Bengal is full of biological diversity, diverging amongst [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reefs), [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuaries), fish spawning and nursery areas, and [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangroves). The Bay of Bengal is one of the World's 64 [largest marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ecoregion) [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ecosystem).

*[Kerilia jerdonii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerilia_jerdonii)* is a sea snake of the Bay of Bengal. Glory of Bengal cone (*[Conus bengalensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conus_bengalensis)*) is just one of the seashells which can be photographed along beaches of the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-67] An [endangered species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_Species_Act), the [olive ridley sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_ridley_sea_turtle) can survive because of the nesting grounds made available at the Gahirmatha Marine Wildlife Sanctuary, [Gahirmatha Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gahirmatha_Beach), [Odisha](250724.md), India. [Marlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlin), [barracuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracuda), [skipjack tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skipjack_tuna) (*Katsuwonus pelamis*), [yellowfin tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowfin_tuna), [Indo-Pacific humpback dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific_humpback_dolphin) (*Sousa chinensis*), and [Bryde's whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryde's_whale) (*Balaenoptera edeni*) are a few of the marine animals. Bay of Bengal hogfish (*[Bodianus neilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodianus_neilli)*) is a type of [wrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrasse) which live in turbid lagoon reefs or shallow coastal reefs. Schools of dolphins can be seen, whether they are the [bottlenose dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottlenose_dolphin) (*Tursiops truncatus*), [pantropical spotted dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantropical_spotted_dolphin) (*Stenella attenuata*) or the [spinner dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinner_dolphin) (*S. longirostris*). [Tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna) and dolphins usually reside in the same waters. In shallower and warmer coastal waters the [Irrawaddy dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_dolphin) (*Orcaella brevirostris*) can be found.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

The [Great Nicobar Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Nicobar_Biosphere_Reserve) provides sanctuary to many animals some of which include the [saltwater crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile) (*Crocodylus porosus*), [leatherback sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherback_sea_turtle) (*Dermochelys coriacea*), and [Malayan box turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amboina_box_turtle) (*Cuora amboinensis kamaroma*) to name a few.[^cite_note-70]

Another endangered species [royal Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger) is supported by [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_National_Park) a large estuarine delta that holds a mangrove area in the [Ganges River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Delta).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

A transboundary issue is defined as an environmental problem in which either the cause of the problem and/or its impact is separated by a national boundary; or the problem contributes to a global environmental problem and finding regional solutions is considered to be a global environmental benefit. The eight Bay of Bengal countries have (2012) identified three major transboundary problems (or areas of concern) affecting the health of the Bay, that they can work on together. With the support of the Bay Of Bengal Large Marine Ecosystem Project (BOBLME), the eight countries are now (2012) developing responses to these issues and their causes, for future implementation as the Strategic Action Programme.

### Ecological degradation

#### Fisheries overexploitation



Some small fishing boats are catching fish & sell them in local coastal markets.

[Fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries) production in the Bay of Bengal is 6,000,000 tonnes per year, more than 7% of the world's catch. The major transboundary issues relating to shared fisheries are: a decline in the overall availability of fish resources; changes in species composition of catches; the high proportion of juvenile fish in the catch; and changes in [marine biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biodiversity), especially through loss of vulnerable and [endangered species](20110668.md). The transboundary nature of these issues are: that many [fish stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_stocks) are shared between BOBLME countries through the transboundary migration of fish, or [larvae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyoplankton). Fishing overlaps national jurisdictions, both legally and illegally – overcapacity and [overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing) in one location forces a migration of fishers and vessels to other locations. All countries (to a greater or lesser degree) are experiencing difficulties in implementing [fisheries management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_management), especially the [ecosystem approach to fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem_approach_to_fisheries). Bay of Bengal countries contribute significantly to the global problem of loss of [vulnerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_species) and endangered species. The main causes of the issues are: open access to fishing grounds; government emphasis on increasing fish catches; inappropriate government subsidies provided to fishers; increasing fishing effort, especially from [trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler) and [purse seiners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purse_seiners); high consumer demand for fish, including for seed and [fishmeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishmeal) for [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture); ineffective fisheries management; and illegal and destructive fishing.

#### Marine habitats degradation

The Bay of Bengal is an area of high biodiversity, with many endangered and vulnerable species. The major transboundary issues relating to habitats are: the loss and degradation of [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) habitats; [degradation of coral reefs](27687935.md); and the loss of, and damage to, [seagrasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrasses). The transboundary nature of these major issues are: that all three critical habitats occur in all BOBLME countries. [Coastal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_development_hazards) for several varying uses of the land and sea are common in all BOBLME countries. Trade in products from all the habitats is transboundary in nature. [Climate change](5042951.md) impacts are shared by all BOBLME countries. The main causes of the issues are: [food security](216361.md) needs of the coastal poor; lack of [coastal development plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_management); increasing trade in products from coastal habitats; coastal development and industrialization; ineffective [marine protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_protected_area) and lack of enforcement; upstream development that affects water-flow; [intensive upstream agricultural practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpoint_source_pollution); and increasing tourism.

### Environmental degradation

#### Environmental hazards

The [Asian brown cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_brown_cloud), a layer of air pollution that covers much of South Asia and the Indian Ocean every year between January and March, and possibly also during earlier and later months, hangs over the Bay of Bengal. It is considered to be a combination of vehicle exhaust, smoke from cooking fires, and industrial discharges.[^cite_note-73] Because of this cloud, satellites attempting to track [ocean acidification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_acidification) and other ocean health indicators in the Bay have difficulty obtaining accurate measurements.[^cite_note-74]

#### Pollution and water quality

The major transboundary issues relating to [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution) and [water quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_quality) are: sewage-borne pathogens and organic load; solid waste/marine litter; increasing nutrient inputs; oil pollution; persistent organic pollutants ([POPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persistent_organic_pollutant)) and persistent toxic substances (PTSs); sedimentation; and heavy metals. The transboundary nature of these issues are: discharge of untreated/partially treated [sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage) being a common problem. Sewage and organic discharges from the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges)-[Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra)-[Meghna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghna) River are likely to be transboundary. Plastics and derelict fishing gear can be transported long distances across national boundaries. Around 4 million tonnes of [microplastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microplastics) are estimated to come from India and Bangladesh travelling into Sundurban and subsequently the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-75] High nutrient discharges from rivers could intensify largescale hypoxia. Atmospheric transport of nutrients is inherently transboundary. Differences between countries with regard to regulation and enforcement of shipping discharges may drive discharges across boundaries.[^cite_note-76] [Tar balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarball_\(oil\)) are transported long distances. POPs/PTSs and [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(element\)), including organo-mercury, undergo long-range transport. [Sedimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentation) and most heavy metal contamination tend to be localized and lack a strong transboundary dimension.[^cite_note-77] The main causes of the issues are: increasing coastal population density and urbanization; higher consumption, resulting in more waste generated per person; insufficient funds allocated to waste management; migration of industry into BOBLME countries; and proliferation of small industries. A pertinent issue is the rapid growth of the shrimp culture industry which requires use of antibiotics and chemicals for export-quality food safety but pollutes the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-78]
