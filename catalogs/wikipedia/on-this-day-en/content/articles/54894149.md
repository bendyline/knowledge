**Josh Kerr** (born 8 October 1997)[^cite_note-WAprofile-4] is a Scottish [middle-distance runner](89280.md) who has held the world record in the [mile](5227492.md) since 2026, with a time of 3\:42.66. In the 1500 metres, Kerr won gold at the [2023 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), silver at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), and bronze at the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres). In 2026 he won the [inaugural](84179088.md) [World Athletics Ultimate Championship](77071612.md) in the event. In the mile, he is the 2026 Commonwealth Games champion. In the 3000 metres, Kerr is the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_3000_metres) and [2026 world indoor champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships). As a junior, Kerr won gold at the [2015 European Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_Athletics_Junior_Championships).&#x20;

Kerr competed for the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico) from 2015 to 2018, winning three NCAA titles. In April 2017, he set a collegiate record in the 1500 metres that stood until May 2021. He turned professional in 2018 and joined the [Brooks Beasts Track Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks_Beasts_Track_Club). In 2023, [Sportscotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportscotland) named him Scottish Sportsperson of the Year.

## Early life and background

Kerr was born on 8 October 1997 in [Edinburgh](9602.md), Scotland.[^cite_note-5] His mother works as a physiotherapist, and his father is a former rugby player. His older brother [Jake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Kerr_\(rugby_union\)) is a professional rugby player. Kerr began running with the Edinburgh Athletics Club at the age of eight. He was educated at [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College).[^cite_note-6] At 16, he started reaching out to athletic coaches at colleges in the United States, aiming to compete in the [National Collegiate Athletics Association](60706.md) (NCAA).[^cite_note-KerrAmericanDream-2][^cite_note-7] Kerr stated that he received several negative responses from coaches, except for the coach at the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico), who offered him a full athletics scholarship. In the summer before he started college, Kerr won gold in the 1500 m at the [2015 European Athletics Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_Athletics_Junior_Championships).[^cite_note-KerrAmericanDream-2] The following month, at age 17, he moved to [Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico) and began competing for the University of New Mexico, majoring in [exercise science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_science).[^cite_note-JoshKerrLoboBio-3]

## Collegiate competition



Kerr (centre) in the 1500 metres at the [2018 NCAA Division I Outdoor Track and Field Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NCAA_Division_I_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships)

While at the University of New Mexico, Kerr won three NCAA titles and set the collegiate record in the 1500 m. In March 2017, he won his first national title in the indoor mile, defeating [Edward Cheserek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Cheserek), the collegiate record holder in the event and a 15-time NCAA champion.[^cite_note-KerrAmericanDream-2] In June 2017, Kerr secured a second national title by winning the outdoor 1500 m, becoming the first man since [Leo Manzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonel_Manzano) in 2008 to achieve both the indoor mile and the outdoor 1500 m titles in the same year.[^cite_note-8] Kerr repeated his success in the indoor mile in March 2018, claiming another national title. On 20 April 2018, Kerr broke the collegiate record in the 1500 m with a time of 3\:35.01, surpassing [Sydney Maree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Maree)'s 1981 record of 3\:35.30.[^cite_note-9] This record stood for 3 years and 24 days, until it was broken by [Yared Nuguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared_Nuguse) in May 2021. Kerr's final collegiate race was in June 2018, where he finished third in the 1500 m at the [NCAA Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Men's_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships).[^cite_note-10]

## Professional competition

### 2018–2020: Early professional career



Kerr at the 2019 [Millrose Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millrose_Games)

Kerr turned professional in June 2018, forgoing his senior year of eligibility in the NCAA.[^cite_note-11] He signed a sponsorship deal with [Brooks Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks_Sports), a Seattle-based company, to train under coach Danny Mackey as part of the [Brooks Beasts Track Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks_Beasts_Track_Club). He divided his training time between Seattle and Albuquerque. In August 2019, he participated in the 1500 m at the [British Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Athletics_Championships), where he won a silver medal, finishing behind [Neil Gourley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Gourley). This performance qualified him to represent Great Britain in the 1500 m at the [2019 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres) later that month, where he placed sixth in the final.[^cite_note-12]

### 2021: Olympic 1500 m bronze

In May 2021, Kerr set a personal best of 1\:45.74 in the [800 metres](585165.md). The following month, he won his first national title in the 1500 m at the [British Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_British_Athletics_Championships).[^cite_note-13] This victory secured his spot to represent the British team at the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) in [Tokyo](30057.md), which were rescheduled to 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). At the [Olympic 1500 m final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), Kerr won a bronze medal in a personal best of 3\:29.05, finishing behind Norway's [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen) and Kenya's [Timothy Cheruiyot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Cheruiyot).[^cite_note-14]

### 2022: European record in indoor mile

On 27 February 2022, Kerr ran 3\:48.87 for the indoor mile at the Boston University Last Chance Meet. This performance broke [Eamonn Coghlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eamonn_Coghlan)'s European indoor mile record, which had stood since 1983, and [Peter Elliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Elliott_\(runner\))'s British indoor mile record from 1990. Additionally, Kerr's 1500 m split of 3\:32.86 en route to the finish set a new British national record for the indoor 1500 m, surpassing Elliot's previous record.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] In July, he competed in the 1500 m at the [2022 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres) in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon). He finished in 5th place with a time of 3\:30.60.[^cite_note-17]

### 2023: Gold medallist at World Championships



Kerr reacts after winning the 1500 metres at the [2023 World Athletics Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres)

On 23 August 2023, Kerr claimed his first major title by winning the men's 1500 metres at the [2023 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres) in a time of 3\:29.38, overcoming Norwegian favourite [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen) in the final when he prevailed in a sprint to the finish line[^cite_note-18].

It was the second successive World Championships where Ingebrigtsen was upset in the men's 1500m final by an athlete from Edinburgh Athletics Club, following teammate and compatriot [Jake Wightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Wightman)'s victory in [Eugene in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres).[^cite_note-19]

Later that month, on 31 August 2023, Kerr competed in the [Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Diamond_League) Final for the 1500 m, aiming to break the British record.[^cite_note-20] He finished second to [Yared Nuguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared_Nuguse) in a time of 3\:30.51. This mark was 1.7 seconds off the national record.

In his final race of the season on 10 September 2023, Kerr won the [New Balance 5th Avenue Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue_Mile), clocking a time of 3\:47.9.[^cite_note-21] He ended the year ranked third in the [World Athletics Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Rankings) for the 1500 m, behind Ingebrigtsen and Nuguse.[^cite_note-22] He was named Scottish sportsperson of the Year in 2023 by [Sportscotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportscotland).[^cite_note-23]

### 2024: World best, world indoor champion, Olympic 1500m silver



Kerr (centre) after winning the men's [3000m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_metres) at the [2024 World Athletics Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in [Glasgow, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow)

In his first race of the year on 11 February, Kerr set a world best in the short track [2 mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_miles) at the [Millrose Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millrose_Games).[^cite_note-24] His time of 8\:00.67 surpassed the previous world best of 8\:03.40 held by [Mo Farah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Farah) since 2015.[^cite_note-25]

On 2 March, Kerr ran 7\:42.98 for [3000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_metres) to claim gold at the [World Athletics Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow).[^cite_note-26]

On 20 April, Kerr opened his outdoor season in the Pro Men's [800 m](585165.md) Challenge at the Oregon Relays, winning in a time of 1\:45.94.[^cite_note-27] On 25 May, at the [Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefontaine_Classic), Kerr won the [Bowerman Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowerman_Mile) in a new [British record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_records_in_athletics) time of 3\:45.34, ahead of Jakob Ingebrigtsen (3\:45.60) and Yared Nuguse (3\:46.22). This eclipsed [Steve Cram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Cram)'s previous British record of 3\:46.32 by almost a full second. The race was a highly anticipated rematch between Kerr and Ingebrigtsen, and as such was billed as the "Mile of the Century."[^cite_note-28] This performance ranked Kerr as the sixth fastest miler in history.[^cite_note-29]

At the [2024 British Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_British_Athletics_Championships) in late June, Kerr chose to compete in the 800 m. In the final, Kerr finished in last after colliding with [Elliot Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Giles) on the home straight.[^cite_note-30] Just days earlier it was announced that he had signed up for the inaugural season of the [Michael Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnson_\(sprinter\)) founded [Grand Slam Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_Track) in 2025.[^cite_note-31]

On 5 July, Kerr was selected to race in 1500 metres at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Just before the athletics events got underway, he was also named as the Great Britain athletics team captain for the Games in [Paris](22989.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The final at these Games was anticipated because of Kerr's rivalry with fellow European middle-distance runner [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen).[^cite_note-37]



1500 m final in Paris; Kerr pictured in fourth position

On 6 August, in the [Olympic men's 1500 metre final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), Kerr unexpectedly finished second to [Cole Hocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_Hocker) of the United States in a sprint finish. Kerr nearly got caught at the finish line by American [Yared Nuguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared_Nuguse) who finished third, while [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen) uncharacteristically finished fourth and had led for nearly the entire race at a fast pace. In the final stretch, Ingebrigtsen was initially leading, and was passed by Kerr, while behind the two was Hocker. However, when he had room, Hocker passed Ingebrigtsen and Kerr to win the race in a new [Olympic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Olympic_records_in_athletics) and [North American area record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North,_Central_American_and_Caribbean_records_in_athletics) of 3\:27.65. Kerr's time to secure the silver medal was a new personal best and new [British national record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_records_in_athletics) of 3\:27.79, breaking [Mo Farah's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Farah) previous British record of 3\:28.81 by over a second. Nuguse was one hundredth of a second behind Kerr in a new personal best of 3\:27.80, while Ingebrigtsen finished in 3\:28.24.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Paris_2024-40]

On 8 September, Kerr won the [Fifth Avenue Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue_Mile) in [New York City](645042.md), running a new course record of 3\:44.3. This eclipsed [Sydney Maree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Maree)'s previous record of 3\:47.52 by more than three seconds, which had stood for 43 years, since the inaugural edition of the race in 1981. Kerr had previously won the race in 2023 as well, in a time of 3\:47.9.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

In October, Kerr was named Scottish Athletics' athlete of the year.[^cite_note-43]

### 2025: British 5000 m champion



Kerr en route to winning gold in the 5000 m at the [2025 UK Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UK_Athletics_Championships).

In February, Kerr intended to compete in the [Wanamaker Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanamaker_Mile) at the [Millrose Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millrose_Games), but dropped out due to illness.[^cite_note-44] In April, he finished seventh overall in the Short Distance event group at the [2025 Kingston Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kingston_Slam). In May, at the [2025 Miami Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Slam), with 16 points earned, Kerr was crowned Slam Champion of the Short Distance event group, having won the 1500 metres, and having finished fifth in the 800 metres, in a new personal best of 1\:45.01.[^cite_note-45]

In July, Kerr finished second to [Phanuel Koech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phanuel_Koech) in the 1500 metres at the [London Athletics Meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_London_Athletics_Meet).[^cite_note-46] In August, he became the [British 5000 metres champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_champions_in_5000_metres) after winning the title at the [2025 UK Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UK_Athletics_Championships).[^cite_note-47]

In September, at the [2025 World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships), Kerr sustained a right calf injury in the final of the [1500 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres).[^cite_note-48]

### 2026: Mile world record

In March, at the [2026 World Athletics Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) held in Toruń in Poland, Kerr won the world [indoor 3000 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Athletics_Indoor_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_3000_metres) title for the second time.[^cite_note-49] That same month, Kerr announced that he would make an attempt at the world record in the mile race at the [London Diamond League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Athletics_Meet) meet in July later that year. At the time, Kerr stated, "It needs to be a legal attempt, not a time trial. That was number one. Second, it needs to be in the UK. And third, I wanted to commit my season around it because this isn't a record that you can steal."[^cite_note-50] Prior to the record attempt, Kerr raced in only two 800 m races in the following months, focusing on training for his record attempt.[^cite_note-51] For five weeks prior to the record attempt, he had been [training at altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_training) in Albuquerque, at 1,626 metres (5,335 ft) of elevation.[^cite_note-TA1-52] In the build-up to his world record attempt, Kerr completed a 1200 m time trial in 2\:42.45.[^cite_note-53]

On 18 July, Kerr broke [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md)'s [mile world record of 3\:43.13](854191.md) at the [London Diamond League](83722177.md), clocking 3\:42.66, 2.68 seconds faster than his prior personal best.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-MH1-56] El Guerrouj had set the previous record in 1999, and the 27-year period between world records was the longest in the men's mile run world record progression.[^cite_note-MH1-56] Kerr called his attempt "Project 222", referring to running a mile in 222 seconds or 3 minutes and 42 seconds. He used a custom, [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics)–compliant pair of spikes and a speed suit during the record attempt.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] Kerr was assisted by two [pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker_\(running\)) in the attempt, [Brannon Kidder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brannon_Kidder) through 800 m and [Žan Rudolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDan_Rudolf) through 1000 m.[^cite_note-MH1-56][^cite_note-TA1-52] Kidder had trained extensively with Kerr before the attempt and was a teammate with him in the Brooks Beasts Track Club, whereas Rudolf filled in after a previously planned pacemaker withdrew, and had one day of training with Kerr before the race.[^cite_note-TA1-52] [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics) awarded Kerr US\$50,000 (approximately £37,000) for the world record performance, the same amount that El Guerrouj received in 1999, to which Kerr said, "It was a good day for me financially but \$50,000 is a ridiculous number. To get the same amount 27 years later is appalling, not to sound greedy."[^cite_note-59] En route to the world record, Kerr ran a new [British national record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_records_in_athletics) of 3\:27.62 for his 1500 m split.[^cite_note-60]

At the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Commonwealth_Games) held in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), Kerr won gold in the mile. In a slow and tactical race, Kerr was able to pull away from second-placed [Cameron Myers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Myers) and Scottish compatriot [Jake Wightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Wightman) over the final 200 m to secure the gold medal infront of a home Scottish crowd.[^cite_note-61] Following the race, Kerr stated "It means everything, this was the championships of my season, this was the one that we had circled on the calendar. I owed the British crowd that."[^cite_note-62]

## Personal life

Kerr and his wife Larimar reside in [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico). He enjoys listening to audiobook podcasts.[^cite_note-\:0-63]

Kerr's team includes a personal chef, nutritionist, physiotherapist, chiropractor, and strength and conditioning experts.[^cite_note-\:0-63]
