The **Gold Coast**, also known by its initials, **GC**, is a coastal city and region in the state of [Queensland](59051.md), Australia,[^cite_note-3] located approximately 66 kilometres (41 mi) south-southeast of the centre of the [state capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Australian_capital_cities), [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane). It is on the central eastern coast of Australia adjoining the Pacific Ocean. It is Queensland's [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Queensland_by_population) after Brisbane, as well as Australia's [sixth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Australia_by_population) and the most populous non-capital city.[^cite_note-4] The city's [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_central_business_district) is located roughly in the centre of the Gold Coast in the suburb of [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_Queensland).[^cite_note-5] The urban area of the Gold Coast is concentrated along the coast, sprawling between 60–70 kilometres, joining up with the Greater Brisbane region to the north and the state border with [New South Wales](21654.md) to the south.[^cite_note-6] Nicknames of the city include the 'Glitter Strip' and the 'Goldy'. The [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym) of a Gold Coast resident is *Gold Coaster*.

The area that became the Gold Coast was originally inhabited by the indigenous [Yugambeh people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugambeh_people). The city grew from a collection of small townships, the earliest being [Nerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang,_Queensland) in 1865. From the 1920s onwards, tourism led to significant economic growth in the region, and by 1959 the Gold Coast was declared a city, with its [first high-rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinkabool) being built in 1960. The Gold Coast boomed from the 1980s onwards with extensive [skyscraper construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_on_the_Gold_Coast). This era was defined by the city's 'white-shoe brigade' developers, [neon lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_lighting), and [organised crime](17588523.md), particularly the [yakuza](44774.md) and the [Russian mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_mafia).[^cite_note-7] The late 20th century saw the city's tourism diversify with theme park openings, and in the early 21st century became an international destination for film production.

The Gold Coast has a diverse economy with strengths in health, tourism, arts and culture, and construction, with a GDP of [AU\$](81215.md)55.1 billion as of 2025.[^cite_note-8] The city ranks highly as one of the country's cultural and creative hotspots,[^cite_note-9] alongside [content creators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_economy),[^cite_note-10] a growing [video games industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_Australia),[^cite_note-11] and leads Australia in [startups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) per capita.[^cite_note-12] The Gold Coast is central to the nation's entertainment industry with a [major film and television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_on_the_Gold_Coast) production industry, leading to the city's metonym of [Goldywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Australia).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The Gold Coast is also host of the [AACTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AACTA_Awards), the [Asia Pacific Screen Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Pacific_Screen_Awards), and the [Gold Coast Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Film_Festival).

The Gold Coast is a major [tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_destination) with a sunny, [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate) and has become widely known for its [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing) [beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaches) (such as [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise,_Queensland)), high-rise buildings, [theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_parks), [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife), and [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_hinterland).

## History

Main article: [History of the Gold Coast, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Gold_Coast,_Queensland)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Gold Coast, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Gold_Coast,_Queensland).

The Gold Coast is the ancestral home of a number of Indigenous clans of the Yugambeh people,[^cite_note-15] including the [Kombumerri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kombumerri_clan),[^cite_note-16] [Mununjali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mununjali_clan),[^cite_note-17] and Wangerriburra clans. Europeans arrived in 1823 when explorer [John Oxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Oxley) explored the Tweed River.[^cite_note-18] The hinterland's timber supply attracted people to the area in the mid-19th century.[^cite_note-19]



Map of the Gold Coast, 1887

A number of small townships developed along the coast and in the hinterland. The western suburb of [Nerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang) was surveyed and established as a base for the industry and by 1870 a town reserve had been set aside.[^cite_note-20] By 1873, the town reserve of [Burleigh Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burleigh_Heads) had also been surveyed and successful land sales had taken place.[^cite_note-21] In 1875, the small settlement opposite the boat passage at the head of the [Nerang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang_River), known as Nerang Heads or Nerang Creek Heads, was surveyed and renamed [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_Queensland), with the first land sales scheduled to take place in Beenleigh.[^cite_note-22] [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_Queensland) quickly grew a reputation as a secluded holiday destination for wealthy [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) residents.[^cite_note-23]



Southport Esplanade in 1928

Post-World War One Era saw the rise of the "seaside shack". The seaside shack provided the opportunity for the coastal "getaway" with modest investment. From 1914 to 1946, they popped up all along the South Coast. Seaside shacks were exceedingly cheap and were an early use of the concept of recycling. Many were built of disused or second grade timber, all kinds of materials were used for the holiday seaside shack – including fibro cement, metal containers, and left-over farm sheds; even disused trams were sold off as seaside shacks.[^cite_note-24]



Surfers Paradise, 1951

&#x20;After the establishment of the [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise) Hotel in the late 1920s, the Gold Coast region grew significantly.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] The Gold Coast was originally known as the South Coast (because it was south of Brisbane). However, over-inflated prices for real estate and other goods and services led to the nickname of "Gold Coast" from 1950.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] South Coast locals initially considered the name "Gold Coast" derogatory.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] However, soon the "Gold Coast" simply became a convenient way to refer to the holiday strip from Southport to [Coolangatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolangatta).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] The Town of South Coast was formed through the amalgamation of [Town of Coolangatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_Coolangatta) and [Town of Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_Southport) along with the coastal areas (such as Burleigh Heads) from the [Shire of Nerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Nerang) on 17 June 1949,[^cite_note-39] with the effect of having the present-day Gold Coast coastal strip as a single local government area. As the tourism industry grew into the 1950s, local businesses began to adopt the term *Gold Coast* in their names, and on 23 October 1958 the [Town of South Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_South_Coast) was renamed [Town of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_of_Gold_Coast).[^cite_note-40] The area was proclaimed a city, less than one year later on 16 May 1959.[^cite_note-41]



Cars on the [Gold Coast Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Highway), in 1961

The area boomed in the 1980s as a leading tourist destination. In 1994, the [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast) [local government area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Australia) was expanded to include the [Shire of Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Albert).[^cite_note-42]

In 2007, the Gold Coast overtook the population of [Newcastle, New South Wales](52665.md), to become the sixth largest city in Australia and the largest non-capital city.[^cite_note-43]

The Gold Coast hosted the [2018 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Commonwealth_Games).[^cite_note-CWHost-44]

## Demographics

In the [2016 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_census), the urban area of the Gold Coast had a population of 540,559 people.[^cite_note-Census2016-45] According to the [2016 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_census), the population of the Gold Coast including rural areas was 569,997. The median age was 39 years old, 1 year older than the nationwide median. The male-to-female ratio was 48.6-to-51.4.[^cite_note-abs2016pop-46] The most commonly nominated ancestries were English (29.3%), Australian (22.5%), Irish (8.2%), Scottish (7.5%), and German (3.6%). 64% of people were born in Australia, while the other most common countries of birth were New Zealand (7.9%), England (5.2%), China (1.2%), South Africa (1.2%), and Japan (0.7%). Indigenous Australians accounted for 1.7% of the population.[^cite_note-abs2016pop-46] The most commonly spoken languages other than English were Mandarin (1.6%), Japanese (1.0%), Korean (0.6%), Spanish (0.6%), and Cantonese (0.5%).

According to the Australian Official Census in 2021, the most common religious affiliations in Gold Coast reported were none (43.4%), Catholic (18.2%), Anglican (11.9%), Uniting Church (2.9%), Non-denominational Christian (3.1%), Presbyterian and Reformed (1.9%), Buddhism (1.4%), Pentecostal (1.3%), Baptist (1.1%), Hinduism (1.1%) and Islam (1.0%).[^cite_note-\:1-47]

## Geography

The Gold Coast is approximately half covered by forests of various types. This includes small patches of near-pristine ancient rainforest, mangrove-covered islands, and patches of coastal heathlands and farmland with areas of uncleared eucalyptus forest. Of the plantation pine forests that were planted in the 1950s and 1960s, when commercial forest planting for tax minimisation was encouraged by the Commonwealth government, tiny remnants remain.[^cite_note-48] Most of the Gold Coast area was covered by forest prior to European human settlement and was exposed to extensive land clearing in the 19th century.[^cite_note-49]

Gold Coast City lies in the southeast corner of [Queensland](59051.md), to the south of [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane), the state capital. The [Albert River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_River,_Queensland) separates the Gold Coast from [Logan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_City,_Queensland), a local government area south of the [City of Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Brisbane).

&#x20;Gold Coast City stretches from the Albert River, [Logan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_River), and [Southern Moreton Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Moreton_Bay_Islands_\(Redland_City\)) to the border with [New South Wales](21654.md) (NSW) approximately 56 km (35 mi) south, and extends from the coast west to the foothills of the [Great Dividing Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Dividing_Range) in [World Heritage](44940.md) listed [Lamington National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamington_National_Park).



Gold Coast skyline seen from Coolangatta

The southernmost town of Gold Coast City, [Coolangatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolangatta,_Queensland), includes [Point Danger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Danger_\(Tweed_Heads\)) and its [lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse). Coolangatta is a twin city with [Tweed Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_Heads) located directly across the NSW border. At [28°10′00″S 153°33′00″E  /  28.1667°S 153.55°E  / -28.1667; 153.55](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Gold_Coast,_Queensland\&params=28.1667_S_153.55_E_region\:AU-QLD), this is the most easterly point on the Queensland mainland (Point Lookout on the offshore island of [North Stradbroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Stradbroke_Island) is slightly further east). From Coolangatta, approximately forty kilometres of holiday resorts and surfing beaches stretch north to the suburb of Main Beach, and then further on Stradbroke Island. The suburbs of [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_Queensland) and [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise) form the Gold Coast's commercial centre. The major river in the area is the [Nerang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang_River). Much of the land between the coastal strip and the hinterland were once [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands) drained by this river, but the swamps have been converted into man-made waterways (over 260 kilometres (160 mi) in length[^cite_note-50] or over 9 times the length of the canals of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), Italy) and artificial islands covered in upmarket homes. The heavily developed coastal strip sits on a narrow barrier [sandbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbar) between these waterways and the sea.

To the west, the city borders a part of the [Great Dividing Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Dividing_Range) commonly referred to as the [Gold Coast hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_hinterland). A 206 km<sup>2</sup> (80 sq mi) section of the mountain range is protected by [Lamington National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamington_National_Park) and has been listed as a [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage) area in recognition of its "outstanding geological features displayed around shield volcanic craters and the high number of rare and threatened rainforest species".[^cite_note-UNESCO-51] The area attracts [bushwalkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushwalking) and day-trippers. Important rainforest pollinating and seed-dispersing Black flying foxes (Pteropus alecto) are found in the area and may be heard foraging at night.[^cite_note-52]

### Waterways

Waterfront canal living is a feature of the Gold Coast. Most canal frontage homes have pontoons. The [Gold Coast Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Seaway), between [The Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport_Spit,_Queensland) and [South Stradbroke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Stradbroke_Island), allows vessels direct access to the Pacific Ocean from [The Broadwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Broadwater) and many of the city's canal estates. Breakwaters on either side of the Seaway prevent [longshore drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longshore_drift) and the bar from silting up. A sand pumping operation on the Spit pipes sand under the Seaway to continue this natural process.

Residential canals were first built in the Gold Coast in the 1950s and construction continues. Most canals are extensions to the Nerang River, but there are more to the south along [Tallebudgera Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallebudgera_Creek) and [Currumbin Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currumbin_Creek) and to the north along the [Gold Coast Broadwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Broadwater), [South Stradbroke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Stradbroke_Island), [Coomera River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coomera_River) and southern Moreton Bay. Early canals included Florida Gardens and [Isle of Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Capri,_Queensland) which were under construction at the time of a 1954 flood.[^cite_note-53] Recently constructed canals include Harbour Quays and Riverlinks completed in 2007. There are over 890 kilometres (550 mi) of constructed residential waterfront land within the city that is home to over 80,000 residents.

**Beach safety and management**

The Gold Coast has Australia's largest[^cite_note-55] professional [surf lifesaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_lifesaving) service to protect people on the beaches and to promote surf safety throughout the community. The [Queensland Department of Primary Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Department_of_Primary_Industries) carries out the Queensland Shark Control Program (SCP) to protect swimmers from sharks.[^cite_note-56] Sharks are caught by using nets and baited drumlines off the major swimming beaches. Even with the SCP, sharks do range within sight of the [patrolled beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_patrol). [Lifeguards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeguard) will clear swimmers from the water if it is considered that there is a safety risk.

Gold Coast beaches have experienced periods of severe [beach erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion). In 1967, a series of 11 cyclones removed most of the sand from Gold Coast beaches. The [Government of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Queensland) engaged engineers from [Delft University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University) in the Netherlands to advise what to do about the beach erosion. The Delft Report[^cite_note-57] was published in 1971 and outlined a series of works for Gold Coast Beaches including [Gold Coast Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Seaway),[^cite_note-58] works at [Narrow Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_Neck,_Queensland) that resulted in the Northern Gold Coast Beach Protection Strategy[^cite_note-59] and works at the [Tweed River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_River_\(New_South_Wales\)) that became the [Tweed River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_River_\(New_South_Wales\)) Entrance Sand Bypassing Project.[^cite_note-60]

By 2005 most of the recommendations of the 1971 Delft Report had been implemented. [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast) commenced implementation of the [Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach,_Queensland) Protection Strategy[^cite_note-61] but ran into considerable opposition from the community participating in a NO REEF protest campaign.[^cite_note-62] The [City of Gold Coast Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast_Council) then committed to completing a review of beach management practices to update the Delft Report. The [Gold Coast Shoreline Management Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Shoreline_Management_Plan)[^cite_note-63] will be delivered by organisations including the [Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Protection_Agency_\(Queensland\)), [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast) and the Griffith Centre for Coastal Management. Gold Coast City is also investing into the quality and capacity of the [Gold Coast Oceanway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Oceanway) that provides [sustainable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transport) along Gold Coast beaches.[^cite_note-64]

### Climate

The Gold Coast experiences a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *[Cfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate)*), with mild to warm winters and hot, humid summers. The city experiences substantial summer precipitation mostly concentrated in thunderstorms and heavy showers with rain events occasionally lasting up to a few weeks at a time,[^cite_note-65] while winter is pleasantly mild to warm with little rain. In fact, it is for this pleasant winter weather that both the city and the [Sunshine Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Coast,_Queensland)—the coastal region north of Brisbane—are internationally renowned. Extreme temperatures recorded at [Gold Coast Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Seaway) have ranged from 2.5 °C (36 °F) on 19 July 2007 to 40.5 °C (105 °F) on 22 February 2005, although the city rarely experiences temperatures above 35 °C (95 °F) in summer or below 5 °C (41 °F) in winter.[^cite_note-66] The average temperature of the sea at [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise) ranges from 21.5 °C (70.7 °F) in July and August to 27.1 °C (80.8 °F) in February.[^cite_note-67]

## Government

Main articles: [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast) and [Government of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Queensland)

Administratively, the Gold Coast is a local government area called the [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast). The City of Gold Coast Council has 14 elected councillors, each representing a division of the city. Businessman Tom Tate is the current [Mayor of the Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_the_Gold_Coast), first elected in 2012. Former mayors include [Ron Clake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Clarke), Gary Baildon, [Lex Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Bell), [Ray Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Stevens_\(politician\)), Ern Harley and [Sir Bruce Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Small), who was responsible for the development of many of the canal estates that are now home to thousands of Gold Coast residents.

At the state level, the Gold Coast area is represented by eleven members in the [Legislative Assembly of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Queensland). The seats they hold are: [Bonney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Bonney), [Broadwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Broadwater), [Burleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Burleigh), [Coomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Coomera), [Currumbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Currumbin), [Gaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Gaven), [Mermaid Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Mermaid_Beach), [Mudgeeraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Mudgeeraba), [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Southport), [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Surfers_Paradise) and [Theodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Theodore). Federally, the Gold Coast area is split between five divisions in the [House of Representatives](61565.md): [Fadden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Fadden) (northern), [Moncrieff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Moncrieff) (central) and [McPherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_McPherson) (southern) are located entirely within the Gold Coast, while [Forde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Forde) (north-west) and [Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Wright) (south-west) encompass parts of the Gold Coast and other areas of Southeast Queensland.



[Southport Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport_Courthouse)

Politically, the Gold Coast has often tilted conservative.[^cite_note-71] It was a [Country Party](21927.md) bastion for most of the first three decades after World War II, but increasing urbanisation has made it a [Liberal](18453.md) stronghold. [Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) has historically only done well around Labrador and Coolangatta. Only one Labor MP has ever represented a significant portion of the Gold Coast at the federal level since 1949; the three Gold Coast divisions have only returned Liberals since 1984. At the state level, Labor was fairly competitive in the Gold Coast for most of the early part of the 21st century. However, as part of its massive landslide in the [2012 state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Queensland_state_election), the [Liberal National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_National_Party_of_Queensland) won every seat there. The LNP repeated its sweep of the Gold Coast seats at the [2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Queensland_state_election), and retained all but one Gold Coast seat at the [2017 state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Queensland_state_election).

[Southport Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport_Courthouse) is the city's major courthouse and has jurisdiction to hear petty criminal offences and civil matters up to A\$250,000. Indictable offences, criminal sentencing and civil matters above A\$250,000 are heard in the higher [Supreme Court of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Queensland) which is located in Brisbane. There is also a subsidiary Magistrates Court, located at the southern suburb of [Coolangatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolangatta,_Queensland).

In 2013 a brawl between members of [Outlaw motorcycle gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_motorcycle_gangs_in_Australia) also called "bikies" who fought each other outside a [Broadbeach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadbeach) restaurant caused mass fear to restaurant patrons and police.[^cite_note-72] This led to the toughest anti-bikie laws introduced in Australia known as [Vicious Lawless Association Disestablishment Act 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicious_Lawless_Association_Disestablishment_Act_2013).

## Economy



Gold Coast CBD, 2011

In fifty years, Gold Coast City has grown from a small beachside holiday destination to Australia's sixth largest city (and the country's most populous non-capital city). Situated within South East Queensland's growth corridor, the Gold Coast is one of Australia's fastest growing large cities, with a 5-year annual average population growth rate to 2015 of 1.8%, compared to 1.5% nationally.[^cite_note-ABSSUA15-73] Gross Regional Product has risen from A\$9.7 billion in 2001, to A\$15.6 billion in 2008, a rise of 61 percent.[^cite_note-74] Tourism remains fundamental to Gold Coast City's economy, with almost 10 million visitors a year to the area.[^cite_note-75] In the past the economy was driven by the population derived industries of construction, tourism and retail. Some diversification has taken place, with the city now having an industrial base formed of marine, education, information communication and technology, food, tourism, creative, environment and sports industries. These nine industries have been identified as the key industries by the [City of Gold Coast Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast_Council) to deliver the city's economic prosperity. Gold Coast City's unemployment rate (5.6 per cent) is below the national level (5.9 per cent).[^cite_note-76] The declaration of Southport as the [Gold Coast central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_central_business_district) (CBD) and a Priority Development Area (PDA), as well as new investment into the CBD, is driving transformative change and creating new business and investment opportunities.

The Gold Coast Economic Development Strategy 2013–2023[^cite_note-77] outlines the framework for the city's long-term growth and prosperity. The strategy outlines actions in the following areas, Innovation, Culture, Infrastructure, Competitive business, Workforce, International.

### Tourism



The [hospitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality) industry contributes significantly to the Gold Coast's economy. The [Palazzo Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Versace_Australia) is a notable hotel on [Main Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Beach,_Queensland).

The Gold Coast is the most popular tourist destination in Queensland.[^cite_note-78] It is Australia's 5th most visited destination by international tourists.[^cite_note-79] Around 10 million tourists visit the Gold Coast area every year consisting of 849,114 international visitors, 3,468,000 domestic overnight visitors and 5,366,000 daytrip visitors. Tourism is the region's biggest industry,[^cite_note-80] directly contributing more than \$4.4 billion into the city economy every year and directly accounting for one in four jobs in the city[^cite_note-81] There are approximately 65,000 beds, 60 kilometres (37 mi) of beach, 600 kilometres (370 mi) of canal, 100,000 hectares of nature reserve, 500 restaurants, 40 golf courses and five major theme parks in the city.

[Gold Coast Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Airport) provides connection across Australia and internationally with airlines including [Jetstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetstar), [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas), [Air New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand), [Scoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoot), [Virgin Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Australia) and [Airasia X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airasia_X). [Brisbane Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Airport) is less than one hour from the centre of Gold Coast, and [direct trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_railway_line,_Brisbane) operate.



[Sea World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_World_\(Australia\)), one of many theme parks on the Gold Coast

Gold Coast City has over 13,000 available guest rooms contributing over \$335 million to the local economy each year. Accommodation options available range from [hostels](233996.md) to five star resorts and hotels. Tourist attractions include surf beaches. The big attractions are the numerous theme parks in the area. Theme parks include [Dreamworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamworld_\(Australian_theme_park\)), [Sea World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_World_\(Australia\)), [Wet'n'Wild Water World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet'n'Wild_Water_World), [Warner Bros. Movie World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Movie_World), [WhiteWater World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhiteWater_World), [Topgolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topgolf), [Currumbin Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currumbin_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [David Fleay Wildlife Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fleay_Wildlife_Park), [Australian Outback Spectacular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Outback_Spectacular?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Paradise Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Country).



[Q1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q1_\(building\)), the [tallest building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Australia) in Australia and the [world's tallest residential building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_residential_buildings_in_the_world) upon completion in 2005 (currently the [seventeenth tallest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_residential_buildings))

Since the opening of what was then the world's highest residential tower in 2005 (it is now the 17th highest), the [Q1 building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q1_\(building\)) has been a destination for tourists and locals alike. It is the second highest public vantage point in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere) after the [Eureka Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_Tower) in [Melbourne](17306237.md). The observation deck at level 77 is the highest of its kind in [Queensland](59051.md) and offers views in all directions, from [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) to [Byron Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Bay). It towers over the [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise) skyline, with the observation deck 230 metres (750 feet) high, and the spire extending nearly another hundred metres up. In total, the Q1 is 322.5 metres (1,058 feet) high, making it the [tallest building in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Australia). Another famous tourist attraction are the [Surfers Paradise Meter Maids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise_Meter_Maids), instituted in 1965 to put a positive spin on new parking regulations. To avoid tickets being issued for expired parking, the Meter Maids dispense coins into the meter and leave a calling card under the windscreen wiper of the vehicle. The Maids are still a part of the Surfers Paradise culture but the scheme is now run by private enterprise.

[Chinatown, Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Gold_Coast), is an integral part of the revitalisation of Southport as an international CBD.[^cite_note-82]

### Film production

&#x20;The Gold Coast is the major film production hub in Queensland and has accounted for 75%[^cite_note-83] of all film production in Queensland since the 1990s, with an expenditure of around \$150 million per year. The Gold Coast is the third largest film production centre in Australia, behind Sydney and Melbourne.[^cite_note-84]

The Queensland Government actively supports the film and television production industry in Queensland and provides both non-financial and financial assistance through the Pacific Film and Television Commission.[^cite_note-87] The Park used to be another film studio.

## Culture



[Home of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_of_the_Arts,_Gold_Coast)

The Gold Coast's culture has been affected by rapid development and traditional marketing programs orbiting around 'sun, sand, surf and sex.'[^cite_note-88]

Despite rapid socio-economic changes and a tourist-centred image, there is evidence of local resident-driven culture (such as [surf gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_culture#Surf_gangs)) in geographical pockets and a broader 'Gold Coaster' identity drawn from [globalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization#Cultural_globalization) resort and real estate marketing material.[^cite_note-89] The Gold Coast hosts cultural activities that attract tourists and residents alike.

### Music

Music groups in this region include the [Northern Rivers Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rivers_Symphony_Orchestra) and [Operator Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_Please). Musicians [Cody Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Simpson) and [Ricki-Lee Coulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricki-Lee_Coulter) are from the Gold Coast. Music events include [Big Day Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Day_Out), [Good Vibrations Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Vibrations_Festival?action=edit\&redlink=1), Summafieldayze, the Blues on Broadbeach Festival and [V Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_Festival_\(Australia\)) (2007–2009).

### Arts

[Home of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_of_the_Arts,_Gold_Coast) (HOTA) is the Gold Coast's premier cultural facility for visual and performing arts with a performance theatre, two cinemas and an underground venue. The theatre has hosted performance by the [Imperial Russian Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Ballet), [The Australian Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Australian_Ballet) and the [Queensland Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Ballet). Musicals, plays and a variety of performances are regularly scheduled. The city is also home to the [Gold Coast City Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_City_Art_Gallery). Film festivals and the Comedy Club host international artists. A redeveloped Gold Coast cultural precinct opened before the city hosted the [2018 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Commonwealth_Games).[^cite_note-90]

The Gold Coast has garnered a reputation as a "sporting graveyard", as many of the professional clubs that have represented the Gold Coast in national leagues since the 1980s experience generally poor on-field performances, consistently struggle to support themselves financially, and have generally folded within a decade of being founded; as of 2019 no Gold Coast-based team has won a premiership in a national professional club competition.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] However, The Gold Coast Suns entered their first [AFL Finals series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_finals_series) in 2025 but lost in the semi finals against cross town rivals [Brisbane Lions](5026.md) at [The Gabba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gabba).[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

### Commonwealth Games

### Olympic and Paralympic Games

Gold Coast will be one of the three zones for the [2032 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2032_Summer_Olympics) and [2032 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2032_Summer_Paralympics) in Brisbane to use the venues as the 2018 Commonwealth Games. The Gold Coast Zone will have seven venues, and will host nine Olympic and six Paralympic sports. The [Gold Coast Convention & Exhibition Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Convention_&_Exhibition_Centre) will be used for the preliminary Volleyball along with Powerlifting and Sitting Volleyball during the Paralympics. [Broadbeach Park Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadbeach,_Queensland) will host Beach Volleyball for Olympics as well in the Football 5-a-side in the Paralympics. The [Gold Coast Sports and Leisure Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Sports_and_Leisure_Centre) will be used for Judo and Wrestling in the Olympics and Boccia in the Paralympics. [Southport Broadwater Parklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport_Broadwater_Parklands) to be used for Triathlon and Marathon Swimming in the Olympics and will be used for Paratriathlon in the Paralympics. [Coomera Indoor Sports Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coomera_Indoor_Sports_Centre) will host the Volleyball for the Olympics and will be used for Wheelchair Rugby in the Paralympics. The [Robina Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robina_Stadium) will host the preliminary football matches. The [Carrara Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrara_Stadium) could potentially host the Cricket matches if the IOC approves cricket in the 2028 Olympic Games in Los Angeles.[^cite_note-113]

### Other events



[Gold Coast Indy 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Indy_300)

The [Gold Coast 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_500) (formerly known as the Gold Coast 600 & [Gold Coast Indy 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Indy_300)) is a car racing event held annually, usually in October. The [Surfers Paradise Street Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise_Street_Circuit) through the streets of [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise) and [Main Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Beach,_Queensland). The Gold Coast 500 typically is accompanied by a range of on and off track events such as the Indy Undie Ball, Miss Indy Competition and the King of Burleigh Hill [Billy cart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_cart) Race which take place in the week prior to and/or during the race weekend. Formerly an [IndyCar](30446232.md) event, [V8 Supercars](54162.md) are now the headline attraction, using a shorter track route, as the circuit was limited following the construction of the [Gold Coast Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G\:link) as the Light Rail line replaced the northbound traffic lanes on Surfers Paradise Boulevard which was previously used as the southern section of the street circuit.

Each June, Coolangatta hosts [Cooly Rocks On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooly_Rocks_On?action=edit\&redlink=1), a two-week 1950s and 1960s nostalgia festival with free entertainment and attractions, including [hot rods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_rod), restored cars and revival bands playing music of the era. Every July, more than 25,000 congregate on the Gold Coast from around the world to participate in the [Gold Coast Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Marathon). It is also the largest annual community sporting event held on the Gold Coast. In 2015, it will be held on 4–5 July and the 37th Gold Coast Airport Marathon is set to motivate and challenge more than 25,000 people of all ages and abilities. The [Gold Coast Airport Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Airport_Marathon) will feature an event for all ages and abilities, including the full Gold Coast Airport Marathon, ASICS Half Marathon, [Southern Cross University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cross_University) 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) Run, Suncorp Bank 5.7 kilometres (3.5 mi) Challenge, and Junior Dash over 4 and 2 kilometres (2.5 and 1.2 mi).

In August [Currumbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currumbin) hosts the annual half distance Challenge Gold Coast triathlon, with the 1.9 km swim taking place in the Currumbin River, the 90 km bike going through the Currumbin and [Tallebudgera Valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallebudgera_Valley) in the Hinterland, and the 21.1 km run going along the beach to Elephant Rock and [Tugun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugun,_Queensland).[^cite_note-114]

Late November to early December sees thousands of school leavers across the country descend on the Gold Coast for [Schoolies week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoolies_week), a two-week period of celebration and parties throughout Surfers Paradise, hosted by the [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast). The event is often criticised nationally and locally for its portrayal of drinking and acts of violence, however every effort by the [Queensland Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Police_Service) and State Government to ensure all school leavers have a good time are put into place, including locals volunteering by walking the streets and keeping an eye out for those in need of assistance.

Early each year the Gold Coast hosts one leg of the [ASP World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASP_World_Tour) of surfing, where some of the world's best surfers compete in the [Quiksilver Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiksilver_Pro) at Coolangatta.

[Home of the Arts, Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_of_the_Arts,_Gold_Coast) located in Evandale, features a fine art gallery featuring local and international works from painting to sculpture and new media. In addition, there is a theatre for live productions including musicals as well two arts cinemas showing foreign and independent films from Australia and abroad.

[Chinatown, Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Gold_Coast), located in Southport, hosts the annual citywide Lunar New Year festival as well as regular monthly events.[^cite_note-115]

## Infrastructure

### Utilities

**Electricity**

Electricity for the Gold Coast is sourced from Powerlink Queensland at bulk supply substations which is provided via the [National Electricity Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Electricity_Market) from an interconnected multi-State power system. The Government-owned electricity corporation [Energex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energex) distributes and retails electricity, natural gas, [liquefied petroleum gas](361384.md) (LPG) and value-added products and services to residential, industrial and commercial customers in South-East Queensland.



[Hinze Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinze_Dam)

**Water supply**

The [Hinze Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinze_Dam) 15 km (9.3 mi) southwest of Nerang is the population's main water supply. The [Little Nerang Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nerang_Dam) which feeds into [Hinze Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinze_Dam) can supplement part of the city area's water needs, and both are managed by the city council directorate [Gold Coast Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Water). Reforms of the way in which the [water industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_industry) is structured have been announced by the State Government, with transfer of ownership and management of water services from local government to the state occurring in 2008–09. [City of Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Gold_Coast) also sources water from [Wivenhoe Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wivenhoe_Dam), west of Brisbane for northern suburbs when the Hinze Dam, at one-tenth of Wivenhoe's capacity, becomes low.

Water shortage and water restrictions have been current local issues, and a few new Gold Coast residential areas have recently included dual reticulation in their planning and development to supply water from a new water recycling plant being built concurrently. This will make available highly treated recycled water for use around the home in addition to potable water. The Gold Coast has received world recognition for this scheme in its Pimpama-Coomera suburbs.[^cite_note-123]

Gold Coast Water has the capacity to supply up to 133 megalitres of desalinated water per day.[^cite_note-124]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport on the Gold Coast, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_on_the_Gold_Coast,_Queensland)

The car is the dominant mode of transport in the Gold Coast, with over 70% of people using it as their sole mode of travelling to work.[^cite_note-125] The Gold Coast has an extensive network of arterial roads that link coastal suburbs with inland suburbs. In recent years, local and state governments have invested money in transport infrastructure on the Gold Coast to combat the increasing congestion on many of the city's roads. The Gold Coast has an extensive public transport network that includes buses, heavy rail and light rail for commuting to work, visiting attractions, and travelling to other destinations.



The [Gold Coast Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Highway) and [G\:link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G\:link) crossing the [Nerang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang_River)

### Road

A number of major roads connect the Gold Coast with Brisbane, New South Wales, and the surrounding areas. The [Pacific Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Motorway_\(Brisbane%E2%80%93Brunswick_Heads\)) (M1) is the main motorway in the area. Beginning at the [Logan Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Motorway) (M6) in Brisbane, it travels through the inland Gold Coast region and links with the [Pacific Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Highway_\(Australia\)) at the New South Wales/Queensland border near [Tweed Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_Heads,_New_South_Wales). The Pacific Motorway is part of the [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) to [Sydney](27862.md) corridor. Before the [Tugun Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugun_Bypass) was completed in 2008, the motorway ended at [Tugun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugun,_Queensland). The [Gold Coast Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Highway) services the coastal suburbs of the Gold Coast, including Surfers Paradise, Southport, and Burleigh Heads. Starting at the Pacific Motorway at Tweed Heads, it runs parallel to the coast until it reaches Labrador, where it turns inland to meet the Pacific Motorway again at [Helensvale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensvale). Other arterial roads include the [Smith Street Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Street_Motorway) which connects Southport, Gold Coast's CBD with the [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Motorway_\(Brisbane%E2%80%93Brunswick_Heads\)) in [Parkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkwood,_Queensland). Other major roads include Reedy Creek Road, [Nerang–Broadbeach Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang%E2%80%93Broadbeach_Road), [Robina Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robina_Parkway) and [Southport–Burleigh Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport_%E2%80%93_Burleigh_Road).

### Light rail



[G\:link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G\:link) trams on Cypress Avenue

The Gold Coast's light rail service is called [G\:link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G\:link), a 20 km (12 mi) line between [Helensvale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensvale,_Queensland) and [Broadbeach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadbeach,_Queensland) that also connects the key activity centres of [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_Queensland) and [Surfers Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise,_Queensland). The G\:link was opened in 2014 between Broadbeach and Southport, with an extension to Helensvale completed in 2017 in preparation for the [2018 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Commonwealth_Games).[^cite_note-126]

### Heavy rail

[Queensland Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Rail) operates an inter-city rail service from Brisbane to the Gold Coast along the [Gold Coast railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_railway_line). The line follows the same route as Brisbane's [Beenleigh railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beenleigh_railway_line), continuing on after reaching [Beenleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beenleigh_railway_station). It then follows a route similar to that of the Pacific Motorway, passing stations at [Ormeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormeau_railway_station), [Coomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coomera_railway_station), [Helensvale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensvale_railway_station), [Nerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerang_railway_station) and [Robina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robina_railway_station) before terminating at [Varsity Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_Lakes_railway_station). An extension of the Gold Coast line to the Gold Coast Airport is proposed.[^cite_note-127]

### Bus



[Kinetic Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_Gold_Coast) buses operating in [Broadbeach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadbeach)

[Kinetic Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_Gold_Coast)[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] operates all public passenger services in the city under contract by [Translink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translink_\(Queensland\)) which coordinates the public transport network in [South East Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Queensland). Services are frequent during the day, with intervals being as little as 5 minutes. Kinetic operates over a fleet of over 400 buses operating on over 70 lines covering the entire city.

### Airports

The main international airport, [Gold Coast Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Airport) is located in Coolangatta, approximately 22 kilometres (14 mi) south of Surfers Paradise. Services are provided to interstate capitals and major cities as well as to New Zealand and Indonesia. In 2019, it was the [sixth busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Australia) in Australia.[^cite_note-130]

Smaller private airfields are located in the northern suburbs of [Coombabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coombabah) ([Southport Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport_Airport)) and [Norwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwell,_Queensland) ([Heck Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heck_Field)), catering to flight training, recreational and [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) users.

## Real estate

The Gold Coast's real estate market is one of the most dynamic in Australia, characterised by a combination of coastal luxury properties, residential developments, and investment opportunities. According to data from SQM Research,[^cite_note-135] the region has experienced significant property value growth in recent years, with both the median house and unit prices showing strong upward trends. The market's appeal is driven by its lifestyle offerings, proximity to major cities like Brisbane, and ongoing infrastructure improvements.
