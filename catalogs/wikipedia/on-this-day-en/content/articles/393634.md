**Emomali Rahmon**[^cite_note-2] (born **Emomali Sharipovich Rahmonov**;[^cite_note-4] 5 October 1952) is a Tajikistani politician who has served as the third [president of Tajikistan](163136.md) since 1994, having previously led the country as Chairman of the [Supreme Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Assembly_\(Tajikistan\)) from 1992 to 1994. Since 1998, he has also served as the leader of the [People's Democratic Party of Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Party_of_Tajikistan), which dominates the [Parliament of Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Tajikistan). On 30 September 1999, he was elected vice-president of the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) for a one-year term.

Born in [Danghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danghara) to a peasant family, he became a rising [apparatchik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparatchik) in Tajikistan as he became the chairman of the collective state farm of his native [Danghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danghara_District). According to his official biography, Rahmon graduated from the [Tajik State National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_National_University) with a specialist's degree in economics in 1982. After working for several years in the Danghara [Sovkhoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovkhoz), Rahmon was appointed chairman of the sovkhoz in 1987 and later as a people’s deputy to the [Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet) for the [Tajik SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-Emomali_Rahmon-6]

He became better known in 1992 after the abolition of the presidency amidst the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistani_Civil_War), when he became Chairman of the Supreme Soviet (Parliament) of Tajikistan as a compromise candidate between [communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) and [neo-communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-communist) on one side and [liberal-democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal-democratic), [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist) and [Islamist](15012.md) forces (the [United Tajik Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Tajik_Opposition)) on the other. Rahmon has won five undemocratic presidential elections. In addition, he extended his powers via constitutional referendums in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Tajik_constitutional_referendum) and [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Tajik_constitutional_referendum). Following the results of another constitutional referendum in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Tajik_constitutional_referendum), amendments were adopted that removed presidential [term limits](505825.md).[^cite_note-7]

Rahmon heads an [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) government in Tajikistan. Political opponents are repressed, violations of human rights and freedoms are severe, elections are not free and fair, and [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Tajikistan) and [nepotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepotism) are rampant.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Various important government positions are occupied by his family members, such as his 38-year-old son [Rustam Emomali](50647638.md), who is the chairman of the country's parliament and the mayor of its capital city, [Dushanbe](56658.md).[^cite_note-10]

## Early life

Rahmon was born as Emomali Sharipovich Rakhmonov[^cite_note-11] to Sharif Rahmonov (c. 1912–1992)[^cite_note-12] and Mayram Sharifova (1910–2004),[^cite_note-13] a peasant family in [Danghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danghara),[^cite_note-Тарҷумаи_Ҳоли_Эмомалии_Раҳмон-14] [Kulob Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulab_Region) (present-day [Khatlon Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatlon_Region)). His father was a [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) veteran of [World War II](32927.md) and recipient of the [Order of Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Glory) in the 2nd and 3rd degrees.[^cite_note-Био-15] From 1971 to 1974, Rahmon served in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)'s [Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_\(Russia\)), during which he was stationed in the [Primorsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai). After completing military service, Rahmon returned to his native village, where he worked for some time as an electrician.[^cite_note-16]

As a rising [apparatchik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparatchik) in Tajikistan, he became the chairman of the collective state farm of his native [Danghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danghara_District). According to his official biography, Rahmon graduated from the [Tajik State National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_National_University) with a specialist's degree in economics in 1982. After working for several years in the Danghara [Sovkhoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovkhoz), Rahmon was appointed chairman of the sovkhoz in 1987.[^cite_note-\:1-5]

## Early politics

In 1990, Rahmon was elected a people's deputy to the [Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet) of the [Tajik SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).[^cite_note-Emomali_Rahmon-6] President [Rahmon Nabiyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahmon_Nabiyev) was forced to resign in the first months of the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistani_Civil_War) in August 1992. [Akbarsho Iskandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbarsho_Iskandrov), Speaker of the Supreme Soviet, became acting president. Iskandarov resigned in November 1992 in an attempt to end the [civil unrest](329906.md). That same month, the Supreme Soviet met in [Khujand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khujand) for its 16th session and declared Tajikistan a parliamentary republic. Rahmon was then elected by the members of the Supreme Soviet as its chairman (as the parliamentary republican system adopted by Tajikistan did not provide for a ceremonial president, he was also head of state) and the head of government.[^cite_note-Emomali_Rahmon-6] Former Interior Minister [Yaqub Salimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqub_Salimov) later recalled that Rahmon's appointment was made because he was "nondescript", as other field commanders thought that he could be cast aside "when he had served his purpose."[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Presidency (1994–present)



Rahmon with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), 2000

In 1994, a new constitution re-established the presidency. Rahmon was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Tajik_presidential_election) to the post on 6 November 1994 and sworn in ten days later. During the civil war that lasted from 1992 to 1997, Rahmon's rule was opposed by the [United Tajik Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Tajik_Opposition). As many as 100,000 people died during the war. He survived an assassination attempt on 30 April 1997 in Khujand,[^cite_note-19] as well as two attempted [coups](25914154.md) in August 1997 and November 1998. He was [re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Tajik_presidential_election) on 6 November 1999 to a seven-year term following constitutional changes, officially taking 97% of the vote.



Tajik president with Iranian president [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) and supreme leader [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), January 2006

On 22 June 2003, a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Tajik_constitutional_referendum) was passed, allowing him to run for two more consecutive seven-year terms after his term expired in 2006. Rahmon was re-elected for a seven-year term in a [controversial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Tajik_presidential_election) on 6 November 2006, with about 79% of the vote, according to official results.

In a [cable dispatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_cable) that was [leaked in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak), the [U.S. embassy in Dushanbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Dushanbe) reported that Tajikistan's [largest bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agroinvestbank) was controlled by Rahmon and his family.[^cite_note-20] The same leaked diplomatic cable also contains [confidential](3539962.md#Confidential) marked information on how the Tajik president [diverts to himself](209411.md) most of the revenues from the country's biggest industries, [hydroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Tajikistan#Energy) and [exported aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_Aluminium_Company).[^cite_note-21]

On 6 November 2013, he was [re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Tajik_presidential_election) for more seven years in office with about 84% of the vote, in an election process which the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) said lacked "genuine choice and meaningful pluralism".[^cite_note-22]

In December 2015, a law passed by Tajikistan's parliament gave Rahmon the title "Founder of Peace and National Unity – Leader of the Nation" ([Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_language): Асосгузори сулҳу ваҳдати миллӣ – Пешвои миллат, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Tajik): *Asosguzori sulhu vahdati millî – Peshvo'i millat*).[^cite_note-23] The "Leader of the Nation" component is used frequently as a shorter title. In addition to granting Rahmon lifelong immunity from prosecution, the law also granted him many other lifelong privileges, including veto power over all major state decisions, the freedom to address the nation and parliament on all matters he deems important, and the privilege of attending all government meetings and parliamentary sessions.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

On 22 May 2016, a [nationwide referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Tajik_constitutional_referendum) approved a number of changes to the country's [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Tajikistan).[^cite_note-26] Among the main changes were the removal of presidential term limits for Rahmon, a ban on religious political parties such as the [Islamic Renaissance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Renaissance_Party_of_Tajikistan), and the reduction of the minimum eligibility age for presidential candidates from 35 to 30, enabling Rahmon's older son, [Rustam Emomali](50647638.md), to run for president any time after 2017.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The conduct for the referendum has been questioned, as [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) had claimed the Tajik government had been "blocking", "intimidating" and "threatening" independent media just before it.[^cite_note-dw-29] In January 2017, Rustam was appointed Mayor of Dushanbe, the country's largest city and capital.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In November 2018, Rahmon relaunched a hydroelectric station project to solve energy problems.[^cite_note-34] In October 2020, he was once again [re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Tajik_presidential_election) as president for a fifth term with a margin of 90.92%, amid fraud allegations.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] In July 2021, over 1,000 Afghan troops and civilians fled to Tajikistan after [Taliban](30635.md) insurgents [took control of many parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) of [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-37] In response, Rahmon ordered 20,000 reserve servicemen of the country's [Ground Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_Ground_Forces) to be sent to the [Afghan–Tajik border](58437163.md).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

### Religious policy



Rahmon with Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) in [Baku](4566.md), December 2012



U.S. Secretary of State [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) with Rahmon, 2015

Rahmon is a [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and has frequently stressed his Muslim background even though his administration has suppressed public display of Islamic devotion.[^cite_note-40] The government imposed bans on beards, attendance at [mosque](19894.md) for women and children under 18, [hajj](23487999.md) for people under 40, studying in Islamic schools outside [Tajikistan](30108.md), the production, import or export of Islamic books without permission, using loudspeakers to broadcast the [adhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhan), [veils](68301.md), [madrassas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa), Islamist political parties, and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic)-sounding names. Furthermore, mosques are heavily regulated; providing unofficial Islamic teaching can lead to up to 12 years of imprisonment, and an arduous process is required to obtain a permit to establish an Islamic organisation, publish an Islamic book, or go on pilgrimage to Mecca.[^cite_note-41] In January 2016, Rahmon performed an [Umrah](1591421.md) with a number of his children and senior members of his government. It was his fourth pilgrimage to Mecca.[^cite_note-42]

&#x20;His reply to critics of the election standards of the 2006 Tajikistani presidential election was:&#x20;

> "In Tajikistan, more than 99 percent of those residing here are Muslim. We have a completely different culture. You have to take that into account".[^cite_note-43]



Rahmon with Putin and other post-Soviet leaders at the [2023 Moscow Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade)

During a 2010 [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) session in [Dushanbe](56658.md), Rahmon spoke against what he called the misuse of Islam for political ends, claiming that "Terrorism, terrorists, have no nation, no country, no religion... Using the name 'Islamic terrorism' only discredits Islam and dishonors the pure and harmless religion of Islam."[^cite_note-44]

Membership in [Hizb ut-Tahrir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizb_ut-Tahrir), a militant Islamic party that aims to overthrow secular governments and unify Muslims under one Islamic state, is illegal and members are subject to arrest and imprisonment.[^cite_note-45] The [Islamic Renaissance Party of Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Renaissance_Party_of_Tajikistan) (IRP) is a banned [Islamist](15012.md) political party and has been designated a [terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization) since 2015.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

In 2017, the government of Tajikistan passed a law requiring people to "stick to traditional national clothes and culture", which has been widely seen as an attempt to prevent women from wearing Islamic clothing, in particular the style of headscarf wrapped under the chin that is in contrast to the traditional Tajik headscarf tied behind the head.[^cite_note-48]

By 2024, the [Salafi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) and [polygamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygyny_in_Islam) became popular in Tajikistan, despite both officially being banned by the government.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### Economic policy

Under Rahmon, the state intervenes in the economy, which a focus more on state-led investment rather than private investment.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] In the late 1990s, the economic system of Tajikistan under Rahmon was described as [statist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statism#Economic_statism).[^cite_note-53]

## Personal life

### Family



Emomali Rahmon with his family, 2011

He is married to [Azizmo Asadullayeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azizmo_Asadullayeva) and has nine children.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Two of his children, [Rustam Emomali](50647638.md) and [Ozoda Rahmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozoda_Rahmon), are senior officials in his administration,[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] while another, Zarina Rahmon, was appointed deputy head of [Orienbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orienbank) in January 2017.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Rustam is widely believed to be his father's successor.[^cite_note-60]

Rahmon had a sister who reportedly died in a hospital of [COVID-19](63030231.md) on 20 July 2021. According to local media, her sons physically assaulted the national [health minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_\(Tajikistan\)) and a senior doctor.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

One of his grandchildren reportedly purchased a luxury three-bedroom beachfront apartment on [Dubai](211583.md)'s [Palm Jumeirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Jumeirah) when he was just nine years old. The property, now valued at over \$1.3 million, continues to generate approximately \$55,000 in annual rental income.[^cite_note-63]

### Name changes

In March 2007, Rahmonov changed his surname to *Rahmon*, dropping the [Russian-style "-ov" ending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Slavic_naming_customs).[^cite_note-64] He also removed the patronymic, *Sharipovich*, from his name altogether. Rahmon explained that he had done so out of respect for his cultural heritage.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Following the move, scores of government officials, including members of parliament and civil servants, also removed Russian-style patronymics and "-ov" endings from their surnames. In April 2016, Tajikistan officially banned giving Russian-style patronymics and surnames to newborn Tajik children,[^cite_note-67] with children from minority and mixed families retaining the right to their traditional surnames.[^cite_note-68]
