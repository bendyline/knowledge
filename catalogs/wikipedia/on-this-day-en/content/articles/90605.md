**Haute-Savoie** (French pronunciation: [\[ot savwa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/74/Fr-Paris--Haute-Savoie.ogg/Fr-Paris--Haute-Savoie.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/74/Fr-Paris--Haute-Savoie.ogg/Fr-Paris--Haute-Savoie.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; **Upper Savoy**)[^cite_note-3] is a [department](38523.md) in the [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](45093325.md) [region](58846.md) of Southeastern [France](5843419.md), bordering both [Switzerland](26748.md) and [Italy](14532.md). Its [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) is [Annecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annecy). To the north is [Lake Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Geneva); to the south and southeast are [Mont Blanc](44768.md) and the [Aravis mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravis_Range).

It holds its name from the [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy) historical region, as does the department of [Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoie), located south of Haute-Savoie. In 2023, it had a population of 861,158.[^cite_note-pophist-4] Its [subprefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefectures_in_France) are [Bonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonneville,_Haute-Savoie), [Saint-Julien-en-Genevois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Julien-en-Genevois) and [Thonon-les-Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thonon-les-Bains). The French entrance to the [Mont Blanc Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Blanc_Tunnel) into Italy is in Haute-Savoie. It is noted for winter sports; the first [Winter Olympic Games](34069.md) were held at [Chamonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamonix) in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Winter_Olympics).

## History

See also: [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy)

The historical region of [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy) was governed by the [House of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy), the ruling dynasty of Savoy from 1032 to 1860. The [Dukes of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukes_of_Savoy) were rulers of the Savoy region from 1416 to 1720.

The territory occupied by modern Haute-Savoie and the adjoining department of [Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoie) became part of the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia) after the [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht) in 1713. Annexation of the region by [France](5843419.md) was formalised in the [Treaty of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turin_\(1860\)) on March 24, 1860. Most of it was the [Neutralized Zone of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutralized_Zone_of_Savoy).

From November 1942 to September 1943, Haute-Savoie was subjected to [military occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_occupation_of_France) by [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy_\(1922-1943\)). The [Maquis des Glières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquis_des_Gli%C3%A8res) (a band of [Free French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquis_\(World_War_II\)) fighters who opposed the [Nazi](21212.md), [Vichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) and [Milice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milice) regimes during [World War II](32927.md)) operated from Haute-Savoie. In the winter of 1943–1944, German troops burned down around 500 farms in response to French Resistance activities.

## Geography

Gray map of former Rhône-Alpes region, with Haute-Savoie in brown

Haute-Savoie highlighted in brown in the former Rhône-Alpes region, with arrondissements outlined

Aerial landscape of Haute-Savoie near the A40 motorway

Haute-Savoie comprises [four arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_the_Haute-Savoie_department), divided into [279 communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Haute-Savoie_department) and [17 cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_the_Haute-Savoie_department).[^cite_note-6] To the north, it borders the Swiss [canton of Geneva](292834.md) and [Lake Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Geneva); to the east the Swiss [canton of Valais](198789.md) and Italy's [Aosta Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aosta_Valley); to the west the French department of [Ain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain); and to the south the department of [Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoie).

Haute-Savoie has the largest range of elevations of all the departments in France; the lowest point is 250 metres (820 ft) in the [Rhône Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne), and the highest [Mont Blanc](44768.md) at 4,810.40 metres (15,782.2 ft).[^cite_note-IFN-7]<sup>: 9 </sup> Some of the world's best-known ski resorts are in Haute-Savoie.

The terrain of the department includes the Alpine [Mont Blanc range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Blanc_massif); the [French Prealps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Prealps) of the [Aravis Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravis_Range), the [Chablais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chablais_Alps), Bornes and Bauges Alps; and the [peneplains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peneplain) of Genevois haut-savoyard and [Albanais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanais) (known collectively as *L'Avant-pays savoyard*).[^cite_note-IFN-7]<sup>: 9 </sup> Its mountainous terrain makes [mountain passes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_pass) important to trade and economic life. Some of the most important are the [Col de la Forclaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_la_Forclaz) (which connects Chamonix to the canton of Valais) and the [Mont Blanc Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Blanc_Tunnel), linking Chamonix to [Courmayeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courmayeur) in the [Aosta Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aosta_Valley).[^cite_note-IFN-7]<sup>: 10 </sup>

### Forests

As of 1996, 178,624 hectares (441,390 acres) of Haute-Savoie is [forested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) (38.8 percent of the total land area), compared to 34.4 percent for the [Rhone-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhone-Alpes) region and 27.1 percent for France as a whole. Of the forested area 141,063 hectares (348,570 acres) (79 percent) is [managed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_management) for [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber) and other [forest products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_product), with the remaining 37,561 hectares (92,820 acres) having no commercial value or used for [outdoor recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outdoor_recreation).[^cite_note-IFN-7]<sup>: 12 </sup>

Management is charged to local organizations, with direction and evaluation focusing on long-term protection for future generations and [environmental education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_education).[^cite_note-8] Of the 37,561 hectares (92,820 acres) of land not managed for timber, Haute-Savoie has nine national nature reserves totaling 24,542 hectares (60,640 acres).[^cite_note-9]

### Lakes

See also: [Lakes in Haute-Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_in_France#Haute-Savoie)

Aerial view of narrow lake between mountains

Aerial view of Lake Annecy from the southeast

Haute-Savoie has significant freshwater resources. [Lake Annecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Annecy) is a major attraction, along with the town of [Évian-les-Bains](550686.md), perhaps the best-known town on the French shore of [Lake Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Geneva), and known worldwide for its [Evian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evian) [mineral water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_water). Haute-Savoie is entirely within the [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_divide) of the [Rhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhone).

### Agriculture

See also: [Abondance (cattle)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abondance_\(cattle\)) and [Savoy wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_wine)

In 2006 approximately 142,000 hectares (350,000 acres) of land was suitable for [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), of which 33,600 hectares (83,000 acres) (24 percent) was [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land) suitable for [market gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_gardening), [cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillage) or [pasture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasture); 600 hectares (1,500 acres) was [orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchards); 300 hectares (740 acres) was [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyards), and 108,300 hectares (268,000 acres) was [alpine tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_tundra) or [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass).[^cite_note-DDAF-12] There were 4,450 farmers in 1999, 4,800 farmers and over 1,700 full-time farm employees at the end of 2006. In 1999, crop production was valued at €71.5 million and [animal production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_production) at €165.4 million.

### Companies

4,301 companies were established in 2004 in Haute-Savoie: nearly 80 percent in the service sector, with a high percentage offering service to individuals (hotels, restaurants, recreational, cultural, sports, personal and household services). This accounted for 21.6 percent of new businesses. The most active sectors were real estate (up 24 percent), construction (up 15.4 percent), business services (up 12.4 percent) and the food industry (up 10 percent).

In 1999, Haute-Savoie had 2,779 industrial companies producing 13.60 percent of all business income.

Screw-cutting is a precision parts-machining industry, and Haute-Savoie generates the bulk of French screws. Firms engaged in screw-cutting are major employers in the department. While the automotive industry is the principal client, firms also service the electronics, household-appliance and medical sectors.[^cite_note-13]

Arve Industries is part of 67 "competitiveness clusters" created in 2005. The cluster is dedicated to [mechatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechatronics) and includes 60,000 industrial jobs in over 280 companies (primarily small), 1,200 researchers and 250 patents in 2002. Among the projects supported by the cluster is [inertial tolerancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_tolerance#An_alternative_view_of_tolerances), a new approach in evaluating the quality of machined parts. Based on the [Taguchi loss function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguchi_loss_function), inertia is defined by its deviation from its target. Inertial tolerancing is a research-and-development program supported by the cluster for its member companies. It is led by a research team from the Symme Laboratory of the University of Savoie and the CTDEC (*Centre Technique du Decolletage*). The publication of the French standard NFX 04-008 demonstrates the relevance of topics covered by the cluster.

Other programs involve the production of clean parts (4P project), developing new models of customer-supplier relationships to improve the effectiveness of simultaneous engineering tasks, and development of the international visibility of the cluster and its members. The companies concerned are involved with industrial mechanics, precision engineering, precision turning and sub-assemblies and mechanical assemblies, often associated with integrating technologies such as plastics, electronics and hydraulics. Markets served by member companies of the cluster include transport (cars, trucks, rail and air), production and distribution of electricity, hydraulics (gas or liquid, high-pressure vacuum), medical and health-related.

### Cross-border workers

Many people who live in Haute-Savoie (more than 52,200 in November 2006) work in [Switzerland](26748.md) (in the cantons of [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaud) and [Valais](198789.md)). The phenomenon has accelerated since bilateral agreements concluded between Switzerland and the [European Union](9317.md), of which a significant part concerned free movement of people. In 2007, commuting increased over 12%.

Effective June 1, 2007, residents of Haute-Savoie may freely work in Switzerland. The department and municipalities receive compensation ("frontier funds") allocated to municipalities in proportion to the number of border residents there. Following an agreement signed in Geneva in 1973, the Canton of Geneva transferred to Haute-Savoie 3.5 percent of total worker compensation, equivalent in December 2006 to €77.687 million.

### Export

Exports are an important part of the economy; forty percent of Haute-Savoie employees work for exporting firms. Exports are primarily to Germany, the United States, Switzerland, Italy and the United Kingdom. Imports come mainly from Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and the United States.

### Taxation

Haute-Savoie has property and income taxes. In 2006, 312,823 households were subject to property taxes and 27,747 were exempt. The average income tax per household was €25,621 in 2007 (compared with the national average of €21,930).

### Transport

Haute Savoie is served by the A41 and A43 highways. Annecy is accessible from Lyon, with an estimated travel time between two and three hours in normal traffic. Since it is closer to Geneva, the new highway connects the two cities in about an hour.

The region has only one main airport, [Chambéry Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamb%C3%A9ry_Airport). However, the airport only provides direct routes to the [United Kingdom](31717.md). Residents in the region normally uses [Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon%E2%80%93Saint-Exup%C3%A9ry_Airport) and [Geneva Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Airport) as both provide more domestic and international destinations.

Saint-Gervais is the only railroad station directly serving a ski resort. The main rail line serves Annecy-Annemasse-Geneva. The Annecy railway station has TGV (high speed trains) departures and arrivals to and from Paris via the high-speed line from Lyon Part-Dieu. Intercity Public transport is run by Lihsa.
