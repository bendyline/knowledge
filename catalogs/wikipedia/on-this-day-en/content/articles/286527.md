**Separatism** is the advocacy of cultural, ethnic, tribal, religious, racial, regional, governmental, or gender separation from the larger group. As with [secession](144732.md), separatism conventionally refers to full political separation. Groups simply seeking greater autonomy are usually not considered separatists.[^cite_note-1] Some discourse settings equate separatism with [religious segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_segregation), [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation), or [sex segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_segregation), while other discourse settings take the broader view that separation by choice may serve useful purposes and is not the same as government-enforced segregation. There is some academic debate about this definition, and in particular how it relates to secessionism.[^cite_note-2]

Separatist groups practice a form of [identity politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics), or political activity and theorizing founded in the shared experiences of the group's members. Such groups believe attempts at integration with dominant groups compromise their identity and ability to pursue greater [self-determination](29269.md).[^cite_note-Stanford-3] However, economic and political factors usually are critical in creating strong separatist movements as opposed to less ambitious identity movements.[^cite_note-Horowitz-4]

## Motivations



Support for [Catalan independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_independence) is based on the idea that [Catalonia](6822.md), as a nation, has the right to the statehood.



The former [KLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Liberation_Army) leader [Hashim Thaçi](3626980.md) (left) and then-U.S. Vice President [Joe Biden](145422.md) with [Declaration of Independence of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence)



In 1861, the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) started after a separatist movement of [southern US states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) seceded from the [United States](3434750.md).

Groups may have one or more motivations for separation, including:[^cite_note-5]

- Emotional resentment and hatred of rival communities.

- Protection from [genocide](12441.md) and ethnic cleansing.

- Resistance by victims of oppression, including denigration of their language, culture or religion.

- Influence and propaganda by those inside and outside the region who hope to gain politically from intergroup conflict and hatred.

- Economic and political dominance of one group that does not share power and privilege in an egalitarian fashion.

- Economic motivations: seeking to end economic exploitation by a more powerful group or, conversely, to escape economic redistribution from a richer to a poorer group.

- Preservation of threatened religious, language, or other cultural tradition.

- Destabilization from one separatist movement giving rise to others.

- Geopolitical power vacuum from breakup of larger states or empires.

- Continuing fragmentation as more and more states break up.

- Feeling that the perceived nation was added to the larger state by illegitimate means.

- The perception that the state can no longer support one's own group or has betrayed their interests.

- Opposition to political decisions.

## Types

[Ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) separatism can be based on cultural, linguistic, as well as [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) or [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\)) differences. Ethnic separatist movements were relevant since they represented historical delineations between [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), or in recent times, were the cause of conflicts between peoples in Europe, Africa, and Asia with different ethnic/linguistic origins.

### &#x20;Gender separatism

The relationship between gender and separatism is complex.[^cite_note-6] [Feminist separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist_separatism) is women's choosing to separate from ostensibly male-defined, male-dominated institutions, relationships, roles and activities.[^cite_note-7] [Lesbian separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian_separatism) advocates [lesbianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian) as the logical result of feminism. Some separatist feminists and lesbian separatists have chosen to live apart in [intentional community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentional_community), [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperatives), and on [land trusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_trust).[^cite_note-8] [Queer nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_nationalism) (or "Gay separatism") seeks a community distinct and separate from other social groups.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] On the other hand, the [MGTOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_Going_Their_Own_Way) movement is sometimes considered a [male-gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masculine) separatism, as at the center of this ideology is the notion of male separatism where men should not be a part of a feminist-biased society. Some fringe elements even propose a utopian no-women state.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### &#x20;Racial separatism

Some separatist groups seek to separate from others along [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_classification\)) lines. They oppose interracial marriage and integration with other races and seek separate schools, businesses, churches and other institutions, and often separate societies, territories, countries, and governments:



Territories considered for "Aztlán"

- [Black separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_separatism) (also known as [black nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_nationalism)) is the most prominent wave advancing the concepts of "Black racial identity" in the United States and has been advanced by black leaders like [Marcus Garvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Garvey) and organizations such as the [Nation of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_of_Islam). [Critical race theorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_race_theory) like [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University)'s [Derrick Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Bell) and [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder)'s [Richard Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Delgado) argue that US legal, education, and political systems are rife with blatant racism. They support efforts like "all-black" schools and dorms and question the [efficacy](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/efficacy) and merit of government-enforced integration.

  [^cite_note-14]

  &#x20;In 2008 statements by Barack Obama's former pastor [Jeremiah Wright, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_Wright,_Jr.), revived the issue of the current relevance of black separatism.

  [^cite_note-15]

- [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American) concepts of racial identity such as the [bronze race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_race) and [La Raza Cósmica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Raza_C%C3%B3smica) are found in the small separatist [Raza Unida Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raza_Unida_Party). The [Chicano Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicano_Movement) (or [Chicano nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicano_nationalism)) in the United States sought to recreate [Aztlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztl%C3%A1n), the mythical homeland of the [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) comprising the [Southwestern United States](179571.md).

  [^cite_note-aztlan.net-16]

- [White separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_separatism) in the United States and Western Europe seeks separation of the white race and limits to nonwhite [immigration](4599312.md) under the argument that these policies are necessary for the white race's survival.

- The demand for an independent Sikh homeland called [Khalistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan) emerged during the 1970s and 1980s amid political tensions in [Punjab, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India), particularly surrounding the [Anandpur Sahib Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anandpur_Resolution), which primarily sought greater autonomy for Punjab and the protection of Sikh identity within the India. The idea of Khalistan holds little to no support among Indian Sikhs, and is generally viewed as a fringe movement, largely sustained by diaspora activism abroad. Attempts to revive it within India have consistently failed to gain traction.

## Governmental responses



Spectrum from separatism to [regional integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_integration)

How far separatist demands will go toward full independence, and whether groups pursue constitutional and nonviolent action or armed violence, depend on a variety of economic, political, social and cultural factors, including movement leadership[^cite_note-27] and the government's response.[^cite_note-Horowitz-4] Governments may respond in several ways, some of which are mutually exclusive. Some include:[^cite_note-28]



[South Sudanese independence referendum](13265409.md) in 2011 marked the end of [South Sudan](32350676.md)'s long struggle for independence.

- accede to separatist demands, [independence](62450.md)

- improve the circumstances of disadvantaged minorities, be they religious, linguistic, territorial, economic or political

- adopt "asymmetric [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism)" where different states have different relations to the central government depending on separatist demands or considerations

- allow minorities to win in political disputes about which they feel strongly, through parliamentary voting, referendum, etc.

- settle for a [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation) or a [commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth) relationship where there are only limited ties among states.
