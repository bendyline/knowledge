**Atlantic Canada**, also called the **Atlantic provinces** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *provinces de l'Atlantique*), is the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_Canada) of [Eastern Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Canada) comprising four provinces: [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), [Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md), [Nova Scotia](21184.md), and [Prince Edward Island](23071.md). As of 2021, the landmass of the four Atlantic provinces was approximately 488,000 km<sup>2</sup> (188,000 sq mi), and had a population of over 2.4 million people. The term *Atlantic Canada* was popularized following the admission of Newfoundland as a Canadian province in 1949. The province of Newfoundland and Labrador is not included in [the Maritimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes), another significant regional term, but *is* included in Atlantic Canada.

## History

The Atlantic Provinces are the historical territories of the [Mi'kmaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi'kmaq),[^cite_note-1] [Wolastoqiyik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolastoqiyik),[^cite_note-2] [Naskapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naskapi),[^cite_note-3] [Beothuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beothuk)[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] and [Nunatsiavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunatsiavut)[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:1-7] peoples. The people of Nunatsiavut are the Labrador [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) (Labradormiut), who are descended from the [Thule people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_people).[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-8]

### Exploration and settlement



Viking migration to modern day Newfoundland

[Leif Erikson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leif_Erikson) and other members of his family began exploring the North American coast in 986 CE.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Leif landed in three places, and in the third established a small settlement called Vinland.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-\:11-12] The location of Vinland is uncertain,[^cite_note-13] but an archaeological site on the northern tip of Newfoundland at [L'Anse aux Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Anse_aux_Meadows)[^cite_note-14] has been identified as a good candidate.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] It was a modest Viking settlement and is the oldest confirmed presence of Europeans in North America.[^cite_note-\:11-12][^cite_note-\:2-17] The Vikings would make brief excursions to North America for the next 200 years, though further attempts at colonization were thwarted.[^cite_note-\:11-12] The site produced the first evidence of [pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_trans-oceanic_contact) of Europeans with the [Americas](29833.md) outside of [Greenland](12118.md).[^cite_note-\:2-17][^cite_note-18]

[Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia), a colony of [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France), was established in areas of present-day Atlantic Canada in 1604, under the leadership of [Samuel de Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_de_Champlain) and [Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Dugua,_Sieur_de_Mons).[^cite_note-19] The French would form alliances with many indigenous groups within Atlantic Canada, including the Mi'kmaq of Acadia, who joined the [Wabanaki Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy), important allies to New France.[^cite_note-20]

### British expansion



Painting shows romanticised view of [United Empire Loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalist) arriving in New Brunswick, ca. 1783

Competition for control of the island of Newfoundland and its waters contributed to major ongoing conflicts and occasional wars between France and Britain.[^cite_note-\:0-21] The first major agreement between the two powers over access to this coastline came with the [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Utrecht) of 1713,[^cite_note-\:10-22] giving Britain governance over the entire island and establishing the first [French Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Shore),[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] giving France and its migratory fishery almost exclusive access to a substantial stretch of the island's coastline.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-\:4-26]

Despite reoccurring wars and conflicts, Britain acquiesced to France's demands for continuing access to this fishery.[^cite_note-\:10-22] Between 1755 and 1764 during the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), the British forcibly removed thousands of Acadians from Nova Scotia and New Brunswick in an event known as the [Great Expulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Acadians) or Le Grand Dérangement.[^cite_note-\:4-26][^cite_note-27] Following the Seven Years' War and the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)) of 1763, Newfoundland's governor, Admiral [Hugh Palliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Palliser), consolidated British control by carrying out the first systematic hydrographic charting of the island,[^cite_note-28] including the [Bay of Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Islands,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador) and [Humber Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_Arm), much of it by the Royal Naval officer [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook).[^cite_note-\:0-21][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

After the signing of the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)) in 1764, some of the Acadians returned and settled in the area that would become New Brunswick.[^cite_note-\:5-31] The effect of this migration can still be seen today, as the province of New Brunswick is the only officially [bilingual province in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_policies_of_Canada's_provinces_and_territories#New_Brunswick), with over a quarter of residents speaking French at home.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Immigration: from loyalists to the Irish



Rose Fortune, daughter of Fortune a free Negro, who immigrated to Nova Scotia as a child after the American Revolution.

[^cite_note-35]

After the conclusion of the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) with the signing of the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)) in 1783, many [loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalist) from the United States settled in the region.[^cite_note-\:6-36][^cite_note-\:7-37] This influx of immigrants caused the [partition of Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Nova_Scotia), creating New Brunswick.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Further, these immigrants changed the culture and character of the region, which had historically been French, towards more British styled communities.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] It also marked one of the first large waves of migration to the area that established a predominantly [Anglo-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadians) population.[^cite_note-\:7-37][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Some of the new settlers brought with them Black slaves.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] An additional 3,000 [Black loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Loyalist) and former slaves who had sided with the British were [emancipated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipated) and evacuated with other Loyalists from New York to Nova Scotia.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Most of the free Blacks settled at [Birchtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birchtown),[^cite_note-48] the most prominent Black township in [North America](21139.md) at the time.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

The [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812) significantly impacted the provinces of Atlantic Canada, where they played crucial roles in naval operations, privateering,[^cite_note-51] and as strategic support bases for the British war effort against the United States.[^cite_note-52]

In the mid-19th century, the region's population grew due to the immigration from Ireland as a result of the [great potato famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_\(Ireland\)).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] [Saint John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John,_New_Brunswick) and [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia), both port cities, particularly received a significant influx of Irish immigrants within the region,[^cite_note-55] with Saint John's quarantine station on [Partridge Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partridge_Island_\(Saint_John_County\)) being the second-busiest in British North America during the [epidemic typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic_typhus) outbreak in 1847.[^cite_note-56]

### Creating "Atlantic Canada"

The first [premier of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Newfoundland_and_Labrador), [Joey Smallwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Smallwood), coined the term "Atlantic Canada" when the [Dominion of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Newfoundland) joined Canada in 1949.[^cite_note-\:8-57] He believed that it would have been presumptuous for Newfoundland to assume that it could include itself within the existing term "[Maritime provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes)," which was used to describe the cultural similarities shared by [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), [Prince Edward Island](23071.md), and [Nova Scotia](21184.md).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-\:9-60] The other provinces of Atlantic Canada entered [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) during the 19th century, with New Brunswick and Nova Scotia being founding members of the Dominion of Canada in 1867,[^cite_note-\:8-57][^cite_note-61] and later Prince Edward Island in 1873.[^cite_note-\:8-57][^cite_note-\:9-60][^cite_note-62]

## Geography



Historical map showing parts of Atlantic Canada

Atlantic Canada is characterized by its rugged coastlines, gravel beaches, [rugged mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield), and dense forests.[^cite_note-\:3-63][^cite_note-\:5-31]

### Region and nearby area

The area is bordered by the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the east and south[^cite_note-64] and [Quebec](7954867.md) to the west.[^cite_note-65] The region shares two international borders one with the United States and its State of [Maine](19977.md),[^cite_note-\:3-63] and another off the coast of [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\)) with France and its overseas collectivity of [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The region's maritime environment has influenced the region's climate, culture, and economy.[^cite_note-\:3-63][^cite_note-68] The area encompasses a mix of urban centers, like [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) and [St. John's](70581.md), and rural communities that rely on fishing and tourism.[^cite_note-\:3-63][^cite_note-69]

### Canadian dividing

Although Quebec has a physical Atlantic coast on the [Gulf of St. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_St._Lawrence),[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] it is generally not considered an Atlantic Province;[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] instead, it is classified as part of [Central Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Canada), along with [Ontario](22218.md).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

Atlantic and Central Canada together are also known as [Eastern Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Canada).[^cite_note-\:3-63][^cite_note-77] Atlantic Canada includes a section of the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) known as the [Appalachian Uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Uplands).[^cite_note-\:3-63][^cite_note-78] In each Atlantic province, Upland regions have been divided into three highland areas. The mountain range results in coastal regions being [fjorded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] Some areas contain [glaciofluvial deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciofluvial_deposits).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

## Economy

See also: [Atlantic Provinces Chambers of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Provinces_Chambers_of_Commerce)

Atlantic Canada's primary industries are [natural resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource) extraction and power generation, including [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry),[^cite_note-84] [hydroelectricity](381399.md),[^cite_note-85] wind power,[^cite_note-86] [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_in_Canada),[^cite_note-87] oil,[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] and [mining](65647120.md).[^cite_note-\:6-36][^cite_note-\:3-63][^cite_note-90]

### Fishing and the Atlantic Ocean

The Atlantic provinces contribute a large part of Canada's fish production,[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] with many coastal communities primarily dependent on fisheries.[^cite_note-93] Over half of all ocean-related jobs in Canada are found in Atlantic Canada, with 75% of the [ocean economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_economy) centered in its provinces.[^cite_note-94] The access point for many such fisheries is the [Gulf of St. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_St._Lawrence)[^cite_note-95] and the Atlantic [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf).[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] Due to the [collapse of the Atlantic northwest cod fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Atlantic_northwest_cod_fishery), Canada imposed a moratorium of [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_cod) fishing in 1992.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] This affected the region significantly, and caused the loss of between 30,000 and 50,000 jobs in the region, which was the largest single layoff in Canadian history.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] The [Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Canada_Opportunities_Agency) is the official agency responsible for creating economic opportunities within Atlantic Canada.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

### Power production

Labrador hosts the second largest hydroelectric system in Canada at [Churchill Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Falls_Generating_Station), where it produces 35,000 [GWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour) of power each year.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Elsewhere in the region, wind power and hydrogen generation have begun to make a large impact on the energy landscape, including exporting energy to Canada and hydrogen overseas.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

### Resources

The region is host to parts of [Canada's eastern boreal forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Canadian_Boreal_Forests), which were historically used for timber production and boat production.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

Nova Scotia has historically been an exporter of [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), and now produces over 60% of the gypsum in Canada.[^cite_note-\:6-36][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] In the Atlantic provinces, [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron),[^cite_note-114] [anhydrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhydrite), [salt](1605200.md), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_dioxide), [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand), [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate), [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite), and [peat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat) are also mined.[^cite_note-115]
