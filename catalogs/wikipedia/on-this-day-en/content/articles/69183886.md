**Trina Lynn Thompson** (born 1961)[^cite_note-2] is an American judge who serves as a [United States district judge](834368.md) of the [United States District Court for the Northern District of California](891692.md). She previously served as a state court judge of the [Alameda County Superior Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alameda_County_Superior_Court).

## Education

Thompson received her [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1983 and her [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) from the [UC Berkeley School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley_School_of_Law) in 1986.[^cite_note-WHBio-3]

## Career

From 1986 to 1991, Thompson served as a [law clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_clerk) and later as a [deputy public defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_defender) in the Alameda County Public Defender's Office. From 1991 to 2000, she served as a [criminal defense attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_defense_attorney) at her own law firm, The Law Offices of Trina Thompson-Stanley, in [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland). From 2000 to 2002, she was a Juvenile Court Commissioner in Alameda County Superior Court.[^cite_note-WHBio-3] In 2002, she was elected as a judge on the Alameda County Superior Court in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California); she was the first [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American) woman elected to the court. She was sworn-in in January 2003.[^cite_note-4] In 2011, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) appointed Thompson to serve on the Coordinating Council on Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention.[^cite_note-5] From 2014 to 2021, she was a lecturer at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and since 2018 she has been an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor) at the [UC Berkeley School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley_School_of_Law).[^cite_note-Questionnaire-6]

### Federal judicial service

On November 3, 2021, President [Joe Biden](145422.md) nominated Thompson to serve as a [United States district judge](834368.md) of the [United States District Court for the Northern District of California](891692.md). President Biden nominated Thompson to the seat vacated by Judge [Phyllis J. Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_J._Hamilton), who assumed [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status) on February 1, 2021.[^cite_note-November3-7] On January 3, 2022, her nomination was returned to the President under [Rule XXXI, Paragraph 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rules_of_the_United_States_Senate,_Rule_XXXI) of the [United States Senate](24909346.md);[^cite_note-8] she was later renominated the same day.[^cite_note-9] On February 16, 2022, a hearing on her nomination was held before the [Senate Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_the_Judiciary).[^cite_note-10] On March 10, 2022, her nomination was reported out of committee by a 12–10 vote.[^cite_note-11] On May 17, 2022, the Senate invoked cloture on her nomination by a 51–46 vote.[^cite_note-12] On May 18, 2022, her nomination was confirmed by a 51–44 vote.[^cite_note-13] She received her judicial commission on August 5, 2022.[^cite_note-14]

### Notable cases

On December 31, 2025, Thompson blocked the Trump administration from removing temporary protected status for immigrants from Honduras, Nepal, & Nicaragua. [^cite_note-15]
