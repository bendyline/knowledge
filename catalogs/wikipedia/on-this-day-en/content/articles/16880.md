**Karnataka**[^cite_note-16] is a [state](375986.md) on the [southwestern coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_coast) of India. It was [formed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Karnataka) as [Mysore State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_State) on 1 November 1956, with the passage of the [States Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act,_1956), and renamed in 1973. The state is bordered by the [Lakshadweep Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep_Sea) to the west, [Goa](40010153.md) to the northwest, [Maharashtra](20629.md) to the north, [Telangana](990267.md) to the northeast, [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) to the east, [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) to the southeast, and [Kerala](4349459.md) to the southwest. With 61,130,704 inhabitants recorded in the 2011 census, Karnataka is the [eighth-largest Indian state by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population); administratively, it is divided into 31 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_India). With over 15 million residents, the state capital, [Bengaluru](44275267.md), is the largest city in Karnataka.[^cite_note-17]

The economy of Karnataka is among the most productive in the country with a [gross state domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_domestic_product) (GSDP) of ₹25.01 trillion (US\$260 billion) and a per capita GSDP of ₹332,926 (US\$3,500) for the financial year 2023–24.[^cite_note-GSDP-18][^cite_note-NSDP-19] The state experienced a GSDP growth of 10.2% for the same fiscal year.[^cite_note-GSDP-18] After [Bengaluru Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore_Urban_district), [Dakshina Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kannada), [Hubli–Dharwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubli%E2%80%93Dharwad), and [Belagavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgaum_district) districts contribute the highest revenue to the state respectively. The capital of the state, Bengaluru, is known as the [Silicon Valley of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley_of_India), for its immense contributions to the country's information technology sector. A total of 1,973 companies in the state were found to have been involved in the IT sector as of 2007.[^cite_note-20]

Karnataka is the only southern state to have land borders with all of the other four southern Indian sister states. The state covers an area of 191,791 km<sup>2</sup> (74,051 sq mi), or 5.83 per cent of the total geographical area of India.[^cite_note-\:3-21] It is the [sixth-largest Indian state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area).[^cite_note-\:3-21] [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada), one of the [classical languages of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India#Classical_languages_of_India), is the most widely spoken and principal official language of the state,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] while [Tulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_language) is an additional official language.[^cite_note-24] Other minority languages spoken include [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_language), [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam), [Kodava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_language) and [Beary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beary). Karnataka also contains some of the only [villages in India where Sanskrit is primarily spoken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_revival#Sanskrit_villages).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Though [several etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Karnataka) have been suggested for the name Karnataka, the generally accepted one is that *Karnataka* is derived from the Kannada words *karu* and *nādu*, meaning "elevated land". *Karu Nadu* may also be read as *karu*, meaning "black" and *nadu*, meaning "region", as a reference to the [black cotton soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertisol) found in the [Bayalu Seeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayalu_Seeme) region of the state. The [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) used the word [Carnatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_region), sometimes *Karnatak*, to describe both sides of peninsular India, south of [the Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River).[^cite_note-28] With an antiquity that dates to the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic), Karnataka has been home to some of the most powerful [empires of ancient and medieval India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_history_of_medieval_Karnataka). The philosophers and musical bards patronised by these empires launched socio-religious and literary movements which have endured to the present day. Karnataka has contributed significantly to both forms of Indian classical music, the [Carnatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_music) and [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_classical_music) traditions.

## History

Main articles: [History of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Karnataka) and [Political history of medieval Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_history_of_medieval_Karnataka)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Karnataka).

Photo of brown cone-topped temple ruins

Mallikarjuna temple and Kashi Vishwanatha temple at [Pattadakal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattadakal), built successively by the kings of the [Chalukya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_Empire) and [Rashtrakuta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_Empire), is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).

Karnataka's pre-history goes back to a [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) hand-axe culture evidenced by discoveries of, among other things, [hand axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_axe) and [cleavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleaver_\(tool\)) in the region.[^cite_note-29] Evidence of [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and [megalithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalithic) cultures have also been found in the state. Gold discovered in [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa) was found to be imported from mines in Karnataka, prompting scholars to hypothesise about contacts between ancient Karnataka and the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) c. 3300 BCE.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Prior to the third century BCE, most of Karnataka formed part of the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire) of [Emperor Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Ashoka). Four centuries of [Satavahana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_Dynasty) rule followed, allowing them to control large areas of Karnataka. The decline of Satavahana power led to the rise of the earliest native kingdoms, the [Kadambas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadambas) and the [Western Gangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Gangas), marking the region's emergence as an independent political entity. The [Kadamba Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_Dynasty), founded by [Mayurasharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurasharma), had its capital at [Banavasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banavasi);[^cite_note-origin-32][^cite_note-origin1-33] the [Western Ganga Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ganga_Dynasty) was formed with [Talakad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakad) as its capital.[^cite_note-gan-34][^cite_note-gan1-35]

These were also the first kingdoms to use [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) in administration, as evidenced by the [Halmidi inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmidi_inscription) and a fifth-century copper coin discovered at Banavasi.[^cite_note-first-36][^cite_note-hal-37] These dynasties were followed by imperial Kannada empires such as the [Badami Chalukyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badami_Chalukyas),[^cite_note-cha-38][^cite_note-cha1-39] the [Rashtrakuta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_Empire)[^cite_note-rash-40][^cite_note-rash1-41] and the [Western Chalukya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_Empire),[^cite_note-west-42][^cite_note-west1-43] which ruled over large parts of the [Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) and had their capitals in what is now Karnataka. The Western Chalukyas patronised a unique style of [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_Architecture) and Kannada [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_literature) which became a precursor to the [Hoysala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala) art of the 12th century.[^cite_note-unique-44][^cite_note-flow-45] Parts of modern-day Southern Karnataka (Gangavadi) were occupied by the [Chola Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_Empire) at the turn of the 11th century.[^cite_note-A_History_of_South_India-46] The Cholas and the Hoysalas fought over the region in the early 12th century before it eventually came under [Hoysala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala_Kingdom) rule.[^cite_note-A_History_of_South_India-46]

At the turn of the first millennium, the [Hoysalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala_Empire) gained power in the region. [Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala_literature) flourished during this time, which led to the emergence of distinctive Kannada [literary metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meter_\(music\)), and the construction of temples and sculptures adhering to the [Vesara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesara) style of architecture.[^cite_note-sang1-47][^cite_note-sang-48][^cite_note-chenna-49][^cite_note-chenna1-50] The expansion of the Hoysala Empire brought minor parts of modern [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) and [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) under its rule. In the early 14th century, [Harihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harihara_I) and [Bukka Raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukka_Raya) established the [Vijayanagara empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_empire) with its capital, *Hosapattana* (later named [Vijayanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara)), on the banks of the [Tungabhadra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungabhadra_River) in the modern Bellary district. Under the rule of [Krishnadevaraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnadevaraya), a distinct form of [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_literature) and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_architecture) evolved.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The empire rose as a bulwark against Muslim advances into South India, which it completely controlled for over two centuries.[^cite_note-vij-53][^cite_note-vij1-54] In 1537, [Kempe Gowda I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempe_Gowda_I), a chieftain of the [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire), widely held as the founder of modern Bengaluru, built a [fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore_Fort) and established the area around it as [Bengaluru Pete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru_Pete).[^cite_note-55]

In 1565, Karnataka and the rest of South India experienced a major geopolitical shift when the Vijayanagara empire fell to a [confederation of Islamic sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_sultanates) in the [Battle of Talikota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Talikota).[^cite_note-bin-56] The [Bijapur Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur_Sultanate), which had risen after the demise of the [Bahmani Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahmani_Sultanate) of Bidar, soon took control of much of the Deccan; it was defeated by the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in the late 17th century.[^cite_note-conf-57][^cite_note-erst-58] The Bahmani and Bijapur rulers encouraged Urdu and Persian literature and [Indo-Saracenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Saracenic) architecture, the [Gol Gumbaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gol_Gumbaz) being one of the high points of this style.[^cite_note-gol-59] During the sixteenth century, [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_people) Hindus migrated to Karnataka, mostly from [Salcette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salcette), [Goa](40010153.md),[^cite_note-konk-60] while during the seventeenth and eighteenth century, [Goan Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goan_Catholics) migrated to [North Canara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Canara) and [South Canara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Canara), especially from [Bardes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardez), Goa, as a result of food shortages, epidemics and heavy taxation imposed by the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people).[^cite_note-61]

In the period that followed, parts of northern Karnataka were ruled by the [Nizam of Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad), the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy), the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), and other powers.[^cite_note-62] In the south, the [Mysore Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Kingdom), a former [vassal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal) of the Vijayanagara Empire, was briefly independent.[^cite_note-ind-63] With the death of [Krishnaraja Wodeyar II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnaraja_Wodeyar_II), [Haidar Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haidar_Ali), the commander-in-chief of the Mysore army, gained control of the region. After his death, the kingdom was inherited by his son [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan).[^cite_note-tip-64] To contain European expansion in South India, [Hyder Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyder_Ali) and later Tipu Sultan fought four significant [Anglo-Mysore Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Mysore_Wars), the last of which resulted in Tippu Sultan's death and the incorporation of Mysore into [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India) in 1799.[^cite_note-tip1-65] Mysore was restored to the Wodeyars, and the [Kingdom of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore) became a [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) outside but in a [subsidiary alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary_alliance) with [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India).[^cite_note-tip-64]



Chief Minister [Devaraj Urs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._Devaraj_Urs) announcing the new name of the Mysore state as 'Karnataka'

As the "[doctrine of lapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_lapse)" gave way to dissent and resistance from princely states across the country, [Kittur Chennamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kittur_Chennamma), Queen of [Kittur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kittur), her military leader [Sangolli Rayanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangolli_Rayanna), and others, spearheaded rebellions in part of what is now Karnataka in 1830, nearly three decades before the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). However, Kitturu was taken over by the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) even before the doctrine was officially articulated by Lord Dalhousie in 1848.[^cite_note-66] Other uprisings followed, such as the ones at [Supa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joida), [Bagalkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagalkot), [Shorapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorapur), [Nargund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nargund) and [Dandeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dandeli). These rebellions—which coincided with the Indian Rebellion of 1857—were led by Mundargi Bhimarao, [Bhaskar Rao Bhave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskar_Rao_Bhave), the Halagali Bedas, [Raja Venkatappa Nayaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Venkatappa_Nayaka) and others. By the late 19th century, the independence movement had gained momentum; Karnad Sadashiva Rao, [Aluru Venkata Raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluru_Venkata_Raya), [S. Nijalingappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Nijalingappa), [Kengal Hanumanthaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengal_Hanumanthaiah), [Nittoor Srinivasa Rau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nittoor_Srinivasa_Rau) and others carried on the struggle into the early 20th century.[^cite_note-67]

After the [independence of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), the Maharaja, [Jayachamarajendra Wodeyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayachamarajendra_Wodeyar), signed an [instrument of accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_accession) to accede his state to the new India. In 1950, Mysore became an Indian state of the same name; the former Maharaja served as its *Rajpramukh* (head of state) until 1975. Following the long-standing demand of the [Ekikarana Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Karnataka), Kodagu- and Kannada-speaking regions from the adjoining states of Madras, Hyderabad and Bombay were incorporated into the Mysore state, under the [States Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act) of 1956. The thus expanded state was renamed Karnataka, seventeen years later, on 1 November 1973.[^cite_note-Unification-68] In the early 1900s through the post-independence era, industrial visionaries such as Sir [Mokshagundam Visvesvarayya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokshagundam_Visvesvarayya), played an important role in the development of Karnataka's strong manufacturing and industrial base.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Karnataka), [Rainfall in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfall_in_Karnataka), and [List of beaches in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_beaches_in_Karnataka)



[Jog Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jog_Falls), formed by Sharavathi River, are the second-highest plunge waterfalls in India.

The state has three principal geographical zones:

1. The coastal region of [Karavali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karavali) and [Tulu Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_Nadu)

2. The hilly [Malenadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malenadu) region comprising the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats)

3. The [Bayaluseeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayaluseeme) region comprising the plains of the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau)

The bulk of the state is in the [Bayaluseeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayalu_Seeme) region, the northern part of which is the second-largest [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) region in India.[^cite_note-arid-71] The highest point in Karnataka is the [Mullayanagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullayanagiri) hills in [Chikmagalur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikmagalur_district) which has an altitude of 1,925 m (6,316 ft). The two main river systems of the state are the [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River) and its tributaries, the [Bhima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima_River), [Ghataprabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghataprabha_River), [Vedavathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedavathi_River), [Malaprabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaprabha_River) and [Tungabhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungabhadra_River) in North Karnataka, and the [Kaveri](177299.md) and its tributaries, the [Hemavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemavati_River), [Shimsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimsha), [Arkavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkavati), [Lakshmana Thirtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmana_Tirtha) and [Kabini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabini_River), in South Karnataka. Most of these rivers flow out of Karnataka eastward, reaching the sea at the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md). Other prominent rivers such as the [Sharavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharavati) in [Shimoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimoga) and [Netravati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netravati_River) in [Dakshina Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kannada) flow westward to the [Lakshadweep Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep_Sea). A large number of [dams and reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_dams_and_reservoirs_in_Karnataka) are constructed across these rivers which richly add to the [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and [hydroelectricity](381399.md) power generation capacities of the state.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

Karnataka consists of four main types of geological formations[^cite_note-geology-74] – the *[Archean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archean) complex* made up of [Dharwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharwad) [schists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schist) and granitic [gneisses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gneiss),[^cite_note-75] the *[Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic)* non-fossiliferous sedimentary formations of the Kaladgi and Bhima series,[^cite_note-76] the *Deccan trappean and intertrappean deposits* and the tertiary and recent [laterites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterite) and alluvial deposits.[^cite_note-77] Laterite cappings that are found in many districts over the [Deccan Traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Traps) were formed after the cessation of volcanic activity in the early tertiary period. Eleven groups of soil orders are found in Karnataka, viz. [Entisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entisol), [Inceptisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inceptisol), [Mollisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollisol), [Spodosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spodosol), [Alfisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfisol), [Ultisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultisol), [Oxisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxisol), [Aridisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridisol), [Vertisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertisol), [Andisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andisol) and [Histosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histosol).[^cite_note-geology-74][^cite_note-\:1-78] Depending on the agricultural capability of the soil, the soil types are divided into six types, *[viz.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz.)* red, [lateritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateritic_soil), black, alluvio-colluvial, forest and coastal soils.[^cite_note-\:1-78]

About 38,284 km<sup>2</sup> (14,782 sq mi) of Karnataka (i.e. 16% of the state's geographic area) is covered by forests.[^cite_note-\:2-79][^cite_note-80] The forests are classified as reserved, protected, unclosed, village and private forests.[^cite_note-\:2-79] The percentage of forested area is slightly less than the all-India average of about 23%,[^cite_note-\:2-79] and significantly less than the 33% prescribed in the National Forest Policy.[^cite_note-forest_area-81]

### Climate

Karnataka experiences four seasons. The winter in January and February is followed by summer between March and May, then the [monsoon](57630.md) season between June and September and the post-monsoon season from October till December. [Meteorologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorology), Karnataka is divided into three zones – coastal, north interior and south interior. Of these, the coastal zone receives the heaviest rainfall with an average rainfall of about 3,638.5 mm (143 in) per annum, far in excess of the state average of 1,139 mm (45 in). [Amagaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagaon) in [Khanapura taluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanapur) of [Belgaum district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgaum_district) received 10,068 mm (396 in) of rainfall in 2010.[^cite_note-82] In 2014 Kokalli in [Sirsi taluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirsi,_Karnataka) of [Uttara Kannada district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttara_Kannada_district) received 8,746 mm (344 in) of rainfall.[^cite_note-83] [Agumbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agumbe) in [Thirthahalli taluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirthahalli) and [Hulikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulikal) of [Hosanagara taluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosanagara_taluk) in [Shimoga district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimoga_district) were the rainiest cities in Karnataka, situated in one of the wettest regions in the world.[^cite_note-second-84]

The state is projected to warm about 2.0 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (4 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) by 2030. The monsoon is set to provide less rainfall. Agriculture in Karnataka is mostly rainfed as opposed to irrigated, making it highly vulnerable to expected changes in the monsoon.[^cite_note-85] The highest recorded temperature was 45.6 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (114 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) in [Raichuru district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raichur). The lowest recorded temperature was 2.8 °C (37 °F) at [Bidar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidar).[^cite_note-86]

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Wildlife of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Karnataka)

The state bird, [Indian roller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_roller)



[Bengal tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger) at [Bannerghatta National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannerghatta_National_Park) near Bengaluru

Karnataka is home to a variety of wildlife. It has a recorded forest area of 38,720 km<sup>2</sup> (14,950 sq mi) which constitutes 12.3% of the total geographical area of the state.[^cite_note-forestdept-87] These forests support 25% of the [Indian elephant](5323507.md) and 10% of the [tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger) population of India. Many regions of Karnataka are as yet unexplored, so new species of flora and fauna are found periodically. The [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats), a [biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot), includes the western region of Karnataka. Bandipur and [Nagarhole National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarhole_National_Park) were included in the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve in 1986, a UNESCO designation.[^cite_note-unesco1-88] The Indian roller and the Indian elephant are recognised as the state bird and animal while [sandalwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandalwood) and the [lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_\(genus\)) are recognised as the state tree and flower respectively. Karnataka has five national parks: [Anshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshi_National_Park), [Bandipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandipur_National_Park), [Bannerghatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannerghatta_National_Park), [Kudremukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudremukh_National_Park) and Nagarhole.[^cite_note-book-89] It also has 27 wildlife sanctuaries of which seven are bird sanctuaries.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-forestdept-87]

Wild animals in Karnataka include the Indian elephant, tiger, [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard), [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur), [sambar deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambar_deer), [chital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chital), [Indian muntjac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_muntjac), [bonnet macaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnet_macaque), [slender loris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender_loris), [Asian palm civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_palm_civet), [small Indian civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Indian_civet), [sloth bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloth_bear), [dhole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhole), [striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena), [Bengal fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_fox) and [golden jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_jackal). Some of the birds present include the [great hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_hornbill), [Malabar pied hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_pied_hornbill), [Ceylon frogmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon_frogmouth), herons, ducks, kites, eagles, [falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon), [quails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World_quail), [partridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partridge), [lapwings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapwing), [sandpipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpiper), pigeons, doves, [parakeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakeet), cuckoos, owls, [nightjars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightjar), [swifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_\(bird\)), [kingfishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingfisher), bee-eaters and [munias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munia).[^cite_note-book-89][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] Some species of trees are *[Calophyllum tomentosum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calophyllum_tomentosum)*, *[Calophyllum apetalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calophyllum_apetalum)*, *[Garcinia cambogia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garcinia_cambogia)*, *[Garcinia morella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garcinia_morella)*, *[Alstonia scholaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstonia_scholaris)*, *[Flacourtia montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flacourtia_montana)*, *[Artocarpus hirsutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artocarpus_hirsutus)*, *[Artocarpus lacucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artocarpus_lacucha)*, *[Cinnamomum zeylanicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamomum_zeylanicum)*, *[Grewia tiliifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grewia_tiliifolia)*, *[Santalum album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santalum_album)*, *Shorea talura*, *[Emblica officinalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblica_officinalis)*, *[Vitex altissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitex_altissima)* and *[Wrightia tinctoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrightia_tinctoria)*. Wildlife in Karnataka is threatened by [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching), [habitat destruction](1707053.md), [human-wildlife conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human%E2%80%93wildlife_conflict) and [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution).[^cite_note-book-89]

## Sub-divisions



Political map of Indian state of Karnataka with the official names of its 31 districts.

Main article: [List of districts of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Karnataka)

There are 31 districts in Karnataka. Each district (zila) is administered by a [Deputy Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_magistrate) (also known as the *District Collector*), who is typically an officer of the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS). The districts are further divided into revenue sub-divisions, which are administered by assistant commissioners. These sub-divisions are further divided into [taluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) (or talukas), which are administered by [Tahsildars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsildar). Taluks comprise [hoblis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobli) (clusters of adjoining villages), which include revenue villages. These revenue sub-divisions, talukas, hoblis, and villages are primarily units of land-revenue administration.[^cite_note-93]

Karnataka has around 6,022 Gram Panchayats, 226 Taluk Panchayats, and 31 Zilla Panchayats in rural areas. In urban areas, there are 318 urban local bodies, including 1 [BBMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat_Bengaluru_Mahanagara_Palike), 13 Municipal Corporations, 60 City Municipal Councils, 126 Town Municipal Councils, 114 Town Panchayats and 4 notified area committees.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

The literacy rate was 75.36% with 82.47% of males and 68.08% of females being literate.[^cite_note-LiteracyandSexCensus-98]

In 2007 the state had a birth rate of 2.2%, a death rate of 0.7%, an infant mortality rate of 5.5% and a maternal mortality rate of 0.2%. The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) was 2.2.[^cite_note-health-99]

Karnataka's private sector speciality health care competes with the best in the world.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-healthcare-101] Karnataka has also established a modicum of public health services having a better record of health care and child care than most other states of India. In spite of these advances, some parts of the state still suffer from the lack of primary health care.[^cite_note-childcare-102]

Karnataka ranked tenth in the Fiscal Health Index (FHI) 2025, with a score of 40.8.[^cite_note-103]

[Gomateswara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahubali) (982–983) at [Shravanabelagola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravanabelagola) is an important centre of [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) pilgrimage.

[Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankaracharya) (788–820 CE) chose [Sringeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sringeri) in Karnataka to establish the first of his four *[mathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matha)* (monastery). [Madhvacharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhvacharya) (1238–1317) was the chief proponent of [Tattvavada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvaita_Vedanta) (philosophy of reality), popularly known as [Dvaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvaita) or Dualistic school of Hindu philosophy – one of the three most influential [Vedanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta) philosophies. Madhvacharya was one of the important philosophers during the [Bhakti movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakti_movement). He was a pioneer in many ways, going against standard conventions and norms. According to tradition, Madhvacharya is believed to be the third incarnation of [Vayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu) (Mukhyaprana), after [Hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman) and [Bhima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima). The [Haridasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridasa) devotional movement is considered one of the turning points in the cultural history of India. Over a span of nearly six centuries, several saints and mystics helped shape the culture, philosophy, and art of South India and Karnataka in particular by exerting considerable spiritual influence over the masses and kingdoms that ruled South India.

This movement was ushered in by the Haridasas (literally "servants of Hari") and took shape in the 13th century – 14th century CE, period, prior to and during the early rule of the Vijayanagara empire. The main objective of this movement was to propagate the Dvaita philosophy of Madhvacharya (Madhva Siddhanta) to the masses through a literary medium known as [Dasa Sahitya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasa_Sahitya). [Purandara Dasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purandara_Dasa) is widely recognised as the *"Pithamaha"* of [Carnatic Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_Music) for his immense contribution. [Ramanuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujacharya), the leading expounder of *[Vishishtadvaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishishtadvaita)*, spent many years in [Melkote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkote). He came to Karnataka in 1098 CE and lived here until 1122 CE. He first lived in Tondanur and then moved to Melkote where the [Cheluvanarayana Swamy Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheluvanarayana_Swamy_Temple) and a well-organised *matha* were built. He was patronised by the Hoysala king, [Vishnuvardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnuvardhana).[^cite_note-Kamath-105]

In the twelfth century, [Lingayatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayatism) emerged in northern Karnataka as a protest against the rigidity of the prevailing social and caste system. Leading figures of this movement were [Basava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basava), [Akka Mahadevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akka_Mahadevi) and [Allama Prabhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allama_Prabhu), who established the [Anubhava Mantapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anubhava_Mantapa) which was the centre of all religious and philosophical thoughts and discussions pertaining to Lingayats. These three social reformers did so by the literary means of *"Vachana Sahitya"* which is very famous for its simple, straight forward and easily understandable Kannada language. Lingayatism preached women equality by letting women wear *Ishtalinga* i.e. Symbol of god around their neck. Basava shunned the sharp hierarchical divisions that existed and sought to remove all distinctions between the hierarchically superior master class and the subordinate, servile class. He also supported inter-caste marriages and Kaay Ta tTatva of Basavanna. This was the basis of the [Lingayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayat) faith which today counts millions among its followers.[^cite_note-basava-106]

The [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) philosophy and literature have contributed immensely to the religious and cultural landscape of Karnataka.

[Islam](6037917.md), which had an early presence on the west coast of India as early as the tenth century, gained a foothold in Karnataka with the rise of the Bahamani and Bijapur sultanates that ruled parts of Karnataka.[^cite_note-bam-107] [Christianity](5211.md) reached Karnataka in the sixteenth century with the arrival of the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) and [St. Francis Xavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_Xavier) in 1545.[^cite_note-chris-108]

[Buddhism](3267529.md) was popular in Karnataka during the first millennium in places such as [Gulbarga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbarga) and [Banavasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banavasi). A chance discovery of edicts and several [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya) relics at [Sannati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sannati) in [Kalaburagi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaburagi_district) in 1986 has proven that the [Krishna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River) basin was once home to both [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) and [Hinayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinayana) Buddhism. There are [Tibetan refugee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_in_India) camps in Karnataka.[^cite_note-109]

[Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) is the official language of the state of Karnataka, as the native language of 66.46% of its population as of 2011 and is one of the [classical languages of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_languages_of_India). [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) is the second largest language, spoken by 10.83% of the population, and is the language of Muslims outside the coastal region. [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language) (5.84%) is a major language in areas bordering Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka as well as Bengaluru, while [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) (3.45%) is a major language of Bengaluru and in the Kolar district. [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language) (3.29%) is concentrated in areas of Uttara Kannada, Belgaum and Bidar districts bordering Maharashtra. [Lambadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambadi) is spoken by the [Lambadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjara) scattered throughout North Karnataka, while [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) is spoken in Bengaluru. [Tulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_language) (2.61%), [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_language) (1.29%), and [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam) (1.27%) are all found in linguistically diverse Coastal Karnataka, where a number of mixed and distinct dialects such as [Are Bhashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arebhashe_dialect), [Beary Bhashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beary_language), and [Nawayathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawayathi_dialect) are found. [Kodava Takk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_language) is the language of Kodagu.[^cite_note-census2011-langreport-111][^cite_note-OfficialLang-112][^cite_note-antiquity-113]

[Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) played a crucial role in the creation of Karnataka: linguistic demographics played a major role in defining the new state in 1956. [Tulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_language), [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_language) and [Kodava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_language) are other minor native languages that share a long history in the state. [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) is spoken widely by the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) population. Less widely spoken languages include [Beary bashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beary_bashe) and certain languages such as [Sankethi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankethi_language). Some of the regional languages in Karnataka are [Tulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_Language), Kodava, Konkani and [Beary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beary_dialect).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

Kannada features a rich and ancient body of [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada_literature) including religious and secular genre, covering topics as diverse as [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) (such as *[Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purana)*), [Lingayatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayatism) (such as [Vachanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vachana)), [Vaishnavism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism) (such as [*Haridasa Sahitya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridasa)) and [modern literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Kannada_literature). Evidence from edicts during the time of [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) (reigned 274–232 BCE) suggest that Buddhist literature influenced the [Kannada script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada_alphabet) and its literature. The [Halmidi inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmidi_inscription), the earliest attested full-length inscription in the Kannada language and script, dates from 450 CE, while the earliest available literary work, the *[Kavirajamarga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavirajamarga)*, has been dated to 850 CE. References made in the *Kavirajamarga*, however, prove that Kannada literature flourished in the native composition metres such as *Chattana*, *Beddande* and *Melvadu* during earlier centuries. The classic refers to several earlier greats (*purvacharyar*) of Kannada poetry and prose.[^cite_note-kavi5-117] [Kuvempu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuvempu), the renowned Kannada poet and writer who wrote [Jaya Bharata Jananiya Tanujate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaya_Bharata_Jananiya_Tanujate), the state anthem of Karnataka[^cite_note-anthem-1] was the first recipient of the [Karnataka Ratna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_Ratna), the highest civilian award bestowed by the [Government of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Karnataka). Contemporary Kannada literature has received considerable acknowledgement in the arena of Indian literature, with eight Kannada writers winning India's highest literary honour, the [Jnanpith award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jnanpith_award).[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

[Tulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_language) is the majority language in the [coastal district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanara) of [Dakshina Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kannada) and is the second most spoken in the [Udupi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udupi_district).[^cite_note-120] This region is also known as [Tulu Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_Nadu).[^cite_note-121] *Tulu Mahabharato*, written by Arunabja in the [Tigalari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigalari_alphabet) script, is the oldest surviving Tulu text.[^cite_note-tuluold-122] Tigalari script was used by Brahmins to write [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) language. The use of the [Kannada script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada_script) for writing Tulu and non-availability of print in Tigalari script contributed to the marginalisation of Tigalari script. In Karnataka [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_language) is mostly spoken in the [Uttara Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttara_Kannada) and Dakshina Kannada districts and in parts of Udupi, Konkani use the Devanagari Script (which is official) Kannada script (Optional) for writing as identified by government of Karnataka.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

The [Kodavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_people) who mainly reside in the [Kodagu district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu_district), speak Kodava Takk. Kodagu was a [separate State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coorg_State) with its own [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._M._Poonacha) and Council of Ministers till 1956. Two regional variations of the language exist, the northern *Mendale Takka* and the southern *Kiggaati Takka*.[^cite_note-takk-125] [Kodava Takk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_language) has its own script, *Karnataka Kodava Sahitya Academy* has accepted [I. M. Muthanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._M._Muthanna)'s script which was developed in 1970 as the official script of Kodava Thakk. English is the medium of education in many schools and widely used for business communication in most private companies. All of the state's languages are patronised and promoted by governmental and quasi-governmental bodies. The *[Kannada Sahitya Parishat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada_Sahitya_Parishat)* and the *Kannada Sahitya Akademi* are responsible for the promotion of Kannada while the *Karnataka Konkani Sahitya Akademi*,[^cite_note-konkani-126] the *[Tulu Sahitya Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_Tulu_Sahitya_Academy)* and the *[Kodava Sahitya Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_Sahitya_Academy)* promote their respective languages.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

## Government and administration

Main articles: [Government of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Karnataka), [Karnataka Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_Legislature), [Unification of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Karnataka), and [List of taluks of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_taluks_of_Karnataka)

Karnataka has a [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary) system of government with two democratically elected houses, the Legislative Assembly and the Legislative Council. The Legislative Assembly consists of 224 members who are elected for five-year terms.[^cite_note-legi-129] The Legislative Council is a permanent body of 75 members with one-third (25 members) retiring every two years.[^cite_note-legi-129]

&#x20;The [government of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Karnataka) is headed by the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Karnataka) who is chosen by the ruling party [members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_\(India\)) of the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly). The Chief Minister, along with the council of ministers, executes the legislative agenda and exercises most of the executive powers.[^cite_note-130] However, the constitutional and formal head of the state is the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_and_Lieutenant-Governors_of_states_of_India) who is appointed for a five-year term by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) on the advice of the Union government.[^cite_note-131] The people of Karnataka also elect 28 members to the *[Lok Sabha](275456.md)*, the lower house of the Indian Parliament.[^cite_note-loksabha-132] The members of the state Legislative Assembly elect 12 members to the *[Rajya Sabha](232210.md)*, the upper house of the [Indian Parliament](410238.md).



The [Vidhana Soudha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhana_Soudha) in [Bengaluru](44275267.md) is the seat of the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly) of Karnataka.

For administrative purposes, Karnataka has been divided into four revenue divisions, 49 sub-divisions, 31 districts, 175 *[taluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taluk)* and 745 *hoblies* / revenue circles.[^cite_note-split-133] The administration in each district is headed by a [Deputy Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Commissioner_\(India\)) who belongs to the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) and is assisted by a number of officers belonging to Karnataka state services. The [Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Police_\(India\)), an officer belonging to the [Indian Police Service](451884.md) and assisted by the officers of the Karnataka Police Service, is entrusted with the responsibility of maintaining law and order and related issues in each district. The Deputy Conservator of Forests, an officer belonging to the [Indian Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Forest_Service), is entrusted with the responsibility of managing forests, environment and wildlife of the district, he will be assisted by the officers belonging to Karnataka Forest Service and officers belonging to Karnataka Forest Subordinate Service. Sectoral development in the districts is looked after by the district head of each development department such as Public Works Department, Health, Education, Agriculture, Animal Husbandry, etc. The judiciary in the state consists of the [Karnataka High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_High_Court) (*Attara Kacheri*) in Bengaluru, Hubli–Dharwad, and Kalaburagi, [district and session courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Courts_of_India) in each district and lower courts and judges at the *taluk* level.

Politics in Karnataka has been dominated by three political parties, the [Indian National Congress](149333.md), the [Janata Dal (Secular)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janata_Dal_\(Secular\)) and the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md).[^cite_note-politics-134] Politicians from Karnataka have played prominent roles in [federal government of India](553883.md) with some of them having held the high positions of [Prime Minister](24452.md) and Vice-President. Border disputes involving Karnataka's claim on the [Kasaragod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasaragod_district)[^cite_note-kasaragod-dispute-135] and [Solapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solapur)[^cite_note-solapur-dispute-136] districts and [Maharashtra](20629.md)'s [claim on Belagavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belagavi_border_dispute) are ongoing since the states reorganisation.[^cite_note-belgaummajority1-137] The official emblem of Karnataka has a [*Ganda Berunda*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandaberunda) in the centre. Surmounting this are four lions facing the four directions, taken from the [Lion Capital of Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Capital_of_Ashoka) at [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath). The emblem also carries two *[Sharabhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharabha)* with the head of an [elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant) and the body of a [lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion).

In 2025, the Karnataka Assembly passed the Hate Speech and Hate Crimes Bill, which threatens offenders with up to ten years of imprisonment and offending organisations with collective liability. Additionally, the bill also allows the state to remove or block content which it perceives to be hateful.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

## Economy

Main articles: [Economy of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Karnataka), [Software industry in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_industry_in_Karnataka), [Banking in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_Karnataka), [Economy of Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Bengaluru), and [Economy of Mangaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Mangaluru)



[Infosys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys), a [Bengaluru](44275267.md)-headquartered [information-technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) company

Karnataka had an estimated GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product) of about US\$115.86 billion in the 2014–15 fiscal year.[^cite_note-IBEF-140] The state registered a GSDP growth rate of 7% for the year 2014–2015.[^cite_note-DES-141] Karnataka's contribution to India's GDP in the year 2014–15 was 7.54%.[^cite_note-IBEF-140] With GDP growth of 17.59% and per capita GDP growth of 16.04%, Karnataka is on the 6th position among all states and union territories.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] In an employment survey conducted for the year 2013–2014, the unemployment rate in Karnataka was 1.8% compared to the national rate of 4.9%.[^cite_note-144] In 2011–2012, Karnataka had an estimated poverty ratio of 20.91% compared to the national ratio of 21.92%.[^cite_note-145] In 2024, Karnataka had a multi-dimensional poverty rate of 5.67%, compared to the all India average of 11.28%.[^cite_note-146]

Nearly 56% of the workforce in Karnataka is engaged in agriculture and related activities.[^cite_note-excel-147] A total of 12.31 million hectares of land, or 64.6% of the state's total area, is cultivated.[^cite_note-stats-148] Much of the agricultural output is dependent on the [southwest monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_monsoon) as only 26.5% of the sown area is irrigated.[^cite_note-stats-148]

According to most recent data, Karnataka is considered the third richest state in India.[^cite_note-149]

Karnataka is the manufacturing hub for some of the largest [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) industries in India, including [Hindustan Aeronautics Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Aeronautics_Limited), [National Aerospace Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aerospace_Laboratories), [Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Heavy_Electricals_Limited), [Bharat Earth Movers Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Earth_Movers_Limited) and [HMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMT_\(India\)) (formerly Hindustan Machine Tools), which are based in Bengaluru. Many of India's premier science and technology research centres, such as [Indian Space Research Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Space_Research_Organisation), [Central Power Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Power_Research_Institute), [Bharat Electronics Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Electronics_Limited) and the [Central Food Technological Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Food_Technological_Research_Institute), are also headquartered in Karnataka. [Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRPL) is an [oil refinery](195137.md), located in Mangalore.

The state has also begun to invest heavily in [solar power](13690575.md) centered on the [Pavagada Solar Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavagada_Solar_Park). As of December 2017, the state has installed an estimated 2.2 gigawatts of block solar panelling and in January 2018 announced a tender to generate a further 1.2 gigawatts in the coming years: Karnataka Renewable Energy Development suggests that this will be based on 24 separate systems (or 'blocks') generating 50 megawatts each.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

Since the 1980s, Karnataka has emerged as the pan-Indian leader in the field of IT ([information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology)). In 2007, there were nearly 2,000 firms operating in Karnataka. Many of them, including two of India's biggest software firms, [Infosys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys) and [Wipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipro), are also headquartered in the state.[^cite_note-it-153] Exports from these firms exceeded ₹500 billion (equivalent to ₹1.8 trillion or US\$18 billion in 2026) in 2006–07, accounting for nearly 38% of all IT exports from India.[^cite_note-it-153] The [Nandi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_Hills,_India) area in the outskirts of [Devanahalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanahalli) is the site of the upcoming \$22 billion, 50 km<sup>2</sup> [BIAL IT Investment Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIAL_IT_Investment_Region), one of the largest infrastructure projects in the history of Karnataka.[^cite_note-154] All this has earned the state capital, Bengaluru, the sobriquet *[Silicon Valley of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley_of_India)*.[^cite_note-business-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

Karnataka also leads the nation in [biotechnology](4502.md). It is home to India's largest biocluster, with 60% of the country's biotechnology firms being based here.[^cite_note-biotech-158][^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] The state has 18,000 hectares of land under [flower cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floriculture), an upcoming industry which supplies flowers and ornamental plants worldwide.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

Seven of India's banks, [Canara Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canara_Bank), [Syndicate Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicate_Bank), [Corporation Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporation_Bank), [Vijaya Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijaya_Bank), [Karnataka Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_Bank), [ING Vysya Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ING_Vysya_Bank) and the [State Bank of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Bank_of_Mysore) originated in this state.[^cite_note-cradle-163] The coastal districts of [Udupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udupi) and [Dakshina Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kannada) have a branch for every 500 persons—the best distribution of banks in India.[^cite_note-fl-164] In March 2002, Karnataka had 4767 branches of different banks with each branch serving 11,000 persons, which is lower than the national average of 16,000.[^cite_note-rbi-165]

A majority of the [silk industry in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_in_the_Indian_subcontinent) is headquartered in Karnataka, much of it in [Doddaballapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doddaballapura) in Bengaluru Rural district and the state government intends to invest ₹700 million (equivalent to ₹1.6 billion or US\$16 million in 2026) in a "Silk City" at [Muddenahalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muddenahalli) in Chikkaballapura district.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

Karnataka also produces silver. The silver production of the state in 2018–19 was 214 kg whereas in 2019–20 it was 187 kg and in 2020–21 the silver production was 120 kg.[^cite_note-169]

Karnataka has the only village in the country which produces authentic [Indian national flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_India) according to manufacturing process and specifications for the flag are laid out by the [Bureau of Indian Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Indian_Standards) at [Hubli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubli).[^cite_note-170]

## Transport

Main articles: [Transport in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Karnataka), [List of National Highways in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Highways_in_Karnataka), and [List of state highways in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Karnataka)

Air transport in Karnataka, as in the rest of the country, is still a fledgling but fast expanding sector. Karnataka has airports at Bengaluru, [Mangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangalore_International_Airport), [Belgaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgaum_Airport), [Hubli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubli_Airport), [Hampi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampi), [Bellary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellary_Airport), [Gulbarga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaburagi_Airport), and [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Airport) with international operations from Bengaluru and Mangalore airports.[^cite_note-5airports-171][^cite_note-\:4-172] [Shimoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimoga_Airport) and [Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur_Airport) airports are being built under the [UDAN Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDAN).[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-TNIE1-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-\:4-172]



Mysore airport

Karnataka has a railway network with a total length of approximately 3,089 km (1,919 mi). Until the creation of the [South-Western Railway Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Western_Railway_zone) headquartered at [Hubli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubli) in 2003, the railway network in the state was in the [Southern Railway zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Railway_zone), [South-Central Railway Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_Railway_zone) and [Western Railway zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Railway_zone). Several parts of the state now come under the South Western Railway zone with 3 Railway Divisions at [Bengaluru](44275267.md), [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore), Hubli, with the remainder under the Southern Railway zone and [Konkan Railway Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkan_Railway), which is considered one of India's biggest railway projects of the century due to the difficult terrain.[^cite_note-konkan-176] Bengaluru and other cities in the state are well-connected with intrastate and inter-state destinations.



Bengaluru-Mumbai Highway

Karnataka has 11 [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports_of_Karnataka), including the [New Mangalore Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mangalore_Port), a major port and ten minor ports, of which three were operational in 2012.[^cite_note-177] The New Mangalore port was incorporated as the ninth major port in India on 4 May 1974.[^cite_note-178] This port handled 32.04 million tonnes of traffic in the fiscal year 2006–07 with 17.92 million tonnes of imports and 14.12 million tonnes of exports. The port also handled 1015 vessels including 18 cruise vessels during the year 2006–07. Foreigners can enter Mangalore through the New Mangalore Port with the help of [Electronic visa (e-visa)](330265.md#Electronic_visas).[^cite_note-179] [Cruise ships](314855.md) from Europe, North America and [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) arrive at New Mangalore Port to visit the tourist places across [Coastal Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Karnataka).[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181] The port of Mangalore is among the 4 major ports of India that receive over 25 international cruise ships every year.[^cite_note-182]

The total lengths of [National Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Highways_in_Karnataka) and [State Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Karnataka) in Karnataka are 3,973 and 9,829 km (2,469 and 6,107 mi), respectively.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

The state transport corporations transport an average of 2.2 million passengers daily and employ about 25,000 people.[^cite_note-ksrtc-185] The four state-owned transport corporations are the [Karnataka State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) (KSRTC) and the [Bengaluru Metropolitan Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore_Metropolitan_Transport_Corporation) (BMTC), the [Kalyana Karnataka Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyana_Karnataka_Road_Transport_Corporation) (KKRTC), and the [North Western Karnataka Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Western_Karnataka_Road_Transport_Corporation) (NWKRTC).

## Culture

Main articles: [Art and culture of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_and_culture_of_Karnataka), [Carnatic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_music), [Karnataka cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_cuisine), and [Kannadigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannadigas)

The diverse linguistic and religious ethnicities that are native to Karnataka, combined with their long histories, have contributed immensely to the varied cultural heritage of the state. Apart from Kannadigas, Karnataka is home to [Tuluvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuluva), [Kodavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_people) and [Konkanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkanis). Minor populations of [Tibetan Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhist) and tribes like the [Soligas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliga_tribe), Yeravas, [Todas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toda_people) and [Siddhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhis_of_Karnataka) also live in Karnataka. The [traditional folk arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_arts_of_Karnataka) cover the entire gamut of music, dance, drama, storytelling by itinerant troupes, etc. *[Yakshagana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakshagana)* of [Tulu Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_Nadu), [Uttara Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttara_Kannada), and Malnad regions Karnataka, a classical dance drama, is one of the major theatrical forms of Karnataka. Contemporary theatre culture in Karnataka remains vibrant with organisations like *[Ninasam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninasam)*, *[Ranga Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranga_Shankara)*, *[Rangayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangayana)* and *[Prabhat Kalavidaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabhat_Kalavidaru)* continuing to build on the foundations laid by [Gubbi Veeranna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubbi_Veeranna), [T. P. Kailasam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._P._Kailasam), [B. V. Karanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._V._Karanth), [K V Subbanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_V_Subbanna), Prasanna and [others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Karnataka#Theatre).[^cite_note-186] *[Veeragase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veeragase)*, *[Kamsale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamsale)*, *[Kolata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolata)* and *[Dollu Kunitha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollu_Kunitha)* are popular dance forms. The [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore) style of *[Bharatanatya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatanatyam)*, nurtured and popularised by the likes of the legendary Jatti Tayamma, continues to hold sway in Karnataka, and Bengaluru also enjoys an eminent place as one of the foremost centres of *Bharatanatya*.[^cite_note-187]

Person with painted eyes in yakshagana costume, as gold-spangled robe with red sheer scarf and spiked headress on gold crown

A *[yakshagana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakshagana)* artist

Karnataka also has a special place in the world of [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music), with both Karnataka[^cite_note-188] ([Carnatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_music)) and [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_music) styles finding place in the state, and Karnataka has produced a number of [stalwarts in both styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Karnataka#Music). The [Haridasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridasa) movement of the sixteenth century contributed significantly to the development of Karnataka (Carnatic) music as a performing art form. [Purandara Dasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purandara_Dasa), one of the most revered Haridasas, is known as the *Karnataka Sangeeta Pitamaha* ('Father of Karnataka a.k.a. Carnatic music').[^cite_note-father-189] Celebrated Hindustani musicians like [Gangubai Hangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangubai_Hangal), [Mallikarjun Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallikarjun_Mansur), [Bhimsen Joshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimsen_Joshi), [Basavaraja Rajaguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basavaraja_Rajaguru), [Sawai Gandharva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Gandharva) and [several others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Karnataka#Classical_Music_%E2%80%93_Hindustani) hail from Karnataka, and some of them have been recipients of the [Kalidas Samman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalidas_Samman), [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan) and [Padma Vibhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Vibhushan) awards. Noted Carnatic musicians include Violin T. Chowdiah, Veena Sheshanna, Mysore Vasudevachar, Doreswamy Iyengar and Thitte Krishna Iyengar.

*[Gamaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamaka_\(music\))* is another [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music) [genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_genre) based on Carnatic music that is practised in Karnataka. *[Kannada Bhavageete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavageete#Kannada_Bhavageete)* is a genre of popular music that draws inspiration from the expressionist poetry of modern poets. The Mysore school of painting has produced painters like Sundarayya, Tanjavur Kondayya, B. Venkatappa and Keshavayya.[^cite_note-play2-190] *[Chitrakala Parishat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitrakala_Parishat)* is an organisation in Karnataka dedicated to promoting painting, mainly in the [Mysore painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_painting) style.

*[Saree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saree)* is the traditional dress of women in Karnataka. Women in Kodagu have a distinct style of wearing the *saree*, different from the rest of Karnataka. *[Dhoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhoti)*, known as *Panche* in Karnataka, is the traditional attire of men. [Shirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirt), [Trousers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trousers) and *[Salwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salwar_kameez)* are widely worn in Urban areas. *[Mysore Peta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Peta)* is the traditional headgear of southern Karnataka, while the *pagadi* or *pataga* (similar to the [Rajasthani turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagri_\(turban\)#Rajastani_Pagari)) is preferred in the northern areas of the state.

[Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and *[Ragi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_millet)* form the staple food in South Karnataka, whereas *[Jolada rotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolada_rotti)*, [Sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum) is staple to North Karnataka. *[Bisi bele bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisi_bele_bath)*, *Jolada rotti*, *[Ragi mudde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragi_mudde)*, *[Uppittu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upma)*, *[Benne Dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benne_Dose)*, *[Masala Dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masala_dosa)* and *[Maddur Vade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddur_vada)* are some of the popular food items in Karnataka. Among sweets, *[Mysore Pak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Pak)*, *[Karadantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karadantu)* of [Gokak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokak) and *[Amingad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunagunda)*, *Belgaavi Kunda* and *[Dharwad pedha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharwad_pedha)* are popular. Apart from this, [coastal Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Karnataka) and Kodagu have distinctive cuisines of their own. [Udupi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udupi_cuisine) of coastal Karnataka is popular all over India.[^cite_note-191]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Karnataka)

See also: [List of institutions of higher education in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_institutions_of_higher_education_in_Karnataka)

3-storey stone building with taller ivory tower

[Indian Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science) is one of the premier institutes of India.

As per the 2011 census, Karnataka had a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_rate) of 75.60%, with 82.85% of males and 68.13% of females in the state being literate.[^cite_note-192]

The [Indian Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science) and [Manipal Academy of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipal_Academy_of_Higher_Education) were ranked within the top 10 universities of India by NIRF 2020.[^cite_note-193] The state is home to some of the premier educational and research institutions of India such as the [Indian Institute of Management – Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management), the [Indian Institute of Technology – Dharwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Dharwad) the [National Institute of Mental Health and Neurosciences – Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Mental_Health_and_Neurosciences), the [National Institute of Technology Karnataka – Surathkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Technology_Karnataka) and the [National Law School of India University – Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Law_School_of_India_University).[^cite_note-\:5-194]

In March 2006, Karnataka had 54,529 primary schools with 252,875 teachers and 8.495 million students,[^cite_note-school-195] and 9498 secondary schools with 92,287 teachers and 1.384 million students.[^cite_note-school-195] There are three kinds of schools in the state, viz., government-run, private aided (financial aid is provided by the government) and private unaided (no financial aid is provided). The primary languages of instruction in most schools are [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-196]

[Statewide board examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSLC) are conducted at the end of secondary education. Students who qualify are allowed to pursue a two-year [pre-university course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre_University_Course), after which they become eligible to pursue [under-graduate](188874.md) degrees.[^cite_note-\:5-194]

There are 481-degree colleges affiliated with one of the [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities) in the state, viz. [Bengaluru University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore_University), [Rani Channamma University, Belagavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_Channamma_University,_Belagavi), [Gulbarga University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbarga_University), [Karnatak University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnatak_University), [Kuvempu University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuvempu_University), [Mangalore University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangalore_University) and [Mysore University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_University).[^cite_note-degree-200] In 1998, the engineering colleges in the state were brought under the newly formed [Visvesvaraya Technological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visvesvaraya_Technological_University) headquartered in [Belgaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgaum), whereas the medical colleges are run under the jurisdiction of the [Rajiv Gandhi University of Health Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_University_of_Health_Sciences) headquartered in [Bengaluru](44275267.md). Some of these baccalaureate colleges are accredited with the status of a [deemed university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deemed_university). There are 186 engineering, 39 medical and 41 dental colleges in the state.[^cite_note-201] [Udupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udupi), [Sringeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sringeri), [Gokarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokarna,_India) and [Melkote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkote) are well-known places of [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) and [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veda) learning. In 2015 the Central Government decided to establish the first [Indian Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology) in Karnataka at [Dharwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharwad).[^cite_note-202] Tulu and Konkani[^cite_note-203] languages are taught as an optional subject in the twin districts of [Dakshina Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kannada) and Udupi.[^cite_note-204]

[Christ University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_University), [Jain University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_University), [CMR University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMR_University), [Dayananda Sagar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayananda_Sagar_University), [PES University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PES_University) and [REVA University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REVA_University) are notable private universities in Karnataka.

On 9 February 2022, Karnataka shut its schools for three days after the regional administration-backed schools imposed a [hijab ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Karnataka_hijab_row), leading to widespread protests and violence. Other universities in the state began enforcing prohibitions after Hindu students, supported by right-wing Hindu groups, argued that if hijabs were allowed in classrooms, they should wear saffron shawls. On 5 February 2022, the Karnataka state government advised colleges to guarantee that "clothes which disturb equality, integrity, and public law and order should not be worn" in apparent support of schools' ability to enforce a ban.[^cite_note-205]

## Media

Main article: [Media in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Karnataka)

The era of Kannada newspapers started in the year 1843 when [Hermann Mögling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_M%C3%B6gling), a [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) from [Basel Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Mission), published the first Kannada newspaper called *[Mangaluru Samachara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangaluru_Samachara)* in [Mangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangalore). The first Kannada periodical, *Mysuru Vrittanta Bodhini* was started by Bhashyam Bhashyacharya in Mysore. Shortly after Indian independence in 1948, [K. N. Guruswamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._N._Guruswamy) founded *The Printers (Mysuru) Private Limited* and began publishing two newspapers, *[Deccan Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Herald)* and *[Prajavani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prajavani)*. Presently *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)* and *[Vijaya Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijaya_Karnataka)* are the largest-selling English and Kannada newspapers respectively.[^cite_note-toi-206][^cite_note-vk-207] A vast number of weekly, biweekly and monthly magazines are under publication in both Kannada and English. *Vijay Karnataka, Vijayvani, Prajavani, Udaywani, Kannada Prabha* are some popular dailies published from Karnataka.[^cite_note-208]

[Doordarshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doordarshan) is the broadcaster of the [Government of India](553883.md) and its channel [DD Chandana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DD_Chandana) is dedicated to Kannada. Prominent [Kannada channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kannada_language_television_channels) include [Colors Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_Kannada), [Zee Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zee_Kannada), [Star Suvarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Suvarna) and [Udaya TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udaya_TV).[^cite_note-209]

Karnataka occupies a special place in the history of Indian radio. In 1935, *Aakashvani*, the first private radio station in India, was started by Prof. M.V. Gopalaswamy in [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore).[^cite_note-gs-210] The popular radio station was taken over by the local municipality and later by [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) (AIR) and moved to Bengaluru in 1955. Later in 1957, AIR adopted the original name of the radio station, *Aakashavani* as its own. Some of the popular programs aired by AIR Bengaluru included *Nisarga Sampada* and *Sasya Sanjeevini* which were programs that taught science through songs, plays, and stories. These two programs became so popular that they were translated and broadcast in 18 different languages and the entire series was recorded on cassettes by the Government of Karnataka and distributed to thousands of schools across the state.[^cite_note-gs-210] Karnataka has witnessed a growth in FM radio channels, mainly in the cities of Bengaluru, Mangalore and Mysore, which has become hugely popular.[^cite_note-fm-211][^cite_note-popfm-212]

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Karnataka)

Karnataka's smallest district, [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu_district), is a major contributor to [Indian field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_in_India), producing numerous players who have represented India at the international level.[^cite_note-213] The annual [Kodava Hockey Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodava_Hockey_Festival) is the largest hockey tournament in the world.[^cite_note-214] [Bengaluru](44275267.md) has hosted a [WTA](57768.md) [tennis](29773.md) event and, in 1997, it hosted the fourth National Games of India.[^cite_note-natlgam-215] The Sports Authority of India, the premier sports institute in the country, and the [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc) Tennis Academy are also situated in Bengaluru. Karnataka has been referred to as the cradle of Indian swimming because of its high standards in comparison to other states.[^cite_note-216]



[M. Chinnaswamy Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Chinnaswamy_Stadium) in [Bengaluru](44275267.md)

One of the most popular sports in Karnataka is [cricket](25675557.md). [The state cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_cricket_team) has won the [Ranji Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranji_Trophy) seven times, second only to [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_cricket_team) in terms of success.[^cite_note-217] [Chinnaswamy Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinnaswamy_Stadium) in Bengaluru regularly hosts [international Cricket matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_cricket) and is also the home of the [National Cricket Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cricket_Academy), which was opened in 2000 to nurture potential international players. Many [cricketers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_cricket_team#Famous_players) have represented [India](407754.md) and in one international match held in the 1990s; players from Karnataka composed the majority of the national team.[^cite_note-odi-218][^cite_note-test-219] The [Royal Challengers Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore), an [Indian Premier League](13260340.md) franchise, the [Bengaluru Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru_Football_Club), an [Indian Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Super_League) franchise, the Bengaluru Yodhas, a [Pro Wrestling League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Wrestling_League) franchise, the [Bengaluru Blasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru_Blasters), a [Premier Badminton League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Badminton_League) franchise and the [Bengaluru Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru_Bulls), a [Pro Kabaddi League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Kabaddi_League) franchise are based in Bengaluru. The [Karnataka Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_Premier_League) is an inter-regional [Twenty20](770690.md) cricket tournament played in the state for eight seasons till 2019.[^cite_note-\:6-220] After 2019, it was replaced by Maharaja Trophy KSCA T20 tournament.[^cite_note-\:6-220]

Notable sportsmen from Karnataka include [B.S. Chandrasekhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S._Chandrasekhar), [Roger Binny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Binny), [E. A. S. ## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Karnataka)

See also: [Architecture of Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Karnataka)

Brown temple on 5-step platform with 2 cone-topped side chambers

[Chennakesava Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennakesava_Temple,_Somanathapura) is a model example of the [Hoysala architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala_architecture), later repaired in the 16th century with financial support and grants by the Vijayanagara Emperors.

7-storey domed building with 4 domed corner towers

[Gol Gumbaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gol_Gumbaz) at [Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur,_Karnataka) has the second largest pre-modern dome in the world after the Byzantine [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia).

[^cite_note-224]

[^cite_note-225]

By virtue of its varied geography and long history, Karnataka hosts numerous spots of interest for tourists. There is an array of ancient sculptured temples, modern cities, scenic hill ranges, forests and beaches. Karnataka has been ranked as the fourth most popular destination for tourism among the states of India.[^cite_note-toptour-226] Karnataka has the second highest number of nationally protected monuments in India, second only to [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md),[^cite_note-protect-227] in addition to 752 monuments protected by the State Directorate of Archaeology and Museums. Another 25,000 monuments are yet to receive protection.[^cite_note-protected_monuments-228][^cite_note-mp-229]

The districts of the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats) and the southern districts of the state have popular eco-tourism locations including [Kudremukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudremukh), [Madikeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madikeri) and [Agumbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agumbe). Karnataka has 25 wildlife sanctuaries and five national parks. Popular among them are [Bandipura National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandipur_National_Park), [Bannerghatta National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannerghatta_National_Park) and [Nagarhole National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarhole_National_Park). The ruins of the Vijayanagara Empire at [Hampi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampi) and the monuments of [Pattadakal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattadakal) are on the list of [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). The cave temples at [Badami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badami) and the rock-cut temples at [Aihole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aihole) representing the Badami Chalukyan style of architecture are also popular tourist destinations. The [Hoysala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala_Empire) temples at [Beluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belur,_Karnataka) and [Halebidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halebidu), which were built with Chloritic schist ([soapstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soapstone)) are proposed UNESCO World Heritage sites.[^cite_note-heritage-230] The [Gol Gumbaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gol_Gumbaz) and [Ibrahim Rauza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Rauza) are famous examples of the [Deccan Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_sultanates) style of architecture. The monolith of Gomateshwara [Bahubali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahubali) at [Shravanabelagola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravanabelagola) is the tallest sculpted monolith in the world, attracting tens of thousands of pilgrims during the [Mahamastakabhisheka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamastakabhisheka) festival.[^cite_note-mono-231]

Golden 5-storey Mysore Palace building with 21 domed towers and central spire

[Mysore Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Palace) in the evening, the official residence and seat of the [Wodeyar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodeyar_dynasty), the [rulers of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_of_Mysore) of the [Mysore Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Kingdom), the royal family of [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore).

The waterfalls of Karnataka and [Kudremukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudremukh) are considered by some to be among the "1001 Natural Wonders of the World".[^cite_note-\:0-232] [Jog Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jog_Falls) is [India's tallest single-tiered waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_waterfalls_in_India) with [Gokak Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokak_Falls), [Unchalli Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unchalli_Falls), [Magod Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magod_Falls), [Abbey Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Falls) and [Shivanasamudra Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivanasamudra_Falls) among other popular waterfalls.[^cite_note-\:0-232]

Several popular beaches dot the coastline, including [Murudeshwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murudeshwara), [Gokarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokarna,_India), [Malpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malpe) and [Karwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karwar). In addition, Karnataka is home to several places of religious importance. Several Hindu temples including the famous [Udupi Sri Krishna Matha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udupi_Sri_Krishna_Matha), the [Marikamba Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikamba_Temple,_Sirsi) at [Sirsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirsi,_Karnataka), the [Kollur Mookambika Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mookambika), the Sri Manjunatha Temple at [Dharmasthala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmasthala), [Kukke Subramanya Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukke_Subramanya_Temple), Janardhana and Mahakali Temple at [Ambalpadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambalpadi), Sharadamba Temple at [Shringeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shringeri) attract pilgrims from all over India. Most of the holy sites of [Lingayatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayatism), like [Kudalasangama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudalasangama) and [Basavana Bagewadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basavana_Bagewadi), are found in northern parts of the state. [Shravanabelagola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravanabelagola), [Mudabidri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudabidri) and [Karkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karkala) are famous for Jain history and monuments. Jainism had a stronghold in Karnataka in the early medieval period with Shravanabelagola as its most important centre. The [Shettihalli Rosary Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shettihalli_Rosary_Church) near [Shettihalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shettihalli), an example of French colonial [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture), is a rare example of a Christian ruin, is a popular tourist site.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234]

Karnataka has become a center of [health care tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_tourism) and has the highest number of approved health systems and alternative therapies in India. Along with some [ISO certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_9000) government-owned hospitals, private institutions which provide international-quality services, Hospitals in Karnataka treat around 8,000 health tourists every year.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-235]
