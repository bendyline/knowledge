The **South Pars/North Dome field** is a [natural-gas condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural-gas_condensate) field located in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). It is by far the world's largest [natural gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_field),[^cite_note-iea2008-1] with ownership of the field shared between [Iran](14653.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] According to the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) (IEA), the field holds an estimated 1,800 trillion cubic feet (51 trillion cubic metres) of in-situ [natural gas](22131.md) and some 50 billion barrels (7.9 billion cubic metres) of [natural gas condensates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_condensate).[^cite_note-4] Ranked against [other natural gas fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_natural_gas_fields), it has almost as much recoverable reserves as all the other fields combined. It has significant [geostrategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostrategy) influence.[^cite_note-5]

As of early 2026, before the beginning of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), Qatar was producing approximately 18.5 billion cubic feet (520 million cubic metres) per day from the field, accounting for around 80% of Qatari government revenues, while daily production on the Iranian side was estimated to be around only 2 billion cubic feet (57 million cubic metres).[^cite_note-\:4-6] Iran's inefficient exploitation of the shared gas field is often attributed to Western sanctions, isolation and mismanagement.[^cite_note-\:0-7] These have also caused the Iranian government to lag behind on pressurization efforts on their side of the field, which causes more gas to migrate to the Qatari side of the field.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

On 18 March 2026, the field [was attacked](82720282.md) by the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md). United States officials denied coordination and approval of the Israeli attack.[^cite_note-\:02-9] Initial reports revealed that the damage caused to sections of South Pars gas field make up nearly 12 percent of Iran's total gas production.[^cite_note-\:5-10] Iran's Ministry of Petroleum said that a number of facilities were damaged and that a fire at the gas field was being put under control. No casualties were immediately reported.[^cite_note-\:2-11] The attack halted output at two refineries with a combined capacity of 100 million cubic metres per day.[^cite_note-12] Parts of the gas field were closed to stop the fire from spreading.[^cite_note-13] Following news of the attack, international oil prices increased from \$103 a barrel to \$108.[^cite_note-14] Gas prices in [Europe](9239.md) increased by 7 percent amid fears of Iranian retaliatory action against energy infrastructure in the [Gulf Arab states](3088555.md).[^cite_note-\:6-15] Iran halted its gas flows to [Iraq](7515928.md), which relies on Iran for between a third and 40 percent of its gas and power needs.[^cite_note-\:12-16]

## Field geology

The field is 3,000 metres (9,800 ft) below the [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed) at a water depth of 65 metres (213 ft),[^cite_note-offshore-technology.com-17] and consists of two independent gas-bearing formations: Kangan ([Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic)) and Upper Dalan ([Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian)). Each formation is divided into two different reservoir layers, separated by impermeable barriers. The field consists of four independent reservoir layers K1, K2, K3, and K4.[^cite_note-18]

The K1 and K3 units are mainly composed of [dolomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)) and [anhydrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhydrite), while K2 and K4, which constitute major gas reservoirs, comprise [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and dolomite. A massive anhydrite (the Nar member) separates the K4 from the underlying K5 unit which has poor reservoir qualities.[^cite_note-onlinelibrary.wiley-19] The gross pay zone in the South Pars field is approximately 450 m thick, extending from depths of approximately 2750 to 3200 m. Reservoir strata dip gently to the NE. The average thickness of the reservoir units declines from South Pars (some 450 metres (1,480 ft)) to North field (385 metres (1,263 ft)). As in other reservoir structures in neighboring areas, the reservoir in the Qatar Arch is cut by a set of NNW-SSE trending faults.[^cite_note-onlinelibrary.wiley-19] Diagenesis has a major effect on reservoir quality of the field.[^cite_note-20]

The field is a part of the N-trending Qatar Arch structural feature that is bounded by the [Zagros fold and thrust belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_fold_and_thrust_belt) to the north and northeast.[^cite_note-SD-21]

In the field, gas accumulation is mostly limited to the Permian–Triassic [stratigraphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratigraphic) units. These units known as the Kangan–Dalan Formations constitute very extensive natural gas reservoirs in the field and Persian Gulf area, which composed of carbonate–evaporite series also known as the Khuff Formation.[^cite_note-SD-21]

Permian–Early Triassic has been divided into Faraghan (Early Permian), Dalan (Late Permian) and Kangan (Early Triassic) Formations.[^cite_note-SD-21]

## Reserves



South Pars/North Dome Field

According to International Energy Agency (IEA) in 2008, the combined structure is the world's largest gas field.[^cite_note-iea2008-1]

[In-place volumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_in_place) are estimated to be around 1,800 trillion cubic feet (51 trillion cubic metres) gas in place and some 50 billion barrels (7.9 billion cubic metres) of natural gas condensate in place.[^cite_note-IRAN-The_Geology-2007-04-02-22] With [in place volumes equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_of_oil_equivalent) to 360 billion barrels (57 billion cubic metres) of oil[^cite_note-23] (310 billion [BOE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_of_oil_equivalent) of gas and 50 billion BOE of natural gas condensate) the field is the world's biggest conventional hydrocarbon accumulation.

The field recoverable gas [reserve](63420573.md) is equivalent to some 215 billion barrels (34.2 billion cubic metres) of oil and it also holds about 16 billion barrels (2.5 billion cubic metres) of recoverable condensate corresponding of about 230 billion barrels (37 billion cubic metres) of oil equivalent recoverable hydrocarbons.

The gas recovery factor of the field is about 70%, corresponding to roughly 1,260 trillion cubic feet (36 trillion cubic metres) of total recoverable gas reserves, which is about 19% of world recoverable gas reserves.[^cite_note-24]

The estimates for the Iranian section are 500 trillion cubic feet (14 trillion cubic metres) of natural gas in place and around 360 trillion cubic feet (10 trillion cubic metres) of recoverable gas which stands for 36% of [Iran's total proven gas reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_reserves_in_Iran) and 5.6% of the world's proven gas reserves as of 2007.[^cite_note-IRAN-The_Geology-2007-04-02-22]

The estimates for the Qatari section are 900 trillion cubic feet (25 trillion cubic metres) of recoverable gas, which amounts to almost 99% of Qatar's total proven gas reserves and 14% of the world's proven gas reserves.[^cite_note-25]

However, since the field is a common field and the reservoir is highly homogeneous, the ultimate recoverable reserves of each country may vary from this technical assessment which only considers the static data and does not include rate of gas migration. So, it is better to say that the ultimate recoverable reserves of each country would be a factor of cumulative gas production by each of them.

The Iranian section also holds 18 billion barrels (2.9 billion cubic metres) of condensate in place of which some 9 billion barrels (1.4 billion cubic metres) are believed to be recoverable,[^cite_note-PO\&G-26] while Qatari section believed to contains some 30 billion barrels (4.8×10<sup>9</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of condensate in place and at least some 10 billion barrels (1.6 billion cubic metres) of recoverable condensate.[^cite_note-27]

The field is rich in liquids and yields approximately 40 barrels (6.4 m<sup>3</sup>) of condensate per 1 million cubic feet (28,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of gas. It has also very high level of well productivity which in average stands for 100 million cubic feet (2,800,000 m<sup>3</sup>) per day per well,[^cite_note-28] while the average natural gas well productivity in Iran is 1.5 million cubic meters per day per well.[^cite_note-29]

In 2005, [QatarEnergy](11292570.md) became worried the North Dome's reserves were being developed too quickly, which could reduce reservoir pressure and possibly damage its long-term production potential. In early 2005, the government placed a moratorium on additional development projects at the North Dome pending a study of the field's reservoirs.[^cite_note-IHSE-30] This assessment is not expected to end until after 2009, meaning new projects are unlikely to be signed before 2010. However, this did not affect projects approved or underway before the moratorium.[^cite_note-31]

The 2005 moratorium by Qatar and the subsequent extension of that raised some questions about the actual proven reserves in Qatari side of the field. There was some news in 2006 that ConocoPhillips drilled unexpectedly dry holes in the North Field and this event was at least a partial catalyst for a revamped perspective on the North field structure and potential.[^cite_note-CohenD-TOD-2006-06-08-32] Further supporting evidence for skepticism about the real scale of Qatari's reserves came from the 2008 exploration round in Qatar to target exploration of gas in the pre-Khuff formation. Even one of the blocks is exactly located beneath the North Dome Field.[^cite_note-33]

On 29 October 2007, Qatargas CEO Faisal Al Suwaidi stated that the 5-year moratorium on new North Field gas development projects, imposed in 2005, could be extended to 2011 or 2012.[^cite_note-IHSE-30] The exploration moratorium was lifted by Qatar in April 2017 with the announcement of a new gas project in the southern part of the field.[^cite_note-34]

QatarEnergy issued a bond prospectus[^cite_note-35] on the 5th of July 2021. This bond issue, which was managed by, amongst others Deutsche Bank and J.P. Morgan included proven and confirmed reserve numbers for 2020 of 818.6 and 1756.1 Tcf respectively. QatarEnergy is the only entity that uses the terminology of *confirmed* reserves and does not provide a definition for it.

The QatarEnergy confidential business plan for 2024 contains a production forecast which implies proven reserves of some 110 Tcf and contingent resources of 85 Tcf as of 2023. No forecast associated with the expansion projects are included in these numbers.

The South Pars Field was discovered in 1990 by [National Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company) (NIOC).[^cite_note-SD-21] The Pars Oil and Gas Company,[^cite_note-PO\&G-26] a subsidiary of NIOC, has jurisdiction over all South Pars-related projects. Field development has been delayed by various problems - technical (i.e., high levels of [mercaptans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercaptans) and foul-smelling [sulfur](27127.md) compounds), contractual issues and politics.

Gas production started from the field by commissioning phase 2 in December 2002 to produce 1 billion cubic feet per day (28,000,000 cubic metres per day) of wet gas. Gas is sent to shore via pipeline, and processed at [Assaluyeh](3393482.md).

As of early 2026, before the beginning of the [US-Iran war](82533582.md), Iran has produced an estimated 2 billion cubic feet per day from the field, compared to 18.5 billion by the much-smaller Qatar.[^cite_note-\:4-6] Iran's inefficient exploitation of the shared gas field is often attributed to Western sanctions, isolation and mismanagement.[^cite_note-\:0-7] These have also caused the Iranian government to lag behind on pressurization efforts on their side of the field, which causes more gas to migrate to the Qatari side of the field.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

NIOC was planning to develop the field in 24 to 30 phases, capable of producing about 25 billion cubic feet (710,000,000 cubic metres) to 30 billion cubic feet (850,000,000 cubic metres) of natural gas per day. Each standard phase was defined for daily production of 1 billion cubic feet (28,000,000 cubic metres) of natural gas, 40,000 barrels (6,400 m<sup>3</sup>) of condensate, 1500 tonnes of [liquefied petroleum gas](361384.md) (LPG) and 200 tonnes of [sulfur](27127.md), however some phases have some different production plans.[^cite_note-36] Each of the phases is estimated to have an average capital spend of around US\$1.5 billion, and most will be led by foreign oil firms working in partnership with local companies.[^cite_note-Iran-37]

Development of a South Pars phase by the Norwegian Statoil company has become infamous after extensive reports of misconduct and [bribery](260979.md) to the Horton Investments, an Iranian consultancy firm owned by [Mehdi Hashemi Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hashemi_Rafsanjani), son of former Iranian president [Hashemi Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemi_Rafsanjani). Statoil committed to spending US\$300 million to construct three production platforms and a pipeline.[^cite_note-38] The government of Ahmadinejad, who came to power in 2005, favoured [local firms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company#NIOC's_major_domestic_contractors) over foreign companies in the energy and other sectors.[^cite_note-Iran-37]

By the beginning of 2008, phases 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 had been brought to production and by the end of 2008, phases 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 were planned to be on stream. Phases 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 27 and 28 were under different development stages.

On 18 March 2026, during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), phases of the Iranian South Pars gas field were [struck by an Israeli airstrike](82720282.md).[^cite_note-39] Following the strike, several gas phases were closed.[^cite_note-40]

### Project finance

As of December 2010, about \$30 billion had been invested in South Pars gas fields' development plan.[^cite_note-payvand-41] It was estimated that the amount would reach over \$40 billion by 2015.[^cite_note-payvand-41] The [Ministry of Petroleum in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_\(Iran\)) said in a revised statement in 2011 that Iran will invest some \$90 billion between 2011 and 2015 (\$60 billion will be allocated to the upstream sector and the rest to the downstream sector).[^cite_note-42] In 2024, Iran scheduled a \$70 billion plan to maintain gas pressure and keep vital petrol production.[^cite_note-43]

Economic studies showed with the operation of each South Pars phase, one percent was added to the country's gross domestic product (GDP), while phase 12 was expected to add more than three percent of GDP.[^cite_note-44]

### Development delays and criticisms

While several phases of South Pars gas field are still waiting for development and the ongoing development phases are facing delays, NIOC authorities are conducting negotiations for development of other Iranian offshore gas fields like North Pars, Kish, Golshan, [Ferdows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdows_Field) and Lavan.

Many Iranian energy analysts believe that NIOC authorities should focus on full development of South Pars field prior to conduction of any new project for development of other undeveloped Iranian offshore gas fields.

The priority of South Pars full development is not only due to its shared nature with Qatar, but also with huge capability of the field to add significant liquid production to Iranian liquid export capacity.

On 27 February 2009, one of the members of Iranian Parliaments criticized lack of attention on the importance of acceleration of South Pars field development and the field development delays.[^cite_note-58]

#### Scale and impacts of delays

By the end of 2008, Qatar's cumulative production from the field was two times higher than Iran's cumulative production from the field. Qatar produced about 20 trillion cubic feet (5.7×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres) of natural gas in the period of 1997 to 2008, while Iran produced about 10 trillion cubic feet (2.8×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres) of natural gas in the period of 2003 to 2008. The 2\:1 ratio of Qatar's cumulative gas production from the field to Iran's is forecasted to continue at least for the short term: by the end of 2011, Qatar's total cumulative production from the field will reach 41 trillion cubic feet (1.2×10<sup>12</sup> cubic metres) of natural gas, while Iran's will stand at 21 trillion cubic feet (5.9×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres) of natural gas in the same year. The ratio is maintained mainly because Qatar's annual production is almost twice the Iranian production level.

In 2011, Qatar will reach an annual production capacity 8 trillion cubic feet (2.3×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres) of natural gas per year, while in that year Iran's production capacity will reach 4 trillion cubic feet (1.1×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres) per year. If Iran could implement all of its South Pars planned development projects on time, then it would reach the production capacity of 8 trillion cubic feet (2.3×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres) of natural gas per year, not earlier than 2015.

The most important impact of delays and lower production in Iranian side would be migration of gas to the Qatari part and a loss of condensate yield due to decreased field pressure.

## North Dome development

The North Dome, also known as *North Field*, was discovered in 1971,[^cite_note-SD-21] with the completion of Shell's North West Dome-1 well.

With falling oil and associated gas production, and depletion of the Khuff reserves, developing the North field became imperative. In 1984 it was decided that development would occur in phases. Phase 1 involved installing production, processing, and transport facilities for 800 million cubic feet (23,000,000 cubic metres) of natural gas per day to serve local utilities and produce 5,000 tons per day of [propane](23643.md), [butane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butane), [gasoline](23639.md), and [naphtha](184342.md). In 1989 a gas sweetening plant and sulfur processing unit were added. Phase one was online by early 1991. Gas from North Field phase one has been primarily used for local demand, and injection into the Dukhan field. Phase two was expected to involve selling North Field gas to its neighbors, possibly through a [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) (GCC) gas grid. Phase three involved exporting to Europe and Asia. Even before the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), this phase ran into trouble. To justify the investment, [QatarEnergy](11292570.md) needed two large-scale long-term supply contracts. Despite efforts from QP managing director Jaber al-Marri, contracts were not forthcoming. This switched emphasis to domestic outlets. In 1988, a firm of international consultants presented a plan to QP for developing domestic projects to utilize Qatari gas. Suggestions included an aluminum smelter, a ferro-alloy production plant, [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol) production facilities, and expansion of petrochemical and fertilizer operations.

Qatar rapidly expanded its production and exports from North Dome Field. Here are a number of milestones:

### Qatar's gas to liquids (GTL) industry

#### Oryx GTL (Sasol)

Main article: [Oryx GTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryx_GTL)

The [ORYX GTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORYX_GTL) plant was commissioned in early 2007, as the first operational GTL plant in Qatar. The plant nameplate capacity is 34,000 barrels per day (5,400 m<sup>3</sup>/d), however, the plant has faced technical challenges and did not reach full capacity during the first year of operation. Modifications recommended by [Sasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasol) assist with overcoming this shortfall and production capacity was reached/ maintained from 2009 onwards. The plant uses 330 cubic feet per day (0.11×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/ks) of natural gas from the Al Khaleej Gas project. The ORYX GTL project uses Sasol's Slurry Phase Distillate (SPD) process.[^cite_note-63]

#### Pearl GTL (Shell)

Main article: [Pearl GTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_GTL)

The project is under construction and will be the world's largest [GTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_to_liquids) plant which will have the capacity of 140,000 barrels per day (22,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) of middle distillates and significant quantities of LPG and condensate. The first of two 70,000 barrels per day (11,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) GTL trains is planned to start production in 2011. Around 1.6 billion cubic feet per day (45,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) of natural gas will be supplied from the North field to the project. Shell has 100% of the equity in the integrated upstream and plant project.[^cite_note-64] In March 2026, the plant was damaged by an attack from Iran.
