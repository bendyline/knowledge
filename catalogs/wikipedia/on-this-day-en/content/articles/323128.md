**Gwangju** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 광주; Korean: [\[kwaŋ.dʑu\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cc/Ko-%EA%B4%91%EC%A3%BC.oga/Ko-%EA%B4%91%EC%A3%BC.oga.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cc/Ko-%EA%B4%91%EC%A3%BC.oga/Ko-%EA%B4%91%EC%A3%BC.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), formerly romanized as **Kwangju**, was a city in South Korea. In July 2026, the city was merged with [South Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jeolla_Province) into a new [provincial-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial-level_cities_of_South_Korea) called [Jeonnam-Gwangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeonnam-Gwangju).[^cite_note-chosun2026-4]

Gwangju was the [sixth-largest metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_South_Korea) in the country. It was a designated [metropolitan city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_city_\(South_Korea\)) under the direct control of the central government's Home Minister. The city was also the capital of [South Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jeolla_Province) until its promotion to a metropolitan city in 2005, upon which the provincial office moved to the southern village of [Namak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namak,_South_Korea) in [Muan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muan_County).

Its name is composed of the words *gwang* ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 광; [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja): [光](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%85%89)) meaning "light" and *ju* (주; [州](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%B7%9E)) meaning "province". Gwangju was historically recorded as *Muju* (무주; 武州), in which "[Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla) merged all of the land to establish the provinces of Gwangju, Ungju, Jeonju, Muju and various counties, plus the southern boundary of [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo) and the ancient territories of Silla" in the *[Samguk sagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samguk_sagi).*[^cite_note-5] In the heart of the agricultural [Jeolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeolla) region, the city is also famous for its rich and diverse [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_regional_cuisine#Jeolla_province).

## History



A memorial to commemorate the lives lost in the 1980 [Gwangju uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_uprising).

The city was established in 57 BC. It was one of the [administrative centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje) during the [Three Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_of_Korea).[^cite_note-auto-6]

During the [Japanese colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), the city was known as Kōshū. On 30 October 1929, a confrontation between Korean and Japanese students at Naju Station, near Gwangju, became the immediate catalyst for the Gwangju Student Independence Movement, which began in Gwangju on 3 November and later spread throughout Korea.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Modern industry was established in Gwangju, and a railway to [Seoul](19159283.md) was constructed. Some industries that took hold include cotton textiles, rice mills, and [breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breweries). The construction of a designated industrial zone in 1967 encouraged growth in industry, especially in the sectors linked to the automobile industry.

In May 1980, peaceful demonstrations took place in Gwangju against [Chun Doo-hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chun_Doo-hwan), leader of the military [coup d'état of 12 December 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_d'%C3%A9tat_of_December_Twelfth). The demonstrations were suppressed by military forces, including elite units of the [Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Operations_Command_Korea). The situation escalated after a violent crackdown, resulting in the [Gwangju Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Uprising), where civilians raided armories and armed themselves. By the time the uprising was suppressed 9 days later, many hundreds of civilians and several police forces/soldiers were dead. After civilian rule was reinstated in 1987, a national cemetery was established to honor the victims of the incident.[^cite_note-9]

In 1986, Gwangju separated from [South Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jeolla_Province) to become a Directly Governed City (Jikhalsi), and then became a Metropolitan City (Gwangyeoksi) in 1995.[^cite_note-auto-6]

Due to a variety of factors, including the ancient rivalry between [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje) and [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla), as well as the biased priority given to the [Gyeongsang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongsang_Province) region by political leaders in the 2nd half of the 20th century, Gwangju has a long history of voting for left-leaning politicians and is the main stronghold for the liberal [Democratic Party of Korea](42316753.md) along with its predecessors, as well as the progressive [Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Party_\(South_Korea\)).&#x20;

Gwangju has held many sports events such as the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), the [2015 Summer Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Summer_Universiade), and the [2019 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Aquatics_Championships).

In July 2026, the city was dissolved and merged with the former territory of [South Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jeolla_Province) into a new [provincial-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial-level_cities_of_South_Korea) called [Jeonnam-Gwangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeonnam-Gwangju).[^cite_note-chosun2026-4]

## Climate

Gwangju has a cooler version of the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa/Cwa*) with four distinct seasons and rainfall year-round but particularly during the [East Asian Monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_Monsoon) Season in the summer months.

Winters, while still somewhat cold, are milder than in Seoul and cities further north due to the city's southwesterly position in the Korean peninsula. Summers are hot and humid with abundant precipitation, particularly in the form of thunderstorms. Gwangju is one of the warmest cities in Korea in the summer due to its geographic location.

## Education

Chonnam National University, Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology, and Gwangju Education University are public universities in Gwangju.

[Honam University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honam_University), [Gwangju University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_University), Gwangshin University, Gwangju Women's University, Nambu University, [Chosun University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosun_University), and Honam Christian University are private universities.

[Gwangju Health University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Health_University) is a private community college offering associate degrees in humanities and social sciences and healthcare sciences, and a bachelor's degree in nursing.

Gwangju has 593 schools, consisting of 234 kindergartens, 145 elementary schools, 84 middle schools, 65 high schools, 1 science high school,[^cite_note-21] 7 junior colleges, 9 universities, 38 graduate schools, and 11 others (as of 1 May 2009) with a total of 406,669 students, or 28.5% of the total city population. The average number of students per household is 0.8.

## Transportation

The city is served by the [Gwangju Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Subway). An extension was completed in April 2008, with the remainder being completed in 2012. The first phase of a second line, which, when completed, will be an orbital loop line, will open in 2026.[^cite_note-22] There are two [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX) stations in the city: [Gwangju station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_station) and [Gwangju Songjeong Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Songjeong_Station). Gwangju Songjeong Station connects to the Gwangju Subway and the local bus system. Now, the Songjeong station is mainly used.

Gwangju has an extensive system of public buses that traverse the city.[^cite_note-23] Bus stops and buses themselves contain stop information in Korean and English. Local buses, but not the subway or KTX, connect to the intercity Gwangju Bus Terminal known as U-Square.[^cite_note-24]

Gwangju is also served by the [Gwangju Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Airport).
