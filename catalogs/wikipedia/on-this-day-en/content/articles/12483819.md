**Jacques Goudstikker** (30 August 1897 – 16 May 1940)[^cite_note-dutch-1] was a Jewish Dutch [art dealer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_dealer) who fled the Netherlands when it was [invaded by Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Netherlands) during World War II, leaving three furnished properties and an extensive and significant art collection including over 1,200 paintings, many of which had been previously catalogued as "[Old Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Masters)". The entire collection, which had been surveyed by [Hermann Göring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_G%C3%B6ring) himself, was subsequently [looted by the Nazis](3005707.md). Between the two World Wars, Jacques Goudstikker had been the most important Dutch dealer of Old Master paintings, according to [Peter C. Sutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_C._Sutton), the former executive director and CEO of the [Bruce Museum of Arts and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Museum_of_Arts_and_Science).[^cite_note-sutton-2] Despite efforts of Goudstikker's widow after the war to regain possession of the collection, it was not until after her death that the Dutch government finally [restituted 202 paintings to the Goudstikker family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Goudstikker_restitution_of_202_paintings) in 2006. To finance efforts to reclaim more of the stolen art, a large portion of them were sold at auction in 2007 for almost \$10 million.[^cite_note-graph-3]

## Biography

Goudstikker was born in [Amsterdam](844.md) as the son of the art dealer Eduard Goudstikker. He studied at the [commercial school](56551.md) in Amsterdam, and pursued further studies with [Wilhelm Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Martin) and [William Vogelsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Vogelsang?action=edit\&redlink=1) at the universities of [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_university) and [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_university).&#x20;

In 1919, he joined his father's Amsterdam gallery, restructured it as a public *[besloten vennootschap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besloten_vennootschap)* with himself as the director and major shareholder, and introduced a notably more [international style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_style_\(art\)); publishing catalogues in French rather than Dutch, and showing for the first time [Italian Renaissance paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance_painting), including *[The Madonna and Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Madonna_and_Child)* by [Francesco Squarcione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Squarcione). This was revolutionary in the Netherlands of the time, where in 1906, [Adriaan Pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriaan_Pit), the director of the [Rijksmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmuseum), had stated "We have become chauvinistic with regard to the field of art. This worship of our old school of painting, which started thirty years ago is still alive and appears not to let us appreciate any foreign art."[^cite_note-hall-4]



*Portrait of [Marie Louise Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Louise_Gonzaga), Queen of Poland* by [Ferdinand Bol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Bol), was part of the large restitution agreement in 2006 and was subsequently loaned to the [Jewish Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_\(Manhattan\)) in Manhattan.

Following World War I, Amsterdam once again became a centre of international commerce, and Goudstikker flourished, along with fellow art dealers, P. de Boer and [Henri Douwes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Douwes?action=edit\&redlink=1); in 1927 he moved to a larger gallery. Goudstikker rose above his contemporaries, presenting works from the [Dutch Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Golden_Age) alongside [panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panel_painting) by 14th-century, 15th-century and 16th-century Dutch, Flemish, German and Italian painters, mixing paintings, sculptures, carpets, and other works of art together, in the sophisticated style of [Wilhelm von Bode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Bode) of Berlin, much emulated in London, Paris, and New York City. Goudstikker's taste extended to the design of his catalogs, which were minor works of art in themselves.[^cite_note-hall-4]

Goudstikker maintained close ties with [art historians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_historian) and collectors. In the introduction to his 1928 catalog, he wrote "\[W]e are happy as a logical development in our Italian department in having obtained the assistance of our compatriot Doctor [Raimond van Marle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raimond_van_Marle)", author of the influential *The Development of the Italian Schools of Painting*. His clients, including [Jan Herman van Heek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Herman_van_Heek), [Daniel G. van Beuningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_G._van_Beuningen), [Heinrich Baron Thyssen-Bornemisza de Kászon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Baron_Thyssen-Bornemisza_de_K%C3%A1szon), and [Otto Lanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Lanz), also partook in this mix of connoisseurship and scholarship.[^cite_note-hall-4]

He staged an exhibit of Dutch and Flemish paintings, including five [van Goghs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Gogh), two [van Dongens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kees_van_Dongen), and a [Mondrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Mondrian), together with a group of 17th-century works including a wooded [landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_art) by [Philips Koninck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips_Koninck), at the [Anderson Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Gallery) in New York City in 1923, organized through the Dutch [Kamer van Koophandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamer_van_Koophandel); the Committee of Patrons included the wives of such upper class notables as [T. J. Oakley Rhinelander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._Oakley_Rhinelander) and Cortland S. Van Rensselaer.[^cite_note-hall-4]

The [stock market crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_crash) and [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) took their toll on the connoisseur art trade, as on other luxury businesses. Goudstikker was forced to economize on production of his catalogs, but he still managed to organize a [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubens) exhibition in 1933, as well as what may have been his ultimate achievement, participating in the exhibition of *Italian Paintings in Dutch Collections* at the [Stedelijk Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stedelijk_Museum) in Amsterdam in 1934, where he personally showed [Queen Wilhelmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmina_of_the_Netherlands) the exhibits.[^cite_note-hall-4]

While escaping the Nazis on the *[SS Bodegraven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Bodegraven?action=edit\&redlink=1)* Goudstikker died on 15 May 1940; on the deck at night, he failed to see an open hatch and fell into the hold, breaking his neck.[^cite_note-prnews-5][^cite_note-6]

### Goudstikker's artistic taste

Goudstikker's main stock in trade were paintings by the [Old Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Masters); he did not express much interest in Italian [Baroque art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_painting) or art of the 18th century. While his specialty was Dutch 17th-century painting, his specific interest was the more stylized painters such as [Cranach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Cranach_the_Elder), [Marco Zoppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Zoppo), [Squarcione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarcione) and [Pesellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesellino), and he was particularly attracted to the unusual. Artists in his collection included [Jan Steen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Steen), [Adriaen van Ostade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriaen_van_Ostade), [Isaac van Ostade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_van_Ostade), and [tonal landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonalism) painters, such as [Jan van Goyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Goyen) and [Salomon van Ruysdael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salomon_van_Ruysdael). He kept several notable paintings by [Jan van der Heyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_der_Heyden) at [Nijenrode Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijenrode_Castle), one of his two country homes where he also entertained clients and exhibited great art. Although he did carry some [still lifes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_life), such as the [Jan van Huysum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Huysum) in the [National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art), his major interest was in [figure painters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_painting), whether [portraitists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait) such as [Jan Antonisz van Ravesteyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Antonisz_van_Ravesteyn) or [Johannes Cornelisz Verspronck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Cornelisz_Verspronck), or subject painters like [Hans Bol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bol), [Aert de Gelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aert_de_Gelder), or [Jan Steen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Steen).[^cite_note-sutton-2][^cite_note-hall-4]

Other notable paintings owned by Goudstikker include *The entrance to a harbor* by [Simon de Vlieger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_de_Vlieger), *Extensive landscape with trees and a cottage* by [Philips Koninck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips_Koninck), the *Ferry Boat with cattle on the River Vecht near Nijenrode* by [Salomon van Ruysdael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salomon_van_Ruysdael), the *Saint Lucy* by [Jacopo del Casentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_del_Casentino), *The Judgment of Paris* by [François Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Boucher), *The Fritole Seller* by [Pietro Longhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Longhi), the *[Madonna and Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_Child)* by [Pacchiarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Pacchiarotti), the *[Christ Carrying the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Carrying_the_Cross_\(Bosch,_1480s\))* by [Hieronymous Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieronymous_Bosch) which now resides in the [Kunsthistorisches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthistorisches_Museum) of Vienna, and *Young Girl with a Flute* by [Vermeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermeer), which was eventually purchased by [Joseph Widener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Widener) to donate to the [National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art) in Washington, DC in 1942. Other American museum purchases from Goudstikker include a large [altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altarpiece) by [Luca Signorelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Signorelli) depicting *The Assumption of the Virgin with Saints Michael and Benedict*, by the [Metropolitan Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum) in New York in 1929, and Pesellino's *King David before the Ark of the Covenant* by the [Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson-Atkins_Museum_of_Art) in Kansas in 1932.[^cite_note-sutton-2][^cite_note-hall-4]

## Fate of collection



[Herengracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canals_of_Amsterdam#Herengracht) 458, in the [Gouden Bocht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouden_Bocht), office and showroom of Jacques Goudstikker from 1927 until 1940

After Goudstikker fled in 1940 the large [Goudstikker collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goudstikker_collection?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1,113 numbered paintings and an unknown quantity of unnumbered paintings)[^cite_note-dutch-1] was left behind to be looted and became the largest claim for [restitution of Nazi-looted art](3005707.md).[^cite_note-prnews-5]

In a forced sale that was typical for him, Reichsmarschall [Hermann Göring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_G%C3%B6ring) obtained the entire collection over the objections of Goudstikker’s widow [Dési von Halban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9si_von_Halban): on 3 June 1940, Goudstikker's employee [Arie ten Broek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arie_ten_Broek?action=edit\&redlink=1) was named director of the company, then, on 13 July, ten Broek and another of Goudstikker's employees, [Jan Dik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Dik?action=edit\&redlink=1) were paid 180 thousand guilders each to sell all paintings and works of art to Göring for two million [guilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilder), a fraction of their value, and the art gallery to German banker [Alois Miedl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Miedl), for 550 thousand guilders. Through a series of transactions later deemed illegal, Miedl acquired title to the J. Goudstikker trade name, what little art remained in the collection, and Goudstikker's real estate (Nijenrode castle in [Breukelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breukelen), the Herengracht 458 building in Amsterdam, and the country estate Oostermeer in [Ouderkerk aan de Amstel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouderkerk_aan_de_Amstel)). Using Goudstikker's internationally renowned trade name as a marketing asset, Miedl went on to make a fortune selling art, particularly to Nazi Germany.[^cite_note-dutch-1][^cite_note-prnews-5]

Parts of the Goudstikker collection were also taken over by [Gauleiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauleiter) [Erich Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Koch); one of these paintings, the *Cottages by the canal* by [Jan van Goyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Goyen), is on display at the [National Museum, Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum,_Gda%C5%84sk), which declined a restitution in 2020.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Following World War II, the Allied forces recovered this art from Germany and gave them to the Dutch government as part of 'amicable restitution of rights', with the intention of returning them to their rightful owners; however, instead of returning them to Goudstikker's wife Desi, who sought their recovery from 1946 to 1952, they were retained as part of the Netherlands' National Collection at the Rijksmuseum. Dutch [investigative journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_journalist) [Pieter den Hollander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_den_Hollander?action=edit\&redlink=1) wrote a 1998 book, *De zaak Goudstikker* (*The Goudstikker Case*), on the subject. Subsequently, Goudstikker's heirs sued for possession of these works, but their claim was rejected by the State Secretary of Education, Culture and Science. Official investigations, however, later confirmed the mishandling of postwar restitutions, and as a result, the Dutch government created the Restitutions Committee to review claims to art treasures in the government's possession. On the recommendations of the *Herkomst Gezocht* Committee chaired by [R. E. O. Ekkart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._E._O._Ekkart?action=edit\&redlink=1), after eight years of legal battles, in 2006 the Dutch government returned 202 paintings to Goudstikker's sole surviving heir, his daughter-in-law, Marei von Saher (Goudstikker's wife, Dési, and their only son, Edo, both having died in 1996). In 2007, von Saher sold many, netting almost \$10 million at auction.[^cite_note-prnews-5]

Some pieces were not returned to von Saher because the Dutch government no longer had them in 2006. In particular, one of the *[Adam and Eve (Cranach)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_and_Eve_\(Cranach\))* paintings had been transferred by the government to another person (under a claim later found to be invalid) and in 2006 were in the United States, out of the control of the Dutch government. Von Saher sued unsuccessfully in U.S. court for their return.[^cite_note-10]

In 2025, a painting attributed to [Giuseppe Ghislandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Ghislandi),[^cite_note-11] *Portrait of a Lady, Contessa Colleoni*, was recovered from the descendants of Nazi Germany financier [Friedrich Kadgien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Kadgien) (1907–1978) in Argentina.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In 2026, it was announced that another painting from the collection - *Portrait of a Young Girl* by a Dutch artist [Toon Kelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toon_Kelder?action=edit\&redlink=1) had been found in the home of descendants of the Dutch collaborator and [Waffen-SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waffen-SS) general [Hendrik Seyffardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Seyffardt).[^cite_note-14]

In July 2026, a 17th-century painting of the inside of [Nieuwe Kerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Kerk,_Amsterdam), Amsterdam, by Hendrick van der Burgh that was looted from the collection during the [Second World War](32927.md), was identified after it was found in a street bin in Amsterdam decades earlier. Dutch art crime investigator [Arthur Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Brand_\(investigator\)) traced the painting's origin and arranged for its return to Goudstikker's heirs.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]
