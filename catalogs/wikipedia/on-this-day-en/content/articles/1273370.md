**Kherson Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Херсонська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Khersonska oblast*, IPA: [\[xerˈsɔnʲsʲkɐ ˈɔblɐsʲtʲ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian); [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Херсонская область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Khersonskaya oblast'*), also known as **Khersonshchyna** (Херсонщина, IPA: [\[xerˈsɔnʃtʃɪnɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian)), is an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) in southern [Ukraine](31750.md). It is located just north of [Crimea](163045.md). Its [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) is [Kherson](1025861.md), on the northern or [right bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_bank) of the [Dnieper](380138.md) river, which bisects the oblast. The oblast has an area of 28,461 km<sup>2</sup> and a population of 1,001,598 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-5] It is considered the 'fruit basket' of Ukraine, as much of its [agricultural produce](81265102.md) gets distributed throughout the country, with production peaking in summer.

Most of Kherson Oblast has been [under Russian military occupation](70396568.md) since it was captured early on in [Russia](25391.md)'s [full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022](70149799.md). In September 2022, Russia claimed to have [annexed Kherson Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts),[^cite_note-\:0-8][^cite_note-\:1-9] after arranging [a disputed referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_annexation_referendums_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine). The referendum and the claimed annexation are internationally unrecognized. As the result of [a counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kherson_counteroffensive) operation, Ukrainian forces retook all the area on the right bank of the Dnieper, including Kherson city, by mid-November 2022.

## History



17th-century view of Kazikermen, now [Beryslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryslav)

At various times throughout its history, the territory was ruled either entirely or partly by [Scythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythia), [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks), [Old Great Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Great_Bulgaria), [Khazars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars), [Kipchaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipchaks), the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Kyivan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyivan_Rus'), [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa),[^cite_note-EOU_Oleshia-10] the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania), the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate), the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland)[^cite_note-sgk-11] and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). In medieval times, the towns of greatest importance were [Oleshia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleshia), a former Byzantine, Kyivan Rus' and Genoese trading port,[^cite_note-EOU_Oleshia-10] and Tawan/Kazikermen, a former Lithuanian customs point and Polish and Turkish fortress, which is now [Beryslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryslav).[^cite_note-sgk-11] Another notable town in the early modern period was *Bilchowisce*, now Kherson.[^cite_note-12]

In 1917–1920 the territory was variously controlled by the [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic), [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) and [White Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Russia_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)), decisively becoming part [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine) in 1920. It was the scene of Soviet genocidal crimes, chiefly the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor) of 1932–1933, and part of the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) of 1940.[^cite_note-13] During [World War II](32927.md), it was occupied by Germany from 1941 to 1944.

The province was established in 1944 within [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine). In the [1991 Ukrainian independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), 90.13% of votes in Kherson Oblast were in favor of the [Declaration of Independence of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-DT150103-14]

A survey conducted by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology in December 2014 found that 90.9% of the oblast's population opposed their region joining Russia, 1% supported the idea, and the rest were undecided or did not respond.[^cite_note-DT150103-14][^cite_note-15]

### Russian invasion



Map showing territorial control in Kherson Oblast

Main article: [Russian occupation of Kherson Oblast](70396568.md)

As a result of the 24 February 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Russian forces occupied most of the oblast. Russian-controlled parts of the oblast were governed by the "Kherson military–civilian administration"[^cite_note-rferlra-16] from 28 April to 30 September 2022.

On 27 July 2022, the Ukrainian army destroyed the [Antonivka Road Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonivka_Road_Bridge), as part of its wider campaign to isolate the Russian forces on the [right bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_bank) of the [Dnieper](380138.md) river.[^cite_note-dasuf-17]

On 23–27 September 2022, the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) held [referendums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_annexation_referendums_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine) in the occupied territories of Kherson Oblast, claiming that the result was in favor of Russian annexation.[^cite_note-\:7-18][^cite_note-\:5-19] Most states have recognized that the referendums were [staged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_election) and against [international law](8195726.md).[^cite_note-\:guterres-20][^cite_note-21]

On 29 September, the Russian Federation recognized Kherson Oblast as an independent state.[^cite_note-22] The next day, Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) announced the [annexation of Kherson Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts) and signed an "accession decree" that is widely considered to be illegal.[^cite_note-\:0-8][^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-23] At that time, Russia was not in control of the province as a whole.[^cite_note-24]

The [United Nations](31769.md) [General Assembly](31957.md) subsequently passed [a resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/4) calling on countries not to recognise what it described as an "attempted illegal annexation" and demanded that Russia "immediately, completely and unconditionally withdraw".[^cite_note-25]

By 11 November 2022, the city of Kherson and all the Russian-held territory on the right bank of the Dnieper had been recaptured by Ukrainian forces.[^cite_note-novemberkherson-26] The territory on the left bank is still under Russian control.

On 6 June 2023, the Kakhovka Dam [was breached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Kakhovka_Dam), causing extensive flooding downstream and prompting mass evacuations in the oblast, while the [Kakhovka Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Reservoir) was drained.[^cite_note-27]

Russian authorities claimed that Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) won 88.12% of the vote in the occupied Kherson region in the [2024 Russian presidential election](56748201.md),[^cite_note-28] which has been described as rigged and fraudulent.[^cite_note-29]

## Geography



Kherson Oblast and subdivisions since July 2020

Kherson Oblast is bordered by [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md) to the north, the [Black Sea](3386.md) and [Crimea](163045.md) to the south, [Mykolaiv Oblast](494928.md) to the west, and the [Azov Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Sea) and [Zaporizhzhia Oblast](494936.md) to the east. The [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River), which includes the [Kakhovka Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Reservoir), runs through the oblast.

Before the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), two bridges spanned the Dniper: the Kakhovka Bridge near [Nova Kakhovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Kakhovka) and the [Antonivka Road Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonivka_Road_Bridge) at Kherson. Another significant bridge, the Daryivka Bridge crosses the [Inhulets river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhulets_river) and connects Kherson via the M14 highway to [Beryslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryslav), the other abutment of the Kakhovka Bridge.

The oblast's [Henichesk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henichesk_Raion) includes the northern portion of the [Arabat Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabat_Spit), a thin strip of land between the brackish [Syvash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syvash) and the [Sea of Azov](78019.md) that is geographically part of the [Crimean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Peninsula). Due to Russia gaining *de facto* control of the [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea) in 2014, this strip within Kherson Oblast was the only part of the Crimean Peninsula under Ukrainian control immediately prior to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).

Historically, it is located in [Yedisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedisan) (north-western part), [Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_\(region\)) (northern part) and [Pryazovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pryazovia) (southern and eastern parts).

[Kherson](1025861.md) is the only city in the oblast with a population over 100,000. Four other cities have over 30,000; they are, in order from largest, [Nova Kakhovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Kakhovka), [Kakhovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka), [Oleshky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleshky) and [Henichesk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henichesk).

The population of the oblast is 1,083,367 (2012), which is 2.4% of the total population of Ukraine. It is ranked 21st by its population. The population density is 38 per km<sup>2</sup>.

About 61.5% or 745,400 people live in urban areas and 38.5% or 467,600 live in agricultural centers/villages. Men make up 46.7% or 565,400 people of the population, women make up 53.3% or 644,600 people, and [pensioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensioner) make up 26.2% or 317,400 people.
