**Sir Tom John Marsters** [KBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Most_Excellent_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (born 4 August 1945) is a [Cook Islands](7067.md) politician who has been the [vice-regal representative in the Cook Islands](2369963.md)[^cite_note-2] since 2013. He is a former [Deputy Prime Minister of the Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_the_Cook_Islands), Foreign Minister, and Deputy Leader of the [Cook Islands Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Party).

## Personal life

Marsters was born on [Palmerston Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston_Island) and educated at Nikao and Avarua Primary Schools before attending [Avele College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avele_College) in [Samoa](27238.md)[^cite_note-3] and [Grimsby Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsby_Institute_of_Further_&_Higher_Education) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-parlbio-4] Before entering politics he worked as a public servant. He was Secretary General of the Cook Islands Party from 1968 to 1999.[^cite_note-parlbio-4]

He was first elected to [Parliament](4504372.md) for the seat of [Murienua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murienua_\(electorate\)) in a by-election in 1991.

## Cabinet

Marsters served as Minister of Works in the Cabinet of [Sir Geoffrey Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Henry), but resigned his position in 1997 in protest at budget cuts.[^cite_note-5] He later served as Minister of Works, Youth, Sport and Recreation in the first coalition Cabinet of [Robert Woonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Woonton) from 2002 to 2003.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] He rejoined Cabinet after the [2004 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Cook_Islands_general_election), when Woonton was trying to put together a new coalition;[^cite_note-8] when Woonton resigned to fight a by-election, he served in the Cabinet of [Jim Marurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Marurai), holding the portfolios of foreign affairs, transport, and youth and sport.[^cite_note-9]

In August 2005, Marurai sacked [Cook Islands Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Party) leader Sir [Geoffrey Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Henry) from Cabinet,[^cite_note-10] causing the CIP to reconsider its role in government. A month later, Marsters was also sacked, and the coalition formally dissolved.[^cite_note-11]

## Opposition

The retirement of Sir Geoffrey Henry in 2006 led to a leadership election, which saw Marsters replaced as Deputy leader by [Tupou Faireka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupou_Faireka).[^cite_note-12] However, both Faireka and party leader [Henry Puna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Puna) lost their seats at the [2006 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Cook_Islands_general_election).[^cite_note-13] While Puna continued to serve as leader outside parliament, Marsters became leader of the opposition.

## Deputy Prime Minister

Marsters was re-elected at the [2010 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Cook_Islands_general_election), which saw the Cook Islands Party win 16 of the 24 seats. [Henry Puna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Puna) was elected Prime Minister, and Marsters was appointed to Cabinet as Deputy Prime Minister, Minister of Foreign Affairs, and Minister of Mining and Natural Resources.[^cite_note-14] As Minister of Mining and Natural Resources he promoted [seabed mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sea_mining),[^cite_note-15] and negotiated a Regional Seabed Mining Framework through the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum).[^cite_note-16] he began negotiations with the Cook Island's neighbours over marine boundaries,[^cite_note-17] and sent a delegation to the [United Nations](31769.md) in New York to negotiate an extension of the country's continental shelf boundaries under the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md).[^cite_note-18]

## Sovereign's Representative

On 5 June 2013, Marsters was appointed as the [Queen's Representative](2369963.md), replacing [Sir Frederick Goodwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Tutu_Goodwin).[^cite_note-19] He resigned from Parliament on 25 July 2013 to take up the role,[^cite_note-20] precipitating the [2013 Murienua by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Murienua_by-election). In June 2016, he made a rare intervention as Queen's Representative, ruling that Parliament had been properly adjourned and that therefore a purported opposition vote of no-confidence ousting the government was of no effect.[^cite_note-21] He was reappointed for a second three-year term in July 2016,[^cite_note-22] and a third one in 2019.[^cite_note-23]

In the [2018 Queen's Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Birthday_Honours), Marsters was appointed a Knight Commander of the [Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire).[^cite_note-24] His investiture took place in November 2018 in a ceremony at Buckingham Palace.[^cite_note-25]

In January 2019, Marsters helped turn the first sod and lay the first stones of Marsters House, a hostel for the various branches of the Marsters family in [Rarotonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga).[^cite_note-26] The hostel was opened in July 2019.[^cite_note-27]

In August 2025, Marsters was reappointed as the representative for a fifth three-year term.[^cite_note-28]
