**Mirra Aleksandrovna Andreeva**[^cite_note-3] (born 29 April 2007) is a Russian professional [tennis](29773.md) player. She has been [ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_rankings) by the [WTA](57768.md) as high as world No. 5 in singles, achieved in July 2025, and No. 12 in doubles, achieved in September 2025. Andreeva has won six [WTA Tour](1398173.md)–level singles titles, including a [major](197638.md#Tournaments) at the [2026 French Open](83033049.md) and two [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000) events.[^cite_note-4] In doubles, Andreeva has won three titles, including two WTA 1000 events, and a silver medal at the [2024 Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), partnering with [Diana Shnaider](68209798.md).[^cite_note-5]

As a junior, Andreeva finished runner-up at [2023 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles). In 2023, she advanced to the fourth rounds at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as a wildcard and at the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as a qualifier, finishing the season inside the top 50. In 2024, she made her first Grand Slam semifinal at [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), beating world No. 2 [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md), and won her first WTA Tour title at the [Iași Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ia%C8%99i_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). In 2025, she won two WTA 1000 titles back-to-back at the [Dubai Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). In 2026, she won her first major title at the French Open.&#x20;

## Early life

Mirra Andreeva was born in 2007 in [Krasnoyarsk](72233.md), Russia,[^cite_note-izvestia-6] to Raisa and Alexander Andreev.[^cite_note-town-7] She has an elder sister, [Erika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erika_Andreeva).[^cite_note-town-7] When Erika was born, Raisa and Alexander debated between tennis and volleyball for their daughters.[^cite_note-town-7] Raisa enrolled Erika in a tennis program first, and Mirra started playing tennis later, at the age of six.[^cite_note-town-7] Mirra first trained in the Krasnoyarsk Tennis Hall club under the guidance of Marina Pavlova.[^cite_note-izvestia-6][^cite_note-gateway-8] To have better training opportunities, her family moved to [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi), where Kirill Kryukov became the sisters' coach.[^cite_note-gateway-8] The sisters also trained at the J-Pro Tennis Academy in [Moscow](19004.md).[^cite_note-izvestia-6]

## Career

Andreeva is a former world No. 1 junior, a position she reached on 29 May 2023.[^cite_note-9]

She was a finalist at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles) girls' final in 2023, losing to [Alina Korneeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alina_Korneeva) in three sets. Their final match took 3 hours and 18 minutes, 22 minutes longer than the men's final.[^cite_note-WLM_Tennis-10]

In April 2023, Andreeva became the only player in the history of the ITF World Tennis Tour to win multiple titles at the W60 level or above before the age of 16.[^cite_note-ITF_Tennis-11]

### 2022: WTA Tour debut

Andreeva made her [WTA Tour](1398173.md) main-draw debut at the [Jasmin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jasmin_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), after receiving a wildcard for the singles event.[^cite_note-12] However, she lost in the first round against sixth seed [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova), in a 2 hours and 35 minutes three-setter.[^cite_note-13]

### 2023: Wimbledon fourth round, top 50

At 15 years of age, ranked No. 194, Andreeva received a wildcard into the main draw of the WTA 1000 [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-leylah-14] She won her first match against [Leylah Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leylah_Fernandez), becoming the third youngest player to win a main-draw match at a WTA 1000 tournament, behind Coco Gauff and [CiCi Bellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CiCi_Bellis),[^cite_note-15] and the second 15-year-old to defeat a top-50 opponent at a WTA 1000 tournament after Bellis. Next, she defeated No. 14 [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia) for her first top-20 win, becoming the seventh player to defeat a top-20 opponent before the age of 16 in the 21st century.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] On her 16th birthday, she claimed victory over another top-20 player, No. 19 [Magda Linette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_Linette).[^cite_note-18] Though she lost in the last 16 to world No. 2 [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md),[^cite_note-19] her ranking jumped to No. 146.[^cite_note-20]

Andreeva made her Grand Slam tournament debut at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) after qualifying. She defeated [Alison Riske-Amritraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Riske-Amritraj) to record her first major win.[^cite_note-21] Next, she defeated wildcard player [Diane Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Parry). As a result, she became the seventh player in the past 30 years to reach the third round of the French Open before turning 17.[^cite_note-22] She lost in the third round to No. 6 [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff) in three sets.[^cite_note-23] As a result, her ranking rose to No. 101.[^cite_note-24]



Andreeva at the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)

Andreeva made her main-draw debut at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) after qualifying.[^cite_note-25] She defeated [Wang Xiyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xiyu) in the first round,[^cite_note-26] and reached the third round when No. 11 [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) retired due to injury while trailing by a set and a double break of serve.[^cite_note-27] Next, she defeated No. 23 Anastasia Potapova, becoming the youngest player to make the fourth round of Wimbledon since Coco Gauff in 2019.[^cite_note-28] Despite losing to No. 18 [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys) in the fourth round in three sets,[^cite_note-29] her ranking rose to No. 64.[^cite_note-30]

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated wildcard entrant [Olivia Gadecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Gadecki)[^cite_note-31] before falling to eventual champion Coco Gauff.[^cite_note-32] Entering the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) after qualifying, she defeated No. 12 Barbora Krejčíková[^cite_note-33] and [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova)[^cite_note-34] to reach the third round, where she lost to No. 5 [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md) in three sets.[^cite_note-35] Andreeva finished the year at world No. 46[^cite_note-36] and was named [WTA Newcomer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Awards).[^cite_note-37]

### 2024: French semis, Iași title, top 20

Andreeva started her 2024 season at the [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). She defeated [Diana Shnaider](68209798.md),[^cite_note-38] No. 15 [Liudmila Samsonova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova),[^cite_note-39] and wildcard entrant [Arina Rodionova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arina_Rodionova),[^cite_note-40] without dropping a set, to reach her first WTA Tour quarterfinal. She lost in the quarterfinals to [Linda Nosková](67929086.md) in straight sets.[^cite_note-41] At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she defeated [Bernarda Pera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernarda_Pera)[^cite_note-42] in the first round. In the second round, she defeated No. 6 [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur) for her first top-10 win, becoming the youngest player to defeat a top-10 player at the Australian Open since Coco Gauff in 2020 and the youngest player in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) to hand a top-10 player a first-set [bagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel_\(tennis\)) at a major tournament.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] In the third round, she defeated Diane Parry, becoming the youngest player since Coco Gauff to have made two round of 16 major appearances.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-ao2024-46] Although she lost in the last 16 to No. 11 Barbora Krejčíková in three sets,[^cite_note-47] her ranking rose to No. 35.[^cite_note-48]

At the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Taylor Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Townsend),[^cite_note-49] Linda Nosková,[^cite_note-50] and No. 7 [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1)[^cite_note-51] to reach the fourth round. On her 17th birthday, she defeated No. 13 [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md) in straight sets to reach her first WTA 1000 quarterfinal.[^cite_note-52] She lost in the quarterfinals to world No. 2 Aryna Sabalenka in straight sets.[^cite_note-53]

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Emina Bektas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emina_Bektas),[^cite_note-54] No. 21 [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka),[^cite_note-55] and [Peyton Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Stearns) to reach the fourth round, becoming the youngest player to reach the fourth round of a major on all three surfaces since [Anna Kournikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Kournikova) in 1998.[^cite_note-56] She defeated [Varvara Gracheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varvara_Gracheva) to reach the quarterfinals, becoming the youngest woman to reach the quarterfinals at Roland Garros since [Sesil Karatantcheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesil_Karatantcheva) in 2005.[^cite_note-57] She defeated world No. 2 Aryna Sabalenka in three sets to reach the semifinals, becoming the youngest player to reach the semifinals of the French Open since [Martina Hingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Hingis) in 1997.[^cite_note-fo2024-58] She lost in the semifinals to No. 15 Jasmine Paolini in straight sets.[^cite_note-59] As a result, Andreeva reached a new career-high ranking of world No. 23.[^cite_note-fo2024-58]

Andreeva went out of [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) in the first round, losing to [Brenda Fruhvirtová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Fruhvirtov%C3%A1).[^cite_note-60]



Andreeva with [Diana Shnaider](68209798.md) at the [2024 Olympic doubles final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles)

Andreeva captured her first WTA Tour title at the [Iași Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ia%C8%99i_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), when [Elina Avanesyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elina_Avanesyan) retired due to injury.[^cite_note-\:0-61] She became the youngest woman to win a tour-level singles title since Coco Gauff in 2021.[^cite_note-\:0-61] At the [Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), Andreeva partnered with Diana Shnaider to win silver in the women's doubles, losing in the final to [Sara Errani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Errani) and Jasmine Paolini.[^cite_note-62]

At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated No. 13 [Emma Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Navarro),[^cite_note-63] [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1) and No. 5 Jasmine Paolini[^cite_note-64] to reach her second WTA 1000 quarterfinal. She lost in the quarterfinals to world No. 1 [Iga Świątek](51838558.md) in three sets.[^cite_note-65] At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she defeated [Camila Osorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Osorio)[^cite_note-66] before losing to [Ashlyn Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashlyn_Krueger).[^cite_note-67]

At the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Irina-Camelia Begu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu),[^cite_note-68] No. 20 [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87),[^cite_note-69] and Magda Linette[^cite_note-top20-70] to reach her third WTA 1000 quarterfinal. She lost in the quarterfinals to No. 7 [Zheng Qinwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen) in three sets.[^cite_note-71] After the China Open, Andreeva broke into the top 20, becoming the youngest woman to achieve this milestone since [Nicole Vaidišová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Vaidi%C5%A1ov%C3%A1) in 2005.[^cite_note-top20-70][^cite_note-72] At the [Ningbo Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ningbo_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), Andreeva defeated qualifier [Varvara Lepchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varvara_Lepchenko)[^cite_note-73] and lucky loser [Tamara Korpatsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Korpatsch) to reach the quarterfinals. She reached her first WTA 500 final after No. 13 Barbora Krejčíková[^cite_note-74] and [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md) retired.[^cite_note-75] She lost in the final to No. 11 [Daria Kasatkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina) in three sets.[^cite_note-76]

### 2025: Two WTA 1000 titles, top 5

Andreeva started her 2025 season at the [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). She overcame [Anna Blinkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Blinkova),[^cite_note-77] No. 26 Linda Nosková,[^cite_note-78] and Ons Jabeur[^cite_note-79] to reached the semifinals, without dropping a set. She lost in the semifinals to world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka in straight sets.[^cite_note-80] At the same tournament in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), Andreeva partnered with Diana Shnaider to win her first WTA Tour doubles title, defeating [Priscilla Hon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priscilla_Hon) and [Anna Kalinskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya) in the final.[^cite_note-81] At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva overcame [Marie Bouzková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1),[^cite_note-82] [Moyuka Uchijima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moyuka_Uchijima),[^cite_note-83] and No. 26 [Magdalena Fręch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Fr%C4%99ch)[^cite_note-84] to reach the fourth round, where she lost again to Aryna Sabalenka in straight sets.[^cite_note-85]

At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated Elina Avanesyan,[^cite_note-86] Markéta Vondroušová,[^cite_note-87] and Peyton Stearns,[^cite_note-88] without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated world No. 2 Iga Świątek in straight sets to reach her first WTA 1000 semifinal,[^cite_note-89] becoming the youngest player to reach the semifinals in the tournament’s history.[^cite_note-90] She defeated No. 7 Elena Rybakina in three sets to reach her first WTA 1000 final.[^cite_note-semi-91] She defeated [Clara Tauson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Tauson) in straight sets to win her first WTA 1000 title. She became the youngest player to reach and win a WTA 1000 final since the WTA 1000 format was introduced in 2009.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-dubai-94] She also became the youngest player to beat three Grand Slam champions in a single tournament since [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) did so in 2004.[^cite_note-semi-91] Following her win in Dubai, Andreeva made her top 10 debut, making her the youngest person to be ranked in the top 10 since Nicole Vaidišová in 2006.[^cite_note-dubai-94]

At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated Varvara Gracheva,[^cite_note-95] No. 21 Clara Tauson,[^cite_note-96] and No. 7 Elena Rybakina,[^cite_note-97] without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated No. 23 [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md) in straight sets to reach the semifinals, becoming the youngest player to reach multiple WTA 1000 semifinals since 2009.[^cite_note-elina-98] She defeated defending champion Iga Świątek in three sets to reach the final.[^cite_note-99] She defeated world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka in three sets to claim back-to-back WTA 1000 titles.[^cite_note-indian-100] She became the tournament’s third-youngest female champion after Martina Hingis in 1998 and [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams) in 1999.[^cite_note-indian-100] She also became the youngest woman since Hingis in 1997 to win back-to-back WTA 1000 events and the youngest player in 40 years to defeat world No. 1 and No. 2 at a WTA event.[^cite_note-indian-100] After her victory at Indian Wells, Andreeva rose to a career-high ranking of No. 6.[^cite_note-indian-100]

At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Veronika Kudermetova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova)[^cite_note-101] before losing to No. 17 [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova).[^cite_note-102] Nevertheless, at the same tournament in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles), Andreeva won her first WTA 1000 doubles title alongside Shnaider, defeating [Cristina Bucșa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Buc%C8%99a) and [Miyu Kato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyu_Kato_\(tennis\)) in the final.[^cite_note-103]

At the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated Marie Bouzkova,[^cite_note-104] No. 27 Magdalena Fręch,[^cite_note-105] and qualifier [Yuliia Starodubtseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuliia_Starodubtseva)[^cite_note-106] to reach the quarterfinals, without dropping a set. She lost in the quarterfinals to No. 4 Coco Gauff in straight sets.[^cite_note-107] At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated qualifier [Emiliana Arango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliana_Arango),[^cite_note-108] Linda Nosková,[^cite_note-109] and No. 23 Clara Tauson[^cite_note-110] to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost again to Coco Gauff in straight sets.[^cite_note-111] At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated Cristina Bucșa,[^cite_note-112] Ashlyn Krueger,[^cite_note-113] [Yulia Putintseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva),[^cite_note-114] and No. 17 Daria Kasatkina,[^cite_note-115] without dropping a set, to reach her second straight French Open quarterfinal. She lost in the quarterfinals to wildcard entrant [Loïs Boisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo%C3%AFs_Boisson) in straight sets.[^cite_note-116]

At the [Bad Homburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bad_Homburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), Andreeva defeated Clara Tauson to reach the quarterfinals,[^cite_note-117] where she lost to Linda Nosková in straight sets. At the [Wimbledon Championships](80007712.md), Andreeva defeated [Mayar Sherif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayar_Sherif),[^cite_note-118] [Lucia Bronzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucia_Bronzetti),[^cite_note-119] [Hailey Baptiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailey_Baptiste),[^cite_note-120] and No. 10 Emma Navarro, without dropping a set, to reach her first Wimbledon quarterfinal.[^cite_note-\:1-121] She became the youngest player to reach Wimbledon quarterfinals since Nicole Vaidišová in 2007.[^cite_note-\:1-121] She lost in the quarterfinals to [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic) in straight sets.[^cite_note-122] As a result, Andreeva broke into the top 5 on rankings, the youngest player to do so since Maria Sharapova in 2004.[^cite_note-123]

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Alycia Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alycia_Parks)[^cite_note-124] and Anastasia Potapova[^cite_note-125] before losing to Taylor Townsend.[^cite_note-126]

At the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated wildcard entrant [Zhu Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Lin_\(tennis\)) and [Jéssica Bouzas Maneiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9ssica_Bouzas_Maneiro) before losing to [Sonay Kartal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonay_Kartal).[^cite_note-127] At the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), she lost her opening match against [Laura Siegemund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Siegemund).[^cite_note-128] At the [Ningbo Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ningbo_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), she lost her opening match to Zhu Lin.[^cite_note-\:2-129] Due to her early losses in China, Andreeva finished the year as No. 9.[^cite_note-\:2-129][^cite_note-130] Andreeva and Shnaider qualified for the doubles event at the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Doubles),[^cite_note-131] but they were eliminated in the group stages.[^cite_note-132]

### 2026: French Open champion

Main article: [2026 Mirra Andreeva tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mirra_Andreeva_tennis_season)

Andreeva began her 2026 season at the [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). She defeated qualifier Olivia Gadecki[^cite_note-133] and No. 12 Linda Nosková[^cite_note-134] to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 26 [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk) in straight sets.[^cite_note-135]

At the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Adelaide_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated lucky loser Marie Bouzková,[^cite_note-136] [Maya Joint](75466424.md),[^cite_note-137] No. 23 Diana Shnaider,[^cite_note-138] and No. 17 [Victoria Mboko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Mboko) to win the title, without dropping a set en route.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

At the [Australian Open](81715708.md), Andreeva defeated Donna Vekić,[^cite_note-141] [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari),[^cite_note-142] and [Elena-Gabriela Ruse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena-Gabriela_Ruse) to reach the fourth round,[^cite_note-143] where she lost to No. 12 Elina Svitolina in straight sets.[^cite_note-144]

At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), Andreeva defeated Magda Linette[^cite_note-145] before losing to No. 13 Victoria Mboko.[^cite_note-146] As the defending champion at the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva advanced to the third round after Daria Kasatkina withdrew due to injury.[^cite_note-147] She defeated [Jaqueline Cristian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaqueline_Cristian) to reach the quarterfinals,[^cite_note-148] where she lost to No. 6 Amanda Anisimova in three sets.[^cite_note-149]

As the defending champion at the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Solana Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solana_Sierra)[^cite_note-150] before losing to [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1).[^cite_note-151] In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she defeated [McCartney Kessler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCartney_Kessler)[^cite_note-152] and Marie Bouzkova[^cite_note-153] before losing again to No. 9 Victoria Mboko.[^cite_note-154]

Opening her clay season at the [Linz Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Singles), Andreeva overcame wildcard entrant [Sloane Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloane_Stephens),[^cite_note-155] No. 29 [Sorana Cîrstea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea),[^cite_note-156] Elena-Gabriela Ruse,[^cite_note-157] and Anastasia Potapova to win her second title of the season.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

At the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles), Andreeva defeated defending champion [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko) in the first round in three sets.[^cite_note-160] She defeated qualifier Alycia Parks to reach the quarterfinals,[^cite_note-161] where she defeated No. 4 Iga Świątek in three sets.[^cite_note-162] She lost in the semifinals to No. 2 Elena Rybakina in straight sets.[^cite_note-163]

At the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva overcame lucky loser [Panna Udvardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panna_Udvardy),[^cite_note-164] qualifier [Dalma Galfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalma_Galfi),[^cite_note-165] [Anna Bondár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Bond%C3%A1r),[^cite_note-166] No. 25 Leylah Fernandez[^cite_note-167] and Hailey Baptiste[^cite_note-168] to reach her third WTA 1000 final. In the final, she lost to No. 23 Marta Kostyuk in straight sets.[^cite_note-169] At the same tournament, she also made it into the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles) final with Diana Shnaider,[^cite_note-170] losing to second seeds Kateřina Siniaková and Taylor Townsend.[^cite_note-171]

At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Andreeva defeated [Antonia Ružić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Ru%C5%BEi%C4%87),[^cite_note-172] [Viktorija Golubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktorija_Golubic),[^cite_note-173] and No. 22 [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens)[^cite_note-174] to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 4 Coco Gauff in three sets.[^cite_note-175] At the same event, she teamed with Shnaider to win her second WTA 1000 [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_doubles) title, defeating seventh seeds Cristina Bucșa and [Nicole Melichar-Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Melichar-Martinez) in the final.[^cite_note-176]

Seeded eighth at the [French Open](83033049.md), Andreeva recorded wins over wildcard entrant [Fiona Ferro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Ferro),[^cite_note-177] qualifier [Marina Bassols Ribera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Bassols_Ribera),[^cite_note-178] No. 28 Marie Bouzková,[^cite_note-179] and [Jil Teichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jil_Teichmann)[^cite_note-180] to reach her third straight French Open quarterfinal. She defeated No. 18 Sorana Cîrstea to advance to her second major semifinal.[^cite_note-181] In a rematch of their Madrid final, she defeated No. 15 Marta Kostyuk in straight sets to reach her first major final.[^cite_note-182] In the final, she defeated qualifier [Maja Chwalińska](53209114.md) in straight sets to win her first major title.[^cite_note-183] She became the youngest [French Open](147724.md) champion since [Monica Seles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Seles) in 1992.[^cite_note-184]

Moving onto the grass court swing of the season, Andreeva lost her first match since her Roland Garros triumph, going down to a straight-sets defeat against [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova) at the [Bad Homburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bad_Homburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-185] At [Wimbledon](83354904.md), she overcame Magda Linette[^cite_note-186] before losing to 2024 champion [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) in the second round.[^cite_note-187]

## Coaches

In January 2022, Mirra and [Erika Andreeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erika_Andreeva) joined the Elite Tennis Center in [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes), France.[^cite_note-izvestia-6]

In April 2024, Mirra Andreeva hired [Conchita Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchita_Mart%C3%ADnez) to be her coach.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-="conchita"-189] Martínez is a former [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) champion and has previously coached [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza) and [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1).[^cite_note-="conchita"-189]
