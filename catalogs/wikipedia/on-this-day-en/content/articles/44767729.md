**Samuel Yves Mézec** (born 26 December 1990) is a Jersey politician who served as a member of the [States Assembly](2860948.md) from 2014 to 2026, first as a Deputy, then as a Senator and subsequently again as a Deputy. He held ministerial office as Minister for Children and Housing from 2018 to 2020 and as Minister for Housing and Communities from 2024 to 2026. He led [Reform Jersey](43334542.md) from 2019 to 2026.

## Political career

Mézec was elected as the Deputy for the St. Helier No. 2 district after winning that seat in a [by-election](155434.md) on [5 March 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Jersey_by-elections). He held the seat in the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_election) held later that year on [15 October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Jersey_general_election).

He co-founded and, until June 2019,[^cite_note-1] was the Chairman of [Reform Jersey](43334542.md), one of the island's political parties.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] He stood in the Senatorial by-election held on 7 September 2016 but came second to [Sarah Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Ferguson_\(politician\)).

Mézec was elected as Senator in the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Jersey_general_election) with 40.8% of the popular vote, and on 3 July 2018, he was the first politician to take on the combined role of Minister of Children and Housing.[^cite_note-4]

After two and a half years in government, on 8 November 2020 Mézec resigned from his Ministerial role to support a vote of no confidence against the Chief Minister, [Senator John Le Fondré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Le_Fondr%C3%A9).[^cite_note-5]

For the 2022 general election, the position of Senator was abolished and all candidates stood as Deputies or Constables,[^cite_note-6] meaning Mézec would be running again for his previous role as a [Deputy](2860948.md#Composition). Electoral reforms introduced that year also saw Jersey's [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) changed, with Mézec's local district now being called 'St Helier South' instead of 'St Helier No. 2'. He was re-elected, along with nine other Reform Jersey members.

Following the party's election success, Deputy Mézec challenged [Kristina Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristina_Moore) for the role of [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Jersey),[^cite_note-7] but he was defeated by 39 votes to 10. He was later elected to Deputy Moore's previous role leading the island's Scrutiny function as the Chairman of the [Corporate Services Scrutiny Panel](2860948.md#Scrutiny_functions).

In January 2024, the [Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristina_Moore) government, led by the [Better Way movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Way_2022), was ousted by Reform Jersey and a group of independents in a [vote of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no_confidence). This led to a [new government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Jersey\)) with [Lyndon Farnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Farnham) as Chief Minister and Sam Mézec as Minister for Housing and Communities.[^cite_note-8]

Mezec lost his seat in the States Assembley at the [2026 General Election](74947690.md) after finishing tenth in the election for [Senator](2860948.md#Composition).[^cite_note-9] He later said it would be the job of "new leadership" to take Reform Jersey forward.[^cite_note-10]
