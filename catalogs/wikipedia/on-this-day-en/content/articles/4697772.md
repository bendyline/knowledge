# Rare-earth mineral



Rare-earth ore, shown with a United States penny for size comparison

A **rare-earth mineral** is a [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) that contains one or more [rare-earth elements](145440.md) as major metal constituents. Rare-earths are to be distinguished from [critical minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_minerals), which are materials of strategic or economic importance that are defined differently by different countries.

## Definition and nomenclature

Minerals are solids composed of various inorganic elements,[^cite_note-1] mixed through processes such as evaporation, pressure or other physical changes.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

Rare-earth minerals are rare because rare-earth elements have unique geochemical properties that prevent them from easily forming minerals,[^cite_note-\:04-4] and are therefore not normally found in deposits large or concentrated enough for mining.[^cite_note-\:04-4] This is the reason they are called "rare earths".[^cite_note-\:04-4][^cite_note-5]

Rare-earth minerals contain one or more of the 17 rare-earth elements, 15 of which are known as the [lanthanides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanthanide), the other two being [scandium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandium) and [yttrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yttrium).[^cite_note-\:12-6]

Rare-earth elements or minerals are distinct from minerals or materials described as [critical minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_minerals) or raw materials, which refers to materials that are considered to be of strategic or economic importance to a country. There is no single list, but individual governments compile lists of materials that are critical for their own economies.[^cite_note-7]

## Common minerals containing rare-earth elements

Rare-earth minerals are usually found in association with [alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline) to [peralkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peralkaline) [igneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous) magmas in [pegmatites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegmatite) or with [carbonatite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonatite) [intrusives](1106531.md). [Perovskite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perovskite_\(structure\)) mineral phases are common hosts to rare-earth elements within the alkaline complexes. [Mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_\(geology\))-derived carbonate melts are also carriers of rare earths. [Hydrothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal) deposits associated with alkaline [magmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magmatism) contain a variety of rare-earth minerals.

## Mining

See also: [Rare-earth element § Production](145440.md#Production)

The presence of rare-earth minerals can be a valuable indicator in geological surveys and mineral resource assessments.[^cite_note-\:22-8] There are over 160 rare-earth minerals known, but only four of these occur in amounts suitable for mining.[^cite_note-9] They can occur in either primary or secondary deposits.[^cite_note-Balaram_1210–1216-10]

[Primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_mineral) deposits result from hydrothermal and igneous processes, while [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_mineral) deposits are sedimentary and formed through weathering processes.[^cite_note-Balaram_1210–1216-10] In the case of primary deposits, the minerals are generally found in the specific location where the elements came together to form the deposit.[^cite_note-\:10-11] Secondary deposits have undergone metamorphic or sedimentary processes in a location different from where the minerals were actually formed.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:03-13] Depending on the type of deposit, various methods can be employed to extract the minerals from both primary and secondary deposits.[^cite_note-\:03-13][^cite_note-Balaram_1210–1216-10]

## Mined rare-earth minerals

### Bastnäsite

[Bastnäsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastn%C3%A4site) is a rare, semi-soluble carbonate mineral, primarily mined for its yttrium, used to make [^cite_note-14] magnets for speakers, microphones, communication devices, and many other modern necessities.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:3-16] Bastnäsite deposits are found in China, Madagascar and the USA.[^cite_note-\:3-16]



Bastnäsite is a dense mineral that contains three carbonate-fluoride atoms

[^cite_note-gandharagems.com-17]

&#x20;and typically forms luminous flattened crystals of a warm yellow honey colour.

[^cite_note-gandharagems.com-17]

### Laterite clays

[Laterite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterite) is a class of materials which contain significant amounts of aluminium and iron.[^cite_note-\:4-18] They can form clays able to hold many minerals within them.[^cite_note-\:4-18] The weathering of rocks by leaching and oxidising conditions results in the formation of clay-like [^cite_note-\:4-18] minerals such as [goethite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethite), [lepidocrocite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidocrocite), and [hematite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematite).[^cite_note-\:4-18] Some of them can hold rare earth minerals as well as iron, nickel and the alumina for which it is often mined.[^cite_note-\:5-19][^cite_note-\:6-20] Laterite results from the weathering of basalt.[^cite_note-\:5-19][^cite_note-\:6-20] It can make a stable basis for construction since it solidifies into rock when exposed to air.[^cite_note-\:6-20] However, its low fertility makes it unsuitable for agricultural use.[^cite_note-\:6-20]

### Monazite

[Monazite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monazite) is a waxy mineral that is formed through the crystallization of igneous rocks and the metamorphism of clastic sedimentary rocks.[^cite_note-\:7-21] This mineral is typically mined in placer deposits, with gold commonly found as a byproduct.[^cite_note-\:7-21] Monazite contains many rare metals such as [neodymium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neodymium), [cerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerium), [lanthanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanthanum), [praseodymium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praseodymium), and samarium, making it a critical material for renewable energy devices.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-\:7-21] Monazite sand and deposits for mining are found in India, Brazil, and Australia.[^cite_note-23]

### Loparite

[Loparite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loparite-\(Ce\)) is a mineral that is mined for the three rare (but not rare earth) elements: [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium), [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium), and [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum) it contains.[^cite_note-\:8-24] Major Loparite deposits can be found in Russia and Paraguay, and although it is present in other countries such as Canada, Norway, Greenland, and Brazil,[^cite_note-\:9-25] Russia remains the primary source for mining this mineral.[^cite_note-\:9-25] The elements in loparite make it useful for conductivity, aircraft assembly, and as a radioactive tracer.[^cite_note-\:8-24]
