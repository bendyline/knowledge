**Chevron Corporation** is an American multinational [energy corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oil_exploration_and_production_companies) predominantly specializing in [oil and gas](994887.md). The second-largest [direct descendant of Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successors_of_Standard_Oil), and originally known as the **Standard Oil Company of California** (shortened to **Socal** or **CalSo**), it is active in more than 180 countries.&#x20;

Founded originally in [Southern California](62520.md) during the 1870s. After the [breakup of Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil_Co._of_New_Jersey_v._United_States), Socal grew quickly on its own by continuing to acquire companies and partnering with others both inside and outside of California, eventually becoming one of the [Big Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Oil) companies that dominated the global petroleum industry from the mid-1940s to the 1970s. In 1985, Socal merged with the Pittsburgh-based [Gulf Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Oil) and rebranded as Chevron; the newly merged company later merged with [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco) in 2001.[^cite_note-6]

Chevron is one of the largest companies in the world and the second-largest oil company based in the United States by revenue, only behind fellow Standard Oil descendant [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil). Within oil and gas, Chevron is [vertically integrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_integration) and is involved in [hydrocarbon exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration), [production](23195.md#Extraction), [refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refining), marketing and transport, chemicals manufacturing and sales, and [power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_generation). Chevron manufactures and sells fuels, lubricants, additives, and petrochemicals, primarily in [Western North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_America), the US [Gulf Coast](11969.md), Southeast Asia, South Korea and Australia. In 2018, the company produced an average of 791,000 barrels (125,800 m<sup>3</sup>) of net oil-equivalent per day in United States.[^cite_note-7] Chevron ranked 10th on the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) in 2023. The company is also the last-remaining oil-and-gas component of the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](47361.md) since the exit of [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil) from the index in 2020.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Chevron has been subject to [numerous controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Chevron) relating to its activities involving [climate change](5042951.md). In particular, one of its most widely covered incidents on social media was its continued denial of ecological damage near the [Lago Agrio oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lago_Agrio_oil_field) and its involvements against [Steven Donziger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Donziger), a controversy rooted and inherited from [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco) when Chevron acquired it in 2001. Chevron has also received negative attention for numerous other oil extraction projects, especially within the Los Angeles metropolitan area and in the Eastern Mediterranean.&#x20;

In 2024, Chevron was responsible for 512 Mt of CO<sub>2</sub> emissions, which was 1.33% of global CO<sub>2</sub> emissions.[^cite_note-10]

## History

### Predecessors

#### Star Oil and Pacific Coast Oil Company

One of Chevron's early predecessors, "Star Oil", discovered oil at the [Pico Canyon Oilfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_Canyon_Oilfield) in the [Santa Susana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Mountains) north of Los Angeles in 1876. The 25 barrels of oil per day well marked the discovery of the Newhall Field, and is considered by [geophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysicist) [Marius Vassiliou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marius_Vassiliou) as the beginning of the modern oil industry in California.[^cite_note-Vassiliou2009-11] Energy analyst [Antonia Juhasz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Juhasz) has said that while Star Oil's founders were influential in establishing an oil industry in California, Union Mattole Company discovered oil in the state eleven years prior.[^cite_note-Juhasz2009-12]

In September 1879, [Charles N. Felton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_N._Felton), [Lloyd Tevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Tevis), George Loomis and others created the "Pacific Coast Oil Company", which acquired the assets of Star Oil[^cite_note-Vassiliou2009-11] with \$1 million in funding.[^cite_note-Karliner1997-13] Pacific Coast Oil eventually became the largest oil interest in California, and in 1900, [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller)'s [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil) acquired Pacific Coast Oil for \$761,000.[^cite_note-Karliner1997-13][^cite_note-Vassiliou2009-11] In 1906, the Pacific Coast acquired the business operations and assets of the [Standard Oil Company (Iowa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil_of_Iowa). At this time, Pacific renamed itself the Standard Oil Company (California).[^cite_note-autogenerated2-14][^cite_note-Falola2005-15]

#### Texaco

Further information: [Texaco § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco#History)

Since the acquisition of the Pacific Coast Oil Company by Standard Oil, the Standard descendant had traditionally worked closely with [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco) for 100 years, before acquiring Texaco outright in 2001. Originally known as the Texas Fuel Company (later the Texas Company), Texaco was founded in [Beaumont, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaumont,_Texas), as an oil-equipment vendor by "Buckskin Joe". The founder's nickname came from being harsh and aggressive.[^cite_note-Juhasz2009-12] Texas Fuel worked closely with Chevron. In 1936, it formed a joint venture with California Standard named [Caltex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltex), to drill and produce oil in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-HallRamírez-Pascualli2012-16] According to energy analyst and activist shareholder [Antonia Juhasz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Juhasz),[^cite_note-huffington280910-17][^cite_note-wsj290910-18][^cite_note-ens140411-19] the Texas Fuel Company and California Standard were often referred to as the "terrible twins" for their cutthroat business practices.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-20][^cite_note-Falola2005-15]

### Formation of the Chevron name



A Chevron station branded under the Standard name in [Paradise, Nevada](124384.md), pictured in 2009

In 1911, the federal government broke Standard Oil into several pieces under the [Sherman Antitrust Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Antitrust_Act). One of those pieces, Standard Oil Co. (California), went on to become Chevron. It became part of the "[Seven Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sisters_\(oil_companies\))", which dominated the world oil industry in the early 20th century. In 1926, the company changed its name to Standard Oil Co. of California (SOCAL).[^cite_note-21] By the terms of the breakup of Standard Oil, at first Standard of California could use the Standard name only within its original geographic area of the Pacific coast states, plus Nevada and Arizona; outside that area, it had to use another name.

Today, Chevron is the owner of the [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil) trademark in 16 states in the western and southeastern United States. Since [American trademark law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_trademark_law) operates under a use-it-or-lose-it rule,[^cite_note-Fishman_Page_242-22] the company owns and operates one Standard-branded Chevron station in each state of the area.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] However, though Chevron (as CalSo) acquired [Kyso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyso) in the 1960s, its status in Kentucky is unclear after Chevron withdrew its brand from retail sales from Kentucky in July 2010.[^cite_note-CSPNet-25][^cite_note-Eigelbach-26]

The 'Chevron' name came into use for some of its retail products in the 1930s. The name "Calso" was also used from 1946 to 1955, in states outside its native West Coast territory.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Standard Oil Company of California ranked 75th among United States corporations in the value of [World War II](32927.md) military production contracts.[^cite_note-29]

In 1933, [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) granted California Standard a concession to find oil, which led to the discovery of oil in 1938. In 1948, California Standard discovered the world's largest oil field in Saudi Arabia, [Ghawar Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghawar_Field).[^cite_note-30] California Standard's subsidiary, California-Arabian Standard Oil Company, grew over the years and became the Arabian American Oil Company (ARAMCO) in 1944. In 1973, the Saudi government began buying into ARAMCO. By 1980, the company was entirely owned by the Saudis, and in 1988, its name was changed to Saudi Arabian Oil Company—[Saudi Aramco](290527.md).[^cite_note-31]

Standard Oil of California and [Gulf Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Oil) merged in 1984,[^cite_note-32] which was the largest merger in history at that time.[^cite_note-gdaebso-33][^cite_note-mwblvr-34] To comply with [U.S. antitrust law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._antitrust_law), California Standard divested many of Gulf's operating subsidiaries, and sold some Gulf stations in the eastern United States and a Philadelphia refinery which has since closed. Among the assets sold off were Gulf's retail outlets in Gulf's home market of [Pittsburgh](25101.md), where Chevron lacks a retail presence but did retain a regional headquarters there between 2010 to 2021, partially for [Marcellus Shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcellus_Formation)-related drilling.[^cite_note-35] The same year, Standard Oil of California also took the opportunity to change its legal name to **Chevron Corporation**, since it had already been using the well-known "Chevron" retail brand name for decades. Chevron would sell the Gulf Oil trademarks for the entire U.S. to [Cumberland Farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_Farms), the parent company of [Gulf Oil LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Oil_LP), in 2010 after Cumberland Farms had a license to the Gulf trademark in the Northeastern United States since 1986.[^cite_note-36]

In 1996, Chevron transferred its natural gas gathering, operating and marketing operation to [NGC Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynegy) (later Dynegy) in exchange for a roughly 25% equity stake in NGC.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Nov-1996-10-Q-37] In a merger completed February 1, 2000, Illinova Corp. became a wholly owned subsidiary of Dynegy Inc. and Chevron's stake increased up to 28%.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Mar-2000-8-K-38] However, in May 2007, Chevron sold its stake in the company for approximately \$985 million, resulting in a gain of \$680 million.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-May-2007-8-K-39][^cite_note-40]

### Acquisitions and diversification



Chevron gas station design used until 2006

#### 2000s

The early 2000s saw Chevron engage in many mergers, acquisitions, and sales, the first largest of which was the \$45 billion acquisition of [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco), announced on October 15, 2000. The acquisition created the second-largest oil company in the United States and the world's fourth-largest publicly traded oil company with a combined market value of approximately \$95 billion.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Oct-2000-8-K-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Completed on October 9, 2001, Chevron temporarily renamed itself to **ChevronTexaco** between 2001 and 2005; after the company reverted its name to Chevron, Texaco became used as a brand by the company for some of its fueling stations.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-May-2005-8-K-45]

2005 also saw Chevron purchase [Unocal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unocal_Corporation) for \$18.4 billion, increasing the company's petroleum and natural gas reserves by about 15%.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Apr-2005-8-K-46][^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Aug-2005-8-K-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Because of Unocal's large South East Asian geothermal operations, Chevron became a large producer of [geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy).[^cite_note-bbc190206-50] The deal did not include Unocal's former retail operations including the [Union 76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76_\(gas_station\)) trademark, as it had sold that off to [Tosco Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosco_Corporation) in 1997.[^cite_note-lat-1996nov19-51] The 76 brand is owned by [Phillips 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_66), unaffiliated with Chevron.

Chevron and the [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory) started a cooperation in 2006, to improve the recovery of hydrocarbons from [oil shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_shale) by developing a [shale oil extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_oil_extraction) process named [Chevron CRUSH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_CRUSH).[^cite_note-gcc-52] In 2006, the [United States Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior) issued a research, development and demonstration lease for Chevron's demonstration oil shale project on public lands in Colorado's [Piceance Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piceance_Basin).[^cite_note-blm-53] In February 2012, Chevron notified the [Bureau of Land Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Land_Management) and the Department of Reclamation, Mining and Safety that it intended to divest this lease.[^cite_note-bizjournals280212-54]

In 2008, Chevron Limited, a subsidiary of Chevron, sold its equity distributor business in the UK to GB Oils Limited for £21.9 million.[^cite_note-55]

#### 2010s

Starting in 2010, Chevron began to reduce its retail footprint and expand in domestic natural gas. In July 2010, Chevron ended retail operations in the Mid-Atlantic United States by removing the Chevron and Texaco names from 1,100 stations.[^cite_note-CSPNet-25][^cite_note-Eigelbach-26] In 2011, Chevron acquired Pennsylvania-based Atlas Energy Inc. for \$3.2 billion in cash and an additional \$1.1 billion in existing debt owed by Atlas.[^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Nov-2010-DFAN14A-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Chevron-Corp-Feb-2011-10-K-58] Three months later, Chevron acquired drilling and development rights for another 228,000 acres in the Marcellus Shale from Chief Oil & Gas LLC and Tug Hill, Inc.[^cite_note-59] In September 2013, [Total S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_S.A.) and its joint-venture partner agreed to buy Chevron's retail distribution business in Pakistan for an undisclosed amount.[^cite_note-60]

In October 2014, Chevron announced that it would sell a 30 percent holding in its Canadian oil shale holdings to [Kuwait](7515890.md)'s state-owned oil company [Kuwait Oil Company](1437937.md) for a fee of \$1.5 billion.[^cite_note-61] Despite these sales, Chevron continued to explore acquisitions, a trend which had reinvigorated in 2019 and extended throughout the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). In April 2019, Chevron announced its intention to acquire Anadarko Petroleum in a deal valued at \$33 billion, but decided to focus on other acquisitions shortly afterwards when a deal could not be reached.[^cite_note-62] Despite the failed acquisition of Anadarko, Chevron did acquire [Noble Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Energy) for \$5 billion in July 2020.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

Chevron sold its [North Sea](21179.md) operations in May 2019 to [Ithaca Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca_Energy) for \$2 billion.[^cite_note-65]

Chevron was not spared from the pandemic, however, as Chevron announced reductions of 10–15% of its workforce due to both the pandemic and a [2020 oil price war between Russia and Saudi Arabia](63341569.md).[^cite_note-\:1-66] During the pandemic, Chevron considered a merger with rival ExxonMobil in 2020 during the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic that drove oil demand sharply down. It would have been one of the largest corporate mergers in history, and a combined Chevron and ExxonMobil (dubbed "Chexxon" by [Reuters](18998750.md)) would have been the second-largest oil company in the world, trailing only [Saudi Aramco](290527.md).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

Later in the pandemic, Chevron began requiring some employees, namely expatriate employees, those working overseas, and workers on U.S.-flagged ships, to receive COVID-19 vaccinations after having some key operations, the off-shore platforms off the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) and [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)) for example. The requirement will begin for workers off the Gulf of Mexico on the first of November.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

#### 2020s

In the 2020s, Chevron's primary focus was on alternative energy solutions, gradual pullouts from Africa and Southeast Asia, and an increased focus on the Americas with a lessened albeit still present interest in natural gas. Chevron in February 2020 joined Marubeni Corporation and WAVE Equity Partners in investing in Carbon Clean Solutions, a company that provides portable carbon capture technology for the oil field and other industrial facilities.[^cite_note-71] Two years later, Chevron announced that it will acquire [Renewable Energy Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_Energy_Group), a biodiesel production company based in [Ames, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames,_Iowa). The acquisition was completed just under four months later on June 13.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

In the Americas, Chevron acquired natural gas company Beyond6, LLC (B6) and its network of 55 [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) stations across the United States from [Mercuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercuria) in November 2022. However, Chevron's largest American moves in the 2020s were in [Venezuela](32374.md), as the [Biden administration](65732174.md) relaxed restrictions on Chevron from pumping oil in the South American nation, originally imposed due to corruption scandals and human rights violations by Venezuelan president [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md). The relaxed restrictions, however, came with severe limitations, including provisions which prohibited Chevron from selling to Russian or Iranian-affiliated agencies and from allowing any direct profits to go to Venezuelan oil company [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

On November 29, 2022, Venezuelan Government Petroleum Minister [Tarek El Aissami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarek_El_Aissami) met in [Caracas](55289.md), Venezuela, with the president of Chevron, Javier La Rosa. The Venezuelan ruling party said it was committed to "the development of oil production" after the easing of sanctions.

The most important joint ventures where Chevron is involved in Venezuela are Petroboscán, in the west of the nation, and Petropiar, in the eastern [Orinoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco) Belt, with a production capacity of close to 180,000 barrels per day between both projects. In the case of Petroboscán, current production is nil and, in Petropiar, current records indicate close to 50,000 barrels per day.[^cite_note-76]

On March 20, 2023, Tareck El Aissami resigned from his government post amid serious corruption allegations. Moreover, El Aissami, a longtime Maduro ally, has a \$10mn US government reward on his head for allegedly facilitating drug trafficking from Venezuela. He played a key role in helping Nicolas Maduro's government dodge US economic sanctions, using his [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian) and [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) parentage to open up new business channels to [Iran](14653.md) and [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-77]

On January 5, 2022, Chevron temporarily decreased production in [Kazakhstan](16642.md)'s [Tengiz Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengiz_Field) due to the [2022 Kazakh protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kazakh_protests), which were motivated by heavy oil price increases.[^cite_note-78] Later that month, Chevron also announced it would end all operations in [Myanmar](19457.md), citing rampant human rights abuses and deteriorating rule of law since the [2021 Myanmar coup d'état](66567997.md).[^cite_note-79] A statement released by the company on its website stated while Chevron was committed to an orderly exit which ensured it could still provide energy to Southeast Asia, Chevron remained firmly opposed to the human rights violations committed by the current [military rule in Myanmar](58874076.md).[^cite_note-80]

Also in 2022, Chevron was reported to explore the sale of stakes in three fields located in [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md). It was suggested by [Reuters](18998750.md) that the sales are intended to attract smaller oil companies.[^cite_note-81]

Chevron, however, did not do business in the 2020s without controversy and regulatory obstacles. [Chevron Phillips Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Phillips_Chemical), a company jointly owned by Chevron and [Phillips 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_66), agreed to pay \$118 million in March 2022 as a result of violating the [Clean Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_\(United_States\)) at three of its chemical production plants in Texas. According to the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) and [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md), Chevron and Phillips failed to properly [flare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_flare) at the plants, causing excess air pollution. The companies agreed to add pollution control systems to the plants as well.[^cite_note-82]

Despite the major oil and gas companies, including Chevron, reporting sharp rises in interim revenues and profits due to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the world's-largest oil companies received immense backlash for such profits. In total, Chevron made US\$246.3 billion in revenue and \$36.5 billion in profit within 2022, both of which are records for the company. In addition, days before the company reported its full year earnings, Chevron increased its dividend and announced a \$75 billion [stock buyback program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_repurchase), a move which attracted a heated response from the Biden administration as well as from news commentators within the United States.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

The 2020s also saw efforts by Chevron to expand into the clean energy industry. Across the 2020s, Chevron invested stakes into [fusion power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power) companies, the two largest of them being [Zap Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zap_Energy) and [TAE Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAE_Technologies).[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] September 2023 saw Chevron acquire a majority stake in a Utah hydrogen storage facility, which is poised to be the world's largest storage facility for [hydrogen in renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_production).[^cite_note-91]

In October 2023, Chevron Corporation acquired [Hess Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hess_Corporation) in an all-stock deal for \$53 billion.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] The acquisition, which was announced on October 23, 2023, opens up new opportunities in the US shale plays and in oil-rich [Guyana](17238691.md). Hess Corporation, [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil) and China's [CNOOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNOOC), key players in Guyana, have been producing a combined 400,000 [barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel) per day from two offshore projects. With the potential to develop 10 more projects, Guyana becomes one of the fastest-growing oil regions that Chevron now has access to.[^cite_note-94] Hess approved the merger in May 2024, but the deal was held up by an arbitration claim from ExxonMobil, which argued that it had right of first refusal on Hess's Guyana oil field shares.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] In September 2024, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission) cleared the merger if Hess CEO [John B. Hess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Hess) is refused a place on Chevron’s board.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

In 2023, Chevron entered into a 10-year agreement with state-owned [Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Petroleum_Corporation_Ltd) (HPCL) to license, manufacture, distribute and sell Chevron [lubricants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubricant) in [India](14533.md) under the [Caltex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltex) brand.[^cite_note-99]

In August 2024, Chevron earmarked \$1 billion in a new research and development (R\&D) hub called Chevron Engineering and Innovation Excellence Center (ENGINE) in [Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore), India.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] The ENGINE facility opened in October 2025.[^cite_note-102]

In October 2024, Chevron sold some of its [Kuparuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuparuk_River_oil_field) and [Prudhoe Bay Oil Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudhoe_Bay_Oil_Field) in [Alaska](624.md) to [ConocoPhillips Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConocoPhillips_Alaska) for \$300 million.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

In February 2025, Chevron announced plans to cut 20% of its workforce by the end of 2026.[^cite_note-105] The company aims to reduce its payroll expenses by \$2 to \$3 billion.[^cite_note-106]

On February 26, Chevron had its license revoked by [Donald Trump](4848272.md), forcing the company to cease operations in [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-107]

On August 15, 2025, Chevron resumed Venezuelan oil exports to the U.S. after receiving a new Treasury license, with two tankers carrying heavy crude bound for Texas and the West Coast.[^cite_note-108]

In April 2026, Chevron agreed to an [asset swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_swap) with Venezuela's state-owned oil company [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA), increasing its interest in the Petroindependencia [joint venture](538782.md) to 49% and giving its Petropiar joint venture rights to develop the adjacent Ayacucho 8 area in the Orinoco Oil Belt. in exchange, Chevron transferred several interests in Venezuelan oil and gas assets to PDVSA.[^cite_note-109]

In July 2026, one year after completing its acquisition of [Hess Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hess_Corporation), Chevron reported that it had fully integrated Hess and had achieved \$1.5 billion in cost synergies, exceeding its initial target of 50% and reaching the target 6 months ahead of schedule.[^cite_note-110]

## Corporate image

### Logo evolution

The first logo featured the legend "Pacific Coast Oil Co.", the name adopted by the company when it was established in 1879. Successive versions showed the word 'Standard' (for "The Standard Oil of California"). In 1968, the company introduced the word 'Chevron' (which was introduced as a brand in the 1930s) for the first time in its logo. In July 2014, the Chevron Corporation logo design was officially changed, although it has been used since 2000. By 2015, the logo had been changed multiple times, with three different color schemes applied in the logo. The logo was gray, then blue, and then turned red before returning to the silver gray it is today.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

### "Human Energy"

Chevron today is well known for its slogan "the human energy company", a campaign first launched in September 2007. In a corporate blog, Chevron states "human energy" was chosen as its campaign's slogan and focus because "human energy captures our positive spirit in delivering energy to a rapidly changing world". The slogan remains prominent in Chevron advertising, and Chevron has derived from this slogan to use phrases in marketing such as "it's only human".[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

## Operations

As of December 31, 2025, Chevron had 43,039 employees, including 37,860 non-service-station employees, of whom 19,366 were based in the United States.[^cite_note-118]

Chevron's dominant regions of production are North America, which produces 1.2 billion barrels of oil equivalent (BBOE), and Eurasia, which produces 1.4 BBOE. Chevron's Eurasian-Pacific operations are concentrated in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Southeast Asia](28741.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [Bangladesh](3454.md), and [greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China). Chevron additionally operates in South America, the west coast of [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) (mainly [Nigeria](21383.md) and [Angola](701.md)), [Egypt](8087628.md), and [Iraq](7515928.md); these four regions collectively produce 0.4 BBOE. Chevron's largest revenue products are [shale and tight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_oil_extraction), though produces considerable revenue from [heavy oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_sands), [deepwater offshore drilling](9969688.md), conventional oil, and [liquefied natural gas](832128.md).[^cite_note-119]

In October 2015, Chevron announced that it was cutting up to 7,000 jobs, or 11 percent of its workforce.[^cite_note-120] Because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and [2020 Russia–Saudi Arabia oil price war](63341569.md), Chevron announced reductions of 10–15% of its workforce.[^cite_note-\:1-66]

### Upstream

Chevron's oil and gas exploration and production operations, which in the oil and gas industry are considered as "[upstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstream_\(petroleum_industry\))" operations, are primarily in the US, Australia, Nigeria, Angola, Kazakhstan, and the Gulf of Mexico. As of December 31, 2018, the company's upstream business reported worldwide net production of 2.930 million oil-equivalent barrels per day.[^cite_note-121]

In the United States, the company operates approximately 11,000 oil and natural gas wells in hundreds of fields occupying 4,000,000 acres (16,000 km<sup>2</sup>) across the [Permian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian_Basin_\(North_America\)), located in [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas) and southeastern [New Mexico](21649.md). In 2010, Chevron was the fourth-largest producer in the region.[^cite_note-122] In February 2011, Chevron celebrated the production of its 5 billionth barrel of Permian Basin oil.[^cite_note-123] The Gulf of Mexico is where the company's deepest offshore drilling takes place at Tahiti and Blind Faith. The company also explored and drilled in the [Marcellus Shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcellus_Shale) formation under several northeastern US states; these operations were sold to the Pittsburgh-based natural gas firm [EQT Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EQT_Corporation) in 2020.[^cite_note-124]

In December 2019, Chevron announced the Anchor Field project, a deepwater high-pressure oil development in the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md).[^cite_note-125] The \$5,7 billion project is able to extract 75,000 barrels of crude oil as well as 28 million cubic feet of natural gas per day and started production in August 2024.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] Co-owner of the project is Total E\&P USA Inc, a subsidiary of [TotalEnergies](804161.md), with 37.14% working interests.[^cite_note-128]

Chevron's largest single resource project is the \$43 billion [Gorgon Gas Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgon_Gas_Project) in Australia. It also produces natural gas from Western Australia. The \$43 billion project was started in 2010, and was expected to be brought online in 2014.[^cite_note-129] The project includes construction of a 15 million tonne per annum [liquefied natural gas plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas_plant) on [Barrow Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow_Island_\(Western_Australia\)), and a domestic gas plant with the capacity to provide 300 terajoules per day to supply gas to Western Australia.[^cite_note-130]

It is also developing the [Wheatstone liquefied natural gas development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheatstone_LNG) in Western Australia. The foundation phase of the project is estimated to cost \$29 billion; it will consist of two LNG processing trains with a combined capacity of 8.9 million tons per annum, a domestic gas plant and associated offshore infrastructure.[^cite_note-131] In August 2014 a significant gas-condensate discovery at the Lasseter-1 exploration well in WA-274-P in Western Australia, in which Chevron has a 50% interest was announced.[^cite_note-SantosWA-132] The company also has an interest in the [North West Shelf Venture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Shelf_Venture), equally shared with five other investors including [BP](18998720.md), [BHP Petroleum](200825.md), [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Australia), [Mitsubishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi)/[Mitsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsui) and [Woodside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodside_Petroleum).[^cite_note-133] Presently, Chevron is looking to convert its Gorgon Island operations from upstream production to [carbon capture and storage](3679268.md).[^cite_note-134]

In the onshore and near-offshore regions of the [Niger Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_Delta), Chevron operates under a joint venture with the [Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_National_Petroleum_Corporation), operating and holding a 40% interest in 13 concessions in the region. In addition, Chevron operates the Escravos Gas Plant and the [Escravos gas-to-liquids plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escravos_GTL).[^cite_note-135]

Chevron has interests in four concessions in Angola, including offshore two concessions in [Cabinda province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinda_province), the Tombua–Landana development and the Mafumeira Norte project, operated by the company. It is also a leading partner in [Angola LNG plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_LNG_plant).[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-angola-today.com-137]

In [Kazakhstan](16642.md), Chevron participate the [Tengiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengiz_Field) and [Karachaganak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachaganak_Field) projects.[^cite_note-138] In 2010, Chevron became the largest private shareholder in the [Caspian Pipeline Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Pipeline_Consortium) pipeline, which transports oil from the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) to the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-139]

As of 2013, the [Rosebank oil and gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosebank_oil_and_gas_field) west of [Shetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shetland) was being evaluated by Chevron and its partners. Chevron drilled its discovery well there in 2004. Production was expected in 2015 if a decision was made to produce from the field. The geology and weather conditions are challenging.[^cite_note-NYT72313-140]

### Midstream

As of 2019, outside of maritime shipping, Chevron did not own significant [midstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midstream) assets;[^cite_note-\:0-141] that year it attempted to purchase [Anadarko Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadarko_Petroleum), which owned pipelines,[^cite_note-\:0-141] but was outbid by [Occidental Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occidental_Petroleum).[^cite_note-142] In 2021, Chevron completed its purchase of Noble Midstream Partners LP, which has crude oil, produced water and gas gathering assets in the Permian Basin in West Texas and the DJ Basin in Colorado. Noble Midstream also has 2 crude oil terminals in the DJ Basin as well as freshwater delivery systems.

### Downstream



Chevron's [El Segundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Segundo,_California) refinery, among its largest refining facilities

#### Refining

Chevron's downstream operations manufacture and sell products such as fuels, lubricants, additives and petrochemicals. The company's most significant areas of operations are the west coast of North America, the U.S. Gulf Coast, Southeast Asia, South Korea, Australia and South Africa. In 2010, Chevron sold an average of 3.1 million barrels per day (490×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d) of refined products like gasoline, diesel and jet fuel.[^cite_note-sec-refining-145] The company operates approximately 19,550 retail sites in 84 countries. Chevron's Asia downstream headquarters is in [Singapore](27318.md), and the company operates gas stations (under the Caltex brand) within the city state, in addition to some gas stations in Western Canada.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] Chevron owns the trademark rights to Texaco and Caltex fuel and lubricant products.[^cite_note-148]

Chevron, with equal partner [Singapore Petroleum Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Petroleum_Company), also owns half of the 285,000 barrels per day (45,300 m<sup>3</sup>/d) Singapore Refining Company (SRC) plant, a complex [refinery](195137.md) capable of cracking crude oil.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] The investment was first made in 1979 when Caltex was a one-third partner. In 2026, however, Chevron agreed to divest key downstream assets in Singapore and across Southeast Asia to ENEOS Holdings in a US\$2.17 billion transaction expected to close in 2027.[^cite_note-151]

In 2010, Chevron processed 1.9 million barrels per day (300×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d) of crude oil.[^cite_note-sec-refining-145] It owns and operates Five active refineries in the United States ([Richmond, CA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_CA), [El Segundo, CA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Segundo,_CA), [Salt Lake City, UT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City,_UT), [Pascagoula, MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascagoula,_MS), [Pasadena, TX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_TX) ). Chevron is the non-operating partner in seven joint venture refineries, located in Australia,[^cite_note-152] Pakistan,[^cite_note-153] Singapore, [Thailand](30128.md), South Korea, and New Zealand.[^cite_note-154] Chevron's United States refineries are located in Gulf and Western states. Chevron also owns an asphalt refinery in Perth Amboy, New Jersey; however, since early 2008 that refinery has primarily operated as a terminal.[^cite_note-155]

#### Chemicals

Main article: [Chevron Phillips Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Phillips_Chemical)

Chevron's primary chemical business is in a 50/50 joint venture with [Phillips 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_66), organized into the [Chevron Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Phillips) Chemical Company. Chevron also operates the Chevron Oronite Company, which develops, manufactures and sells fuel and lubricant additives.[^cite_note-156]

#### Retail



A Chevron gas station in [Diamondville, Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamondville,_Wyoming) (taken on May 27, 2018)

In the United States, the Chevron brand is the most widely used, at 6,880 locations as of September 2022 spread across 21 states. Chevron's highest concentration of stations branded as Chevron are in California (mostly in the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area), [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_\(California\)), and [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles)), [Las Vegas](47737.md), [Anchorage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchorage,_Alaska), the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) (especially [Seattle](11388236.md)), [Phoenix](49121.md), [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City,_Utah), [Atlanta](3138.md), the [Texas Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Triangle), and [South Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Florida).[^cite_note-157] The world's largest Chevron gas station is located at [Terrible's Road House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrible's_Road_House) in Nevada.

Chevron also utilizes the [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco) brand within the United States, though its locations are much more sparsely-spread than that of Chevron. Texaco is used at 1,346 locations across 17 states, mostly in [Washington](13015878.md), [Texas](29810.md), [Louisiana](18130.md), [Alabama](303.md), [Mississippi](16949861.md), [Georgia](48830.md), and [Hawaii](13270.md).[^cite_note-158] Additionally, Texaco licenses its brand to [Valero Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valero_Energy) to use in the United Kingdom, and over 730 Texaco stations exist in Britain.[^cite_note-159]

Chevron primarily uses the [Caltex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltex) brand outside of the United States, primarily in Southeast Asia, Hong Kong, Pakistan, New Zealand, and South Africa. In 2015, Chevron sold its 50% stake in Caltex Australia, while allowing the company to continue using the Caltex brand. In 2019, Chevron announced it would re-enter the Australian market by purchasing [Puma Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_Energy)'s operations in the country. The acquisition was completed in July 2020. Chevron relaunched the Caltex brand in Australia in 2022, after the expiration of Caltex Australia's license to use the Caltex brand.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

### Alternative energy



Chevron's 500kW [Solarmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Solarmine) [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_array) solar project in [Fellows, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellows,_California)&#x20;

Chevron's alternative energy operations include geothermal [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy), wind, [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel), fuel cells, and [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen).[^cite_note-latimes220310-166] In 2021 it significantly increased its use of biofuel from dairy farms, like biomethane.[^cite_note-167]

Chevron has claimed to be the world's largest producer of [geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy).[^cite_note-bbc190206-50] The company's primary geothermal operations were located in Southeast Asia, but these assets were sold in 2017.[^cite_note-bloomberg070711-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

Prior, Chevron operated geothermal wells in [Indonesia](14579.md) providing power to Jakarta and the surrounding area. In the Philippines, Chevron also operated geothermal wells at Tiwi field in Albay province, the Makiling-Banahaw field in Laguna and Quezon provinces.[^cite_note-172]

In 2007, Chevron and the [United States Department of Energy](62866.md)'s [National Renewable Energy Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renewable_Energy_Laboratory) (NREL) started collaboration to develop and produce [algae fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_fuel), which could be converted into transportation fuels, such as [jet fuel](1205435.md).[^cite_note-173] In 2008, Chevron and [Weyerhaeuser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyerhaeuser) created Catchlight Energy LLC, which researches the conversion of cellulose-based biomass into biofuels.[^cite_note-174] In 2013, the Catchlight plan was downsized due to competition with fossil fuel projects for funds.[^cite_note-175]

Between 2006 and 2011, Chevron contributed up to \$12 million to a strategic research alliance with the Georgia Institute of Technology to develop cellulosic biofuels and to create a process to convert biomass like wood or switchgrass into fuels. Additionally, Chevron holds a 22% stake in Galveston Bay Biodiesel LP, which produces up to 110 million US gallons (420,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of renewable biodiesel fuel a year.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177]

In 2010, the Chevron announced a 740 kW [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic) demonstration project in [Bakersfield, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield,_California), called Project Brightfield, for exploring possibilities to use [solar power](13690575.md) for powering Chevron's facilities. It consists of technologies from seven companies, which Chevron is evaluating for large-scale use.[^cite_note-ustoday260511-178][^cite_note-nyt220510-179] In [Fellows, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellows,_California), Chevron has invested in the 500 kW [Solarmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Solarmine) photovoltaic solar project, which supplies daytime power to the [Midway-Sunset Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway-Sunset_Oil_Field).[^cite_note-ogj220510-180] In Questa, Chevron has built a 1 MW [concentrated photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_photovoltaic) plant that comprises 173 solar arrays, which use [Fresnel lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_lens).[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] In October 2011, Chevron launched a 29-MW [thermal solar-to-steam facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_thermal_energy) in the [Coalinga Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalinga_Field) to produce the steam for [enhanced oil recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_oil_recovery). As of 2012, the project is the largest of its kind in the world.[^cite_note-183]

In 2014, Chevron began reducing its investment in renewable energy technologies, reducing headcount and selling alternative energy-related assets.[^cite_note-184]

In 2015, the [Shell Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Canada) Quest Energy project was launched[^cite_note-Canada-185] of which Chevron Canada Limited holds a 20% share.[^cite_note-186] The project is based within the Athabasca Oil Sands Project near Fort McMurray, Alberta. It is the world's first [CCS](3679268.md) project on a commercial-scale.[^cite_note-Canada-185]

In 2025, Chevron launches a [lithium](17561.md) project in the US by purchasing 125,000 acres in Texas and Arkansas. The [Smackover formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smackover_Formation) located there will allow the development of a high-lithium brine.[^cite_note-187]

### Headquarters and Offices

#### Texas



Chevron tower in Houston

Chevron's corporate headquarters operates from office towers in downtown [Houston](13774.md), Texas, where it purchased [1500 Louisiana Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1500_Louisiana_Street) and [1400 Smith Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1400_Smith_Street).

Upon Chevron announcing that it was selling its San Ramon headquarters in 2022, the company offered to cover moving costs for employees who wished to relocate to Texas.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]

On August 2, 2024, Chevron announced that it would relocate its headquarters from San Ramon, California to Houston, Texas by January 2025, citing a number of factors.[^cite_note-ChevronRelocatingToTexas-206]

#### California

Chevron has a corporate office located in [San Ramon, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ramon,_California), at 5001 Executive Parkway.

The company’s earlier headquarters was located at [555 Market Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/555_Market_Street) in San Francisco, California, the city where it had been located since its inception in 1879.[^cite_note-207] Then in 2002, Chevron moved to a 92-acre campus in San Ramon, California, located at 6001 Bollinger Canyon Road.

Chevron sold its San Ramon headquarters to the local Sunset Development Co. in September 2022, from whom it originally bought the land which the Bollinger Canyon Road office previously stood, and leases a space in San Ramon's [Bishop Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Ranch), also owned by Sunset, as its new office, as it continues to shift more operations to Texas.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209]

## Criticism and concerns

Main article: [Criticism of Chevron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Chevron)

See also: [Chevron environmental record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_environmental_record)



Anti-Chevron protest banner in support of Lago Agrio

Chevron has been widely criticized and attacked for scandals, accidents, and activities mostly related to [climate change](5042951.md). Chevron has been fined by the governments of Angola, for oil spills within its waters, and the United States through its [EPA](58666.md) for violations of the US Clean Air Act and pollutive activities arising out of its [Richmond Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Richmond_Refinery) in California.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-\:32-213] On multiple instances, authorities in oil-heavy countries have fired rounds onto protestors against Chevron.[^cite_note-csmonitor.com-214] Environmental activists have held annual anti-Chevron protests, usually within a week of Chevron's annual meeting of shareholders.[^cite_note-215]

One case heavily promoted on [social media](5897742.md) is about work done in the [Lago Agrio oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lago_Agrio_oil_field) by [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco), which Chevron acquired in 2001. Texaco dumped 18 billion tons of toxic waste and spilled 17 million US gallons (64,000,000 L) of petroleum.[^cite_note-216] Texaco's activities were the subject of an Ecuadorian court case brought by [Steven Donziger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Donziger), which claimed an \$18 billion and later reduced \$9.5 billion judgment.

Chevron has played a significant role in the exploitation of natural gas resources in the Eastern Mediterranean, including the [Leviathan](30257584.md) and [Tamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamar_gas_field) fields off [Israel](9282173.md)'s coast. The company has financially benefited from Israel's control over maritime resources to which Palestinians have been denied access due to the [blockade of the Gaza Strip](19224310.md) and lack of maritime sovereignty.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

A major point of contention involves Texaco's operations in the [Ecuadorian Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_Amazon) from the 1960s until 1992. Critics allege that during this period, the company deliberately discharged approximately 72 billion liters (18 billion gallons) of toxic waste directly into the rainforest's rivers and lands as a cost-saving measure instead of utilizing proper disposal methods. This contamination is reported to have devastated local ecosystems, poisoned water sources used by indigenous communities for drinking and sustenance, and led to widespread health issues, including illnesses and physical ailments. Some experts and affected parties have referred to this environmental damage as the "Amazon Chernobyl". It is claimed that these were not accidental spills but rather the result of systematically engineered dumping practices. Local communities initially reported changes in water taste but were allegedly dismissed by the company, which purportedly suggested the water contained beneficial "vitamins".

The environmental damage in Ecuador led to one of the largest environmental lawsuits in history (*[Aguinda v. Texaco, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguinda_v._Texaco,_Inc.)*). In 2011, an Ecuadorian court found Chevron liable and ordered the company to pay \$9.5 billion in damages to the affected communities.[^cite_note-Nagarkatti-220] Chevron refused to pay the judgment, removed its assets from Ecuador, and vowed to fight the ruling indefinitely, with a company lawyer stating it would contest it "until hell freezes over, and then fight it out on the ice". Chevron allegedly lobbied the US government to impose sanctions on Ecuador following the ruling. In 2018, an international tribunal in [The Hague](30269.md) ruled in favor of Chevron, stating that the 2011 Ecuador Supreme Court ruling had been obtained through fraud, bribery and corruption.[^cite_note-Nagarkatti-220] The majority of Chevron's crude oil from the Amazon is processed in California at the El Segundo refinery.[^cite_note-Kim-221]

[Steven Donziger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Donziger), an American lawyer who represented the Ecuadorian [plaintiffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaintiff) for decades, became a central figure in the subsequent legal battles. Chevron pursued legal action against Donziger in the United States, accusing him of obtaining the Ecuadorian judgment through bribery and fraud. A key witness in Chevron's US case against Donziger, Alberto Guerra, later said under oath in an international tribunal that Chevron had paid him substantial sums (reportedly over \$1 million and ongoing benefits) to provide false testimony against Donziger, requiring extensive coaching over 50 days.

The US case against Donziger was overseen by Judge Lewis A. Kaplan, who critics note had financial ties to Chevron. When Donziger appealed Kaplan's order to turn over his computer and phone citing attorney-client privilege, Kaplan referred the matter for criminal contempt charges. After the US Attorney's office declined to prosecute, Judge Kaplan appointed a private law firm, [Gibson Dunn & Crutcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibson,_Dunn,_&_Crutcher) (which has represented Chevron), to prosecute Donziger in the court's name – an unusual move described as the first corporate criminal prosecution in US history. Donziger was subsequently placed under house arrest for 993 days while awaiting trial on the misdemeanor charge. He was ultimately convicted of contempt of court and served time in prison, receiving a sentence critics claim exceeded the legal maximum for the offense. As a result of these legal battles, Donziger was disbarred, had his bank accounts frozen, and reportedly faces ongoing restrictions preventing him from working, holding accounts, or leaving the US. Chevron also sued Donziger for \$60 billion, described as the largest amount sought from an individual in US history. Critics assert that Chevron's actions against Donziger were intended to make an example of him and deter other lawyers from challenging the company over environmental damages worldwide.

Concerns have also been raised about Chevron's oil extraction activities in urban settings like Los Angeles. Observers note the presence of active oil wells located directly adjacent to homes and schools, particularly in lower-income areas. In wealthier neighborhoods, such as [Beverly Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California), Chevron allegedly camouflages its drilling operations within large, windowless structures designed to look like ordinary office buildings or service facilities, hiding numerous wells from public view. One such disguised oil pump at [Beverly Hills High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills_High_School), dubbed the "Tower of Hope," reportedly drew attention only after it caught fire. Furthermore, Chevron is accused of improperly abandoning depleted wells in the city, leaving behind toxic sites that pose ongoing health risks (such as [nosebleeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nosebleed) or respiratory issues) to nearby residents and can potentially lead to hazardous events like eruptions of toxic sludge. Critics argue that, similar to the situation in [Ecuador](9334.md), regulatory oversight in these areas appears insufficient to prevent these practices or hold the company accountable for remediation.

Critics argue that these incidents reflect a broader business model employed by Chevron and other large [resource extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_extraction) companies. This alleged model involves entering countries, extracting resources as cheaply as possible (often with minimal environmental safeguards), dumping waste products to cut costs, and leaving local communities to deal with the long-term environmental and health consequences. The company is accused of using its significant financial and legal resources (employing thousands of lawyers and spending billions on litigation in the Ecuador case) to overwhelm opponents, delay legal proceedings, influence governments, and avoid accountability for damages caused by its operations globally.
