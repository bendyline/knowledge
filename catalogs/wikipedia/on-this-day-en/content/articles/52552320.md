The **Lebanese Resistance Companies** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic): سرايا المقاومة اللبنانية; *Saraya al-Muqawama al-Lubnaniya)*, commonly referred to as the **Lebanese Resistance Brigades** is a [non-denominational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational) Lebanese paramilitary group affiliated with [Hezbollah](13919.md). The group is an [irregular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irregular_military) militia that is composed of [Shia](26961.md) and [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) Muslims, [Christians](18337522.md), and [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) who subscribe to [Lebanese nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_nationalism) and strong [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) beliefs.[^cite_note-ibtimes.com-1] The Resistance Companies are funded, trained, armed, and founded by Hezbollah. The group's manpower, composition, and strength are unclear.

The group was founded in 1997 to counter [Israel](9282173.md), but its focus has since expanded to countering Sunni extremists like [ISIL](9087364.md) as well.[^cite_note-ibtimes.com-1][^cite_note-usma.edu-2]

## Founding

The Resistance Companies were founded to increase the manpower of anti-Israeli forces in Lebanon and to deny Israel freedom of movement in non-Shia areas.[^cite_note-usma.edu-2] West Point's [Combating Terrorism Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combating_Terrorism_Center) reports that the Resistance Companies were founded in November 1997,[^cite_note-usma.edu-2] while other sources claim early 1990s, 2004, or 2009.[^cite_note-economist.com-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-auto-5]

## Training

Resistance Companies members are trained in Hezbollah-run camps alongside normal Hezbollah recruits. However, they do not receive Hezbollah's ideological training.[^cite_note-ibtimes.com-1] A recruiter described the group as "made for non-extremist people."[^cite_note-ibtimes.com-1] The militia is popular among Christians in [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md) and [north Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Governorate).[^cite_note-6]

## History

The Companies first saw combat in 1998 during the [South Lebanon conflict (1985–2000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\)).[^cite_note-usma.edu-2] They claim to have engaged in over three hundred operations before Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon in 2000. The group was also involved in the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) with Israel.[^cite_note-usma.edu-2]

The militia is described as an "operational auxiliary" or subsidiary of Hezbollah.[^cite_note-usma.edu-2][^cite_note-7] Group members fight under the Hezbollah flag and command structure in combat, but are at least nominally separate in peacetime. They also receive intelligence from Hezbollah.[^cite_note-usma.edu-2][^cite_note-economist.com-3]

### Involvement in Syria

Resistance Companies units have been involved in the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md). They do not engage in combat but rather support Hezbollah logistically and act as interlocutors between Hezbollah and Christian and Sunni communities.[^cite_note-ibtimes.com-1] Despite this, some fighters have been killed in Syria.

### Israel–Hezbollah conflict (2023–present)

The Companies have been involved in the ongoing [Hezbollah-Israel conflict (2023-present)](75005425.md). Some members of the group were killed during clashes with Israeli forces in October 2023.[^cite_note-8] In July 2024, the Brigades conducted their first military operations against Israel, using missiles to strike sites in the Israeli-occupied [Shebaa Farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebaa_Farms).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Criticism

Resistance Companies fighters are less disciplined and subject to less strict rules than Hezbollah fighters.[^cite_note-auto-5][^cite_note-al-akhbar.com-11] Consequently, the brigades have been involved in a large number of violent confrontations with police, the [Lebanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Army), and various Lebanese partisans.[^cite_note-al-akhbar.com-11] Lebanese Interior Minister [Nohad Machnouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nohad_Machnouk), member of the [Future Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Movement), criticized the group for challenging the sovereignty of the Lebanese government.[^cite_note-orient-news-12]

Critics contend that the group is no longer focused on fighting Israel, but rather exists as political cover for Hezbollah's actions and as a containment group for thugs and troublemakers.[^cite_note-al-akhbar.com-11] Likewise, some Sunnis have charged the Resistance Brigades with focusing more on countering Sunni fundamentalism than Israel.[^cite_note-ibtimes.com-1]

The Resistance Companies have also been accused of converting Sunni youth to Shia Islam, a charge the group denies.[^cite_note-al-akhbar.com-11]
