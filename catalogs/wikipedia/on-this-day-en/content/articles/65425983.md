# Azerbaijan–Georgia border



Map of Azerbaijan, with Georgia to the north-west





Azerbaijani and Georgian boundary markers

The **Azerbaijan–Georgia border** ([Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language): *Azərbaycan–Gürcüstan sərhədi*, [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): აზერბაიჯან-საქართველოს საზღვარი, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Georgian): azerbaijan-sakartvelos sazghvari) is the international boundary between [Azerbaijan](746.md) and [Georgia](48768.md). It is 428 km (266 mi) in length and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Armenia](10918072.md) in the west to the tripoint with [Russia](25391.md) in the east.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the west at the tripoint with Armenia and proceeds overland to the north-east, cutting through [Jandari Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jandari_Lake), before turning to the south-east down to the vicinity of Azerbaijan's [Mingachevir reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingachevir_reservoir). The border then follows the [Alazani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alazani) river as it flows north-east and then north-west, leaving the river due east of [Tsnori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsnori) and proceeding overland to the Russian tripoint.

## History

During the 19th century the Caucasus region was contested between the declining [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and [Russia](25391.md), which was expanding southwards. Russia formally annexed the eastern Georgian [Kingdom of Kartli and Kakheti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kartli_and_Kakheti) in 1801, followed by the western Georgian [Kingdom of Imereti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Imereti) in 1804. Over the course of the 1800s Russian pushed its southern frontier southwards, at the expense of the Persian and Ottoman Empires.[^cite_note-CIA-1952-2] By the [Russo-Persian War (1804–1813)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%931813\)) and the subsequent [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan), Russia acquired the bulk of what is now Azerbaijan and parts of Armenia.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-CIA-1951-4] Russia organised its Georgian and Azeri territories into the governorates of [Tiflis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiflis_Governorate), [Kutaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutaisi_Governorate), [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Governorate), [Elisabethpol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabethpol_Governorate).



Map of the former Tiflis Governorate, with the disputed Zakatala okrug in the east

Following the [1917 Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Russian_Revolution), the peoples of the southern Caucasus had declared the [Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Democratic_Federative_Republic) (TDFR) in 1918 and started [peace talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebizond_Peace_Conference) with the Ottomans.[^cite_note-ric-5][^cite_note-ottoman1-6] Internal disagreements led to [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Georgia) leaving the federation in May 1918, followed shortly thereafter by [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) and [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Democratic_Republic), however the borders between the three republic were contested. The dispute between Azerbaijan and Georgia centred on the area of [Zakatal Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakatal_Okrug) (Zaqatala) within the former Tiflis Governorate.[^cite_note-CSS-7] When Russia recognised the independence of Georgia via the [Treaty of Moscow (1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Moscow_\(1920\)), it recognised Georgian ownership of Zakatal, prompting protests by the Azeri government.[^cite_note-Lang-8][^cite_note-CSS-7] In May 1920 it was agreed that a Russian-sponsored delimitation would decide the fate of the area.[^cite_note-CSS-7] Much of the border was agreed upon at peace talks held on 12 June 1920, with Zaqatala to be decided by the Russian-led commission.[^cite_note-CSS-7][^cite_note-9]

In 1920 Russia's [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) [invaded Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Azerbaijan) and [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Armenia), ending the independence of both, followed [in February–March 1921](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Georgia) by Georgia. On 5 July 1921 Russia confirmed that the non-disputed sections of the Azeri-Georgian border would remain as they were, whilst transferring Zakatal to Azerbaijan, with this arrangement finalised by treaty on 15 November 1921.[^cite_note-CSS-7] In 1922 all three states were incorporated into the [Transcaucasian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Socialist_Federative_Soviet_Republic) within the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR), before being separated in 1936.

The boundary became an international frontier in 1991 following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) and the independence of its constituent republics. Work began in 1994 on the delimitation of the border but progress has been slow due to overlapping claims.[^cite_note-CSS-7][^cite_note-10] Part of the border around the [Red Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bridge_\(border\)) remains mined, a legacy of the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) in the 1990s when Azerbaijan feared that Armenia would take advantage of chaos in Georgia's and utilise the area to launch attacks on its territory.[^cite_note-11] The site of the [David Gareja monastery complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gareja_monastery_complex), a sacred site to Georgians which sits directly by the border, has proven especially contentious.[^cite_note-CSS-7][^cite_note-az-dispute2-12] There is a sizeable [Azeri minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis_in_Georgia) in Georgia, concentrated especially in the southern municipalities of [Kvemo Kartli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvemo_Kartli) region,[^cite_note-obstacles-13][^cite_note-census2014-14] as well as a Georgian minority in Azerbaijan, concentrated especially in the [Saingilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saingilo) region where they are known as [Ingiloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingiloys).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]
