**Laghmān** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto),[^cite_note-3] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari):[^cite_note-4] لغمان), historically known as **Lamghan**, or **Lamghanat**, is one of the 34 [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md), located in the eastern part of the country. It has a population of about 502,148,[^cite_note-nsia-2] which is multi-ethnic and mostly a rural society. Laghman hosts a large number of historical landmarks, minarets, monuments, and other cultural relics that are manifestation of its old history and culture. The city of [Mihtarlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihtarlam) serves as the capital of the province.

In 2021, the [Taliban](30635.md) gained control of the province during the [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive).

## Etymology

In the Pashayi language, lam means “village”. This is sometimes rendered as ram, depending on the dialect. The province was formerly referred to as Lmaghan, Lmagan, or Lamghan, meaning “owner of a village” or ”village dwellers”.[^cite_note-5]

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)



A village sits in a valley in the [Hindu Kush Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush_mountain_range) in Laghman Province

Located currently at the [Kabul Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Museum) are [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic) inscriptions that were found in Laghman which indicated an ancient trade route from India to Palmyra.[^cite_note-6] [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic) was the bureaucratic script language of the [Achaemenids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenids) whose influence had extended toward Laghman.[^cite_note-7] During the invasions of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), the area was known as Lampaka.[^cite_note-8]

[Inscriptions in Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_Inscription_of_Laghman) dating from the [Mauryan Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Dynasty) were found in Laghman which discussed the conversion of [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) to Buddhism.[^cite_note-9] The inscription mentions that the distance to [Palmyra](46709309.md) is 300 dhanusha or [yojana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yojana).

The [Mahamayuri Tantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamayuri) dated to between the 1-3rd century mentions several popular Yaksha shrines. It mentions Yaksha Kalahapriya being worshipped in Lampaka.[^cite_note-10]

In the seventh century, the Chinese pilgrim [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) visited Laghman, which he called "Lan-pro" and considered part of India. He indicated the presence of [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) and numerous [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus), including [Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin):[^cite_note-\:0-11]

> "For several centuries the native dynasty had ceased to exist, great families fought for preeminence, and the state had recently become a dependency of [Kapis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapisa_Province). The country produced upland rice and sugar cane, and it had much wood but little fruit; the climate was mild with little frost and no snow. \[...] There were above ten Buddhist monasteries and a few Brethren the most of whom were [Mahayanists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana). The non-Buddhists had a score or two of temples and they were very numerous."[^cite_note-12]

The [Ḥudūd al-ʿĀlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudud_al-'alam) which was finished in 982 AD mentioned the presence of some idol worshipping temples in the area, and describes the province as an emporium of [Hindūstān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan).[^cite_note-encyclopedia-13][^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Muslim conquest

Further information: [First Battle of Laghman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Laghman) and [Second Battle of Laghman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Laghman)

The Kabul Shahis only retained Lamghan in the [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabulistan)-[Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara) area by the time of [Alp-tegin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alp-tegin?action=edit\&redlink=1). According to [Firishta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firishta), [Sabuktigin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabuktigin) had already begun raiding Lamghan under Alp-begin.[^cite_note-14] He crossed the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass) many times and raided the territory of [Jayapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayapala).[^cite_note-Sastri-15] He plundered the forts in the outlying provinces of the Kabul Shahi and captured many cities, acquiring huge booty.[^cite_note-Strugglemajumdar-16] He also established Islam at many places. Jaipal in retaliation marched with a large force into the valley of Lamghan ([Jalalabad](16274.md)) where he clashed with Sabuktigin and his son. The battle stretched on several days until a snowstorm affected Jaipala's strategies, forcing him to sue for peace.[^cite_note-Sastri-15]

Jayapala then returned to Waihind but broke the treaty and mistreated the amirs sent to collect the tribute. Sabuktigin launched another invasion in retaliation.[^cite_note-17] According to al-Utbi, Sabuktigin attacked Lamghan, conquering it and burning the residences of the "infidels" while also demolishing its idol-temples and establishing Islam.[^cite_note-18] He advanced and butchered the idolaters, destroying the temples and plundering their shrines, even risking frostbite on their hands counting the large booty.[^cite_note-19]

To avenge the savage attack of Sabuktigin, Jayapala, who has earlier taken his envoys as hostage, decided to go to war again in revenge. The forces of Kabul Shahi were however routed and those still alive were killed in the forest or drowned in the river.[^cite_note-20] The second battle that took place between Sabuktigin and Jayapala in 988 A.D., resulted in the former capturing territory between Lamghan and [Peshawar](66230.md). Al-Ubti also states that the Afghans and [Khaljis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaljis), living there as nomads, took the oath of allegiance to him and were recruited into his army.[^cite_note-21] Sabuktigin won one of his greatest battles in Laghman against Jayapala and his army numbering 100,000.[^cite_note-22] [Ghaznavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) Sultan [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) built the Tomb of Lamech, amid gardens, over the site of his presumed grave, 50 kilometres from [Mihtarlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihtarlam).[^cite_note-23]

In the 14th and 15th centuries, Khārkhay Pashtuns such as Yusupzai and Tarkāṇī invaded the Laghmān valley and displaced the native Indo-Aryans.[^cite_note-24]

During the early years of the 16th century, the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moghul_Empire) ruler [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) spent much time in Laghman, and in *[Baburnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baburnama)* (memoirs of Babur) he expatiated on the beauty of forested hillsides and the fertility of the valley bottoms of the region.[^cite_note-encyclopedia-13] Laghman was recognized as a dependent district of [Kabulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabulistan) in the Mughal era,[^cite_note-25] and according to Baburnama, "Greater Lamghanat" included the Muslim-settled part of the [Kafiristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafiristan), including the easterly one of [Kunar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River). Laghman was the base for expeditions against the [non-believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafirs) and was frequently mentioned in accounts of [jihads](16203.md) led by Mughal emperor [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar)'s younger brother, Mohammad Hakim, who was the governor of Kabul.[^cite_note-encyclopedia-13] In 1747, [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) defeated the Mughals and made the territory part of the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire). In the late nineteenth century, [Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir) [Abdur Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Khan) forced the remaining kafirs ([Nuristani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristani_people)) to convert to [Islam](6037917.md).

### Recent history

See also: [Laghman massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laghman_massacre)



U.S. Army Gen. [David Petraeus](3223944.md) walking with Governor [Iqbal Azizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Iqbal_Azizi) in 2011.

During the [Soviet-Afghan war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet-Afghan_war) and the battles that followed between the rivaling warlords, many homes and business establishments in the province were destroyed. The mujahideen were blamed for burning down every school in the province.[^cite_note-GWfC-26] In addition, the [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviets) are said to have employed a strategy that targeted and destroyed the agricultural infrastructure of Laghman.[^cite_note-27] As of 2007, an [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) [Provincial Reconstruction Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Reconstruction_Team) led by the [United States](3434750.md) is based at Mihtarlam.

In 2021, the first stadium in Laghman opened in [Mihtarlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihtarlam).[^cite_note-28]

### Agriculture

The [Alingar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alingar_River) and [Alishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alishang) rivers pass through Laghman, as the province is known for its lushness. Laghman has sizable amounts of irrigated land as one can find scores of fruits and vegetables from Laghman in [Kabul](16826.md). Other main crops in Laghman include rice, wheat and cotton as many people living in the area are involved in agricultural trade and business.

### Mineral wealth



[Spodumene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spodumene) mined at Alinghar Pegmatite Field.

Laghman also has an array of precious stones and minerals,[^cite_note-36] as it is well known for being a relatively untapped source of the [Tourmaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourmaline) and [Spodumene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spodumene) gemstones which are reported to be in abundance at the northern portions of the province.[^cite_note-37] There are also [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble) mines.[^cite_note-38]

### Ethnicity, languages and religion

Laghman is multi-ethnic and mostly a rural society. According to the [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School), as of 2010 the ethnic groups of the province are as follows: 52.0% [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people), 26.7% [Pashai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashai_people) and [Nuristani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristani_people) (Kata), and 21.7% [Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_people).[^cite_note-nps-39] The people of Laghman are overwhelmingly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim).

Laghman has a population of 396,000 people in 2008. The province has 60,048 households, with an average of six persons per home. Rural areas are home to 99 percent of the population. Sapi, Tajik, Nasir, [Ibrahimkhail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahimkhel), Hoodkhail, Nuristani, [Kharoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharoti), Jabarkhail, Pashai, [Niazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niazi), Pashtun, and [Gujjars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjar) are the major ethnic groupings. [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) is spoken in 345 of the 620 villages, accounting for about 58 percent of the population. [Pashayi languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashayi_languages) are the second most common language, spoken by a third of the people in 210 localities. [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari) is spoken in 57 localities, accounting for slightly more than 9% of the population. [Kochi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi_people) (nomads) live in Laghman province, and their numbers fluctuate depending on the season. In the winter, 94,020 people, or around 4% of the Kuchi population, stay in Laghman living in 40 communities.[^cite_note-40]

### Education

Further information: [Education in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Afghanistan)

The overall literacy rate (6+ years of age) increased from 14% in 2005 to 26% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-54] The overall net enrolment rate (6–13 years of age) increased from 48% in 2005 to 52% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-54]

Universities include:

- [Laghman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laghman_University)

### Health

Further information: [Health in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan)

The percentage of households with clean drinking water fell from 39% in 2005 to 34% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-54] The percentage of births attended to by a skilled birth attendant increased from 3% in 2005 to 36% in 2011.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-54] There are 57 medical health clinics and 2 government hospitals.[^cite_note-55]
