Following the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) after [offensives](78460726.md) by [opposition groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) in early December 2024, there were clashes between Assad loyalists and forces of the new [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government) in the [Alawite](436616.md)-majority [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus_Governorate) and [Latakia Governorates](7445289.md) as well as in the western parts of the [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Governorate) and [Homs Governorates](7445154.md).

The government took action through pursuing [Assadist](36629941.md) officers and officials in villages within Western Syria. The clashes were triggered by the proliferation of videos on social media showing footage of an attack on the Abu Abdullah al-Hussein al-Khusseibi shrine in Aleppo, which occurred in November. The incitement of violence and civil unrest among Alawite communities by [Assadist](36629941.md) elements has led to the formation of Alawite insurgent groups in Western Syria.[^cite_note-lwj29jan-64]

Following the collapse of [Ba'athist Syria](57134800.md), there were frequent demands of vengeance against individuals involved with the deposed regime across parts of Western Syria.[^cite_note-65] Sources such as the UK-based [Syrian Observatory for Human Rights](34029669.md) group also reported numerous extrajudicial executions of mainly Alawite civilians and former security personnel in the region, carried out by unidentified gunmen and fighters aligned with the [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government)[^cite_note-France_24-66] and nominally a part of the new Syrian Army[^cite_note-67] as well as by Assad loyalists,[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] resulting in the deaths of more than 2,388 civilians, at least 2,127 of whom were confirmed to have been killed in sectarian violence.[^cite_note-74]

In early March, Assadist insurgents launched a coordinated attack on government forces in the coastal region, and government forces launched a counter-offensive. This was accompanied by sectarian violence, mainly [against Alawites](79404646.md). The Syrian caretaker government announced it had completed its military operation against Assad loyalists on 10 March 2025,[^cite_note-Euronews_March-1] though attacks continued. By late May 2025, the [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) reported that Assadist insurgent attacks had largely stopped since March[^cite_note-ISW_May-75] until later on in August when insurgent attacks continued.[^cite_note-isw15aug-76]

## Background

Further information: [2024 Syrian opposition offensives](78460726.md)

In December 2024, a surprise [offensive](78460726.md) by [Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham](53015755.md) (HTS) and other rebel forces, some backed by [Turkey](11125639.md), overthrew the government of [Syrian President](516106.md) [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md), [ending over five decades](78548066.md) of [Assad family rule](27334130.md) in Syria. The surprise offensive, originating in the country's northwestern region, resulted in al-Assad and his family seeking asylum in [Russia](25391.md). HTS [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) and military commander [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) emerged as Syria's [de facto](43594.md) leader following the [fall of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)), with the majority of [Syrian Armed Forces](27479.md) troops surrendering, fleeing the country, or defecting.[^cite_note-\:re-77]

### Civil unrest

After the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md), escalating tensions were reported in Western Syria, particularly in regions with significant [Alawite](436616.md) populations, with riots and civil unrest emerging in multiple locations including [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus), [Latakia](195594.md), and Bashar's birthplace of [Qardaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qardaha).[^cite_note-France2427dec-21][^cite_note-78]

On 15 December 2024, the UK-based [Syrian Observatory for Human Rights](34029669.md) (SOHR) reported that pro-Assad insurgents were meeting with village elders in [Latakia Governorate](7445289.md), instructing them to resist actions from the newly implemented Military Operations Administration in order to defend "the Alawite sect".[^cite_note-79]

On 22 December 2024, SOHR reported that civilian demonstrations erupted in the [Al-Bahluliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bahluliyah) district east of [Latakia](195594.md), following allegations of misconduct by the caretaker government's security forces, demanding the complete withdrawal of security forces from the village. SOHR said that the incident involved armed individuals claiming affiliation with the new government forcibly entering the residence of [Al-Bahluliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bahluliyah)'s [Mukhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhtar). The intruders reportedly brandished weapons at young residents in the neighborhood and committed various acts of misconduct, including [verbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbal_abuse) and [physical abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_abuse) against family members and a girl. SOHR reported that protesters chanted "Death rather than humiliation" in response.[^cite_note-80]

On 23 December 2024, hundreds of [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Syria) and their supporters marched through Damascus after foreign fighters from [Ansar al-Tawhid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Tawhid_\(Syria\)) set fire to a [Christmas tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree) in [Suqaylabiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Suqaylabiyah), a Christian town near [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama). Protestors urged the HTS authorities to protect minorities and for foreign fighters to leave Syria. HTS later said they had detained the arsonists.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

[Catholic News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_News_Agency) reported on 31 December 2024 that some Muslim residents who were displaced from the town of [Maaloula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maaloula) by the Assad regime, were threatening and harassing some Christian residents who were accused of collaborating with pro-Assad militant groups.[^cite_note-84]

#### Attack on Alawite shrine

On 25 December, tensions inflamed amongst segments of the Syrian Alawite population in response to online circulation of videos showing footage of an attack on the Alawite shrine of [Abu Abdullah al-Hussein al-Khusseibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khasibi) in [Aleppo](159244.md). Though the incident had occurred weeks earlier, disinformation campaigns spread the footage as recent, aiming to incite discord and violent riots.[^cite_note-Hilton,_al-Aswad-85] The footage depicted attackers killing five shrine attendants, mutilating their bodies, damaging the site, and setting fire to the structure.[^cite_note-France2427dec-21][^cite_note-shra25dec-86] The video provoked outrage among sections of Syria's Alawite population.[^cite_note-mee-87]

The demonstrations erupted across multiple Alawite neighborhoods in [Homs](7445154.md), including [Al-Khudari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khudari?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Wadi Al-Dhahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Al-Dhahab?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Al-Zahraa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Zahraa), [Al-Sabil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Sabil), [Al-Abbasiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Abbasiya?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Al-Muhajireen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Muhajireen?action=edit\&redlink=1). Protesters expressed outrage over the shrine attack through sectarian chants and public demonstrations, with religious and community leaders denouncing it as an attack on Alawite religious heritage and identity while calling for the perpetrators to be brought to justice. Protesters also demanded greater security be granted to the community by the government, and for it to remove all of the extremist and foreign elements from its military. Several protesters were arrested in Homs, [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus), and [Jableh](2889131.md).[^cite_note-88] The Syrian [General Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Security_Service_\(Syria\)), operating under the new government's Military Operations Department, opened fire to disperse the crowd in Homs, resulting in one protester's death and five others sustaining injuries from gunfire.[^cite_note-shra25dec-86]

It was soon discovered that the attack had occurred weeks earlier during clashes between Syrian opposition and Assad regime forces in Aleppo, and that the Alawite religious site had only been partially damaged, contrary to numerous social media posts that claimed it had been demolished.[^cite_note-89] Verify Syria, a fact-checking organization, reported that a co-ordinated [disinformation campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinformation_campaign) was launched to circulate misleading posts related to the shrine attack through social media networks of Assad loyalists, who aimed to exacerbate sectarian tensions and provide cover for Assad regime officials fleeing Syria.[^cite_note-Hilton,_al-Aswad-85] In response to rising tensions, the new government enhanced its security presence in Alawite-dominated regions. Public Security Forces implemented a curfew across Homs, [Jableh](2889131.md), and Banias, while military reinforcements established security perimeters around the Akrama and [Al-Nahda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nahda,_Syria) neighborhoods to stop further unrest. They also established [military checkpoints](8116691.md) for civilians to hand over their weapons, and set up loudspeakers in mosques instructing them to do so within 24 hours. Many, especially commanders and officers associated with the Assad regime, refused to do so.[^cite_note-France2427dec-21][^cite_note-shra26dec-90]

### Pro-Assad disinformation campaign

Since the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) in December 2024, Syria has experienced a surge in [disinformation](142690.md) on social media, targeting both Syrian and international audiences. *[DW News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW_News)* reported that the flood of online disinformation and inflammatory social media posts has had destabilizing consequences on the ground, such as triggering Alawite riots after the widespread online circulation of misleading posts of a shrine attack that occurred in November.[^cite_note-Schaer-91][^cite_note-Hilton,_al-Aswad-85]

Fact-checking organizations such as Verify-Sy and [Misbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misbar) have attributed these campaigns to social media accounts associated with Assad loyalists, as well as state [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda) outlets of Russia and Iran. [Ba'athist Syria](57134800.md) had maintained an extensive state propaganda apparatus, and its collapse created an information vacuum. Researchers have noted that [Russian](70210841.md) and [Iranian disinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_propaganda) networks remain active in social media, deploying fake accounts to stoke tensions amongst minorities, particularly the Alawite community, and incite sectarian clashes. Accounts linked to [far-right](221220.md) groups in the West have also been amplifying [Islamophobic](162173.md) posts targeting [Syrian revolutionary](39750126.md) activists.[^cite_note-Schaer-91]

### December

On 14 December, pro-Assad gunmen ambushed [Sham Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_Legion) fighters in al-Muzayri'a in the Latakia countryside, killing four and wounding 11 others.[^cite_note-syriahr.com-31]

On 14 December, a raid on [Al-Mazra'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mazraa,_Homs) in Homs Governorate, regarded as a significant Hezbollah stronghold, was launched by the Syrian Military Operations Administration, resulting in the arrests of "dozens of young men accused of committing previous violations against the people of the area".[^cite_note-92] In an act of sectarian violence, on 1 February, Sunni gunmen killed 10 Alawites including a child and an elderly woman in the village of Arzah.[^cite_note-93]

On 18 December, the Military Operations Administration conducted several raids in Hama and Homs Governorates and in several coastal areas to seek out Assad-associated figures and war criminals.[^cite_note-94]

On 25 December, unidentified armed groups conducted synchronized assaults on multiple security checkpoints in the western Hama countryside with [RPG launchers](26286.md) and [heavy machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_machine_gun), killing one Syrian government troop and injuring another.[^cite_note-27Dec24-37] On the same day, a contingent of the General Security Service – a police unit loyal to the new government – made their way to the [Khirbet al-Ma'zah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_al-Ma'zah) village in the southern Tartus Governorate. They intended to arrest Major General [Mohammad Kanjo Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Kanjo_Hassan), who had headed the Military Justice Administration and Field Court during the rule of the Assad regime. He was regarded as one of those responsible for the mass murders in [Saydnaya Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saydnaya_Prison). Upon entering the village, the General Security Service were ambushed by militants, with 14 policemen and three attackers being killed. According to *[SOHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOHR)*, loyalists of Hassan and another former Ba'athist officer, [Suhayl al-Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhayl_al-Hasan), were responsible for the ambush.[^cite_note-SOHR25Dec25-25] The militants were led by Mohammad Kanjo Hassan's brother and expelled the security forces from the village.[^cite_note-SOHR25Dec26-95] The caretaker government described the ambush as an attack by Assad loyalists. A curfew was declared at several cities.[^cite_note-mee-87]

The government's [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) subsequently ordered the arrest of the attackers and sent reinforcements to secure Khirbet al-Ma'zah.[^cite_note-SOHR25Dec25-25] By 26 December, the Military Operations Command was conducting a large-scale campaign across Tartus Governorate, searching homes and securing the countryside. Hassan was reportedly captured in Khirbet al-Ma'zah.[^cite_note-SOHR25Dec26-95][^cite_note-shra26deck-54] Three gunmen associated with Hassan were also killed in the gunfight.[^cite_note-27Dec24.2-53] Hassan was former head of the military judiciary under the [Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime) and a Ba'athist prison officer at the [Sednaya prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sednaya_prison).[^cite_note-France2427dec-21][^cite_note-96]

Other villages in the Tartus Governorate saw an "mass escape of former regime members".[^cite_note-SOHR25Dec26-95] Two armed individuals from the village of Al-Zarqat, identified as regime loyalists or "[shabiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabiha)," were killed after engaging in armed conflict with General Security Service. The security campaign prompted many former regime officials implicated in crimes against Syrian civilians to flee from several villages, including Al-Zuraiqat, [Khirbet al-Ma'zah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_al-Ma'zah), and surrounding areas.[^cite_note-shra26deck-54] In addition, four Syrian government troops were killed during a raid on a pro-Assad holdout containing trafficking ringleader Shujaa al-Ali in Balqs&#x61;**,** western [Homs Governorate](7445154.md).[^cite_note-26Dec24-38]

On 29 December 2024, a pro-Assadist group named the [Syrian Popular Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Popular_Resistance) announced their opposition to the [HTS](53015755.md)-led government and threatened to attack HTS forces in response to the civil unrest since the toppling of the Assad regime.[^cite_note-Institute_for_the_Study_of_War-7]

### January

On 14 January, soldiers of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) were kidnapped and taken as hostages by the [Assadist](57134800.md) commander Bassam Hussam al-Din in Latakia Governorate. Bassam demanded [Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham](53015755.md) (HTS) forces in a video to withdraw from the area within 24 hours or else the soldiers taken hostage would be "slaughtered". Later that day, HTS found Bassam's location using a drone and sent reinforcements to the area to rescue the units taken hostage, and surrounded him in his hideout, ultimately leading to him ending his life by suicide bombing himself. Seven Assadist militants (such as Jaafar Mahmoud al-Said[^cite_note-97]) were killed in clashes following the incident, and all kidnapped units were rescued safely and unharmed without any casualties.[^cite_note-98]

On 16 January 2025, General Security Administration (GSA) ended an effort by pro-Iranian trafficking networks to smuggle weapons from the Tartus region to Lebanon.[^cite_note-lv2419jan-99]

On 18 January 2025, a commander in the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) was killed during clashes with pro-Assad gunmen in the Talkalakh area in the western Homs countryside.[^cite_note-18Jan25-46]

On 19 January, Syrian security forces captured and destroyed a massive [Captagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captagon) shipment belonging to drug trafficking networks linked to [Maher al-Assad](3139640.md). The Syrian Ministry of Interior announced that roughly 100 million Captagon pills were seized in warehouses near the port of Latakia.[^cite_note-100]

On 22 January, pro-Assad gunmen targeted a checkpoint of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) with machine guns and grenades in the industrial area at the entrance of [Jableh](2889131.md) in the Latakia countryside. The attack left two members of the Military Operations Command dead and two other members injured.[^cite_note-22Jan25-41]

On 24 January, a group of pro-Assad militants attacked a security checkpoint in al-Muzayri'a in the Latakia countryside. The [General Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Security_Service_\(Syria\)) repelled the attack and confiscated a vehicle and several firearms from the gunmen who attacked the security checkpoint.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

### February

Calls for resistance by Assad loyalists in Western Syria have resulted in the formation of Alawite insurgent groups in the region. Iran has also been accused of sponsoring the insurgency against the Syrian caretaker government.[^cite_note-lwj29jan-64]

On 1 February, members of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) were ambushed by the [Syrian Popular Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Popular_Resistance) on the Latakia-Aleppo highway near Al-Mukhtariyah in Latakia countryside. As a result, one member was killed, two others were injured and a fourth one went missing.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-1Feb25-42]

On 2 February, unknown gunmen attacked the southern outpost of the [General Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Security_Service_\(Syria\)) in [Jableh](2889131.md) city, Latakia countryside, in the early hours of Sunday morning. Clashes ensued between the attackers and security forces stationed at the outpost.[^cite_note-104]

On 6 February 2025, the [Syrian Popular Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Popular_Resistance) claimed that it killed 9 fighters of the Syrian security forces, including a former HTS commander and a [Turkistan Islamic Party in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkistan_Islamic_Party_in_Syria) commander.[^cite_note-105]

On 6 February 2025, Syrian border security forces launched a large-scale security operation in the Western region of Homs, along the Lebanese border to dismantle smuggling networks and armed groups linked to Hezbollah, resulting in violent clashes, arrests, and the rescue of two kidnapped Syrian soldiers.[^cite_note-l24c7feb-106][^cite_note-sohr6feb-107] The crackdown was part of the [Syrian caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government)'s broader campaign to expel Hezbollah militants, dismantle trafficking activities in the Syrian border regions with Lebanon and eliminate Assadist insurgent groups.[^cite_note-l24c7feb-106][^cite_note-sohr6feb-107]

On 9 February 2025, unidentified gunmen threw grenades on the surroundings of the criminal security branch in [Latakia](195594.md) city in the early hours of Sunday morning before escaping.[^cite_note-108]

By 10 February 2025, the Syrian military forces had established control over several border villages which were affected by the activities of smuggling networks linked to Hezbollah.[^cite_note-l2410feb-12]

On 15 February 2025, four gunmen in a car threw two grenades at members of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) stationed at the traffic building in [Al-Sheikh Daher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Sheikh_Daher?action=edit\&redlink=1) neighborhood in [Latakia](195594.md) city. Clashes erupted between the attackers and the security forces, but no casualties were reported.[^cite_note-109]

On 15 February 2025, a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) was injured following an attack by unknown gunmen on a checkpoint at the entrance of Ba’areen town in western [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama) countryside. The attack occurred in the early hours of the morning and triggered an armed clash between the gunmen and the security forces. Medium and machine guns were used during the attack, resulting in intense clashes between both sides.[^cite_note-110]

On the same day, a new group called "[Saraya Ansar al-Sunnah](79264370.md)" emerged after it stated that its fighters attacked the town of Arzah and killed 12 Alawites and another 5 people it claimed were affiliated with the former Ba'athist government in [Tell Dahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Dahab,_Hama). The group stated that it would continue their attacks until the Alawites and Shia were "eliminated" or displaced from the region, it also stated that the group is decentralized with no formal headquarters, It is not clear who formed Saraya Ansar al Sunnah. The group also opposes the interim government's efforts to pardon former Ba'athist Syrian members.[^cite_note-isw14feb-6]

On 23 February, a member of the Ministry of Defence was killed due to the targeting of a pick-up near Al-Jumhoriya Street in [Latakia](195594.md) by gunmen. The vehicle deviated from the road as a result and injured a civilian and children.[^cite_note-23Feb25-43]

On 23 February 2025, a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) died from injuries sustained during a combing operation a few days earlier in a village in the western Homs countryside.[^cite_note-23Feb25.2-47]

On 27 February, a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) was killed due to injuries sustained in an attack on his vehicle carried out by pro-Assad gunmen near Al-Joumhouryah Street in [Latakia](195594.md).[^cite_note-27Feb25-44]

On 28 February 2025, unidentified gunmen on two motorcycles threw grenades on an outpost in Safita town in [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus) countryside, before clashes erupted with members of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) stationed at the outpost. As a result of the clashes, a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) was injured and a civilian was killed after being caught in the crossfire.[^cite_note-111] On the same day, a civilian and a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) were found dead in the former Russian base of Al-Su'ayrah, after having gone missing in Kaaf village in Al-Qadmous area in [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus) countryside.[^cite_note-28Feb25-39] As a result, a curfew was imposed on the village and clashes took place between the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) and local gunmen, which led to the injury of 10 people and the deaths of a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) and a local gunman.[^cite_note-28Feb25.2-40]

### Early March offensive

Main article: [March 2025 Western Syria clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2025_Western_Syria_clashes)

On 4 March, two members of Internal Security Forces were kidnapped by pro-Assad gunmen in [Latakia](195594.md), who were later found dead. Later during the day, pro-Assad gunmen attacked a patrol of Internal Security Forces near Al-Azhari roundabout in Latakia, where the two sides traded heavy gunfire.[^cite_note-4March25-45]

On the same day, members of [Mohsen al-Haymed's group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohsen_al-Haymed#Mohsen_al-Haymed's_group) had engaged with gunmen, reportedly part of a group led by Ahmad al-Labbad, in the city of [Sanamayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamayn), [Daraa](5317030.md) after leaving a funeral. Three members of al-Haymed's group died as a result of the attack, while one member and a child were injured. [General Security Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Security_Service_\(Syria\)) arrived to restore order, but al-Haymed's group opened fire, wounding one officer, which led to clashes taking place between al-Haymed's group and security forces.[^cite_note-112]

A wave of unrest struck the Western Syria coast (especially the villages of Beit Ana and Dalia in Latakia Province[^cite_note-alaraby6mar-113]) on 6 March led by Alawites and Ba'athist insurgents. In Latakia, they killed 15 Syrian security personnel.[^cite_note-aje6march-114] In response to the uprising, the Syrian caretaker government sent reinforcements to the Western Syrian coast from Idlib, Hama, and Homs.[^cite_note-aje6march-114] The unrest started with Alawite figures in Tartus calling for protests against the new Syrian government, led by the Supreme Alawite Islamic Council.[^cite_note-annahar-115] Sunnis in Jableh reacted by demanding the local police offices give them weapons to arm themselves.[^cite_note-annahar-115] A curfew was also imposed on Homs. A public security force was also ambushed on the Banias-Jableh road and suffered casualties.[^cite_note-alaraby6mar-113]

The pro-Assad insurgent group [Coastal Shield Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Shield_Brigade) was involved in the fighting. The [Military Council for the Liberation of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Council_for_the_Liberation_of_Syria) was formed just prior to the uprising, and was also significantly involved in combat.[^cite_note-DailyNewsEgypt7March-8] [Latakia](195594.md)'s province director stated that an [Assadist](36629941.md) militant group affiliated with the Ba'athist military commander [Suhayl al-Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhayl_al-Hasan) and the newly-formed pro-Assad group [Military Council for the Liberation of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Council_for_the_Liberation_of_Syria) conducted an attack in the city. Clashes in the village resulted in one security force member killed and two wounded.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-DailyNewsEgypt7March-8] On 6 March 16 members of [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) were killed in [Jableh](2889131.md).[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

On the day of the uprising, Syrian military expert Esmat al-Absi stated that Ba'athist supporters were trying to reignite strife in the region, but argued that the Syrian government would be able to keep the situation under control.[^cite_note-120]

In [Daraa](5317030.md), eight security forces were killed between 5 and 6 March, with three dying on 5 March. Two civilians also died, as well as nine members of [al-Haymed's group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohsen_al-Haymed).[^cite_note-9636march-121] 60 militants were arrested as a result,[^cite_note-rudaw6march-122] though al-Haymed escaped the raid.[^cite_note-123]

UK-based SOHR reported that [Syrian security forces](27479.md) and pro-government fighters [killed](79404646.md) more than 300 Alawite civilians across parts of the Latakia countryside.[^cite_note-france248march-124] Alawite civilians and their families fled to the Russian [Khmeimim Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmeimim_Air_Base) in Latakia province to seek refuge.[^cite_note-aljazeera-alawites-125] [Syrian Network for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Network_for_Human_Rights) (SNHR) reported that pro-Assad militant groups killed approximately 100 members of the [Syrian General Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Security_Service_\(Syria\)) by 7 March 2025. Assadist insurgents also killed 15 civilians in the town of [Jableh](2889131.md).[^cite_note-126]

Syrian President [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) warned in a speech broadcast on [Telegram](41501305.md) that the Assad loyalists had made an "unforgivable mistake," saying that "The riposte has come, and you have not been able to withstand it. Lay down your weapons and surrender before it's too late."[^cite_note-127] The advisor to the former Alawite Council president, Muhammad Nasser, claimed to [Al-Ahed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahed_\(newspaper\)) that entire families had been executed. He and local Syrian sources alleged that over 1,700 civilians had been killed.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

The Syrian caretaker government announced it had completed its military operation against Assad loyalists in western Syria on 10 March 2025.[^cite_note-Euronews_March-1]

Also on 10 March 2025, the [SDF](48205248.md) reached an agreement with the Syrian caretaker government to support the latter's military operations against the [Assad loyalist insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_loyalist_insurgency).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

### After the March offensive

On 12 March, the [Syrian Popular Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Popular_Resistance) launched an attack on the government and seized fuel trucks, with the government likely failing to arrest those responsible.[^cite_note-isw14mar-132]

On 13 March 2025, the Syrian Ministry of Defence announced the capture of four Assadist insurgents after a failed attempt to attack an army barracks in the countryside of Latakia.[^cite_note-133]

By 24 March, the insurgency entered what the Coastal Shield Brigade termed its "second phase" by utilizing IEDs and assassinations against Islamist forces.[^cite_note-134]

On 28 March, a member of the General Security Service died of injuries sustained the previous day in an armed attack on a security checkpoint by unidentified gunmen in Salanfah, in the northern countryside of Latakia.[^cite_note-135]

On 28 March 2025 unknown gunmen ambushed a patrol of Internal Security Forces on the highway near a village adjacent to the city of [Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus). The attack left two members of the security forces dead, while the gunmen managed to escape.[^cite_note-136]

On 30 March 2025, two members of the [General Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Security_Service_\(Syria\)) were killed after unidentified gunmen in a Jeep opened fire on them in Talkalakh city, west of Homs. Following the attack, the security administration in Talkalakh imposed a curfew, ordered the closure of commercial shops, and instructed civilians to remain in their homes.[^cite_note-137]

On 1 April, a member of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) was shot dead by unknown gunmen in Latakia countryside.[^cite_note-138]

On 16 April, an Assadist insurgent was killed and another one captured in clashes at security headquarters in Hama Province.[^cite_note-auto-55]

In August, Ba'athist insurgents continued attacking the Syrian government, including attacks on Syrian MoD vehicles in the Latakia province.[^cite_note-isw15aug-76]

On 4 September, IED attack on a Syrian government vehicle, where [Rijal al Nour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijal_al_Nour) declared that it was carried out in revenge for the assassination of Bashar Mihoub in Tartus, the video only shows an explosion, not the target or the location.[^cite_note-139]

On 9 September, Ba'athist militias assassinated a man from [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama) Province for being an informant and a collaborator with Damascus.

On 20 September, a Government security forces member was killed and two others injured by an unidentified gunmen attack in Homs countryside.[^cite_note-auto2-51]

On 24 September, government security forces attacked the villages of Hawarat Amurin, [Nahr al-Bared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahr_al-Bared,_Syria), and [Saqiyat Najm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqiyat_Najm), northwest of [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama), killing two residents and arresting many.[^cite_note-HawarNews_PCCWS_calls_boycott-140]

On 29 September, Rijal al Nour detonated a roadside IED on a likely government position near [Besayasin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besayasin?action=edit\&redlink=1), Latakia Province and two other IEDs the next day. None of Rijal al Nour's IED attacks caused casualties. The group said that it conducted the attack in revenge for the killing of four Alawite construction workers in [Jardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardin,_Syria?action=edit\&redlink=1), Hama Province on 28 September.[^cite_note-141]

#### Late 2025 pro-federalization protests

On 25 November, a protest by two hundred Alawites in Latakia called for [federalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalization_of_Syria#Central_and_western_Syria). A rival, pro-government protest took place. Gunshots were heard from unknown sources, and security forces fired gunshots into the air. Shots were fired at both protestors and security forces.[^cite_note-Reuters_gunfire_rival_protests-142]

On 28 December, a pro-federalization Alawite-led protest of two thousand people in Latakia turned violent after security forces opened fire on the crowd. Three protesters and one security officer were killed while over 60 protesters were injured during the event. Alawite protests in Tartus and Jableh also took place. An unidentified person threw a grenade at a police station in Tartous, injuring two policemen.[^cite_note-AJE_clashes_several_cities-4][^cite_note-Reuters_3dead_Alawite_protests-17] Following the protests, videos circulated on [online social media](2041117.md), with one video appearing to show government forces firing directly at protestors in Latakia, and another appearing to show a car driving through the crowd of protestors at Azhari Roundabout in Latakia.[^cite_note-HawarNews_videos_transgovt_attacks-143]

On 14 December, UK-based [Syrian Observatory of Human Rights](34029669.md) (SOHR) NGO reported that the brother of the [imam](15240.md) of the Great Prophet Mosque in [Masyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masyaf), Hama, was executed with three bullets by masked assailants who pursued and abducted him, after the imam was accused of being associated with Iranian and Shia militias and conducting funerals for regional civilians.[^cite_note-144] Two civilians were kidnapped in [Al-Zahraa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Zarah), Homs. Their bodies were discovered in a cooling unit three days later.[^cite_note-shra17deca-145]

On 15 December, SOHR reported that three civilians were killed in separate instances by unknown gunmen, one of whom was accused of being an Assad loyalist and tortured before being executed in [Halfaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halfaya), Hama.[^cite_note-146]

On 16 December, SOHR reported that a former [Syrian Arab Army](3423927.md) soldier and his brother were kidnapped by unknown assailants in a military vehicle as they were in line at a settlement center meant for resolving their security status in the new state. The two were killed, with their bodies disposed of in a forest Wa'er.[^cite_note-147] Masked assailants kidnapped a civilian at gunpoint in [Al-Shajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shajar), and executed him near an [al-Ghab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghab_Plain) agricultural research center northwest of Hama.

On 17 December, SOHR reported that one civilian was killed by gunmen in [Al-Qusayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qusayr,_Syria). Two more civilians were killed by unknown gunmen in [Al-Suwaydah, Masyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Suwaydah,_Masyaf).[^cite_note-shra17deca-145] An armed group attacked civilians in [Al-Shajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shajar), and kidnapped a farmer who was later found dead with signs of having been tortured beforehand.[^cite_note-148]

On 18 December, SOHR reported that a man in al-Tuwaim in the western Hama countryside was killed by two unidentified gunmen after he had tried to stop them from stealing his sheep.[^cite_note-149] On 21 December, SOHR reported that a young man was abducted from his home in Tartus by an unknown armed group after they promised to help him with resolving his security status in the new state, who executed him and mutilated his corpse.[^cite_note-150] On 22 December, SOHR reported that a young man was kidnapped near Homs, taken to [Baniyas](30871895.md), and executed in a field by unknown gunmen.[^cite_note-151]

On 23 December, SOHR reported that a man and woman were killed in [Wadi Al-Nasara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_al-Nasara) by unknown perpetrators.[^cite_note-152] A Yahmour, Homs resident was killed by unknown gunmen after being accused of loyalty to the Assad regime.[^cite_note-153] An unidentified man was found killed by a "field execution" near the Ras Al-Shamra roundabout in Latakia. After one group of unknown gunmen raided and left [Hadidah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadidah), a second group entered and killed two civilians for unknown reasons.[^cite_note-154]

On 24 December, three Alawite judges were executed by unknown gunmen while travelling in the western Hama countryside.[^cite_note-shra25decaj-155] The judges, who handled administrative cases related to land ownership, had traveled for discussions with officials of the new government. Photographs of their bodies were circulated online. Their widows stated that the judges were optimistic about their professional prospects and had been in talks about continuing their work under the new administration.[^cite_note-156] According to SOHR, several gunmen attacked and looted the residents of seven houses in the Alawite-majority village of [Jidrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jidrin).[^cite_note-157]

On 25 December, SOHR reported that a medical student was shot dead by an unknown gunman in Latakia.[^cite_note-158] One civilian was found dead on the [M4 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_Motorway_\(Syria\)) in [Ariha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariha,_Idlib), [Idlib](2528679.md), having been killed by a "field execution". Three more were killed by unknown gunmen in [Tal Sarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal_Sarin?action=edit\&redlink=1), Hama.[^cite_note-159] An [information engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_engineering) university student was executed in the countryside of [Jableh](2889131.md) by unknown gunmen.[^cite_note-shra26decaj-160]

On 26 December, SOHR reported that an armed group raided the western [Hama Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Governorate) village of al-Aziziyah located in the [Ghab Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghab_Plain), and executed a civilian before arresting eight others and taking them to an unknown location.[^cite_note-161]

On 5 January 2025, it was reported that six bodies of workers belonging to the [Sayyida Zeinab Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyida_Zaynab_Mosque,_Syria) were found. The pro-Assad [Syrian Popular Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Popular_Resistance) accused the HTS-led government of killing the workers.[^cite_note-isw5jan-162]

On 8 January 2025, SOHR reported that three civilians of one family were killed in a field execution by unidentified gunmen in [Latakia](195594.md). Another civilian was shot dead by unidentified gunmen in his agricultural land in [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama). Four more people, including three brothers from Tasnin Village near [Talbiseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talbiseh) were shot dead by gunmen, while working on the field. It was also reported that the bodies of six civilians were found, two of which were from [Khirbet al-Hamam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_al-Hamam) and four from al-Ghazila, where they were reported missing a few days ago in mysterious circumstances while heading to their work and visiting their relatives in [Homs](77533.md) countryside.[^cite_note-163]

On 10 January 2025, UK-based SOHR reported that a young man from [Qardaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qardaha) in [Latakia](195594.md) countryside was killed by unknown gunmen in [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama), where his body was moved to Hama National Hospital. Additionally, a young man, who a student at the College of Engineering, was killed with several gunshots to the head by unknown gunmen, after which his body was thrown into an irrigation canal and later moved to the hospital in Al-Zahra'a neighborhood in [Homs](77533.md).[^cite_note-164]

On 13 January 2025, SOHR alleged that two gunmen claiming to belong to the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) kidnapped a young man in al-Samiya Village in [Latakia](195594.md) countryside, whose body was later found with gunshot wounds, showing signs of a field execution. Meanwhile, the body of another young man, who had been kidnapped from al-Mazra'a Village in the Homs countryside four days earlier, was discovered with gunshot wounds.[^cite_note-165]

On 14 January 2025, SOHR asserted that Alawite civilians of [Tasnin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasnin) Village in [Homs](77533.md) countryside were assaulted by gunmen who presented themselves as members of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command). According to SOHR, an arrest campaign was launched in the village, which lasted from the early hours of morning to the afternoon of 16 January, during which suspects showed resistance. Moreover, the attackers set fire to seven houses and killed six civilians from the village. Residents and elders of Tasnin Village and other neighbouring villages attempted to communicate with security and political officials and police command in Homs, but received no responses regarding the violations.[^cite_note-166]

On 16 January 2025, SOHR reported that a young man was killed in indiscriminate gunfire in [Jableh](2889131.md) in [Latakia](195594.md) countryside, while he was celebrating his release from the grip of a group of gunmen.[^cite_note-167] Additionally, two civilians from Al-Majdal village in Mahradah area in the north-western countryside of [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama) were kidnapped and shot dead by unidentified gunmen.[^cite_note-168] On 21 January, SOHR asserted that four civilians were killed by local gunmen in a town in the north-western countryside of Homs, during a security operation initiated by the Syrian caretaker government. Residents of the town confirmed that troops of the caretaker government treated them fairly and were not involved in the killing.[^cite_note-shr21jan-169]

On 23 January 2025, SOHR reported that a large-scale security campaign was launched by the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) with the participation of local gunmen in the villages of al-Ghozaylah, al-Gharbiyah and al-Hamam in the western countryside of [Homs](77533.md), during which four civilians were extrajudicially executed, ten civilians were injured, and five others were arrested. Several additional assaults and abuses against civilians, such as forcing them to bray and bark, as well as the destruction of tombstones were also documented.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

On 30 January 2025, SOHR reported that 10 civilians were executed by unknown gunmen in Arzah village in the north-western countryside of [Homs](77533.md). The gunmen knocked on the doors of several houses in the village and opened fire on civilians, using guns with silencers, and then fled.[^cite_note-172]

On 1 February 2025, a young man died under torture at a security centre after being arrested during a security campaign in Al-Shaniyah village in [Homs](77533.md) countryside. Preliminary reports indicated that the young man was subjected to brutal torture prior to his death.[^cite_note-173] On the same day, two young men from Al-Kanisa village in Homs countryside were killed in prisons after being arrested during a security campaign on the village on 22 December. According to reliable SOHR sources, one of the men had been shot in the foot during the campaign. Both men died in the prisons of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command), and their bodies were handed over to their families today.[^cite_note-174] Also on the same day, unidentified gunmen stormed Tel Dahab village, inhabited by Shiite residents, in eastern [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama) countryside. The attackers executed five people before fleeing to an unknown location. In a separate incident, a young man from Al-Mazra’a neighbourhood in [Homs](77533.md) city was shot dead by unidentified gunmen in a sectarian killing.[^cite_note-175]

On 2 February 2025, a group of 12 armed individuals on four motorcycles attacked a house in Shammyah Al-Muhalabah village, [Latakia](195594.md) countryside. According to reliable SOHR sources, the gunmen forced their way into the house, then planted drugs and bullets, falsely accusing the residents of possession. They also stole US\$1,000. The attackers shouted sectarian insults, issued threats of murder and rape if the victims reported the incident, and then fled to an unknown destination.[^cite_note-176] On the same day, the body of a young man was found near Tel Al-Nasr graveyard in [Homs](77533.md) countryside. He had been shot in the head with his body showing signs of torture. According to reliable SOHR sources, the young man, from Al-Kanisa village in western Homs countryside, had been arrested by gunmen affiliated with the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) on 22 December.[^cite_note-177] In an unrelated incident on the same day, the body of a former engineer colonel from the Scientific Research Department was brought to Homs Hospital, ten days after being arrested during a security campaign by factions of the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command) in Damascus on 22 December. ## Analysis

In a report published on 10 March, the Institute for the Study of War reported that insurgency was not confined only to the Western coastline, but also in the Euphrates River Valley in eastern Syria, alongside other parts of the country. It found that areas with a heavy insurgent presence included the city of Latakia, Tartus, Qardaha, Masyaf, parts of [Homs](77533.md), parts of Aleppo, areas near [Damascus](8914.md), alongside the Deir-ez-Zor area. Though it underscored this by highlighting that likely many more parts of Syria have insurgent presences. It argued that these insurgents likely enjoyed support from Hezbollah's smuggling networks alongside Alawite mountain villages.[^cite_note-isw10march-232]

The 6 March Uprising was analysed to not likely have had the goal of overthrowing the government or seizing territory as they did not attempt to seize "key government infrastructure and \[defend] the approaches to the city...".[^cite_note-isw10march-232]

By late May 2025, the [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) reported that Assadist insurgent attacks had largely stopped since the coordinated attacks in March. The ISW said "This suggests that the insurgency has weakened significantly" and it suggested this was due to "Multiple factors, including a lack of Alawite support, government pressure, and poorly executed insurgent operations".[^cite_note-ISW_May-75]
