**Emilia-Romagna** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ɪˌmiːliə roʊˈmɑːnjə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/eɪˌ-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), both also [/ɛˌ-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English),[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Italian: [\[eˈmiːlja roˈmaɲɲa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian))[^cite_note-11] is an [administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of [northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy), comprising the historical regions of [Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia_\(region\)) and [Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagna). Its capital is [Bologna](21069333.md). It has a population of over 4.4 million in an area of 22,509.67 km<sup>2</sup> (8,691 sq mi).[^cite_note-population-3][^cite_note-area-2]

Emilia-Romagna is one of the wealthiest and most developed regions in Europe, with the third highest [gross domestic product](12594.md) per capita in Italy. It is also a cultural centre, being the home of the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), the oldest university in the world. Some of its cities, such as [Modena](62418.md), [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara), and [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna), are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. It is a centre for [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food) and [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) production (such as [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari), [Lamborghini](18271.md), and [Maserati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati)). It has coastal resorts such as [Cervia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervia), [Cesenatico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesenatico), and [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini). In 2018, the [Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet) guide named Emilia-Romagna as the best place to see in Europe.[^cite_note-12]

## Etymology

The name *Emilia-Romagna* dates back to [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome). *Emilia* derives from the *[via Aemilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Aemilia)*, the Roman road connecting [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza) to [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini), completed in 187 BC, and named after the consul [Marcus Aemilius Lepidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aemilius_Lepidus_\(consul_187_BC\)).[^cite_note-13] *Romagna* derives from *Romània*, the name of the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) applied to [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna) by the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) when the [western Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) had ceased to exist and Ravenna was [an outpost of the east (540–751)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna).

## History

### Prehistory and antiquity

Before the Romans took control of present-day Emilia-Romagna, it was part of the [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) world and later the [Gauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls). During the first thousand years of Christianity, trade flourished, as did culture and religion, thanks to the region's numerous [monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasteries).

### Early origins



[Mausoleum of Galla Placidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Galla_Placidia), early Christian Monuments of [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna)

The history of Emilia-Romagna dates back to Roman times, when imperial judges linked to the nearby regions of either [Liguria](71864.md) or [Tuscany](21967242.md) ruled the region of Emilia. After the fall of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) in the 5th century, the Lombards, a Germanic tribe, founded the [kingdom of Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards) in northern and central Italy. This kingdom, which included the region known as Emilia, flourished until the Lombard dynasty was overthrown by the Frankish king [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) in 774. From the 6th to 8th centuries, the region of Romagna was under Byzantine rule, and Ravenna was the capital of the Exarchate of Italy within the Eastern Roman Empire. In the 8th century, this region became a province of the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States) when Pepin, Charlemagne's father, donated the land to the Pope in 754.

### High Middle Ages to early modern period

In the 10th century, northern Italy became part of the Holy Roman Empire under the Germanic leader Otto I. Holy Roman emperors held varying degrees of control over northern Italy until the end of the Middle Ages. In the 12th century, the papacy extended its political influence, and city-states began to form in opposition to the Holy Roman emperors.

The northern cities, supported by the Pope, formed the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League) and reduced the Hohenstaufen dynasty's influence over their lands. Division between imperial partisans and their opponents created factions called the Guelphs and the Ghibellines, which would divide the cities for centuries. For the next few centuries, papal legates, or representatives of the Pope, ruled both Emilia and Romagna.



The [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), founded in 1088, is the [world's oldest university in continuous operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation).

The [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna)—the world's oldest university, established in AD 1088—and its bustling towns kept trade and intellectual life alive. Local nobility like the [Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Este) of Ferrara, the [Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Malatesta) of Rimini, the [Popes of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_State), the [Farnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Farnese) of Parma and Piacenza, and the [Duchy of Modena and Reggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Modena_and_Reggio), jostled for power and influence.



[Castle Estense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castello_Estense) in [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara)

The [House of Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Este) gained a notable profile for its political and military might and its patronage of the arts: it left behind a vast heritage of splendid Renaissance palaces, precious paintings and literary masterpieces, such as the works of [Ludovico Ariosto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovico_Ariosto), [Torquato Tasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquato_Tasso) and [Matteo Maria Boiardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Maria_Boiardo).



Flag of the [Cispadane Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cispadane_Republic), which was the first [Italian tricolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy) adopted by a sovereign Italian state (1797)

Following the rise of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon), the region of Emilia came under French control.[^cite_note-14] The first red, white and green national flag of a sovereign Italian state was adopted on 7 January 1797, when the Fourteenth Parliament of the [Cispadane Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cispadane_Republic) (1797), on the proposal of deputy [Giuseppe Compagnoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Compagnoni), decreed "to make universal the ... standard or flag of three colours, green, white, and red ...":[^cite_note-15] For having proposed the green, white and red tricolour flag, Giuseppe Compagnoni is considered the "father of the [Italian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_flag)".[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

After the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) in 1815, a growing movement for Italian national unity and independence emerged. In 1848, a revolution in Vienna initiated uprisings against Austrian rule. Over the following decades, uprisings spread to several regions, and in 1861 the Kingdom of Italy was established. During this [Italian Unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Unification), the territories of Emilia and Romagna were incorporated into the new nation.

### Late modern and contemporary

In the 16th century, the Papal States seized most of what would become Emilia-Romagna, but the territories of Parma, Piacenza, and Modena remained independent until Emilia-Romagna became part of the [Italian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) between 1859 and 1861.

After the First World War, Emilia-Romagna was at the center of the so-called [Biennio Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biennio_Rosso), a period of left-wing agitations that paved the way for [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)'s coup d'état in 1922 and the birth of the Fascist regime in Italy. Mussolini, a native of Emilia-Romagna, sponsored the rise of many hierarchs from his region, such as [Italo Balbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo_Balbo), [Dino Grandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Grandi), and [Edmondo Rossoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmondo_Rossoni).

Towards the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Emilia-Romagna was [occupied by Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Achse) and was the theatre of numerous [Nazi war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_war_crimes), such as the [Marzabotto massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzabotto_massacre) in which 770 innocent civilians were brutally executed by German troops.

During the [Cold war era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_war_era), Bologna, traditionally a [left-wing](18499.md) city, was particularly hit by political street violence and [terrorism](30636.md); in 1980 a [far-right terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_terrorist) group [detonated a bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_massacre) at the city's main railway station, killing 85 people and wounding more than 200.

After the referendum of 2006, seven municipalities of [Montefeltro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montefeltro) were detached from the [Province of Pesaro and Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pesaro_and_Urbino) ([Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche)) to join that of [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Rimini) on 15 August 2009.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The municipalities are [Casteldelci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casteldelci), [Maiolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiolo), [Novafeltria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novafeltria), [Pennabilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennabilli), [San Leo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Leo), [Sant'Agata Feltria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Agata_Feltria) and [Talamello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talamello).

On 20 and 29 May 2012, two [powerful earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Emilia_earthquakes) struck the central area of the region, killing 27 people and causing substantial damage to the region's artistic heritage as well as to numerous manufacturing facilities. The 5.8 magnitude earthquake left 14,000 people temporarily homeless.[^cite_note-20]

## Geography

See also: [Po Delta Interregional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Delta_Interregional_Park) and [Lake della Ninfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_della_Ninfa)



Relief map of Emilia-Romagna



Lagoons along the [Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_\(river\)) delta

The region of Emilia-Romagna consists of nine provinces and covers an area of 22,446 km<sup>2</sup> (8,666 sq mi), ranking sixth in Italy. Nearly half of the region (48%) consists of plains while 27% is hilly and 25% mountainous. The region's section of the [Apennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennines) is marked by areas of [flysch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flysch), [badland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badland) erosion ([calanques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calanques)) and [caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave). The mountains stretch for more than 300 km (186.41 mi) from the north to the south-east, with only three peaks above 2,000 m – [Monte Cimone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Cimone) (2,165 m), [Monte Cusna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Cusna) (2,121 m) and [Alpe di Succiso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpe_di_Succiso) (2,017 m).

The plain was formed by the gradual retreat of the sea from the [Po basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_basin) and by the detritus deposited by the rivers. Almost entirely [marshland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshland) in ancient times, its history is characterised by the hard work of its people to reclaim and reshape the land in order to achieve a better standard of living.

The [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology) varies, with [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) and saline areas in the north and many [thermal springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_spring) throughout the rest of the region as a result of groundwater rising towards the surface at different periods of history. All the rivers rise locally in the Apennines except for the Po, which has its source in the [Alps](981.md) in [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont). The northern border of Emilia-Romagna follows the path of the river for 263 km (163+1⁄2 mi).

The region has temperate broadleaved and mixed forests and the vegetation may be divided into belts: the [Common oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)-[European hornbeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpinus_betulus) belt ([Padan plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padan_plain) and adriatic coast) which is now covered (apart from the Mesóla forest in [Province of Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Ferrara)) with fruit orchards and fields of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [sugar beet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet), the [Pubescent oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_pubescens)-[European hop-hornbeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrya_carpinifolia) belt on the lower slopes up to 800–900 m, the [European beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagus_sylvatica)-[Silver fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abies_alba) belt between 800–900 m and 1,700 m and the final mountain [heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath) belt above 1,700–1,800 m. Emilia-Romagna has two Italian National Parks, the [Foreste Casentinesi National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreste_Casentinesi,_Monte_Falterona,_Campigna_National_Park) and the [Appennino Tosco-Emiliano National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appennino_Tosco-Emiliano_National_Park).

### Land use



Val Trebbia, near [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza)



[Casentinesi Forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreste_Casentinesi,_Monte_Falterona,_Campigna_National_Park)

Emilia-Romagna has been a highly populated area since ancient times. Inhabitants over the centuries have radically altered the [landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape), building cities, reclaiming wetlands, and establishing large agricultural areas. All these transformations in past centuries changed the aspect of the region, converting large natural areas to cultivation, up until the 1960s. The trend then changed, and agricultural lands began giving way to residential and industrial areas. The increase of urban-industrial areas continued at very high rates until the end of the 2010s. In the same period, hilly and mountainous areas saw an increase in the registration of semi-natural areas, because of the abandonment of agricultural lands.

[Land use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use) changes can have strong effects on ecological functions. Human interactions such as agriculture, forestation and deforestation affect [soil function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_functions), e.g. food and other biomass production, storing, filtering and transformation, habitat and gene pool.[^cite_note-21]

In the Emilia-Romagna plain, which represents half of the region and where three quarters of the population of the region live, the agricultural land area has been reduced by 157 km<sup>2</sup> while urban and industrial areas have increased to over 130 km<sup>2</sup> between 2003 and 2008. The impact of [land use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use), particularly the urbanisation of the Emilia-Romagna plain during this period, has had significant consequences for the economic and ecological assessment. The loss of arable land is equivalent to a permanent loss of the capacity to feed 440,000 persons per year from resources grown within the region. Increased [water runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_runoff) from [soil sealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_sealing) requires adaptation measures for rivers and irrigation canals, such as building [retention basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retention_basin), at a total cost estimated in the order of billions of euros.[^cite_note-22]

In 2000 there were 103,700 farm holdings and in 2010 there were 73,470, or a -29.2% loss in holdings for the region. The total utilised agricultural area (UAA) was 1,114,590 hectares (2,754,200 acres) in 2000 and 1,064,210 hectares (2,629,700 acres) in 2014 for a loss of 4.5%, indicating a downturn of smaller farm ownership. During this same timeframe there was a 14.5% decrease in the farm labor workforce.[^cite_note-23]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Emilia-Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Emilia-Romagna)



Seat of the [Regional Assembly of Emilia-Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Emilia-Romagna) in [Bologna](21069333.md)

The Regional Government (*Giunta Regionale*) is presided by the President of the Region (*Presidente della Regione*), who is elected for a five-year term. The Regional Government is composed of the President and the Ministers (*Assessori*), of which there are currently twelve including the Vice President and the Under-Secretary for the President's office.[^cite_note-24]

Apart from the [province of Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Piacenza), Emilia-Romagna was historically a stronghold of the [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party), forming the Italian "Red Quadrilateral"[^cite_note-25] or sometimes called the "[Red Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_belt_\(Italy\))" with [Tuscany](21967242.md), [Umbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria) and [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche). This is probably due to the strength of the anti-fascist resistance around the time of World War II as well as a strong tradition of [anti-clericalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clericalism) dating from the 19th century, when part of the region belonged to the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States). The strength of the anti-fascist resistance is one of the main factors, along with the effectiveness of trade-unionism, that led to the dominance of the PCI in the region.

Emilia-Romagna has since World War II been a left-wing stronghold, nowadays led by the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)), since its creation in 2007.

As of 2026, the population is 4,477,009, of which 49.2% are male, and 50.8% are female. Minors make up 14.4% of the population, and seniors make up 25.2%.[^cite_note-population-3]

The population density of 198.9 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup> is slightly above the national average of 195.1. The population of this region is traditionally evenly distributed, with no dominant metropolis but rather a line of medium-sized cities along the [Via Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Emilia), where two thirds of the population and the majority of the industrial production are concentrated. The coast of [Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagna) is also densely populated due to the booming seaside tourism in recent decades. In the peripheral areas of the Apennine Mountains and the agricultural plains around Ferrara and Piacenza, the population is less dense.

### Language

Main articles: [Emilian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language) and [Romagnol language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagnol_language)



&#x20; [Emilian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language)

&#x20; [Romagnol language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagnol_language)

&#x20; Transition between Emilian and [Lombard language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language)

[Emilian–Romagnol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian%E2%80%93Romagnol) ([Italian](14708.md): *emiliano-romagnolo*) is a [linguistic continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect_continuum) that is part of the [Gallo-Italic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_languages) spoken in the northern Italian region of Emilia-Romagna.[^cite_note-\:0-29] It is divided into two main [varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(linguistics\)), [Emilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_dialect) and [Romagnol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagnol).

As part of the [Gallo-Italic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_languages), Emilian–Romagnol is most closely related to the [Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language), [Piedmontese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmontese_language) and [Ligurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_\(Romance_language\)) languages, all of which are spoken in neighboring regions.

Among other Gallo-Italic languages, Emilian–Romagnol is characterized by systematic [raising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_\(sound_change\)) and [diphthongization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vowel_breaking) of Latin [stressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(linguistics\)) [vowels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vowel) in [open syllables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syllable), as well as widespread [syncope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncope_\(phonology\)) of unstressed vowels other than /a/ and use of [vowel gradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apophony) in the formation of plurals and certain verb tenses.[^cite_note-\:0-29]

Apart from standard [Italian](14708.md), [Emilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language) and [Romagnolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagnolo_language), two closely related languages that are part of the [Emiliano-Romagnolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliano-Romagnolo) [language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_family), are the local languages of Emilia-Romagna. They are [Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_language) [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) spoken in the region, in Northern Marche and other nearby areas such as parts of [Massa-Carrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Massa_Carrara), [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Mantua), [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pavia) and [Rovigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Rovigo) provinces and in [San Marino](27248.md). The [Sillaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillaro) river ([Emilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_language): *Séllar* in Emilian), near the town of [Castel San Pietro Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_San_Pietro_Terme), is the border between Emilia and Romagna. They belong to the [Northern Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italian) group within Romance languages (like [Piedmontese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmontese_language), [Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language), [Ligurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_language_\(Romance\)) and [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language)), which is included in the wider group of western Romance languages (including [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language) and [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language)). They are considered minority languages, structurally separated from Italian by the [Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue) and by the *[Red Book of Endangered Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Book_of_Endangered_Languages)* of [UNESCO](21786641.md).

### Cinema



[Federico Fellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Fellini)

Emilia-Romagna has given birth to a number of important filmmakers and actors and was the main setting for numerous important movies. [Bernardo Bertolucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Bertolucci) was a native of Parma and his 1976 masterpiece, *[1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_\(film\))*, was partially set in Emilia-Romagna. [Federico Fellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Fellini), a native of Rimini, shot many movies in the region, among them *[Amarcord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarcord)*. [Pier Paolo Pasolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Paolo_Pasolini), a native of Bologna, in addition to being a film director, was a poet, writer, and intellectual, who also distinguished himself as an actor, journalist, novelist, playwright, and political figure. [Michelangelo Antonioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo_Antonioni), a native of Ferrara, shot his 1964 movie *[Red Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Desert_\(film\))* in Ravenna. [Florestano Vancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florestano_Vancini), also from Ferrara, shot there his 1960 film *[Long Night in 1943](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Night_in_1943)*. [Pupi Avati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pupi_Avati), a native of Bologna, shot numerous movies in the region, including the 1976 horror-thriller *[The House with Laughing Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_House_with_Laughing_Windows)*. [Marco Bellocchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Bellocchio), a native of Bobbio, near Piacenza, directed many award-winning movies, such as his 2009 biopic *[Vincere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincere)*. Liliana Cavani, a native of Carpi, near Modena, became internationally known after the success of her 1974 feature film *[The Night Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Porter)*. In addition, actor and filmmaker Vittorio De Sica shot in Ferrara his 1970 movie *[The Garden of the Finzi-Continis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_of_the_Finzi-Continis_\(film\))*. Other actors from Emilia-Romagna include [Gino Cervi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gino_Cervi), who played [Peppone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppone) in the *[Don Camillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Camillo)* 1950s–1960s movie series; [Rossano Brazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossano_Brazzi), who acted in numerous English-language films, including the 1954 drama film *[The Barefoot Contessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Barefoot_Contessa)*; and the 1980s comedy duo [Gigi e Andrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_e_Andrea).

### Cuisine and gastronomy

Main article: [Emilian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilian_cuisine)



*[Piadina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piadina)*



Tagliatelle with [ragù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolognese_sauce)

Emilia-Romagna is considered one of the richest regions of Italy with regard to its gastronomic and wine-making tradition. The region is known for its egg and filled pasta made with soft wheat flour. [Bologna](21069333.md) is notable for pasta dishes like *[tortellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortellini)*, *[lasagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasagne_verdi)*, *[gramigna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramigna_\(pasta\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and *[tagliatelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagliatelle)* made by *[sfoglini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfoglini)* out of *[sfoglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfoglia)*. These are found also in many other parts of the region in different declinations. The [Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagna) subregion is known as well for pasta dishes like *[garganelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garganelli)*, *[strozzapreti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strozzapreti)*, *[sfoglia lorda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfoglia_lorda?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and *[tortelli alla lastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortelli_alla_lastra?action=edit\&redlink=1)*. In the [Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia_\(region_of_Italy\)) subregion, except [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza) which is heavily influenced by the cuisines of [Lombardy](43807.md) where rice is more common, rice is eaten to a lesser extent. *[Polenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polenta)*, a maize-based dish, is common both in Emilia and Romagna. The celebrated [balsamic vinegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balsamic_vinegar) is made only in the Emilian cities of [Modena](62418.md) and [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia), following legally binding traditional procedures.[^cite_note-30] [Parmigiano Reggiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmigiano_Reggiano) (Parmesan Cheese) is produced in Reggio Emilia, Parma, Modena and Bologna and is much used in cooking, while [Grana Padano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano) variety is produced in the rest of the region.



[Parmesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmesan) cheese

Although the Adriatic coast is a major fishing area which produces eels and clams, the region produces more meat products, especially pork-based, including Parma's *[prosciutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosciutto)*, *[culatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culatello)* and *[Salame Felino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salame_Felino)*, Piacenza's *[pancetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancetta)*, *[coppa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capicola)* and salami, Bologna's *[mortadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortadella)* and *[salame rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salame_rosa?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, Modena's *[zampone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zampone)*, *[cotechino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotechino)* and *[cappello del prete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappello_del_prete)* and [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara)'s *[salama da sugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salama_da_sugo)*. [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia) is the origin of the fresh egg-made pasta *[cappelletti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappelletti_\(pasta\))* (similar to Bologna's tortellini but differing in size), the typical *Erbazzone Reggiano* (Emilian spinach cheese pie) and *[Parmigiano Reggiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmigiano_Reggiano)* salted cake, and *[Gnocco Fritto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnocco_Fritto?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, mixed flour stripes fried in boiling oil, eaten in combination with ham or salami. *[Crescentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescentina_modenese)*, also improperly called *tigella*, is a thin round bread that originates in the Apennines around Modena and is usually filled with *cunza* (a spread made from pork lard and flavoured with garlic and rosemary) or with cold cuts, cheese and salty dressings or sweet spreads. Piacenza and Ferrara are also known for some dishes prepared with horse and donkey meat. Regional desserts include *[zuppa inglese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuppa_inglese)* (custard-based dessert made with sponge cake and [Alchermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchermes) liqueur) and *[panpepato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panpepato)* (Christmas cake made with pepper, chocolate, spices, and almonds). An exhaustive list of the most important regional wines should include [Sangiovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangiovese) from Romagna, [Lambrusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambrusco) from [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia) or [Modena](62418.md), [Cagnina di Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagnina_di_Romagna), [Gutturnio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutturnio) and [Trebbiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebbiano) from [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza).

### Music



[Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi), one of the most popular and acclaimed opera composers

Emilia-Romagna gave birth to one of the most important composers in the history of music, [Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi), as well as [Arturo Toscanini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Toscanini), one of the most acclaimed conductors of the 20th century, and the operatic tenor [Luciano Pavarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti).

The region is well known in Italy for its [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll) and [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) musicians, such as [Laura Pausini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Pausini), [Raffaella Carrà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaella_Carr%C3%A0), [Samuele Bersani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuele_Bersani), [Luciano Ligabue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Ligabue), [Lucio Dalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucio_Dalla), [Francesco Guccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Guccini), [Vasco Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_Rossi) and [Zucchero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchero). "[Romagna mia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagna_mia?action=edit\&redlink=1)", a song written in 1954 by [Secondo Casadei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondo_Casadei), is considered by many as the unofficial anthem of [Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romagna).

## Economy

Emilia-Romagna today is considered one of the richest European regions and the third wealthiest Italian region by gross domestic product (GDP) per capita.[^cite_note-epp.eurostat.ec.europa.eu-31] These results have been achieved by developing a balanced integration of agriculture and manufacturing and of different industries.[^cite_note-32]

### Agriculture



Wheat fields in [province of Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Reggio_Emilia)

In spite of the depth and variety of industrial activities in the region, agriculture has not been eclipsed. Emilia-Romagna is among the leading regions in the country, with farming contributing 5.8% of the gross regional product. The agricultural sector has aimed for increased competitiveness by means of structural reorganisation and high-quality products, and this has led to the success of marketed brands. [Cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal), [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato) and [onions](51258.md) are the most important products, along with [fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit) and [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape) for the production of [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) (of which the best known are Emilia's [Lambrusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambrusco), Bologna's [Pignoletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pignoletto?action=edit\&redlink=1), Romagna's [Sangiovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangiovese) and white [Albana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albana_\(grape\))). Alongside cereals, which for centuries remained the first local product, the cultivation of fruit trees has developed (especially peaches, but also apricots, plums, apples and pears).[^cite_note-33]

[Cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_breeding) and [pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig) breeding are also highly developed. Farm [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative) have been working along these lines in recent years. With their long tradition in the region there are now about 8,100 cooperatives, generally in the agricultural sector and mainly located in the provinces of [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Bologna) (2,160) and [Forlì-Cesena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Forl%C3%AC-Cesena) (1,300).[^cite_note-regportraits1-34]

### Industry



[Maserati Alfieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_Alfieri)



[Ferrari 458 Spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_458_Italia#458_Spider)



[Lamborghini Aventador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Aventador)



[Pagani Huayra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagani_Huayra)

The regional economy is more geared to export markets than other regions in the country: the main exports are from mechanical engineering (53%), the extraction of non-metallic minerals (13%) and the [clothing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_industry) (10%).[^cite_note-regportraits1-34] Industry in the region presents a varied and complex picture and is located along the Via Emilia.

The food industry (e.g. [Barilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barilla_Group), [Parmalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmalat), [Granarolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granarolo_\(company\)), [Zanetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Zanetti_Beverage_Group), Grandi Salumifici Italiani, Cremonini, Fini, [Conserve Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conserve_Italia?action=edit\&redlink=1)) is particularly concentrated in [Parma](24231.md), [Modena](62418.md) and [Bologna](21069333.md). Very important is production of [Parma ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parma_ham), [Parmesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmesan) and [Grana Padano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano) cheeses, [Modena balsamic vinegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_balsamic_vinegar), and [Mortadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortadella) sausages. It is not restricted to these famous products, but also include production of sausages, other cheese, dairy products, coffee, sugar, fruit and vegetable conserves and [stuffed pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortellini).

Automotive industry produces sports cars ([Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari), [Lamborghini](18271.md), [Maserati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati), [Pagani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagani_\(automobile\))), trucks ([Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astra_Veicoli_Industriali)), buses ([Menarinibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BredaMenarinibus#Industria_Italiana_Autobus)) and motorcycles ([Ducati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati), [Bimota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimota)).[^cite_note-BelussiGottardi2003-35]

Machine building is represented with fork-lifts ([OM Still](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KION_Group), FMTH Fantuzzi), [skid-steer loader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skid-steer_loader) ([CNH Industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Holland_Construction)), tractors ([Argo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARGO_SpA), [Goldoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldoni_\(company\)), [Arbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbos)), motors ([VM Motori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VM_Motori), [Lombardini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardini_S.r.l.)), vehicle gas-fuel equipment ([Landi Renzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landi_Renzo)), undercarriages ([ThyssenKrupp Berco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berco_S.p.A.)), ceramic machine ([SACMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SACMI)), packaging machine ([Coesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coesia?action=edit\&redlink=1), [SACMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SACMI), [IMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_\(company\))), pumps ([Interpump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpump_Group)), wood-working machine tools (SCM Group), home appliance ([Smeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smeg_\(appliances\)), [Saeco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeco)), [automatic data capture equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_identification_and_data_capture) ([Datalogic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datalogic)) etc.

There is a [chemical industrial park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_industrial_park?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara), where different companies manufacturing [polyethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene), [polypropylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypropylene), [synthetic rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_rubber) and [nitrogenous fertilizers](37401.md#Nitrogen_fertilizers). Other industrial park is [Mirandola Biomedical District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirandola_Biomedical_District). In [Parma](24231.md) there is pharmaceutical manufacturing from [Chiesi Farmaceutici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesi_Farmaceutici).

Sport and fitness articles is manufacturing by [Technogym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technogym) in [Cesena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesena).

The [ceramic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic) sector is concentrated in [Faenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faenza) and [Sassuolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassuolo).

Footwear industry is well developed and located in 2 industrial districts [San Mauro Pascoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mauro_Pascoli) and between [Fusignano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusignano) and [Bagnacavallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagnacavallo).

## Sport

### Motorsports



A [Ferrari 248 F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_248_F1) by [Scuderia Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari), the oldest surviving team in [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motor_racing) racing,

[^cite_note-targaflorio-44]

&#x20;having competed since 1948, and statistically the [most successful Formula One team in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_Grand_Prix_winners_\(constructors\))

[Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari)'s motorsports division [Scuderia Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari) is also run out of [Maranello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranello) in the [Province of Modena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Modena), the teams' colours being red. Ferrari's Formula One team has won 15 [Drivers' titles](3437295.md) and 16 [Constructors' titles](3437393.md). The team has also won multiple [Le Mans 24 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans_24_Hours) in sports car racing. The most successful Ferrari driver is German racer [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher), who won five consecutive Formula One titles between [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Formula_One_World_Championship) with Ferrari, being the first Formula One driver to achieve that milestone. Among other legendary Ferrari drivers include pre-Formula One era [Tazio Nuvolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tazio_Nuvolari), and in the Formula One era [Alberto Ascari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Ascari), [Juan Manuel Fangio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Fangio), [John Surtees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Surtees), [Niki Lauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Lauda) and [Kimi Räikkönen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimi_R%C3%A4ikk%C3%B6nen) include among drivers to have won the title in a Ferrari car. Another Formula 1 team is based here, [RB Formula One Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RB_Formula_One_Team) in Faenza, the heir of [Minardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minardi) and [Scuderia Toro Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Toro_Rosso).



[Ducati Desmosedici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati_Desmosedici) by [Ducati Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati_Corse), one of the most successful motorcycle racing teams in history

[Ducati Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati_Corse) is the motorsports division of [Ducati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati)'s motorcycle company, being the predominant Italian constructor in [MotoGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MotoGP) and the [Superbike World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superbike_World_Championship). Ducati has won two MotoGP titles, with Australian [Casey Stoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Stoner) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing_season) and with Italian [Pecco Bagnaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Bagnaia) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_MotoGP_World_Championship). Stoner is also the most successful rider for the team in MotoGP, having won 23 Grands Prix in his four seasons. Ducati have had multiple World Champions [Valentino Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_Rossi) and [Jorge Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Lorenzo) race for the team. In 2017, Emilia-Romagna native [Andrea Dovizioso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Dovizioso) finished second in the MotoGP championship for Ducati. The team has frequently had at least one Italian rider in its factory team since its entry into the premier class in 2003 at the beginning of the four-stroke engine era. Its first Grand Prix winner was Emilia-Romagna native [Loris Capirossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loris_Capirossi) in the team's inaugural season. Ducati have also won multiple Superbike titles with riders such as [Carl Fogarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Fogarty), [Troy Bayliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Bayliss), and [Alvaro Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Bautista) being among title winners.

Aside from Dovizioso and Capirossi, high-profile racers such as [Marco Melandri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Melandri) and [Marco Simoncelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Simoncelli) have also come out of Emilia-Romagna. Simoncelli died in an accident when he was run over on track in the [2011 Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Malaysian_motorcycle_Grand_Prix) at the age of 24, and was honoured by having the [Misano World Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misano_World_Circuit_Marco_Simoncelli) named after him.

Emilia-Romagna have two major international race circuits; [Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodromo_Enzo_e_Dino_Ferrari) in [Imola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imola) and the aforementioned [circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misano_World_Circuit) in [Misano Adriatico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misano_Adriatico). Imola used to host Formula One between 1980 and 2006, under the banner of [San Marino Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino_Grand_Prix) on all but one occasion; as well as hosting two non-championship races in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Imola_Grand_Prix) and [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Dino_Ferrari_Grand_Prix). The track was the site for the [fatal crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ayrton_Senna) of three-time world champion [Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) on [1 May 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_San_Marino_Grand_Prix), along with a fatal crash the day before of Austrian [Roland Ratzenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Ratzenberger). The track was rebuilt after the tragedies and returned to the calendar in a new guise already the following year. Imola was a happy hunting ground for Emilia-Romagna team Scuderia Ferrari during the era on the re-built track, with Michael Schumacher winning the race five times in front of the home crowd. In [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Formula_One_World_Championship), Imola returned to the calendar due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and hosted the [Emilia Romagna Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Emilia_Romagna_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-45]

## Transport



[Autostrada A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A1_\(Italy\)) at [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia)

The transport infrastructure system of Emilia-Romagna consists of railways, airports, motorways, roads, maritime and river lines. The region of Emilia-Romagna has a very good system of transport, with 574 km (357 mi) of motorways, 1,053 km (654 mi) of railways and airports in [Bologna](21069333.md), [Forlì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forl%C3%AC), [Parma](24231.md) and [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini). The main motorway crosses the region from north-west ([Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza)) to the south-east (Adriatic coast), connecting the main cities of [Parma](24231.md), [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia), [Modena](62418.md), [Bologna](21069333.md), and from here further to [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna), [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini) and the Adriatic coast.[^cite_note-regportraits1-34] The region is the most important commercial strategic point of the country: [Bologna](21069333.md) is a railway hub of primary importance in the North and its freight station is the largest in Italy in terms of traffic volume. Some of the main motorways of the country converge in Emilia ([Autostrada A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A1_\(Italy\)), [Autostrada A13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A13_\(Italy\)), [Autostrada A14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A14_\(Italy\)), [Autostrada A15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A15_\(Italy\)), [Autostrada A21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A21_\(Italy\)) e [Autostrada A22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A22_\(Italy\))). The [port of Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Ravenna) is the largest in the region, and with 26,256,248 tons of freight traffic in 2019,[^cite_note-48] it is the sixth port in Italy in terms of goods flow and the second in the Adriatic Sea after the [port of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Trieste).
