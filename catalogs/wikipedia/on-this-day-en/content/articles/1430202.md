# National Memorial Arboretum

[52°43′42″N 1°43′43″W  /  52.7282°N 1.7285°W  / 52.7282; -1.7285](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=National_Memorial_Arboretum\&params=52.7282_N_1.7285_W_)



Aerial view of the National Memorial Arboretum, with the River Tame at bottom, 2011

The **National Memorial Arboretum** is a British site of national remembrance at [Alrewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alrewas), near [Lichfield](159948.md), [Staffordshire](58733.md), England. Its objective is to honour the fallen, recognise service and sacrifice, and foster pride in [His Majesty's Armed Forces](31730.md) and the associated civilian community.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

In 2017, after undergoing a large scale regeneration project, the arboretum's new award-winning Remembrance Centre was officially opened by the [Duke of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales) on 23 March.[^cite_note-thenma.org.uk-2] It features three exhibition galleries, a larger restaurant and shop, separate coffee shop and a courtyard with garden.[^cite_note-thenma.org.uk-2]

In 2018, the National Memorial Arboretum was awarded Gold Large Visitor Attraction of the Year in [VisitEngland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VisitEngland)'s Awards for Excellence, as well as Coach Friendly Attraction of the Year in the British Coach Tourism Awards.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-3]

In 2026, the [Royal British Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_British_Legion), custodians of the site, announced that it would be renamed to the **Royal British Legion National Remembrance Gardens**; the name change was criticised by some armed forces veterans for "diminishing" the status of the various non-military organisations memorialised there.[^cite_note-4]

## Origins

The idea for the arboretum was conceived by [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(Royal_Navy\)) David Childs, [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire), in 1988, who wished to establish a national focus or 'centre' for remembrance to those that had died after giving to their community or country. An appeal was launched in 1994 by the then [Prime Minister](24150.md), [John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Major). He believed that the arboretum would form a living tribute to service men and women for future generations to reflect upon and enjoy while walking through a beautiful green scenery.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-5]

The future of the project became assured when three proposals were agreed. These were: for the site to be the location of the [Armed Forces Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Memorial); for the [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) to pay a significant grant-in-aid to allow for free entry and that [The Royal British Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_British_Legion) would accept the gift of the site as the focus for the nation's year-round remembrance.

The project began with no money, no land, no staff and no trees, just an idea which was yet to exist in reality. [The National Lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lottery_\(United_Kingdom\)), in the form of the Millennium Commission, granted some forty per cent of the funds needed and this was matched by thousands of donations from a wide variety of organisations both military and civilian, men and women, corporate and voluntary.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-5]

Planting began on the reclaimed gravel workings, bordered by the [Rivers Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent) and [Tame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tame_\(West_Midlands\)), gifted to the charity by [Lafarge](41902084.md) in 1997. The initial planting took place thanks to grants from the [Forestry Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_Commission) and the [National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_Forest_\(England\)), and since then has been shaped by a staff of thousands: a small paid group; a dedicated and active Friends of the National Memorial Arboretum organisation; and countless others who have either planted individual trees or helped create a memorial for their organisation.

The arboretum was officially opened on 16 May 2001. It is a [registered charity](1176679.md) and is part of The [Royal British Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_British_Legion) family of charities.[^cite_note-6]

## Location

The National Memorial Arboretum is situated just south of [Alrewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alrewas) on approximately 150 acres (61 ha; 0.23 sq mi) of old gravel workings, 5 miles (8.0 km) north of [Lichfield](159948.md), [Staffordshire](58733.md). It is adjacent to the confluence of the [River Tame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tame,_West_Midlands) with the [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), and directly neighbouring [Croxall Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croxall_Lakes). It is at the western end of the [National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Forest,_England), just off the [A38 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A38_road). The site is also served by Diamond Buses [NMA1](https://www.diamondbuses.com/media/5546/nma1-timetable_010125.pdf) service that runs as a limited stop service from Tamworth.

## Features

The Polar Bear Memorial

The Polar Bear Memorial was the first memorial dedicated on site, on 7 June 1998.

The arboretum contains more than 25,000 trees.[^cite_note-\:0-1] There are more than 400 memorials for the armed forces, civilian organisations and voluntary bodies who have played a part in serving the country; and even [HMS *Amethyst's* cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_\(cat\)) Simon.[^cite_note-7]

The largest section is the [Armed Forces Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Memorial), at the heart of the arboretum, which is a tribute to over 16,000 service personnel who have lost their lives in conflict or as a result of terrorism since the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). At 11 am on 11 November each year the sun shines through two slits in the outer and inner walls of the memorial, casting a shaft of light across a wreath in the centre. The Armed Forces Memorial was dedicated in October 2007 by the [Archbishop of Canterbury](2345.md) in the presence of [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-8]

Other areas of the site include an RAF 'wing', Naval review and Army parade. Civilian areas include 'The Beat' which contains memorials and dedications to police forces from around the UK and commonwealth. There is a Wartime Nurses Memorial, to 1,300 [VAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_Aid_Detachment) and professional nurses who died in the First and Second World Wars.[^cite_note-9]

Within the arboretum is the Millennium Chapel of Peace and Forgiveness where, at 11 am each day, an act of remembrance takes place. Following the [two-minute silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-minute_silence), accompanied by the [Last Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Post) and [Reveille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reveille), there is an introductory talk about the arboretum.[^cite_note-10] The Bastion Memorial commemorates British casualties of the [War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md).[^cite_note-BBCOct21-11] A memorial to the *[Lisbon Maru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Maru)* was unveiled in 2021.[^cite_note-LitchOct21-12]

A 'remembrance glade' was unveiled at the arboretum by the [Royal British Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_British_Legion) in September 2021, featuring plants chosen for their symbolic meanings in relation to grief and new beginnings.[^cite_note-LitchSep21-13]

Daily activities on site include guided walks, buggy tours, [land train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_train) rides and free talks, as well as the Daily Act of Remembrance.[^cite_note-14] Most of these activities are conducted by a team of over 260 volunteers, who collectively dedicate more than 52,000 hours of their time per year to the arboretum. In 2010, the Volunteers of the National Memorial Arboretum were awarded [The Queen's Award for Voluntary Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Award_for_Voluntary_Service).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-3]

### Memorials

Main article: [List of memorials at the National Memorial Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_memorials_at_the_National_Memorial_Arboretum)

The [Armed Forces Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Memorial) commemorates service men and women killed since 1945. It was designed by Liam O'Connor Architects. The project design team consisted of Liam O'Connor (architect), Christopher Barrett (project manager), Alan Baxter & Associates (structural & civil engineers), Christina Godiksen, Robert Rhodes, and Daniel Benson. The two curved walls and two straight walls are made from bricks covered with Portland stone panels. At the centre of the Memorial are two bronze sculptures created by [Ian Rank-Broadley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Rank-Broadley). This memorial remembers over 16,000 people who have been killed or sacrificed their lives while on duty or targeted by terrorists or who died in operational theatres. Their names have been written in a panel to signify their significance in the Armed Forces.&#x20;

This memorial consists of a reproduction of part of the Association badge – the wreath in copper and the [Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairbairn%E2%80%93Sykes_fighting_knife) in stainless steel. In the Second World War they were stationed in [Iceland](14531.md) and as they were snowed in under 20 feet (6.1 m) of snow for most of the campaign, their commanding officer called the men 'his Polar Bears' and the [polar bear](24408.md) on a block of ice was soon adopted as their mascot and [shoulder flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_flash). |  | **[Stillbirth and Neonatal Death Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth_and_Neonatal_Death_Society) (SANDS) Garden** The SANDS Garden is for bereaved parents.
