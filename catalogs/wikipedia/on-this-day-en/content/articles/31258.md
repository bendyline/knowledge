**Toruń** (Polish: [\[ˈtɔruɲ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1d/Pl-Toru%C5%84.ogg/Pl-Toru%C5%84.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1d/Pl-Toru%C5%84.ogg/Pl-Toru%C5%84.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>;[^cite_note-8] German: *Thorn* [\[ˈtoːɐ̯n\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b7/De-Thorn.oga/De-Thorn.oga.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b7/De-Thorn.oga/De-Thorn.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city on the [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula) River in north-central [Poland](22936.md)[^cite_note-TERYT-9] and a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites_of_Poland). Its population was 196,935 as of December 2021.[^cite_note-population-1] Previously, it was the capital of the [Toruń Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Voivodeship) (1975–1998) and the [Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Voivodeship_\(1919%E2%80%931939\)) (1921–1945). Since 1999, Toruń has been a seat of the local government of the [Kuyavian–Pomeranian Voivodeship](85887.md) and is one of its two capitals, together with [Bydgoszcz](74593.md). The cities and neighboring counties form the [Bydgoszcz–Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz%E2%80%93Toru%C5%84) twin city metropolitan area.&#x20;

Toruń is one of the oldest cities in Poland; it was first settled in the 8th century and in 1233 was expanded by the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights).[^cite_note-10] For centuries it was home to people of diverse backgrounds and religions. From 1264 until 1411, Toruń was part of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) and by the 17th century a leading trading point, which greatly affected the city's architecture, ranging from [Brick Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Gothic) to [Mannerist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannerism) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture).&#x20;

In the Early Modern period, Toruń was a [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city) of Poland and one of Poland's four largest cities.[^cite_note-11] With the [partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) in the late 18th century, it became part of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia), then of the short-lived [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw), serving as the temporary Polish capital in 1809,[^cite_note-inh-12] then again of Prussia, of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and, after [World War I](4764461.md), of the reborn [Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic). During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Toruń was spared bombing and destruction; its Old Town and iconic central marketplace have been entirely preserved.[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

Toruń is renowned for its [gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_gingerbread) – the gingerbread-baking tradition dates back nearly a [millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium) – as well as for its large [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Cathedral). It hosts the [Camerimage International Film Festival of the Art of Cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_International_Film_Festival_of_the_Art_of_Cinematography_Camerimage). Toruń is noted for its very high [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) and quality of life.[^cite_note-14] In 1997 the medieval part of the city was designated a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites_of_Poland). In 2007 the Old Town of Toruń was added to the list of [Seven Wonders of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Wonders_of_Poland).&#x20;

## Toponymy

The exact origin of the city's name is unknown. According to the findings of some linguists and historians, it is derived from the Old Slavic word "tor" (meaning "a beaten path").[^cite_note-15] The name "Toruń" would mean a roadside settlement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16]&#x20;

The settlement was first mentioned in 1226 under the name *Thorun*.[^cite_note-rymut-17] In later documents, it also appeared as *Turon*, *Turun*, and *Thoron*.[^cite_note-rymut-17] From the 15th century onward, the monosyllabic form *Thorn* came into use.[^cite_note-rymut-17]&#x20;

The city's [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) name may be given as either *Thorunium* or *Thorunum*.[^cite_note-18]&#x20;

## History

Further information: [History of Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Toru%C5%84)

### Middle Ages

The first settlement in the vicinity of Toruń is dated by [archaeologists](18951655.md) to 1100 BC ([Lusatian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusatian_culture)).[^cite_note-19] From the 5th century BCE to the 8th century CE, there are few archaeological traces in the vicinity of Toruń. For the Roman period, two settlements and a cemetery have been discovered. In the later period, traces of a river crossing are visible near the village of [Stary Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stary_Toru%C5%84).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16]&#x20;

During early medieval times, in the 7th through 13th centuries, it was the location of an old [Slavonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples) settlement,[^cite_note-PWN-20] at a ford of the river [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula), located on the site where the later Teutonic Castle was built.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16] In the 10th century it became part of the emerging Polish state ruled by the [Piast dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_dynasty). The settlement was expanded and transformed into a fortified stronghold. This stronghold was destroyed at the turn of the 12th and 13th centuries, most likely as a result of a Prussian raid.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16]&#x20;

At that time, this area formed part of the [Chełmno Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno_Land), a region within the [Duchy of Masovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Masovia) ruled by [Konrad I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_I_of_Masovia). He organized campaigns against the Prussians, but their failures prompted him to settle the Teutonic Knights in the area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16] They arrived in Chełmno Land at the turn of 1227 and 1228, founding a small stronghold called Vogelsang, before later moving to [Nieszawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieszawa).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16]&#x20;



The Gothic Old Town Hall (*Ratusz Staromiejski*) dates back to the 13th century

In spring 1231 the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights) along with Mazovian knights crossed the river Vistula near [Nieszawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieszawa) and established a fortress near today's village of [Stary Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stary_Toru%C5%84).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16] On 28 December 1233, Teutonic Knights [Hermann von Salza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_von_Salza) and [Hermann Balk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Balk)[^cite_note-21] signed the [city charters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_charter) for Toruń (*Thorn*) and [Chełmno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno) (*Kulm*). The original document was lost in 1244. The set of rights in general is known as [Kulm law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulm_law). The municipal law was modeled on Silesian precedents, and the original inhabitants of both towns likewise came predominantly from Silesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16]&#x20;

In 1236, due to frequent flooding,[^cite_note-22] it was relocated to the present site of the Old Town. In 1239 [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) friars settled in the city, followed in 1263 by [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order). In 1264 the adjacent New Town was founded, predominantly to house Torun's growing population of craftsmen and artisans, who predominantly came from German-speaking lands.[^cite_note-23] In 1280, the city (or as it was then, both cities) joined the mercantile [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League), and thus became an important [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) trade centre.&#x20;

In the 14th century, papal verdicts ordered the restoration of the area to Poland; however, the Teutonic Knights did not comply and continued to occupy the region.[^cite_note-24] The city was recaptured by Poland in 1410 during the [Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian%E2%80%93Teutonic_War). During the war, the city council maintained contact and cooperated with Polish King [Władysław II Jagiełło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_II_Jagie%C5%82%C5%82o);[^cite_note-25] however, after the [First Peace of Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Thorn_\(1411\)) was signed in the city in February 1411, the city fell back to the Teutonic Order. In 1411, the city left the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League). In the 1420s, Polish King Władysław II Jagiełło built the [Dybów Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyb%C3%B3w_Castle), located in present-day left-bank Toruń, which he visited numerous times.[^cite_note-lgk-26] During the next big [Polish–Teutonic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Teutonic_War_\(1431%E2%80%931435\)), Dybów Castle was occupied by the Teutonic Knights from 1431 to 1435.[^cite_note-lgk-26] The city refused to pay taxes to the Teutonic Knights, not wanting to finance their war against Poland.[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

In 1440, the gentry of Toruń co-founded the [Prussian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Confederation) to further oppose the Knights' policies. From 1452, talks between Polish King [Casimir IV Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon) and the burghers of the Confederation were held at Dybów Castle.[^cite_note-lgk-26] The Confederation rose against the [Monastic state of the Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_state_of_the_Teutonic_Knights) in 1454 and its delegation submitted a petition to Polish King Casimir IV Jagiellon asking him to regain power over the region as its rightful ruler. An act of incorporation was signed in [Kraków](16815.md) 6 March 1454, recognizing the region (including Toruń), as part[^cite_note-28] of the [Polish Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland).&#x20;



*Second Peace of Toruń*, 19th-century painting by Toruń-born painter Marian Jaroczyński, exhibited in the local [District Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Museum_in_Toru%C5%84)

These events led to the [Thirteen Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Years%27_War_\(1454%E2%80%9366\)). The citizens of the city, enraged by the Order's ruthless exploitation, conquered the Teutonic castle, and dismantled the fortifications brick by brick, except for the Gdanisko tower which was used until the 18th century to store gunpowder.[^cite_note-torun.pl-29][^cite_note-torun.pl2-30] The local mayor pledged allegiance to the Polish king during the incorporation in March 1454 in Kraków,[^cite_note-31] and then in May 1454, an official ceremony was held in Toruń, in which the nobility, knights, landowners, mayors, and local officials from [Chełmno Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno_Land), including Toruń, again solemnly swore allegiance to the Polish king and the Kingdom of Poland.[^cite_note-32] Since 1454, the city has been authorized by King Casimir IV to mint Polish coins.[^cite_note-33] During the war, Casimir IV often stayed at the [Dybów Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyb%C3%B3w_Castle)[^cite_note-34] and Toruń financially supported the Polish Army. The New Town and the Old Town amalgamated in March 1454.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETandecki19955-16]&#x20;

The Thirteen Years' War ended in 1466, with the [Second Peace of Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Peace_of_Thorn_\(1466\)), in which the [Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Order) renounced any claims to the city and recognised it as part of Poland.[^cite_note-35] The Polish king granted the town great privileges, similar to those of [Gdańsk](12099.md). Also in 1454 at [Dybów Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyb%C3%B3w_Castle), the King issued the famous [Statutes of Nieszawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutes_of_Nieszawa), covering a set of privileges for the [Polish nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta); an event that is regarded as the birth of the noble democracy in Poland, which lasted until the country's [demise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) in 1795.&#x20;

### Early modern period



[Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus)' House, currently a museum

In 1473, [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) was born, and in 1501 Polish king [John I Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_I_Albert) died in Toruń; his heart was buried in [St. John's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Cathedral). In 1500, the [Tuba Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuba_Dei), the largest [church bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_bell) in Poland at the time, was installed at Toruń Cathedral, and a bridge across the Vistula was built, the country's longest wooden bridge at the time. In 1506, Toruń became a [royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city) of Poland. In 1528, the [royal mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_mint) started operating in Toruń. In 1568, a gymnasium was founded, which after 1594 became one of the leading schools of northern Poland for centuries to come.[^cite_note-LO-36] Also in 1594, Toruń's first museum (*Musaeum*) was established at the school, beginning the city's museal traditions. A city of great wealth and influence, it enjoyed voting rights during the [royal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_elections_in_Poland) period.[^cite_note-37] [Sejms of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) were held in Toruń in 1576 and 1626.[^cite_note-38]&#x20;



Toruń in 1641

In 1557, during the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), the city adopted [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism). Under Mayor [Henryk Stroband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Stroband) (1586–1609), the city became centralized. Administrative power passed into the hands of the city council. In 1595, [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) arrived to promote the [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation), taking control of St John's Church. Protestant city officials tried to limit the influx of [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) into the city, as Catholics (Jesuits and [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) friars) already controlled most of the churches, leaving only St Mary's for Protestant citizens. In 1645, at a time when religious conflicts occurred in many other European countries and the disastrous [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War) was fought west of Poland, in Toruń, on the initiative of King [Władysław IV Vasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_IV_Vasa), a three-month congress of European Catholics, [Lutherans](23371382.md), and [Calvinists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinist) was held, known as *Colloquium Charitativum;* an important event in the history of interreligious dialogue.[^cite_note-39]&#x20;

During the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) (1700–21), the city was besieged by Swedish troops. The restoration of [Augustus II the Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_II_the_Strong) as King of Poland was prepared in the city in the [Treaty of Thorn (1709)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Thorn_\(1709\)) by the Russian tsar [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia). In the second half of the 17th century, tensions between Catholics and Protestants grew. In the early 18th century about 50 percent of the populace, especially the gentry and middle class, were German-speaking Protestants, while the other 50 percent were Polish-speaking [Roman Catholics](606848.md).[^cite_note-40] Protestant influence was subsequently pushed back after the [Tumult of Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumult_of_Thorn) of 1724.&#x20;

### Late modern period (from 1793)



Birthplace and house of Polish economist and writer [Fryderyk Skarbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Skarbek), residence of his godson [Fryderyk Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin) in 1825

After the [Second Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_Poland) in 1793, the city was annexed by [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia). It was briefly regained by Poles as part of the [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw) in 1807–1815, even serving as the temporary capital in April and May 1809.[^cite_note-inh-12] During these years the city began to attract a growing [Jewish](25955086.md) community.[^cite_note-sztetl.org.pl-41] In 1809, Toruń was successfully defended by the Poles against the Austrians. After being re-annexed by Prussia in 1815, Toruń was subjected to [Germanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisation_of_Poles_during_the_Partitions) and became a strong center of [Polish resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_movements_in_partitioned_Poland_\(1795%E2%80%931918\)) against such policies. The city's first synagogue was inaugurated in 1847.[^cite_note-sztetl.org.pl-41] New Polish institutions were established, such as Towarzystwo Naukowe w Toruniu (*Toruń Scientific Society*), a major Polish institution in the [Prussian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Partition) of Poland, founded in 1875. After [World War I](4764461.md), Poland declared independence and regained control over the city. In [interwar Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic), Toruń was the capital of the [Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Voivodeship_\(1919%E2%80%931939\)).&#x20;

### World War II



Arrested Poles and German guards at the gate of Fort VII in 1939

During [World War II](32927.md), Germany [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) the city from 7 September 1939 to 1 February 1945. *[Einsatzkommando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzkommando) 16* entered the city to commit various [crimes against Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation).[^cite_note-42] Under German occupation, local people were subjected to arrests, [expulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Poles_by_Nazi_Germany), [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II), deportations to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps) and executions, especially the Polish elites as part of the *[Intelligenzaktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligenzaktion)*.&#x20;

A group of Polish railwaymen and policemen from Toruń were murdered by the German [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) and *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* in [Gąbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%85bin) on 19–21 September 1939.[^cite_note-43] Local Poles, including activists, teachers and priests, arrested in Toruń and [Toruń County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_County) beginning in September 1939, were initially held in the pre-war prison, and after it became overcrowded in October 1939, the Germans imprisoned Poles in Fort VII of the [Toruń Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Fortress).[^cite_note-mw-44] On 17–19 October 1939 alone, the German police and the *[Selbstschutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selbstschutz)* arrested 1,200 Poles in Toruń and Toruń County.[^cite_note-mw-44] In early November 1939, the Germans carried out further mass arrests of Polish teachers, farmers and priests in Toruń and the county, who were then imprisoned in Fort VII.[^cite_note-mw-44] Imprisoned Poles were then either deported to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps) or murdered onsite.[^cite_note-mw-44]&#x20;



1939 [Barbarka massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarka_massacre)

Large [massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarka_massacre) of over 1,100 Poles from the city and region, including teachers, school principals, local officials, restaurateurs, shop owners, merchants, farmers, railwaymen, policemen, craftsmen, students, priests, workers, doctors, were carried out in the present-day district of [Barbarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarka,_Toru%C5%84).[^cite_note-45] Six mass graves were discovered after the war, in five of which the bodies of the victims were burned, as the Germans had tried to cover up the crime.[^cite_note-46] Local teachers were also among Polish teachers murdered in the [Sachsenhausen-Oranienburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp), [Mauthausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen_concentration_camp) and [Dachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp) concentration camps.[^cite_note-47] Nonetheless, the [Polish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) was active in the city, and Toruń was the seat of one of the six main commands of the [Union of Armed Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Armed_Struggle) in occupied Poland (alongside [Warsaw](32908.md), [Kraków](16815.md), [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84), [Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok) and [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w)).[^cite_note-48]&#x20;

During the occupation, Germany established and operated [Stalag XX-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_XX-A) [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) in the city, with multiple forced labour subcamps in the region, in which Polish, British, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II), Australian and [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_atrocities_committed_against_Soviet_prisoners_of_war) POWs were held. From 1940 to 1943, in the northern part of the city the German transit camp [Umsiedlungslager Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Umsiedlungslager_Thorn\&action=edit\&redlink=1) for Poles expelled from Toruń and the surrounding area, became infamous for inhuman sanitary conditions.[^cite_note-SM-49] Over 12,000 Poles passed through the camp, and around 1,000 died there, including about 400 children.[^cite_note-SM-49] From 1941 to 1945, a German [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) camp was located in the city.[^cite_note-50] In the spring of 1942, the Germans murdered 30 Polish scouts aged 13–16 in Fort VII.[^cite_note-51]&#x20;

While the city's population suffered many atrocities, as described, there were no battles or bombings that damaged its buildings. Thus, the city avoided damage during both World Wars, and retained its historic architecture, ranging from [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) through [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) to 19th and 20th century styles.&#x20;

## Sights

Listed on the UNESCO list of [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites) since 1997, Toruń has many monuments of architecture dating back to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). The city is famous for having preserved almost intact its medieval spatial layout and many Gothic buildings, all built from [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick), including monumental churches, the Town Hall and many burgher houses.&#x20;

- City fortifications, begun in the 13th century, extended between the 14th and 15th centuries, mostly demolished in the 19th century, but partially preserved with a few city gates and watchtowers (among them the so-called [Leaning Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaning_Tower_of_Toru%C5%84)) from the Vistula side. See also: [Toruń Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Fortress)

- A 15th-century Gothic house ([now a museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Regional_Museum)) where Copernicus was reputedly born

- [Ruins of 13th-century Teutonic Knights' castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Castle)

- House at the sign of the Star ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Kamienica Pod Gwiazdą*, the East Asian Museum, previously Gothic, briefly owned by [Filip Callimachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Callimachus), then rebuilt in the 16th century and in 1697, with a richly decorated stucco façade and wooden spiral stairs.

- [Monastery Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery_Gate), a medieval gate that has survived to the present day

Toruń, unlike many other historic cities in Poland, escaped substantial destruction in World War II. Particularly left intact was the Old Town, all of whose important architectural monuments are originals, not reconstructions.&#x20;

Major renovation projects have been undertaken in recent years to improve the condition and external presentation of the Old Town. Besides the renovation of various buildings, projects such as the reconstruction of the pavement of the streets and squares (reversing them to their historical appearance), and the introduction of new plants, trees and objects of 'small architecture', are underway.&#x20;

Numerous buildings and other constructions, including the city walls along the boulevard, are illuminated at night, creating an impressive effect – probably unique among Polish cities with respect to the size of Toruń's Old Town and the scale of the illumination project itself.&#x20;

Toruń is also home to the [Zoo and Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo_and_Botanical_Garden_in_Toru%C5%84) opened in 1965 and 1797 respectively and is one of the city's popular tourist attractions.&#x20;

## Symbols

The colors of Toruń are white and blue in the horizontal arrangement, white top, blue bottom, equal in size. The flag of the city of Toruń is a bipartite sheet. The upper field is white, the lower field is blue. If the flag is hung vertically, the upper edge of the flag must be on the left.[^cite_note-52]&#x20;

The flag with the coat of arms is also in use. The ratio of the height of the coat of arms to the width of the flag is 1\:2.[^cite_note-53]&#x20;

## Climate

The climate can be described as [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfb*) if the isotherm of 0 °C (32 °F) is used or an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (*Cfb*) if the −3 °C (27 °F) isotherm is adopted. Back in 1930s, the city passed close to the original boundary and dividing line of climates C and D groups in the north–south direction proposed by climatologist [Wladimir Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wladimir_K%C3%B6ppen).[^cite_note-54] Toruń is in the transition between the milder climates of the west and north of the [Poland](22936.md) and the more extreme ones like the south (warmer summer) and the east (colder winter). It is not much different from the climates of more southerly [Kraków](16815.md)[^cite_note-55] and easterly [Warsaw](32908.md),[^cite_note-56] though it has slightly milder winters and more moderate summers.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-noaa-58]&#x20;

Being close to definitely [continental climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate), it has a high variability caused by the contact of eastern continental air masses and western oceanic ones. This is influenced by the geographical location of the city – the Toruń Basin to the south, and the Vistula Valley to the north.[^cite_note-59]&#x20;

Dąmbski Palace – a baroque residence located on Żeglarska Street

The most recent statistics show a decrease in the population of the city, from 211,169 in 2001 (highest) to 202,562 in 2018. Among the demographic trends influencing this decline, are: suburbanisation, migration to larger urban centres, and wider trends observed in the whole of Poland such as general population decline, slowed down by immigration in 2017. The birth rate in the city in 2017 was 0.75. Low birth rates have been consistent in the city for the first two decades of the 21st century.&#x20;

The official forecasts from [Statistics Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Poland) state that by 2050 the city population will have declined to 157,949.&#x20;

Inside the city itself, most of the population is concentrated on the right (northern) bank of the Vistula river. Two of the most densely populated areas are Rubinkowo and Na Skarpie, housing projects built mostly in the 1970s and 1980s, located between the central and easternmost districts; their total population is about 70,000.&#x20;

The [Bydgoszcz–Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz%E2%80%93Toru%C5%84) metro area of Toruń and [Bydgoszcz](74593.md), their counties, and a number of smaller towns, may in total have a population of as much as 800,000. Thus the area contains about one third of the population of the Kuyavia-Pomerania region (which has about 2.1 million inhabitants).[^cite_note-74]&#x20;

## Transport

See also: [Trams in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Toru%C5%84)

The transport network in the city has undergone major development in recent years. The partial completion of ring road (East and South), the completion of the second bridge (2013) and various road, and cycling lane improvements, including construction of [Trasa Średnicowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drogowa_Trasa_%C5%9Arednicowa), have decidedly improved the traffic in the city. However, noise barriers that have been erected along the new or refurbished roads have been criticised as not conducive to a beautiful urban landscape. The extensive roadworks have also drawn attention to the declining population numbers, casting doubt that the city might over-deliver for the future number of road users, as the demographic trends forecast from [Statistics Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Poland) predicts a reduction of population by almost 1/4 by year 2050.[^cite_note-wliczbach-71]&#x20;



[Toruń Główny railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_G%C5%82%C3%B3wny_railway_station) (main railway station)



Main Bus Station

The city's public transport system consists of an extensive [tram network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Toru%C5%84) with seven lines. The Tram network operates exclusively on the northern bank of the Vistula river.&#x20;

In addition to the tram network, the city runs 40 day time bus routes and six night time bus routes, covering the city and some of the neighboring communities including the southern side of the city.&#x20;

Toruń is situated at a major road junction, one of the most important in Poland. The [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_autostrada_\(Poland\)) [highway](48519.md) reaches Toruń, and a southern [beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltway) surrounds the city. Besides these, the [European route E75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E75) and a number of domestic roads (numbered 10, 15, and 80) run through the city.&#x20;

With three main railway stations ([Toruń Główny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_G%C5%82%C3%B3wny_\(PKP_station\)), Toruń Miasto and Toruń Wschodni), the city is a major rail junction, with two important lines crossing there ([Warsaw](32908.md)–[Bydgoszcz](74593.md) and [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw)–[Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn)). Two other lines stem from Toruń, toward [Malbork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malbork) and [Sierpc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpc).&#x20;



Józef Piłsudski Bridge over the [Vistula river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula_river), the older of the two road bridges in Toruń

The rail connection with [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) is run under a name "[BiT City](446704.md)" as a "metropolitan rail". Its main purpose is to allow traveling between and within these cities using one ticket. A joint venture of Toruń, [Bydgoszcz](74593.md), [Solec Kujawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solec_Kujawski) and the voivodeship, it is considered as important in integrating [Bydgoszcz-Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz-Toru%C5%84) metropolitan area. A major modernization of BiT City railroute, as well as a purchase of completely new vehicles to serve the line, is planned for 2008 and 2009. Technically, it will allow to travel between Toruń-East and Bydgoszcz-Airport stations at a speed of 120 km/h (75 mph) in a time of approximately half an hour. In a few years' time "BiT City" will be integrated with local transportation systems of Toruń and Bydgoszcz, thus creating a uniform metropolitan transportation network – with all necessary funds having been secured in 2008.&#x20;

Since September 2008, the "one-ticket" solution has been introduced also as regards a rail connection with [Włocławek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82oc%C5%82awek), as a "regional ticket". The same is planned for connection with [Grudziądz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudzi%C4%85dz).&#x20;

Two bus depots serve to connect the city with other towns and cities in Poland.&#x20;

As of 2008, a [small sport airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_Airport) exists in Toruń; however, a modernization of the airport is seriously considered with a number of investors interested in it. Independently of this, [Bydgoszcz Ignacy Jan Paderewski Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski_Airport), located about 50 kilometres (31 miles) from Toruń city centre, serves the whole [Bydgoszcz-Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz-Toru%C5%84) metropolitan area, with a number of regular flights to European cities.&#x20;

## Economy



Toruń's Technology Park

Although a medium-sized city, Toruń is the site of the headquarters of some of the largest companies in Poland, or at least of their subsidiaries. The official unemployment rate, as of September 2008, is 5.4%.&#x20;

In 2006, construction of new plants owned by [Sharp Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation) and other companies of mainly Japanese origin has started in the neighboring community of [Łysomice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81ysomice) – about 10 kilometres (6 miles) from city centre. The facilities under construction are located in a newly created special economic zone. As a result of cooperation of the companies mentioned above, a vast high-tech complex is to be constructed in the next few years, providing as many as 10,000 jobs (a prediction for ) at the cost of about 450 million euros. As of 2008, the creation of another special economic zone is being considered, this time inside city limits.&#x20;



Toruń's city centre incorporates a large commercial district

Thanks to its architectural heritage Toruń is visited by more than 1.5 million tourists a year (1.6 million in 2007). This makes tourism an important branch of the local economy, although time spent in the city by individual tourists or the number of hotels, which can serve them, are still not considered satisfactory. Major investments in renovation of the city's monuments, building new hotels (including high-standard ones), improvement in promotion, as well as launching new cultural and scientific events and facilities, give very good prospects for Toruń's tourism.&#x20;



Małe Garbary, a typical street in the Old Town of Toruń

In recent years Toruń has been a site of intense building construction investments, mainly residential and in its transportation network. The latter has been possible partly due to the use of European Union funds assigned for new member states. Toruń city county generates by far the highest number of new dwellings built each year among all Kuyavian-Pomeranian counties, both relative to its population as well as in absolute values. It has led to almost complete rebuilding of some districts. As of 2008, many major constructions are either under development or are to be launched soon – the value of some of them exceeding 100 million euros. They include a new speedway stadium, major shopping and entertainment centres, a commercial complex popularly called a "New Centre of Toruń", a music theater, a centre of contemporary art, hotels, office buildings, facilities for the [Nicolaus Copernicus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University_in_Toru%C5%84), roads and tram routes, sewage and fresh water delivery systems, residential projects, the possibility of a new bridge over the Vistula, and more. Construction of the [A1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_autostrada_\(Poland\)) and th[e BiT City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polregio) fast metropolitan railway also directly affects the city. About 25,000 local firms are registered in Toruń.&#x20;

## Culture

The internationally renowned film festival [Camerimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camerimage) was founded in Toruń in 1993, and has been held annually in the city since 2019.[^cite_note-75] In 2024, with Australian actress [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) as jury president,[^cite_note-76] the [32nd International Film Festival of the Art of Cinematography Camerimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_International_Film_Festival_of_the_Art_of_Cinematography_Camerimage) screened [Steve McQueen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_McQueen_\(director\))'s [historical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_fiction) [war drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_drama) *[Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitz_\(2024_film\))*, the first half of [Jon M. Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_M._Chu)'s [two-part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-part_film) [musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_film) [fantasy film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_film) *[Wicked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicked_\(2024_film\))*, and the world premiere of [Joel Souza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Souza)'s [western film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_film) *[Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_\(2024_film\))*.[^cite_note-Lineup-77][^cite_note-78]&#x20;



Toruń's main stage, the Wilam Horzyca Theatre

Toruń has two drama theatres (*Teatr im. Wilama Horzycy* with three stages and *Teatr Wiczy*), two children's theatres (*[Baj Pomorski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baj_Pomorski_Theater)* and *Zaczarowany Świat*), two music theatres (*Mała Rewia*, *Studencki Teatr Tańca*), and numerous other theatre groups. The city hosts, among others events, the international theatre festival, "Kontakt", annually in May.&#x20;

A building called *Baj Pomorski* has recently been completely reconstructed. It is now one of the most modern cultural facilities in the city, with its front elevation in the shape of a gigantic chest of drawers. It is located at the south-east edge of the Old Town. Toruń has two cinemas including a [Cinema City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_City_Poland), which has over 2,000 seats.&#x20;

Over ten major museums document the history of Toruń and the region. Among others, the "House of Kopernik" and the accompanying museum commemorate [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) and his revolutionary work, the university museum reveals the history of the city's academic past.&#x20;



Toruń [planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetarium)

The [Tony Halik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Halik) Travelers' Museum ([Muzeum Podróżników im. Tony Halika](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Muzeum_Podr%C3%B3%C5%BCnik%C3%B3w_im._Tony_Halika\&action=edit\&redlink=1)) was established in 2003 after [Elżbieta Dzikowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El%C5%BCbieta_Dzikowska) donated to citizens of Toruń a collection of objects from various countries and cultures following the death of her husband, famous explorer and writer, Toruń native, [Tony Halik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Halik). It is managed by the [District Museum in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Museum_in_Toru%C5%84).&#x20;

The Centre of Contemporary Art (*Centrum Sztuki Współczesnej* – *CSW*) opened in June 2008 and is one of the most important cultural facilities of this kind in Poland. The modern building is located in the very centre of the city, adjacent to the Old Town. The Toruń Symphonic Orchestra (formerly the Toruń Chamber Orchestra) is well-rooted in the Toruń cultural landscape.&#x20;

Toruń is home to a [planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetarium) (located downtown) and an [astronomical observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piwnice_radio_observatory) (located in nearby village of [Piwnice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piwnice,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship)). The latter boasts the largest [radio telescope](46656.md) in Central Europe with a diameter of 32 m (104.99 ft), second only to the [Effelsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effelsberg) 100 m (328.08 ft) radio telescope.&#x20;



[Toruń gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_gingerbread)

Toruń is well known for [gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_gingerbread), a type of [piernik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierniki) often made in elaborate molds. [Museum of Toruń Gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Toru%C5%84_Gingerbread) is a large museum dedicated to it.[^cite_note-79] There are also several gingerbread workshops in the town that offer hands-on experiences for tourists, including the popular [Living Museum of Gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Museum_of_Gingerbread). The 15-year-old composer [Fryderyk Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Chopin) was smitten with Toruń gingerbread when he visited his godfather, [Fryderyk Skarbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Skarbek), there in the summer of 1825.&#x20;

Toruń is a center of conservative Roman Catholic culture. [Redemptorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Most_Holy_Redeemer) [Tadeusz Rydzyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Rydzyk) set up [Radio Maryja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Maryja) and [Telewizja Trwam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telewizja_Trwam), a college where students can contribute to Radio Maryja.[^cite_note-80] Now a museum is being constructed.&#x20;

The [12999 Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12999_Toru%C5%84) [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) is named after the city.&#x20;

## Education

## International relations

Honouring Toruń's sister relationship with [Philadelphia, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania), the Bulwar Filadelfijski (*Philadelphia Boulevard*), a 2 km (1.2 mi) long street running mostly between Vistula River and walls of the Old Town and the boulevard itself, bears its name. The Ślimak Getyński is one of the lanes connecting Piłsudski Bridge / John Paul II Avenue with Philadelphia Boulevard at their downtown interchange. It honours the relationship with Göttingen, its name derived from the street's half-circular shape (Polish word *ślimak* meaning "snail").&#x20;

## In popular culture

- Thorn (Toruń) is one of the starting towns of the [State of the Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order) in the turn-based strategy game *[Medieval II: Total War: Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_II:_Total_War:_Kingdoms#Teutonic_campaign)*.

  [^cite_note-90]

- Toruń also makes an appearance in the alternate history real-time strategy video game *[Command and Conquer: Red Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_Conquer:_Red_Alert)*; in the Soviet campaign of the game, the player is tasked on liquidating the city's inhabitants after the Soviet leadership discovers resistance fighters had aided escaped test subjects.
