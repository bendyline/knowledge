**Energy supply** is the delivery of fuels or transformed fuels to point of consumption. It potentially encompasses the [extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_extraction), [transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transmission), [generation](212141.md), [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(business\)) and [storage of fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_storage). It is also sometimes called **energy flow**.

This supply of energy can be disrupted by several factors, including imposition of higher energy prices due to action by [OPEC](166346.md) or other [cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel), war, political disputes, economic disputes, or physical damage to the energy infrastructure due to [terrorism](30636.md). The [security of the energy supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security) is a major concern of [national security](240468.md) and [energy law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_law).

## Other uses

[New York Consolidated Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Consolidated_Laws) includes a statutory code called "[Energy Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_energy_law)".[^cite_note-NYLaw-1] Article 21 of this code is called "Energy Supply and Production", but rather than a comprehensive code, only consists of one section dealing with [renewable energy](25784.md).[^cite_note-NYLaw-1]
