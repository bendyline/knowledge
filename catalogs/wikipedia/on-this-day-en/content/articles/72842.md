The **Antofagasta Region** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Región de Antofagasta*, pronounced [\[antofaˈɣasta\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is one of Chile's [sixteen first-order administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Chile). Being the second-largest region of Chile in area, it comprises three provinces, [Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antofagasta_Province), [El Loa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Loa) and [Tocopilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocopilla_Province). It is bordered to the north by [Tarapacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapac%C3%A1_Region), by [Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Region) to the south, and to the east by [Bolivia](3462.md) and [Argentina](18951905.md). The region's capital is the port city of [Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antofagasta); another one of its important cities is [Calama](1090788.md). The region's main economic activity is [copper mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_mining_in_Chile) in its giant inland [porphyry copper systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper).

Antofagasta's [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Chile) is extremely arid, albeit somewhat milder near the coast. Nearly all of the region is devoid of vegetation, except close to the [Loa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loa_River) and at oases such as [San Pedro de Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_de_Atacama). Much of the inland is covered by [salt flats](390410.md), [tephra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tephra) and [lava flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_flow), and the coast exhibits [prominent cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Cliff_of_northern_Chile).

The region was sparsely populated by indigenous [Changos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changos) and [Atacameños](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacame%C3%B1os) until massive Chilean immigration in conjunction with a [saltpeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltpeter) boom in the later 19th century. Most of the region was [Bolivia](3462.md)'s [only coastal territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litoral_Department) until being annexed by Chile in 1879, at the onset of the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific).

## History

See also: [History of mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mining_in_Chile)

Antofagasta's history is divided, as the territory itself, into two sections: the coastal region, and the highlands plateau or [altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano) around the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes). In pre-Columbian times, the coastline was populated by nomadic fishing clans of [Changos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changos) Indians, of which very little is known due to very limited contact with the Spanish [conquerors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistadors).

The inland section was populated by the [Atacaman culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacaman_culture) around the great dry salt lake [Salar de Atacama](9980356.md), the [Loa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loa_River) basin, and valleys and oases across the altiplano, with the most important settlement being the village of [San Pedro de Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_de_Atacama).

The Atacaman culture was deeply influenced by [Tiwanaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwanaku) culture and later fell under [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca) rule. The Atacamans harvested mainly corn and beans and developed trade as far as the [Amazon basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_basin) and Pacific shores. The arrival of the Spaniards in the 16th century did not destroy the culture but transformed it deeply through the process of [mestizaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizaje), in which both cultures mixed. Under Spanish rule, Atacaman territory was placed under the administration of [the Audiencia Real de Charcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Audiencia_of_Charcas), though it is disputed whether the Audiencia Real de Charcas was to administer only the inland portion or the coast as well. At the time of independence general [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) integrated it into the new Republic of [Bolivia](3462.md), under the name of "[Litoral Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litoral_Department)". This decision was disputed by the Chilean Government and has been a source of conflict until present times. Chile claimed that according to the [Uti possidetis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uti_possidetis) of the Spanish crown, the coastal region belong to them and their territory bordered directly with [Peru](170691.md).

Chilean explorers such as [Juan López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_%22Chango%22_L%C3%B3pez?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [José Santos Ossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Santos_Ossa) discovered rich [nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrate) and [guano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guano) deposits which produced a massive Chilean colonization of the coastline. Friction between the new settlers from both countries grew until 1879 when the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) erupted. Antofagasta was permanently annexed by the Chilean government at the end of the war.

Colonization by Chileans followed mainly from [Norte Chico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Chico,_Chile) (the contemporary regions of [Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Region) and [Coquimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquimbo_Region), also known as the III and IV regions), into the new territories of Antofagasta and [Tarapacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapac%C3%A1_Province,_Chile?action=edit\&redlink=1), known as [Norte Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Grande).

In the early 20th century, the region became a significant base of Chile's union-organizing movements. It continued to depend economically on the nitrate-extraction industry until its replacement by copper mining. Two of the largest and richest open pit mines in the world are located inland from Antofagasta: [La Escondida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escondida_Mine) and [Chuquicamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuquicamata).

## Geography



The city of [Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antofagasta)

See also: [Coastal Cliff of northern Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Cliff_of_northern_Chile)

The main river is the [Loa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loa_River).

Along the [Coastal Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Coast_Range) lies [Atacama Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Fault). The area of the Coastal Cordillera around the fault is rich in iron ore constituting the northern part of the so-called [Chilean Iron Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Iron_Belt).[^cite_note-Tornosetal2020-5] These ore deposits are often of the manto-type deposits and are chiefly emplaced on rocks of [La Negra Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Negra_Formation).[^cite_note-Barraetal2017-6]

### Climate



Atacama Desert, vista before sunset

Further information: [Atacama Desert](18952975.md)

The average rainfall in the [Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antofagasta_\(region\)) is just 1 millimetre (0.04 in) per year. From the coast, east to the [Chilean Coast Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Coast_Range), is the south-central part of the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md), the driest desert in the world. Further to the east, it is part of the less arid [Central Andean dry puna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Andean_dry_puna) ecoregion. The surroundings of abandoned Yungay town have been named the driest place in the world.[^cite_note-TWP-7]

## Demography



Overview of the Port Angamos at evening (Mejillones)

Most of the population lives on the coast in [Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antofagasta) and [Mejillones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mejillones), or in [Calama](1090788.md) in the interior, the hub of the mining industry and the home of a large part of its work force.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, immigrant settlers also arrived from Europe (mainly [Croatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatians), Italians, [Spaniards](963152.md), [Greeks](42056.md), English, French, [German](152735.md), and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) peoples), from the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant), and in smaller numbers from China, Japan, [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), [Peru](170691.md) and [Bolivia](3462.md). Various immigration flows joined with the culture of the altiplano region creating the modern culture of the north of Chile, which arguably presents more Andean- and multi-European-features than the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_of_Chile) and mainstream Chilean culture.[^cite_note-8]

The Antofagasta Region is the heart of the mining industry, Chile's main source of export revenue. It represents 53% of Chile's mining output, led by copper and followed by [potassium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_nitrate), gold, [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine), and [lithium](17561.md), which means it could have had [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) higher than [Luxembourg](17515.md) had it had a large degree of autonomy. The mining industry regularly accounts for more than 90% of the region's exports.[^cite_note-11] In 2024 mining stood for 69% of the region's [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP).[^cite_note-12]

[Fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_in_Chile) and manufacturing also contribute to the income of the area.

The availability of infrastructure and services, due to the region's mining boom, together with its abundance of beautiful natural scenery, have opened vast prospects for the travel industry, both in the interior and on the coast. Interesting tourist attractions include the small town of [San Pedro de Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_de_Atacama), once the center of the Atacameño culture, Atacama Salt Flat, the Valley of the Moon, the Quitor Pukará, the Puritama hot springs and the numerous astronomical observatories including the [Very Large Telescope](151648.md) and [ALMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Large_Millimeter_Array).
