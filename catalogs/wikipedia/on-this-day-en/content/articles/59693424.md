**Isabel Natividad Díaz Ayuso** (Spanish: [\[isaˈβel ˈdi.aθ aˈʝuso\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); born 17 October 1978) is a Spanish politician who has served as the [president of the Community of Madrid](608649.md) since 2019.[^cite_note-1] She is also the president of the [People's Party of the Community of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_of_the_Community_of_Madrid) since 2022.

A member of the [People's Party](404727.md), and the vice-secretary of communication and spokeswoman of the [party's Madrilenian branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_of_the_Community_of_Madrid),[^cite_note-2] she was the regional candidate for president of the Community of Madrid ahead of the [2019 Madrilenian autonomous election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Madrilenian_regional_election).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Although her party did not win the most seats the [autonomous elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Madrilenian_regional_election) for the first time since May [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Madrilenian_regional_election), she was later elected president by the [Assembly of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Madrid). Her administration represented several firsts: it was the first time that the region was run by a coalition government—formed by Ayuso's own conservative People's Party (PP) and [Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\))—and it was the first time that [Vox](41928488.md) propped up an autonomous executive in Madrid.[^cite_note-5] Under her leadership, the regional branch of the People's Party has obtained both its worst result (in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Madrilenian_regional_election)) and its best result ever (in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Madrilenian_regional_election)) in number of votes.

## Personal life

Born on 17 October 1978 in Madrid in the [Chamberí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber%C3%AD) district, her parents were involved in the trade of medical and orthopaedic goods.[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-7] She has a degree in journalism from the [Complutense University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid) (UCM)[^cite_note-8] and a master's degree in Political Communication and Protocol.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

Her partner between 2016 and 2020 was the hairdresser Jairo Alonso.[^cite_note-9] Her current partner is Alberto González Amador.[^cite_note-10]

Ayuso was baptized as a Catholic but became irreligious at age nine after her grandfather's death.[^cite_note-11] During the COVID-19 pandemic, she returned to the Catholic faith, stressing the importance of seeing people pray for her well-being.[^cite_note-12]

She is a co-owner of a property management business she inherited from her father.[^cite_note-13]

## Political career

Affiliated to the Popular Party (PP) in 2005, when [Pablo Casado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Casado) was the president of New Generations in Madrid, in 2006, she was hired by Alfredo Prada, Minister of Justice and the Interior of the Government of the [Community of Madrid](69802.md), for his press department, gaining the confidence of Esperanza Aguirre.[^cite_note-14] Specialized in political communication, she directed the online area of the PP, taking charge of [Cristina Cifuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Cifuentes)' digital campaign in 2015.[^cite_note-15]

A candidate on the PP list for the 2011 Madrid Assembly elections, she was not elected as a Member of Parliament at the time. She entered the ninth legislature of the regional parliament on 15 July, filling the vacancy caused by Engracia Hidalgo's resignation.[^cite_note-16] She renewed her act as a deputy in the 2015 elections. During the X legislature she served as deputy spokesperson for her group, a position she abandoned along with her status as a deputy when she was appointed vice-counselor of the Presidency and Justice of the regional government.[^cite_note-17]

On 11 January 2019, PP president Pablo Casado appointed her to lead the party in the [2019 Madrilenian regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Madrilenian_regional_election). In that election on 26 May, the PP list obtained 22.23% of valid votes and 30 seats, the second most voted list after the [PSOE](272596.md), with 27.31% of valid votes and 37 seats.[^cite_note-18]

## Presidency of the Community of Madrid

### First Madrid government

Main article: [First government of Isabel Díaz Ayuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_government_of_Isabel_D%C3%ADaz_Ayuso)



Díaz Ayuso at the Assembly of Madrid, during the investiture session

Proposed as a candidate for the Presidency of the Community of Madrid by the President of the [Assembly of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Madrid), [Juan Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Trinidad?action=edit\&redlink=1), who had previously prevented an investiture session for Ángel Gabilondo by scheduling an investiture session without a candidate in July 2019,[^cite_note-19] Díaz Ayuso was sworn in as President of the regional government on 14 August, with 68 votes in favour (corresponding to members of the Popular, Citizens' and Vox parliamentary groups in Madrid) and 64 against (corresponding to members of the Socialist, Más Madrid and United We Can-Madrid en Pie parliamentary groups).

In January 2020, she hired [Miguel Ángel Rodríguez Bajón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Baj%C3%B3n) as chief of staff. Her vice president, [Ignacio Aguado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Aguado), then explained his disagreement with the decision.[^cite_note-20]

On 1 December 2020, Díaz Ayuso inaugurated the [Hospital Isabel Zendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_de_Emergencias_Enfermera_Isabel_Zendal),[^cite_note-21] praised by her government but heavily criticised by the opposition for its cost overruns and its use as an "advertising campaign".[^cite_note-22]

After [Storm Filomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Filomena), the opposition criticised her government performance, accusing it of "lack of anticipation" against the incidences caused by the storm, that seriously damaged some infrastructure and parks in the region as well as trapping many citizens of the region for the night on the road, with snow accumulation of more than 50 centimeters in some points.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### COVID-19 pandemic

Six months after Díaz Ayuso's inauguration as President of the Community of Madrid, the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) reached Spain with special virulence in the Community of Madrid. In accordance with the [state of alarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_alarm_\(Spain\)) decreed by the [Spanish government](1306113.md) on 14 March 2020, the government of the Community of Madrid, like the other autonomous communities, took immediate measures \[required clarification] such as the closure of schools and leisure centres, shows and sports events, retired people's day centres, religious celebrations, the closure of hotels and tourist accommodation, shops considered not to be of basic need and the limitation of travel to those needed to go to work or buy food. It also adopted protection measures in public transport, and tax reductions in the payment of Business Tax (IAE) and Property Tax (IBI) for leisure, hotel and commercial premises, travel agencies and large stores, on the condition that they maintain the jobs until the end of the year. Presential activity was suspended in all Community Citizen Service offices, and [remote work](158555.md) was encouraged.[^cite_note-25]

In view of the saturation of hospital services, Díaz Ayuso's government used the ferial enclosure IFEMA as an emergency hospital, which emploed 5,500 hospital beds in order to attend to all patients. IFEMA hospital was built in a record time of less than 2 weeks, and the idea of the emergency hospital gave rise to the future Zendal Hospital, inaugurated a few months later. Hotels in Madrid were also used to look after less sick patients.[^cite_note-26] Ayuso stopped appearing before the Madrid Assembly on 5 March 2020, and the chamber was closed for more than a month.[^cite_note-27]

Díaz Ayuso was criticised for delivering high-quality FFP2 and FFP3 protective masks to the citizens of the [Community of Madrid](69802.md) for free, which arguably could result in these kinds of masks becoming scarce in hospitals – it was later revealed that hospitals were also well-supplied with this equipment.[^cite_note-28] In May 2020, during the coronavirus pandemic in Spain, her Director General of Public Health, [Yolanda Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolanda_Fuentes?action=edit\&redlink=1), resigned, disagreeing with Díaz Ayuso's decision to request the transfer from phase 0 to phase 1 of the confinement, because it was not based on "health criteria", a position Ayuso reportedly took after a meeting with businessmen.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

Díaz Ayuso opposed the request of her coalition partners, the [Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\)) party, for the army to intervene in retirement homes.[^cite_note-\:1-31] However, the army did so, against her own criteria, finding corpses and people "in extreme situations and in poor sanitary conditions.[^cite_note-\:1-31]

In October 2020, Alberto Reyero, the regional minister responsible for nursing homes, announced his resignation, wishing "good luck and success in the task that she has ahead" to Díaz Ayuso. He also mentioned that "the unity of the institutions is the most successful way to defeat the virus", criticizing the confrontation between the regional and national administrations.[^cite_note-32] However, six days later, the central government approved the State of Alarm for the Community of Madrid following unsuccessful talks and confrontation between Díaz Ayuso and PM Pedro Sánchez.[^cite_note-33] On 20 October 2020, two health officials of the Ayuso administration (the manager of Primary Attention and the manager of Hospitals) handed in their resignation.[^cite_note-34]

### 2021 snap election

On 10 March 2021, following an announcement by the [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_the_Region_of_Murcia) (PSOE) and [Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\)) (Cs) to bring down the [People's Party](404727.md)-led government in the [Region of Murcia](20515376.md), Díaz Ayuso announced the breakup of her alliance with Cs in her own region and called a [snap election](462915.md) in the Community of Madrid scheduled for 4 May. However, both the PSOE and [Más Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1s_Madrid) preventively filed [motions of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motions_of_no_confidence_in_Spain) in an attempt to thwart Díaz Ayuso's move.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

She approved tax reform in April 2021 to lower the estate and gift tax.[^cite_note-38]

Following her party's victory in the [May elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Madrilenian_regional_election), she was able to form a new government of the [People's Party of the Community of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_of_the_Community_of_Madrid) with the external support of [Vox](41928488.md), after her former colleagues of Citizens failed to gain a single seat.[^cite_note-39]

### Second Madrid government

Main article: [Second government of Isabel Díaz Ayuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_government_of_Isabel_D%C3%ADaz_Ayuso)

On 31 August 2021, she announced her intention to contest the leadership of the regional branch of the party, a position left vacant since the resignation of former President [Cristina Cifuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Cifuentes).[^cite_note-40]

In February 2022, Spanish media revealed Diaz Ayuso's People's Party made repeated attempts to illegally spy on her in the hope of obtaining sensitive information and blackmailing her out of the race to seize control of the Madrid branch of the party. The party's federal leadership closed in on then-unreleased allegations Ayuso's brother was involved in €1.5 M worth of healthcare material purchased by her administration at the height of the COVID-19 pandemic in April 2020 in Spain. When disclosed, she reacted by insisting her brother's business deals were performed according to law and received no special treatment, whilst publicly accusing her party's top officials of conspiring against her. The scandal was sealed with the resignation of both PP President Pablo Casado and Deputy President García Egea, Ayuso herself managing to survive relatively unscathed and emboldened in her bid for the party's leadership in Madrid.

Between 200,000 and 670,000 demonstrators gather on 13 November 2022 in Madrid to defend the public health system in the region and against a proposed reform of the sector. The demonstration is aimed at the health policies of Isabel Diaz Ayuso, who wants to develop [public-private partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership) and restructure the community care system. According to the unions, these services have been under pressure for several years, due to a lack of resources and personnel, and to poor regional management.[^cite_note-41]

### Third Madrid government

Main article: [Third government of Isabel Díaz Ayuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_government_of_Isabel_D%C3%ADaz_Ayuso)

Following the [2023 Madrilenian regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Madrilenian_regional_election), the People's Party obtained its first absolute majority under her leadership, which allowed her to govern without the need of the far-right Vox for the first time.

Díaz Ayuso is [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) on economic issues.[^cite_note-42] She has been described as a [populist](8354382.md) by several international newspapers, including *[Politico](9172097.md)*,[^cite_note-43] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*,[^cite_note-Guardian_May_2023-44] and *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)*.[^cite_note-45]

Having broken in as leader of the PP candidacy with a harsh speech close in style to that of [José María Aznar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Aznar), [Esperanza Aguirre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanza_Aguirre) and [Pablo Casado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Casado), Díaz Ayuso declared herself to be "next to [Vox](41928488.md), not in front of it".[^cite_note-publico_5-4-21-46] In May 2021, she stated that "when they call you a fascist you know you’re doing something right."[^cite_note-47] In April 2021, she stated that she and PP "agreed on fundamental issues" with the far-right party Vox and "that will continue to be the case."[^cite_note-48]

In 2023, she called for the Basque nationalist party [EH Bildu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EH_Bildu) to be banned, claiming that "[ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\)) is still alive" in the party.[^cite_note-Guardian_May_2023-44] She has claimed that former [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_\(Spanish_political_party\)) secretary general [Pablo Iglesias Turrión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Iglesias_Turri%C3%B3n) was "born from evil, to do evil."[^cite_note-49]

Some of her positions and comments strained relations with her 2019-2021 government partners, [Ciudadanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\)), which led to an early election.[^cite_note-50] She has been compared to current American president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) by several of her critics.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

### Social issues

In the same month of April, when she was already the candidate for the presidency of the [Community of Madrid](69802.md), she proposed that children conceived but not born yet be counted as members of family units for the purpose of applying for social aid or school places. She did not clarify whether these aids would be maintained in the event that the baby was not born.[^cite_note-53] In February 2020, she stated that the LGBT law of the Community of Madrid approved by her predecessor in office – who also belonged to the [People's Party](404727.md) and of which she herself voted in favour – was a consequence of "tyrannical progressivism" and that, if it were up to her, "some articles would have to be repealed".[^cite_note-54] During a heated debate with [Rocío Monasterio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc%C3%ADo_Monasterio), the president of [Vox](41928488.md) Madrid, Díaz Ayuso stated that she does not want to repeal the LGBT law or any other social laws. Instead, she believed that these laws can be "improved upon".[^cite_note-55] In December 2023, Díaz Ayuso's government approved reforms to the regional LGBT laws that eliminated penalties for discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity, becoming the first jurisdiction in Europe to do so.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

### Colonialism

She joined a chorus of politicians, including Spain's Socialist Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md), that repudiated the demand for an apology for colonialism by the President of Mexico, [Andrés Manuel López Obrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Manuel_L%C3%B3pez_Obrador).[^cite_note-58] Ayuso accused the Mexican President of instigating an "indigenism that is the new communism", and later said that indigenous movements across Latin America were promoting "a simplistic revision of Spanish history", the wrecking of "Spain's legacy in the Americas…\[such as] the mixing and fusion of cultures that have forged such strong Atlantic links", and that Spain "took Spanish and... Catholicism, and therefore civilisation and freedom, to the American continent".[^cite_note-jones-59] She further disagreed with [Pope Francis](1687680.md)'s statement that the Catholic Church should ask forgiveness for its actions in Mexico.[^cite_note-jones-59]

In May 2026. Ayuso participated in an event titled "Celebration of Evangelization and Mestizaje in Mexico: Malinche and Cortés" in Mexico City (at Frontón México, after a planned [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City_Metropolitan_Cathedral) venue was reportedly changed). She defended the Spanish Conquest as a positive process of "hope, joy, alliances" and five centuries of [mestizaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), paying tribute to figures like [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s) and Queen Isabella. This drew strong backlash in both Spain and Mexico.[^cite_note-60] Ayuso had previously called Mexico a "narco-state" and President Claudia Sheinbaum responded by criticizing Ayuso's views on the Conquest, calling such vindications of Cortés "destined for defeat".[^cite_note-61]

The Mexican newspaper *[El Pais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pais)* noted that Díaz Ayuso spells the country as "Méjico";[^cite_note-elpais-ortografia-62] The spelling with "j" was introduced by Spain's [Real Academia Española](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Academia_Espa%C3%B1ola) [in a spelling reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_orthography#History) around 1815, concurrent with the [Mexican war of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_war_of_independence) from Spain, and [the "j" spelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Mexico#Normative_spelling_in_Spanish) was suggested by RAE until the early 21st century.[^cite_note-natgeo-63] However, in Mexico itself the spelling with "x" has been used universally since its independence, with Mexicans "resist\[ing] the change of the spelling of their country's name."[^cite_note-natgeo-63] The spelling "México" is now recommended by the RAE, as elsewhere in the Spanish-speaking world.[^cite_note-natgeo-63]

### Climate

Regarding [climate change](5042951.md), she says it "[has always existed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial)" and [dismissed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_skepticism) "apocalyptic claims" as part of a "communist [plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_conspiracy_theory)".[^cite_note-64] In April 2019, she claimed night traffic jams were a "hallmark" of the city of Madrid, lamenting their disappearance with the start-up of the low-emission zone [Madrid Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Central).[^cite_note-65] Later, in an interview with *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)*, she clarified that "... I love the nightlife here, I have lived it with intensity. I hate traffic jams: I hate them. I just miss that nightlife and everyone has understood what I meant".[^cite_note-publico_5-4-21-46]

## Honours

In September 2021 Díaz Ayuso was awarded the Bruno Leoni Prize in recognition of the policies implemented by the Community of Madrid to address the COVID-19 epidemic, avoiding indiscriminate [lockdowns](2530301.md) and aiming to preserve [individual freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_freedom) and economic activities. The prize is awarded by [Istituto Bruno Leoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Bruno_Leoni), an Italian [classical-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberalism) [think tank](37101.md).[^cite_note-66] This was the first instance of a government personality being selected while in office.[^cite_note-67] Previously, the prize had been awarded to personalities such as [Nobel](21201.md) laureates [Vernon L. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon_L._Smith) and [Mario Vargas Llosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vargas_Llosa), academics ([Richard Pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pipes) and [Deirdre McCloskey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deirdre_McCloskey)), and democracy activists ([Leopoldo López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_L%C3%B3pez) and [Canan Arın](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canan_Ar%C4%B1n)).[^cite_note-68]
