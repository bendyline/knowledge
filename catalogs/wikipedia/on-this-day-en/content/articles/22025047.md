1947 map of mines in Chile

The mining sector in [Chile](5489.md) has historically been and continues to be one of the pillars of the [Chilean economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Chile).[^cite_note-3] **Mining in Chile** is concentrated in 14 mining districts, all of them in the northern half of the country and in particular in the [Norte Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Grande,_Chile) region spanning most of the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndradeMuñozSalazar20254-4]

Chile was, in 2025, the world's largest producer of copper,[^cite_note-usgscopper-5][^cite_note-cifras-6] [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine)[^cite_note-usgsiodine-7] and [rhenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenium),[^cite_note-usgsrhenium-8] the third largest producer of [lithium](17561.md)[^cite_note-usgslithium-9] and [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum),[^cite_note-cifras-6] the fifth largest producer of [salt](1605200.md),[^cite_note-usgssalt-10] the sixth largest producer of [silver](27119.md),[^cite_note-cifras-6] the ninth largest producer of [potash](56509.md),[^cite_note-usgspotash-11] the thirteenth producer of [sulfur](27127.md)[^cite_note-usgssulfur-12] and the fourteenth producer of [iron ore](145352.md)[^cite_note-usgsiron-1] in the world. As a by-product of copper mining Chile produces also large quantities of gold and silver, but there are also a number of mines extracting gold as their primary resource.[^cite_note-cifrasoro-13]

In 2021 [mining taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_taxes) stood for 19% of the Chilean state's incomes.[^cite_note-magdalena-14] Mining stood for about 14% of [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) but by estimates including economic activity linked to mining it stood for 20% of GDP.[^cite_note-magdalena-14] About 3% of Chile's workforce work in mines and quarries but in a wider sense about 10% of the country's employment is linked to mining.[^cite_note-magdalena-14]

The governance of mining in Chile is done by non-overlapping bodies; the [Chilean Copper Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Copper_Commission), [ENAMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENAMI_\(Chile\)), the [National Geology and Mining Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geology_and_Mining_Service) (SERNAGEOMIN) and the [Ministry of Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Mining_\(Chile\)).[^cite_note-soren-15] [SONAMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SONAMI) and [Consejo Minero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consejo_Minero) are guilds associations grouping corporate mining interests in Chile.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUlloa_UrrutiaContreras_BiekertGana_AravenaMiranda_Toledo201750-16] Chile's [National Statistics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Statistics_Institute_\(Chile\)) reports on the state of the mining industry through the monthly publication of the [Mining Production Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_Production_Index).[^cite_note-17]

Some challenges of the Chilean mining industry come from overall [mine aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_aging?action=edit\&redlink=1), remoteness and harsh climatic conditions of mining in the high [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes),[^cite_note-21] and increased water demand coupled with water scarcity.[^cite_note-23] Other challenges are related to increasingly complex legal frameworks or the fact that important mineral deposits lie below or next to glaciers along the [Argentina–Chile border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Chile_border) and have thus both issues relating to the bi-nationality and of [environmental impacts on glaciers and rock glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_mining_on_glaciers?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-treinta-24]

## Copper

Main article: [Copper mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_mining_in_Chile)



[Slag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slag) disposal from the copper smelter of [Caletones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundici%C3%B3n_Caletones) next to [El Teniente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Teniente), an [underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_mining_\(hard_rock\)) [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) [mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) in the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) of [Central Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Chile) (2005)



Chilean copper miners

Chile is the world's largest producer of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper)[^cite_note-usgscopper-5] and has been so uninterruptedly since 1983. This activity provides a substantial part of the Chilean state's revenue: slightly less than 6% in 2020, with state-owned copper company [Codelco](985804.md) alone generating 2.6% of state revenue.[^cite_note-corvera-25]

Mining of copper in Chile is done chiefly on giant [low-grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore_grade) [porphyry copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper) deposits. As of 2023 the most productive copper mine is [Escondida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escondida) owned by [BHP](200825.md), [Rio Tinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_\(corporation\)) and two other private companies and located in inland [Antofagasta Region](72842.md).[^cite_note-fivecopper-26]

Part of the state's income from copper mining goes to the [Economic and Social Stabilization Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Social_Stabilization_Fund) which is since 207 the successor to the [Copper Stabilization Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Stabilization_Fund) established in 1987.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECeballosTilton2005295-27] This fund allows for more precise annual government budget planning given that [copper prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_prices) can exhibit [strong fluctuations](11930108.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECeballosTilton2005298-28]

## Lithium

Main article: [Lithium mining in Chile](69112591.md)

Northern Chile forms part of the [Lithium Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_Triangle) with substantial [reserves](17561.md#Reserves_and_occurrence) in the form of brine. The explosive growth in electric vehicles since 2015 has triggered increased demand.

Chile is the main producer of lithium from [brine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brine).[^cite_note-cabello2022-29] Until 2017, when it was [surpassed by Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_mining_in_Australia), Chile was the over-all main producer of lithium.[^cite_note-TheEco22-30] Estimates show that Chile is expected to be surpassed also by Argentina and China in lithium production by 2030.[^cite_note-TheEco22-30] Lithium-expert [Gustavo Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Lagos) suggests that lithium production in Chile will by 2030 represent be about 8% of the world's total production.[^cite_note-31] Chile has the world's cheapest production costs for lithium and this could be an advantage for mining in Chile once [recycled lithium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_recycling?action=edit\&redlink=1) enters the market competing with costly mining operations in the future.[^cite_note-32]

Most of Chile's lithium reserves are in [Salar de Atacama](9980356.md) and [Salar de Maricunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_de_Maricunga?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-cabello2022-29] and all lithium extracted in Chile as of 2023 comes from Salar de Atacama.[^cite_note-Munita-33] The only two lithium-extracting companies currently operating in Chile, [SQM](7290045.md) and [Albemarle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_Corporation), have licences to extract lithium until 2030 and 2043 respectively.[^cite_note-Munita-33][^cite_note-exante21-34] In April 2023 Chilean government announced plans for nationalizing its lithium industry.[^cite_note-reuternat21-35] The state-owned copper company [Codelco](985804.md) was commissioned by the government to negotiate nationalization with SQM.[^cite_note-exante21-34]

## Gold

Main article: [Gold mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_mining_in_Chile)



Gold mine in [Andacollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andacollo).

The amount of gold mined in Chile has fluctuated in the 2010–2023 period from a high of 50,852 kg in 2013 to a low of 30,907 kg in 2022.[^cite_note-cifrasoro-13] Also in the same period 36% to 72% of the gold produced annually in Chile was a [by-product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-product) of copper mining.[^cite_note-cifrasoro-13] The share of [medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-scale_mining) and small-scale mining in gold production in Chile has dropped from an average of 45% for the 2003–2005 period to 9% in 2023.[^cite_note-cifrasoro-13]

Most of the economically viable gold deposits in Chile belong to two types of deposits; high-sulfidation epithermal and porphyry type.[^cite_note-goldcabello-36] Most of these deposits formed in the last 66 millions years ([Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic)) in connection to magmatic activity in the Andes.[^cite_note-goldcabello-36] Gold from [iron oxide copper gold ore deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_oxide_copper_gold_ore_deposits) (IOCG), from [mesothermal deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orogenic_gold_deposit#Historical_term), or of Mesozoic age (formed 66 to 252 million years ago) may in some cases be recurrent geological features but lack often large concentrations to make them profitable.[^cite_note-goldcabello-36] Almost all valuable non-placer gold in Chile occur in the northern half of the country and some deposits are grouped into belts like the [Maricunga Gold Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricunga_Gold_Belt) and [El Indio Gold Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Indio_Gold_Belt).[^cite_note-goldcabello-36]

Almost no mining of [placer gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placer_deposit) occurs today.[^cite_note-placer19-37] The placer deposits of some areas of difficult access in [Patagonia](65433.md) are subject to sporadic small-scale [illegal gold mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_mining_in_Chile).[^cite_note-soren-15] A 2019 study found that seven of Chile's ten best placer gold prospects lie around [Cordillera de Nahuelbuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Nahuelbuta).[^cite_note-placer19-37]

## Iron

Main article: [Iron mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_mining_in_Chile)



Train moving iron ore in the industrial area of [Los Colorados mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Colorados_mine).

Since at least 2010 Chile's has each year produced 0.6% to 0.7% of all iron mined in the world.[^cite_note-cifras-6] Production has risen from 6.8 million metric tonnes in 2010 to more than 10 million metric tonnes each year beginning 2021.[^cite_note-cifras-6] Mining of iron ore deposits along the [Chilean Iron Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Iron_Belt) have been facilitated by their proximity to the ports of export at the coast, and this had in particular had an impact for the economic viability of small iron ore deposits.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillán199992-38] [Compañía Minera del Pacífico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compa%C3%B1%C3%ADa_Minera_del_Pac%C3%ADfico) (CMP) is largest iron mining company in Chile.[^cite_note-CMCMP-39] Is through its parent company [Compañía de Acero del Pacífico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compa%C3%B1%C3%ADa_de_Acero_del_Pac%C3%ADfico) (CAP) a member of [Consejo Minero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consejo_Minero), a guild of large mining companies in the country.[^cite_note-40] Compañia Minera del Pacífico has three main mines each with its own port for export.[^cite_note-CMPself-41] Near [Copiapó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copiap%C3%B3) the company owns [Cerro Negro Norte mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Negro_Norte_mine) which uses the port of [Punta Totoralillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Totoralillo), further south the company is in ownership of [Los Colorados mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Colorados_mine) which uses the port of Guacolda II, and near the city of [La Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Serena,_Chile) [El Romeral mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Romeral_mine) is operated using the port of [Guayacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Guayac%C3%A1n?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Coquimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquimbo).[^cite_note-CMPself-41]

In [medium-scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-scale_mining) iron mining in Chile the mines and deposits of [El Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Carmen_mine), [Huantemé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huantem%C3%A9), [Cerro Imán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Im%C3%A1n) and [El Dorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dorado_mine) have been important in the second half of the 20th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillán1999192-42]

The [Dominga project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominga) led by [Andes Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Iron) seeks to establish a new iron and copper mine near the coast of northern Coquimbo Region.[^cite_note-laboerde24-43] This project has proved controversial for political and environmental reasons.[^cite_note-laboerde24-43]

## Iodine and nitrate

See also: [List of Saltpeter works in Tarapacá and Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Saltpeter_works_in_Tarapac%C3%A1_and_Antofagasta)

In the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md) in northern [Chile](5489.md) there are vast superficial deposits of [caliche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliche), a mixture of [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), [sodium chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_chloride) and other salts, and sand. It is associated to the mineral [nitratine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitratine) also known as "Chile saltpeter" (Spanish: *salitre*). The deposits contain an average of 7.5% sodium nitrate, as well as [sodium sulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_sulfate) (18.87%), [sodium chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_chloride) (4.8%), and smaller amounts of [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium), [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium), [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium), [borate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borate), [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine), and [perchlorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perchlorate). About two-thirds of the deposits are insoluble [gangue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangue) minerals. The caliche beds are from 2 cm to several meters thick in [alluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial) deposits, where the soluble minerals form a cement in unconsolidated [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith). Nitrate-bearing caliche is also found permeating bedrock to form bedrock deposits.[^cite_note-WisniakGarces2001-44]

Nitratine is a composite of [sodium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_nitrate) (NaNO<sub>3</sub>) and [potassium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_nitrate) (KNO<sub>3</sub>). Nitratine was an important source of export revenue for Chile until [World War I](4764461.md), when Europe began to produce both nitrates [industrially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber_Process) in large quantities.[^cite_note-WisniakGarces2001-44] Mining nitrate in the [Far North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Grande) of Chile was arguably the main economic activity of the country from 1880 to 1930.[^cite_note-memsalitre-45]

Caliche is the main [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine) ore in Chile and the country is the world's prime producer of this element in addition to hosting over half of the worlds [reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_reserve) of iodine.[^cite_note-lifeiodine-46][^cite_note-USGS_Iodine_Production_Statistics-47] [SQM](7290045.md) and [Cosayach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosayach) are Chile's first and second largest iodine producers.[^cite_note-lifeiodine-46][^cite_note-48] Iodine at SQM is extracted from [caliche ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliche) and requires also the consumables [sulphur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur), [ammonium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_nitrate), [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid), kerosene, water, electricity and fossil fuel, mainly diesel.[^cite_note-lifeiodine-46]

## Other minerals

See also: [Chilean silver rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_silver_rush)

Since the late 1970s, the production of [gold](12240.md) and [silver](27119.md) has increased greatly. The lead, iron and petroleum industries have shrunk since the mid-1970s, the result of both adverse international market conditions and declines in the availability of some of these resources. With a combined total value of about US\$4 billion, two of the largest investments planned in Chile in the early 1990s were designated for an [aluminium smelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_smelter) projects in the [Puerto Aisén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ais%C3%A9n)[^cite_note-EyN06-49] and [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan) areas.

In the 2005–2024 period more than half of the silver produced annually in Chile was a [by-product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-product) of copper mining.[^cite_note-cifras-6]

### Coal

Main article: [Coal mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_Chile)

Historically, [coal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_Chile) had some importance in the southern half of the country from the 1850s to the 1990s[^cite_note-Davis1990-50][^cite_note-CNEexplo-51] with a brief revival in [Invierno mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invierno_mine) from 2013 to 2020.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-Reportemin-53]

### Cobalt

There is no primary mining of [cobalt](24580536.md) in Chile with the last activity ending in 1944.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETownleyDíazLuca2017b-54] Cobalt resources are known from the [Chilean Iron Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Iron_Belt) near the coast of [Coquimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquimbo_Region) and [Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Region) regions and in the site of El Volcán in [Cajón del Maipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caj%C3%B3n_del_Maipo) in the Andes near Santiago.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETownleyDíazLuca2017b-54] Cobalt is a potential by-product of iron and copper mining along the iron belt.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETownleyDíazLuca2017b-54] It is known to be found in considerable concentrations among discarded material –mainly [tailings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailings)– of copper, iron and gold mining in Chile.[^cite_note-gastoncobalt-55] [Capstone Copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capstone_Copper)'s mines of [Mantoverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantoverde_mine) and [Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo_mine) are thought to be able to produce battery-grade [cobalt](24580536.md).[^cite_note-inmin-56]

### Manganese

There is no [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) mining in Chile since 2009 when Empresa Manganeso Atacama ceased operations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETownleyDíazLuca201749-57] Until then about half of the Chilean manganese had been exported to [Argentina](18951905.md), and mining was mainly done in underground mines.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETownleyDíazLuca201749-57] Historically [Corral Quemado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corral_Quemado_mine) and other areas of [Coquimbo Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquimbo_Region) have produced most manganese in Chile.[^cite_note-manghis-58] Manganese mining in Chile and Corral Quemado had a strong peak in 1943 when it came to produce more of what was being purchased leading to large [stockpiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockpile) accumulating in ports and railway stations and ultimately to a halt in mining and thus mass unemployment.[^cite_note-manghis-58] Transport costs have been a [comparative disadvantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_disadvantage) for the commercialization of manganese mined in Chile.[^cite_note-manghis-58] The area around [Taltal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taltal) was second to Corral Quemado in importance in manganese mining in the 1940s.[^cite_note-manghis-58] Known manganese deposits are scattered along the length of Chile from [Arica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arica) (18.5° S) to [Valdivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdivia) (39.5° S).[^cite_note-manghis-58] In detail, the known manganese deposits concentrate in three areas; the [Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano) in northernmost Chile, the Coquimbo and [Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Region) regions and the [metasedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock) of the provinces of [Cautín](4138209.md) and [Valdivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdivia_Province).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETownleyDíazLuca201728-59] In the regions of Coquimbo and Atacama manganese appear as stratabound layers in the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) volcanic and sedimentary formations of [Arqueros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arqueros_Formation) and [Quebrada Marquesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebrada_Marquesa_Formation).[^cite_note-aguirre-60] In Arqueros Formation it occurs in some locations together with stratabound copper.[^cite_note-aguirre-60] By 1964 87% of the magnesium mined in Chile came from these formations in Coquimbo Region.[^cite_note-aguirre-60]

### Rare-earth metals

Main article: [Rare-earth resources in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare-earth_resources_in_Chile)

While relatively [unexplored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_exploration)[^cite_note-REElegal-61] for [rare-earth metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare-earth_metal) Chile's main [resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_resource_classification) have been identified as of 2026 to lie in the commune of [Penco](6686023.md) near the coast in [central Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Chile) and in the [Norte Chico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Chico,_Chile) between [Coquimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquimbo) and [Copiapó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copiap%C3%B3).[^cite_note-REEbb-62] Since at least 2016 the company [Aclara Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aclara_Resources) has been developing plans to commercially exploit rare-earth metals in Penco.[^cite_note-ltree-63] As of March 2026 its [environmental impact assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_assessment) was under evaluation by the [Environmental Assessment Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Assessment_Service).[^cite_note-ltree-63] If approved the mine is planned to produce an average of 811 tons of RRE-oxides.[^cite_note-ltree-63] About 60 km south of Penco [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith) formed from [granites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) of the [Coastal Batholith of central Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Batholith_of_central_Chile) in [Cordillera de Nahuelbuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Nahuelbuta) have been investigated as a source of rare-earth metals.[^cite_note-64] North of Penco NeoRe and Chilean Cobalt Corp have together explored for REE in the coastal communes of [Chanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanco,_Chile), [Cobquecura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobquecura) and [Pelluhue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelluhue).[^cite_note-ltree-63]

Old [tailings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailings) from the mining of other resources have also been identified as potential sources of rare-earth metals in Chile.[^cite_note-REElegal-61]

Most [medium-scale mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-scale_mining) is concentrated near roads or other pre-existing infrastructure, and lie thus away from the high Andes where nearly all mines belong to the large-scale mining category.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndradeMuñozSalazar202524-65] The mining districts of [Chañaral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha%C3%B1aral), [Copiapó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copiap%C3%B3), [Huasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huasco) and [Andacollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andacollo) have most of their mining done by medium-scale mining companies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndradeMuñozSalazar202524-65] The state-owned enterprise [ENAMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENAMI_\(Chile\)) has among its goals supporting medium-scale mining.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUlloa_UrrutiaContreras_BiekertGana_AravenaMiranda_Toledo201750-16] Medium-scale mining has a larger share of mining properties in the country as whole, and in [Atacama Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Region) in particular, relative to large-scale mining that is dominant in the regions of [Tarapacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapac%C3%A1_Region) and [Antofagasta](72842.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUlloa_UrrutiaContreras_BiekertGana_AravenaMiranda_Toledo2017283-66]

Medium-scale mining in Chile tends to focus on copper and produced about 4.5% of the copper mined in the country from 2017 to 2021.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuajardoMarañónCiudaddel_Mauro202322-67] In that period the copper extracted by medium-scale mining increased each year starting from 256 [kt](31185.md#Derived_units) in 2017 ato 313 kt in 2021.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuajardoMarañónCiudaddel_Mauro202322-67] Besides copper other medium-scale mining activity in Chile involve gold, iron, [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) and [lead](17747.md).[^cite_note-endboommedium-68] The amount and share of the gold mined by small and medium scale mining in Chile has declined significantly from 2003 to 2023.[^cite_note-cifrasoro-13] In iron mining [El Carmen mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Carmen_mine) near [Chañaral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha%C3%B1aral) is a leading medium-scale producer.[^cite_note-endboommedium-68] According to figures from 2013 all zinc and lead mining in Chile was done by medium and small-scale miners.[^cite_note-endboommedium-68]

Most mineral exploration efforts by medium-scale mining are done near established mines (brownfield exploration), and as of 2023 about three quartes of these exploration projects are for copper and the remaining for gold.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuajardoMarañónCiudaddel_Mauro202337-69]

Some medium-scale mining companies in Chile are [Grupo Minero Las Cenizas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Minero_Las_Cenizas?action=edit\&redlink=1) (copper), [Sierra Atacama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Atacama) (copper),[^cite_note-72] [Haldeman Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldeman_Mining) (copper and gold),[^cite_note-74] [Cosayach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosayach) (iodine) and [Santa Fe Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Mining) (iron).

## Small-scale mining

As with medium-scale mining, small scale mining concentrates in lowlands and the lower elevations of the Andes, usually near roads or other relevant infrastructure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndradeMuñozSalazar20256–7-75] The number of artisan miners in Chile, often known as *[pirquineros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirquinero)*, has varied widely over the years.[^cite_note-soren-15] Since 2000 in some years with high metal prices have had up to c. 14,000 small-scale miners active. On average 95% of small-scale miners work in copper mining.[^cite_note-soren-15] These miners are supported by [ENAMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENAMI_\(Chile\)) which processes copper ore it purchases at stabilized prices.[^cite_note-soren-15][^cite_note-sona-76] The levels of [illegal mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_mining_in_Chile) are low relative to neighbouring countries.[^cite_note-emolcifras-77]

## Tailings

| \[icon] | This section **needs expansion**. You can help by [adding missing information](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mining_in_Chile\&action=edit\&section=). *(September 2025)* |
| --- | --- |

Chile hosts as of 2025 836 [tailings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailings) deposits of which 627 are inactive and 53 are abandoned.[^cite_note-relavnme-79] The remaining is ctailings, 129 are actively used by mines.[^cite_note-relavnme-79] Some companies like Minera Valle Central and [Compañía Minera del Pacífico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compa%C3%B1%C3%ADa_Minera_del_Pac%C3%ADfico) processes the tailings of third parties. Minera Valle Central processes the tailings of [El Teniente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Teniente) and recovers molybdenum and copper. [Planta Magnetita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planta_Magnetita) of Compañía Minera del Pacífico processes tailings from [Candelaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candelaria_mine) copper mine and some [ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore) of [Los Colorados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Colorados_mine) iron mine.[^cite_note-conplantamag-80][^cite_note-concolorado-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-circular-83]

From 1978 to 2010 [Planta de Pellets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planta_de_Pellets) in [Huasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huasco) disposed its tailings legally in the sea, being the only marine disposal of tailings in Chile.[^cite_note-84]

## History

Main article: [History of mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mining_in_Chile)

The [history of mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mining_in_Chile) spans more than thousand years, with early copper mining in [Chiquicamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiquicamata) dating to the 6th century[^cite_note-85] and cultures and groups such as the [El Molle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Molle_culture), [Diaguita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaguita) and [Mapuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche) mining or using gold adornments well before the [Inca invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incas_in_Central_Chile) in the 15th century.[^cite_note-indag2021-86][^cite_note-treinta-24] The successive establishment of [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire) and [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) rule in the northern half of Chile intensified mining and brought new techniques to the industry. In the early Spanish period (1542–1600) there was significant mining of [gold placers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placer_mining) which fueled [Spanish–Mapuche conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arauco_War) climaxing with [a collapse of Spanish rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Seven_Cities) in [the foremost gold district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Nahuelbuta) and a reorentation of Spanish economy towards agriculture.[^cite_note-Zavala2014-87][^cite_note-placer19-37][^cite_note-villalobosHacienda-88] Gold, silver and copper mining had a resurgence in the late colonial period (18th century).[^cite_note-villalobosMining-89] Exports of silver and copper were instrumental to finance the [Chilean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_War_of_Independence) (1810–1826)[^cite_note-Cortes2017p25-90] and then to prevent Chile [defaulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Default_\(finance\)) in its [independence debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_independence_debt) the 1830s and 1840s.[^cite_note-perez2015-91] In the 19th century Chile was [a major producer of silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_silver_rush) (1830s to 1850s) and copper (1850s to 1870s),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESutulov19753-92][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECamus2005233-93] but towards the end of the century mining of gold, silver and copper were in decline.[^cite_note-94] An exception to this was the [Tierra del Fuego gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego_gold_rush) (1883–1906) in southernmost Chile. [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_Chile) and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_mining_in_Chile) mining in Chile took off in the mid-19th century and early 20th century respectively. From 1870 to the 1930 nitrate mining in the far north was an immerse source of wealth and employment in Chile.[^cite_note-memsalitre-45][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] Modern [copper mining in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_mining_in_Chile) begun in the 1900s and 1910s with the arrival of companies from the United States which were [fully nationalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_nationalization_of_copper) by 1971 under the state-owned copper company [Codelco](985804.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESutulov197531-97][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESalazarPinto2002124–125-98][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECamus2005245-99] A new wave of foreign investment of mining begun following the [Decreto Ley 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decreto_Ley_600) law of 1974 and by the 1990s the country was experiencing a new mining boom.[^cite_note-ximena-100][^cite_note-valenz-101]
