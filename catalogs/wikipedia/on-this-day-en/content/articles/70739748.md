**Vikram-1** ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), lit. 'Brave', also known as **Vikram-I**) is a four-stage, [expendable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expendable_launch_system) orbital [small-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-lift_launch_vehicle) developed by [Skyroot Aerospace](64795373.md). Its first three stages are [solid stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-propellant_rocket), while the fourth stage is a [liquid stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-propellant_rocket), powered by [hypergolic propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergolic_propellant).

The first flight, known as Aagaman, flew on 18 July 2026. The launch was a success, making India the third country with a private orbital launch capability, after the U.S. and China.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## Development



Schematic representation of Vikram-1

Skyroot Aerospace unveiled the Vikram-1 launch vehicle in October 2023 at its MAX-Q headquarters in [Hyderabad](37534.md).[^cite_note-3] The rocket is named after [Vikram Sarabhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram_Sarabhai), often described as the father of the Indian space program.[^cite_note-4] The company stated that the rocket was developed to serve the growing global [small satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite) launch market.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

The vehicle's development followed the successful launch of the suborbital demonstrator [Vikram-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram-S) in November 2022, which made Skyroot the first Indian private company to launch a rocket from [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO)'s launch facilities.[^cite_note-6]

In May 2022, Skyroot completed a full-duration static test of the Kalam-100 solid rocket stage, a component of Vikram-1. Additional qualification tests, including stage separation tests and avionics validation, were carried out through 2024 and 2025.[^cite_note-7]

In November 2025, Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) unveiled the flight-ready configuration of Vikram-1 during the inauguration of Skyroot's Infinity Campus in Hyderabad.[^cite_note-8]

## Design

Vikram-1 is a four-stage orbital launch vehicle approximately 20 metres (65 ft 7 in) tall and 1.7 metres (5 ft 7 in) in diameter.[^cite_note-9] The first three stages use solid propulsion, while the upper kick stage uses liquid propulsion for orbital insertion and adjustment manoeuvres.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

The rocket incorporates [carbon-composite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-fiber_reinforced_polymer) airframes intended to reduce structural mass. Skyroot has also stated that the vehicle uses [3D-printed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing) liquid engines and modular avionics systems.[^cite_note-11]

The Kalam-250 has [thrust vector control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_vectoring) for steering. The OAM (Orbital Adjustment Module) upper stage features [RCS thrusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_control_system) for attitude control.[^cite_note-12]

Skyroot advertises Vikram-1 as a responsive launch system capable of rapid integration and launch operations with minimal launch infrastructure requirements.[^cite_note-13] It is capable of conducting dedicated launches or rideshare missions.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

## Launch history

The inaugural flight of this launch vehicle was the Aagaman ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), 'Arrival') mission on 18 July 2026, which successfully launched miniaturized satellites into low Earth orbit.[^cite_note-\:2-10][^cite_note-\:1-14][^cite_note-15] Skyroot described Aagaman as a test flight and stated that it planned to conduct additional test flights before beginning routine commercial launches. The mission's payload was injected into orbit approximately 15 minutes after liftoff.[^cite_note-16]
