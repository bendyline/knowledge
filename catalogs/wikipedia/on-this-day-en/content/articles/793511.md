**Diyarbakır Province** ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Diyarbakır ili*; [Zazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazaki_language): *Suke Dîyarbekîr*;[^cite_note-2] [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): *Parêzgeha Amedê*)[^cite_note-3] is a [province](428896.md) and [metropolitan municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_municipalities_in_Turkey) in southeastern [Turkey](11125639.md). Its area is 15,101 km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-4] and its population is 1,804,880 (2022).[^cite_note-tuik-1] The provincial capital is the city of [Diyarbakır](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbak%C4%B1r). The [Kurdish](17068.md) majority province is part of [Turkish Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Kurdistan).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## History



The [Great Mosque of Diyarbakır](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Diyarbak%C4%B1r)

It has been home to many civilisations and the surrounding area including itself is home to many Mesolithic era stone carvings and artifacts. The province has been ruled by the [Akkadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadians), [Hurrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurrians), [Mittani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittani), [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes), [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites), [Armenians](387816.md), [Arameans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arameans), [Neo-Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonians), [Achaemenids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Greeks](42056.md), [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire), [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium), [Sassanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanids), [Arabs](2185.md), [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire), [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire), [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran), [Marwanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwanids_\(Diyar_Bakr\)), and [Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubids).

### Administrative history

In June 1927, the Law 1164 was passed[^cite_note-7] allowing the creation of [Inspectorates-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspectorates-General_\(Turkey\)) ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Umumi Müffetişlik*).[^cite_note-\:1-8]

The Diyarbakır province was therefore included in the [First Inspectorate General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Inspectorate-General_\(Turkey\)) ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Birinci Umumi Müffetişlik*), which was created on the 1 January 1928 and also included [Hakkâri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakk%C3%A2ri_Province), [Siirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siirt_Province), [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Province), [Mardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardin_Province), [Bitlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitlis_Province), [Sanlıurfa](1353138.md), and [Elaziğ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaz%C4%B1%C4%9F_Province).[^cite_note-\:02-9][^cite_note-10]

The Inspectorate-General was governed by an [Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_general), who governed with a wide-ranging authority over civilian, juridical and military matters.[^cite_note-\:1-8] The office of the Inspector General was dissolved in 1952 during the government of the [Democrat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Turkey,_1946%E2%80%9361\)).[^cite_note-11]

During the 1930s, several place-names in the province were renamed into names which denoted a Turkish origin as part of the nationalist [Turkification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkification) policy of the [Kemalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemalist) government.[^cite_note-12] Travel to Diyarbakır province was banned for foreign citizens until 1965.[^cite_note-\:02-9]

#### Modern history

In the [1975 Lice earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Lice_earthquake) a [M<sub>s</sub>](6207163.md#Ms) 6.7 struck the town of [Lice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lice,_Turkey). The town was re-established about 2 km (1.2 mi) south of its original location.

From 1987 to 2002, Diyarbakır Province was part of the [OHAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHAL) (state of emergency) region which was declared to counter the [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md) (PKK) and governed by a so-called Supergovernor who got invested with additional powers than a normal Governor. In 1987 he was given the power to relocate and resettle whole villages, settlements and hamlets.[^cite_note-\:0-13] In December 1990 with the Decree No. 430, the supergovernor and the provincial governors in the OHAL region received immunity against any legal prosecution in connections with actions they made due to the powers they received with the Decree No. 430.[^cite_note-14]

### Archaeology

Archaeologists headed by the vice-rector of [Dicle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicle_University), professor [Ahmet Tanyıldız](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmet_Tany%C4%B1ld%C4%B1z?action=edit\&redlink=1), have claimed to discover the graves of the Seljuk Sultan of Rum [Kilij Arslan I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilij_Arslan_I), who defeated the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades). They also revealed his daughter [Saide Hatun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saide_Hatun?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s burial in [Silvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvan,_Diyarbak%C4%B1r). Researchers dug 2 meters deep across a 35-square-meter area and focused their works on two gravesites in [Orta Çeşme Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orta_%C3%87e%C5%9Fme_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

In 2026, archaeologists announced genetic research on skeletal remains from Çayönü Hill in the Ergani district, shedding light on the lives of people who inhabited the site around 12,000 years ago. Çayönü, a major [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) settlement on the [Tigris River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris) with occupation layers dating back to 10,000 BCE, has yielded extensive architectural remains, burials and artefacts that illustrate early transitions from nomadic to settled life and the development of agriculture. Excavations resumed in 2025 under the direction of archaeologists from [Çanakkale Onsekiz Mart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87anakkale_Onsekiz_Mart_University) and [Hacettepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacettepe_University) universities, involving researchers from multiple Turkish institutions. Analysis of bones and [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) from individuals at the site including skeletal material from Neolithic, Pottery Neolithic and Early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) contexts aims to reconstruct population history, health, sociocultural practices and connections with broader regions such as [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) and the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus). Researchers report that skeletal and genetic evidence indicates a heterogeneous community with varied burial traditions, shared subsistence activities and regional interaction networks, and that forthcoming DNA results could clarify how these ancient populations fit into wider prehistoric sociocultural landscapes.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]
