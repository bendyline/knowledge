**Automobili Lamborghini S.p.A.[^cite_note-5]** (colloquially **Lambo**) is an Italian manufacturer of luxury [sports cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_car) and [SUVs](47696.md) based in [Sant'Agata Bolognese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Agata_Bolognese). The company is owned by the [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group) through its subsidiary [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi).

[Ferruccio Lamborghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferruccio_Lamborghini) (1916–1993), an Italian [manufacturing magnate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_magnate), founded Automobili Ferruccio Lamborghini S.p.A. in 1963 to compete with [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari). The company was noted for using a [rear mid-engine, rear-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_mid-engine,_rear-wheel-drive_layout) layout. Lamborghini grew rapidly during its first decade, but sales plunged in the wake of the [1973 worldwide financial downturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%931974_stock_market_crash) and the [oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis). The firm's ownership changed three times after 1973, including a bankruptcy in 1978. American [Chrysler Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) took control of Lamborghini in 1987 and sold it to Malaysian investment group Mycom Setdco and Indonesian group V'Power Corporation in 1994. In 1998, Mycom Setdco and V'Power sold Lamborghini to the Volkswagen Group where it was placed under the control of the group's Audi division.

New products and model lines were introduced to the brand's portfolio and brought to the market and saw an increased productivity for the brand. In the late 2000s, during the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), Lamborghini's sales dropped nearly 50%.

Lamborghini currently produces the [twin-turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin-turbo) [V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine) [plug-in Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) [Temerario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Temerario), the [Urus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Urus) SUV powered by a twin-turbo V8 engine, and the [Revuelto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Revuelto), a V12/electric hybrid, as of 2025. In addition, the company produces [V12 engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V12_engine) for [offshore powerboat racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_powerboat_racing).

[Lamborghini Trattori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Trattori), founded in 1948 by Ferruccio Lamborghini, is headquartered in [Pieve di Cento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieve_di_Cento), Italy, and continues to produce [tractors](152692.md). Since 1973, Lamborghini Trattori has been a separate entity from the Lamborghini's automobile division.

## History

Main article: [History of Lamborghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lamborghini)



[Ferruccio Lamborghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferruccio_Lamborghini) with a [Jarama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Jarama) and a tractor of his brand

Manufacturing magnate Italian [Ferruccio Lamborghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferruccio_Lamborghini) founded the company in 1963 with the objective of producing a refined [grand touring car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_tourer) to compete with offerings from established marques such as [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari). The company's first models, such as the [350 GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_350_GT), were released in the mid-1960s. Lamborghini was noted for the 1966 [Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Miura) sports [coupé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup%C3%A9), which used a [rear mid-engine, rear-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_mid-engine,_rear-wheel-drive_layout) layout.

Ferruccio Lamborghini, already an established industrialist who manufactured tractors, boilers, and air conditioners, founded Lamborghini Automobili on 7 May 1963.[^cite_note-6]

The company was headquartered in a purpose-built facility in [Sant'Agata Bolognese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Agata_Bolognese). He surrounded himself with highly capable engineers and technicians: [Giotto Bizzarrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto_Bizzarrini) designed the engine, [Gian Paolo Dallara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giampaolo_Dallara) and Paolo Stanzani developed the chassis, and [Franco Scaglione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Scaglione) designed the bodywork. The first model, the 350 GTV, was not a success due to its futuristic style and remained a one-off prototype. The project was then handed over to the Milanese coachbuilder Touring, which created a more classic and sober design. The new car, named the 350 GT, was a fast and elegant two-seater grand tourer (according to Ferruccio's standards) and was the first series-produced car by Lamborghini. It achieved moderate sales success and was followed by the 400 GT (which benefited from an increased engine capacity) and the 400 GT 2+2, both presented in 1966.

Lamborghini grew rapidly during its first ten years, but sales fell in the wake of the [1973 worldwide financial downturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%931974_stock_market_crash) and the [oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis). Ferruccio Lamborghini sold a majority stake in the company to Georges-Henri Rossetti in 1972 and sold his remaining interest to René Leimer, fully retiring from the business in 1974. The company went bankrupt in 1978, and was placed in the receivership of brothers Jean-Claude and [Patrick Mimran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Mimran) in 1980. The Mimrans purchased the company out of receivership by 1984 and invested heavily in its expansion. Under the Mimrans' management, Lamborghini's model line was expanded from the [Countach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Countach) to include the [Jalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Jalpa) sports car and the [LM002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_LM002) high-performance off-road vehicle.

The Mimrans sold Lamborghini to the [Chrysler Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) in 1987. After replacing the Countach with the [Diablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Diablo) and discontinuing the Jalpa and the LM002, Chrysler sold Lamborghini to MegaTech Ltd in 1994; the following year, Malaysian investment group Mycom Setdco and Indonesian group V'Power Corporation acquired the company. In 1998, Mycom Setdco and V'Power sold Lamborghini to the [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group) where it was placed under the control of the group's [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi) division. New products and model lines were introduced to the brand's portfolio and brought to the market and saw an increased productivity for the brand Lamborghini. In the late 2000s, during the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), Lamborghini's sales dropped nearly 50%.

In 2021, the CEO of Lamborghini said that by 2024 all its models will be [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_vehicle).[^cite_note-7]

## Products

### Automobiles

Main article: [List of Lamborghini automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lamborghini_automobiles)

As of 2026, Lamborghini's automobile product range consists of three model lines: [Revuelto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Revuelto), [Temerario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Temerario), and [Urus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Urus).[^cite_note-8]

#### Models in production

- **Revuelto**

Main article: [Lamborghini Revuelto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Revuelto)



Lamborghini Revuelto coupe

Production of the new Revuelto began in mid-2023 and was delivered in late 2023 as a 2024 model. A 6.5L naturally aspirated [V12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V12_engine) engine and three magnetic motors power the car for a combined output of 1,001 hp (1,015 PS). The Revuelto is the successor to the [Aventador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Aventador).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

- **Temerario**

Main article: [Lamborghini Temerario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Temerario)



Lamborghini Temerario

The Temerario is a [mid-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-engine_design) [plug-in hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) [sports car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_car). It was officially unveiled on 16 August 2024 and is the successor to the [Huracán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Hurac%C3%A1n).

- **Urus**

Main article: [Lamborghini Urus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Urus)



Lamborghini Urus

Intending to double its sales volume by 2019, Lamborghini also added an [SUV](47696.md) named Urus in its line-up which is powered by a [twin-turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin-turbo) [V8 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine) and utilizes a front engine, all-wheel drive layout.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Marine engines

Motori Marini Lamborghini produces a large V12 marine engine block for use in [World Offshore Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_powerboat_racing) Class 1 powerboats. A Lamborghini branded marine engine displaces approximately 8,171 cc (8.2 L) and outputs approximately 940 hp (700 kW).[^cite_note-13]

In 1968, company founder Ferruccio Lamborghini commissioned a Riva Aquarama speedboat powered by a pair of V12 engines from the Lamborghini 350 GT.[^cite_note-14]

### Lamborghini motorcycle

In the mid-1980s, Lamborghini produced a limited-production run of a 1,000 cc (61.0 cu in) [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportbike) motorcycle. UK weekly newspaper *[Motor Cycle News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Cycle_News)* reported in 1994—when featuring an example available through an [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex) motorcycle retailer—that 24 examples were produced with a Lamborghini [alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_alloy) frame having [adjustable steering head angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_tube), [Kawasaki GPz1000RX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_GPZ1000RX) engine/transmission unit, [Ceriani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceriani) [front forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_fork) and Marvic wheels. The bodywork was [plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_fairing#Materials) and fully integrated with [front fairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_fairing) merged into fuel tank and seat cover ending in a rear tail-fairing. The motorcycles were designed by Lamborghini stylists and produced by French business Boxer Bikes.[^cite_note-MCN_1994-15]

### Events held

#### Lamborghini Super Trofeo

Main article: [Lamborghini Super Trofeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Super_Trofeo)



[Lamborghini Gallardo Super Trofeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Gallardo) 2011 in [Hockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockenheimring)



[Lamborghini Huracán Super Trofeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Hurac%C3%A1n)

The Super Trofeo is a series of Motorsport events held by Squadra corse using its Super Trofeo model vehicles (currently the [Huracán Super Trofeo EVO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Hurac%C3%A1n#Hurac%C3%A1n_LP_620-2_Super_Trofeo_EVO2_\(2022%E2%80%93present\))) which are racing versions of the road-approved models ([Huracán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Hurac%C3%A1n) and [Gallardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Gallardo) models).

The Super Trofeo events are held in three different series, in three continents: America, Asia and Europe. Many private race teams participate in each of these events.

Every series consists of six rounds, each of which feature free practice sessions, qualifying and two races lasting 50 minutes each. There are four categories of drivers: Pro, Pro-Am, Am and Lamborghini Cup. The season ends in the Lamborghini Super Trofeo World Final.[^cite_note-18]

#### Lamborghini GT3



Lamborghini Huracán GT3 front view

The Lamborghini GT3 is a series of Motorsport events held by The Squadra Corse using [Huracán GT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Hurac%C3%A1n) cars that comply with the [FIA GT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_GT3) regulations. The racing event is open to any Huracán GT3 customer.

Lamborghini currently uses [Huracán GT3 Evo 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Hurac%C3%A1n#Hurac%C3%A1n_GT3_Evo_2_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)) cars for these events and more than 60 private race teams participate in these events.[^cite_note-19]

The Miura began as a clandestine prototype, a car that had racing potential in a company that was entirely against motorsport.

In contrast to his rival [Enzo Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Ferrari), Ferruccio Lamborghini had decided early on that there would be no factory-supported racing of Lamborghinis, viewing motorsport as too expensive and too draining on company resources.[^cite_note-23] This was unusual for the time, as many sports car manufacturers sought to demonstrate speed, reliability, and technical superiority through motorsport participation. Enzo Ferrari in particular was known for considering his road car business mostly a source of funding for his participation in motor racing. Ferruccio's policy led to tensions between him and his engineers, many of whom were racing enthusiasts; some had previously worked at Ferrari. When Dallara, Stanzani, and Wallace began dedicating their spare time to the development of the P400 prototype, they designed it to be a road car with racing potential, one that could win on the track and also be driven on the road by enthusiasts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJolliffeWillard200429-24] When Ferruccio discovered the project, he allowed them to go ahead, seeing it as a potential marketing device for the company, while insisting that it would not be raced. The P400 went on to become the Miura. The closest the company came to building a true race car under Lamborghini's supervision were a few highly modified prototypes, including those built by factory test driver [Bob Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Wallace_\(test_driver\)), such as the Miura SV-based "Jota" and the Jarama S-based "Bob Wallace Special".

In the mid-1970s, while Lamborghini was under the management of Georges-Henri Rossetti, Lamborghini entered into an agreement with [BMW](3772.md) to develop, then manufacture 400 cars for BMW in order to meet [Group 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_4_\(racing\)) [homologation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homologation_\(motorsport\)) requirements. BMW lacked experience developing a mid-engined vehicle and believed that Lamborghini's experience in that area would make Lamborghini an ideal choice of partner. Due to Lamborghini's shaky finances, Lamborghini fell behind schedule developing the car's structure and running gear. When Lamborghini failed to deliver working prototypes on time, BMW took the program in house, finishing development without Lamborghini. BMW contracted with [Baur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karosserie_Baur) to produce the car, which BMW named the [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_M1), delivering the first vehicle in October 1978.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewin2004119–120-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMitchel2005219-26]

In 1985, Lamborghini's British importer developed the [Countach QVX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Countach_QVX), in conjunction with [Spice Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_Engineering), for the 1986 [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_C) championship season. One car was built, but lack of sponsorship caused it to miss the season. The QVX competed in only one race, the non-championship 1986 Southern Suns 500 km race at [Kyalami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyalami) in South Africa, driven by [Tiff Needell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiff_Needell). Despite the car finishing better than it started, sponsorship could once again not be found and the programme was cancelled.[^cite_note-27]



The 1990 [Lotus 102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_102) featured a Lamborghini V12 engine.

Lamborghini was an engine supplier in [Formula One](10854.md) for the [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Formula_One_season) through [1993 Formula One seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Formula_One_season). It supplied engines to [Larrousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larrousse) (1989–1990, 1992–1993), [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Lotus) (1990), [Ligier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89quipe_Ligier) (1991), [Minardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minardi) (1992), and to the [Modena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modena_\(racing_team\)) team in 1991. While the latter is commonly referred to as a factory team, the company saw itself as a supplier, not a backer. The 1992 Larrousse—Lamborghini was largely uncompetitive but noteworthy in its tendency to spew oil from its exhaust system. Cars following closely behind the Larrousse were commonly coloured yellowish-brown by the end of the race.[^cite_note-28] Lamborghini's best result was achieved with Larrousse at the [1990 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Japanese_Grand_Prix), when [Aguri Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguri_Suzuki) finished third on home soil.[^cite_note-29]

In 1991, a Lamborghini Formula One motor was used in the [Konrad KM-011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_KM-011) [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_C) sports car, but the car only lasted a few races before the project was cancelled. The same engine, re-badged a [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler), Lamborghini's then-parent company, was tested by [McLaren](20994.md) towards the end of the 1993 season, with the intent of using it during the [1994 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Formula_One_season). Although driver [Ayrton Senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) was reportedly impressed with the engine's performance, McLaren pulled out of negotiations, choosing a [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot) engine instead, and Chrysler ended the project.



A Murciélago R-GT participating in the FIA GT Championship at [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverstone_Circuit) in 2006

Two racing versions of the Diablo were built for the Diablo Supertrophy, a single-model racing series held annually from 1996 to 1999. In the first year, the model used in the series was the Diablo SVR, while the Diablo 6.0 GTR was used for the remaining three years.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Lamborghini developed the Murciélago R-GT as a production racing car to compete in the [FIA GT Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_GT_Championship), the [Super GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_GT) Championship and the [American Le Mans Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Le_Mans_Series) in 2004. The car's highest placing in any race that year was the opening round of the FIA GT Championship at [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Valencia), where the car entered by [Reiter Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiter_Engineering) finished third from a fifth-place start.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] In 2006, during the opening round of the Super GT championship at [Suzuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_Circuit), a car run by the Japan Lamborghini Owners Club garnered the first victory (in class) by an R-GT. A [GT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_GT3_European_Championship) version of the Gallardo has been developed by [Reiter Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiter_Engineering).[^cite_note-34] A Murciélago R-GT entered by All-Inkl.com racing, driven by [Christophe Bouchut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Bouchut) and Stefan Mücke, won the opening round of the [FIA GT Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_GT_Championship) held at [Zhuhai International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai_International_Circuit), achieving the first major international race victory for Lamborghini.[^cite_note-35]

## Marketing

### Brand identity



The Lamborghini wordmark, as displayed on the back of its cars

The world of [bullfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfighting) is a key part of Lamborghini's identity.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] In 1962, Ferruccio Lamborghini visited the Seville ranch of [Don Eduardo Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_bull), a renowned breeder of [Spanish fighting bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Fighting_Bull). Lamborghini was so impressed by the majestic Miura animals that he decided to adopt a raging [bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovinae) as the [emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem) for the automaker he would open shortly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESackey200815-39]

### Branded Residences

Lamborghini is diversifying its brand identity into Branded Residences. Tierra Viva By Lamborghini[^cite_note-40] is the new luxury branded residences project inspired by Automobili Lamborghini. It is located in Spain´s Costa del Sol and is expected to be completed in June 2027.

### Vehicle nomenclature

After producing two cars with alphanumeric designations, Lamborghini once again turned to the bull breeder for inspiration. Don Eduardo was filled with pride when he learned that Ferruccio had named a car for his family and their line of bulls; the fourth Miura to be produced was unveiled to him at his ranch in Seville.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESackey200815-39][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJolliffeWillard200431-41]

The automaker would continue to draw upon the bullfighting connection in future years. The [Islero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Islero) was named for the [Miura bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islero) that killed the famed [bullfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torero_\(bullfighter\)) [Manolete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolete) in 1947. *Espada* is the Spanish word for [sword](27863.md), sometimes used to refer to the bullfighter himself. The Jarama's name carried a special double meaning; though it was intended to refer only to the historic bullfighting region in Spain, Ferruccio was concerned about confusion with the also historic [Jarama motor racing track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuito_Permanente_Del_Jarama).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJolliffeWillard200443-42]



The Diablo (background) was named for a legendary bull, while the Countach (foreground) broke from the bullfighting tradition.

After christening the [Urraco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Urraco) after a bull breed, in 1974, Lamborghini broke from tradition, naming the [Countach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Countach) ([/ˈkuːntɑːʃ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/73/Ipa-countach.ogg/Ipa-countach.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/73/Ipa-countach.ogg/Ipa-countach.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*KOON-tahsh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) not for a bull,[^cite_note-lamboregLP500-43] but for *contacc* (pronounced [\[kʊŋˈtɑtʃ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Piedmontese)), a [Piedmontese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmontese_language) expletive.[^cite_note-lamboregLP500-43] Legend has it that [Nuccio Bertone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuccio_Bertone) uttered the word in surprise when he first saw the Countach prototype, "Project 112".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELawrence1996183-44] The LM002 (LM for Lamborghini Militaire) sport utility vehicle and the Silhouette (named after the popular racing category of the time) were other exceptions to the tradition.

The Jalpa of 1982 was named for a bull breed; Diablo, for the [Duke of Veragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Veragua)'s ferocious bull famous for fighting an epic battle against El Chicorro in Madrid in 1869;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJolliffeWillard200490-45][^cite_note-lambocarsdia-46][^cite_note-47] [Murciélago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murci%C3%A9lago), the legendary bull whose life was spared by El Lagartijo for his performance in 1879; Gallardo, named for one of the five ancestral castes of the Spanish fighting bull breed;[^cite_note-48] and Reventón, the bull that defeated young Mexican *torero* Félix Guzmán in 1943. The [Estoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estoque) concept of 2008 was named for the [estoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estoc), the sword traditionally used by matadors during bullfights.[^cite_note-EdmundsFirstLook-49]

### Concept vehicles

See also: [List of Lamborghini concept vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lamborghini_concept_vehicles)

Throughout its history, Lamborghini has envisioned and presented a variety of [concept cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_cars), beginning in 1963 with the first Lamborghini prototype, the [350GTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_350GTV). Other famous models include Bertone's 1967 [Marzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Marzal), 1974 [Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Bravo), and 1980 [Athon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Athon), Chrysler's 1987 [Portofino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Portofino), the [Italdesign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italdesign)-styled [Cala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Cala) from 1995, the [Zagato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagato)-built [Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagato_Raptor) from 1996.

A retro-styled [Lamborghini Miura concept car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Miura_concept), the first creation of chief designer Walter de'Silva, was presented in 2006. President and CEO Stephan Winkelmann denied that the concept would be put into production, saying that the Miura concept was "a celebration of our history, but Lamborghini is about the future. Retro design is not what we are here for. So we won't do the \[new] Miura."[^cite_note-50]



The [Estoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estoque), a 2008 sedan concept

At the [2008 Paris Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Paris_Motor_Show), Lamborghini revealed the [Estoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Estoque), a four-door [sedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedan_\(automobile\)) concept. Although there had been much speculation regarding the Estoque's eventual production,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Lamborghini management has not made a decision regarding production of what might be the first four-door car to roll out of the Sant'Agata factory.[^cite_note-53]



The [Concept S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Gallardo#Lamborghini_Concept_S), a Gallardo derivative

At the [2010 Paris Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Paris_Motor_Show), Lamborghini unveiled the [Sesto Elemento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Sesto_Elemento). The concept car is made almost entirely of carbon fibre making it extremely light, with a weight of 999 kg (2,202 lb). The Sesto Elemento shares the same V10 engine found in the Lamborghini Gallardo. Lamborghini hopes to signal a shift in the company's direction from making super cars focused on top speed to producing more agile, track focused cars with the Sesto Elemento. The concept car can reach 0–62 mph (0–100 km/h) in 2.5 seconds and can reach a top speed of over 180 mph (290 km/h).[^cite_note-54]

At the 2012 Geneva Motor Show, Lamborghini unveiled the Aventador J—a roofless, windowless version of the [Lamborghini Aventador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Aventador). The Aventador J uses the same 700 hp engine and seven-speed transmission as the standard Aventador.[^cite_note-55]

At the 2012 Beijing Motor Show, Lamborghini unveiled the [Urus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Urus) SUV. This is the first SUV built by Lamborghini since the LM002.

As part of the celebration of 50 years of Lamborghini, the company created the [Egoista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Egoista). [Egoista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Egoista) is for one person's driving and only one Egoista is to be made.[^cite_note-56]

At the [2014 Paris Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Paris_Motor_Show), Lamborghini unveiled the Asterion LPI910-4 hybrid concept car. Named after the half-man, half-bull hybrid ([Minotaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minotaur)) of Greek legend, it is the first hybrid Lamborghini in the history of the company. Utilizing the Huracán's 5.2 litre V10 producing 607 hp (453 kW; 615 PS), along with one electric motor mounted on the transaxle and an additional two on the front axle, developing an additional 300 hp (224 kW; 304 PS). This puts the power at a combined figure of 907 hp (676 kW; 920 PS). The 0–100 km/h (0–62 mph) time is claimed to be just above 3 seconds, with a claimed top speed of 185 mph (298 km/h).[^cite_note-57]

## Corporate affairs

### Structure

As of 2011, Lamborghini is structured as a wholly owned subsidiary of [Audi AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi) named Automobili Lamborghini S.p.A.[^cite_note-SpA-58][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAudi_AG2011a62-59]

Automobili Lamborghini S.p.A. controls five principal subsidiaries: [Ducati Motor Holding S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati), a manufacturer of motorcycles; [Italdesign Giugiaro S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italdesign_Giugiaro), a design and prototyping firm that provides services to the entire Volkswagen Group; MML S.p.A. (Motori Marini Lamborghini), a manufacturer of marine engine blocks; and Volkswagen Group Italia S.p.A. (formerly Autogerma S.p.A.), which sells Audi and other Volkswagen Group vehicles in Italy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAudi_AG2011a62-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAudi_AG2012b24-60]

The Lamborghini headquarters and main production site is located in [Sant'Agata Bolognese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Agata_Bolognese), Italy. With the launch of its Urus [SUV](47696.md), the production site expanded from 80,000 m<sup>2</sup> (8.0 ha) to 160,000 m<sup>2</sup> (16 ha).[^cite_note-61]

On 13 November 2020, [Stephan Winkelmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephan_Winkelmann), current President of [Bugatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugatti), was appointed to be the new [CEO](52234.md) of Lamborghini. He took his new position on 1 December 2020.[^cite_note-luxus-plus.com-1]

## Licensing

### Automóviles Lamborghini Latinoamérica

Automóviles Lamborghini Latinoamérica [S.A. de C.V.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.A._\(corporation\)) (Lamborghini Automobiles of Latin America Public Limited Company) is an authorized distributor and manufacturer of Lamborghini-branded vehicles and merchandise in Latin America and South America.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAutomóviles_Lamborghini_Latinoamérica_S.A._de_C.V1995-97]

In 1995, Indonesian corporation MegaTech, Lamborghini's owner at the time, entered into distribution and license agreements with Mexican businessman Jorge Antonio Fernández García. The agreements give Automóviles Lamborghini Latinoamérica S.A. de C.V. the exclusive distributorship of Lamborghini vehicles and branded merchandise in Latin America and South America. Under the agreements, Automóviles Lamborghini is also allowed to manufacture Lamborghini vehicles and market them worldwide under the Lamborghini brand.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAutomóviles_Lamborghini_Latinoamérica_S.A._de_C.V1995-97]

Automóviles Lamborghini has produced two rebodied versions of the [Diablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Diablo) called the Eros and the Coatl. In 2015, Automóviles Lamborghini transferred the IP-rights to the Coatl foundation (chamber of commerce no. 63393700) in The Netherlands in order to secure these rights and to make them more marketable.[^cite_note-98] The company has announced the production of a speedboat called the Lamborghini Glamour.[^cite_note-99]

## Museums

There are two museums in [Bologna](21069333.md), Emilia-Romagna, centered around the brand.

### Museo Lamborghini

Main article: [Museo Lamborghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Lamborghini)



Museo Lamborghini

This two-storey museum is attached to the headquarters, and covers the history of Lamborghini cars and sport utility vehicles, showcasing a variety of modern and vintage models. The museum uses displays of cars, engines and photos to provide a history and review important milestones of Lamborghini.

### Museo Ferruccio Lamborghini

Main article: [Museo Ferruccio Lamborghini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Ferruccio_Lamborghini)

A 9,000 square-foot museum about Ferruccio Lamborghini houses several cars, industrial prototypes, sketches, personal objects and family photos from Ferruccio's early life.[^cite_note-100]
