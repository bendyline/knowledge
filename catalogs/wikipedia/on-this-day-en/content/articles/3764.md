**Bavaria**,[^cite_note-5] officially the **Free State of Bavaria**,[^cite_note-7] is a landlocked [state of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Germany). It borders the states of [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) to the west, [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) to the north-west, and [Thuringia](31130.md) and [Saxony](28395.md) to the north, as well as the countries of [Switzerland](26748.md) and [Austria](26964606.md) to the south and the [Czech Republic](5321.md) to the east. The state's capital and largest city is [Munich](19058.md); [other major cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Bavaria_by_population) include [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg), [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg), [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg), and [Ingolstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingolstadt). With an area of 70,550 square kilometres (27,249 sq mi), it is the [largest German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_area), and with 13.08 million inhabitants, the [second-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_population).[^cite_note-8]

The [history of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bavaria) begins with the settlement of [Celtic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) during the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). The territory was then ruled by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and incorporated into the [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia) and [Noricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noricum) in the first century AD.[^cite_note-\:3-9] Following the [collapse of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Roman_Empire), the [Duchy of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria) was created in [Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francia) in 555 AD. It later became part of [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia) and controlled territory in modern-day Austria, [Slovenia](27338.md) and [Italy](14532.md). Within the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) it gained [elector status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Bavaria) in 1623 during the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), eventually becoming the [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) in 1806.[^cite_note-\:3-9] Following the [Unification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany), Bavaria remained a kingdom within the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) until the end of [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-10] The modern state of Bavaria was established in 1945 and became part of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in 1949.[^cite_note-11]

The [culture of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Bavaria) is heavily influenced by its [Catholic heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Germany).[^cite_note-12] The [Bavarian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) is a group of [German dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_dialects) in both Bavaria and Austria with around 12 million speakers, making it the largest German dialect.[^cite_note-13] Other major cultural influences of Bavaria include [its cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_cuisine), consisting of foods such as [Weißwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei%C3%9Fwurst) and [Leberkäse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leberk%C3%A4se), its architecture, largely focused on [Alpine symbolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_chalet_style) in southern [Alpine](981.md) regions, and its major festivals such as the [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest) in Munich.[^cite_note-14]

Modern Bavaria's borders consist of [Altbayern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altbayern) as well as parts of the historical regions of [Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia) and [Swabia](616237.md). There are 7 [administrative regions of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Bavaria), 3 of which are in Franconia and Altbayern and 1 of which is in Swabia. Bavaria has a tradition of conservatism; the [CSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_in_Bavaria) is the largest [party in Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Bavaria) and is known as the 'sister party' of the national [CDU](60584.md), being collectively known as [the Union](10796483.md).[^cite_note-15]

## History

Main article: [History of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bavaria)

### Antiquity

Though Bavaria has been occupied by humans since the Paleolithic era, [Celtic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) of the Bronze Age, such as the [Boii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boii_\(Bohemia\)) were the first documented inhabitants of the [Bavarian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Alps). In June 2023, archeologists discovered a bronze sword, dated to the [14th century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_century_BC), in a former Celtic village; its workmanship so well-preserved "it almost shines."[^cite_note-16] During the early modern era, these peoples were retrospectively romanticized as the most ancient culture of Bavaria,[^cite_note-17] even though the [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages) were relative newcomers to the region. Evidence of the ancient [Straubing culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straubing_culture), [Únětice culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9An%C4%9Btice_culture) and [La Tène culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_culture) may be found in what is Bavaria today.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-\:1-20]

Archeologists know of a large Celtic [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) settlement which was founded in [Feldmoching-Hasenbergl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldmoching-Hasenbergl) north of suburban Munich.[^cite_note-\:1-20] Evidence suggests up to 500 people lived in the village from 450 BC.[^cite_note-\:1-20] Local life appears to have centred around what could be a town hall or temple, and continued in different forms up to 1000 AD.[^cite_note-\:1-20] In [Manching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manching), Upper Bavaria, an unfortified and semi-urban society appears to have prospered between the [3rd century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_century_BC) until the early [1st century AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_century_AD).[^cite_note-\:0-21] The settlement featured food ovens, pottery kilns and metallurgical furnaces.[^cite_note-\:0-21] By 200 BC the community there was active in trade—finds of coins, along with an icon-like golden tree suggest it was trading with distant Italo-Greek communities.[^cite_note-\:0-21]

In the 1st century BC, Bavaria was conquered by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-22] An imperial military camp was built 60 km north-west of where Munich sits today, under orders of [Augustus Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus), between 8 and 5 BC.[^cite_note-\:2-23] The camp later became the town of [Augusta Vindelicorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Vindelicorum), which would become the capital of the [Roman province of Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia).[^cite_note-\:2-23] Another fort was founded in 60 AD, west of modern-day Manching, as evidenced by a legionnaire's sandal found near remains of an ancient fort.[^cite_note-24]

### Middle Ages

In the 530s, the [Merovingian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty) incorporated the kingdom of [Thuringia](31130.md) after their defeat by the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks). The [Baiuvarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiuvarii) were Frankicised a century later.[^cite_note-25] The [Lex Thuringorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Thuringorum) documents an upper class nobility of *adalingi*.[^cite_note-26] From about 554 to 788, the house of [Agilolfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agilolfing) ruled the [Duchy of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria), ending with [Tassilo III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tassilo_III) who was deposed by [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne).[^cite_note-27]

[Tassilo I of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tassilo_I_of_Bavaria) tried unsuccessfully to hold the eastern frontier against the expansion of [Slavic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples) and the [Pannonian Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars) around 600. [Garibald II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garibald_II_of_Bavaria) seems to have achieved a balance of power between 610 and 616.[^cite_note-28]

At Hugbert's death in 735, the [duchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy) passed to [Odilo of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odilo_of_Bavaria) from the neighboring [Alemannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannia). Odilo issued a [Lex Baiuvariorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Baiuvariorum) for Bavaria, completed the process of church organization in partnership with [Saint Boniface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Boniface) in 739, and tried to intervene in Frankish succession disputes by fighting for the claims of the [Carolingian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty). He was defeated near [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg) in 743 but continued to rule until his death in 748.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

[Saint Boniface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Boniface) completed the people's conversion to Christianity in the early 8th century. [Tassilo III of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tassilo_III_of_Bavaria) succeeded to rule Bavaria. He initially ruled under Frankish oversight but began to function independently from 763 onward. He was particularly noted for founding new monasteries and for expanding eastwards, oppressing [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) in the [eastern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Alps) and along the [Danube](21209639.md) and colonizing these lands. After 781, however, Charlemagne began to exert pressure and Tassilo III was deposed in 788. Dissenters attempted a coup against [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) at [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg) in 792, led by [Pepin the Hunchback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_the_Hunchback).



A map of Bavaria in the 10th century

With the revolt of [Henry II, Duke of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II,_Duke_of_Bavaria) in 976, Bavaria lost large territories in the south and southeast.

One of the most important dukes of Bavaria was [Henry the Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Lion) of the [house of Welf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Welf), founder of [Munich](19058.md), and *de facto* the second most powerful man in the empire as the ruler of two duchies. When in 1180, Henry the Lion was deposed as Duke of [Saxony](28395.md) and Bavaria by his cousin, [Frederick I, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) (a.k.a. "Barbarossa" for his red beard), Bavaria was awarded as [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) to the [Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittelsbach) family, counts palatinate of Schyren ("Scheyern" in modern German). They ruled for 738 years, from 1180 to 1918. In 1180, however, [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria) was also separated from Bavaria. The [Electorate of the Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_the_Palatinate) by Rhine (*Kurpfalz* in German) was also acquired by the [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach) in 1214, which they would subsequently hold for six centuries.[^cite_note-31]

The first of several divisions of the duchy of Bavaria occurred in 1255. With the extinction of the [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenstaufen) in 1268, [Swabian](616237.md) territories were acquired by the Wittelsbach dukes. [Emperor Louis the Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) acquired [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg), [Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Tyrol), [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Holland) and [Hainaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Hainaut) for his House but released the [Upper Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Palatinate) for the Palatinate branch of the Wittelsbach in 1329. That time also [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg_\(state\)) finally became independent from the [Duchy of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria).

In the 14th and 15th centuries, upper and lower Bavaria were repeatedly subdivided. Four Duchies existed after the division of 1392: [Bavaria-Straubing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria-Straubing), [Bavaria-Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria-Landshut), [Bavaria-Ingolstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria-Ingolstadt) and [Bavaria-Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria-Munich). In 1506 with the [Landshut War of Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landshut_War_of_Succession), the other parts of Bavaria were reunited, and Munich became the sole capital. The country became a center of the Jesuit-inspired [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation).

### Electorate of Bavaria

Main article: [Electorate of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Bavaria)

In 1623, early in the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), the Bavarian duke replaced his relative the [Elector Palatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_the_Palatinate) among the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire)'s powerful [prince-elector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-elector), gaining both a vote in determining the Emperor thenceforth and special legal status under the Empire's laws. During the early and mid-18th century, the ambitions of the Bavarian prince-electors led to several wars with Austria as well as to occupations by Austria during the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) and the [War of the Austrian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession); on one occasion, the Bavarian House of Wittelsbach secured the election of one of its own members rather than a Habsburg as emperor.[^cite_note-32]

To mark the unification of Bavaria and the [Electoral Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Palatinate), both being principal Wittelsbach territories, Elector [Maximilian IV Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_IV_Joseph) was crowned king of Bavaria. King Maximilian Joseph was quick to change the coat of arms. The various heraldic symbols were replaced and a classical Wittelsbach pattern introduced. The white and blue [lozenges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozenge_\(heraldry\)) symbolized the unity of the territories within the Bavarian kingdom.[^cite_note-33]

The new state also comprised the [Duchy of Jülich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_J%C3%BClich) and [Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_\(state\)) as these on their part were in personal union with the Palatinate.[^cite_note-34]

### Kingdom of Bavaria

Main article: [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria)



A map of Bavaria in the 19th century

When the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) dissolved under [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon)'s onslaught, Bavaria became a [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) in 1806 and joined the [Confederation of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Rhine).[^cite_note-35]

The Duchy of Jülich was ceded to France and the Electoral Palatinate was divided between France and the [Grand Duchy of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden). The Duchy of Berg was given to [Joachim Murat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Murat). The [County of Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Tyrol) and the federal state of [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg_\(state\)) were temporarily annexed with Bavaria but eventually ceded to Austria at the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna). In return, Bavaria was allowed to annex the modern-day region of [Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatinate_\(region\)) to the west of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) and [Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia) in 1815.

Between 1799 and 1817, the leading minister, Count [Montgelas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_Joseph_von_Montgelas), followed a strict policy of modernization copying Napoleonic France; he laid the foundations of centralized administrative structures that survived the monarchy and, in part, have retained core validity through to the 21st century. In May 1808, a first constitution was passed by [Maximilian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I_Joseph_of_Bavaria),[^cite_note-36] being modernized in 1818. This second version established a bicameral Parliament with a House of Lords (*Kammer der Reichsräte*) and a House of Commons (*Kammer der Abgeordneten*). That constitution was followed until the collapse of the monarchy at the end of [World War I](4764461.md).

After the rise of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) in the early 18th century, Bavaria preserved its independence by playing off the rivalry of Prussia and [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire). Allied to Austria, it was defeated along with Austria in the 1866 [Austro-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War) and was not incorporated into the [North German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_German_Federation) of 1867, but the question of [German unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany) was still alive. When [France declared war on Prussia in 1870](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War), all the south German states (Baden, Württemberg, Hessen-Darmstadt and Bavaria) aside from Austria joined the Prussian forces and ultimately joined the Federation, which was renamed *Deutsches Reich* ([German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire)) in 1871.

Bavaria continued as a monarchy, and retained some special rights within the federation (such as railways and postal services and control of its army in peace times).

### Part of the German Empire



A map of Bavaria within the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), which was formed in 1871 and ended in 1918

When Bavaria became part of the newly formed [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), this action was considered controversial by [Bavarian nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_nationalism) who had wanted to retain independence from the rest of Germany, as had Austria.

As Bavaria had a heavily [Catholic](606848.md) majority population, many people resented being ruled by the mostly [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Germany) northerners in [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia). As a direct result of the Bavarian-Prussian feud, political parties formed to encourage Bavaria to break away and regain its independence.[^cite_note-Minahan2000-37]

In the early 20th century, [Wassily Kandinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassily_Kandinsky), [Paul Klee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Klee), [Henrik Ibsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen), and other artists were drawn to Bavaria, especially to the [Schwabing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwabing) district in Munich, a center of international artistic activity at the time.

### Free State of Bavaria



A memorial to soldiers who died in [World War I](4764461.md) and [World War II](32927.md) in [Kröning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%B6ning), Bavaria

[World War I](4764461.md) led to the abolition of the monarchy all over Germany in 1918. The Bavarian monarchy was the first to fall when on 8 November 1918 Socialist politician [Kurt Eisner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Eisner) proclaimed the [People's State of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_State_of_Bavaria). Eisner headed a new, republican government as minister-president. On 12 November, King [Ludwig III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_III_of_Bavaria) signed the [Anif declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anif_declaration), releasing both civil and military officers from their oaths,[^cite_note-38] which the Eisner government interpreted as an abdication.[^cite_note-39]

After losing the [January 1919 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Landtag_elections_in_the_Weimar_Republic), Eisner was assassinated in February 1919, ultimately leading to a Communist revolt and the short-lived [Bavarian Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Soviet_Republic) being proclaimed 6 April 1919. After violent suppression by elements of the German Army and notably the [Freikorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freikorps), the Bavarian Soviet Republic fell in May 1919. The [Bamberg Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bavaria) (*_Bamberger Verfassung_*) was enacted on 12 or 14 August 1919 and came into force on 15 September 1919, placing the [Free State of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Bavaria_\(Weimar_Republic\)) inside the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic).

Extremist activity further increased, notably the 1923 [Beer Hall Putsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch) led by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSDAP), and Munich and Nuremberg became seen as strongholds of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) during the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) and [Nazi dictatorship](21212.md). In the crucial [German federal election, March 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_federal_election,_March_1933), the Nazis received around 43% of the votes cast in Bavaria.

As a manufacturing centre, Munich was heavily bombed during [World War II](32927.md) and was occupied by [United States Armed Forces](32212.md), becoming a major part of the American Zone of [Allied-occupied Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany), which lasted from 1945 to 1947, and then of [Bizone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bizone).

The Rhenish Palatinate was detached from Bavaria in 1946 and made part of the new state [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md). In 1949, Bavaria became part of the [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany), despite the [Bavarian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Bavaria) voting against adopting the [Basic Law of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_of_Germany), mainly because it was seen as not granting sufficient powers to the individual states (*Länder*), but at the same time declared that it would accept it if two-thirds of the other *Länder* ratified it. All of the other states ratified it, so it became law.[^cite_note-40] Thus, during the [Cold War](325329.md), Bavaria was part of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany).

### Bavarian identity

Bavarians have often emphasized a separate national identity and considered themselves as "Bavarians" first, "Germans" second.[^cite_note-41] In the 19th-century sense, an independent [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) existed from only 1806 to 1871. A separate Bavarian identity was emphasized more strongly when Bavaria joined the Prussia-dominated [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1871, while the [Bavarian nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_nationalism) wanted to keep Bavaria as Catholic and an independent state. Aside from the minority [Bavaria Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria_Party), most Bavarians now accept Bavaria as part of Germany.[^cite_note-42]

Another consideration is that Bavaria is not culturally uniform. While inhabitants of [Altbayern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altbayern) ("Old Bavaria"), the regions forming the historic Bavaria before further acquisitions in 1806–1815, speak a Bavarian dialect of German, [Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia) in the north and [Bavarian Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabia_\(Bavaria\)) in the southwest have a distinct culture including different dialects of German, [East Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Franconian_German) and [Swabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_German), respectively.

## Flags and coat of arms

### Flags

Main article: [Flag of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Bavaria)

Uniquely among German states, Bavaria has two official flags of equal status, one with a white and blue stripe, the other with white and blue diamond-shaped [lozenges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozenge_\(heraldry\)).[^cite_note-43] Either may be used by civilians and government offices, who are free to choose between them.[^cite_note-fotw_54-44] Unofficial versions of the flag, especially a lozenge style with coat of arms, are sometimes used by civilians.

### Coat of arms

Main article: [Coat of arms of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Bavaria)

Bavaria has two official designs for its coat of arms; they are referred to as the "greater" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *das große*) coat of arms and the "lesser" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *das kleine*) coat of arms. Both were formally adopted by the Bavarian State Parliament on 5 June 1950.[^cite_note-45]

Munich artist [Eduard Ege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Ege?action=edit\&redlink=1) designed the greater Bavarian coat of arms in 1946. He primarily based his design on the royal coat of arms of [Ludwig I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_I_of_Bavaria). His design includes several heraldic elements of historical significance for Bavaria.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

- The Golden Lion: At the dexter chief, sable, a [lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_\(heraldry\)) rampant Or, armed and langued gules. This represents the administrative region of Upper Palatinate.

- The "[Franconian Rake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconian_Rake)": At the sinister chief, per [fess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fess) dancetty, gules, and argent. This represents the administrative regions of Upper, Middle and Lower Franconia.

- The Blue "Pantier" (mythical creature from [French heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_heraldry), sporting a flame instead of a tongue): At the dexter base, argent, a Pantier rampant azure, armed Or and langued gules. This represents the regions of Lower and Upper Bavaria.

- The Three Lions: At the sinister base, Or, three lions passant guardant sable, armed and langued gules. This represents Swabia.

- The White-And-Blue inescutcheon: The [inescutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escutcheon_\(heraldry\)) of white and blue fusils askance was originally the coat of arms of the Counts of Bogen, adopted in 1247 by the House of Wittelsbach. The white-and-blue fusils are indisputably the emblem of Bavaria and these arms today symbolize Bavaria as a whole. Along with the People's Crown, it is officially used as the Minor Coat of Arms.

- The People's Crown (*Volkskrone*): The coat of arms is surmounted by a [crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_\(heraldry\)) with a golden band inset with precious stones and decorated with five ornamental leaves. This crown first appeared in the coat of arms to symbolize sovereignty of [the people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_people) after the royal crown was eschewed in 1923.

## Geography



The [Bavarian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Alps) (foreground) and [Tyrol](148994.md) in [Austria](26964606.md) (background), including the [Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inn_\(river\)) valley (center), [Kaisergebirge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Mountains) (left), [Pendling](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendling) (right), and the snow-capped [High Tauern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Tauern) (center left)

Bavaria shares international borders with [Austria](26964606.md) ([Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg_\(state\)), [Tyrol](148994.md), [Upper Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria) and [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg)) and the [Czech Republic](5321.md) ([Karlovy Vary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovy_Vary_Region), [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plze%C5%88_Region) and [South Bohemian Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bohemian_Region)), as well as with [Switzerland](26748.md) (across [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance) to the [Canton of St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_St._Gallen)).

Neighboring states within Germany are [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md), [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse), [Thuringia](31130.md), and [Saxony](28395.md). Two major rivers flow through the state: the [Danube](21209639.md) (*Donau*) and the [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)). The [Bavarian Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Forest) and the [Bohemian Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Forest) form the vast majority of the frontier with the Czech Republic and Bohemia.

The [geographic center of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_midpoint_of_Europe#Geographic_centre_of_the_European_Union) is located in the northwestern corner of Bavaria.[^cite_note-48]

### Climate

At lower elevations the climate is classified according to [Köppen's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) guide as "[Cfb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate)" or "[Dfb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate)". At higher altitudes the climate becomes "[Dfc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate)" and "[ET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_climate)".

The summer months have been getting hotter in recent years.[^cite_note-suedd-4539824-49] For example, June 2019 was the warmest June in Bavaria since weather observations have been recorded[^cite_note-suedd-4539824-49] and the winter 2019/2020 was 3 degrees Celsius warmer than the average temperature for many years all over Bavaria. On 20 December 2019 a record temperature of 20.2 °C (68.4 °F) was recorded in [Piding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piding).[^cite_note-50] In general winter months are seeing more precipitation which is taking the form of rain more often than that of snow compared to the past.[^cite_note-suedd-4539824-49] [Extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) like the [2013 European floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_European_floods) or the [2019 European heavy snowfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_heavy_snowfalls?action=edit\&redlink=1) is occurring more and more often. One effect of the continuing warming is the melting of almost all Bavarian [Alpine glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_glaciers): Of the five glaciers of Bavaria only the [Höllentalferner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6llentalferner) is predicted to exist over a longer time perspective. The [Südliche Schneeferner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schneeferner#S%C3%BCdlicher_Schneeferner) has almost vanished since the 1980s.[^cite_note-suedd-4539824-49]

### Bezirke

*_Bezirke_* (regional districts) are the third communal layer in Bavaria; the others are the *_Landkreise_* and the *_Gemeinden_* or *_Städte_*. The *_Bezirke_* in Bavaria are territorially identical with the *_Regierungsbezirke_*, but they are self-governing regional corporation, having their own parliaments. In the other larger states of Germany, there are only *_Regierungsbezirke_* as administrative divisions and no self-governing entities at the level of the *_Regierungsbezirke_* as the *_Bezirke_* in Bavaria.

### Municipalities

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Bavaria)



The [Bavarian State Chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayerische_Staatskanzlei) in [Munich](19058.md)

Bavaria has a multiparty system dominated by the conservative [Christian Social Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_of_Bavaria) (CSU), which has won every election since 1945 with the exception of the 1950 ballot. Other important parties are the [Free Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Voters), which became the second largest party in the [2023 Bavarian state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Bavarian_state_election), [The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens), which became the second biggest political party in the [2018 Bavarian state elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bavarian_state_election), and the center-left [Social Democrats](60585.md) (SPD), who had dominated the city of [Munich](19058.md) until 2020. Hitherto, [Wilhelm Hoegner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Hoegner) has been the only SPD candidate to ever become Minister-President; notable successors in office include multi-term Federal Minister [Franz Josef Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Josef_Strauss), a key figure among [West German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_German) conservatives during the [Cold War](325329.md) years, and [Edmund Stoiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Stoiber), who both failed with their bids for [Chancellorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Chancellor_of_Germany).

The German [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens) and the center-right [Free Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Voters_of_Bavaria) have been represented in the state parliament since 1986 and 2008 respectively.

In the [2003 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria_state_election,_2003) the CSU won a [two-thirds supermajority](638313.md) – something no party had ever achieved in postwar Germany. However, in the subsequent [2008 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria_state_election,_2008) the CSU lost the absolute majority for the first time in 46 years.[^cite_note-54]

The losses were partly attributed by some to the CSU's stance for an anti-smoking bill. (A first anti-smoking law had been proposed by the CSU and passed but was watered down after the election, after which a referendum enforced a strict antismoking bill with a large majority).

### Government

See also: [Third Söder cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_S%C3%B6der_cabinet)

The [Constitution of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bavaria) of the Free State of Bavaria was enacted on 8 December 1946. The new Bavarian Constitution became the basis for the Bavarian State after the Second World War.

Bavaria has a [unicameral](204304.md) *_[Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Bavaria)_* (English: State Parliament), elected by universal suffrage.[^cite_note-57] Until December 1999, there was also a *_Senat_*, or [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Senate), whose members were chosen by social and economic groups in Bavaria, but following a referendum in 1998, this institution was abolished.[^cite_note-58]

The Bavarian State Government consists of the [Minister-President of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-President_of_Bavaria), eleven Ministers and six Secretaries of State. The Minister-President is elected for a period of five years by the State Parliament and is head of state. With the approval of the State Parliament he appoints the members of the State Government. The State Government is composed of the:

- [State Chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayerische_Staatskanzlei) (*_Staatskanzlei_*)

- Ministry of the [Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Ministry_of_the_Interior), for Sport and Integration (*_Staatsministerium des Innern, für Sport und Integration_*)

- Ministry for Housing, Construction and Transport (*_Staatsministerium für Wohnen, Bau und Verkehr_*)

- Ministry of Justice (*_Staatsministerium der Justiz_*)

- Ministry for Education and Culture (*_Staatsministerium für Bildung und Kultus_*)

- Ministry for Science and Art (*_Staatsministerium für Wissenschaft und Kunst_*)

- Ministry of Finance and for Home Affairs (*_Staatsministerium der Finanzen und für Heimat_*)

- Ministry for Economic Affairs, Regional Development and Energy (*_Staatsministerium für Wirtschaft, Landesentwicklung und Energie_*)

- Ministry for Environment and Consumer Protection (*_Staatsministerium für Umwelt und Verbraucherschutz_*)

- Ministry for Food, Agriculture and Forestry (*_Staatsministerium für Ernährung, Landwirtschaft und Forsten_*)

- Ministry for Family, Labour and Social Affairs (*_Staatsministerium für Familie, Arbeit und Soziales_*)

- Ministry for Health and Care (*_Staatsministerium für Gesundheit und Pflege_*)

- Ministry for Digital Affairs (*_Staatsministerium für Digitales_*)

  [^cite_note-59]

Political processes also take place in the seven regions (*_Regierungsbezirke_* or *_Bezirke_*) in Bavaria, in the 71 rural districts (*_Landkreise_*) and the 25 towns and cities forming their own districts (*_kreisfreie Städte_*), and in the 2,031 local authorities (*_Gemeinden_*).

In 1995 Bavaria introduced [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy) on the local level in a [referendum](51582.md). This was initiated bottom-up by an association called *Mehr Demokratie* (English: More Democracy). This is a grass-roots organization which campaigns for the right to citizen-initiated referendums. In 1997 the Bavarian Supreme Court tightened the regulations considerably (including by introducing a turn-out quorum). Nevertheless, Bavaria has the most advanced regulations on local direct democracy in Germany. This has led to a spirited citizens' participation in communal and municipal affairs—835 referendums took place from 1995 through 2005.

### Designation as a "free state"

Unlike most German states (*Länder*), which simply designate themselves as "State of" (*Land \[...]*), Bavaria uses the style of "Free State of Bavaria" (*Freistaat Bayern*). The difference from other states is purely terminological, as German constitutional law does not draw a distinction between "States" and "Free States". The situation is thus analogous to the United States, where [some states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_\(U.S._state\)) use the style "Commonwealth" rather than "State". The term "Free State", a creation of the 19th century and intended to be a German alternative to (or translation of) the Latin-derived *republic,* was common among the states of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), after German monarchies had been abolished. Unlike most other states – many of which were new creations – Bavaria has resumed this terminology after [World War II](32927.md). Two other states, [Saxony](28395.md) and [Thuringia](31130.md), also call themselves "Free States".

### Arbitrary arrest and human rights

In July 2017, Bavaria's parliament enacted a new revision of the "Gefährdergesetz", allowing the authorities to imprison a person for a three months term, renewable indefinitely, when they have not committed a crime but it is assumed that they might commit a crime "in the near future".[^cite_note-60] Critics like the prominent journalist [Heribert Prantl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heribert_Prantl) have called the law "shameful" and compared it to [Guantanamo Bay detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp),[^cite_note-61] assessed it to be in violation of the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights),[^cite_note-62] and also compared it to the legal situation in Russia, where a similar law allows for imprisonment for a maximum term of two years (i.e., not indefinitely).[^cite_note-63]

## Economy



[BMW Welt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Welt) and [BMW Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Headquarters) in [Munich](19058.md)

Bavaria has one of the largest economies in [Germany](11867.md) and the [European Union](9317.md) as a whole, with a [GDP](12594.md) of €824.243 billion (\$963.47 billion) in 2025, the [second highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_GRDP) of the 16 [German states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) behind only [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md), which had a GDP of €909.411 billion (\$1.062 trillion) in 2025.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Bavaria had a GDP per capita of €53,768 (\$56,456) in 2022, the third highest in Germany behind [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\)) (second) and [Hamburg](13467.md) (first).[^cite_note-66] One of Bavaria's largest and most developed industries is the [automotive industry](163778.md), with the state housing four [BMW](3772.md) and two [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi) manufacturing plants while also headquartering both companies. Bavaria has the second-most employees (207,829) in the automotive industry of all German states after [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) as of 2018.[^cite_note-67] Other countries such as the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Austria](26964606.md), [Switzerland](26748.md) and [Italy](14532.md) have strong economic ties with Bavaria.[^cite_note-68]



[Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas)' headquarters in [Herzogenaurach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzogenaurach)

Bavaria also is home to the headquarters of [commercial vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_vehicle) manufacturer [MAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Truck_&_Bus) and [aircraft engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_engine) manufacturer [MTU Aero Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Aero_Engines). Many other global companies such as [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas), [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), and [Allianz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz) also have headquarters in Bavarian cities and towns.[^cite_note-69] Several American companies have set up [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) facilities in Bavaria such as [Apple](856.md), [Google](1092923.md), [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel), [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments) and [Coherent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent,_Inc.).[^cite_note-70] Despite being hundreds of miles from the sea, companies such as [Bavaria Yachtbau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria_Yachtbau) produce [sailing yachts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_yacht) and [motorboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorboat).[^cite_note-71]

Bavaria is the most visited state in Germany, as over 38.86 million tourists visited Bavaria in 2023 alone, significantly higher than North Rhine-Westphalia's 23.58 million tourists.[^cite_note-72] In 2019, tourism brought in a [gross value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added) of €28.1 billion (\$28.918 billion), making up 4.9% of Bavaria's economy.[^cite_note-73] Some significant tourist destinations include the [Bavarian National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_National_Museum), [Margravial Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margravial_Opera_House), [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum), [Christmas markets](1340652.md) in [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) and [Munich](19058.md) and the annually held [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest) event, which made €1.2 billion (\$1.236 billion) in 2018 alone.[^cite_note-74]

The [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) stood at 2.9% in October 2018, the [lowest in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_unemployment_rate) and one of the lowest in the European Union.[^cite_note-75]

## Culture

Some features of the Bavarian culture and mentality are remarkably distinct from the rest of Germany. Noteworthy differences (especially in rural areas, less significant in the major cities) can be found with respect to religion, traditions, and language.

A [Catholic Church](606848.md) near [Füssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCssen) with the [Alps](981.md) in the background

Bavarian culture (*[Altbayern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altbayern)*) has a long and predominant tradition of [Roman Catholic](606848.md) faith. Pope [Benedict XVI (Joseph Alois Ratzinger)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) was born in [Marktl am Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marktl_am_Inn) in [Upper Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bavaria) and was [Cardinal-Archbishop of Munich and Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Munich_and_Freising). Otherwise, the culturally [Franconian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia) and [Swabian](616237.md) regions of the modern State of Bavaria are historically more diverse in religiosity, with both Catholic and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) traditions. In 1925, 70.0% of the Bavarian population was [Catholic](606848.md), 28.8% was Protestant, 0.7% was [Jewish](25955086.md), and 0.5% was placed in other religious categories.[^cite_note-85]

As of 2020, 46.9% of Bavarians adhered to Catholicism (a decline from 70.4% in 1970).[^cite_note-BR-86][^cite_note-ekd2020-84] 17.2 percent of the population adheres to the [Evangelical Lutheran Church in Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_Bavaria), which has also declined since 1970.[^cite_note-BR-86][^cite_note-ekd2020-84] Three percent was [Orthodox](10186.md) and [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) make up 4.0% of the population of Bavaria. 28.9 percent of Bavarians are irreligious or adhere to other religions.

### Traditions

Bavarians commonly emphasize pride in their traditions. Traditional costumes collectively known as [Tracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracht) are worn on special occasions and include in [Altbayern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altbayern) [Lederhosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lederhosen) for males and [Dirndl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirndl) for females. Centuries-old [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) is performed. The [Maibaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maypole_tradition_in_Bavaria), or Maypole (which in the Middle Ages served as the community's business directory, as figures on the pole represented the trades of the village), and the bagpipes of the Upper Palatinate region bear witness to the [ancient Celtic and Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism_in_the_Eastern_Alps) remnants of cultural heritage of the region. There are many traditional Bavarian sports disciplines, e.g. the [Aperschnalzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aperschnalzen), competitive [whipcracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whipcracking).

Whether in Bavaria, overseas or with citizens from other nations Bavarians continue to cultivate their traditions. They hold festivals and dances to keep their heritage alive.

### Food and drink

Main article: [Bavarian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_cuisine)

Bavarians tend to place a great value on food and drink. In addition to their renowned dishes, Bavarians also consume many items of food and drink which are unusual elsewhere in Germany; for example *[Weißwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei%C3%9Fwurst)* ("white sausage") or in some instances a variety of entrails.&#x20;

More than 285 typical Bavarian products have been recorded in the Bavarian specialities database '[GenussBayern](https://www.genusserbe.bayern.de/index.php)' since the 1990s. Recipes and museums can also be found there. With a total of 54 [specialities protected under European law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_indications_and_traditional_specialities_in_the_European_Union), Bavaria is the no. 1 speciality region in Germany.[^cite_note-87] Bavarian products such as 'Bavarian beer', 'Nuremberg bratwurst', 'Allgäu mountain cheese' and 'Schrobenhausen asparagus' are just as much a part of the official EU list '[eAmbrosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAmbrosia)' of prestigious regional culinary specialities as the protected names "Champagne" and 'Prosciutto di Parma'.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] Bavarian specialities, which are protected as geographical indications, are deeply rooted in their region of origin, important anchors of local identity and also tourist flagships – they are therefore at the heart of Bavarian cuisine.

Restaurants that carry the '[Ausgezeichnete GenussKüche](https://www.bayerischekueche.de/)' award (recognizable by a sign near the door) are known by locals for their certified, authentic Bavarian cuisine.[^cite_note-90]

At folk festivals and in many [beer gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_garden), beer is traditionally served by the litre (in a *[Maß](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%C3%9F)*). Bavarians are particularly proud[^cite_note-91] of the traditional *[Reinheitsgebot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinheitsgebot)*, or beer purity law, initially established by the [Duke of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Bavaria) for the City of Munich (i.e. the court) in 1487 and the duchy in 1516. According to this law, only three ingredients were allowed in beer: water, [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), and [hops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hops). In 1906 the *Reinheitsgebot* made its way to all-German law, and remained a law in Germany until the EU partly struck it down in 1987 as incompatible with the European common market.[^cite_note-92] German breweries, however, cling to the principle, and Bavarian breweries still comply with it in order to distinguish their beer brands.[^cite_note-93] Bavarians are also known as some of the world's most prolific beer drinkers, with an average annual consumption of 130–135 liters per capita, as of 2018.[^cite_note-94]

Bavaria is also home to the [Franconia wine region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia_\(wine_region\)), which is situated along the river [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)) in Franconia. The region has produced wine (*Frankenwein*) for over 1,000 years and is famous for its use of the [Bocksbeutel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocksbeutel) wine bottle. The production of wine forms an integral part of the regional culture, and many of its villages and cities hold their own wine festivals (Weinfeste) throughout the year.

### Language and dialects

A native [Bavarian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) speaker recorded in Germany



[Upper German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_German) and [Central German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_German) form the [German language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language); [Austro-Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) dialects are highlighted in blue.

Three German dialects are most commonly spoken in Bavaria: [Austro-Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Bavarian) in Old Bavaria (Upper Bavaria, Lower Bavaria, and the Upper Palatinate), [Swabian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_German) (an [Alemannic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) dialect) in the Bavarian part of Swabia (southwest) and [East Franconian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Franconian_German) in Franconia (north). In the small town [Ludwigsstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigsstadt) in the north, district Kronach in Upper Franconia, [Thuringian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringian_dialect) is spoken. During the 20th century an increasing part of the population began to speak [Standard German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German) (Hochdeutsch), mainly in the cities.

### Ethnography

Bavarians consider themselves to be [egalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism) and informal.[^cite_note-95] Their sociability can be experienced at the annual [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest), the world's largest beer festival, which welcomes around six million visitors every year, or in the famous [beer gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_garden). In traditional Bavarian beer gardens, patrons may bring their own food but buy beer only from the brewery that runs the beer garden.[^cite_note-96]

### Museums

There are around 1,300 museums in Bavaria, including museums of art and cultural history, castles and palaces, archaeological and natural history collections, museums of technological and industrial history, and farm and open-air museums. The history of Bavarian museums dates back to manorial [cabinets of curiosities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_curiosities) and [treasuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury). The art holdings of the [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach) thus formed the first and essential foundation of later state museums. As early as the mid-16th century, Duke Albrecht V (r. 1550–1579) had collected paintings as well as Greek and Roman sculptures (or copies made of them). He had the Antiquarium in the Munich Residence built specifically for his collection of antique sculptures. The electors [Maximilian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I,_Elector_of_Bavaria) (r. 1594–1651) and [Max II. Emanuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_II_Emanuel,_Elector_of_Bavaria) (r. 1679–1726) expanded the art collections considerably. In the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) at the end of the 18th century, there was a demand to open up art collections to the general public in the spirit of "popular education". But Museums were not founded by the state until the time of the art-loving [King Ludwig I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_I_of_Bavaria) (r. 1825–1848). In Munich, he built [Glyptothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyptothek) (opened 1830), [Alte Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Pinakothek) (opened 1836), and [Neue Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Pinakothek) (opened 1853). Also, the foundation of the [Germanisches Nationalmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisches_Nationalmuseum) in Nuremberg (1852), the establishment of the Neue Pinakothek, which opened in 1853, and the [Bavarian National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_National_Museum) (1867) in Munich were of central importance for the development of museums in Bavaria in the 19th century. With the end of the monarchy in 1918, many castles and formerly Wittelsbach property passed to the young Free State. In particular, the castles of king [Ludwig II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_II_of_Bavaria) (r. 1864–1886) [Neuschwanstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuschwanstein_Castle), [Linderhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linderhof_Palace) and [Herrenchiemsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herrenchiemsee), quickly became magnets for the public. Since then, the number of Bavarian Museums has grown considerably, from 125 in 1907 to around 1,300 today.[^cite_note-97]

## Transport

### Air

Bavaria's main airport is [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) which is also Germany's [second-busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Germany) airport after [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) and the [eleventh-busiest in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe), handling 41.6 million passengers in 2024.[^cite_note-98] The airport had been ranked as the [39th-busiest airport worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) that year.[^cite_note-99]

Other airports serving the region include [Nuremberg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Airport) which is the second busiest in Bavaria, and [Memmingen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memmingen_Airport), also known as Allgäu Airport.
