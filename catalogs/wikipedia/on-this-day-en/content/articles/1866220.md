The **Office of the Ombudsman** (**OMB**; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Tanggapan ng Tanodbayan*) is a constitutional body established under the [Constitution of the Philippines](19554336.md), tasked with investigating and prosecuting government officials accused of crimes, particularly those involving graft and corruption.[^cite_note-4]

[Jesus Crispin Remulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Crispin_Remulla), the 58th [secretary of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Justice_\(Philippines\)), has been serving as the 7th Ombudsman of the Philippines since 2025, following his appointment by President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md).

## Functions

Under the [1987 Philippine Constitution](19554336.md) and the [Ombudsman Act of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombudsman_Act_of_1989), the Office of the Ombudsman independently monitors all three branches of the government for political corruption. The [ombudsman](26289198.md) "is principally tasked to investigate on its own or upon complaint by any person, in any form or manner, any act or omission of any public officer or employee, including those in government-owned or controlled corporations, which appears to be illegal, unjust, improper or inefficient."[^cite_note-Primer-5] After an investigation, the ombudsman files charges at the [Sandiganbayan](8563865.md), a special anti-graft court.

The Offices of the Ombudsman includes the ombudsman's own office, along with offices for a team composed of a [sheriff](89516.md#Philippines), the ombudsman's second in command, and six other deputies who lead their respective divisions or bureaus.

## History

The Office of the Ombudsman predates the 1987 Constitution. There have been several offices established under various presidents of the Philippines whose duties are now subsumed under the Office of the Ombudsman:[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6]

- Integrity Board ([Elpidio Quirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elpidio_Quirino), established 1950)

- Presidential Complaints and Action Commission ([Ramon Magsaysay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Magsaysay), established 1957)

- Presidential Committee on Administration Performance Efficiency ([Carlos P. Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_P._Garcia), established 1958)

- Presidential Anti-Graft Committee ([Diosdado Macapagal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Macapagal), established 1962)

- Presidential Agency on Reform and Government Operations ([Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), established 1966)

In 1969, the Office of the Citizens Counselor was created by the Republic Act No. 6028.[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6] It was primarily designed to conduct fact-finding investigations and make recommendations to Congress and the President.[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6] The office was "not at all implemented."[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6] Subsequently, Marcos created the Complaints and Investigation Office in 1970 and the Presidential Administrative Assistance Committee in 1971.[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6] None of these were successful nor were independent.[^cite_note-eLegalForum-7]

In the martial law-era 1973 Philippine Constitution (Sections 5 and 6, Article XIII), provided for the establishment of a special court called the [Sandiganbayan](8563865.md) and an office of the ombudsman called the t*anodbayan*.[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6] On June 11, 1978, during [martial law](50477.md), then President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) created by [presidential decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_decree) the office of the Tanodbayan.[^cite_note-HistoryOmbud-6][^cite_note-PD148-8] The Tanodbayan was not independent but served at the pleasure of the president and could be removed at any time.[^cite_note-PD148-8]

After Marcos was overthrown in the 1986 [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution), [President Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) issued two executive orders (nos. 243 and 244) in July 1987 that dictated a new Office of the Ombudsman and transformed the Tanodbayan into the Office of the Special Prosecutor under the ombudsman.[^cite_note-Primer-5] Following the passage of the 1987 Constitution, the Ombudsman Act of 1989 was passed to define the roles and structure of the Office.[^cite_note-Primer-5]

In March 2011, President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) ordered the dismissal of Emilio Gonzales III as deputy ombudsman for military and other law enforcement officers for neglecting to properly handle the complaint filed by policeman Rolando Mendoza prior to the [Manila hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_hostage_crisis) in 2010.[^cite_note-9] In September 2012, Gonzales was ordered reinstated by the [Supreme Court](1335534.md), which argued among others that he did not commit an offense that could be considered "intentional wrongdoing\[...] amounting to betrayal of public trust".[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] By January 2014, the Supreme Court ruled the president's dismissal of a deputy ombudsman to be unconstitutional as it violates the independence of the Office of the Ombudsman provided by the [Constitution](19554336.md).[^cite_note-12]

In 2016, Overall Deputy Ombudsman [Melchor Arthur Carandang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchor_Arthur_Carandang) began investigating a plunder complaint filed by Senator [Antonio Trillanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Trillanes) regarding the allegedly questionable bank accounts of President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) and his family from 2006 to 2016.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-INQDizon-14] By July 2018, Carandang was ordered dismissed from office by [Executive Secretary](7438844.md) [Salvador Medialdea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Medialdea) for allegedly committing graft, corruption and betrayal of public trust in issuing statements about the Duterte family's bank records.[^cite_note-15] In spite of Carandang's appeal, as well as criticism from observers that Medialdea's order violates the Supreme Court ruling from 2014 regarding the independence of the agency,[^cite_note-PSPatag-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-VFsacking-18] the dismissal order was enforced on June 17, 2019;[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-INQEditorial-20] Duterte later appointed procurement director Warren Rex Liong of the [Department of Budget and Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Budget_and_Management) as ODO in November 2020.[^cite_note-21]

In August 2023, a special panel of investigators at the Ombudsman recommended that Liong be charged with three counts of graft for his alleged involvement in the [Pharmally scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmally_scandal) that was uncovered in 2021.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## Officials

The ombudsman and its subordinates are appointed by the [president of the Philippines](271126.md) from a list submitted by the [Judicial and Bar Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_and_Bar_Council) for a non-renewable seven-year term. The ombudsman can be removed from office only through impeachment.
