**Plovdiv** ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Пловдив, pronounced [\[ˈpɫɔvdif\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bulgarian)) is the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Bulgaria) in [Bulgaria](3415.md) and the most populous city in the [Bulgarian part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thrace) of the historic region of [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace), located 144 km (93 miles) southeast of the capital [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia). It is considered the [oldest European continually inhabited city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities#Europe), having been inhabited since c. 6,000 BCE. It had a population of 329,489 as of 2024 and 540,000 in its greater metropolitan area. An important economic, transport, cultural, and educational hub of Bulgaria, Plovdiv was the [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2019, and joined the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities in 2016.[^cite_note-UNESCO_UIL-GNLC-4]

Plovdiv is in a fertile region of south-central Bulgaria on the two banks of the [Maritsa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa_River). The city has historically developed on seven [syenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syenite) hills, some of which are 250 metres (820 feet) high. Because of these hills, Plovdiv is often referred to in Bulgaria as "The City of the Seven Hills". There is evidence of habitation in the area dating back to the 6th millennium BC, when the first [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) settlements were established. The city was subsequently a [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians) settlement named Pulpudeva, later being conquered and ruled also by [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Ancient Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Macedonians), [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_settlement_of_Southeast_Europe), [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths), [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns), [Bulgarians](151876.md), [Thraco-Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thraco-Roman), [Bulgars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgars), [Slavic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Slavic_tribes), [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Empire), and [Ottoman Turks](22278.md).[^cite_note-plovdiv1-5]

Philippopolis ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Φιλιππούπολις) was founded as a *[polis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polis)* by the father of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), [Philip the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_the_Great) (r. 359–336 BC), the king of ancient [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(ancient_kingdom\)), settling there both Thracians and 2,000 [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Macedonians) ([Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks)) in 342 BC.[^cite_note-\:0-6] Control of the city alternated between the Macedonian kingdom and the Thracian [Odrysian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom) during the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period); the Macedonian king [Philip V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Macedon) (r. 221–179 BC) reoccupied the city in 183 BC and his successor [Perseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseus_of_Macedon) (r. 179–168 BC) held the city with the Odrysians until the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) conquered the Macedonian kingdom in 168 BC.[^cite_note-\:0-6] Philippopolis became the capital of the [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Thracia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracia).[^cite_note-\:0-6] The city was at the centre of the road network of inland Thrace, and the strategic *[Via Militaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Militaris)* was crossed by several other roads at the site, leading to the [Danube](21209639.md), the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea), and the [Black Sea](3386.md). The [Roman emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor) [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius) (r. 161–180 AD) built a new [wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall) around the city.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

In [Late Antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Antiquity), Philippopolis was an important stronghold, but was sacked in 250 during the [Crisis of the Third Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_of_the_Third_Century),[^cite_note-\:0-6] after the [Siege of Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Philippopolis_\(250\)) by the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) led by [Cniva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cniva). After this the settlement contracted, though it remained a major city, with the city walls rebuilt and new Christian [basilicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicas) and [Roman baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_baths) constructed in the 4th century.[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-\:2-8] The city was again sacked by the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) in 441/442, and the walls were again rebuilt.[^cite_note-\:2-8] Roman Philippopolis resisted another attack, by the [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars) in the 580s, after the walls were renewed yet again by [Justinian the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_the_Great) (r. 527–565).[^cite_note-\:2-8]

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Philippopolis fell to the [Bulgars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgars) of the [First Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire) in 863, during the reign of [Boris I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_I) (r. 852–889), having been briefly abandoned by the Christian inhabitants in 813 during a dispute with the *[khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_of_Bulgaria)* [Krum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krum) (r. c. 803 – 814).[^cite_note-\:2-8][^cite_note-\:1-7] During the [Byzantine–Bulgarian wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Bulgarian_wars), the emperor [Basil the Bulgar-Slayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_the_Bulgar-Slayer) (r. 960–1025) used Philippopolis as a major strategic fortification, governed by the *[protospatharios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protospatharios)* [Nikephoros Xiphias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikephoros_Xiphias).[^cite_note-\:1-7] In the middle 11th century, the city was attacked by the [Pechenegs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechenegs), who occupied it briefly around 1090.[^cite_note-\:1-7] The city continued to prosper, with the walls restored in the 12th century, during which the historian and politician [Niketas Choniates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niketas_Choniates) was its governor and the physician [Michael Italikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Italikos) was its [metropolitan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop).[^cite_note-\:1-7]

According to the Latin historian of the [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade), [Geoffrey of Villehardouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Villehardouin), Philippopolis was the third largest city in the Byzantine Empire, after [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) ([Istanbul](3391396.md)) and [Thessalonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessalonica) ([Thessaloniki](40471.md)).[^cite_note-\:1-7] It suffered damage from the armies passing through the city during the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) as well as from sectarian violence between the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) and the [Armenian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Orthodox) and [Paulician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulician) denominations.[^cite_note-\:1-7] The city was destroyed by [Kaloyan of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaloyan_of_Bulgaria) (r. 1196–1207) in 1206 and rebuilt thereafter.[^cite_note-\:1-7] In 1219, the city became the capital of the Crusader [Duchy of Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Philippopolis), part of the [Latin Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Empire).[^cite_note-\:1-7] The [Second Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Bulgarian_Empire) recovered the city in 1263, but lost it to Byzantine control before recapturing it in 1323.[^cite_note-\:1-7] The [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) conquered Philippopolis ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Filibe*) in 1363 or 1364.[^cite_note-\:1-7] During the 500 years of Ottoman rule, Filibe served as one of the important commercial and transportation nodes in the Ottoman Balkans. It also played a role as an administrative centre of various [sanjaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak) and [eyalets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyalet).

On 4 January 1878, at the end of the [Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)), Plovdiv was taken away from Ottoman rule by the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) army. It remained within the borders of Bulgaria until July of the same year, when it became the capital of the autonomous Ottoman region of [Eastern Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Rumelia). In 1885, Plovdiv and Eastern Rumelia [joined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_unification) Bulgaria.

There are many preserved ruins such as the ancient [Plovdiv Roman theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Roman_theatre), a Roman [odeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_\(building\)), a [Roman aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct), the [Plovdiv Roman Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Roman_Stadium), the archaeological complex Eirene, and others. Plovdiv is host to a huge variety of cultural events such as the [International Fair Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fair_Plovdiv), the international theatrical [festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival) "A stage on a crossroad", the TV festival "The golden chest", and many more novel festivals, such as Night/Plovdiv in September, Kapana Fest, and Opera Open. The oldest American educational institution outside the [United States](3434750.md), the [American College of Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Sofia), was founded in Plovdiv in 1860 and later moved to Sofia.

## Etymology



Ancient settlements with names related to "deva". Pulpudeva denotes Plovdiv in which the latter name is rooted.



Map describing the city as "Philippopolis, que et Poneropolis, Duloupolis, Eumolpiada, item Trimontium, at que Pulpudena"

Plovdiv has been given various names throughout its long history. The [Odrysian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom) capital *Odrysa* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Οδρύσα), is suggested to have been modern Plovdiv by numismatic research[^cite_note-arc-9][^cite_note-10] or [Odrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edirne) (Adrianople).[^cite_note-academia-11] The Greek historian [Theopompus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theopompus)[^cite_note-theopompi-12] mentioned it in the 4th century BC as a town named *Poneropolis* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Πονηρόπολις) "town of villains") in pejorative relation to the conquest by king [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon) who is said to have settled the town with 2,000 men who were false-accusers, sycophants, lawyers, and other possible disreputables.[^cite_note-13] According to [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch), the town was named by this king after he had populated it with a crew of rogues and vagabonds,[^cite_note-14] but this is possibly a folk name that did not actually exist.[^cite_note-academia-11] The names *Dulon polis* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Δουλωνπολις "slaves' town") and possibly *Moichopolis* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Μοιχοπολις "adulterer's town") likely have similar origins.

The city has been called *Philippopolis* (Φιλιππόπολις) pronounced [\[pʰilipopolis\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek); Modern [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Φιλιππούπολη, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek): *Philippoupoli* pronounced [\[filipupoli\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek)) or "the city of Philip", from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Φίλιππος, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek): *Philippos* "horse-lover", most likely in honor of [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon)[^cite_note-col-15] after his death or in honor of [Philip V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Macedon),[^cite_note-arc-9][^cite_note-pld-16] as this name was first mentioned in the 2nd century BC by [Polybius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polybius) in connection with the campaign of Philip V.[^cite_note-arc-9][^cite_note-pld-16] *Philippopolis* was identified later by [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch) and [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) as the former *Poneropolis*. [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) identified Philip II's settlement of most "evil, wicked" (Modern [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): πονηροτάτους, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek): *ponerotatous* ) as Cabyle ([Kabyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabyle_\(ancient_city\))),[^cite_note-17] whereas [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) considered the location of Poneropolis different from the rest.

*Kendrisia* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Κενδρεισεία) was an old name of the city.[^cite_note-plovdiv1-5] Its earliest recorded use is on an artifact mentioning that king [Beithys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beithys), priest of the Syrian goddess, brought gifts to Kendriso [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo);[^cite_note-18] the deity is recorded to be named multiple times after different cities. Later Roman coins mentioned the name which is possibly derived from Thracian god Kendriso who is equated with Apollo,[^cite_note-19] the [cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus) forests, or from the Thracian tribe artifacts known as the kendrisi.[^cite_note-plovdiv1-5][^cite_note-pld-16] Another assumed name is the 1st century AD *Tiberias* in honor of the Roman emperor [Tiberius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius), under whom the [Odrysian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_Kingdom) was a [client](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state) of Rome.[^cite_note-academia-11] After the Romans had taken control of the area, the city was named in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Trimontium*, meaning "The Three Hills", and mentioned in the 1st century by [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder). At times the name was *[Ulpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulpia_gens)*, *[Flavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavia_gens)*, *[Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_gens)* after the Roman families.

[Ammianus Marcellinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus) wrote in the 4th century AD that the then city had been the old *Eumolpias*/*Eumolpiada,*,[^cite_note-20] the oldest name chronologically.[^cite_note-academia-11] It was named after the mythical Thracian king [Eumolpos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eumolpos), son of [Poseidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poseidon)[^cite_note-21] or [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_\(mythology\)),[^cite_note-22] who may have founded the city around 1200 BC [^cite_note-23] or 1350 BC.[^cite_note-24] It is also possible that it was named after the [Vestal Virgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestal_Virgins) in the temples – evmolpeya.[^cite_note-plovdiv1-5]

In the 6th century AD, [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes) wrote that the former name of the city was *Pulpudeva* and that [Philip the Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_the_Arab) named the city after himself. This name is most likely a [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_language) oral translation[^cite_note-plovdiv1-5] of the other as it kept all consonants of the name Philip + [deva (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dava_\(Dacian\)). Although the two names sound similar, they may not share the same origin as [Adrianople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrianople) does, and *Pulpudeva* may have predated the other names[^cite_note-uk-25][^cite_note-26] meaning "lake city" in [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_language).[^cite_note-pld-16] Since the 9th century AD the Slavic name began to appear as *Papaldiv/n, Plo(v)div, Pladiv, Pladin, Plapdiv, Plovdin,* which originate from *Pulpudeva*.[^cite_note-27] As a result, the name has lost any meaning. In British English the Bulgarian variant Пловдив *Plòvdiv* has become prevalent after [World War I](4764461.md). The [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusaders) mentioned the city as *Prineople*, *Sinople* and *Phinepople*.[^cite_note-pld-16] The Ottomans called the city *Filibe*, a corruption of "Philip", in a document from 1448.[^cite_note-28]

The history of the region spans more than eight millennia. Numerous nations have left their traces on the twelve-metre-thick (39-[foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_\(unit\))) cultural layers of the city. The earliest signs of habitation in the territory of Plovdiv date as far back as the 6th millennium BC.[^cite_note-filipopol-29][^cite_note-plovdiv1-5] Plovdiv has settlement traces including necropolises dating from the Neolithic era (roughly 6000–5000 BC) like the mounds Yasa Tepe 1 in the Philipovo district and Yasa Tepe 2 in Lauta park.[^cite_note-райчевски-30][^cite_note-кесякова-31][^cite_note-32]

Archaeologists have discovered fine pottery and objects of everyday life on [Nebet Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebet_Tepe) from as early as the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) era, showing that at the end of the 4th millennium BC, there was already an established settlement there which was [continuously inhabited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities) since then.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Thracian necropolises dating back to the 2nd–3rd millennium BC have been discovered, while the Thracian town was established between the 2nd and the 1st millennium BC.

The town was a fort of the independent local [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians) tribe [Bessi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessi).[^cite_note-36] In 516 BC during the rule of [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great), Thrace was included in the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire).[^cite_note-37] In 492 BC, the Persian general [Mardonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardonius_\(general\)) subjugated Thrace again, and it nominally became a vassal of Persia until 479 BC and the early rule of [Xerxes I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerxes_I).[^cite_note-38] The town became part of the [Odrysian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom) (460 BC – 46 AD), a [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians) tribal union. The town was conquered by [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon),[^cite_note-39] and the Odrysian king was deposed in 342 BC. Ten years after the Macedonian invasion, the Thracian kings started to exercise power again after the Odrysian [Seuthes III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuthes_III) had re-established their kingdom under Macedonian suzerainty as a result of a successful revolt against [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great)'s rule resulting in a stalemate.[^cite_note-40] The Odrysian kingdom gradually overcame the Macedonian suzerainty, while the city was destroyed by the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) as part of the [Celtic settlement of Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_settlement_of_Eastern_Europe), most likely in the 270s BC.[^cite_note-41] In 183 BC, [Philip V of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Macedon) conquered the city, but shortly after, the Thracians re-conquered it.

In 72 BC, the city was seized by the Roman general [Marcus Lucullus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Terentius_Varro_Lucullus) but was soon restored to Thracian control. In 46 AD, the city was finally incorporated into the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) by emperor [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius);[^cite_note-42] it served as the capital of the province of [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace). Although it was not the capital of the province of Thrace, the city was the largest and most important centre in the province.[^cite_note-43] As such, the city was the seat of the Union of Thracians.[^cite_note-44] In those times, the [Via Militaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Militaris) (or Via Diagonalis), the most important military road in the [Balkans](4829.md), passed through the city.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The Roman times were a period of growth and cultural excellence.[^cite_note-47] The ancient ruins tell a story of a vibrant, growing city with numerous public buildings, shrines, baths, theatres, a stadium, and the only developed ancient water supply system in Bulgaria. The city had an advanced water system and [sewerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitary_sewer). In 179 a second wall was built to encompass Trimontium which had already extended out of the Three hills into the valley. Many of those are still preserved and can be seen by tourists. Today only a small part of the ancient city has been excavated.[^cite_note-pldiv-48]

In 250 the city was captured and looted after the [Battle of Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Philippopolis_\(250\)) by the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths), led by their ruler [Cniva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cniva). Many of its citizens, 100,000 according to [Ammianus Marcellinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus), died or were taken captive.[^cite_note-49] It took a century and hard work to recover the city. However, it was destroyed again by [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila)'s [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hun) in 441–442 and by the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) of [Theodoric Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoric_Strabo) in 471.[^cite_note-50]

An ancient Roman inscription written in Ancient Greek dated to 253 – 255 AD were discovered in the [Great Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basilica,_Plovdiv). The inscription refers to the [Dionysian Mysteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysian_Mysteries) and also mentions Roman Emperors [Valerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerian_\(emperor\)) and [Gallienus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallienus). It has been found on a large stele which was used as construction material during the building of the Great Basilica.[^cite_note-51]

In August 2024, archaeologists from the Regional Archaeological Museum announced the discovery of a well-preserved Thracian temple dated to the third century BC. The 10-metre-long temple is made of dry joints and clay-sand mortar and has two rooms.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Middle Ages



Monument of [Krum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krum_of_Bulgaria) in Plovdiv, who was the first Bulgarian ruler to capture Plovdiv.

The [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples) had fully settled in the area by the middle of the 6th century. This was done peacefully as there are no records for their attacks.[^cite_note-54] With the establishment of [Bulgaria](3415.md) in 681, Philippoupolis, the name of the city then, became an important border fortress of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). It was captured by Khan [Krum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krum_of_Bulgaria) in 812, but the region was fully incorporated into the [Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire) in 834 during the reign of Khan [Malamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malamir_of_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-55] It was reconquered by the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) in 855–856 for a short time until it was returned to [Boris I of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_I_of_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] From Philippopolis, the influence of [dualistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualistic_cosmology) doctrines spread to Bulgaria forming the basis of the [Bogomil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogomil) heresy. The city remained in Bulgarian hands until 970.[^cite_note-58] However, the city is described at the time of [Constantine VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_VII) in the 10th century as being within the Byzantine province ([theme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_\(Byzantine_district\)) of [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(theme\))). Philippopolis was captured by the Byzantines in 969, shortly before it was sacked by the ruler of [Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') [Sviatoslav I of Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_I_of_Kiev) who impaled 20,000 citizens.[^cite_note-59] Before and after the Rus' massacre, the city was settled by [Paulician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulician) heretics transported from Syria and Armenia to serve as military settlers on the European frontier with Bulgaria. Aime de Varennes in 1180 encountered the singing of Byzantine songs in the city that recounted the deeds of Alexander the Great and his predecessors over 1300 years before.[^cite_note-60]

Byzantine rule was interrupted by the [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade) (1189–1192) when the army of the [Holy Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) emperor [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) conquered Philippopolis. [Ivanko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanko_\(boyar\)) was appointed as the governor of the Byzantine Theme of Philippopolis in 1196, but between 1198 and 1200 separated it from Byzantium in a union with [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Bulgarian_Empire). The [Latin Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Empire) conquered Philippopolis in 1204, and there were two short interregnum periods as the city was twice occupied by [Kaloyan of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaloyan_of_Bulgaria) before his death in 1207.[^cite_note-pldv-61] In 1208, Kaloyan's successor [Boril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boril_of_Bulgaria) was defeated by the Latins in the [Battle of Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Philippopolis_\(1208\)).[^cite_note-62] Under Latin rule, Philippopolis was the capital of the [Duchy of Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Philippopolis), which was governed by [Renier de Trit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renier_de_Trit) and later on by Gerard de Strem. The city was possibly at times a vassal of Bulgaria or [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice). [Ivan Asen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Asen_II_of_Bulgaria) conquered the duchy finally in 1230 but the city had possibly been conquered earlier.[^cite_note-63] Afterwards, Philippopolis was conquered by Byzantium. According to some information, by 1300 Philippopolis was a possession of [Theodore Svetoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Svetoslav) of Bulgaria. It was conquered from Byzantium by [George Terter II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Bulgaria) of Bulgaria in 1322.[^cite_note-64] [Andronikos III Palaiologos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andronikos_III_Palaiologos) unsuccessfully besieged the city, but a treaty restored Byzantine rule once again in 1323. In 1344 the city and eight other cities were surrendered to Bulgaria by the regency for [John V Palaiologos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_V_Palaiologos) as the price for [Ivan Alexander of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Alexander_of_Bulgaria)'s support in the [Byzantine civil war of 1341–47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_civil_war_of_1341%E2%80%9347).[^cite_note-65]

### Ottoman rule

In 1364 the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) under [Lala Shahin Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lala_Shahin_Pasha) seized Plovdiv.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-pat-67] According to other data, Plovdiv was not an Ottoman possession until the [Battle of Maritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maritsa) in 1371, after which, the citizens and the Bulgarian army fled leaving the city without resistance. Refugees settled in [Stanimaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asenovgrad). During the [Ottoman Interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Interregnum) in 1410, [Musa Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_%C3%87elebi) conquered the city killing and displacing inhabitants.[^cite_note-egeweb2.ege.edu.tr-68] The city was the centre of the [Rumelia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumelia_Eyalet) from 1364 until 1443, when it was replaced by [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) as the capital of [Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumelia). Plovdiv served as a sanjak centre within Rumelia between 1443 and 1593, the sanjak centre in [Silistra Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silistra_Eyalet) between 1593 and 1826, the sanjak centre in [Eyalet of Adrianople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyalet_of_Adrianople) between 1826 and 1867, and the sanjak centre of [Edirne Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edirne_Vilayet) between 1867 and 1878. During that period, Plovdiv was one of the major economic centers in the Balkans, along with [Istanbul](3391396.md) ([Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople)), [Edirne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edirne), [Thessaloniki](40471.md), and Sofia. The richer citizens constructed beautiful houses, many of which can still be seen in the architectural reserve of Old Plovdiv. From the early 15th century till the end of 17th century the city was predominantly inhabited by Muslims.[^cite_note-69]

#### National revival

Main article: [Bulgarian National Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Revival)



The [Virgin Mary Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Mother_of_God,_Plovdiv).

Under the rule of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), Filibe (as the city was known at that time) was a focal point for the Bulgarian national movement and survived as one of the major cultural centers for Bulgarian culture and tradition.

Filibe was described as consisting of Turks, Bulgarians, Hellenized Bulgarians, Armenians, Jews, Vlachs, Arvanites, Greeks, and Roma people. In the 16–17 century a significant number of [Sephardic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews) settled along with a smaller Armenian community from [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)). The [Paulicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulician) adopted Catholicism or lost their identity. The abolition of [Slavonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic) as the language of the [Bulgarian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Ohrid) as well as the complete abolition of the church in 1767 and the introduction of the [Millet System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet_\(Ottoman_Empire\)) led to ethnic division among people of different religions. Christian and Muslim Bulgarians were subjected to [Hellenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization) and [Turkification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkification) respectively. A major part of the inhabitants was fully or partly Hellenized due to the Greek patriarchate. The "Langeris" are described as Greeks from the area of the nearby [Stenimachos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asenovgrad). The process of Hellenization flourished until the 1830s but declined with the [Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat) as the idea of the [Hellenic nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece_\(Wittelsbach\)) of Christians grew and was associated with ethnic Greeks.

The re-establishment of the Bulgarian Church in 1870 was a sign of ethnic and national consciousness. Thus, although there is a little doubt about the Bulgarian origin of the Gulidas, the city could be considered of Greek or Bulgarian majority in the 19th century.[^cite_note-70] Raymond Detrez has suggested that when the Gudilas and Langeris claimed to be Greek it was more in the sense of "[Romei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greels?action=edit\&redlink=1) than Ellines, in a cultural rather than an ethnic sense".[^cite_note-balkanologie-71] According to the statistics by the Bulgarian and Greek authors, there were no Turks in the city; according to an alternative estimate the city was of Turkish majority.[^cite_note-72]

Filibe had an important role in the struggle for Church independence which was, according to some historians, a peaceful bourgeois revolution. Filibe became the center of that struggle with leaders such as [Nayden Gerov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayden_Gerov), Dr Valkovich, [Joakim Gruev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joakim_Gruev), and whole families. In 1836 the first Bulgarian school was inaugurated, and in 1850, modern secular education began when the "St Cyrill and Methodius" school was opened. On 11 May 1858, the day of [Saints Cyril and Methodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius) was celebrated for the first time; this later became a National holiday which is still celebrated today (but on 24 May due to Bulgaria's 1916 transition from the [Old Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style) ([Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar)) to the [New Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption_of_the_Gregorian_calendar#Adoption_in_Eastern_Europe) ([Gregorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar)) calendar). In 1858 in the [Church of Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Mother_of_God,_Plovdiv), the Christmas liturgy was served for the first time in the [Bulgarian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language) since the beginning of the Turkish occupation in the 14th century. Until 1906 there were Bulgarian and Greek bishops in the city. In 1868 the school expanded into the first grammar school. Some of the intellectuals, politicians, and spiritual leaders of the nation graduated that school.[^cite_note-pld-16]

The city was conquered by the Russians under [Aleksandr Burago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Burago) for several hours during the [Battle of Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Philippopolis_\(1878\)) on 17 January 1878.[^cite_note-pat-67] It was the capital of the [Provisional Russian Administration in Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Russian_Administration_in_Bulgaria) between May and October. According to the Russian census of the same year, Plovdiv had a population of 24,000 citizens, of which ethnic [Bulgarians](151876.md) comprised 45.4%, [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) 23.1% and [Greeks](42056.md) 19.9%.

### Eastern Rumelia

Main article: [Bulgarian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_unification)



Nebet Tepe, drawing from The Graphic – London, 1885



Taat tepe, in Plovdiv, with the governor's palace and Maritsa river in the foreground. Drawing from The Graphic – London, 1885

According to the [Treaty of San Stefano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_San_Stefano) on 3 March 1878, the Principality of Bulgaria included the lands with predominantly Bulgarian population. Plovdiv which was the biggest and most vibrant Bulgarian city was selected as a capital of the restored country and for a seat of the Temporary Russian Government.[^cite_note-ocertsi-73] Great Britain and [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), however, did not approve that treaty and the final result of the war was concluded in the [Congress of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Berlin) which divided the newly liberated country into several parts. It separated the autonomous region of [Eastern Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Rumelia) from Bulgaria, and Plovdiv became its capital. The Ottoman Empire created a constitution and appointed a governor.[^cite_note-74]

In the spring of 1885, [Zahari Stoyanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahari_Stoyanov) formed the Secret Bulgarian Central Revolutionary Committee in the city which actively conducted propaganda for the unification of Bulgaria and Eastern Rumelia. On 5 September, several hundred armed rebels from Golyamo Konare (now [Saedinenie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saedinenie,_Plovdiv_Province)) marched to Plovdiv. In the night of 5–6 September, these men, led by Danail Nikolaev, took control of the city and removed from office the General-Governor [Gavril Krastevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavril_Krastevich). A provisional government was formed led by [Georgi Stranski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Stranski), and universal mobilization was announced.[^cite_note-75] After the Serbs were defeated in the [Serbo-Bulgarian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Bulgarian_War), Bulgaria and Turkey reached an agreement that the Principality of Bulgaria and Eastern Rumelia had a common government, Parliament, administration, and army. Today, 6 September is celebrated as the Unification Day and the Day of Plovdiv.

### Recent history

After the unification, Plovdiv remained the second most populous city in Bulgaria after the nation's capital [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia). The first railway in the city was built in 1874 connecting it with the Ottoman capital [Constantinople](3391396.md), and in 1888, it was linked with Sofia. In 1892 Plovdiv became the host of the First Bulgarian Fair with international participation which was succeeded by the [International Fair Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fair_Plovdiv). After the liberation, the first brewery was inaugurated in the city.

The noteworthy English travel writer Patrick Leigh Fermor visited Plovdiv in the late summer of 1934 and he was charmed by the town and a local woman named Nadejda.[^cite_note-76]

In the beginning of the 20th century, Plovdiv grew as a significant industrial and commercial center with a well-developed light and food industry. In 1927 the electrification of Plovdiv has started. German, French, and [Belgian](3343.md) capital was invested in the city in the development of modern trade, banking, and industry. In 1939, there were 16,000 craftsmen and 17,000 workers in manufacturing factories, mainly for food and tobacco processing. During the Second World War, the tobacco industry expanded as well as the export of fruit and vegetables. In 1943, 1,500 Jews were saved from deportation in [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camp) by the archbishop of Plovdiv, [Cyril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_Cyril_of_Bulgaria), who later became the Bulgarian Patriarch. In 1944, the city was bombed by the British-American coalition.

Tobacco Depot workers [went on strike on 4 May 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_tobacco_workers'_strike).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

On 6 April 1956, the first trolleybus line was opened and in the 1950s the Trimontsium Hotel was constructed. In the 1960s and 1970s, there was a construction boom and many of the modern neighborhoods took shape. In the 1970s and 1980s, antique remains were excavated and the Old Town was fully restored. In 1990 the "Plovdiv" sports complex finished construction, and it included the largest stadium and rowing canal in the country. In that period, Plovdiv became the birthplace of Bulgaria's movement for democratic reform, which by 1989 had garnered enough support to enter government.

Plovdiv has hosted specialized exhibitions of the [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Fair) in 1981, 1985, and 1991.





## Geography



Plovdiv seen from space



A view of Nebet tepe hill



A view of Plovdiv with the [Stara Planina Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Mountains) in the background.

Plovdiv is located on the banks of the [Maritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa) river, southeast of the Bulgarian capital [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia). The city is in the southern part of the *Plain of Plovdiv*, an [alluvial plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain) that forms the western portion of the [Upper Thracian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Thracian_Plain). From there, the peaks of the [Sredna Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sredna_Gora) mountain range rise to the northwest, the Chirpan Heights to the east, and the [Rhodope Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodope_Mountains) to the south.[^cite_note-79] Originally, Plovdiv's development occurred south of Maritsa, with expansion across the river taking place only within the last 100 years. Modern Plovdiv covers an area of 101 km<sup>2</sup> (39 sq mi), less than 0.1% of Bulgaria's total area. It is the most densely populated city in Bulgaria, with 3,769 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>.

Inside the city proper are six [syenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syenite) hills. At the beginning of the 20th century, there were seven syenite hills, but one ([Markovo tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markovo_tepe)) was destroyed. Three of them are called the Three Hills ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Трихълмие *Trihalmie*), the others are called the Hill of the Youth ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Младежки хълм, *Mladezhki halm*), the Hill of the Liberators ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Хълм на освободителите, *Halm na osvoboditelite*), and the Hill of [Danov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hristo_G._Danov) ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Данов хълм, *Danov halm*).[^cite_note-80]

### Climate

Plovdiv has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) with considerable [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental) influences. There are four distinct seasons with large temperature jumps between seasons.

Summer (mid-May to late September) is hot, moderately dry and sunny, with July and August having an average high of 33 °C (91 °F). Plovdiv sometimes experiences very hot days which are typical in the interior of the country. Summer nights are mild.

Autumn starts in late September; days are long and relatively warm in early autumn. The nights become chilly by September. The first frost usually occurs by November.

Winter is normally cold and snow is common. The average number of days with snow coverage in Plovdiv is 15. The average depth of snow coverage is 2 to 4 cm (1 to 2 in), and the maximum is normally 6 to 13 cm (2 to 5 in), but some winters coverage can reach 70 cm (28 in) or more. The average January temperature is −0.4 °C (31 °F).

Spring begins in March and is cooler than autumn. The frost season ends in March. The days are mild and relatively warm in mid-spring.

The average relative humidity is 73% and is highest in December at 86% and the lowest in August at 62%. The total precipitation is 540 mm (21.26 in) and is fairly evenly distributed throughout the year. The wettest months of the year are May and June, with an average precipitation of 66.2 mm (2.61 in), and the driest month is August, with an average precipitation of 31 mm (1.22 in).

Gentle winds (0 to 5 m/s) are predominant in the city with wind speeds of up to 1 m/s, representing 95% of all winds during the year. Mists are common in the cooler months, especially along the banks of the Maritsa. On average there are 33 days with [mist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mist) during the year.[^cite_note-81]

In its ethnic character Plovdiv is the second or the third-largest cosmopolitan city inhabited by [Bulgarians](151876.md), after [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) and possibly [Varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria). According to the 2001 census, out of a population of 338,224 inhabitants, Bulgarians accounted for 90% of the city's population (302,858 people). [Stolipinovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolipinovo) in Plovdiv is the largest Roma neighbourhood in the [Balkans](4829.md), having a population of around 20,000 alone; further Roma ghettos are [Hadji Hassan Mahala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadji_Hassan_Mahala) and [Sheker Mahala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheker_Mahala). Therefore, the census number is a deflation of the number of Roma people, and they are most likely the second-largest group after the Bulgarians, most of all because the Muslim Roma in Plovdiv claim to be of Turkish ethnicity and Turkish-speaking at the census ("[Xoraxane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xoraxane) Roma").[^cite_note-101] For further information see the article [Roma people in Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roma_in_Plovdiv). Like elsewhere in the country, Roma people are subjected to discrimination and segregation (See the Bulgaria section of the article [Antiziganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiziganism)).

After the Wars for National Union ([Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars) and World War I), the city became home for thousands of refugees from the former Bulgarian lands in [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(region\)), [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Thrace) and [Eastern Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Thrace). Many of the old neighbourhoods are still referred to as *Belomorski* or *Vardarski*. Most of the Jews left the city after the foundation of [Israel](9282173.md) in 1948, as well as most of the Turks and Greeks. Prior to the population exchange, as of 1 January 1885, the city of Plovdiv had a population of 33,442, of which 16,752 were [Bulgarians](151876.md) (50%), 7,144 [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) (21%), 5,497 [Greeks](42056.md) (16%), 2,168 Jews (6%), 1,061 [Armenians](387816.md) (3%), 151 [Italians](385155.md), 112 [Germans](152735.md), 112 [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people), 80 [French people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), 61 [Russians](59510.md) and 304 people of other nationalities.[^cite_note-histmuseum-97]

The vast majority of the inhabitants are Christians, mostly [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox), Catholics, [Eastern Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic), and Protestant trends ([Adventists](28632.md), [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) and others). There are also some [Muslims](19541.md) and Jews. In Plovdiv, there are many churches, two mosques and one synagogue (see [Plovdiv Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Synagogue)).

Some [Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) also inhabit Plovdiv.[^cite_note-102]

## City government

Plovdiv is the administrative center of [Plovdiv Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Province) which consists of the Municipality of Plovdiv, the [Maritsa municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa_municipality), and the [Rodopi municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodopi_municipality). The mayor of the City of Plovdiv (Plovdiv Municipality), [Kostadin Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostadin_Dimitrov?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-103] with the six district mayors represent the local executive authorities. The Municipal Council which consists of 51 municipal counsellors, represents the legislative power, and is elected according to the proportional system by parties' lists.[^cite_note-104] The executive government of the City of Plovdiv (Plovdiv Municipality) consists of a mayor who is elected by majority representation, five deputy mayors, and one administrative secretary. All the deputy mayors and the secretary control their administrative structured units.

According to the *Law for the territorial subdivision of the Capital municipality and the large cities,*[^cite_note-105] the territory of Plovdiv Municipality is subdivided into six district administrations with their mayors being appointed following approval by the Municipal Council.

## Main sights

The city has more than 200 archaeological sites,[^cite_note-107] 30 of which are of national importance. There are many remains from antiquity. Plovdiv is among the few cities with two ancient theatres; remains of the medieval walls and towers; Ottoman baths and mosques; a well-preserved old quarter from the National Revival period with beautiful houses; churches; and narrow paved streets. There are numerous museums, art, galleries and cultural institutions. Plovdiv is host to musical, theatrical, and film events. The [Knyaz Alexander I Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knyaz_Alexander_I_Street?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the main street in Plovdiv.

The city is a starting point for trips to places in the region, such as the [Bachkovo Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachkovo_Monastery) at 30 km (19 mi) to the south, the ski-resort [Pamporovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamporovo) at 90 km (56 mi) to the south or the [spa resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_resort) to the north [Hisarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisarya), [Banya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banya,_Plovdiv_Province), Krasnovo, and [Strelcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strelcha).[^cite_note-autogenerated3-108]

Ancient monuments in Plovdiv

The [Ancient theatre (Antichen teatur)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Roman_theatre) is probably the best-known monument from [antiquity](252905.md) in Bulgaria.[^cite_note-109] During recent archaeological survey, an inscription was found on a postament of a statue at the theatre. It revealed that the site was constructed at the 90s of the 1st century CE. The inscription itself refers to Titus Flavius Cotis, the ruler of the ancient city during the reign of Emperor Domitian.

The Ancient theatre is situated in the natural saddle between two of the Three Hills. It is divided into two parts with 14 rows each divided with a horizontal lane. The theatre could accommodate up to 7,000 people.[^cite_note-110] The three-story scene is on the southern part and is decorated with [friezes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieze), cornices, and statues. The theatre was studied, conserved, and restored between 1968 and 1984. Many events are still held on the scene[^cite_note-111] including the Opera Festival Opera Open, the Rock Festival Sounds of the Ages, and the International Folklore festival. The Roman Odeon was restored in 2004.[^cite_note-112] It was built in the 2nd–5th centuries and is the second (and smaller) antique [theatre of Philipopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_of_Philippopolis) with 350 seats. It was initially built as a bulevterion, an edifice of the city council, and was later reconstructed as a theatre.

The [Ancient Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Roman_Stadium)[^cite_note-113] is another important monument of the ancient city. It was built in the 2nd century during the reign of the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) Emperor [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian). It is situated between Danov Hill and one of the Three Hills, beneath the main street from Dzhumaya Square to Kamenitsa Square. It was modelled after the stadium in [Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi). It was approximately 240 metres (790 feet) long and 50 metres (160 feet) wide, and could seat up to 30,000 spectators. The athletic games at the stadium were organised by the General Assembly of the province of Thrace. In their honour, the royal mint of Philippopolis coined money featuring the face of the ruling emperor as well as the types of athletic events held in the stadium. Only a small part of the northern section with 14 seat rows can be seen today; the larger part lies under the main street and a number of buildings.

[The Roman forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum_\(Plovdiv\)) dates from the reign of [Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian) in the 1st century and was finished in the 2nd century. It is near the modern post office next to the Odeon. It has an area of 11 hectares and was surrounded by shops and public buildings. The forum was a focal point of the streets of the ancient city.[^cite_note-ppdv-114]

The [Eirene Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eirene_Residence_\(Plovdiv\)) is in the southern part of the Three Hills on the northern part of an ancient street in the Archeological underpass. It includes remains of a public building from the 3rd to 4th centuries which belonged to a noble citizen. Eirene is the Christian name for Penelopa, a maiden from Megadon, who was converted to Christianity in the 2nd century. There are colourful mosaics which have geometrical forms and figures.[^cite_note-115]

On [Nebet hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebet_Tepe) are the remains of the first settlement which in the 12th century BC grew to the [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace) city of Eumolpias, one of the first cities in Southeastern Europe. Massive walls surrounding a temple and a palace have been excavated. The oldest part of the fortress was constructed from large syenite blocks, the so-called "cyclopean construction".

### Museums and protected sites

The Archaeological Museum was established in 1882 as the People's Museum of [Eastern Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Rumelia).[^cite_note-116] In 1928 the museum was moved to a 19th-century edifice on Saedinenie Square built by Plovdiv architect [Josef Schnitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Schnitter). The museum contains a rich collection of Thracian art. The three sections "Prehistory",[^cite_note-117] "Antiquity",[^cite_note-118] and "Middle Ages"[^cite_note-119] contain precious artifacts from the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) to the early Ottoman period (15th–16th centuries).[^cite_note-mus-120] The famous [Panagyurishte treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagyurishte_treasure) is part of the museum's collection.[^cite_note-121]

The [Plovdiv Regional Historical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Regional_Historical_Museum)[^cite_note-122] was founded in 1951 as a scientific and cultural institute for collecting, saving, and researching historical evidence about Plovdiv and the surrounding region from 16th to 20th centuries. The exhibition is situated in three buildings.[^cite_note-mus-120]

The [Plovdiv Regional Ethnographic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Regional_Ethnographic_Museum) was inaugurated in 1917. On 14 October 1943, it was moved to a house in the Old Town. In 1949 the Municipal House-museum was reorganized as a People's Ethnographic Museum and in 1962 it was renovated. There are more than 40,000 objects.[^cite_note-mus-120]

The Museum of Natural Science was inaugurated in 1955 in the old edifice of the Plovdiv Municipality built in 1880. It is among the most important museums in the country with rich collections in its [Paleontology](23084.md), [Mineralogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralogy), and [Botanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany) sections. There are several rooms for wildlife and it contains Bulgaria's largest freshwater aquarium with 40 fish species.[^cite_note-mus-120] It has a collection of minerals from the [Rhodope Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodope_Mountains).

The Museum of Aviation was established on 21 September 1991 on the territory of the Krumovo airbase[^cite_note-123] 12 km (7 mi) to the southeast of the city. The museum possesses 59 aircraft and indoor and outdoor exhibitions.[^cite_note-mus-120]

The Old Town of Plovdiv is a historic preservation site known for its Bulgarian Renaissance architectural style. The Old Town covers the area of the three central hills (Трихълмие, *Trihalmie*). Almost every house in the Old Town has its characteristic exterior and interior decoration.

### Churches, mosques and synagogues

There are a number of 19th-century churches, most of which follow the distinctive [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) construction style. They are the Saint Constantine and Saint Helena, the Saint Marina, the Saint Nedelya, the Saint Petka, and the Holy Mother of God churches. As the city has been a gathering center for Orthodox Christians for a long period of time, Plovdiv is surrounded by several monasteries located at the foot of the Rhodope Mountains such as "St. George", "St. Kozma and Damian", St. Kirik, and Yulita (Ulita). They remain good examples of the late Middle Age Orthodox architecture and iconography masterpieces typical for the region. There are also Roman Catholic cathedrals in Plovdiv, the [Cathedral of St Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St_Louis,_Plovdiv) being the largest. There are several more modern Baptist, Methodist, Presbyterian, and other Protestant churches, as well as older style [Apostolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic) churches.

Two mosques remain in Plovdiv from the time of Ottoman rule. The [Djumaya Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzhumaya_Mosque) is considered the oldest European mosque outside of Moorish Spain.

The [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic) [Plovdiv Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Synagogue) is at 13 Tsar Kaloyan Street in the remnants of a small courtyard in what was once a large Jewish quarter. Dating to the 19th century, it is one of the best-preserved examples of the so-called "Ottoman-style" synagogues in the Balkans. According to author Ruth E. Gruber, the interior of the [Plovdiv Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Synagogue) is a "hidden treasure...a glorious, if run-down, burst of color." An exquisite Venetian glass chandelier hangs from the center of the ceiling, which has a richly painted dome. All surfaces are covered in elaborate, Moorish-style, geometric designs in once-bright greens and blues. Torah scrolls are kept in the gilded Aron-ha-Kodesh.[^cite_note-124]

## Culture

### Theatre and music



A preserved medieval street in the Old town



A performance in the [Roman Odeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_of_Philippopolis)

The Plovdiv Drama Theatre[^cite_note-125] is a successor of the first professional theatre group in Bulgaria founded in 1881. The Plovdiv Puppet Theatre, founded in 1948, remains one of the leading institutions in this genre. The Plovdiv Opera was established in 1953.

Another pillar of Plovdiv's culture is the Philharmonic, founded in 1945.[^cite_note-126] Soloists such as [Dmitri Shostakovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Shostakovich), [Sviatoslav Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_Richter), [Mstislav Rostropovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mstislav_Rostropovich), Yuri Boukov, and Mincho Minchev have worked with the Plovdiv Philharmonic. The orchestra has toured in almost all of the European countries.

The Trakiya Folklore Ensemble, founded in 1974, has performed thousands of concerts in Bulgaria and more than 42 countries.[^cite_note-127] The Trakiya Traditional Choir was nominated for a [Grammy Award](44635.md). The [Detska Kitka Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detska_Kitka_Choir) is one of the oldest and best-known youth choirs in Bulgaria and the winner of numerous awards from international choral competitions. [The Evmolpeya choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evmolpeya) is another girls' choir from Plovdiv, whose establishing patron, [Ivan Chomakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Chomakov), became the then mayor in 2006. The choir was appointed a [Goodwill Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwill_Ambassador) and a municipal choir.

### Literature

Plovdiv is among the nation's primary literary centres. In 1855 [Hristo G. Danov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hristo_G._Danov) created the first Bulgarian publishing company and printing-press.[^cite_note-128] The city's traditions as a literary centre are preserved by the first public library in Bulgaria, the [Ivan Vazov National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Vazov_National_Library), the 19 *[chitalishta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitalishta)* (cultural centres), and by numerous booksellers and publishers. The library was founded in 1879[^cite_note-129] and named after the famous Bulgarian writer and poet [Ivan Vazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Vazov) who worked in Plovdiv for five years creating some of his best works.[^cite_note-130] Today the Ivan Vazov National Library is the second largest national library institution with more than 1.5 million books,[^cite_note-131] owning rare Bulgarian and European publications.

### Arts



The Art Gallery of Plovdiv

The city has traditions in [iconography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconography) since the Middle Ages. During the Period of National Revival, a number of notable icon-painters (called in Bulgarian *zografi*, *зографи*) from all regions of the country worked in Plovdiv such as –  [Dimitar Zograf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitar_Zograf), his son Zafir Zograf, [Zahari Zograf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahari_Zograf), Georgi Danchov, and others.[^cite_note-pat-67] After the Liberation, the Bulgarian painter of [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) origin [Ivan Mrkvička](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Mrkvi%C4%8Dka) came to work in the city. The Painters' Society was established there by artists from southern Bulgaria in 1912 whose members included painters [Zlatyu Boyadzhiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatyu_Boyadzhiev), [Tsanko Lavrenov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsanko_Lavrenov) and [Sirak Skitnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirak_Skitnik).

Today the city has more than 40 art galleries with most of them being privately owned. The Art Gallery of Plovdiv was founded in the late 19th century.[^cite_note-132] It possesses 5,000 pieces of art in four buildings. Since 1981, it has had a section for [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Mexico) art donated by Mexican painters in honour of the 1,300-year anniversary of the Bulgarian State.

### European Capital of Culture

On 5 September 2014, Plovdiv was selected as the Bulgarian host of [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2019.[^cite_note-culturecapital-133] The city co-hosted the event with [Matera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matera).

After Plovdiv was elected as European Capital of Culture in 2019, an ambitious cultural program has started its realisation. For 2019 the City Under the Hills had a number of concerts, including "Balkan Music in Plovdiv". The city held the Plovdiv Biennale and a number of international forums, such as a meeting of collectors from Europe, a summer art school, dance projects, etc.[^cite_note-134]

The Fusion 2019 Urban Games Festival in Plovdiv was one of the standout events of the year, bringing together participants from across Europe—including Bulgaria, Italy, Poland, Germany, the UK, and Russia — to engage in five innovative games and cultural volunteering. This collaborative initiative, part of the European Capitals of Culture project, united Plovdiv and Matera in a two-part journey to explore and reimagine the cultural and historical dimensions of these cities. The diverse backgrounds of the participants and game scenarios (spy plots, space adventures, and mysterious stories, etc.) played a crucial role in this exploration.[^cite_note-135]

The interview with Italian volunteers Giuseppe Nuzzi and Matteo Lilliu, which offers a closer look at their experiences, was conducted by Tatyana Garkavaya. An award-winning multilingual communication specialist, Garkavaya is renowned for her support of the volunteers involved in Plovdiv 2019. Having conducted dozens of interviews and written numerous articles in English, Bulgarian, and Italian, she has been instrumental in promoting the activities and stories of these volunteers [^cite_note-136] amid scandals surrounded the implementation of the Plovdiv 2019 programme.[^cite_note-137]

Plovdiv 2019 has become a promising opportunity for the advancement of volunteering in Bulgaria through innovative creative practices. The country has historically exhibited some of the lowest rates of both formal and informal volunteering in the European Union. According to Eurostat's comparative statistics on voluntary activities, only about 6% of Bulgarians participate in organized volunteering, compared to the EU average of 19%—placing the country at the bottom of the rankings.[^cite_note-138]

Although it is located in the middle of a rich agricultural region, Plovdiv's economy has shifted from agriculture to industry since the beginning of the 20th century. [Food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [tobacco](30942.md), [brewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing), and [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles) formed the pillars of the industrial economic shift.[^cite_note-139] During [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) rule, the city's economy expanded and was dominated by heavy industry. After the fall of Communism in 1989 and the collapse of Bulgaria's [planned economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_economy), a number of industrial complexes were closed; production of [lead](17747.md), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), [motor trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_truck), [chemicals](58721.md), and cosmetics have continued.

Plovdiv is the economic capital of Bulgaria as it has the country's largest economy and contributes 7.5% of Bulgaria's GDP as of 2014.[^cite_note-nsi-140] In 2014, more than 35 thousand companies operate in the region which create jobs for 285,000 people.[^cite_note-nsi-140] The advantages of Plovdiv include the central geographic location, good infrastructure, and large population. Plovdiv has an international airport, terminal for intermodal transport, several connections with [Trakia motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trakia_motorway) (connecting [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) and [Burgas](86758.md)), in close proximity to the [Maritsa motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa_motorway) (the main corridor to [Turkey](11125639.md)), and well-developed road and rail infrastructure which all led to the development of the city as the leading city in terms of industrial output in Bulgaria. Established in 1970, the [Toplofikatsiya Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toplofikatsiya_Plovdiv) company provides generation of electric power and heat and heat distribution for Plovdiv.[^cite_note-141]

The economy of Plovdiv has long tradition in [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), [commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce), [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport), [communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism). Apart from the industrial development of Plovdiv, there has been a significant surge in the IT and outsourcing service sector in the recent years, as well as a double-digit increase in the tourism growth in the city every year for the past 5 years.[^cite_note-tourism-142]

### Industry

Industry has been the sole leader in attracting investment. Industry has been expanding since the late 1990s, with manufacturing plants being built in the city or in its outskirts mainly the municipality of Maritsa. In this period, some €500,000,000 has been invested in the construction of new factories. [Trakia Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trakia_Economic_Zone) which is one of the largest industrial zones in Eastern Europe, is located around Plovdiv. Some of the biggest companies in the region include the Austrian utility company [EVN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EVN_Group), PIMK (transport), Insa Oil (fuels), [Liebherr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebherr) (refrigerator plant), [Magna International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_International) (automotive industry), Bella Bulgaria (food manufacturing), Socotab (tobacco processing), [ABB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABB), [Schneider Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schneider_Electric), [Osram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osram), Sensata Technologies, etc.

### Shopping and commerce

The city's commercial sector is developing quickly. Shopping centers have been built mainly in the Central district and the district of Trakiya. Those include Shopping Center Grand,[^cite_note-143] Market Center,[^cite_note-autogenerated2-144] and two more all on the Kapitan Raycho Street,[^cite_note-autogenerated2-144] Forum in Trakiya, Excelsior, and others. Plovdiv has three large shopping centers: the €40 million Mall of Plovdiv (opened 2009) with a shopping area of 22,000 m<sup>2</sup> (236,806.03 sq ft), 11 cinema halls, and parking for 700 cars;[^cite_note-145] Markovo tepe Mall (opened 2016);[^cite_note-146] and Plovdiv Plaza Mall which is 6 stories high with 127 000 m<sup>2</sup> area, half of which is the parking lot and the rest is shopping area.

Due to the high demand for business office space, new office and commercial buildings have been built. Several hypermarkets have been built mainly on the outskirts of the city: [Metro](305874.md), [Kaufland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaufland), [Triumf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumf_\(hypermarket\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Praktiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praktiker), [Billa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billa_\(supermarket\)), [Mr. Bricolage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Bricolage), [Baumax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baumax), Technopolis, Technomarket Europa, and others. The main shopping area is the central street with its shops, cafés, and restaurants. A number of cafés, craftsmen workshops, and souvenir shops are in the Old Town and the small streets in the centre, known among the locals as "The Trap" ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Капана).

The [Plovdiv International Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_International_Fair), held annually since 1892, is the largest and oldest fair in the country and all of southeastern Europe, gathering companies from all over the world in an exhibition area of 138,000 m<sup>2</sup> (1,485,419.64 sq ft) located on a territory of 352,000 m<sup>2</sup> (3,788,896.47 sq ft) on the northern banks of the Maristsa river.[^cite_note-147] It attracts more than 600,000 visitors from many countries.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-148]

The city has had a duty-free zone since 1987. It has a customs terminal handling cargo from trucks and trains.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-148]

## Transport



[Plovdiv Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Central_railway_station).

Plovdiv's geographical position makes it an international transport hub. Three of the ten [Pan-European corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_corridors) run into or near the city: Corridor IV ([Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden)–[Bucharest](36877.md)–[Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia)-Plovdiv-[Istanbul](3391396.md)), Corridor VIII ([Durrës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durr%C3%ABs)-Sofia-Plovdiv-[Varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria)/[Burgas](86758.md)), and [Corridor X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_X) ([Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg)–[Belgrade](55904.md)-Plovdiv-Istanbul).[^cite_note-trans-149][^cite_note-150] A major tourist centre, Plovdiv lies at the foot of the [Rhodope Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodope_Mountains), and most people wishing to explore the mountains choose it as their trip's starting point.

The city is a major road and railway hub in southern Bulgaria with[^cite_note-151] the [Trakia motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trakia_motorway) (A1) only 5 km (3 mi) to the north. It lies on the important national route from Sofia to Burgas via Stara Zagora. First-class roads lead to Sofia to the west, [Karlovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovo) to the north, [Asenovgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asenovgrad) and [Kardzhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardzhali) to the south, and [Stara Zagora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stara_Zagora) and [Haskovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskovo) to the east. There are [intercity buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus) which link Plovdiv with cities and towns all over the country and many European countries. They are based in three [bus stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station): South, Rodopi, and North.

Railway transport in the city dates back to 1872 when it became a station on the [Lyubimets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyubimets)–[Belovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belovo,_Bulgaria) railway line. There are railway lines to Sofia, [Panagyurishte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagyurishte), Karlovo, [Peshtera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshtera), Stara Zagora, [Dimitrovgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrovgrad,_Bulgaria), and Asenovgrad. There are three [railway stations](26161590.md): –  [Plovdiv Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Central_railway_station), Trakia, and Filipovo –  as well as a freight station.[^cite_note-trans-149]

## Education

Around two thirds of the citizens (62,38%) have secondary, specialized, or higher education. That percentage increased from 1992 to 2001.[^cite_note-edu-156]

Plovdiv has 78 schools including elementary, high, foreign language, mathematics, technical, and art schools. There are also 10 private schools and a [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary). The number of pupils in 2005 was 36,964 and has been constantly decreasing since the mid-1990 due to lower birth rate.[^cite_note-edu-156] Among the most prestigious schools are the [Plovdiv Language School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Language_School), the High School of Mathematics, the Ivan Vazov Language School, the [Sts. Cyril and Methodius High School of Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sts._Cyril_and_Methodius_High_School_of_Humanities,_Plovdiv), the National School of Commerce, and the French High School.[^cite_note-157]

The city has six universities and a number of state and private colleges and branches of other universities. Those include [Plovdiv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_University),[^cite_note-158] with 900 lecturers and employees and 13,000 students; the [Plovdiv Medical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Medical_University), with 2,600 students;[^cite_note-159] the Medical College; the Technical University of Sofia – Branch Plovdiv;[^cite_note-160] the Agricultural University –  Plovdiv;[^cite_note-161] the University of Food Technologies;[^cite_note-162] the Academy for Music, Dance and Fine Arts;[^cite_note-163] and others.[^cite_note-edu-156]

The 2009 [International Olympiad in Informatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympiad_in_Informatics) (IOI) was held at the [University of Plovdiv "Paisiy Hilendarski"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_University), between 8 and 15 August 2009. The 2009 IOI Honorary Patron was [Bulgarian President](335070.md) [Georgi Parvanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Parvanov).

Between 1875 and 1906, the [Zariphios School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariphios_School) was one of the local Greek educational institutions that provided elementary and secondary education.[^cite_note-164]

## Sports and recreation

[Plovdiv Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Sports_Complex?action=edit\&redlink=1) consists of [Plovdiv Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Stadium) with several additional football fields, tennis courts, [swimming pools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_pool), a [rowing base](25736.md) with a 2 km-long channel, restaurants, and cafés in a spacious park in the western part of the city just south of the [Maritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa) river. There are also playgrounds for children. It is popular among the citizens and guests of Plovdiv who use it for jogging, walking, and relaxation. Plovdiv Stadium (55,000 seats) is the largest football venue in Bulgaria.[^cite_note-165]

Other stadiums include [Stadion Botev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hristo_Botev_Stadium_\(Plovdiv\)) (19,000 seats), [Lokomotiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokomotiv_Stadium_\(Plovdiv\)) (10,000 seats), [Maritsa Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa_Stadium?action=edit\&redlink=1) (5,000 seats), and [Todor Diev Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todor_Diev_Stadium) (7,000 seats). There are seven indoor [sports halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_hall): [Kolodruma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolodruma), University Hall, Olimpia, Lokomotiv, Dunav, Stroitel, Chaika, Akademik, and Total Sport. In 2006, [Aqualand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqualand), a water park, was opened near the city centre.[^cite_note-166] Several smaller water parks are in the city as well.

[Football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in the city; Plovdiv has four professional teams. The city has [PFC Botev Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_Botev_Plovdiv), founded in 1912 and [PFC Lokomotiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_Lokomotiv_Plovdiv), founded in 1926.[^cite_note-167] Both teams are a regular fixture in the [top Bulgarian league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_A_Professional_Football_Group). The rivalry between them is considered to be even more fierce than the one between [Levski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_Levski_Sofia) and [CSKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_CSKA_Sofia) of [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia). There are two other football clubs in the city – [Maritsa FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa_Plovdiv) (founded in 1921) and [Spartak Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartak_Plovdiv) (1947).[^cite_note-168]

Plovdiv is host of the international [boxing](4243.md) tournament "Strandzha" which has taken place since 1949.[^cite_note-169] In 2007, 96 boxers from 20 countries participated in the tournament. There is a [horse racing](74711.md) club and a horse base near the city. Plovdiv has several volleyball and basketball teams.



A view from the "singing fountains" in Tsar Simeon's garden.



A view from the City garden.

Three of the city's seven hills are protected natural territories since 1995. Two of the first parks in Bulgaria are located in the city center  –  *Tsar Simeon garden – city garden*, where the very first work of the Italian sculptor [Arnoldo Zocchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnoldo_Zocchi) could be seen, and *Dondukov garden – old city garden*. Some of the larger parks include the *Botanical garden*, *Beli Brezi*, *Ribnitsa*, and *Lauta*.

## Honour

The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) ([minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet)) [3860 Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minor_planets:_3001%E2%80%934000#860) is named after the city. It was discovered by the [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgians) astronomer [Eric W. Elst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Walter_Elst) and the [Bulgarian](151876.md) astronomer [Violeta G. Ivanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_G._Ivanova) on 8 August 1986. [Plovdiv Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Peak) (1,040 m or 3,412 ft) on [Livingston Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston_Island) in the [South Shetland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Shetland_Islands), [Antarctica](18959138.md), is also named after Plovdiv.
