The **Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas** (lit. 'Central Bank of the Philippines'; commonly abbreviated as **BSP** in both Filipino and English) is the [government-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-owned_and_controlled_corporation) [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) of the [Philippines](23440.md). It was established on January 3, 1949, and then re-established on July 3, 1993, pursuant to the provision of Republic Act 7653 or the New Central Bank Act of 1993[^cite_note-ra7653-3] as amended by Republic Act 11211 or the New Central Bank Act of 2019.[^cite_note-ra11211-4]

In 1900, the [First Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schurman_Commission) passed Act No. 52.[^cite_note-act52-5][^cite_note-6] This placed all banks under the Bureau of the Treasury and authorized the Insular Treasurer to supervise and examine banks and all banking activity. In 1929, the [Department of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Finance_\(Philippines\)), through the Bureau of Banking, took over bank supervision.



BSP Complex in [Manila](184334.md) still bearing the 2010 logo, January 2021

By 1933, a group of [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_people) had conceptualized a central bank for the [Philippine Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Government).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontemayor19987-7] It came up with the rudiments of a bill for the establishment of a central bank after a careful study of the economic provisions of the [Hare–Hawes–Cutting Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare%E2%80%93Hawes%E2%80%93Cutting_Act), which would grant Philippine independence after 12 years, but reserving military and naval bases for the [United States](3434750.md) and imposing tariffs and quotas on Philippine exports. However, the Hare–Hawes–Cutting Act would be rejected by the [Senate of the Philippines](299521.md) at the urging of [Commonwealth President](271126.md#Philippine_Commonwealth) [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon). This Senate then advocated a new bill that won [United States President](24113.md) [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt)'s support, this would be the [Tydings–McDuffie Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tydings%E2%80%93McDuffie_Act), which would grant Philippine independence on July 4, 1946.

Under the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines), discussions continued regarding the idea of a Philippine central bank that would promote [price stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_stability) and economic growth. The country's monetary system then was administered by the Department of Finance and the National Treasury, and the [Philippine peso](227672.md) was on the exchange standard using the [United States dollar](18717338.md), which was backed by 100 percent [gold reserve](1056205.md), as the standard currency.

As required by the [Tydings–McDuffie Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tydings%E2%80%93McDuffie_Act), the [National Assembly of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Philippines) in 1939 passed a law establishing a central bank. As it was a monetary law, it required the approval of the [President of the United States](24113.md); [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) did not give his. A second law was passed in 1944 under the [Japanese-controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines) [Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Philippine_Republic) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), but the 1945 arrival of American liberation forces, aided by Philippine Commonwealth troops and recognised guerrillas, aborted its implementation.

### Third Republic and martial law

Shortly after [President](271126.md) [Manuel Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Roxas) assumed office in 1946, he instructed then-[Finance Secretary](36182226.md) [Miguel Cuaderno, Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Cuaderno_Sr.) to draw up a charter for a central bank.[^cite_note-8] The establishment of a monetary authority became imperative a year later as a result of the findings of the Joint Philippine-American Finance Commission chaired by Cuaderno. The commission, which studied Philippine financial, monetary, and fiscal problems in 1947, recommended a shift from the dollar exchange standard to a [managed currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_exchange_rate_system) system. A central bank was needed to implement this proposed shift.

Roxas then created the Central Bank Council to prepare the charter of a proposed monetary authority. It was submitted to [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) in February 1948. The Central Bank Act authored by then Congressman [José J. Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Roy) was signed into law in June of the same year [^cite_note-9] by the newly proclaimed President [Elpidio Quirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elpidio_Quirino), who succeeded the late President Roxas, affixing his signature on [Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) (RA) No. 265 or the Central Bank Act of 1948.[^cite_note-ra265-10] On January 3, 1949, the Central Bank of the Philippines was formally inaugurated with Cuaderno as the first governor.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontemayor199818–20-11] The main duties and responsibilities of the Central Bank were to promote economic development and maintain internal and external monetary stability.[^cite_note-12]

Over the years, changes were introduced to make the charter more responsive to the needs of the economy. On November 29, 1972, President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos)' Presidential Decree No. 72[^cite_note-pd72-13] amended Republic Act No. 265, emphasizing the maintenance of domestic and international monetary stability as the primary objective of the Central Bank. The Bank's authority was also expanded to include regulation of the nation's entire financial system just supervision of the banking system. In 1981, RA 265, as amended, was further improved to strengthen the financial system,[^cite_note-ra265-10] among the changes was the increase in the capitalization of the Central Bank from [₱](227672.md)10 million to ₱10 billion.[^cite_note-pd72-13]

In the [1973 Constitution](19554336.md), the interim *[Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa)* (National Assembly) was mandated to establish an independent central monetary authority. Presidential Decree No. 1801 designated the Central Bank of the Philippines as the central monetary authority (CMA).[^cite_note-pd1801-14]

The Central Bank facilitated loans to [Marcos cronies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_cronies) at Marcos's behest. Through these [behest loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behest_loans), large sums of money went to cronies' projects, including several that were not considered feasible.[^cite_note-15]

In 1984, a Monetary Board report discovered that the Central Bank overstated the country's dollar reserves by approximately \$600 million, which was caused by anomalous transactions made to overseas branches of the [Philippine National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Bank) in a desperate effort to generate non-existent foreign exchange reserves to increase lending and credit creation.[^cite_note-Gonzales2019-16][^cite_note-Chanco2022-17] The Central Bank's financial stability was also undermined by large emergency loans that it made to failing institutions such as [Banco Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Filipino) and financing of government deficits and loss-making assets, which eroded its [capital requirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_requirement).[^cite_note-DohnerIntal1989-18] This eventually caused the Central Bank to become [insolvent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolvency), effectively becoming [bankrupt](4695.md).[^cite_note-Chanco2022-17][^cite_note-Gonzales2019-16]

### Present

Following the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution) which overthrew President Marcos, the [1987 Constitution](19554336.md) adopted the CMA provisions from the 1973 Constitution that were aimed at establishing an independent monetary authority through increased capitalization and greater private sector representation in the Monetary Board.[^cite_note-pd1801-14]

In accordance with this provision, President [Fidel V. Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_V._Ramos) signed [Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) No. 7653, otherwise known as *The New Central Bank Act*,[^cite_note-ra7653-3] into law on June 14, 1993.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontemayor1998128-20] Taking on the reins of the bankrupt Central Bank, the new law provided for the establishment of an independent monetary authority to be known as the "*Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas*", with its primary objective being the maintenance of price stability, which was previously only implied in the old Central Bank charter. The law also gives the *Bangko Sentral* fiscal and administrative autonomy which the old Central Bank did not have. On July 3, 1993, the New Central Bank Act took effect.[^cite_note-ra7653-3]

## Roles and responsibilities

As prescribed by the *New Central Bank Act*,[^cite_note-30] the main functions of the Bangko Sentral are:

1. **Liquidity management**, by formulating and implementing monetary policy aimed at influencing money supply, consistent with its primary objective to maintain price stability,

2. **Currency issue**. The BSP has the exclusive power to issue the national currency. All notes and coins issued by the BSP are fully guaranteed by the [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Philippines) and are considered [legal tender](267793.md) for all private and public debts,

3. **[Lender of last resort](921278.md)**, by extending discounts, loans and advances to banking institutions for [liquidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity) purposes,

4. **Financial supervision**, by supervising banks and exercising regulatory powers over non-bank institutions performing quasi-banking functions,

5. **Management of foreign currency reserves**, by maintaining sufficient [international reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign-exchange_reserves) to meet any foreseeable net demands for foreign currencies in order to preserve the international stability and convertibility of the Philippine peso,

6. **Determination of [exchange rate policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate_policy)**, by determining the exchange rate policy of the Philippines. Currently, the BSP adheres to a [market-oriented foreign exchange rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_exchange_rate) policy, and

7. Being the banker, financial advisor and official depository of the Government, its political subdivisions and instrumentalities and fellow [GOCCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_owned_and_controlled_corporation).

## Organization

The basic structure[^cite_note-31] of the Bangko Sentral includes:

- The **Monetary Board**, which exercises the powers and functions of the BSP, such as the conduct of monetary policy and supervision of the financial system;

- The **Monetary and Economics Sector**, which takes charge of the formulation and implementation of the BSP's monetary policy, including serving the banking needs of all banks through accepting deposits, servicing withdrawals and extending credit through the rediscounting facility;

- The **Financial Supervision Sector**, which enforces and monitors compliance to banking laws to promote a sound and healthy banking system;

- The **Payments and Currency Management Sector,** which maintains the safety and integrity of the Philippine currency and ensuring a well-functioning payments and cash ecosystem;

- The **Corporate Services Sector**, which serves the human, financial and physical resource needs of the BSP; and

- The **Regional Operations and Advocacy Sector**, which manages activities related to regional operations, consumer empowerment and advocacy, and communications.

  [^cite_note-BSPgovernance23-32]

The powers and function of Bangko Sentral are exercised by its Monetary Board, whose seven members are appointed by the President of the Philippines. As provided for by RA 7653 or the New Central Bank Act, one of the government sector members of the Monetary Board must also be a member of [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Philippines). Members of the Monetary Board are prohibited from holding certain positions in other government agencies and private institutions that may give rise to conflicts of interest. The members have fixed and overlapping terms, except for the Cabinet Secretary representing the incumbent administration.[^cite_note-33]

## Microfinance and financial inclusion

In 2000, the General Banking Law[^cite_note-ra8791-36] mandated the BSP to recognize [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance) as a legitimate banking activity and to set the rules and regulations for its practice within the banking sector. In the same year, the BSP declared microfinance as its flagship program for [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty) alleviation. The BSP has become the prime advocate for the development of microfinance. To this end, the Bangko Sentral aims to:

1. Provide the enabling policy and regulatory environment;

2. Increase the capacity of the BSP and banking sector on microfinance operations; and

3. Promote and advocate for the development of sound and sustainable microfinance operations.

The Bank is active in promoting a [financial inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_inclusion) policy and is a leading member of the Alliance for Financial Inclusion.[^cite_note-37] It is also one of the original 17 regulatory institutions to make specific national commitments to financial inclusion under the [Maya Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maya_Declaration)[^cite_note-38] during the 2011 [Global Policy Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI_Global_Policy_Forum) held in [Mexico](3966054.md).

## Anti-money laundering

With [money laundering](19390.md) being one of the problems of the Philippines,[^cite_note-39] the BSP has issued a number of measures to bring the Philippines' regulatory regime on money laundering closer to international standards. In September 2001, the *Anti-Money Laundering Act*, or AMLA, was made into law.[^cite_note-ra9160-40][^cite_note-41] The AMLA defined money laundering a criminal offense, and prescribed corresponding penalties. It also provided the foundation for a central monitoring and implementing council called the *[Anti-Money Laundering Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Money_Laundering_Council_\(Philippines\))* (AMLC). The AMLC is composed of the Governor of the Bangko Sentral as chair, and the Commissioner of the Insurance Commission and the Chairman of the [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission_\(Philippines\)) as members, all acting unanimously in the discharge of the group's mandate.[^cite_note-42]

In February 2013, Philippine President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) signed "Republic Act No. 10365" known as *An Act Further Strengthening the Anti-Money Laundering Law*,[^cite_note-ra10365-43] which aims to strengthen the AMLC by requiring that any suspicious transaction in foreign exchange, real estate, and jewelry and precious metal trading be reported.[^cite_note-44]

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) introduced regulations in September 2025 requiring enhanced due diligence for cash transactions exceeding ₱500,000 per day, as part of efforts to combat money laundering and corruption.[^cite_note-45]

## Logo



Logo used since March 2010. Still used secondarily nowadays

The 2020 logo of Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas was first adopted as the central bank's primary logo on November 20, 2020, with the design receiving endorsement by the [National Historical Commission of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Commission_of_the_Philippines) (NHCP).[^cite_note-46] The circular symbol features a full-bodied gold-colored [Philippine eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_eagle) based on actual photographs of the bird and three stars.[^cite_note-47]

The logo featuring a more stylized rendition of the eagle is still used since March 2010 upon the announcement of an imminent start of print run and subsequent release to the public of [New Generation Currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Generation_Currency_Series) (NGC) from November to December 2010 where an earlier design that is closer to the final during its production was previewed and then unveiled to the public on May 7, 2010. Six months after the unveiling, it was used on NGC banknotes printed from its initial batch in November 2010 to December 2022 and coins from November 30, 2017, until the said month of 2022, and on the central bank's headquarters in Manila and Security Plant Complex in Quezon City from 2012 to 2022. It is concurrently used with the 1993 and 2020 logos since March 2010 and November 20, 2020, respectively.[^cite_note-bsp2010logo-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Museum

Within the main Manila complex of the BSP is the *Museo ng Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas* (English: Museum of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas). Inaugurated on January 3, 1999, as part of the golden jubilee of central banking in the country, the *Museo* showcases the BSP's collection of currencies.[^cite_note-52]

As repository and custodian of the country's [numismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numismatics) heritage, the *Museo* collects, studies and preserves coins, paper notes, medals, artifacts and monetary items found in the Philippines during its different historical periods. These collections have been placed on permanent display at the *Museo*. Designed to "walk" the visitor through a number of galleries dedicated to a specific historical period of the country, the *Museo* visually narrates the development of the Philippine economy, parallel to the evolution of its currency.[^cite_note-50_Years_of_Central_Banking_in_the_Philippines-53] Complementary paintings from the BSP's art collection,[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] together with chosen artifacts, enhance each gallery.

A panoramic memorabilia of central banking in the Philippines, it showcases the strides made in bringing about price stability to sustain economic growth in the country. The exhibition hall also features portrait busts of previous governors.

## Security Plant Complex



Security Plant Complex in Quezon City.

The Security Plant Complex, or SPC, was formally established on September 7, 1978, to safeguard the printing, minting, refining, issuance, distribution and durability of coins, [banknotes](208286.md), [gold bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_bar), government official receipts, [lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery) tickets, internal [revenue stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_stamp), [passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport), seaman identification record books, strip [stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamp), official documents, registration certificates, [Torrens titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrens_title), [treasury warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_\(finance\)), [stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock) and [bonds](60737.md), government contracts, [ration coupons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ration_stamp), [official ballots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot), [election return forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_Philippines), [checks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheque) and other [security printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_printing) or minting jobs of the Philippine government.

Printing of official ballots and other public documents was later transferred to the [National Printing Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Printing_Office) pursuant to Executive Order No. 285[^cite_note-eo285-56] issued on July 25, 1987.[^cite_note-npo-57]

On August 4, 2003, President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) issued "Administrative Order No. 79",[^cite_note-58] which designated the SPC as the sole producer of [insignia of national orders, decorations, and medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders,_decorations,_and_medals_of_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-npo-2-59]

The BSP will relocate its security plant complex from [East Avenue, Quezon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Avenue,_Quezon_City) to the [National Government Administrative Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Government_Administrative_Center) district of [New Clark City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Clark_City) in [Capas, Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capas,_Tarlac) after it signed a memorandum of agreement with the [Bases Conversion and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bases_Conversion_and_Development_Authority) in September 2019. The new currency production facility will be located on a 29-hectare (72-acre) plot near the access road connecting [New Clark City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Clark_City) in [Pampanga](255602.md) to the [Subic–Clark–Tarlac Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic%E2%80%93Clark%E2%80%93Tarlac_Expressway) and it is expected to be completed within two years.[^cite_note-60]
