The **Chess World Cup 2025** was a 206-player single-elimination chess tournament that took place in [Goa](40010153.md), India, from 31 October to 27 November 2025.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] It was the 11th edition of the [Chess World Cup](17269063.md). [Javokhir Sindarov](58816590.md) won the tournament, becoming at age 19, the youngest World Cup winner to date.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The winner, runner-up, and third-place finisher (Sindarov, [Wei Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Yi) and [Andrey Esipenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Esipenko)) earned the right to play in the [Candidates Tournament 2026](75933820.md).

## Format

The tournament is an eight-round knockout event, with the top 50 seeds given a bye into the second round. The losers of the two semi-finals play a match for third place. The players who finish first, second, and third qualify for the [Candidates Tournament 2026](75933820.md), a tournament to decide the challenger for the [World Chess Championship 2026](75936044.md).[^cite_note-\:0-6]

Each round consists of classical time limit games on the first two days, plus tie-breaks on the third day if they are required. The time limits are as follows:

- The two classical games have a time limit of 90 minutes each side for the first 40 moves, then 30 extra minutes for the rest of the game, + 30 seconds increment per move starting from move 41.

- If the match is tied 1–1 after the two classical games, two [rapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_chess) games are played with 15 minutes + 10 seconds increment.

- If the match is tied 2–2, two rapid games of 10 minutes + 10 seconds increment are played.

- If the match is tied 3–3, two [blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitz_chess) games of 5 minutes + 3 seconds increment are played.

- If the match is tied 4–4, two blitz games of 3 minutes + 2 seconds increment are played.

- If the match is tied 5–5, a single sudden death [armageddon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armageddon_chess) game is played. White has 4 minutes + 2 seconds increment. Each player bids for Black's starting time + 2 seconds increment, and the player who submits the lowest time wins the bid and plays with Black. If both players make the same bid, a drawing of lots shall determine the colours. If the armageddon game results in a draw, Black wins the match.

### Schedule

Each round lasts three days: two for classical time control games and a third, if necessary, for tie-breaks. Rounds 1 to 3 run from 1 to 9 November; 10 November is a rest day; Rounds 4 to 6 run from 11 to 19 November; 20 November is a rest day; Rounds 7 (semi-final) and 8 (final and 3rd/4th play off) run from 21 to 26 November.

### Qualification

The following players qualified for the World Cup:[^cite_note-\:0-6]

- The world chess champion as of 1 June 2025

- The women's world chess champion as of 1 June 2025 (WWC)

- The top four players in the [Chess World Cup 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2023) (WC)

- The 2024 World Junior Champion U20 (U20)

- 100 players qualifying from Continental and Zonal events:&#x20;

  - Europe (30+11) including European Championships 2024 (E24, 20) and 2025 (E25, 20), and Zonal 1.10 (Z1.10, 1)

  - Africa (3) including [African Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Chess_Championship) 2024 (AF24, 1) and 2025 (AF25, 2)

- The 13 highest rated players from the June 2025 [FIDE World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_rankings)
