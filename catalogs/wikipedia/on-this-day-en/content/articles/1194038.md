**Anapa** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Анапа, [Adyghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adyghe_language): Быгъуркъал, *Bəġ˚rq̇al*, [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Анапа) is a [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) in [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md), Russia, located on the northern coast of the [Black Sea](3386.md) near the [Sea of Azov](78019.md). As of the [2021 Russian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census), it had a population of 81,863.[^cite_note-10] It is one of the largest children's resorts in Russia.[^cite_note-11]

In ancient times, Anapa was the site of a major seaport and a capital of the [Sindi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindi_people). In the 6th century BC, it was settled by Greeks, who called it **Gorgippia**. In later centuries, the settlement came under the control of [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) and then the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). In 1781, a fortress was constructed there, which became the site of multiple sieges during the [Russo-Turkish wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_wars). It was finally annexed by the Russian Empire in 1829, after several occupations.

### Antiquity



The archaeological site of Gorgippia

The area around Anapa was settled in antiquity. It was originally a major seaport (Sinda) and then the capital of [Sindica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindi_people). The colony of Gorgippia ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Γοργιππία) was built on the site of Sinda in the 6th century BC by [Pontic Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Greeks), who named it after a king of the [Cimmerian Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait).

In the 2nd and 3rd centuries BC, Gorgippia flourished as part of the [Bosporan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporan_Kingdom), as did its guild of shipowners, which controlled maritime trade in the eastern part of the Black Sea. A fine statue of Neokles (a local potentate, son of Herodoros) was unearthed by Russian archaeologists and is now on exhibit at the [Russian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Museum).

An inscription from the ancient city, dated to c. 60 AD, mentions individuals described as "Jews by race".[^cite_note-\:0-12]

Gorgippia was inhabited until the 3rd century AD, when it was overrun by neighbouring native tribes. These tribes, of Circassian or Adyghe origin (specifically of the [Natkhuay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natukhai_people) tribe), gave Anapa its modern name.

### Middle Ages

Later the Black Sea littoral was overrun by successive waves of Asiatic nomads, including the [Sarmatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatians), [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns), [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avars_\(Carpathians\)), [Gokturks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokturks), [Khazars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars), [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) who are native to the Northwestern Caucasus. The settlement was renamed Mapa by the [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) at the turn of the 14th century. Genoese domination lasted until the arrival of an Ottoman fleet in 1475.

### Modern period

In 1781, during the reign of [Abdul Hamid I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_I), French engineers built a fortress at Anapa for the Ottomans.[^cite_note-13]

The fortress was repeatedly attacked by the Russian Empire and was all but destroyed during its last siege in 1829. The town was passed to Russia after the Treaty of Adrianople (1829). See [Russian conquest of the Caucasus § Black Sea Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_the_Caucasus#Black_Sea_Coast). It was included in [Black Sea Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Okrug?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Kuban Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_Oblast) and was granted town status in 1846.[^cite_note-about-3]

It was occupied by Ottomans between 1853 and 1856 during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War). It became part of [Black Sea Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Governorate) in 1896. [Elizabeth Pilenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Pilenko), later named as a saint in the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md), was the mayor during the Russian Revolution. It became part of [Kuban-Black Sea Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban-Black_Sea_Oblast) in 1920, and then the [North Caucasus Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus_Krai) in 1924. According to the 1926 census, the population was 49.6% Russian, 32.7% Ukrainian, 5.7% Greek, and 3.9% Armenian.[^cite_note-14]

During [World War II](32927.md), it was occupied and totally demolished by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) with the help of [Romanian troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Land_Forces) between August 30, 1942 and September 22, 1943.

## Administrative and municipal status

Within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), Anapa serves as the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of [Anapsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapsky_District), even though it is not a part of it.[^cite_note-KrasnodarK_admlist-1] As an administrative division, it is, together with three [rural localities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia), incorporated separately as the **[Town of Anapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance)**—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Krasnodar_Krai).[^cite_note-KrasnodarK_admlist-1] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the territories of the Town of Anapa and of [Anapsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapsky_District) are incorporated as [Anapa Urban Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapa_Urban_Okrug).[^cite_note-AnapaCharter-6][^cite_note-KrasnodarKAnapa_mun-7]

## Tourism

The town boasts a number of [sanatoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanatorium) and hotels. Anapa is served by the [Anapa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapa_Airport). Anapa seldom attracts tourists from outside Russia due to its modest infrastructure and its inconvenient accessibility from Europe via Moscow or [Krasnodar](474128.md).

## Climate

Anapa has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) with mediterranean (*Csa*) influences at the lower elevations.[^cite_note-chen-18] Compared to cities further south along the coast like [Novorossiysk](1190336.md) and [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi), Anapa receives considerably less rainfall but has somewhat colder winters.

## Culture

The Town Theater of Anapa is located on Krymskaya Street. It was opened after the reconstruction of the Town Cultural Center.[^cite_note-25] There are twenty-nine public libraries, including four for children. In 2010 the libraries of Anapa received more than 8,000 books, and magazines and newspapers were ordered costing more than 1,000,000 rubles; in addition, nine hundred CDs were purchased.[^cite_note-26]
