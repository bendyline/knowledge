**Bint Jbeil** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بنت جبيل, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Bint Jubayl*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'daughter of (the) little mountain / [Byblos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byblos)'; Levantine Arabic pronunciation: [\[bɪnt ʒbeːl\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)) is a depopulated municipality that was the second largest municipality in the [Nabatiye Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatiye_Governorate) in [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md) before 2026.

In 2001 the town's population was estimated at 30,000, with the exact population unknown because [Lebanon](17771.md) had not conducted a population [census](6889.md) since 1932.[^cite_note-1] The Shi'ite town was a major [Hezbollah](13919.md) stronghold from 2000 to 2026, when it was depopulated and heavily damaged [in a battle](82919076.md) between [Hezbollah and Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Lebanon_War), and then subsequently occupied and destroyed by Israel.

## History

### Ancient history

&#x20;The [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) origin of the towns' name suggest it dates back to that period. The name itself has several meanings as the word Bint in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) means Daughter. Therefore it could be "daughter of the mountain". In the town, there is evidence from the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) period, as seen in temple ruins, columns and houses.[^cite_note-2]



Bint Jbeil in the early 1850s, by [van de Velde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_William_Meredith_van_de_Velde)

#### Ottoman era

Ottoman rule in [Jabal Amel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amel), which lasted from 1516 to 1918,[^cite_note-3] was often harsh, marked by heavy taxation, periodic military repression of the local Shiʿa communities, and punishment of revolts that disrupted everyday life and stability.[^cite_note-4]

In 1596, it was named as a village, "Bint Jubayl" in the [Ottoman](22278.md) *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of [Tibnin](6787186.md) under the *[liwa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* (district) of [Safad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed), with a population of 238 households and 60 bachelors, all Muslim. The villagers paid taxes on agricultural products, such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), olive trees, fruit trees, goats and beehives, in addition to "occasional revenues", a press for [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) or [grape syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape_syrup), and a fixed sum; a total of 25,220 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-Hutteroth-5][^cite_note-6]

In 1838 [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) noted it as a large [Shia](26961.md) village.[^cite_note-7]

In 1875, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) found it to be a village with one thousand [Metualis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metualis), spread over a low hill and into a valley, with many recently rebuilt houses. He notes that its inhabitants draw water from a well, several ancient cisterns, and two large reservoirs used for animals, and observes around one of these basins scattered stones, including finely cut blocks from a ruined structure and the decorated lid of a broken [sarcophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus). He concluded that the village stands on the site of a former [Jewish](25955086.md) settlement whose original name has been lost.[^cite_note-8]

In 1881, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s [*Survey of Western Palestine* (SWP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine) described it: "A very large [Metawileh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metawileh) village, containing about 1,100 to 1,500 Metawileh. A market is held here every Thursday. The village is well built, and has a [mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_of_Bint_Jbeil). The situation is surrounded by higher hills, though the village is on high ground. The cultivation around is grapes, olives, and arable land. Water is supplied from a spring and many [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) and large birket."[^cite_note-9]

### French Mandate

During the turbulent transition to the [French Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon) in the spring of 1920, Bint Jbeil emerged as a central stronghold for the anti-French [Jabal Amil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amil) revolt. On May 5, 1920—coinciding directly with the fatal assault on the neighboring Christian village of [Ain Ebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Ebel)—armed Shia Muslim irregulars overran the Christian quarter of Bint Jbeil. The resident Melkite Greek Catholic and Maronite Christian families, who had formed a long-standing segment of the town's population, were forcibly expelled from the municipality. This targeted displacement was executed by insurgent leaders to secure Bint Jbeil as the uncontested political and military capital of the regional insurrection, ensuring an internally cohesive base of operations against arriving French colonial forces.[^cite_note-Harris1996-10]

In the 1930s, Bint Jbeil was a major [tobacco](30942.md)-producing town with an educated populace. The town and its vicinity produced around 40,000 kilograms a year by 1936.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

During the wider 1936 labor strikes and nationalist protests across the Levant, French authorities sought to suppress burgeoning independence movements in the South.[^cite_note-Weiss_2010-12] In 1936, the tobacco revolt against [French rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon) broke out in Bint Jbeil following the killing of three protesters by [gendarmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie).[^cite_note-Weiss_2010-12] The town, politically divided between the pro-French Bazzi landowners and [Beydoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beydoun) merchants, was a significant tobacco producer in Lebanon. The revolt was led by young 'Amili intellectuals disillusioned with both traditional leadership and French Mandate policies. Key figures included Musa al-Zayn Sharara, who later became the town's mayor, and 'Abd al-Husayn al-Abdallah. These leaders, representing a new generation educated in [Najaf](200512.md), were critical of the traditional leadership and their pro-Mandate stance. The conflict also revealed tensions between the religious leadership, represented by 'Abd al-Husayn Sharaf al-Din, and the emerging political activists. Sharaf al-Din supported the return of Muhammad Said Bazzi, a pro-French figure, to Bint Jbeil, which led to public backlash and mockery.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

The town was also a shoemaking center exporting to the Palestinian market, but the establishment of the State of Israel and the subsequent closure of the border led to the collapse of the industry, a major factor in the migration of residents to Beirut and abroad, particularly to Africa and the United States.[^cite_note-Fawwaz-13]

### Modern era

During the [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba), Palestinian refugees from the Galilee fled to villages in [Jabal Amel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amel), including Bint Jbeil, before continuing on foot toward Damascus, where they believed conditions were safer.[^cite_note-Abdo-14]



Bint Jbeil in 1989

With the rise of Palestinian militias in Lebanon the [Lebanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Army) attempted to control their activities. In October 1969 the army surrounded 150 Palestinians near Bint Jbeil. In six days of fighting sixteen of them were killed.[^cite_note-15]

Bint Jbeil was briefly occupied by [Israel](9282173.md) in 1978 during [Operation Litani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Litani), and again from 1982 until 2000 during the [1982–2000 South Lebanon conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982%E2%80%932000_South_Lebanon_conflict), when it was severely depopulated; as much as 75% of the population was reported to have left for other parts of Lebanon.[^cite_note-16] It was the scene of occasional attacks on [Israeli military forces](39237.md), such as a [car bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_bombing) carried out by a [Hezbollah](13919.md) member on 25 April 1995 which destroyed the Israeli administrative headquarters in the town.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Almost three weeks later, 15 May, a bomb near Bint Jbeil killed six Israeli soldiers and wounded four.[^cite_note-19] [Hezbollah](13919.md) took control of the town following the Israeli withdrawal from South Lebanon.



Center of Bint Jbeil after the Israeli incursion into southern Lebanon in 2006

As the largest town in the area, Bint Jbeil is sometimes known as the "Capital of the Liberated South" (among Lebanese Shi'ites). It is considered one of the centers with symbolic history for [Hezbollah](13919.md).[^cite_note-20] Under Lebanon's complicated system of [sectarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectarian) electoral representation, the [Bint Jbeil electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bint_Jbeil_electoral_district) is allocated 3 Shi'ite seats in the country's parliament.[^cite_note-21] Hezbollah did well in the area in the 2005 [elections in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Lebanon), winning the local seats to add to its nationwide tally of 14.

During the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War), Israel began an offensive against [Hezbollah](13919.md) and the conquest of Bint Jbeil was one of the first objectives for the [IDF](39237.md). At the start of the operation a [Maglan](26576560.md) reconnaissance unit was ambushed and had to be rescued by [Egoz](4311894.md) commandos.[^cite_note-22] In four days of fighting seventeen Israeli soldiers were killed and most of the town destroyed (See [Battle of Bint Jbeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bint_Jbeil_\(2006\))). On July 15, Israeli missile killed 4 civilians, aged between 60 and 85.[^cite_note-23]

Reconstruction as of early 2007 had been going very slowly, leading to reports of dissatisfaction among the residents.[^cite_note-24] Then-Iranian president [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) visited the town in 2010 to show solidarity for Hezbollah and the local victims of Israel's attacks.[^cite_note-25]

In 2024, during the [Israeli invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon), [Lebanese Army](17779.md) troops opened fire on Israeli soldiers after strikes that killed two soldiers.[^cite_note-26]

During the [2026 Lebanon war](82549840.md) amidst a ground offensive by the Israeli army the settlement became the site of a [large battle](82919076.md) alongside bombing from Israel.[^cite_note-27] The town was subsequently depopulated and destroyed.[^cite_note-28]

In 2014, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) made up 99.50% of registered voters in Bint Jbeil. 97.87% of the voters were [Shiite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims).[^cite_note-30]

At the end of 2022, the town had 586 registered Syrian refugees, which represented a decline from the 1,180 registered there in the summer of 2014.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In 2026 the town was entirely depopulated and destroyed.
