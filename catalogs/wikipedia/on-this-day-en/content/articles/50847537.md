The **Judiciary of Bangladesh** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): বাংলাদেশের বিচার বিভাগ — *_Bānlādēśēra bicāra bibhāga_*) or **Judicial system of Bangladesh** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): বাংলাদেশের বিচার ব্যবস্থা — *_Bānlādēśēra bicāra byabasthā_*) is based on the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh), along with laws enacted by the legislature and judicial precedents established by the [Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh), which is the highest court in the country. The jurisdiction of the Supreme Court has been described in Article 94(1) of the [Constitution of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh).[^cite_note-1] It consists of two divisions, namely the [High Court Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh) and the [Appellate Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Supreme_Court,_Appellate_Division). These two divisions of the Supreme Court have separate jurisdictions.[^cite_note-2]

According to a report, the Bangladeshi judiciary faces a severe shortage of judges. As of July 2017, 1,268 judges deal with over 2.7 million cases in lower courts, 86 High Court Division judges deal with 431,000 cases and 6 Appellate Division judges deal with 13,000 cases.[^cite_note-3]

## Supreme Court

### Chief Justice

The [Chief Justice of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Bangladesh) is the highest-ranking judge of the Supreme Court of Bangladesh and the head of the country's judiciary, overseeing both the Supreme Court and all subordinate courts. The chief justice is appointed by the [President of Bangladesh](404877.md). The Chief Justice sits in the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court with other judges to hear and decide cases, presides over meetings of the full Supreme Court to transact business relating to administration of the court, and supervises the discipline of the judges and magistrates of the subordinate courts.[^cite_note-4]

### Appellate Division

The Supreme Court of Bangladesh has two divisions: the Appellate Division and the High Court Division. The Appellate Division hears both civil and criminal [appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) from the High Court Division. The Appellate Division may also decide a point of law reserved for its decision by the High Court Division, as well as any point of law of public interest arising in the course of an appeal from a subordinate court to the High Court Division, which has been reserved by the High Court Division for the decision of the Appellate Division.

### High Court Division

Article 101 of the Constitution provides that the High Court Division shall have such original, appellate and other jurisdictions, powers and functions as are or may be conferred on it by the Constitution or any other law.

## City Criminal Courts

Main article: [Metropolitan courts in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_courts_in_Bangladesh)

The Code of Criminal Procedure provides for the establishment of separate metropolitan courts in metropolitan areas. As of 2024, the only cities in Bangladesh that have metropolitan courts are [Dhaka](56656.md), [Chittagong](380451.md), [Rajshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi), [Khulna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khulna), [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet), [Barisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal), [Rangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur,_Bangladesh) and [Gazipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazipur).[^cite_note-7]

Metropolitan courts generally deal with criminal offenses committed within metropolitan areas. Currently, metropolitan sessions courts and metropolitan magistrate courts are in operation Metropolitan cities. These courts do not have jurisdiction over civil matters.

## Bangladesh Judicial Service Association

Main article: [Bangladesh Judicial Service Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Judicial_Service_Association)

[Bangladesh Judicial Service Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Judicial_Service_Association) (BJSA) (Bengali: বাংলাদেশ জুডিসিয়াল সার্ভিস এসোসিয়েশন (বিজেএসএ)) is a professional association of judges and magistrates of Bangladesh. The Association headquarters is located at Dhanmondi, Dhaka.

Md. Helal Chowdhury, the District and Sessions Judge Dhaka, is the current President and Bikash Kumar Saha, Joint Secretary at the Ministry of Law, Justice and Parliamentary Affairs, is the Secretary-General of the Association.[^cite_note-8]

## Judicial corruption

This section is an excerpt from [Corruption in Bangladesh § Judiciary](38163102.md#Judiciary).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Corruption_in_Bangladesh\&action=edit)]

The Bangladeshi judicial system is inefficient and under the influence of the executive branch. Political appointments, promotions, and firings are routine, and it can be difficult to enforce contracts and resolve disputes.[^cite_note-Corruption_in_Bangladesh_trans-9] Prosecutors, who earn only \$37.50 a month, are especially susceptible to bribery.[^cite_note-Corruption_in_Bangladesh_biz-10]

In 2010, Judiciary of Bangladesh was ranked as the most corrupt institution of the country.[^cite_note-11] The most prominent aspect of the judiciary of Bangladesh is, it is not an independent institution of the state in that both judiciary and the executive branch of the government are overlapped.[^cite_note-12] Bangladesh's Judicial system is infested by partisanship,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] governmental or political influence,[^cite_note-16] judicial corruption,[^cite_note-17] delays in verdicts,[^cite_note-18] and abuse of power.[^cite_note-19]

#### Judges

On 3 March 2007, [University of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chittagong) revoked the [LLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws) certificate of the judge [Faisal Mahmud Faizee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Mahmud_Faizee) and seventy others for tampering with their mark-sheets, asking them to immediately return their certificates.[^cite_note-20]

On 26 July 2014, it has been reported that three senior judges, AKM Ishtiaque Hussain, Md Mizanur Rahman, Salauddin Mohammad Akram, would lose their jobs because of various types of corruptions.[^cite_note-21]

On 14 August 2016, Bangladesh Ministry of Law said that they have started the process of dismissing four judges, SM Aminul Islam, Ruhul Amin Khandaker, Sirajul Islam and Moinul Haque, for grave corruptions.[^cite_note-22]

#### Impunity

Bangladesh has a huge backlog of unresolved cases both civil and criminal.[^cite_note-23] Political clout is believed to be the main factor behind most of the unsolved murder cases.[^cite_note-24]

According to Global Impunity Index of [CJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists) published on 27 October 2016, Bangladesh occupies 11th position in the list where journalists are slain and killers go free.[^cite_note-25]

#### Politics of trial

In Bangladesh, generally the government files politically motivated cases against the opposition, and these cases get withdrawn or quashed when the opposition takes control of the government in turn.[^cite_note-26]
