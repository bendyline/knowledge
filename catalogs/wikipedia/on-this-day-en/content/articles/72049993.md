General elections were held in [Sweden](5058739.md) on 13 September 2026 to elect the 349 members of the [Riksdag](158651.md), alongside [municipal and regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Swedish_local_elections). The incumbent [Ulf Kristersson cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristersson_cabinet) was a [coalition](6036.md) [minority](271434.md) between the [Moderate Party](202985.md) (M), the [Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democrats_\(Sweden\)) (KD), and the [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals_\(Sweden\)) (L), with external support from the [Sweden Democrats](65873.md) (SD) under the [Tidö Agreement](72007581.md). It was the first general election since Sweden joined [NATO](21133.md) in 2024 and resulted in the left-leaning opposition winning a narrow [parliamentary majority](465640.md).

The main parties contested the election within two broad political blocs. The governing Tidö parties consisted of M, KD, L, and SD, while the opposition was led by the [Social Democrats](202978.md) (S) under [Magdalena Andersson](41178429.md), together with the [Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_\(Sweden\)) (V), [Centre Party](217370.md) (C), and [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Sweden\)) (MP). The campaign focused heavily on [immigration](24537353.md) and integration, [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Sweden) and [law and order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_order_\(politics\)), the [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Sweden), [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Sweden), [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education), [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Sweden), and [energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_Sweden). SD sought a formal role in government after supporting the Tidö government from outside the cabinet during the previous parliamentary term, making its potential entry into government one of the central issues of the campaign.

[Opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2026_Swedish_general_election) showed a closely contested election throughout the campaign. The opposition generally held an advantage in the final weeks, with several polls placing the four opposition parties above 50% of the vote, although their lead narrowed as election day approached. Exit polls initially indicated a wider opposition victory than the final result, with the first projections giving the opposition a clearer parliamentary advantage. The election nevertheless remained close until the final votes were counted, with the outcome ultimately determined by a small difference in support between the two blocs. The opposition primarily gained ground in the bigger cities and won unprecedented landslides in the four largest cities. The opposition also reduced deficits in much of the south and the outer county surrounding [Stockholm](26741.md), whereas the Tidö coalition made big gains in central and northern Sweden. This notably resulted in the Tidö coalition winning historically socialist counties [Gävleborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A4vleborg_County_\(Riksdag_constituency\)) and [Värmland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4rmland_County_\(Riksdag_constituency\)) against the national trend. Many municipalities in former socialist strongholds likewise voted for the right for the first time in a national election, resulting in a very polarised map.

The opposition parties won a narrow parliamentary majority, securing 176 of the Riksdag's 349 seats, compared with 173 for the Tidö parties. S remained the largest party with 28.0% of the vote and 99 seats, while M overtook SD to become the second-largest party with 19.9% and 70 seats, as SD fell to third place with 17.5% and 62 seats. The result marked the first decline in support for SD at a general election since [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Swedish_general_election), and S recorded their lowest vote and seat share since [1911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Swedish_general_election). Following the result, [Kristersson](3246360.md) announced his resignation as prime minister and Andersson began negotiations with the opposition parties over the formation of a new government.

## Background

After the [2022 Swedish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Swedish_general_election), a right-wing [bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloc_\(politics\)) consisting of the [Moderates](202985.md) (M), [Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democrats_\(Sweden\)) (KD), [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals_\(Sweden\)) (L), and [Sweden Democrats](65873.md) (SD) secured a narrow majority of seats in the Riksdag. One month of negotiations between them followed, leading to the [Tidö Agreement](72007581.md) that allowed the M party leader [Ulf Kristersson](3246360.md) to become prime minister and lead the [Kristersson cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristersson_cabinet). It was a [minority government](271434.md) of M, KD, and L with [confidence and supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) from SD.[^cite_note-1] During the following term, the four parties became known as the Tidö parties.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

The 2026 general election was the first parliamentary election in Sweden since the country acceded to [NATO](21133.md) on 7 March 2024, which ended [Swedish neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_neutrality) after more than 200 years of military non-alignment.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-nato-reuter-4] Sweden applied for NATO membership together with [Finland](10577.md) in 2022, following the [February 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-nato-reuter-4][^cite_note-5]

## Electoral system

See also: [Elections in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Sweden)

The [Riksdag](158651.md) is made up of 349 seats elected by [open lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) and [proportional representation](23298.md),[^cite_note-6] with an electoral threshold of 4% of the national vote or alternatively 12% within a single constituency. Of the 349 seats, 310 are elected from 29 constituencies ranging in size from 2 to 41 seats, while the other 39 seats are apportioned nationally as [levelling seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levelling_seat) to ensure parties that passed the 4% national threshold hold a proportional number of seats; these levelling seats are allocated to particular districts. If a party wins more constituency seats than it is entitled to overall, a redistribution of constituency seats may occur to reduce the number of constituency seats won by that party.[^cite_note-7]

General elections are held on a fixed date, the second Sunday of September, at the same time as the municipal and regional elections.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] If early elections are called, the newly elected legislature only serves out the remainder of the four-year term begun by the previous legislature.[^cite_note-11] Polls opened for Swedish citizens who resided abroad on 30 July 2026.[^cite_note-12]

## Campaign



A [ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot) for the Riksdag election, enclosed in an envelope, being dropped into a sealed [ballot box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot_box) by a voting official at Stångenässkolan, [Brastad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brastad), Sweden.

According to an August 2026 [Novus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novus_Group_International) poll, voters considered the ten most important political areas—ranked from most to least important—to be healthcare, education, law and order, immigration/integration, elderly care, the economy, employment, the climate, taxes, and energy policy.[^cite_note-25] Following the [Fagersta school attack](84032991.md) on 21 August 2026, several party leaders cancelled planned speeches ahead of the election.[^cite_note-\:7-26] On the morning after the attack, more than 70 people, including party leaders Ulf Kristersson, Magdalena Andersson, Simona Mohamsson, and Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist gathered at a memorial site for the victims of the attack carrying flowers and lighting candles.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Potential governments

The 2010s and 2020s have seen an increased difficulty for Swedish parties to reach agreements on government formation, keeping with a broader European trend of increased [political fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_fragmentation).[^cite_note-29]

#### Tidö

During the 2022–2026 Riksdag term, the government has been formed by the Moderates (M), Christian Democrats (KD), and Liberals (L), with the support and co-operation of the Sweden Democrats (SD), outside of the government but intimately involved in policy decisions, as regulated by the [Tidö Agreement](72007581.md). This group is referred to as the "Tidö parties" or the "blue-yellow" alliance. In 2025, the Tidö parties announced their intention to continue their cooperation after the 2026 election.[^cite_note-tido-coop-30] How they were going to cooperate was unclear, as SD said they would vote against any government which did not grant them cabinet positions, while L said they would continue to block SD from receiving cabinet positions.[^cite_note-tido-coop-30][^cite_note-SD-Expressen-31][^cite_note-32]

On 13 March 2026, L and SD announced an agreement ("The Sweden Promise"), which would give SD cabinet positions if the Tidö coalition received a majority of seats.[^cite_note-\:0-33] The agreement also included policy goals they would cooperate on, including a national referendum on [adopting the euro as currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_and_the_euro), coinciding with the [2030 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Swedish_general_election?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:0-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-\:1-2] For most of the period 2022–2026, support for L has been well below the Riksdag's 4% electoral threshold according to opinion polls. This has been a concern for the Tidö parties' ability to maintain power and caused some in-fighting, with for example SD's Jimmie Åkesson telling Tidö supporters not to consider lending their vote to L unless they reach at least 3.5% in polls by 3 September.[^cite_note-35]

#### Opposition

The opposition to the Tidö government consisted of the left-leaning red-green bloc, consisting of the Social Democrats (S), Greens (MP) and Left Party (V), alongside the liberal and economically right-wing Center Party (C). The Center Party were in [a coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Alliance_\(Sweden\)) with the Moderates, Christian Democrats and Liberals from 2004 up until the 2019 [January Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Agreement),[^cite_note-36] but unlike their former allies was not a part of the [Tidö Agreement](72007581.md) following the 2022 election, opposing entering into any coalition with the Sweden Democrats and any government reliant on their support.[^cite_note-37] During the campaigning for the election, the Center Party, although maintaining that their ideal government would be themselves, M, KD and L, reaffirmed their total opposition to working with SD;[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] however, they also campaigned on a promise to oppose any government that included the Left Party.[^cite_note-40] The Left Party, meanwhile, campaigned on a promise to refuse to act as [a support party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) for a government led by the Social Democrats, as the party has for much of its history, stating that they would oppose any government that they were not a part of.[^cite_note-41] During the campaign, the contradictory positions of C and V raised questions over the feasibility of a left-wing government, with opinion polling showing that all four parties would very likely be needed for the opposition to command a majority.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

#### Centrist government

A third possible government that has received media attention is a centrist government containing S, MP, C, and KD. This is a preferred option for C,[^cite_note-c-centrist-option-45] but MP are skeptical of the idea.[^cite_note-c-centrist-option-45] Despite some prior "flirting" between Andersson (S) and Busch (KD),[^cite_note-flirting-46] which had drawn criticism from Åkesson (SD),[^cite_note-flirting-46] Busch said on 2 September that KD would vote against Andersson even if it would trigger a new election.[^cite_note-47]

### Returning politicians

Former minister and contender for the leadership of the Liberals [Birgitta Ohlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgitta_Ohlsson) announced on 18 March 2026 that she would be a parliamentary candidate for the Centre party.[^cite_note-48] On 20 March, former Liberal leader (1997–2007) [Lars Leijonborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Leijonborg) announced his return to politics as a parliamentary candidate for the Liberals.[^cite_note-49] Approximately a quarter of members of the Riksdag did not seek reelection, including half of the Liberal representatives.[^cite_note-50]

Graphical summary of the election results

In the preliminary result, only the votes of the eight parties that are represented in the Riksdag are reported separately. The votes cast for all other parties are reported collectively.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] The preliminary count on election night indicated that the opposition left-leaning parties were leading by three seats against the right-leaning government parties and the Sweden Democrats.[^cite_note-58]

With 80% of the votes counted, the opposition parties led by the Social Democrats and Andersson held 175 seats against 174 for the governing Tidö parties led by Prime Minister Kristersson.[^cite_note-59] As counting continued, the opposition's lead increased to three seats, with 176 seats against 173 for the Tidö parties,[^cite_note-auto1-60] although around 30,000 votes separated the blocs and a substantial number of advance votes remained to be counted.[^cite_note-61] The final result was therefore not yet clear, with the remaining postal and late advance votes potentially affecting the distribution of seats.[^cite_note-62]

The result remained subject to formal certification by the [Swedish Election Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Election_Authority),[^cite_note-63] but the last votes cast from abroad and late-arriving early votes confirmed the opposition victory, and the completion of the count also confirmed the three-seat difference between the two blocs.[^cite_note-64] By 17 September, the vote count had been completed, with the opposition parties (49.6%) securing 176 of the Riksdag's 349 seats and the Tidö parties (48.8%) the remaining 173 seats.[^cite_note-auto2-65]

## Analysis

The result of the election marked a change in the balance within the two main blocs.[^cite_note-69] The Sweden Democrats remained a major parliamentary force, but their decline to 17.5% and 62 seats represented their first fall in support at a general election after reaching 20.5% in 2022.[^cite_note-70] At the same time, the Moderates consolidated their position as the second-largest party, while the Social Democrats remained the largest party despite a decline in their vote share,[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] in what was its lowest since 1911.[^cite_note-73] The Social Democrats received 28.0% of the vote, followed by the Moderates on 19.9%, the Sweden Democrats on 17.5%, the Left Party on 8.4%, the Centre Party on 7%, the Christian Democrats on 6.2%, the Green Party on 6.1%, and the Liberals on 5.3%.[^cite_note-auto1-60]

The Social Democrats won every county, performing best in the northern Norrbotten County with 37.5% of the vote.[^cite_note-74] However, the opposition bloc won just nine counties out of twenty one: Norrbotten, Västerbotten,[^cite_note-75] Västernorrland,[^cite_note-76] Jämtland,[^cite_note-77] Västra Götaland, Örebro,[^cite_note-78] Uppsala,[^cite_note-79] and Gotland,[^cite_note-80] as well as the capital, Stockholm.[^cite_note-81] The latter saw the strongest result for the Left Party at 10.0% and the weakest for Sweden Democrats and Christian Democrats at 12.2% and 5.0%, respectively. Conversely, the right-wing bloc led by the Moderates prevailed in twelve counties;[^cite_note-82] the party recorded its strongest result in Halland, where they also performed the best in regional elections.[^cite_note-83] Overall, the Moderates improved to reach second place, while the Sweden Democrats fell to third, recording the first time the party's support declined.[^cite_note-Bryant_2026-84] Nonetheless, the Sweden Democrats outran the Moderates in nine Tidö-won counties, performing well in Dalarna,[^cite_note-85] Gävleborg,[^cite_note-86] Västmanland,[^cite_note-87] Södermanland,[^cite_note-88] Kronoberg,[^cite_note-89] Skåne,[^cite_note-90] Kalmar,[^cite_note-91] and Blekinge.[^cite_note-92]

In respect to the constituencies, the Social Democrats won in 27 out of 29 districts, slipping behind the Moderates in Skåne S[^cite_note-93] and ceding ground to the Sweden Democrats in Skåne NE.[^cite_note-94] Overall, the left-wing bloc won in ten constituencies, performing strongest in the [Malmö Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6_Municipality_\(Riksdag_constituency\)) with 61.3% of the vote behind a very strong performance for the Left Party; having earned 17.2% of the vote,[^cite_note-95] it beat out both the Moderates and the Sweden Democrats.[^cite_note-96] Similarly, the party performed well in the [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg_Municipality_\(Riksdag_constituency\)) and [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Municipality_\(Riksdag_constituency\)) Municipalities.[^cite_note-97]

The narrow parliamentary majority of the opposition parties contrasted with the stronger position suggested by the final opinion polls as several surveys placed the four opposition parties above 50% of the vote, although their lead had narrowed in the final days of the campaign.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] The exit polls initially pointed to a wider opposition advantage, with SVT's Valu projecting 51.3% for the opposition against 46.8% for the Tidö parties and the Sweden Democrats, although the subsequent count produced a considerably closer result.[^cite_note-100] As a result, the narrow parliamentary majority of the opposition left-leaning parties resulted less from a decisive shift toward one bloc than from changes in the relative strength of individual parties within both alliances.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-auto-103]

The outcome left the opposition with a potentially difficult governing task.[^cite_note-Bryant_2026-84] Although the four opposition parties together held 176 seats, differences between the Social Democrats, Left Party, Greens, and Centre Party could complicate the formation of a stable government.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-auto-103] The result also disrupted the Sweden Democrats' previously expected path towards a formal role in government, which would have marked the first time that a far-right party entered the Swedish government,[^cite_note-105] although the party remained influential within the broader right-wing bloc.[^cite_note-106] Coalition negotiations consequently became central to the post-election process, with both blocs seeking to secure a workable parliamentary majority.[^cite_note-Bryant_2026-84]

## Aftermath and government formation

Main article: [2026 Swedish government formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Swedish_government_formation)

On 17 September, with the vote count completed, Prime Minister Kristersson announced that he had handed in his resignation to the speaker to allow the process of forming a new government to begin, with his government assuming a care-taker role pending the completion of the process.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] On 18 September, Speaker Andreas Norlén formally gave Magdalena Andersson the assignment of forming a new government.[^cite_note-110]

### First Social Democrat government formation attempt

Following the resignation, *[Aftonbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftonbladet)* sources reported that the Center Party only saw two options for a government, one consisting of themselves, the Social Democrats and the Greens, supported by the Left Party, who would be given service positions in the government chancellery in exchange, and one consisting solely of the Social Democrats.[^cite_note-111] [Ida Gabrielsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Gabrielsson), vice-leader of the Left Party, commented on the reports, referring to the single-party alternative as unrealistic and calling for talks to form a government including all four opposition parties. That evening, *[Svenska Dagbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svenska_Dagbladet)* sources reported that the Center Party had declined an invitation by Magdalena Andersson to four-party talks, being unwilling to participate until the Left Party dropped their demand to be included in a government.[^cite_note-112]

On 20 September, Gabrielsson stated in an interview that the Left Party was prepared to stand firm in their demand to be included in the government.[^cite_note-SVT28turbulenta-113]

### Electoral fraud investigations

On 21 September, *[Dagens industri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagens_industri)* reported that a preliminary investigation into potential [electoral fraud](382165.md) in [Borlänge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borl%C3%A4nge_Municipality), [Dalarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalarna_County), was underway.[^cite_note-SVT21-114][^cite_note-115] At the center of the investigation was the election of [Mohamed Abdukardir Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Abdukardir_Ali) to the Riksdag and Ahmed Abas Omar to the [municipal council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council_\(Sweden\)) of Borlänge. Both managed to get elected ahead of candidates with higher positions on the Left Party lists by virtue of personal votes. Ali, a relatively unknown politician who had been a member of the Left Party for less than a year and a citizen since February, having immigrated from Somalia, received 648 personal votes, enough to take the Riksdag seat of Left Party's national climate spokesperson [Kajsa Fredholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajsa_Fredholm), who received about half as many.[^cite_note-SVT22-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-Exp20-118]

Police began surveillance of suspicious activities during early voting in Borlänge in August, following an anonymous tip. People were observed attempting to get early voters to use Left Party ballots that had been pre-marked with personal votes.[^cite_note-SVT22-116] [Aftonbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftonbladet) reported that police had documented a person withdrawing money from an ATM and later handing out both money and ballots to voters,[^cite_note-SVT22-116][^cite_note-AB22-119] while *[SVT Nyheter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Television)* reported that they had received information about financial transactions being involved in the investigation, but could not confirm allegations of vote-buying.[^cite_note-SVT23krim-120] It was also reported that the police observed a car driving voters to and from the early election locales;[^cite_note-SVT21-114] according to *[Expressen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressen)*, the driver was a man of Somalian origin with a known criminal background,[^cite_note-121] and according to *SVT Nyheter* the police noticed that rides were being given to voting locales when they were surveilling persons of interests in other criminal investigations.[^cite_note-SVT23krim-120] *[Dagens Nyheter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagens_Nyheter)* reported that the activities suspected to be fraudulent targeted Somalian immigrants of three groups – women, men who did not intend on voting and men struggling with substance abuse.[^cite_note-122]

The police reported their findings to the [Swedish Prosecution Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Prosecution_Authority) on 1 September. The investigation was halted on 8 September after the Prosecution Authority deemed there was insufficient evidence to move forward with it.[^cite_note-SVT23dok-123] A separate criminal complaint was filed by Dalarna's [county administrative board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_administrative_board) on 13 September after they received reports of electoral fraud.[^cite_note-SVT21-114][^cite_note-SVT22-116] The Prosecution Authority resumed the investigation after the election, which according to prosecutor Edvard Oskarsson was because new evidence had become available.[^cite_note-AB22-119]

Ali denied having any involvement in the alleged incidents, stating that he was shocked when informed of the investigation.[^cite_note-124] On 22 September, after nearly a full day of declining to comment, the Left Party went out with a statement saying that Ali had agreed to take a "time out" from politics while the investigation was ongoing. Ahmed Abas Omar stated that he would do the same.[^cite_note-SVT22-116] Ali later criticized the media reporting on the investigation for focusing on his Somalian background, economic situation[^cite_note-125] and for portraying him as not having participated in the Left Party's campaigning for the election, which he stated was incorrect.[^cite_note-Exp20-118][^cite_note-126] Party leader Nooshi Dadgostar continued to refuse to give comment until 24 September, attributing the delay to a need to investigate first.[^cite_note-SVT28turbulenta-113] She stated that the allegations were serious but that the Left Party would wait for the investigation to finish, and that the situation would not affect their position on government formation.[^cite_note-127]

On 24 September, *[Svenska Dagbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svenska_Dagbladet)* published an editorial alleging improprieties in the election in Järva, [Solna Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_Municipality), based on anonymous sources.[^cite_note-128] The following day, [Swedish police](47359232.md) announced that they were opening investigations into five separate complaints of electoral fraud in [Stockholm County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_County), including one in Järva, one in [Nacka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacka), two in [Sigtuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigtuna) and one covering several locations across the entire country.[^cite_note-129]

As of 29 September 2026, the Election Review Board had received 15,000 appeals against the election, a large increase from the 700 and 400 appeals of 2018 and 2022 respectively, and vastly more than seen in any previous election.[^cite_note-130]

### Speaker election

After Magdalena Andersson was tasked with forming a new government, a week was passed by without any success in beginning talks. A new mandate period began on 28 September, and the election of a speaker and vice-speakers were also held on that day. The Social Democrats sought to have [Johanna Haraldsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johanna_Haraldsson) of their party become speaker, which received the support of the Greens and Center Party; however, the Left Party supported [Andreas Norlén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Norl%C3%A9n) of the Moderates, who had held the position since 2018. The Left Party stated that they had confidence in Norlén personally and in his ability to handle government formation talks, and that they believed it would grant stability and that he would be able to act as a neutral outside voice during negotiations between the opposition parties.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-SVT28talman-132]

Andersson responded to the decision by returning to the speaker the task of government formation unfulfilled, stating that she saw no possibility of forming a government as things stood. The Greens stated that the Left Party's decision would make government formation more difficult, and that they were "disappointed".[^cite_note-SVT28sond-133] Social Democrats parliamentary group leader [Lena Hallengren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_Hallengren) stated that the Left Party's decision was a "betrayal".[^cite_note-SVT28talman-132] Center Party leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist accused the Left Party of violating an agreement between the opposition parties to jointly nominate one of the Social Democrats as speaker, while the Greens stated there had been discussions but no agreement.[^cite_note-SVT28talman-132] Ida Gabrielsson denied that the Left Party had entered into any agreement, calling the situation "the most dishonest theatre" she had ever experienced.[^cite_note-134] The Left Party also reaffirmed that they wanted to see an Andersson-led government.[^cite_note-SVT28talman-132][^cite_note-SVT28sond-133]

After being re-elected as Speaker, Norlén called the party leaders to a new series of one-on-one meetings on 30 September to discuss government formation.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-SVT30talman-135] The Social Democrats, Center Party and Greens stated that Ulf Kristersson should be given the task of government formation. Kristersson had the day before stated that it is up to the Speaker to test where the parliamentary majority lies, and that he would be ready to form a government if the parliamentary prerequisites to do so were there, but also commented "last I checked there were four parties proclaiming victory on election night, so that is where the parliamentary prerequisites should exist".[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-SVT29-138] The Liberals, Christian Democrats and Sweden Democrats stated that Andersson should face a Riksdag vote on becoming Prime Minister.[^cite_note-SVT29-138]
