The **Americas**, sometimes collectively called **America**,[^cite_note-oxfordc-3][^cite_note-Burchfield-4][^cite_note-5] are a landmass comprising the totality of [North America](21139.md) and [South America](26769.md).[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] When viewed as a single continent, the Americas are the second largest continent by area after Asia and the third largest continent by population. The Americas make up most of the land in Earth's [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) and constitute the "[New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World)", in contrast to the "[Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World)" of [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia).[^cite_note-oxfordc-3]

Along with their [associated islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_islands_of_the_Americas), the Americas cover 8% of Earth's total surface area and 28.4% of its land area. The topography is dominated by the [American Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cordillera), a long chain of mountains that runs the length of the west coast. The flatter eastern side of the Americas is dominated by large river basins, such as the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_basin), [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River)–[Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes), [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River_System), and [La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata_Basin) basins. Since the Americas extend 14,000 km (8,700 mi) from north to south, the climate and ecology vary widely, from the arctic [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) of [Northern Canada](79987.md), [Greenland](12118.md), and [Alaska](624.md), to the [tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) in [Central America](6121.md) and South America.

Humans first [settled the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_of_the_Americas) from Asia between 20,000 and 16,000 years ago. A second migration of [Na-Dene speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na-Dene_languages) followed later from Asia. The subsequent migration of the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) into the [neoarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Arctic) c. 3500 BCE completed what is generally regarded as the settlement by the [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas). The [Norse colonization of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_North_America) saw several settlements established in Greenland, and perhaps on the mainland, however, these were later abandoned. The Spanish [voyages of Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus) from 1492 to 1504 resulted in permanent contact with European (and subsequently, other Old World) powers, which eventually led to the [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange) and inaugurated a period of [exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_North_America), [conquest, and colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) whose effects and consequences persist to the present.

The Spanish presence involved the [enslavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_colonial_Spanish_America) of large numbers of the indigenous population of the Americas.[^cite_note-9] Diseases introduced from Europe and [West Africa](67393.md) [devastated the indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), and the European powers colonized the Americas.[^cite_note-Taylor_2001-10] Mass [emigration from Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emigration), including large numbers of [indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servitude), and [importation of African slaves](49005.md) largely replaced the indigenous peoples in much of the Americas. [Decolonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_the_Americas) began with the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) in the 1770s and largely ended with the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) in the late 1890s. Currently, almost all of the population of the Americas resides in independent countries; however, the legacy of the colonization and settlement by Europeans is that the Americas share many common cultural traits, most notably [Christianity](5211.md) and the use of West European languages: primarily [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Americas), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language_in_the_Americas), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_French), and, to a lesser extent, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_Dutch).

The Americas are home to more than a billion inhabitants, two-thirds of whom reside in the United States, Brazil, and Mexico. It is home to eight [megacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity) ([metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) with 10 million inhabitants or more): [Greater Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Mexico_City) (21.2 million), [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_Paulo_Metropolitan_Area) (21.2 million), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) (19.7 million), [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) (18.8 million), [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_metropolitan_area) (15.6 million),[^cite_note-indecpop-11] [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro_metropolitan_area) (13.0 million), [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Area_of_Bogot%C3%A1) (10.4 million), and [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_metropolitan_area) (10.1 million).

## Etymology and naming

Main article: [Naming of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_of_the_Americas)



America is named after Italian explorer [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci).

[^cite_note-13]

[^cite_note-14]

The name "America" was first recorded in 1507. A world map created by [Martin Waldseemüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Waldseem%C3%BCller) was the earliest recorded use of the term.[^cite_note-15] The name was also used (together with the related term *Amerigen*) in the *[Cosmographiae Introductio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmographiae_Introductio)*, apparently written by [Matthias Ringmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Ringmann), in reference to South America.[^cite_note-16] It was applied to both North and South America by [Gerardus Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator) in 1538.&#x20;

It is generally accepted that he derived the "America" nomenclature from the name of [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci), an Italian explorer, who explored the two continents. However, [Bristol](36741.md) [antiquarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarian) [Alfred Hudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hudd) suggested in 1908 that the name was derived from the surname "Amerike" or "ap Meryk" and was used on early British maps that have since been lost. Richard ap Meryk, anglicised to [Richard Amerike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Amerike), was a wealthy Anglo-Welsh merchant, royal customs officer and [sheriff](89516.md) of Bristol.[^cite_note-BBC_Hist-17] According to some writers, he was the principal owner of the *[Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_\(1497_ship\))*, the ship sailed by [John Cabot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot) during his voyage of exploration to North America in 1497.[^cite_note-BBC_Hist-17] The idea that Richard Amerike was a 'principal supporter' of Cabot has gained popular currency in the 21st century.[^cite_note-BBC_Hist-17] There is no evidence to support this.[^cite_note-18] Similarly, and contrary to a recent tradition that names Amerike as principal owner and main funder of the *Matthew*, Cabot's ship of 1497,[^cite_note-BBC_Hist-17] academic enquiry does not connect Amerike with the ship. Her ownership at that date remains uncertain.[^cite_note-19] Macdonald asserts that the caravel was specifically built for the Atlantic crossing.[^cite_note-20]

The feminine form *America* was originally used to refer to the newly discovered continent, which is why it was accorded with the feminine names of the other continents: [Asia](689.md), [Africa](5334607.md), and [Europa](9239.md).[^cite_note-Smiths-21]

Since the 1950s,[^cite_note-22] however, North America and South America have generally been considered by English speakers as separate continents, and taken together are called *the Americas*, or more rarely *America*.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-oxfordc-3][^cite_note-27] When conceived as a unitary continent, the form is generally *the continent of America* in the singular. However, without a clarifying context, singular *America* in English commonly refers to the [United States of America](3434750.md).[^cite_note-oxfordc-3]

### English

Main article: [*American* (word)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_\(word\))

Speakers of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) generally refer to the [landmasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmass) of North America and South America as *the Americas*, the *[Western Hemisphere](73162.md)*, or the *[New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World)*.[^cite_note-Burchfield-4] The adjective *American* may be used to indicate something pertaining to the Americas,[^cite_note-OEDAMERICAN-2] but this term is primarily used in English to indicate something pertaining to the United States.[^cite_note-OEDAMERICAN-2][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-oxfcdn-30] Some non-ambiguous alternatives exist, such as the adjective *Pan-American*,[^cite_note-31] or *New Worlder* as a demonym for a resident of the closely related New World.[^cite_note-OED|New_Worlder-32] Use of *America* in the hemispherical sense is sometimes retained, or can occur when translated from other languages.[^cite_note-33] For example, the [Association of National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_National_Olympic_Committees) (ANOC) in Paris maintains a single continental association for "America", represented by one of the five [Olympic rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_rings).[^cite_note-34]

American essayist [H. L. Mencken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._L._Mencken) said, "The Latin-Americans use Norteamericano in formal writing, but, save in Panama, prefer nicknames in colloquial speech."[^cite_note-Mencken-35] To avoid "American" one can use constructed terms in their languages derived from "United States" or even "North America".[^cite_note-oxfcdn-30][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In Canada, its southern neighbor is often referred to as "the United States", "the U.S.A.", or (informally) "the States", while U.S. citizens are generally referred to as "Americans".[^cite_note-oxfcdn-30] Most Canadians resent being referred to as "Americans".[^cite_note-oxfcdn-30]

### Spanish

In Spanish, *América* is a single continent composed of the [subcontinents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcontinent) of *América del Sur* and *América del Norte*, the [land bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_bridge) of *América Central*, and the islands of the *[Antillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles)*. *Americano* or *americana* in Spanish refers to a person from *América* in a similar way that in which *europeo* or *europea* refers to a person from *Europa*. The terms *sudamericano/a*, *centroamericano/a*, *antillano/a* and *norteamericano/a* can be used to more specifically refer to the location where a person may live.

Citizens of the United States of America are normally referred to by the terms *estadounidense* and *estadunidense*[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]\(rough literal translation: "[United Statesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Statesian)") instead of *americano* or *americana* which is discouraged,[^cite_note-norteamerica-40][^cite_note-estadosunidos-41] and the country's name itself is officially translated as *Estados Unidos de América* (United States of America), commonly abbreviated as *Estados Unidos* (EE. UU.).[^cite_note-estadosunidos-41] Also, the term *norteamericano* (North American) may refer to a citizen of the United States, though in formal writing it is less common since it is used for the inhabitants of North America.[^cite_note-42]

### Portuguese

In Portuguese, *[América](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9rica)*[^cite_note-AmericaPortBE-43] is a single continent composed of *América do Sul* (South America), *América Central* (Central America) and *América do Norte* (North America).[^cite_note-AmericaME-44] It can be ambiguous, as *América* can be used to refer to the United States of America, but is avoided in print and formal environments.[^cite_note-EstadosUnidosIt-45][^cite_note-EstadosUnidosESPN-46] The demonym 'American' (americano) is commonly used in Portuguese to refer to people from the United States, although the terms '[norte-americano](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/norte-americano)', '[estadounidense](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/estadounidense)' or '[estadunidense](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/estadunidense)' also formally exist and are preferred by those who wish to distinguish it from the continental demonym.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

### French

In French, the word *américain* may be used for things relating to the Americas; however, similar to English, it is most often used for things relating to the United States, with the term *états-unien* sometimes used for clarity. *Panaméricain* may be used as an adjective to refer to the Americas without ambiguity.[^cite_note-49] French speakers may use the noun *Amérique* to refer to the whole landmass as one continent, or two continents, *Amérique du Nord* and *Amérique du Sud*. In French, *Amérique* is seldom used to refer to the United States, leading to some ambiguity when it is. Similar to English usage, *les Amériques* or *des Amériques* is used to refer unambiguously to the Americas.

### Dutch

In Dutch, the word *Amerika* mostly refers to the United States.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Although the United States is equally often referred to as *de Verenigde Staten* ("the United States") or *de VS* ("the US"), *Amerika* relatively rarely refers to the Americas, but it is the only commonly used Dutch word for the Americas. This often leads to ambiguity; and to stress that something concerns the Americas as a whole, Dutch uses a combination, namely *Noord- en Zuid-Amerika* (North and South America).

Latin America and Central America are generally referred to as *Latijns Amerika* and *Midden-Amerika* respectively.

The adjective *Amerikaans* is most often used for things or people relating to the United States. There are no alternative words to distinguish between things relating to the United States or to the Americas. Dutch uses the local alternative for things relating to elsewhere in the Americas, such as *Argentijns* for [Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_people), etc.

## History

### Pre-Columbian era

Main article: [Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era)



The Plaza Occidental in [Copán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop%C3%A1n), Honduras

The pre-Columbian era incorporates all [period subdivisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_the_Americas) in the [history and prehistory of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Americas) before the appearance of significant European influences on the American continents, spanning the time of the [original settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_to_the_New_World) in the [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic) to [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) during the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period). The term *pre-Columbian* is used especially often in the context of the great [indigenous civilizations of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pre-Columbian_civilizations), such as those of [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) ([Olmec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec), [Toltec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toltec), [Teotihuacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teotihuacano), [Zapotec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapotec_civilization), [Mixtec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixtec_civilization), [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec), [Maya](18449273.md)) and the [Andean civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_civilizations) ([Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahuantinsuyu), [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)), [Chavín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn_culture), [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca_people), [Cañari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1ari)).

Many pre-Columbian [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization) established characteristics and hallmarks which included permanent or urban settlements, agriculture, civic and monumental architecture, and [complex societal hierarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_society). Some of these civilizations had long faded by the time of the first permanent European arrivals (c. late 15th–early 16th centuries), and are known only through [archeological](18951655.md) investigations. Others were contemporary with this period, and are also known from historical accounts of the time. A few, such as the Maya, had their own written records. However, most Europeans of the time viewed such texts as pagan, and much was destroyed in Christian pyres. Only a few hidden documents remain today, leaving modern historians with glimpses of ancient culture and knowledge.[^cite_note-52]

#### Settlement

Further information on theories of Paleo-Indian migration: [Peopling of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peopling_of_the_Americas)



Map of [early human migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_human_migrations) based on the [Out of Africa theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans)

[^cite_note-53]

[The first inhabitants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians) migrated into the Americas from Asia. Habitation sites are known in [Alaska](624.md) and [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon) from at least 20,000 years ago, with suggested ages of up to 40,000 years.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-kind-56] Beyond that, the specifics of the [Paleo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indian) migration to and throughout the Americas, including the dates and routes traveled, are subject to ongoing research and discussion.[^cite_note-national-57] Widespread habitation of the Americas occurred after the [Late Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Glacial_Maximum#North_America), from 16,000 to 13,000 years ago.[^cite_note-kind-56][^cite_note-58]



Statue representing the Americas at [Palazzo Ferreria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Ferreria), in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta), [Malta](19137.md)

The traditional theory has been that these early migrants moved into the [Beringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringia) land bridge between eastern Siberia and present-day Alaska about 40,000–17,000 years ago,[^cite_note-SpencerWells2-59] when sea levels were significantly lowered during the [Quaternary glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_glaciation).[^cite_note-national-57][^cite_note-Smithsonian-60] These people are believed to have followed herds of now-extinct [pleistocene megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_megafauna) along *ice-free corridors* that stretched between the [Laurentide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentide_Ice_Sheet) and [Cordilleran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordilleran_Ice_Sheet) ice sheets.[^cite_note-61] Another route proposed is that, either on foot or using [primitive boats](3996.md#History), they migrated down the Pacific coast to South America.[^cite_note-62] Evidence of the latter would since have been covered by a [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) of hundreds of meters following the last ice age.[^cite_note-63] Both routes may have been taken, although the genetic evidences suggests a single founding population.[^cite_note-64] The [micro-satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsatellite_\(genetics\)) diversity and distributions specific to [South American Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) indicates that certain populations have been isolated since the initial colonization of the region.[^cite_note-subclades-65]

A second migration occurred after the initial peopling of the Americas;[^cite_note-Meltzer2009-66] [Na Dene speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na-Dene_languages), found predominantly in North American groups at varying genetic rates, with the highest frequency found among the [Athabaskans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabaskan_languages) at 42%, derive from this second wave.[^cite_note-67] [Linguists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguists) and [biologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist) have reached a similar conclusion based on analysis of [Amerindian language groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas) and [ABO blood group system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABO_blood_group_system) distributions.[^cite_note-Meltzer2009-66][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] Then the people of the [Arctic small tool tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_small_tool_tradition), a broad cultural entity that developed along the [Alaska Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Peninsula), around [Bristol Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Bay), and on the eastern shores of the Bering Strait c. 2500 BCE, moved into North America.[^cite_note-Fagan-71] The Arctic small tool tradition, a [Paleo-Eskimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Eskimo) culture, branched off into two cultural variants, including the [Pre-Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Dorset) and the [Independence traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_II_culture) of Greenland.[^cite_note-YoungBjerregaard2008-72] The descendants of the Pre-Dorset cultural group, the [Dorset culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset_culture), were displaced by the final migrants from the Bering sea coast line, the [Thule people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_people) (the ancestors of modern [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit)), by 1000 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era).[^cite_note-YoungBjerregaard2008-72]

#### Norse colonization

Main article: [Norse colonization of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_North_America)

&#x20;Around the same time as the Inuit migrated into Greenland, Viking settlers began arriving in [Greenland](12118.md) in 982 and [Vinland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinland) shortly thereafter, establishing a settlement at [L'Anse aux Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Anse_aux_Meadows), near the northernmost tip of [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\)).[^cite_note-73] Contact between the Norse colonies and Europe was maintained, as [James Watson Curran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watson_Curran) states:

> From 985 to 1410, Greenland was in touch with the world. Then silence. In 1492 the [Vatican](13393.md) noted that no news of that country "at the end of the world" had been received for 80 years, and the bishopric of the colony was offered to a certain ecclesiastic if he would go and "restore Christianity" there. He didn't go.[^cite_note-74]

### Large-scale European colonization

Main article: [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas)



*[Landing of Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_of_Columbus)* by [John Vanderlyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vanderlyn), 1847. [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) leads expedition to the New World, 1492.

Although there had been previous [trans-oceanic contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_trans-oceanic_contact_theories), large-scale European colonization of the Americas began with the first voyage of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) in 1492. The first Spanish settlement in the Americas was [La Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Isabela) in northern [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola). This town was abandoned shortly after in favor of [Santo Domingo de Guzmán](57638.md), founded in 1496, the oldest American city of European foundation. This was the base from which the Spanish monarchy administered its new colonies and their expansion. Santo Domingo was subject to frequent raids by English and French [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_Caribbean).

On the continent, [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City) on the Pacific coast of Central America, founded on August 15, 1519, played an important role, being the base for the Spanish conquest of South America. Conquistador [Lucas Vázquez de Ayllón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_V%C3%A1zquez_de_Ayll%C3%B3n) established [San Miguel de Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_de_Guadalupe), the first European settlement in what is now the [United States](3434750.md), on the [Pee Dee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pee_Dee_River) in [South Carolina](27956.md).[^cite_note-75] During the first half of the 16th century, Spanish colonists conducted raids throughout the [Caribbean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Basin), bringing captives from Central America, northern South America, and [Florida](18933066.md) back to Hispaniola and other Spanish settlements.[^cite_note-76]

France, led by [Jacques Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Cartier) and [Giovanni da Verrazzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Verrazzano),[^cite_note-77] focused primarily on North America. English explorations of the Americas were led by [Giovanni Caboto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot)[^cite_note-78] and Sir [Walter Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Raleigh). The Dutch in [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) confined their operations to Manhattan Island, Long Island, the Hudson River Valley, and what later became New Jersey. The spread of new diseases brought by Europeans and African slaves killed many of the inhabitants of North America and South America,[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] with a general [population crash of Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) occurring in the mid-16th century, often well ahead of European contact.[^cite_note-81] One of the most devastating diseases was [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox).[^cite_note-82]

European immigrants were often part of state-sponsored attempts to found colonies in the Americas. Migration continued as people moved to the Americas fleeing [religious persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution) or seeking economic opportunities. Millions of individuals were forcibly transported to the Americas as [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery), prisoners or [indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servitude).



Map showing the dates of independence from European powers. Black signifies areas that are [dependent territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory) or parts of countries with a capital outside the Americas.

[Decolonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_the_Americas) began with the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) and the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) in the late 1700s. This was followed by numerous [Latin American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) in the early 1800s. Between 1811 and 1825, [Paraguay](23420.md), [Argentina](18951905.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia), the [United Provinces of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Central_America), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Peru](170691.md), and [Bolivia](3462.md) gained independence from Spain and Portugal in armed revolutions. After the [Dominican Republic won independence from Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_War_of_Independence), it was re-annexed by Spain in 1861, but reclaimed its independence in 1865 at the conclusion of the [Dominican Restoration War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Restoration_War). The last violent episode of decolonization was the [Cuban War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_War_of_Independence) which became the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War), which resulted in the independence of [Cuba](5042481.md) in 1898, and the transfer of sovereignty over [Puerto Rico](23041.md) from Spain to the United States.

Peaceful decolonization began with the United States's purchase of [Louisiana from France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) in 1803, [Florida from Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams%E2%80%93On%C3%ADs_Treaty) in 1819, [Alaska from Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Purchase) in 1867, and the [Danish West Indies from Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_the_Danish_West_Indies) in 1916. [Canada](5042916.md) became independent of the United Kingdom, starting with the [Balfour Declaration of 1926](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration_of_1926) and the [Statute of Westminster 1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931) and ending with the [patriation of the Canadian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriation_of_the_Canadian_Constitution) in 1982. The [Dominion of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Newfoundland) similarly achieved independence under the Balfour Declaration and Statute of Westminster, but relinquished self-rule in 1934.[^cite_note-83] It was subsequently [confederated with Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_Act) in 1949.

The remaining European colonies in the Caribbean began to achieve peaceful independence well after [World War II](32927.md). [Jamaica](15660.md) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) became independent in 1962, and [Guyana](17238691.md) and [Barbados](3455.md) both achieved independence in 1966. In the 1970s, the [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica), [St. Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lucia), and [St. Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent_and_the_Grenadines) all became independent of the United Kingdom, and [Suriname](26828.md) became independent of the Netherlands. [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md), and [Saint Kitts and Nevis](27198.md) achieved independence from the United Kingdom in the 1980s.

## Geography

Further information: [Geography of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_North_America) and [Geography of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_South_America)



Satellite photo of the Americas on [Earth](9228.md)

### Extent

The Americas make up most of the land in Earth's [Western Hemisphere](73162.md).[^cite_note-84] The northernmost point of the Americas is [Kaffeklubben Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaffeklubben_Island), which is the most northerly point of land on Earth.[^cite_note-85] The southernmost undisputed point is [Águila Islet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81guila_Islet), although the [Antarctic](18959138.md) islands of [Southern Thule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Thule) are sometimes taken into account.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] The mainland of the Americas is the world's longest north-to-south landmass. The distance between its two polar extremities, [Murchison Promontory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murchison_Promontory) on the [Boothia Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boothia_Peninsula) in northern Canada and [Cape Froward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Froward) in Chilean [Patagonia](65433.md), is roughly 14,000 km (8,700 mi).[^cite_note-worldbook-88] The mainland's most westerly point is the end of the [Seward Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seward_Peninsula) in Alaska; [Attu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attu_Island), further off the Alaskan coast to the west, is considered the westernmost point of the Americas. [Ponta do Seixas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponta_do_Seixas) in northeastern Brazil forms the easternmost extremity of the mainland,[^cite_note-worldbook-88] while [Nordostrundingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordostrundingen), in Greenland, is the most easterly point of the continental shelf.

### Geology

South America broke off from the west of the supercontinent [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana) about 135 million years ago, forming its own continent.[^cite_note-89] About 15 million years ago, the collision of the [Caribbean Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Plate) and the [Pacific Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Plate) resulted in the emergence of a series of volcanoes along the border that created a number of islands. The gaps in the archipelago of Central America filled in with material eroded off North America and South America, plus new land created by continued volcanism. By three million years ago, the continents of North America and South America were linked by the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama), thereby forming the single landmass of the Americas.[^cite_note-90] The [Great American Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Interchange) resulted in many species being spread across the Americas, such as the [cougar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar), [procyonids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procyonidae), [porcupines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_porcupine), [opossums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opossum), [armadillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadillo), and [hummingbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hummingbird).[^cite_note-91]

### Topography



[Aconcagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aconcagua), in [Argentina](18951905.md), is the highest peak in the Americas.

The geography of the western Americas is dominated by the [American Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cordillera), with the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) running along the west coast of South America[^cite_note-92] and the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) and other [North American Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Cordillera) ranges running along the western side of North America.[^cite_note-93] The 2,300-kilometer-long (1,400 mi) [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) run along the east coast of North America from [Alabama](303.md) to [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\)).[^cite_note-94] North of the Appalachians, the [Arctic Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Cordillera) runs along the eastern coast of Canada.[^cite_note-95]

The largest mountain ranges are the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) and [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains). The [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) and the [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range) reach similar altitudes as the Rocky Mountains, but are significantly smaller. In North America, the greatest number of [fourteeners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteener) are in the United States, and more specifically in the US state of [Colorado](5399.md). The highest peaks of the Americas are located in the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_the_Andes), with [Aconcagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aconcagua) of [Argentina](18951905.md) being the highest; in North America [Denali](207247.md) (Mount McKinley) in the US state of [Alaska](624.md) is the tallest.

Between its coastal mountain ranges, North America has vast flat areas. The [Interior Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Plains) spread over much of the continent, with low relief.[^cite_note-96] The [Canadian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield) covers almost 5 million km<sup>2</sup> of North America and is generally quite flat.[^cite_note-97] Similarly, the north-east of South America is covered by the flat [Amazon basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_basin).[^cite_note-98] The [Brazilian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Highlands) on the east coast are fairly smooth but show some variations in landform, while farther south the [Gran Chaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Chaco) and [Pampas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampas) are broad [lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland).[^cite_note-99]

### Climate



Climate zones of the Americas in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system

The climate of the Americas varies significantly from region to region. [Tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) occurs in the latitudes of the [Amazon](48139.md), American [cloud forests](58903.md), southeastern Florida and [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap). In the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) and [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes), dry and continental climates are observed. Often the higher altitudes of these mountains are snow-capped.

Southeastern North America is well known for its occurrence of [tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornadoes) and [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricanes), of which the vast majority of tornadoes occur in the United States' [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley),[^cite_note-Science_News_1-100] as well as in the southerly [Dixie Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Alley) in the North American late-winter and early spring seasons. Often parts of the Caribbean are exposed to the violent effects of hurricanes. These weather systems are formed by the collision of dry, cool air from Canada and wet, warm air from the Atlantic.

### Hydrology

With coastal mountains and interior plains, the Americas have several large [river basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_basin) that drain the continents. The largest river basin in North America is that of the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River), covering the second-largest watershed on the planet.[^cite_note-101] The Mississippi–Missouri river system drains most of 31 states of the United States, most of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains), and large areas between the Rocky and Appalachian mountains. The Mississippi–Missouri is the [fourth-longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_by_length) river system and has the [tenth-greatest discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_by_discharge) in the world.

In North America, to the east of the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains), there are no major rivers but rather a series of rivers and streams that flow east with their terminus in the Atlantic Ocean, such as the [Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River), [Saint John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John_River_\(Bay_of_Fundy\)), and [Savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_River) rivers. A similar instance arises with central Canadian rivers that drain into [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay); the largest being the [Churchill River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_River_\(Hudson_Bay\)). On the west coast of North America, the main rivers are the [Colorado](180968.md), [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River), [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon_River), [Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_River), and [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_River) rivers.

The Colorado River drains much of the [Southern Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Rockies) and parts of the [Basin and Range Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basin_and_Range_Province). The river flows approximately 1,450 miles (2,330 km) into the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California),[^cite_note-largest-102] during which over time it has carved out natural phenomena such as the [Grand Canyon](46989.md) and created phenomena such as the [Salton Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salton_Sea). The Columbia is a large river, 1,243 miles (2,000 km) long, in central western North America and is the most powerful river on the West Coast of the Americas. In the far northwest of North America, the Yukon drains much of the Alaskan peninsula and flows 1,980 miles (3,190 km)[^cite_note-103] from parts of Yukon and the Northwest Territory to the Pacific. Draining to the Arctic Ocean of Canada, the [Mackenzie River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackenzie_River) drains waters from the Arctic Great Lakes of Arctic Canada, as opposed to the St Lawrence River that drains the Great Lakes of southern Canada into the Atlantic Ocean. The Mackenzie River is the largest in Canada and drains 1,805,200 km<sup>2</sup> (697,000 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-104]

The largest river basin in South America is that of the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin), which has the highest volume flow of any river on Earth.[^cite_note-105] The second largest watershed of South America is that of the [Paraná River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River), which covers about 2,582,074 km<sup>2</sup> (996,944 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-106]

### Ecology

North America and South America began to develop a shared population of flora and fauna about 2.5 million years ago, when [continental drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift) brought the two continents into contact via the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama). Initially, the exchange of biota was roughly equal, with North American genera migrating into South America in about the same proportions as South American genera migrated into North America. This exchange is known as the [Great American Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Interchange). The exchange became lopsided after roughly a million years, with the total spread of South American genera into North America far more limited in scope than the spread of North American genera into South America.[^cite_note-107]

In exports and imports, in 2020, the United States was the world's second largest exporter (US\$1.64 trillion) and the largest importer (US\$2.56 trillion). Mexico was the tenth largest exporter and importer. Canada was the twelfth largest exporter and importer. Brazil was the 24th largest exporter and the 28th largest importer. Chile was the 45th largest exporter and the 47th largest importer. Argentina was the 46th largest exporter and the 52nd largest importer. Colombia was the 54th largest exporter and the 51st largest importer; among others.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

The [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) of the continent is very strong and varied. Countries such as [United States](3434750.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), and [Argentina](18951905.md) are among the largest agricultural producers on the planet. In 2019, the continent dominated the world production of [soy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy) (almost 90% of the world total, with Brazil, the United States, Argentina, Paraguay, Canada and Bolivia among the 10 largest on the planet), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) (about 55% of the world total, with Brazil, Mexico, the United States, and Guatemala among the 10 largest on the planet), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) (about 55% of the world total, with Brazil, Colombia, Honduras, Peru, and Guatemala among the 10 largest on the planet) and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) (about 48% of the world total, with the United States, Brazil, Argentina, and Mexico among the 10 largest on the planet). The continent also produces almost 40% of world's [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)) (with Brazil, the United States, and Mexico among the top 10 producers), about 37% of world's [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple) (with Costa Rica, Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia among the 10 largest producers), about 35% of world's [lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon) (with Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, and the United States among the 10 largest producers) and about 30% of world's [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) (with the United States, Brazil, Mexico and Argentina among the top 10 producers), among several other products.[^cite_note-auto-142]

In [livestock](25160767.md), the Americas are major producers. In 2018, the continent produced about 45% of the world's [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef) (with the United States, Brazil, Argentina, Mexico, and Canada among the world's 10 largest producers); about 36% of the world's [chicken meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_meat) (with the United States, Brazil, and Mexico among the world's 10 largest producers), and about 28% of the world's [cow's milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow's_milk) (with the United States and Brazil among the 10 largest producers in the world), among other products.[^cite_note-auto-142]

In industrial terms, the [World Bank](36752.md) lists the top producing countries each year, based on the total value of production. According to the 2019 list, the United States has the second most valuable industry in the world (US\$2.3 trillion), Mexico has the 12th most valuable industry in the world (US\$217.8 billion), Brazil has the 13th most valuable industry in the world (US\$173.6 billion), Canada has the 15th most valuable industry in the world (US\$151.7 billion), Venezuela the 30th largest (US\$58.2 billion, but depends on the oil to obtain this amount), Argentina was the 31st largest (US\$57.7 billion), Colombia the 46th largest (US\$35.4 billion), Peru the 50th largest (\$28.7 billion), and Chile the 51st largest (US\$28.3 billion), among others.[^cite_note-143]

In the production of [oil](23195.md), the continent had 8 of the 30 largest world producers in 2020: United States (1st), Canada (4th), Brazil (8th), Mexico (14th), Colombia (20th), Venezuela (26th), Ecuador (27th), and Argentina (28th).[^cite_note-144]

In the production of [natural gas](22131.md), the continent had 8 of the 32 largest world producers in 2015: United States (1st), Canada (5th), Argentina (18th), Trinidad and Tobago (20th), Mexico (21st), Venezuela (28th), Bolivia (31st), and Brazil (32nd).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

In the production of [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), the continent had 5 of the 30 largest world producers in 2018: United States (3rd), Colombia (12th), Canada (13th), Mexico (24th), and Brazil (27th).[^cite_note-147]

In the production of [vehicles](163778.md), the continent had 5 of the 30 largest world producers in 2019: United States (2nd), Mexico (7th), Brazil (9th), Canada (12th), and Argentina (28th).[^cite_note-148]

In the production of [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel), the continent had 5 of the 31 largest world producers in 2019: United States (4th), Brazil (9th), Mexico (15th), Canada (18th), and Argentina (31st).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

In [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), the continent has large productions of [gold](12240.md) (mainly in the United States, Canada, Peru, Mexico, Brazil, and Argentina);[^cite_note-151] [silver](27119.md) (mainly in Mexico, Peru, Chile, Bolivia, Argentina, and the United States);[^cite_note-152] [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) (mainly in Chile, Peru, the United States, Mexico, and Brazil);[^cite_note-153] [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum) (Canada and the United States);[^cite_note-154] [iron ore](145352.md) (Brazil, Canada, the United States, Peru, and Chile);[^cite_note-155] [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) (Peru, the United States, Mexico, Bolivia, Canada, and Brazil);[^cite_note-156] [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum) (Chile, Peru, Mexico, Canada, and the United States);[^cite_note-157] [lithium](17561.md) (Chile, Argentina, Brazil, and Canada);[^cite_note-158] [lead](17747.md) (Peru, the United States, Mexico, and Bolivia);[^cite_note-159] [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) (Brazil, Jamaica, Canada, and the United States);[^cite_note-160] [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) (Peru, Bolivia, and Brazil);[^cite_note-161] [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) (Brazil and Mexico);[^cite_note-162] [antimony](898.md) (Bolivia, Mexico, Guatemala, Canada, and Ecuador);[^cite_note-163] [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) (Canada, Brazil, Dominican Republic, Cuba, and the United States);[^cite_note-164] [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium) (Brazil and Canada);[^cite_note-165] [rhenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenium) (Chile and the United States);[^cite_note-166] and [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine) (Chile),[^cite_note-167] among others.

[Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica), [Panama](22997.md), and the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) have the fastest-growing economy in the Americas according to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF),[^cite_note-168] 16, five to seven countries in the southern part of the Americas had weakening economies in decline, compared to only three countries in the northern part of the Americas.[^cite_note-IMFNorthAmericaGDP-169][^cite_note-IMFUNASURGDP-170] Haiti has the lowest GDP per capita in the Americas, although its economy was growing slightly as of 2016.[^cite_note-IMFNorthAmericaGDP-169][^cite_note-IMFUNASURGDP-170]

### Largest urban centers

See also: [Largest cities in the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_in_the_Americas) and [List of metropolitan areas in the Americas by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_the_Americas_by_population)

There are three urban centers that each hold titles for being the largest population area based on the three main demographic concepts:[^cite_note-wider-171]

- [City proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_proper)

A city proper is the locality with legally fixed boundaries and an administratively recognized urban status that is usually characterized by some form of local government.

- [Urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area)

An urban area is characterized by higher population density and vast human features in comparison to areas surrounding it. Urban areas may be cities, towns or conurbations, but the term is not commonly extended to rural settlements such as villages and hamlets. Urban areas are created and further developed by the process of urbanization and do not include large swaths of rural land, as do metropolitan areas.

- [Metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area)

Unlike an urban area, a metropolitan area includes not only the urban area, but also satellite cities *plus intervening rural land* that is socio-economically connected to the urban core city, typically by employment ties through commuting, with the urban core city being the primary labor market.

In accordance with these definitions, the three largest population centers in the Americas are: [Mexico City](18987.md), anchor to the largest metropolitan area in the Americas; New York City, anchor to the largest urban area in the Americas; and São Paulo, the largest city proper in the Americas. All three cities maintain [Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network#Alpha) classification and large scale influence.

The population of the Americas is made up of the descendants of four large [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) and their combinations.

- The [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), being Amerindians and [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit)-[Yupik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yupik_peoples)-[Unangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleut).

- Those of European ancestry, mainly Spanish, English, Irish, [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), [German](152735.md), [Italian](385155.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people).

- Those of [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora_in_the_Americas) ancestry, mainly of West African descent.

- [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people), that is, those of [East Asian](19605700.md), [Southeast Asian](28741.md), and [South Asian](21566765.md) ancestry.

- [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) ([Métis people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis) in Canada), those of mixed European and Amerindian ancestry.

- [Mulattoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulatto), people of mixed African and European ancestry.

- [Zambos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambo) (Spanish) or Cafuzos (Portuguese), those of mixed African and Indigenous ancestry.

The majority of the population lives in [Latin America](18524.md), named for its predominant cultures, rooted in [Latin Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Europe#Romance) (including the two dominant languages, Spanish and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), both [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages)), more specifically in the [Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) nations of [Portugal](23033.md) and Spain (hence the use of the term [Ibero-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibero-America) as a synonym). Latin America is typically contrasted with [Anglo-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-America), where English, a [Germanic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages), is prevalent, and which comprises Canada (with the exception of [Francophone Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_French) rooted in Latin Europe \[France]—see [Quebec](7954867.md) and [Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia)) and the United States. Both countries are located in North America, with cultures deriving predominantly from [British](19097669.md) and other [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) roots.

#### Black population



*Slavery in Brazil* by [Jean-Baptiste Debret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Debret) (1834–1839)

The transatlantic slave trade brought millions of Africans to the territories of the Americas under the colonial rule of European powers, especially South America. In South America, [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) played a significant role in the trafficking of enslaved Africans, with estimates suggesting that about 40% of all Africans forcibly brought to the Americas were taken to Brazil alone, to work primarily in sugar cane plantations, mining, and agricultural endeavors.[^cite_note-184] In North America, the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) was heavily involved in the transatlantic slave trade, with the establishment of colonies such as Virginia, where enslaved Africans were primarily used as labor in tobacco plantations and later in other agricultural and domestic sectors. This system perpetuated for centuries, shaping the socio-economic landscape of all nations of the hemisphere.

After the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) led by [Jean-Jacques Dessalines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Dessalines), which started in 1791 and was the only successful slave revolt in history, the world's first black republic was established. It started with the massacre of the white population, between 3,000 and 5,000 white men and women of all ages were killed. Dessalines declared:

Oui, nous avons rendu à ces vrais cannibales guerre pour guerre, crimes pour crimes, outrages pour outrages.\
&#x20;Oui, j’ai sauvé mon pays, j’ai vengé l’Amérique.

Yes, we have rendered to these true cannibals war for war, crime for crime, outrage for outrage;\
&#x20;Yes, I have saved my country – I have avenged America.

—  Dessalines, first president of the Republic of Haiti, 1804[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]&#x20;

### Languages

Main articles: [Indigenous languages of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas), [Languages of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_North_America), and [Languages of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_South_America)



Languages spoken in the Americas

Various [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) are spoken in the Americas. Some are of European origin, others are spoken by indigenous peoples or are the mixture of various languages like the different creoles.[^cite_note-latbar-200]

The most widely spoken [first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) in the Americas is [Spanish](26825.md), followed by [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language).[^cite_note-212] The dominant language of [Latin America](18524.md) is Spanish, though the most populous nation in Latin America, [Brazil](3383.md), speaks Portuguese. Small enclaves of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)-, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language)- and English-speaking regions also exist in Latin America, notably in [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana), [Suriname](26828.md), and [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) and [Guyana](17238691.md) respectively. [Haitian Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Creole) is dominant in the nation of [Haiti](13373.md), where French is also spoken. [Native languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas) are more prominent in Latin America than in [Anglo-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-America), with [Nahuatl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl_language), [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_languages), [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_language), and [Guaraní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaran%C3%AD_language) as the most common. Various other native languages are spoken with less frequency across both Anglo-America and Latin America. [Creole languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) other than Haitian Creole are also spoken in parts of Latin America.

The dominant language of Anglo-America is English. French is also official in [Canada](5042916.md), where it is the predominant language in [Quebec](7954867.md) and an official language in [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick) along with English. It is also an important language in [Louisiana](18130.md), and in parts of [New Hampshire](21134.md), [Maine](19977.md), and [Vermont](32578.md). Spanish has kept an ongoing presence in the [Southwestern United States](179571.md), which formed part of the [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain), especially in [California](5407.md) and [New Mexico](21649.md), where [a distinct variety of Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexican_Spanish) spoken since the 17th century has survived. It has more recently become widely spoken in other parts of the [United States](3434750.md) because of heavy immigration from Latin America. High levels of immigration in general have brought great linguistic diversity to Anglo-America, with over 300 languages known to be spoken in the United States alone, but most languages are spoken only in small enclaves and by relatively small immigrant groups.

The nations of Guyana, Suriname, and Belize are generally considered not to fall into either Anglo-America or Latin America because of their language differences from Latin America, geographic differences from Anglo-America, and cultural and historical differences from both regions; English is the primary language of Guyana and Belize, and Dutch is the primary language of Suriname.

Most of the non-native languages have, to different degrees, evolved differently from the mother country, but are usually still mutually intelligible. Some have combined, however, which has even resulted in completely new languages, such as [Papiamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papiamento), which is a combination of Portuguese, Spanish, Dutch (representing the respective colonizers), native [Arawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawak), various [African languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Africa), and, more recently English. The [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) [Portuñol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portu%C3%B1ol), a mixture of Portuguese and Spanish, is spoken in the border regions of Brazil and neighboring Spanish-speaking countries.[^cite_note-213] More specifically, [Riverense Portuñol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverense_Portu%C3%B1ol_language) is spoken by about 100,000 people in the border regions of Brazil and [Uruguay](31843.md). Because of [immigration](4599312.md), there are many communities where other languages are spoken from all parts of the world, especially in the United States, Brazil, [Argentina](18951905.md), Canada, [Chile](5489.md), [Costa Rica](5551.md), and Uruguay—very important destinations for immigrants.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216]

### Sports

See also: [Pan American Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Championship)

[Baseball](3850.md) is one of the most popular sports in the Americas, having spread through the hemisphere after the growth of US influence in the late 19th century. It [overtook cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket#Decline_in_baseball-playing_countries), a fellow bat-and-ball sport spread by earlier British influence, in most places outside of the [Commonwealth Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Caribbean). Other [American sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_sports), such as [basketball](3921.md), have grown throughout the region over time.[^cite_note-217]

[Football](10568.md) is perhaps the most popular and followed sport in the Americas. The North American region which also includes the Central and Caribbean national associations is overseen by [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_North,_Central_America_and_Caribbean_Association_Football), while the South American region and its national teams are represented by [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL).
